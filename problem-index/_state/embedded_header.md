# Embedded-Research Index

**Second cut across the same 1,530 pockets.** The `_scorecard-index.md` rubric was built around one question — *is the insight the invoice?* — and it rejects, by design, every business where research is a **required step in delivering something else**. This index recovers that population.

The distinction matters commercially. In the primary index the research *is* the product, so improving it is a product decision and the seller is protective of it. Here the research is a cost of delivery: the customer is buying a policy, a certificate, a prescription, a settlement, a formula. Efficiency in the research step goes straight to margin, and there is no fear of commoditising the thing being sold.

---

## Method

Same five scores, read differently.

**Eligibility gate — Q1 = 3 exactly.** Q1 measures how directly the analysis is invoiced. A 5 is a research firm and belongs in the primary index. A 2 or below is an internal cost centre with no external customer for the work. A **3** is the target: the research materially gates a deliverable that is sold to someone else. Q1 is therefore a gate, not a scored term.

**Fit score = Q2×3 + Q3×2 + Q4×2 + Q5×2 — maximum 45.**

| Term | Weight | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Q2 Research labour mass + repeatable | ×3 | The discriminating variable — how many people perform the research step |
| Q3 Proprietary data moat | ×2 | What the delivery step accumulates and discards |
| Q4 External clock | ×2 | Delivery deadlines are usually harder here than in the primary index |
| Q5 Buyer + market | ×2 | Unchanged |

**Kill switches** are applied exactly as before, and cross-referenced duplicates are excluded.

**Threshold: {{THRESH}}/45.**

Scores are read from the existing Pass 2 scorecards. **No pocket has been re-scored** — a pocket that scores 47/60 in the primary index still scores 47/60 there. This is a different question asked of the same evidence.

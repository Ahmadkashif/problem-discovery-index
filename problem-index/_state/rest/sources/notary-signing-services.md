# Sources — Notary & Signing Services

## Checked
- About 4.4 million commissioned notaries in the US, a figure the NNA says stayed stable in its 2022 Notary Census (the NNA counts every five years from state data and notary surveys); the 2012 census counted 4,380,351 commission-holders (nationalnotary.org returned 403 to direct fetch; figures from search-result extracts of the NNA pages) — https://nationalnotary.org/notary-bulletin/blog/2022/08/2022-nna-notary-census-part-2-4-4-million-u-s-notaries-are-adapting-to-serve-the-nation-today and https://www.nationalnotary.org/press-center/nna-press-releases/official-2012-nna-census (accessed 2026-10-22)
- NAICS 541120 Offices of Notaries covers establishments, other than lawyers' offices, that mainly draft, approve and execute legal documents and receive, index and store them; notaries who only administer oaths, take affidavits or certify signatures, without power to draft documents, are classified in 541199 All Other Legal Services (Census definition as reproduced by IBISWorld and item.com) — https://www.ibisworld.com/classifications/naics/541120/offices-of-notaries/ and https://www.item.com/naics/541120 (accessed 2026-10-22)
- ALTA Seller Impersonation Fraud survey (sponsored by CertifID, published 14 September 2026; 245 respondents): ~59% of title firms saw at least one seller impersonation attempt in 2025, up from 28% in the 2024 survey (783 respondents); 45% saw one in the last month (from 19%); 23% saw three or more (from 4%); 1 in 4 firms with an attempt paid a claim, and half of those disclosing said the average payout exceeded $100,000; vacant land the most common target; absentee, free-and-clear and recently deceased owners targeted; 58% said image or voice deepfakes were at least somewhat common; top red flags are sellers avoiding meetings or calls, sellers requesting a remote signing or a seller-chosen notary, and cash transactions; 98% use at least one fraud tool, about five on average — https://www.nar.realtor/news/real-estate-news/6-in-10-title-insurance-firms-experienced-seller-impersonation-fraud-attempts-last-year-study-finds (accessed 2026-10-22). The 2024 ALTA prevention-tool shares (ID verification 92%, contacting the seller 90%, multifactor authentication 89%, approved notary 88%, in-person closing 76%) come from a search-result summary of the survey coverage — https://www.scotsmanguide.com/?p=125760 (accessed 2026-10-22, via search-result extract)
- FBI IC3 real estate fraud: 12,368 complaints and more than $275M in losses in 2025; 9,359 complaints and more than $173M in 2024 (NAR, 13 April 2026) — https://www.nar.realtor/news/real-estate-news/online-real-estate-fraud-climbed-to-275m-in-2025-fbi-says (accessed 2026-10-22)
- MBA October 2025 forecast: total originations of 5.4M loans expected in 2025 rising 7.6% to 5.8M loans in 2026; $2.0T in 2025 to $2.2T in 2026; purchase $1.46T and refinance $737B in 2026 (mba.org returned 403; figures from the search-result extract of the release) — https://www.mba.org/news-and-research/newsroom/news/2025/10/19/mba-forecast--total-single-family-mortgage-originations-to-increase-8-percent-to--2.2-trillion-in-2026 (accessed 2026-10-22). August 2026 revision: 2026 one-to-four family originations $2.147T, refinance $713B, purchase $1.434T — https://www.nationalmortgagenews.com/news/shift-to-purchases-higher-rates-squeeze-2026-refi-volume (accessed 2026-10-22)
- California Government Code §8206: one active sequential journal kept locked under the notary's direct and exclusive control; each entry records date, time and type of act, character of the instrument, signer's signature, the identity evidence (ID type, issuing agency, number, issue or expiry date) or the credible witnesses' signatures and IDs, and the fee; a right thumbprint is required for deeds, quitclaim deeds, deeds of trust, other documents affecting real property and powers of attorney (foreclosure trustee's deeds and reconveyances excepted); the journal is the notary's property and cannot be surrendered to an employer; loss must be reported to the Secretary of State; journal produced under subpoena — https://california.public.law/codes/government_code_section_8206 (accessed 2026-10-22)
- California Government Code §8211: maximum fee $15 per signature for an acknowledgment, $15 for an oath or jurat — https://california.public.law/codes/government_code_section_8211 (accessed 2026-10-22, via search-result extract)
- California Government Code §8219.5(c) bars the literal translation of "notary public" into Spanish as "notario publico" or "notario"; violation means suspension for at least a year or revocation, and permanent revocation on a second offence — https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/government-code/gov-sect-8219-5/ (accessed 2026-10-22, via search-result extract)
- Remote online notarization status (Proof, updated 8 July 2026): the body text says 42 states plus DC had RON laws in effect as of late 2025, three states had laws not yet in effect and five had none; California's law, enacted in 2023, authorises RON by California notaries from 1 January 2030; Californians can use out-of-state RON notaries meanwhile. The page's own summary line says "49 states and DC", which conflicts with its body; the vault uses the body figure — https://proof.com/blog/is-remote-online-notarization-legal-in-all-states (accessed 2026-10-22)
- SECURE Notarization Act: the House passed H.R. 1059 by voice vote in February 2024 (118th Congress) and the Senate did not act; the 119th Congress versions are H.R. 1777 (introduced 3 March 2025) and S. 1561 (introduced 1 May 2025, Cramer and Warner), which would set minimum federal standards for remote notarization and require interstate recognition; trackers show both still in committee — https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/BILLS-119s1561is/xhtml/BILLS-119s1561is.html and https://www.thelobbynews.com/congress/bills/119/house-bill/1777 (accessed 2026-10-22)
- MISMO RON standard as described by vendors and state rules: knowledge-based authentication of at least five dynamic questions from public-records data, four answered correctly within two minutes; Missouri's rule requires RON software to support at least two of credential analysis, dynamic KBA and biometrics — https://www.sos.mo.gov/cmsimages/AdRules/csr/current/15csr/15c30-110.pdf and https://regulations.justia.com/states/missouri/title-15/division-30/chapter-110/section-15-csr-30-110-050 (accessed 2026-10-22, via search-result extract)
- KBA attempt limits: a RON vendor's KBA policy page describes the MISMO format (five questions, four correct, two minutes) with two attempts before a 24-hour wait (low-quality vendor page; used in the vault only as "vendor descriptions") — https://nexus-seal-connect.lovable.app/legal/kba (accessed 2026-10-22, via search-result extract)
- Remote ink-signed notarization: the signer appears by audio-video but signs paper; Fannie Mae set minimum RIN standards for loans from 1 July 2021 and Freddie Mac followed with multifactor authentication; permanent RIN statutes are rare — https://nationalnotary.org/notary-bulletin/blog/2020/04/10-standards-video-conference-notarizations and https://blog.docmagic.com/remote-notarization-roundup-ron-rin-ipen (accessed 2026-10-22, via search-result extract)
- Snapdocs: agents use it free and Snapdocs takes no share of the fee; the fee on an offer is what the signing service or title company is willing to pay; hundreds of signing services and title companies use it to assign notaries; ranking depends on order volume and quality — https://support.snapdocs.com/snapdocs-cost and https://support.snapdocs.com/how-to-get-started (accessed 2026-10-22, via search-result extract)
- Snapdocs 2025 State of eClose Adoption Report (about 100 lenders, data collected February-March 2025): 90% of lenders offer digital closings (up 22% since 2023); 14% of lenders with eClosing technology close more than 80% of loans digitally; 31% close more than 60% (28% in 2023); hybrid offering near flat; barriers are technology cost (50%), stakeholder usage (42%) and technology issues (41%) — https://newslink.mba.org/?p=251692 and https://www.housingwire.com/articles/snapdocs-eclosings-survey-mortgage-lenders/ (accessed 2026-10-22, via search-result extract)
- NNA signing agent certification is voluntary and requires a current commission; NNA says the mortgage industry requires an annual background screening and compliance training for everyone in the lending process; screening follows Signing Professionals Workgroup standards with a 10-year lookback across federal, state and county records; the exam pass mark is 80%; screening is valid for a year — https://www.nationalnotary.org/support/nna-certification/notary-signing-agent-certification-standards and https://www.nationalnotary.org/knowledge-center/signing-agent-resources/background-screenings (accessed 2026-10-22, via search-result extract)
- Notary litigation: an attorney quoted by the NNA says more than half of lawsuits against notaries involve notarised forged signatures, including forged seals, missing ID checks and fake IDs; failing to require personal appearance is described as the most common mistake; capacity claims often arise in probate and elder-abuse matters; the journal is described as the notary's best protection (NNA articles; advocacy-adjacent source) — https://nationalnotary.org/notary-bulletin/blog/2018/06/legal-expert-discusses-common-notary-mistakes and https://nationalnotary.org/notary-bulletin/blog/2011/08/notary-lawsuits-prevented-sound-practices (accessed 2026-10-22, via search-result extract)
- Rhode Island Secretary of State "Common Notarial Errors and Consequences" page (illustrative hypothetical of a $360,000 judgment where a spouse's signature was notarised without the signer present) — https://sos.ri.gov/Divisions/NotaryPublic/CommonErrors (accessed 2026-10-22, via search-result extract)

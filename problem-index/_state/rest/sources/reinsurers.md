# Sources — Reinsurers

## Checked
- Global reinsurer capital a record $785B at year-end 2025, up almost 10%; traditional capital $649B (up $49B, more than 8%); alternative (third-party) capital a record $136B, up more than 18%; average reinsurance-specific combined ratio 88.5% in 2025 across 18 companies (90.1% in 2024); ROE ~17%, roughly double the cost of equity (Aon April 2026 Reinsurance Market Dynamics, via Reinsurance News, 1 Apr 2026) — https://www.reinsurancene.ws/new-highs-for-traditional-alternative-takes-global-reinsurer-capital-to-record-785bn-aon/ (accessed 2026-10-23)
- NAICS 524130 Reinsurance Carriers, US private sector, 2024 annual averages: 1,192 establishments, 30,896 employees, $5.30B total wages, $171,573 average annual pay; largest state employment Illinois 4,323, New York 2,887, New Jersey 2,394, Missouri 1,834, Georgia 1,665, Connecticut 1,616 (BLS QCEW open data) — https://data.bls.gov/cew/data/api/2024/a/industry/524130.csv (accessed 2026-10-23)
- Guy Carpenter US Property Catastrophe Rate-on-Line Index fell 12% at the 1 January 2026 renewals and was down 16% for 2026 after the June/July renewals, down 22% from the 2024 hard-market peak and ~62% above the 2017 soft-market low; the 16% fall is the largest annual decline for the index since 2014 — https://www.artemis.bm/news/global-and-us-property-cat-rates-down-16-apac-19-after-july-renewals-in-2026-guy-carpenter/ (accessed 2026-10-23)
- Catastrophe bond market (144A plus private deals tracked by Artemis) ended H1 2026 at a record almost $65.6B outstanding (almost $63.9B at end-Q1 2026); H1 2026 issuance almost $18B (just under $17.98B), a first-half record — https://www.artemis.bm/news/catastrophe-bond-market-records-that-were-broken-in-h1-2026/ (accessed 2026-10-23)
- Industry loss index triggers (predominantly PCS, given the volume of US deals) accounted for almost 27%, or $8.3B, of outstanding cat bond risk capital at the end of 2017 (Artemis, 4 Jan 2018) — https://www.artemis.bm/news/use-of-pcs-data-in-2017-catastrophe-bond-triggers-rises-with-issuance/ (accessed 2026-10-23)
- PERILS AG extended severe convective storm industry loss reporting to ten European countries, Japan and New Zealand from 1 August 2025 (thresholds EUR 500M, JPY 100bn, NZD 300M), adding to Australia and Canada; exposures and event losses are based on data reported to PERILS by participating insurers at CRESTA-zone and line level (PERILS CORE methodology); reports used for ILW and cat bond triggers and cat model verification — https://www.artemis.bm/news/perils-to-report-scs-industry-losses-for-europe-japan-and-new-zealand/ and https://www.perils.org/ (accessed 2026-10-23)
- NAIC adopted revisions to the Credit for Reinsurance Model Law (#785) in June 2019 creating "Reciprocal Jurisdictions" and eliminating collateral and local-presence requirements for EU and UK reinsurers with at least $250M own funds and a 100% Solvency II SCR; states had to adopt before 1 Sep 2022 or face federal preemption; all 56 NAIC jurisdictions had adopted by 22 Sep 2022 — https://content.naic.org/insurance-topics/covered-agreement (accessed 2026-10-23)
- Swiss Re Institute "Social inflation: indexing liability claims trends" (published 7 Sep 2024): social inflation increased US liability claims by 57% over the past decade, with an annual peak of 7% in 2023; US liability lines exposed to bodily injury claims had cumulative underwriting losses of $43B over five years — https://intelligentinsurer.com/social-inflation-is-the-main-growth-driver-of-us-liability-claims (accessed 2026-10-23)
- Fermat Capital Management passed $11B of cat bond and ILS assets under management by 1 July 2026, up from $9.9B at end-2025 (Artemis, 16 Jul 2026) — https://www.artemis.bm/news/fermat-capital-management-hits-11bn-in-cat-bond-and-ils-assets-under-management/ (accessed 2026-10-23)
- Verisk estimated insured property losses from the Palisades and Eaton fires (January 2025) at $28B–$35B on 22 Jan 2025; Moody's RMS put them at $20B–$30B and CoreLogic at $35B–$45B — https://www.reinsurancene.ws/verisk-puts-la-wildfire-insurance-industry-loss-at-between-28bn-and-35bn/ (accessed 2026-10-23)
- Riskbook rebranded as Supercede in February 2021; its Packs product prepares and validates cedent reinsurance submission data; at launch the data submission process was described as typically taking several months of spreadsheets sent to cedents, brokers and reinsurer panels — https://www.reinsurancene.ws/rebranded-supercede-launches-reinsurance-trading-platform/ (accessed 2026-10-23)
- Milliman sells catastrophe model review and adjustment: adjusting vendor model inputs (vulnerability) to an internal view, blending models and calibrating low return periods to a cedent's own experience; S&P takes a negative view of reinsurers that do not vet and validate vendor models for their own view of risk (CARe 2014 handout) — https://www.milliman.com/en/services/model-review-and-adjustment and https://casact.org/sites/default/files/presentation/reinsure_2014_handouts_paper_3248_handout_2144_0.pdf (accessed 2026-10-23)
- Schedule F surplus penalty: a reinsurer is a "slow" payer if paid-loss recoverables more than 90 days overdue are 20% or more of paid-loss recoverables plus amounts recovered in the prior 90 days; for fast payers the penalty is 20% of paid-loss recoverables more than 90 days overdue, and slow payers face a larger 20% charge, introduced with the early-1990s Schedule F revisions ("The Schedule F Penalty — Effective or Evaded?", CAS Discussion Paper Program, 1992; text read with pdftotext). Current NAIC annual statement instructions were not read, so the vault describes the rule as dating from those revisions — https://www.casact.org/sites/default/files/database/dpp_dpp92_92dpp819.pdf (accessed 2026-10-23)
- The Bermuda Monetary Authority requires all Class 3B and Class 4 insurers to submit a Capital and Solvency Return that includes a Catastrophe Risk Return and Schedule of Risk Management; its staff analyse the returns for an annual Catastrophe Risk Modelling Report (the 2021 report, published December 2022, was the third stand-alone edition); no team size is given — https://cdn.bma.bm/documents/2022-12-06-09-58-13-Catastrophe-Risk-Modelling---2021-Report.pdf (accessed 2026-10-23; text read with pdftotext)

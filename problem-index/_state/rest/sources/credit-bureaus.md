# Sources — Credit Bureaus

## Checked
- NAICS 561450 Credit Bureaus, 2022: 268 firms, 456 establishments, 29,131 employees, $5.42B annual payroll and $16.45B receipts; 17 firms with 500+ employees run 189 establishments, employ 24,969 people and take $14.92B (~91%) of receipts; 134 firms have fewer than 5 employees and take $49M; 199 firms have fewer than 20 employees — Census Bureau, Statistics of U.S. Businesses 2022, U.S. & states 6-digit NAICS table (released 2025-04-10), https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/susb/tables/2022/us_state_6digitnaics_2022.xlsx via https://www.census.gov/data/tables/2022/econ/susb/2022-susb-annual.html (accessed 2026-10-23).
- NAICS 561450, 2024 annual average (private ownership): 1,292 establishments, 22,140 employees, average annual pay $185,184 — BLS Quarterly Census of Employment and Wages, https://data.bls.gov/cew/data/api/2024/a/industry/561450.csv (accessed 2026-10-23).
- CFPB 2025 Consumer Response Annual Report (released 31 March 2026): 6.6M complaints received in 2025, more than double ~3.2M in 2024; ~5.8M (88%) about credit or consumer reporting, the top product for the fifth straight year; credit reporting complaints up 182% on the prior two years' monthly average; the CFPB attributed part of the rise to credit repair organisations and to large language models and AI agents submitting "duplicative and spurious" complaints; more than 5.9M sent to over 4,000 companies; companies gave timely responses to 99.6%; consumers are now directed to dispute with the consumer reporting agency before complaining — https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_2025-cr-annual-report_2026-03.pdf and https://infobytes.orrick.com/2026-04-10/cfpb-reports-complaint-volume-doubled-in-2025-citing-surge-in-credit-reporting-disputes/ (accessed 2026-10-23, figures via the Orrick summary). The 2024 report put credit reporting at 85% of complaints, mostly about Equifax, Experian and TransUnion — https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_cr-annual-report_2025-05.pdf (via search extract).
- FCRA §611 (15 U.S.C. 1681i): reinvestigation within 30 days of receiving the dispute, extendable by up to 15 days only if the consumer supplies information during the 30 days; notice to the furnisher within 5 business days; a frivolous or irrelevant determination must be notified within 5 business days; results within 5 business days of completion; written notice within 5 business days of any reinsertion — https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681i (accessed 2026-10-23).
- Furnisher dispute survey (February 2025): 53% of responding furnishers process no more than 250 disputes a month and 34% more than 1,000; 86% use the e-OSCAR web interface for indirect disputes, while direct disputes run more on standard office tools — BusinessWire release reprinted at https://accesswdun.com/print/2025/2/1285886 (accessed 2026-10-23, via search extract; the release itself returned 403). e-OSCAR was developed by the nationwide bureaus in 1993 and carries the ACDV used when a bureau sends a consumer's dispute to the furnisher; Metro 2 is the reporting format — https://www.switchlabs.dev/resources/e-oscar-the-online-solution-for-complete-and-accurate-credit-reporting-disputes (via search extract).
- ACA International 2019 survey of collection agencies (170 respondents): average 60,408 disputes received a year, median 4,100; median indirect disputes seven times median direct disputes — https://policymakers.acainternational.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/mass-generic-disputes-flyer.pdf (accessed 2026-10-23, via search extract).
- Equifax FY2025 revenue $6,074.5M: Workforce Solutions $2,582.3M, U.S. Information Solutions $2,078.5M, International $1,413.7M; 2026 guidance assumes all mortgage credit scores are FICO Scores and says mortgage customers moving to VantageScore get a lower price — https://investor.equifax.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/1391/equifax-delivers-fourth-quarter-and-2025 (accessed 2026-10-23). FY2024: $5.68B total, Workforce Solutions $2.43B, USIS $1.89B, International $1.35B (search extract).
- The Work Number held ~209M active and ~813M total (active and historic) employment records at 31 December 2025 — Equifax 10-K FY2025, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/33185/000003318526000010/efx-20251231.htm (accessed 2026-10-23, via search extract). Q1 2025 call: 191M active records.
- TransUnion FY2025 U.S. Markets gross revenue $3,578.7M, up 10.5%: Financial Services $1,684.6M (from $1,433.8M), Emerging Verticals $1,318.8M, Consumer Interactive $575.3M — TransUnion 10-K FY2025, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1552033/000155203326000012/tru-20251231.htm (accessed 2026-10-23, via search extract).
- Experian FY26 (year to 31 March 2026): total revenue growth 13%, organic 8%; North America 10% — https://www.experianplc.com/newsroom/press-releases/2026/full-year-results-fy26 (accessed 2026-10-23, via search extract). No North America dollar figure taken.
- FHFA and HUD announced on 22 April 2026 that Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac (and FHA) will accept VantageScore 4.0 and FICO Score 10T; VantageScore 4.0 is available immediately to a limited set of approved lenders, choosing between it and Classic FICO on a tri-merge report; non-participating lenders must continue with Classic FICO; FICO 10T to follow with no date; historical score data to be published from summer 2026 — https://infobytes.orrick.com/2026-04-24/fannie-mae-freddie-mac-and-fha-announce-new-credit-score-models-accepted-for-mortgage-underwriting/ (accessed 2026-10-23). FHA acceptance of VantageScore 4.0 collateral from 1 January 2027 per VantageScore's release — https://www.vantagescore.com/resources/knowledge-center/fhfa-director-and-hud-secretary-jointly-announce-vantagescore-4-0-implementation-for-fannie-mae-freddie-mac-and-fha (via search extract; vendor source).
- FICO Mortgage Direct License Program (announced 1 October 2025): resellers may calculate and distribute FICO mortgage scores directly; performance model $4.95 per score plus a $33 funded-loan fee per borrower per score, or $10 per score; HousingWire reported resellers slow to adopt — https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251001652134/en and https://www.housingwire.com/articles/mortgage-credit-report-costs-2026/ (accessed 2026-10-23, via search extract).
- Homebuyers Privacy Protection Act (H.R. 2808), signed 5 September 2025, effective 5 March 2026: amends the FCRA so bureaus may furnish mortgage trigger leads only for a firm offer of credit or insurance to a recipient with the consumer's authorisation, the originator or servicer of the consumer's mortgage, or an institution holding the consumer's account — https://infobytes.orrick.com/2025-09-09/president-trump-signs-homebuyers-privacy-protection-act-restricting-mortgage-trigger-leads/ and https://www.namb.org/understanding-the-trigger-leads-ban (accessed 2026-10-23).
- FTC and CFPB settlement with TransUnion and TransUnion Rental Screening Solutions (October 2023): $15M ($11M consumer redress, $4M civil penalty) over inaccurate and incomplete eviction records and failure to name the vendors supplying eviction and criminal data; with a separate CFPB security-freeze action, $23M in total — https://www.consumerfinancialserviceslawmonitor.com/2023/10/trans-union-settles-for-15-million-with-cfpb-and-ftc-over-tenant-screening-reports/ and https://www.housingwire.com/articles/transunion-to-pay-23m-to-settle-complaints-of-inaccurate-tenant-screening-security-freeze-failures/ (accessed 2026-10-23).
- SafeRent Solutions class settlement (~$2.3M, final approval November 2024) over scores alleged to disadvantage Black and Hispanic applicants and voucher holders; for five years SafeRent may not provide a score or accept/decline recommendation for voucher applicants — https://cohenmilstein.com/ai-landlord-screening-tool-will-stop-scoring-low-income-tenants-after-discrimination-suit and https://news.bgov.com/insurance/saferents-2-3-million-deal-in-ai-screening-tool-suit-approved (accessed 2026-10-23, via search extract).
- CFPB technical correction of credit invisibles (23 June 2025): 2.7% of adults (~7M) credit invisible in December 2020; the 2010 figure was revised from 11% (25.9M) to 5.8% (13.5M); the unscorable share in 2010 revised up from 7.4% (17.2M) to 12.7% (29.7M) — https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_update-credit-invisibles-estimate_2025-06.pdf and https://infobytes.orrick.com/2025-06-27/cfpb-issues-correction-of-credit-invisibles-estimate/ (accessed 2026-10-23, via search extract).
- FTC 2012 congressionally mandated accuracy study: one in five consumers had an error on at least one of their three reports; 5% had errors that could lead to paying more for auto loans and insurance; four in five consumers who disputed saw some modification — https://www.ftc.gov/node/44410 (accessed 2026-10-23, via search extract).
- CFPB medical debt credit reporting rule vacated in mid-July 2025 by Judge Sean Jordan (E.D. Tex.); the rule would have removed ~$49B of medical debt from ~15M consumers' reports; CDIA sued and the administration joined the request to vacate — https://wjla.com/news/nation-world/federal-judge-consumer-friendly-medical-debt-rule-cfpb-biden-administration-future-lending-decisions-us-district-court-california-colorado-washington-state-cdia-ceo (accessed 2026-10-23).
- Small Business Financial Exchange: a member-controlled nonprofit of lenders that passes members' payment data to certified vendors (Dun & Bradstreet first certified January 2015, Equifax 2017, Experian May 2018, LexisNexis Risk Solutions); SBFE "Members own, control the use of, and manage the quality and accuracy of their customer data"; only members may buy SBFE-based credit risk products — https://investor.equifax.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/320/equifax-designated-small-business-financial-exchange and https://www.experianplc.com/newsroom/press-releases/2018/experian-and-small-business-financial-exchange (accessed 2026-10-23, via search extract; 2015–2018 releases, current roster not checked).
- Early Warning Services describes itself as owned by seven of the largest US banks; its Deposit Chek product screens items against the National Shared Database and predicts return likelihood; ChexSystems is a deposit-account consumer reporting agency within FIS; NCLC reported over 80% of banks use an account screening agency — https://earlywarning.com/sites/default/files/2024-09/Deposit%20Chek%20Product%20Brief%20name%20change%202024%2008.pdf, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ChexSystems and https://www.nclc.org/images/pdf/credit_reports/ib-cra-screening.pdf (accessed 2026-10-23, via search extract).
- Plaid Check is a separate FCRA consumer reporting agency whose Consumer Report bundles bank-account, identity, income and cash-flow attributes; its two main uses are a second look at declined applicants and better pricing for marginal approvals; Prism Data's CashScore is a cash-flow risk score used in it — https://plaid.com/check/consumer-report and https://fintechnexus.com/?p=59956 (accessed 2026-10-23, via search extract).
- New York Fed Consumer Credit Panel is "constructed from a nationally representative random sample of Equifax credit report data" and feeds the quarterly Household Debt and Credit Report — https://www.newyorkfed.org/microeconomics/hhdc/background.html (accessed 2026-10-23).
- Medicaid community engagement (work) requirements under the 2025 reconciliation law: expansion adults must show 80 hours a month of work or qualifying activity; states must begin by 1 January 2027 (HHS may extend to 31 December 2028); states verify at least twice a year and for the month before application, checking data sources before asking enrollees; NHeLP's paper "Got Your (Work) Number" examines employment databases for this — https://healthlaw.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/Hartly_EmploymentDatabases_revd_12112025.pdf and https://www.jems.com/ems-management/states-face-tight-timeline-as-feds-unveil-new-medicaid-work-requirement-rules/ (accessed 2026-10-23, via search extract).

- TransUnion completed its purchase of Verisk Financial Services (Argus card and deposit benchmarking consortium) on 8 April 2022 for $505.7M after adjustment — https://www.biia.com/transunion-completes-acquisition-of-verisk-financial-services/ (accessed 2026-10-22 by the credit-card-issuers builder; cited for the roll-up pocket).

- FCRA §605(a) seven-year limit on collection accounts, charge-offs and other adverse items; §605(c) starts the seven-year period for a delinquent account at the expiration of the 180-day period beginning on the commencement of the delinquency — https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681c (accessed 2026-10-23).
- The nationwide bureaus stopped reporting paid medical collections from 1 July 2022, lengthened the wait before unpaid medical collections appear from six months to one year, and on 11 April 2023 removed medical collections with an initial reported balance under $500, bringing removals to nearly 70% of medical collection tradelines — https://newsroom.transunion.com/equifax-experian-and-transunion-remove-medical-collections-debt-under-500-from-us-credit-reports/ (accessed 2026-10-23). Whether these voluntary policies remain unchanged in October 2026 was not checked.

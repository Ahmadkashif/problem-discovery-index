# Sources — Auctioneers

## Checked
- NAICS 561990 All Other Support Services, 2022: 10,864 firms, 12,668 establishments, 176,554 employees, $9.63B annual payroll and $30.96B receipts; 6,778 firms have fewer than 5 employees; 187 firms with 500+ employees take $11.79B of receipts — Census Bureau, Statistics of U.S. Businesses 2022, U.S. & states 6-digit NAICS table (released 2025-04-10), https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/susb/tables/2022/us_state_6digitnaics_2022.xlsx (accessed 2026-10-23, parsed locally). Note: 561990 is a residual code holding many other support services, so these are not auctioneer figures; the vault uses them only as the ceiling of the code.

- Auction Technology Group FY25 (year to 30 September 2025): revenue $190.2M; group GMV $3.3bn (Arts & Antiques $0.8bn, Industrial & Commercial $2.5bn); A&A total hammer value $5.2bn with a 16% conversion rate (GMV as a share of hammer value); I&C hammer value $6.9bn with a 36% conversion rate; take rate 4.8% (A&A 10.3%); 26.8m lots listed, 99,000+ auctions, 85m bids; ten branded marketplaces; an in-house AI model predicting lot categories from current and historical inventory; Chairish acquired for $85m in August 2025; atgPay processed 67% of U.S. gross transaction value on LiveAuctioneers — https://cdn.yano.digital/media/xbtnoncj/atg-plc-fy25-results-statement-final-251125.pdf (accessed 2026-10-23, PDF text extracted locally).
- Florida Statutes s. 468.388 (conduct of an auction): written agreement with the owner before the auction, kept 2 years; sale proceeds not settled immediately go into an in-state insured escrow or trust account "within 2 working days after the auction"; separate ledger reconciled monthly; any buyer's premium announced at the start and posted; terms (with reserve, without reserve, absolute, minimum bid) announced at the start; in an absolute auction an item may not be withdrawn once a bid is accepted — https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/0468.388 (accessed 2026-10-23). Part VI also sets licensing (s. 468.385), apprenticeship (s. 468.3855) and an Auctioneer Recovery Fund (s. 468.392) — https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/Chapter468/Part_VI (accessed 2026-10-23).
- Number of states licensing auctioneers — sources conflict: 24 licensing states plus 1 registering state on roughly 2022 data (West Virginia University Knee Center blog, January 2024), https://knee.wvu.edu/publications/knee-center-blog/2024/01/16/the-right-price-or-the-winner-s-curse-auctioneer-licensing-in-the-u-s; ~33 states per https://getlicensemap.com/auctioneer/illinois; a 2026 guide lists 18 non-licensing states including California, Colorado, Iowa, Kansas, Michigan and Missouri, https://djwillgill.com/?p=32287 (accessed 2026-10-23, via search summary). - The National Auctioneers Association renamed itself the National Auction Association in July 2023 and describes itself as representing ~2,500 members (February 2026 press release); CAI and AARE are its designations — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Auctioneers_Association and https://www.accessnewswire.com/newsroom/en/consumer-and-retail-products/national-auction-association-and-u.s.-land-broker-co-op-form-strategic-1138067 (accessed 2026-10-23, via search summary). - Mei and Moses (2005), "Vested Interest and Biased Price Estimates: Evidence from an Auction Market", Journal of Finance 60(5): 2409–2435: presale estimates for expensive paintings were upward-biased over 30 years and high estimates predicted poor subsequent returns — https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v60y2005i5p2409-2435.html (accessed 2026-10-23, via search summary). Later work stresses that unsold (bought-in) lots bias estimate studies — https://www.scitepress.org/Papers/2022/113172/113172.pdf (accessed 2026-10-23, via search summary).

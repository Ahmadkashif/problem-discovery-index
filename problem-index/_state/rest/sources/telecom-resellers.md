# Sources — Telecom Resellers

## Checked
- NAICS 2022 517121 covers establishments that buy access and network capacity from network owners and resell wired and wireless service (except satellite) to businesses and households without operating transmission facilities, with MVNOs explicitly included and commission agents placed in 517122; 517810 covers specialised services such as satellite tracking, communications telemetry and radar station operation, satellite terminal stations, and "Internet services or Voice over Internet protocol (VoIP) services via client-supplied telecommunications connections", and its establishments do not operate as carriers — definition text as mirrored by https://www.worldoftaxonomy.com/codes/naics_2022/517810 ; https://www.worldoftaxonomy.com/system/naics_2022/node/517121 ; https://highergov.com/naics/517121-telecommunications-resellers ; https://insurancexdate.com/naics/517810 (secondary mirrors of the Census manual, accessed 2026-10-22)
- Telecommunications resellers (NAICS 517121) had 955 firms, 1,537 establishments, ~$14.72B in receipts ($14,722,818K), ~$1.35B annual payroll and 22,547 employees in 2022; all other telecommunications (517810) had 1,673 firms, 1,888 establishments, ~$21.26B in receipts ($21,256,687K), ~$3.25B payroll and 31,723 employees, of which "internet service providers (except broadband)" were 682 firms and ~$0.74B and "other telecommunications" 993 firms, ~$20.52B and 27,887 employees; agents for wireless telecommunications services (517122, outside this slug) had 4,191 firms and ~$20.40B — 2022 Economic Census, Sector 51 basic table EC2251BASIC (US rows 517121, 517122, 517810, 5178101, 5178109), https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/economic-census/data/2022/sector51/EC2251BASIC.zip (accessed 2026-10-22)
- Under the 2017 codes County Business Patterns still uses, telecommunications resellers (517911) had 3,744 establishments and 40,319 employees in 2023, with 2,107 establishments under five employees, 979 at 5–9, 384 at 10–19, 145 at 20–49, 82 at 50–99, 32 at 100–249, 9 at 250–499 and 5 at 500–999; all other telecommunications (517919) had 1,932 establishments and 26,776 employees, 1,295 of them under five employees — County Business Patterns 2023, US file, https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/cbp/datasets/2023/cbp23us.zip (accessed 2026-10-22). The Economic Census and CBP establishment counts for resellers differ (1,537 versus 3,744) because the programmes classify differently; both are quoted, neither is reconciled.
- Interconnected VoIP subscriptions were 63 million at June 30, 2025 (64 million at December 2024), against 15 million end-user switched access lines and 395 million mobile subscriptions, 473 million retail voice connections in all; VoIP subscriptions fell at a 2.3% compound annual rate over three years; 67% of the 78 million wireline connections were business connections — FCC, Voice Telephone Services: Status as of June 30, 2025, https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DOC-421558A1.pdf ; https://incompliancemag.com/fcc-releases-annual-voice-telephone-services-report/ (search-result excerpts, accessed 2026-10-22)
- The FCC removed more than 1,200 voice service providers from the Robocall Mitigation Database in August 2025, after a December 2024 order told more than 2,400 providers to cure deficient filings; removed providers' traffic may not be carried until the Enforcement and Wireline Competition Bureaus approve re-entry; an earlier August 2025 action removed 185 providers — https://www.accountsrecovery.net/2025/08/27/fcc-removes-1200-providers-in-sweeping-robocall-crackdown/ (FCC file DA-25-737A1 linked) ; https://www.accountsrecovery.net/2025/08/07/fcc-removes-185-providers-from-robocall-database-blocks-access-to-telecom-networks/ ; https://www.consumeraffairs.com/news/fcc-bars-more-than-1200-telecom-providers-for-robocall-violations-082525.html (accessed 2026-10-22). The source said "more than 2,400"; a search summary gave 2,411, not confirmed.
- On September 2, 2026 the Enforcement Bureau removed the certifications of 14 more companies whose RMD filings and mitigation plans remained deficient, with downstream providers given two days to start blocking their traffic — https://tcpaworld.com/2026/09/03/fcc-crackdown-on-illegal-robocallers-the-federal-communications-commission-removes-14-named-companies-from-its-robocall-mitigation-database/ ; https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DA-26-872A1.pdf (search-result excerpts, accessed 2026-10-22)
- New RMD rules (Report and Order FCC 24-135) took effect February 5, 2026: annual recertification by March 1 (window opens February 1), updates within 10 business days of a change, $10,000 per false or inaccurate filing and $1,000 for failing to update; the $100 filing and recertification fee awaits a separate effective-date announcement — https://transnexus.com/blog/2026/rmd-recertification-guidance/ ; https://www.wiley.law/alert-FCC-Strengthens-RMD-Filing-Requirements-and-Enforcement-Capability ; https://www.lermansenter.com/fcc-adopts-new-robocall-mitigation-database-filing-requirements-fees-and-fines/ (accessed 2026-10-22)
- The Eighth Report and Order ends third-party caller ID authentication on the third party's credentials: a provider may use a third party only if it makes the attestation decisions itself and every call is signed with its own SPC token and certificate; pure resellers without network control select "No STIR/SHAKEN Implementation" in the RMD — https://support.telnyx.com/en/articles/10806916-understanding-the-fcc-s-eighth-report-and-order-on-third-party-authentication ; https://www.bandwidth.com/blog/abcs-of-attestation-and-analytics/ (accessed 2026-10-22). Sources differ on the compliance date: Bandwidth and forum posts say June 20, 2025, Telnyx says September 18, 2025. The vault says "2025" and gives neither date as fact.
- The Seventh Report and Order (released May 19, 2023) requires every voice service provider to respond fully to traceback requests from the FCC, law enforcement and the traceback consortium within 24 hours; the Enforcement Bureau found SK Teleco LLC in violation of 47 CFR 64.1200(n)(1) in an April 20, 2026 initial determination order — https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DA-26-384A1.pdf ; https://www.jdsupra.com:443/legalnews/life-support-sk-teleco-llc-in-huge-1865391/ (search-result excerpts, accessed 2026-10-22)
- The Industry Traceback Group (run by USTelecom; its progress reports are published at https://www.ustelecom.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/ITG-2019-Progress-Report.pdf) ran 3,606 tracebacks of suspected unlawful robocalls in 2024 (3,737 in 2023), identified 714 providers in call paths (275 of them new) and counted 77 non-responsive providers (94 in 2023) — ITG letter to the FCC, DA 25-1100, https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DA-25-1100A2.pdf (search-result excerpt, accessed 2026-10-22)
- USTelecom's robocall-mitigation best practices say the depth of know-your-customer vetting should match the service a customer gets (call volume, calls per second, which caller IDs it may use), flag throwaway emails and anonymous sign-ups, and recommend denying service or limiting volume "when in doubt"; TransNexus writes that almost all illegal robocalls are short, averaging under 20 seconds — https://www.ustelecom.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/Mitigating-Robocalls-Best-Practices-Presentation-November-2020.pdf ; https://transnexus.com/blog/2022/robocall-mitigation-reasonable-steps/ (search-result excerpts, accessed 2026-10-22)
- The proposed universal service contribution factor for Q4 2026 is 42.0% (Public Notice DA 26-946, September 14, 2026), on a $2.026B program collection and $6.910B of projected contribution-base revenue; Q4 2025 was 38.1% — https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DA-26-946A1.pdf ; https://www.benton.org/headlines/proposed-fourth-quarter-2026-universal-service-fund-contribution-factor ; https://broadbandbreakfast.com/usf-contribution-factor-to-hit-42-percent-in-q4/ (accessed 2026-10-22)
- FCC Form 499-A (prior-year revenue) is due April 1 and Form 499-Q on February 1, May 1, August 1 and November 1, filed through USAC E-File; resellers and interconnected VoIP providers file; a wholesaler may exclude reseller revenue only with a reasonable expectation the customer contributes, evidenced by an annual reseller certification (USAC template since December 2018) — https://www.usac.org/wp-content/uploads/service-providers/documents/training/2022/2022-February-New-499-ID-Basics.pdf ; https://www.usac.org/wp-content/uploads/service-providers/documents/newsletters/2019/1Q2019-498.499-Spotlight.pdf ; https://www.commlawblog.com/2023/03/articles/fcc/upcoming-fcc-telecom-deadlines/ (search-result excerpts, accessed 2026-10-22)
- A reseller compliance checklist lists a USAC filer ID and annual 499-A, monthly USF payments, an OCN from NECA, RMD registration and a mitigation plan, an SPC token from iconectiv and a certificate from an approved certificate authority, CALEA, CPNI, CVAA, Kari's Law and RAY BAUM's Act 911 obligations, outage reporting, Form 477, and state tax, 911 fee and PUC obligations — ClearlyIP, Compliance Checklist for Telecommunications Resellers (May 2024), https://clearlyip.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/CIP-Compliance_Checklist-Telecom_Resellers.pdf (accessed 2026-10-22)
- Carriers and interconnected VoIP providers must file an annual CPNI compliance certificate (deadline given as March 1, 2024 in the reminder found) — https://www.kelleydrye.com/viewpoints/client-advisories/reminder-federal-communications-commission-february-march-april-1st-filings.md (search-result excerpt, accessed 2026-10-22)
- RAY BAUM's Act dispatchable-location rules applied to fixed interconnected VoIP and fixed MLTS from January 6, 2021 and to non-fixed (nomadic) devices from January 6, 2022 — https://www.bandwidth.com/ray-baums-act-compliance/ ; https://www.daypitney.com/epc/article/print/9015 ; https://www.911.gov/assets/911_RayBaumsActKarisLaw_flyer_Final.pdf (search-result excerpts, accessed 2026-10-22)
- The FCC's SIM swap and port-out fraud order (Federal Register December 8, 2023) requires wireless providers to authenticate customers securely before moving a number to a new device or provider and to notify customers of SIM change and port-out requests; on July 5, 2024 the Wireline Competition Bureau waived compliance until OMB review and a Federal Register compliance-date notice (the batch review supplies the docket, WC Docket 21-341, the bureau order number, DA 24-649, and the finding that no compliance-date notice had appeared by October 2026; the vault uses its standard sentence) — https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2023-12-08/html/2023-26338.htm ; https://www.tlp.law/2024/07/08/fcc-amends-sim-swapping-port-out-fraud-compliance-date/ (accessed 2026-10-22)

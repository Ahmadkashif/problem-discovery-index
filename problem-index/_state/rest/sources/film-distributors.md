# Sources — Film Distributors

## Checked
- NAICS 512120 Motion Picture and Video Distribution: 493 establishments and 2,525 employees in County Business Patterns 2023; ~$1.4B "market size" from the 2022 Economic Census (third-party page citing Census) — https://vantainsights.com/industry/512120-motion-picture-and-video-distribution (accessed 2026-10-22)
- NAICS 512199 Other Motion Picture and Video Industries: 291 establishments and 865 employees in County Business Patterns 2023; ~$235.9M "market size" from the 2022 Economic Census (third-party page citing Census) — https://vantainsights.com/industry/512199-other-motion-picture-and-video-industries (accessed 2026-10-22)
- Census Service Annual Survey, total revenue for Other Motion Picture and Video Industries (512199), all establishments, employer firms: $256M (2018), $305M (2019), $277M (2020), $371M (2021), $426M (2022) (series REVEF512199ALLEST) — https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/REVEF512199ALLEST (accessed 2026-10-22)
- Census Service Annual Survey publishes production and distribution together as 5121X; total revenue for Motion Picture and Video Production and Distribution, employer firms: $73.95B (2018), $92.89B (2022) (series REVEF5121XALLEST) — https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/REVEF5121XALLEST (accessed 2026-10-22)
- Same survey, sources of revenue: domestic licensing of rights to motion picture films $12.94B (2018), $12.48B (2019), $10.71B (2020), $10.23B (2021), $12.57B (2022) (series REVMPFEF5121XALLEST) — https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/REVMPFEF5121XALLEST (accessed 2026-10-22)
- Same survey: international licensing of rights to motion picture films $7.96B (2015), $7.86B (2016), $6.61B (2017), $6.65B (2018), $6.90B (2019) (series REVILMEF5121XALLEST) — https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/REVILMEF5121XALLEST (accessed 2026-10-22)
- 2022 NAICS 512199 definition and index entries: booking agencies (motion picture), film libraries and stock footage libraries, film preservation and restoration services, motion picture film laboratories, and reproduction of motion picture films for theatrical distribution; excludes production, distribution, exhibition and teleproduction/postproduction — https://www.naics.com/naics-code-description/?v=2022&code=512199 (accessed 2026-10-22)
- 512120 is defined as establishments primarily engaged in acquiring distribution rights and distributing film and video productions to theaters, TV networks and stations, and exhibitors (search-result summary of the NAICS definition) — https://www.naics.com/naics-code-description/?code=5121 (accessed 2026-10-22)
- US/Canada domestic box office (Box Office Mojo): $8,656,750,459 across 670 releases in 2025; $8,570,611,181 across 677 releases in 2024; $8,907,742,585 across 593 releases in 2023; $11,363,364,803 across 910 releases in 2019 — https://www.boxofficemojo.com/year/ (accessed 2026-10-22)
- Comscore's preliminary 2025 domestic total was reported at ~$8.87B, up ~1.5% on 2024 (Deadline/Forbes, via search-result summaries; articles paywalled) — https://deadline.com/2025/12/box-office-u-s-ticket-sales-2025-1236640883/ (accessed 2026-10-22, search result only)
- AMC Entertainment FY2025 Form 10-K gives 2025 North American box office of ~$8.9B, up ~1.5%, the industry (Comscore) figure; recorded in the movie-theaters source log and used here as the headline for consistency with that build, with Box Office Mojo's $8.66B noted beside it — https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1411579/000141157926000016/amc-20251231x10k.htm (accessed 2026-10-22, per `_state/rest/sources/movie-theaters.md`)
- AMC Entertainment FY2025 Form 10-K: film licenses are negotiated "on a film-by-film and theatre-by-theatre basis"; rental fees are "typically" based on each film's box office performance, less often on a fixed mutually agreed settlement rate; film exhibition costs "are accrued based on estimates of the final settlement"; some European territories set rental weekly and some licenses use a flat per-capita amount — https://investor.amctheatres.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0001411579-26-000016/amc-20251231x10k.htm (accessed 2026-10-22)
- Same 10-K, via search-result snippet (MD&A not opened): film exhibition costs 48.1% of admissions in 2025 vs 48.4% in 2024, with a second pair (51.2% vs 51.6%) that appears to be the US segment — same URL (accessed 2026-10-22). Written in the vault as "roughly half of admissions".
- DOJ: Judge Analisa Torres (SDNY) terminated the Paramount consent decrees in August 2020, with a two-year sunset on the block-booking and circuit-dealing bans — https://www.justice.gov/archives/opa/pr/federal-court-terminates-paramount-consent-decrees (accessed 2026-10-22, via search result text)
- Rightsline agreed on 2 May 2024 to acquire FilmTrack from City National Bank; FilmTrack (founded 1996) had "nearly 200 entertainment customers" and covers contracts, rights, financials, royalties and participations; Rightsline had "over 170 dedicated professionals" — https://www.rightsline.com/press-release/rightsline-strengthens-global-ip-commerce-leadership-with-acquisition-of-filmtrack/ (accessed 2026-10-22)
- Rightsline announced the acquisition of RSG Media from Naviga on 12 September 2024 — https://www.rightsline.com/press-release/rightsline-amplifies-rights-royalties-platform-capabilities-with-rsg-media-acquisition/ (accessed 2026-10-22, via search result text)
- Third-party review: Rightsline's core strength is multi-platform, multi-territory, multi-window avails queries; contracts stored as structured grants, holdbacks and reversion triggers; enterprise-only sales and no public pricing — https://openrights.blog/reviews/rightsline-review (accessed 2026-10-22, via search result text)
- 98th Academy Awards rules: general entry needs a run of at least seven consecutive days in one theatre in one of six US metro areas, three screenings a day with one between 6 and 10 p.m.; Best Picture requires an expanded run in at least ten of the top 50 US markets within 45 days of the qualifying run; failure may revoke a nomination — https://www.oscars.org/sites/oscars/files/2025-04/98th_aa_best_picture.pdf (accessed 2026-10-22, via search result text)
- 317 feature films were eligible for the 98th Academy Awards, 201 of them for Best Picture — https://press.oscars.org/news/317-feature-films-contention-98th-academy-awardsr (accessed 2026-10-22, via search result text)
- Sundance Institute survey of distributors (January 2019): Juno Films "a lean machine of three"; Indie Rights "2 full-time, 3 part-time"; FilmRise "over 60 and continuing to grow"; 1091 Media acquires 8–10 theatrical slate titles a year; Juno six to eight; Gravitas "theatrically releases three films every month"; Greenwich asks "Do we love the film?"; term lengths "still astronomically high" — https://www.sundance.org/blogs/we-spoke-to-dozens-of-independent-film-distributors-this-is-what-they-want-in-a-project-6/ (accessed 2026-10-22)
- FilmTake (12 November 2025): a strong indie once sold 20–35 territories; typical post-premiere titles now close ~10–14; deal values down 30–70% from the mid-2010s; European advances down 30–60% versus 2014–2017; Pay-1 output deals "largely evaporated" (trade-blog estimates) — https://www.filmtake.com/distribution/afm-2025-the-truth-about-minimum-guarantees-shrinking-territories-and-surviving-the-reset/ (accessed 2026-10-22)
- Mark Litwak (entertainment lawyer's blog, 2025): Neon paid ~$17M for *Together* at Sundance 2025; Neon spent ~$18M on P&A and awards for *Anora* (budget ~$6M); indie streamer licences "typically" $1,000–10,000 per title; theatrical windows now 17–45 days — https://www.marklitwak.com/blog/the-new-reality-for-independent-filmmakers-navigating-a-transformed-industry-landscape-in-2025 (accessed 2026-10-22)
- Cineverse FY2025 Form 10-K (year to 31 March 2025): rights to a library of "over 71,000" film and TV titles and episodes; an appraisal valued the digital library at ~$40M against a ~$2.5M book value — https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1173204/000095017025091741/cnvs-20250331.htm (accessed 2026-10-22, via search result text)
- Swank Motion Pictures: founded 1937, family-owned non-theatrical distributor licensing public performance to colleges, K-12 schools, libraries and other venues; describes itself as the sole source of public performance licensing from all major studios in the K-12 market; college licences are per movie, per day — https://www.swank.com/k-12-schools/faq/ (accessed 2026-10-22, via search result text)
- Producers Library archival footage rate card: documentary use $49 per second (regular) and $79 (premium) for non-theatrical use, $539 licence minimum per project — https://producerslibrary.com/pricing-details (accessed 2026-10-22, via search result text)
- CONUS Archive news footage generally starts at $40 per second with a 30-second minimum or $60 per second with a 10-second minimum — https://conus.com/our-rates/ (accessed 2026-10-22, via search result text)
- Film Independent: a collection account management agreement (CAMA) opens a bank account managed by a third party such as Fintage House or Freeway Entertainment; setup can be ~$8,000 on a low-budget film plus a percentage of revenue (2018 article) — https://www.filmindependent.org/blog/mo-money-no-problems-producers-guide-residuals/ (accessed 2026-10-22, via search result text)
- Parrot Analytics markets a content-valuation product converting audience demand into dollar estimates split by acquisition, retention and engagement, and positions demand data for anchoring minimum guarantees and licence fees by territory (vendor claims) — https://www.parrotanalytics.com/products/content-valuation/ (accessed 2026-10-22, via search result text)
- The Quorum, a film tracking firm, was sold to Greenlight Analytics in June 2025; NRG supplies studios with pre-release tracking; PostTrak exit polls (Screen Engine/ASI with Comscore) are still cited in 2026 — https://thequorum.com/film/ (accessed 2026-10-22, via search result text)
- Gower Street Analytics revised its 2026 global box office estimate to $34.7B in April 2026 from $35.0B; its DRIVERS report is available to clients — https://gower.st/articles/gower-street-revises-2026-global-box-office-estimate-ahead-cinemacon/ (accessed 2026-10-22, via search result text)
- CARA (MPA ratings board) rates roughly 700 films a year with a board of about ten (CNBC, July 2022); raters must be parents; funded by fees from producers and distributors — https://www.cnbc.com/2022/07/09/how-are-movie-ratings-assigned-film-industry-relies-on-obscure-panel.html (accessed 2026-10-22, via search result text)

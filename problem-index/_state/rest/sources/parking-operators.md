## Checked
- NAICS 812930 Parking Lots and Garages, 2022: 2,777 firms, 11,944 establishments, 121,135 employees, annual payroll $3,591,227K, receipts $10,662,408K (~$10.7B). The 43 firms with 500+ employees had 7,606 establishments (~64%), 74,867 employees (~62%) and $6,143,753K of receipts (~58%); the 12 firms with 5,000+ employees had 5,400 establishments, 42,325 employees and $3,516,411K (~33%); the 2,235 firms under 20 employees (~80% of firms) had 10,036 employees and $1,406,532K (~13%) — Census SUSB 2022, US and states by 6-digit NAICS and enterprise size (released 2025-04-10) — https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/susb/tables/2022/us_state_6digitnaics_2022.xlsx (parsed 2026-10-28). Receipts count operators' own revenue: at managed facilities that is the management fee, not the parking revenue collected for the owner.
- SP Plus Corporation 10-K for 2023 (filed 2024-02-28), its last before going private: ~19,900 employees (13,100 full-time, 6,800 part-time), ~31% under collective bargaining; in the Commercial segment ~88% of locations under management type contracts and ~12% under lease type contracts; 159 airports served in Aviation; services revenue $883.2M (management type $590.0M, lease type $293.2M) plus reimbursed management type contract revenue $899.1M, total $1,782.3M; Commercial management contracts $307.7M revenue / $138.9M gross profit, lease contracts $277.8M / $47.8M; management contracts typically 1–3 years, lease contracts 3–10 years; "revenue from management type contracts only includes our contractually agreed upon management fees ... Gross customer collections at facilities under management type contracts, therefore, are not included in our revenue"; the operator provides "accounting and revenue control functions" and monthly reporting; it also sells "municipal meter revenue collection and enforcement services"; acquisitions of DIVRT (frictionless parking software, $17.6M, 2022) and Roker (permits, violations and enforcement, ~$3.1M); competition named from parking aggregators and "third-party technology-driven mobility solutions" — https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1059262/000095017024021554/sp-20231231.htm (accessed 2026-10-28)
- Metropolis closed its take-private of SP+ on 2024-05-16: $54.00 a share, enterprise value ~$1.5B, $1.8B financing led by Eldridge ($1.05B Series C preferred, $550M term debt, $175M PNC revolver); combined company "more than 4,000 locations" and "nearly 20,000-person team"; "computer vision platform enables checkout-free payment experiences" — https://www.metropolis.io/newsroom/metropolis-closes-acquisition-of-sp-plus (accessed 2026-10-28)
- Metropolis financing announced 2025-11-06: $1.6B in total, a ~$500M Series D led by a fund managed by LionTree and a $1.1B Term Loan B arranged by JPMorgan Chase; valuation ~$5B; serves 50 million customers and nearly 20 million Members; over $5B in annual transactions; plans to expand in "parking, retail, hospitality, fueling and mobility" — https://www.metropolis.io/newsroom/metropolis-secures-financing-and-closes-series-d (accessed 2026-10-28); press list (Joby vertiport partnership 2025-12-18; Bilt partnership 2025-10-25) — https://www.metropolis.io/press (accessed 2026-10-28)
- LAZ Parking acquired a 60% stake in INDIGO Park Canada (announced 2025-07-01/02); the combined company runs "more than 5,300 locations" in 44 states and 639 cities in the US and Canada, with "over 20,000 people" and "over 2 million parking spaces"; Argo Infrastructure Partners' funds are LAZ investors — https://www.parking.net/parking-news/laz-parking/acquires-indigo-park-canada (accessed 2026-10-28)
- Mobile Infrastructure Corporation (NYSE American: BEEP) 10-K for 2025 (filed 2026-03-05): 36 parking facilities in 19 markets, ~13,500 spaces, ~4.7M sq ft; 18 employees; operators "may act as agents collecting revenues on our behalf or may act as lessee"; locations where Metropolis is lessee or operator agent produced 63.1% of revenue (excluding commercial) in 2025 (55.7% in 2024), and LAZ is also named; leases converted to management contracts from 2024 because the move "properly aligns the incentives and rewards for revenue growth between the third-party operator and the Company"; RevPAS (revenue per available stall) is a key measure; total revenues $35.1M ($28.6M managed property revenue), NOI $20.7M; strategy to "collaborate with third-party operators to actively manage parking rates based on local insights" — https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1847874/ (10-K beep20251231_10k.htm, accessed 2026-10-28)
- Arlington County, VA internal audit by RSM US LLP, report dated 2026-05-20: contract compliance audit of SP Plus Corporation d/b/a SP+ Municipal Services, Contract No. C24541 (Ballston, Arlington Mill and Lubber Run garages), period 2025-01-01 to 2025-12-31; the County collected ~$3.6M of revenue and incurred $1.9M of expenses; Lubber Run and Arlington Mill ran expenses above revenue; observations: living-wage monitoring gaps rated High (latest SP+ living-wage documentation from Q3 2023), incomplete insurance verification, no periodic background-check monitoring, plus two process improvements (performance-monitoring framework, contract terms aligned to current operations); notes "a transition to more automated systems and reduced reliance on cash handling" — https://www.arlingtonva.us/files/sharedassets/public/v/2/budget/documents/internal-audit/sp-contract-compliance-audit-final-2.pdf (downloaded and read 2026-10-28)
- Maine Public Law 2025, chapter 350 (S.P. 696, L.D. 1794), approved 2025-06-18, enacts 10 MRSA §1500-Z: any owner, operator, manager or collection/enforcement provider on property accessible to the public for parking may impose a fine, fee or charge only if the amount is reasonable and conspicuously posted at every entrance and exit; written notice must go to the registered owner with date and time, the rule broken, the amount, a payment due date at least 10 business days after the notice, payment instructions and contact details; "Title 29-A, section 2117-A prohibit[s] the use of an automated license plate recognition system at a property accessible to the public for the parking of motor vehicles, subject to the exceptions enumerated in that section"; a violation is an unfair trade practice with damages of 3 times the amount charged — https://legislature.maine.gov/ros/LawsOfMaine/breeze/Law/getDocById/?docId=120410 (chapter PDF, read 2026-10-28); status page https://legislature.maine.gov/bills/display_ps.asp?snum=132&paper=SP0696PID%3D1456
- Crostarosa v. LAZ Parking Ltd., Asura Technologies USA, Inc. and Parking Revenue Recovery Services, Inc., D. Colo. No. 1:25-cv-00211-NYW-KAS, Order on Motion to Compel Arbitration filed 2026-01-23: compelled arbitration of the DPPA claim against PRRS, denied it as to Asura and LAZ; the complaint as summarised by the court says PRRS enforces payment at LAZ-managed lots using Asura video analytics, obtains motor vehicle records for vehicles deemed unpaid, and mails notices to registered owners' home addresses with timestamped entry and exit photos — https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-cod-1_25-cv-00211/pdf/USCOURTS-cod-1_25-cv-00211-0.pdf (read 2026-10-28)
- Seattle open data (SDOT): "Paid Parking Occupancy (Last 30 Days)" (dataset rke9-rsvs, updated 2026-10-27, 13,401,759 rows on 2026-10-28) gives per-minute paid occupancy by blockface with parkingspacecount, area, subarea and time-limit category; annual datasets run from 2012 to 2026 (2026: q2e4-e7e5; 2025: 7c2e-uany); "Paid Parking Transaction Data" (gg89-k5p6, updated 2026-10-27) gives meter-level transactions with payment means, amount and duration; "Public Garages and Parking Lots" (3qbw-quib) — https://data.seattle.gov/resource/rke9-rsvs.json and https://data.seattle.gov/api/views/metadata/v1 (test calls 2026-10-28). Occupancy is derived from payments, so placard, permit and unpaid parkers are not counted.

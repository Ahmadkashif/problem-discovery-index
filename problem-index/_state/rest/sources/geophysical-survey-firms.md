# Sources — Geophysical Survey Firms

## Checked
- 968 US geophysical surveying and mapping firms (NAICS 541360) operating 1,019 establishments with 7,249 employees, $710.4M annual payroll and $1,993.0M receipts in 2022; 726 firms (75%) have fewer than 5 employees, 890 (92%) fewer than 20; 17 firms have 100–499 and 16 have 500+ enterprise employment — Census Bureau, Statistics of U.S. Businesses 2022, U.S. & states 6-digit NAICS table (released 2025-04-10), https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/susb/tables/2022/us_state_6digitnaics_2022.xlsx via https://www.census.gov/data/tables/2022/econ/susb/2022-susb-annual.html (accessed 2026-10-22). Derived: ~$275,000 receipts and ~$98,000 payroll per employee.
- Texas holds 206 firms, 2,187 employees and $699.2M receipts (35% of US receipts); Colorado $337.2M; Virginia $162.2M; Montana $124.0M; Oklahoma 241 firms but only 407 employees and $100.7M — same SUSB 2022 table, state rows (accessed 2026-10-22). State firm counts double-count multi-state firms.
- Dawson Geophysical: 2025 revenues $75.6M ($74.2M in 2024); 269 full-time employees at 2025-12-31, 50 of them management, sales and administrative; ~130 vibrator source units and ~280,000 recording channels; crews of ~40–100 technicians; one client ~51% of 2025 revenue; "The results of seismic surveys conducted for a client belong to that client"; serves mainly multi-client library providers; surveys cannot begin without governmental permits, which are not always granted in the expected time; Canadian work seasonal with the thaw; competitors named SAExploration, Echo Seismic and Paragon Geophysical; Wilks Brothers controls ~80% and has been in transaction discussions — Form 10-K FY2025, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/799165/000110465926037886/dwsn-20251231x10k.htm and Q4 2025 release summary https://www.barchart.com/story/news/1051253/dawson-geophysical-reports-fourth-quarter-and-year-end-2025-results (accessed 2026-10-22).
- Viridien (formerly CGG) 2025 segment revenue $1,165M, up 4%; Geoscience $444M (+10%), Earth Data $406M (+6%); segment adjusted EBITDA $551M — https://www.viridiengroup.com/company/newsroom/press-release/viridien-announces-its-2025-annual-financial-results (accessed 2026-10-22, via search summary of the release).
- TGS–PGS merger completed 1 July 2024; Q1 2025 multi-client revenue USD 267M; Q2 2025 multi-client sales ~USD 135–137M; 2025 described as a transitional year after the merger — https://www.tgs.com/press-releases/tgs-q2-2025-operational-and-financial-update and https://www.tgs.com/hubfs/Financial%20Reports/Annual%20Reports/2025%20Annual%20Report/TGS%202025%20Annual%20Report.pdf (accessed 2026-10-22, via search summaries).
- Pulse Seismic: 2025 revenue C$51.1M, up 118%; owns the largest licensable seismic library in Canada, ~65,310 km² of 3D and ~829,207 km of 2D, mostly in the Western Canada Sedimentary Basin; single licences of C$10.0M (Jan 2025) and C$13.5M (Jun 2025) — https://boereport.com/2026/02/24/pulse-seismic-inc-reports-2025-financial-results-and-declares-regular-and-special-dividends/ and https://www.barchart.com/story/news/32876839/pulse-announces-a-13-5-million-seismic-data-license-sale-and-provides-revenue-update (accessed 2026-10-22).
- Multi-client licences are non-assignable and charge a transfer or change-of-control fee: SEITEL's licences require a surviving entity to pay to keep data it did not previously license; Global Geophysical's licences transfer only on payment of a specified fee; AAPG Explorer notes sharply differing views between owners and licensees on non-exclusive licence rights in M&A — https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000750813/000075081318000006/a4q-2017x10kdocument.htm, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001311486/000117184312000586/f10k_021512.htm, https://www.aapg.org/publications/news/explorer/details/articleid/33165 (accessed 2026-10-22, via search summaries).
- ASCE 38-22 utility quality levels: QL-D records, QL-C surveyed visible features correlated with records, QL-B surface geophysical "designating" of horizontal position, QL-A vacuum-excavation exposure for horizontal and vertical position at selected points; MassDOT directive lets providers fall back to a lower level where a segment cannot be shown at QL-B — https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/programadmin/sueindex.cfm and https://www.mass.gov/doc/policy-directive-e-26-002-massdot-requirements-for-performing-subsurface-utility-engineering-investigations/download (accessed 2026-10-22).
- DoD Advanced Geophysical Classification Accreditation Program (DAGCAP): created by the DoD Environmental Data Quality Workgroup to accredit organisations performing advanced classification at munitions response sites through two third-party accreditation bodies; "approximately 95% of excavated objects are not buried munitions"; SERDP/ESTCP demonstrated classification of targets of interest (TOI) from debris — DAGCAP fact sheet, https://www.denix.osd.mil/mmrp/denix-files/sites/46/2017/05/2_DAGCAP-Fact-Sheet.pdf (accessed 2026-10-22).
- DAGCAP demonstration of capability: the dig list is a CSV with a 1/0 dig decision; passing requires every TOI marked for digging (FN = 0) and false positives at or below 40% of emplaced clutter; 0.25 m halo per dig location; SOP-1 v6.0 dated August 2025 — https://www.denix.osd.mil/mmrp/denix-files/sites/46/2026/03/DAGCAP-SOP-1-v6.0-APG-DOC_508c.pdf (accessed 2026-10-22, via search summary).
- Seequent markets UX-Analyze inside the UXO Land extension of Oasis montaj: dipole inversion for polarizabilities, library matching, ranked dig lists, and dynamic (one-pass) classification since Oasis montaj 2022.2 — https://www.seequent.com/uxo-land-ux-analyze/ and https://www.seequent.com/accelerating-agc-surveys-with-dynamic-classification-in-ux-analyze/ (accessed 2026-10-22).
- BOEM proprietary terms for permit data acquired under 30 CFR 551: geological data and information 10 years, geophysical data 50 years, geophysical information 25 years after permit issue — 30 CFR 550.197, https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/30/550.197 (accessed 2026-10-22).
- Exodigo closed a $96M Series B on 16 July 2025, $214M raised since 2021; multi-sensor non-intrusive subsurface mapping fused by AI; customers include Amtrak, Florida DOT, California High-Speed Rail Authority, Kansas DOT, LA Metro and SEPTA — https://www.undergroundinfrastructure.com/news/2025/july/exodigo-raises-96-million-to-expand-ai-underground-mapping-for-us-global-projects and https://siliconangle.com/2025/07/16/investors-dig-75m-funding-underground-intelligence-startup-exodigo/ (accessed 2026-10-22).
- GPRS (Ground Penetrating Radar Systems, Toledo, Ohio) provides private utility locating and concrete scanning nationally with a network of more than 250 project managers (2020); Kohlberg & Company took a majority stake from CIVC in June 2020 — https://www.ropesgray.com/en/newsroom/news/2020/06/Ropes-Gray-Represents-Kohlberg-Co-in-its-Acquisition-of-GPRS-Holdings-LLC-from-CIVC-Partners-LP and https://peprofessional.com/2020/06/kohlberg-returns-utility-services/ (accessed 2026-10-22). Roark Capital's January 2025 partnership and the August 2025 Accu-Scan GPR acquisition were seen in search summaries only and are not used as headline figures.
- California licenses Professional Geophysicists (PGp) through BPELSG: annual four-hour computer-based exam, five years' professional experience, references from people in responsible charge of geophysical work — https://bpelsg.ca.gov/applicants/exa_app_info_for_gg.shtml and https://bpelsg.ca.gov/applicants/faq_gg (accessed 2026-10-22). A 2020-era Cardno release said ~155 active PGp licensees (undated; not used as a figure).
- Geoscientists (SOC 19-2042): ~24,620 employed, mean wage ~$104,000 (May 2023 OEWS); architectural, engineering and related services is the largest employing industry — https://www.bls.gov/oes/current/oes192042.htm (accessed 2026-10-22).
- GPR interpretation is judgement: locators "learn early that not all hyperbolas are utilities" (many are rocks or debris) — Sensors & Software webinar, https://www.sensoft.ca/training-events/webinars/webinar-getting-the-most-from-your-gpr-utility-data/ (accessed 2026-10-22).
- Unmarked-grave GPR searches report "reflections of interest", not confirmed burials; the Star Blanket search reported more than 2,000 anomalies; archaeologists warn both against anomalies becoming "children" and against discrediting the method; confirming a burial requires ground-truthing — https://guelph.ctvnews.ca/canada/article/in-the-search-for-unmarked-graves-at-residential-school-sites-what-do-radar-anomalies-mean and https://aptnnews.ca/?p=229167 and https://nctr.dyndns.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/RTC-Discussion-Paper-2-On-the-Ground-Searches-1.pdf (accessed 2026-10-22).
- USGS Earth MRI flew airborne magnetic and radiometric surveys over Missouri, Illinois, Iowa and Wisconsin (announced Nov 2025), eastern Maine and the Blue Mountains (2025–26), and New Mexico (Mar–Oct 2026) with state geological surveys — https://www.usgs.gov/news/state-news-release/media-alert-low-level-airplane-flights-scan-geology-over-parts-four and https://www.usgs.gov/news/state-news-release/media-alert-low-level-airplane-and-helicopter-flights-resume-scanning (accessed 2026-10-22).
- Texas Railroad Commission seismic response areas (Northern Culberson-Reeves, Gardendale, Stanton) restrict produced-water disposal; RRC monitors USGS, TexNet and private operator stations; it warns of a 12–18 month lag between injection cuts and seismicity decline — https://rrc.texas.gov/oil-and-gas/applications-and-permits/injection-storage-permits/oil-and-gas-waste-disposal/injection-disposal-permit-procedures/seismicity-review/seismicity-response and https://www.rrc.texas.gov/news/111722-reeves-county-seismic-activity (accessed 2026-10-22).
- Seismic access on private land requires landowner or occupant permission, negotiated by permit agents (Alberta guidance); Texas University Lands permits set a surface damage rate at issue and cover one crew for one client — https://www.pembina.org/sites/default/files/2025-10/Landowners_Guide_2025-Sec3.pdf and https://universitylands.utsystem.edu/OilGas/Permits (accessed 2026-10-22).

- A New Mexico dispute over a 3D survey on ranch land turned on vibration damage to plaster and truck tracks, and whether permits required compensation for surface damage — https://law.co/app/cases/15837 (accessed 2026-10-22, via search summary).

- Global seismic and geophysical data, acquisition and processing spend ~$8–10B — derived from Viridien ($1.17B segment revenue) and TGS (~$1B+ a year implied by 2025 quarterly figures) plus Shearwater, SLB's data business, BGP and near-surface spend by judgement.
- US near-surface geophysics (SUE designating, UXO classification, concrete scanning, engineering and environmental surveys) at roughly half of 541360 receipts — judgement from the state pattern; concrete scanning and private locating firms such as GPRS may be coded elsewhere (561990 or 238) in Census data.
- Niche market sizes (energy seismic ~$1.1B US-serving; SUE designating ~$600M; seismic permitting ~$120M; legacy data rescue ~$80M; junior mining geophysics ~$250M North America; burial and archaeological GPR ~$40M; UXO classification ~$300M; concrete scanning ~$500M) and their shares — allocation by judgement, partly outside 541360.
- Permit agent day rates, permit refusal rates (~5–20% of tracts), skid and offset practices, damage payment ranges — recalled from industry practice; no public benchmark found.

- Share of QL-B designations that miss QL-A test-hole positions by more than tolerance, and the share of concrete cores that hit embedded items — no public data; described qualitatively.

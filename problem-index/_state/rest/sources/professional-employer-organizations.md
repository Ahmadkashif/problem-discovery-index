# Sources — Professional Employer Organizations

## Checked
- End of 2022: 523 PEOs in the US, 4.5 million worksite employees (WSEs) paid $308 billion (the paper's key-statistics table prints $305 billion), working for over 208,000 PEO clients, a count equal to 17% of all US businesses with 10-99 employees (the authors say the true penetration among that size band is likely closer to 12-13%); average annual growth 7.5% compounded, 2008-2022; the 5 largest PEOs hold an estimated 39% of WSEs, the next 25 another 19%, and the remaining 493 PEOs ~42% (NAPEO, "PEO Industry Footprint 2023", Laurie Bassi and Dan McMurrer, McBassi & Company) — https://napeo.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/peoindustryfootprint2023_finalweb.pdf (accessed 2026-10-23)
- PEO client profile from data on 50,000+ clients, 2023 to early 2025: half of PEO clients have 10-49 employees and another 35% fewer than 10; 14% penetration among employers with 20-499 employees and 15% among those with 50-99; Professional, Scientific and Technical Services is 19% of clients and Construction 12%, and four industries hold almost half of all clients (NAPEO, "PEO Clients: Who They Are, Where They Are, and What They Do", October 2025, McBassi & Company) — https://napeo.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/PEOClients2025_WhitePaper_Web.pdf (accessed 2026-10-23)
- NAICS 561330 in 2022: 2,959 firms, 4,161 establishments, 3,713,938 employees, annual payroll ~$286.8B, receipts ~$189.9B; the 467 firms with 500+ employees account for 3,581,804 employees and ~$178.0B of receipts (94% of receipts); 2,492 firms have fewer than 500 employees and 1,421 fewer than 20. Employment counts the co-employed worksite employees (Census Statistics of US Businesses, 2022, US by 6-digit NAICS and enterprise size) — https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/susb/tables/2022/us_state_6digitnaics_2022.xlsx via https://www.census.gov/data/tables/2022/econ/susb/2022-susb-annual.html (accessed 2026-10-23)
- NAICS 561330 in 2024, private ownership, national: 19,039 establishments, 422,331 average employees, ~$32.6B total wages, average annual pay $77,240, employment down 6.1% on 2023; largest states by employment California (60,705), Florida (56,114), Arizona (48,051), New York (45,142) (BLS QCEW annual file). The gap against Census SUSB (3.7M) and NAPEO (4.5M) is my inference from the reporting rules below: in client-level reporting states the worksite employees are filed under the client's own unemployment account and industry — https://data.bls.gov/cew/data/api/2024/a/industry/561330.csv (accessed 2026-10-23)
- IRS active CPEO list (report date 7 August 2026): 122 certified legal entities; by my count of entity names they belong to roughly 60-65 PEO groups, with ADP TotalSource alone holding 18 certified entities, and smaller listings including trucking-focused PEOs (Transport Tax Consulting, TransComp) and Hawaii PEOs; the IRS updates the list by the 15th day of the first month of every calendar quarter and publishes separate suspended and revoked lists — https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-sbse/active-cpeos.pdf and https://www.irs.gov/node/24376 (accessed 2026-10-23)
- CPEO bond: "All bonds must be equal to 5 percent of the CPEO liability under section 3511", minimum $50,000 and maximum $1,000,000, proof on Form 14751 due within 30 days of the notice of certification; material changes reported within 30 days (45 for a new responsible individual) — https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/certified-professional-employer-organizations-what-you-need-to-know (accessed 2026-10-23)
- CPEO maintenance: a quarterly assertion, signed by a responsible individual, that all federal employment taxes were withheld and deposited, with an examination-level CPA attestation, due by the last day of the second month after each calendar quarter, plus a working-capital statement; annual audited financial statements with an unmodified CPA opinion due by the last day of the sixth month after fiscal year end; late filings can lead to suspension or revocation (Rev. Proc. 2023-18 as summarised by NAPEO and practitioners; the IRS maintenance page itself was not read) — https://napeo.org/advocacy/federal-issues/irs-certification/roadmap-to-irs-certification/attestation-requirements/ and https://www.eitc.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rp-23-18.pdf (accessed 2026-10-23, via search results)
- Insperity full-year 2025 (release of 10 February 2026): average paid worksite employees per month up 1% to 310,089; revenue up 4% to $6.8B; gross profit down 14% to $900M, "primarily" from unfavourable benefits results partly offset by pricing; net loss $7M; operating expenses included $48M for the Workday partnership; in mid-2025 Insperity had raised its full-year benefits cost per employee guidance (by 75-100 basis points, per one earnings summary) — https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260210510166/en/Insperity-Announces-Fourth-Quarter-and-Full-Year-2025-Results and https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250801061380/en (accessed 2026-10-23, via search results)
- Insperity FY2025 Form 10-K: UnitedHealthcare provides ~85% of participants' health coverage under a contract expiring 31 December 2028 (180 days' notice to cancel), and Insperity "retain[s] an obligation to United to fund the cost of the plan"; workers' compensation through Chubb since 2007, fully insured, with Insperity responsible for the first $1.5M per occurrence on claims since 1 October 2019 and up to a $6M aggregate per policy year, current coverage expiring 30 September 2026; "42 states have enacted legislation either recognizing PEOs or requiring licensing, registration, or certification"; its two primary PEO subsidiaries are CPEOs; client retention ~83% in 2025 and 81% in 2024 (five-year average ~84%); an average of 612 Business Performance Advisors in 2025 against 769 in 2024; prospects undergo "a comprehensive analysis of employer-related risks" covering workers' compensation, unemployment history and group medical information, and Insperity avoids certain higher-risk NAICS codes; client pricing is generally set for one year (two for middle-market clients) from a proprietary pricing model applied to census data; named competitors are the PEO arms of ADP and Paychex, TriNet, Vensure and Rippling; HRScale, built with Workday, has first clients from Q1 2026 — https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1000753/000100075326000011/nsp-20251231.htm (accessed 2026-10-23)
- TriNet FY2025 Form 10-K: total revenues $5.0B, down 1%; insurance cost ratio (insurance costs over insurance service revenues) 91%; average WSEs ~334,000, down 5%; ~323,000 total WSEs at year end including 294,000 co-employed; net income $155M; 2026 insurance cost ratio guidance 89.25-90.75% — https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/937098/000093709826000010/tnet-20251231.htm and https://seekingalpha.com/pr/20397309 (accessed 2026-10-23, via search results)
- ADP fiscal 2026: PEO average worksite employees up 2% to ~762,000 for the year and ~775,000 in Q4; PEO revenue up 7% (5% excluding zero-margin benefits pass-throughs); PEO margin down ~110 basis points for the year, attributed on the call to pass-through growth and higher workers' compensation and selling costs — https://sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/8670/000000867026000025/q4fy26exhibit99.htm and https://www.marketbeat.com/instant-alerts/automatic-data-processing-q4-earnings-call-highlights-2026-07-29/ (accessed 2026-10-23, via search results)
- PEO health underwriting (PrestigePEO webinar deck "PEO Underwriting and Risk", 11 June 2025, presented by its VP of Underwriting, formerly of PEO Velocity, where she worked with 40+ PEOs on master health plan risk): NAPEO estimates over 1M people receive medical insurance through PEOs; PEO plans average member age in the mid to upper 30s; underwriters use forecasted morbidity scores (the deck names SAIL and Curv), personal and group health questionnaires, incumbent renewals and loss ratios; a PEO Velocity study of a plan with $135M premium and 26,000 members found 540 claimants over $50,000, the top 2% of members with nearly 50% of claims and mean cost $125,000 per high-cost claimant; first- and later-year worksite MLRs: 15% of groups at 125%+ MLR carried 50% of claims, against a 75% target; "for every 165 groups, 25 perform unexpectedly well, 25 perform expectedly poorly, and 115 perform generally near forecasted"; R-squared on all models "still relatively low"; "we shouldn't override declines"; roughly 11 eligible employees are needed per $25,000 of forecast expense, against an average PEO client of 25-35 eligibles; a cross-PEO size table of 10,726 subscriber worksites shows MLR rising from 81% (1-9 subscribers) to 97% (100+); a 3,000-subscriber plan with 88% retention needs ~150 new-business quotes a year to grow 7.6% — https://prestigepeo.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/PrestigePEO-Presents-PEO-Underwriting-and-Risks.pdf (accessed 2026-10-23)
- NAPEO-commissioned McBassi study (September 2024, data January 2023 to January 2024): PEO clients grew 4.3% against 1.6% for comparable firms, turnover 50.4% against 57.6% (about 12% lower), and PEO clients were 50% less likely to go out of business; the authors note selection effects — https://napeo.org/press-releases/new-economic-data-shows-peo-engagement-doubles-growth-rate-for-businesses/ and https://www.prismhr.com/?p=32014 (accessed 2026-10-23, via search results)
- Workers' comp policy forms for PEOs: under a master policy NCCI requires client-level payroll and loss data reported alongside the combined data so a mod can be calculated for the master policy and for each client (Nevada DIR circular); multiple coordinated policies (a PEO-held policy per client) are authorised by state endorsements such as North Carolina's Rule 3D; New Hampshire urged insurers to consider MCPs for any voluntary-market policy issued to a PEO; West Virginia requires a master policy to cover all direct-hire employees and every client's covered employees — https://dir.nv.gov/uploadedFiles/dirnvgov/content/WCS/EmployersDocs/PEOCircNV-2012-01.pdf , https://webha.ncrb.org/Portals/0/ncrb/circular%20letters/workers%20comp/2016/C-16-22%20Revisions%20to%20NC%20Basic%20Manual%20Rule%203D%20PEO%20State%20Specific%20Endorsements.pdf , https://mm.nh.gov/files/uploads/nhid/documents/wc-peo-120709.pdf and https://law.cornell.edu/regulations/west-virginia/W-Va-C-S-R-SS-85-31-6 (accessed 2026-10-23, via search results)
- Amwins Underwriting (Amwins Specialty Casualty Solutions) runs a workers' compensation programme for PEOs and mixed-class payrolls; submissions need payroll by class and loss history, and accounts with inadequate payroll detail or poor loss history without a remediation plan are likely declined — https://completemarkets.com/company/Amwinsunderwriting/Professional-Employer-Organizations-Workers-Comp/ (accessed 2026-10-23, via search result)
- State unemployment reporting for PEOs falls into PEO-level, client-level and hybrid regimes; Deel lists Arkansas, Kansas, Louisiana, Maine and Tennessee as hybrid and California, Massachusetts, Ohio, Pennsylvania and ~20 others as client-level, some needing a power of attorney or TPA authorisation; Justworks lists 22 PEO-reporting states, four of which require client-level reporting in some situations; Indiana DWD confirms one PEO account for all clients where the PEO reports at PEO level; the vendor lists disagree in places — https://www.deel.com/blog/peo-services-state-by-state-guide/ , https://help.justworks.com/hc/en-us/articles/10868185054747-State-Unemployment-Insurance-SUI and https://www.in.gov/dwd/indiana-unemployment/employers/professional-employer-organizations-faq/ (accessed 2026-10-23, via search results)
- ESAC accreditation: ESAC buys a $15M aggregate surety bond each year for accredited PEOs, each of which also buys an individual bond of $250,000-$1M at 1% of the bond amount; accredited PEOs provide audited financial statements and quarterly independent CPA verification that taxes, benefit contributions and insurance premiums were paid — https://www.withum.com/resources/why-esac-accreditation-matters-for-peos-and-how-to-get-it/ and https://www.pr.com/press-release/578404 (accessed 2026-10-23, via search results)

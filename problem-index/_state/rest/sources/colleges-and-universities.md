- Higher education R&D: $117.7B in FY2024, up 8.1% ($8.9B) on FY2023; federally funded R&D "surpassed $64 billion", 55% of the total; HHS (including NIH) $35.5B, up $2.4B; census of 925 universities and colleges — NCSES InfoBrief NSF 26-305, 10 February 2026, https://ncses.nsf.gov/pubs/nsf26305 (accessed 2026-10-24).
- Tuition discounting: NACUBO projected the average institutional discount rate for first-time, full-time undergraduates at private nonprofits at 57.1% for 2025-26 (all undergraduates 51.3%) across 258 institutions; average net tuition revenue per undergraduate fell 1.9% after inflation in 2024-25 (2.2% for first-time, full-time students) — Higher Ed Dive, 1 June 2026, https://www.highereddive.com/news/tuition-discount-rate-reaches-57-for-private-nonprofits-nacubo-says/821554/ (accessed 2026-10-24). The page gives the prior-year first-time rate as 54.5%; an earlier NACUBO release had put preliminary 2024-25 at 56.3% (search summary, https://www.insidehighered.com/news/business/revenue-strategies/2025/06/24/tuition-discounting-hits-another-high). The vault uses only the 57.1% 2025-26 figure.
- Demographic cliff: "in 2025 the nation will see the highest number of high school graduates (3.9 million)" and by 2041 "there will be 3.4 million graduates, or a 13% decline" — WICHE, Knocking at the College Door, 11th edition press release, December 2024, https://www.wiche.edu/?p=47414 (accessed 2026-10-24). Regional detail (steeper declines in the Northeast, Midwest and West; growth in the South) from the search summary of https://wiche.edu/knocking/.
- Fall 2025 enrollment: over 19.4 million total, up 1.0%; undergraduate 16.2 million (+1.2%); graduate 3.2 million (−0.3%); private nonprofit four-year undergraduate −1.6%; public four-year undergraduate +1.4%; graduate international −5.9% (about 10,000 students) — National Student Clearinghouse Research Center, final fall 2025 enrollment trends, released 15 January 2026, https://nscresearchcenter.org/final-fall-enrollment-trends/ (accessed 2026-10-24).
- Ratings outlooks for 2026: Fitch "deteriorating" (5 December 2025 report); Moody's outlook "remains negative for 2026" (21 November 2025); S&P negative for nonprofit colleges, citing "mounting operating pressures and uncertainty" (3 December 2025) — Higher Ed Dive, 5 January 2026, https://www.highereddive.com/news/ratings-agencies-higher-ed-finance-2026-outlooks-moodys-sp-fitch-federal-policy/808596/ (accessed 2026-10-24). The article gives no revenue-versus-expense numbers.
- One Big Beautiful Bill Act student-aid changes effective 1 July 2026: Grad PLUS eliminated for new borrowers (continuing borrowers keep access up to three years); graduate loans capped at $20,500 a year and $100,000 aggregate, professional students $50,000 and $200,000; Parent PLUS capped at $20,000 a year and $65,000 per dependent student; new Repayment Assistance Plan (1–10% of AGI, $10 minimum); Workforce Pell effective 20 July 2026 (early implementation 1 July); students whose non-federal grants cover full cost of attendance become Pell-ineligible from 19 May 2026 — Governors State University financial aid page, https://govst.edu/OBBB (accessed 2026-10-24).
- College Scorecard data: institution-level files 1996-97 through 2025-26 covering "institutional characteristics, enrollment, student aid, costs, and student outcomes"; field-of-study files by credential level and 4-digit CIP with "cumulative debt at graduation and earnings one year after graduation"; OPEID–UNITID crosswalks; an API; data "last updated October 15, 2026" — https://collegescorecard.ed.gov/data/ (accessed 2026-10-24).
- Transfer credit loss: "students who transferred from 2004 to 2009 lost, on average, an estimated 43 percent of their credits"; "an estimated 35 percent of college students transferred to a new school at least once from 2004 to 2009" — GAO-17-574, https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-17-574 (accessed 2026-10-24). By-path figures (22% for public two-year to public four-year; 94% for for-profit to public) are from the search summary of the same report.
- Some College, No Credential, 2025 report (4 June 2025): 43.1 million people stopped out without a credential at the start of 2023-24, 37.6 million of working age (under 65), up 2.2%; 42 states and DC saw re-enrollment increases in 2023-24; the share of re-enrollees earning a credential in their first year rose to 4.7% — https://nscresearchcenter.org/some-college-no-credential/ (accessed 2026-10-24). "Over one million adults returned" is from the search summary of the report coverage.
- FERPA school-official exception: an outside party to whom an institution "has outsourced institutional services or functions may be considered a school official" only if it performs a function the institution would otherwise use employees for, "is under the direct control of the agency or institution with respect to the use and maintenance of education records", and is bound by §99.33(a); §99.33(a)(2) lets recipients use the information "only for the purposes for which the disclosure was made"; §99.31(a)(6) permits disclosure to organisations conducting studies for institutions to develop predictive tests, administer student aid or improve instruction — 34 CFR 99.31 and 99.33, eCFR versioner API, https://www.ecfr.gov/api/versioner/v1/full/2026-10-01/title-34.xml?part=99&section=99.31 and https://www.ecfr.gov/api/versioner/v1/full/2026-10-01/title-34.xml?part=99&section=99.33 (accessed 2026-10-24). This is the basis for treating student records held by vendors under contract as a live "data isn't theirs" switch unless checked terms give product rights.

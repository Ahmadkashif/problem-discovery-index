# Sources — Self-Insured Benefit & Risk Pools

## Checked
- NAICS 525120 (Health and Welfare Funds) in 2024, national level: private ownership 739 establishments, 5,174 average employees, ~$473M total wages, average annual pay $91,439; state government 1 establishment and local government 3 (employment suppressed). NAICS 525190 (Other Insurance Funds): private 390 establishments, 2,398 average employees, ~$318M total wages, average annual pay $132,552; state 5 and local 6 establishments (suppressed). Both codes badly undercount the activity: Taft-Hartley fund offices are often staffed by third-party administrators filed under insurance agencies and TPAs, and public entity pools and state health benefit programmes report largely under public administration (BLS QCEW annual files) — https://data.bls.gov/cew/data/api/2024/a/industry/525120.csv and https://data.bls.gov/cew/data/api/2024/a/industry/525190.csv (accessed 2026-10-22)
- 67% of covered workers were in self-funded plans in 2025: 27% at firms with 10 to 199 workers and 80% at larger firms; 37% of covered workers at firms with 10 to 199 workers were in level-funded plans, which pair a small self-funded component with stop-loss insurance; average annual premiums $9,325 single and $26,993 family (KFF 2025 Employer Health Benefits Survey) — https://www.kff.org/health-costs/report/2025-employer-health-benefits-survey/ (accessed 2026-10-22)
- 1,220 multiemployer (Taft-Hartley) health and welfare plans covering about 4.8 million members in 2023, from Form 5500 filings; average plan held net assets of about one year and four months of benefits and expenses; income exceeded expenses by 14.1%; per-member expenses rose 5.8% and contribution income 3.2%; 2023 investment return 9.8% (Milliman, Multiemployer health and welfare fund statistics: 2025 report). The 2023 edition counted 1,226 plans and ~4.6 million members as of 2021 — https://www.milliman.com/en/insight/multiemployer-health-and-welfare-fund-statistics-2025 and https://www.milliman.com/en/insight/multiemployer-health-and-welfare-fund-statistics-2023 (accessed 2026-10-22)
- "Over 500 risk-sharing pools serving municipalities, school districts, and other public entities in the United States and Canada"; "more than 90,000 public entities in the United States"; AGRiP "estimates that at least 80 percent of all local public entities participate in one or more risk pools"; most pools are authorised by state law to cover only public entities in their state (AGRiP PR toolkit messaging document, © 2014, so the counts are dated) — https://higherlogicdownload.s3.amazonaws.com/AGRIP/613d38fc-c2ec-4e1a-b31f-03fa706321aa/UploadedImages/documents/PR_Toolkit_Messaging_Document.pdf (accessed 2026-10-22)
- AGRiP Pooling Basics textbook (updated 2025): pools set annual contributions from actuarial loss projections (incurred and paid development methods) at a stated confidence level, with 55% the standard best-estimate level; case reserves are set by a claims adjuster, lawyer or other professional; "many pools maintain exposures, rates and associated underwriting calculations on a spreadsheet or similar tool", and spreadsheets are used for one-off member calculations even where a pool has an underwriting system; AGRiP's own staff directory lists seven people (executive director, managing director, program manager, member data specialist, engagement manager, marketing and operations) — https://higherlogicdownload.s3.amazonaws.com/AGRIP/613d38fc-c2ec-4e1a-b31f-03fa706321aa/UploadedImages/Pooling_Basics_Textbook_-_Updated_2025.pdf (accessed 2026-10-22)
- CIRSA (Colorado Intergovernmental Risk Sharing Agency), "Factors Affecting 2024 Contributions": police liability named the first driver of 2024 cost; Colorado S.B. 20-217 expanded police liability under state law; an $8.75M settlement by a CIRSA member department, then a $9.5M sheriff's settlement, a $14M Denver jury verdict in 2022 from the 2020 protests, and a $19M settlement in 2023 involving a sheriff's department, two CIRSA members and state agencies; long-time liability reinsurers left the market; members with police departments saw a 150% increase in the cost of law enforcement liability coverage for 2024, covering defence and settlements in CIRSA's retention, a possible retention increase from $1M to $2M and reinsurance; blended liability increase 47%; for 2024 and beyond members choose liability limits of $2M, $5M or $10M per claim or occurrence, where CIRSA previously provided $10M for most liability coverages; the rating method multiplies each coverage's rate by exposure, a deductible factor and an experience modifier, and property is not experience-rated; 2023 hail claims reserved at $15.4M — https://www.cirsa.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/Factors-Affecting-2024-Contributions-The-CIRSA-Rating-Methodology.pdf (accessed 2026-10-22)
- New Mexico Counties' insurance authority 2025 annual report attributes liability cost increases largely to the New Mexico Civil Rights Act (higher damages and attorney fees in state court) and records Bernalillo County leaving the pool's liability programme for a largely self-insured model; Utah and Texas pool packets show flat-to-modest law enforcement liability changes for 2026 (search-result summaries; used only as context that the pressure is uneven by state) — https://www.nmcounties.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/2025-NMCIA-Annual-Report-_-final.pdf and https://info.tmlirp.org/hubfs/Risk%20Pool%20Report%20newsletter%20graphics/April-May-June%202026/2026-2027%20Rates%20TTC%20Article%20(FINAL)%20(003).pdf (accessed 2026-10-22, via search results)
- Origami Risk markets one platform for public entity pools (claims, underwriting, renewals, billing, loss control, member portals); Montana Schools Group Insurance Authority had done underwriting entirely in spreadsheets before adopting it; Washington Schools Risk Management Pool chose it in 2016; Public Risk Underwriters of Florida (manager of Preferred Governmental Insurance Trust) selected it for policy administration and billing in March 2024; New Jersey Schools Insurance Group also uses it (vendor case studies and press releases) — https://www.origamirisk.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/OrigamiRisk_SellSheet_Risk_Pools_20251111.pdf , https://www.origamirisk.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/MSGIA-CS.pdf and https://fintechfutures.com/press-releases/origami-risk-selected-by-public-risk-underwriters-of-florida-for-policy-administration-and-billing (accessed 2026-10-22, via search results)
- Stop-loss insurance carrier premiums reached $39B in 2024 (Oliver Wyman, as reported by Insurance Business; the original report was not read); Segal recorded a 9.4% average stop-loss premium increase in 2024 — https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/us/premium/stoploss-is-leaving-onesizefitsall-behind-583808.aspx (accessed 2026-10-22)
- Sun Life's 13th annual high-cost claims report (June 4, 2025): million-dollar stop-loss claims rose 29% on a claims-per-million-covered-employees basis and 61% over four years, from more than 65,000 claims across 3,000 employers, 2021–2024; Sun Life paid $6.8M for one Elevidys gene therapy in June 2024 — https://www.benefitspro.com/2025/06/09/employers-1m-health-claim-rate-rises-29 and https://www.sunlife.com/en/newsroom/news-releases/announcement/cancer-still-tops-high-cost-health-claims-but-muscle-and-orthopedic-conditions-are-making-surprise-surge-sun-life-report/123968/ (accessed 2026-10-22, via search results)
- Self-insured employers paid 25.6% of the $64.1B of workers' compensation benefits in 2023 (private insurers 55.6%, state funds 13.3%, federal 5.5%); medical benefits were more than 47% of the total; ~150 million jobs covered; employer cost $0.98 per $100 of covered payroll (NASI, Workers' Compensation: Benefits, Costs, and Coverage, 2023 data, published August 11, 2026) — https://www.nasi.org/2026/08/11/workers-compensation-benefits-costs-and-coverage-2023-data/ (accessed 2026-10-22)
- UAW Retiree Medical Benefits Trust, fiscal year ending December 2024 (Form 990 filed November 14, 2025): total revenue ~$3.50B, total expenses ~$3.30B, total assets ~$62.86B, net assets ~$60.20B — https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/900424876 (accessed 2026-10-22); the trust reported covering over 535,000 members in 2024 and more than 860,000 people at its 2010 launch (via search result)
- Gag Clause Prohibition Compliance Attestation: group health plans and issuers attest annually, with subsequent attestations due by December 31 each year (the first was due December 31, 2023) — https://www.cms.gov/marketplace/about/oversight/other-insurance-protections/gag-clause-prohibition-compliance-attestation (accessed 2026-10-22)
- RxDC (prescription drug and health care spending) reports are due June 1 each year for the prior reference year; the 2024 submission fell on June 3 because June 1 was a Saturday (benefits-compliance bulletins; the CMS RxDC page itself was not read) — https://www.hubinternational.com/hub-resources/employee-benefits-compliance-bulletins/2023/03/gag-clause-prohibition-attestation and https://www.newfront.com/blog/year-end-eb-compliance-items-to-address (accessed 2026-10-22, via search results)
- On May 15, 2025 the Departments announced non-enforcement of the 2024 MHPAEA final rule's new requirements; the NQTL comparative analysis duty enacted by the Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2021 is unaffected — https://www.spencerfane.com/insight/mental-health-parity-government-announces-non-enforcement-of-nqtl-2024-final-rule/ and https://blog.ifebp.org/mental-health-parity-what-plan-sponsors-need-to-know-during-period-of-nonenforcement-of-2024-final-rule/ (accessed 2026-10-22, via search results)
- Hour-bank eligibility examples: TeamCare's Hourly Plan determines eligibility monthly from reported hours, banked dollars or self-payments, with coverage in the second month after the month worked; the Southern California IBEW-NECA plan credits contributions arriving more than three months after the work month to the hours bank in the month received, because service-provider contracts prohibit retroactive eligibility, and requires 100 hours a month; the Northern California Laborers plan grants initial eligibility after 440 banked hours and deducts 110 hours a month — https://myteamcare.org/-/media/TeamCare/Files/Other-Documents/Summit/Hourly-Plan-Brochure_2025---web.pdf , https://www.scibew-neca.org/html/supplement.pdf and https://norcalaborers.org/assets/FormsPublication/Nov1SOB-ENG.pdf (accessed 2026-10-22, via search results)
- SIIA 2025 captive survey: medical stop-loss captives were the fastest-growing area of activity, cited by 62% of respondents, for the third consecutive year — https://www.siia.org/i4a/doclibrary/getfile.cfm?doc_id=89 (accessed 2026-10-22, via search result)
- Segal Health Plan Cost Trend Survey: the 2026 edition (September 9, 2025) projected a median 9% medical trend and 11% prescription drug trend, with respondents being insurers, MCOs, PBMs and TPAs (via search result); the 2027 edition (Segal release of July 23, 2026, headlined "Segal Survey Projects Health Plan Cost Trend to Reach 15-Year Historic Highs") expects the median medical trend to reach 9.9% and prescription drug trend 11.5%, with trends "approaching their highest levels in 15 years" — https://www.segalco.com/consulting-insights/segal-survey-projects-health-plan-cost-trend-to-reach-15-year-historic-highs/ (accessed 2026-10-23) and https://www.segalco.com/consulting-insights/2026-health-plan-cost-trend-survey/ (accessed 2026-10-22, via search result)
- Lexipol: some risk pools offer premium discounts to jurisdictions that subscribe to its policy manuals, and Lexipol markets discounts through partnerships with risk pools; its law enforcement manual runs to ~150 policies built on federal and state law and case law and is updated for legislation and case law (Eagly and Schwartz, Texas Law Review, 2018; Lexipol marketing; city contract documents) — https://texaslawreview.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/04/Eagly.pdf and https://www.lexipol.com/?p=88 (accessed 2026-10-22, via search results)

- ~$20–30B a year of contributions to US public entity risk pools across property, liability, workers' compensation and health lines — recalled order of magnitude, no current national total found
- ~$15B a year of non-claim spend (administration, claims handling, consulting, actuarial, audit, reinsurance brokerage and systems) across self-insured funds and pools, used as the parent denominator for niche shares — derived from the two estimates above at ~8–20% expense ratios

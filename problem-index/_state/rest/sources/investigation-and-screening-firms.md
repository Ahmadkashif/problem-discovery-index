# Sources — Investigation & Background Screening Firms

## Checked
- NAICS 561611 (the 2022 code is titled "Investigation and Personal Background Check Services"; the SUSB table still carries the 2017 title "Investigation Services") in 2022: 3,767 employer firms, 4,085 establishments, 43,409 employees, $2,476.6M annual payroll and $6,878.7M receipts (~$6.9B); 2,806 firms with fewer than 5 employees took $658.1M; 3,497 firms with fewer than 20 employees took $1,446.7M; 192 firms with 20–99 employees took $1,056.9M; 46 firms with 100–499 employees took $1,186.4M; 32 firms with 500+ employees ran 274 establishments, employed 16,840 people and took $3,188.6M (~46% of receipts); firms with fewer than ten employees (the under-5 and 5–9 bands summed) number 3,249 and took ~$1,034M (~$1.0B, ~15%) — Census Bureau, Statistics of US Businesses 2022, https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/susb/tables/2022/us_state_6digitnaics_2022.xlsx (accessed 2026-10-23).

- On 9 May 2025 the CFPB withdrew dozens of guidance documents, including the 2024 advisory opinion "Fair Credit Reporting; Background Screening" (89 FR 4171, on sealed and expunged records, missing dispositions and duplicative entries) and the 2021 "Name-Only Matching Procedures" opinion (86 FR 62468) — https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/cfpb-s-withdrawal-of-interpretive-9264859 (accessed 2026-10-23); background on the January 2024 opinion: https://www.consumerfinancemonitor.com/2024/01/16/cfpb-issues-two-new-fcra-advisory-opinions-on-background-screening-reports-and-disclosure-of-credit-files-to-consumers/ (via search summary).
- FCRA §613 (15 U.S.C. §1681k(a)): a CRA furnishing adverse public-record information for employment purposes must either notify the consumer at the time of reporting or "maintain strict procedures designed to insure" the information is complete and up to date — https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681k (accessed 2026-10-23).
- FCRA §605 (15 U.S.C. §1681c): civil suits, judgments and arrest records older than seven years, and other adverse items other than "records of convictions of crimes", may not be reported; the limits do not apply to employment at an annual salary of $75,000 or more — https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681c (accessed 2026-10-23).
- All of Us or None – Riverside Chapter v. - A typical county criminal search returns in 1–3 business days, with slow-access courts taking a week or more — recalled industry norms; LA's delays are the checked example.

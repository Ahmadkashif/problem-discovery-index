# Sources — Recording Studios

## Checked
- NAICS 2022 512240 Sound Recording Studios: establishments providing the facilities and technical expertise for sound recording in a studio, including audio production and postproduction services to produce master recordings, and possibly audio services for film, television and video; index entries are audio recording postproduction services, audio recording restoration services, recording studios operating on a contract or fee basis, and sound recording studios (except integrated record companies) — https://www.naics.com/naics-code-description/?v=2022&code=512240 (accessed 2026-10-22)
- NAICS 2022 512290 Other Sound Recording Industries: sound recording services except record production, distribution, music publishing and studio recording; index entries include audio recording of meetings or conferences, recorded radio show producers and radio program recording production (except independent producers), recording books on tape or disc (except publishers), recording seminars and conferences, stock music and other audio services, and stock sound libraries; cross-references send studio facilities to 512240, record production and distribution to 512250 and mass duplication to 334610 — https://www.naics.com/naics-code-description/?v=2022&code=512290 (accessed 2026-10-22)
- 512240 in the 2022 Economic Census: 2,057 employer firms, ~$1.577B receipts, top 4 firms 25.7% of receipts, top 8 31.1%, top 20 39.7%, top 50 51.1%; County Business Patterns 2023: 2,131 establishments, 6,596 paid employees, ~$480.7M annual payroll; the page also cites an IBISWorld estimate of ~22,000 studios and ~$1.7B in 2026 revenue including nonemployers (third-party page citing Census tables) — https://www.histometrics.com/primers/512240 and https://vantainsights.com/industry/512240-sound-recording-studios (accessed 2026-10-22)
- 512290 in the 2022 Economic Census: 468 employer firms, ~$889.3M receipts, top 4 firms 57.6% of receipts, top 8 65.4%, top 20 76.2%, top 50 86.2%; County Business Patterns 2023: 733 establishments, 2,605 paid employees, ~$205.9M annual payroll (third-party page citing Census tables) — https://www.histometrics.com/primers/512290 (accessed 2026-10-22)
- Service Annual Survey via FRED: total revenue of 512240 employer firms $1,423M in 2022 (from $1,133M in 2020 and $922M in 2017); of which "studio recording" as a revenue source $662M in 2022; total revenue of 51229 employer firms $748M in 2022 (from $519M in 2020) — https://fred.stlouisfed.org/data/REVEF51224ALLEST and https://fred.stlouisfed.org/data/REVSRGEF51224ALLEST and https://fred.stlouisfed.org/data/REVEF51229ALLEST (accessed 2026-10-22)
- Census "Stats for Stories" (Grammy Awards, February 2023): sound recording studio establishments rose from 1,840 (2017) to 1,955 (2020); employees 5,112 (2017) to 5,441 (2020) — https://census.gov/library/stories/2023/02/grammy-awards-statistics-on-the-recording-industry.html (accessed 2026-10-22)
- BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook: 15,000 sound engineering technicians in 2025, median pay $73,130 (May 2025), projected to fall 3% (-500 jobs) to 14,600 by 2035; 87,600 audio and video technicians, median $58,100; 12% of broadcast, sound and video technicians work in motion picture and sound recording industries and 11% are self-employed; BLS pay figures exclude the self-employed — https://www.bls.gov/ooh/media-and-communication/broadcast-and-sound-engineering-technicians.htm (accessed 2026-10-22)
- Audio Publishers Association: US audiobook sales $2.22B in 2024 (+13%); $2.43B in 2025 (+9%); more than 750,000 active titles in 2025 (+43%); Audio First revenue $136M (from $91.1M); AI-narrated sales 0.03% of 2025 revenue; 16% of listeners have heard an AI-voiced audiobook and willingness to try fell from 70% to 61%; sales survey run by Toluna, consumer survey by Edison Research at SSRS — https://www.infodocket.com/2026/06/05/audio-publishers-association-reports-audiobook-sales-jump-9-to-2-43-billion/ and https://www.infodocket.com/2025/06/02/audiobook-sales-grow-to-2-22-billion-dollars-according-to-audio-publishers-association-apa-annual-survey-data/ (accessed 2026-10-22)
- Audible announced AI narration for select publishers on 13 May 2025, with an end-to-end managed service or self-service path, 100+ AI voices in English, Spanish, French and Italian, and AI translation in beta; PANA (Professional Audiobook Narrators Association) and narrators objected — https://techcrunch.com/2025/05/13/audible-is-expanding-its-ai-narrated-audiobook-library/ and https://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/print/20250519/97756-audible-expands-catalog-with-ai-narration-and-translation-services.html (accessed 2026-10-22, via search-result extracts)
- ACX audio submission requirements: each file between -23 dB and -18 dB RMS, peaks no higher than -3 dB, noise floor no higher than -60 dB RMS, 192 kbps or higher 44.1 kHz constant-bit-rate MP3, consistent channel format — https://help.acx.com/s/article/acx-audio-submission-requirements (accessed 2026-10-22, via search-result extract)
- Per-finished-hour (PFH) narration: Backstage says the SAG-AFTRA minimum on ACX is $250 PFH and suggests ~$200 PFH for beginners; new narrators take ~10 hours to complete one finished hour and experienced ones ~6; a budgeting guide lists audio editing and mastering at $90–150 PFH and proofing at $30–75 PFH (trade and vendor sources; they conflict) — https://www.backstage.com/magazine/article/pfh-audiobook-rates-explained-76681/ and https://www.travsonic.com/the-complete-guide-to-audiobook-production-budgeting/ (accessed 2026-10-22, via search-result extracts)
- Pozotron sells an AI proofing tool that matches a script against narration and flags missed words, added words, misreads and long pauses, with proofers making final calls; vendor claims a 10-hour book scanned in ~20 minutes; a separate guide estimates ~1.5 hours of manual proofing per finished hour — https://pozotron.com/proofing and https://blog.pozotron.com/a-realistic-audiobook-production-timeline (accessed 2026-10-22, vendor sources)
- Studio rates: a consumer pricing guide puts most independent and mid-level studios at $100–200 an hour, home and project studios at $25–75, and top facilities at $500–2,500+; SoundBetter listings show mixing at ~$75–400 per song and mastering at ~$70–100 per song, with revision terms varying from "free tweaks within reason" to five revisions (directory and marketplace listings, undated) — https://fash.com/costs/recording-studio-rates and https://www.soundbetter.com/profiles/557523-dankrochmal (accessed 2026-10-22, via search-result extracts)
- Mix feedback: engineer-facing guides describe notes such as "warmer", "punchier" or "the chorus feels small" as emotional reactions the engineer must translate into concrete moves, recommend timestamps, reference tracks and batched notes per round, and note that each wrong guess costs a revision round — https://violetrecording.com/how-to-handle-mix-revisions/ and https://notetracks.com/blog/mix-revision-notes (accessed 2026-10-22, via search-result extracts)
- Analog recall: forum engineers report about an hour to write down console and outboard settings and 15–20 minutes to recall a complex mix, photos of every unit filed per song, and that consoles with memory still need every changed control reset by hand — https://gearspace.com/threads/recalling-a-mix.65668/ and https://gearspace.com/threads/console-recall-sheets.152379/ (accessed 2026-10-22, via search-result extracts)
- SoundExchange distributed $253.2M in Q1 2025 and $241.5M in Q2 2025 ($494.7M first half); it pays producers, mixers and engineers only when a featured artist (direct registrant) files a Letter of Direction, which sends a percentage of the artist's share for named recordings and is not retroactive unless stated; the AMP Act (Music Modernization Act, 2018) codified the letter-of-direction process — https://www.soundexchange.com/faq/i-am-a-producer-mixer-or-engineer-for-several-big-artists-how-do-i-claim-royalties-for-these-recordings/ and https://www.copyright.gov/music-modernization/amp/ (accessed 2026-10-22)
- Jaxsta, an Australian official music credits database founded in 2015 that claimed 100M+ credits including 150,000 producers and 100,000 engineers at its 2019 launch, closed in November 2025 saying "the current model does not scale" — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaxsta (accessed 2026-10-22)
- Apple Music gives up to a 10% boost on recorded-royalty payments for tracks available in Spatial Audio (Dolby Atmos) from 2024, based on availability rather than listening; older catalogue remixes qualify; industry estimates put an Atmos mix at roughly $1,000 per track; indie labels objected that the bonus comes out of the shared pool — https://musictech.com/news/music/apple-music-higher-royalties-spatial-audio and https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/2024/02/05/apple-music-spatial-audio-royalties-policy-criticism/ (accessed 2026-10-22, via search-result extracts)
- Spotify normalises playback to -14 dB LUFS (ITU 1770; Loud -11, Quiet -19 for Premium) and recommends true peak below -1 dBTP, or below -2 dBTP for masters louder than -14 LUFS — https://support.spotify.com/us/artists/article/loudness-normalization/ (accessed 2026-10-22)
- LANDR sells a single mastered WAV for ~$9.99 and subscriptions from ~$8–16 a month (sources conflict); iZotope Ozone 12 (Native Instruments) added Stem EQ, Bass Control and Unlimiter and an updated Master Assistant with LUFS targets, from ~$55; RoEx Automix, a Queen Mary University of London spin-out, mixes uploaded labelled stems and integrated with Ableton Live in January 2025 — https://freshonthenet.co.uk/2025/11/mastering-on-a-budget-what-you-get-for-0-10-and-100/ and https://www.prosoundweb.com/izotope-announces-new-ozone-12-mastering-suite/ and https://www.musicweek.com/digital/read/tech-start-up-roex-unveils-ai-powered-music-mixing-tool-automix/088623 (accessed 2026-10-22, via search-result extracts)
- A February 2020 fire destroyed Apollo/Transco's Banning, California plant, by far the larger of the world's two lacquer master disc makers and a supplier of cutting styli; Japan's MDC was left as the only lacquer disc factory and said it could not take new customers — https://www.thevinylfactory.com/news/apollo-transco-vinyl-lacquer-fire and https://djmag.com/news/global-vinyl-production-threatened-after-fire-major-us-plant (accessed 2026-10-22, via search-result extracts)
- IASA's technical committee estimated in 2011 that a critical share of tape recordings would become inaccessible within ~15 years even in good storage, and its TC-03 rationale puts the digitisation window at ~10–15 years; archive bodies cite an international consensus that magnetic media not digitised by 2025 will likely be lost, a date others dispute (one report cites a 2022 Library of Congress study suggesting well-stored polyester tape may last far longer); TC-04 is IASA's guideline for producing and preserving digital audio objects — https://iasa-web.org/node/3824 and https://iasa-web.org/node/1136 (accessed 2026-10-22, via search-result extracts)
- Tennessee's ELVIS Act took effect on 1 July 2024, extending the state's Personal Rights Protection Act to voice and to AI imitation, with civil and criminal enforcement; California AB 2602 (2024) makes contract provisions for digital replicas unenforceable unless they describe intended uses with reasonable specificity and the performer had counsel or union representation, and AB 1836 covers deceased performers — https://www.americanbar.org/groups/litigation/resources/newsletters/intellectual-property/elvis-act-new-law-addresses-impact-ai-music-industry/ and https://www.insideglobaltech.com/2024/09/11/california-passes-digital-replica-legislation-as-congress-considers-federal-approach/ (accessed 2026-10-22, via search-result extracts)
- Pirate Studios raised $20M led by Talis Capital in November 2018 to expand self-service, keycode-access rehearsal and recording studios across the UK, Germany and the US; it then ran ~350 studios in 21 locations — https://techcrunch.com/2018/11/14/pirate-studios/ (accessed 2026-10-22)
- Epidemic Sound reported 2024 revenue of SEK 1.921B (+29%); its 2025 annual report cites 500+ employees and a catalogue of 250,000+ tracks and sound effects heard 3B+ times a day — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epidemic_Sound and https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/oscar-hoglund-talks-epidemic-sounds-revenue-growth-and-vast-reach-we-get-roughly-3bn-daily-views-thats-20-super-bowls-per-day-365-days-per-year (accessed 2026-10-22, via search-result extracts)
- The AFM & SAG-AFTRA Intellectual Property Rights Distribution Fund collects and distributes performance royalties to non-featured (backing) musicians and vocalists, union or not; it keeps an unclaimed-royalties list of credited performers it cannot reach and investigates claims from uncredited performers; it reported a record distribution of over $70M on 30 April 2021 and over $500M since inception; Kobalt, Wixen, Sony and others alleged flawed identification of participants held back up to ~$25M — https://local802afm.org/allegro/articles/afm-sag-aftra-fund and https://www.musicconnection.com/industry-profile-afm-sag-aftra-fund/ (accessed 2026-10-22, via search-result extracts)
- SCAPR (Societies' Council for the Collective Management of Performers' Rights) public report 2024: 61 member organisations from 45 countries; performers' rights collected by members reached €955M in 2023; members distributed €633M; €191M exchanged within the membership under bilateral agreements; members represent an estimated 588,000+ performers; shared tools are the International Performers Database (IPD, with the International Performer Number) and the Virtual Recording Database (VRDB); the website gives 60 CMOs in 45 countries — https://www.scapr.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/SCAPR_PUBLIC-REPORT_2024.pdf and https://scapr.org/about-us (accessed 2026-10-22)
- IFPI Global Music Report 2026: performance-rights revenue for recorded music grew 0.3% in 2025 to ~$2.9B, 9.3% of global recorded-music revenue (after +5.3% in 2024) — https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/2026/03/18/global-music-industry-revenue-2025/ and https://diymusician.cdbaby.com/music-news/ifpi-global-music-report-2026/ (accessed 2026-10-22, via search-result extracts)
- Directive 2014/26/EU, Article 13: collective management organisations must distribute amounts due no later than nine months from the end of the financial year of collection, must take measures to trace rightholders, and amounts still undistributable three years after the end of the collection year are deemed non-distributable; the directive governs collective management of copyright and related rights — https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eudr/2014/26/article/13 (accessed 2026-10-22, retained text as at end of the transition period)
- 17 U.S.C. §114(g)(2): of statutory licence receipts distributed by the designated collective, 50% to the copyright owner, 2.5% to an escrow account for nonfeatured musicians (independent administrator jointly appointed with the AFM), 2.5% for nonfeatured vocalists (jointly appointed with AFTRA, now SAG-AFTRA) and 45% to featured artists; §114(g)(5) requires the collective to accept letters of direction to producers, mixers and sound engineers — https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/114 (accessed 2026-10-22)
- When Audible announced AI narration (May 2025), its CEO estimated that audiobooks exist for somewhere between 2% and 5% of available books and framed AI narration as a way to expand the catalogue — https://techcrunch.com/2025/05/13/audible-is-expanding-its-ai-narrated-audiobook-library/ (accessed 2026-10-22, via search-result extract)
- The APA's 2026 release says the number of AI-narrated audiobooks published rose in 2025, without a count — https://www.infodocket.com/2026/06/05/audio-publishers-association-reports-audiobook-sales-jump-9-to-2-43-billion/ (accessed 2026-10-22)

# Sources — Wireless Retail Agents

## Checked
- Agents for wireless telecommunications services (NAICS 517122) had 4,191 firms, 13,804 establishments, ~$20.4B in receipts ($20,396,505K), ~$3.91B annual payroll and 83,434 employees in 2022; the parent 51712 (resellers plus agents) had 5,134 firms and ~$35.1B, and telecom resellers (517121) 955 firms and ~$14.7B — 2022 Economic Census, Sector 51 basic table EC2251BASIC (US rows 51712, 517121, 517122), https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/economic-census/data/2022/sector51/EC2251BASIC.zip (accessed 2026-10-22). Derived: ~3.3 establishments per firm, ~6 employees and ~$1.5M receipts per establishment. A third-party page (fairmarketvalue.com) reports "$20M" for the code, which is the same Census figure misread in thousands.
- Victra calls itself the nation's largest authorized Verizon retailer and "Verizon's largest exclusive premium retailer", with more than 1,800 stores in all 50 states, close to 7,000 team members and approximately $3B in annual revenue (company figures) — Victra press release, 18 February 2026, https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/victra-marks-30-years-of-connecting-technology-to-life-302691933.html (accessed 2026-10-22). An older Fierce Network article put Round Room at 1,241 stores, Cellular Sales near 800 and Russell Cellular ~720 — https://www.fierce-network.com/wireless/victra-becomes-verizons-largest-authorized-retailer (search-result excerpt; dated ~2021, not current).
- T-Mobile "began closing certain dealer and corporate owned stores in the second quarter of 2026" as part of digital initiatives and a partial shift to large-format experience stores, and recorded a pre-tax charge of $108 million for these retail initiatives (contract termination, severance, lease costs), with further restructuring "dependent on negotiations with certain counterparties" — T-Mobile Form 10-Q for the quarter ended June 30, 2026, Note 15, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001283699/000128369926000101/tmus-20260630.htm (accessed 2026-10-22)
- Wireless Vision, backed by Gemspring Capital, describes itself as the largest authorized T-Mobile retailer with over 450 locations; Debtwire reported it may have to shrink substantially and exit leases after T-Mobile's retail restructuring — https://ionanalytics.com/insights/debtwire/t-mobile-retail-restructuring-leaves-wireless-vision-other-dealers-hanging/ (search-result excerpt, accessed 2026-10-22)
- Verizon announced in July 2026 that it would sell 274 company-owned stores to about six operators who will run them as authorized retailers, effective August 16, 2026, leaving about 1,000 corporate-owned stores and affecting ~3,000 roles; a November 2025 transfer preceded it — https://www.fierce-network.com/wireless/verizon-sell-274-stores-cut-500-corporate-jobs ; https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-16/verizon-cuts-store-workers-in-reorganization-strategy ; https://qz.com/verizon-store-sales-layoffs-3000-jobs-071626 (search-result excerpts; the pages returned 403 on fetch, accessed 2026-10-22)
- Wave7 Research (Jeff Moore): Verizon, T-Mobile and AT&T together had "just under 17,000" outlets in 2026, down ~400 since spring 2025; Metro by T-Mobile, Cricket, Boost and Total Wireless had more than 16,000 stores combined by mid-2026; Total Wireless more than 2,300 stores; Boost sells Starlink through 2,000 of its stores; Costco kiosks at 647 locations and T-Mobile kiosks at 602 Sam's Clubs — RCR Wireless Analyst Angle, 19 October 2026, https://rcrwireless.com/20261019/analyst-angle/us-telecom-retail-holds-up-where-might-starlink-fit-analyst-angle (accessed 2026-10-22)
- Wave7 Research estimated more than 7,000 multi-carrier prepaid dealer stores; postpaid carrier stores ~17,600 in mid-2024 (18,500 in mid-2023); Costco kiosks operated by AT&T or T-Mobile; most Walmart Supercenters have third-party wireless reps with quotas — RCR Wireless Analyst Angle, 9 December 2024, https://rcrwireless.com/20241209/analyst-angle/telecom-retail (accessed 2026-10-22)
- Wave7's quarterly prepaid dealer survey interviewed 30 independent multi-carrier prepaid dealers in November–December 2025; Ultra Mobile edged Simple Mobile, which had won 42 of 44 editions since 1Q15; one dealer said Boost "has held back commissions" — https://bestmvno.com/featured/ultra-mobile-wins-4q25-dealer-survey/ (accessed 2026-10-22)
- GameStop's Spring Mobile operated 1,329 AT&T branded stores at February 3, 2018; it recognised commission revenue on activation "net of an allowance for chargebacks from AT&T for estimated customer cancellations"; AT&T could terminate the relationship "upon a short notice period"; it impaired Spring Mobile's AT&T dealer agreements by $328.8M to a fair value of $77.0M after "changes made by AT&T to the compensation structure in 2017" and a longer upgrade cycle — GameStop FY2017 Form 10-K, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1326380/000132638018000033/a10k-fy17q4.htm (accessed 2026-10-22)
- GameStop sold Spring Mobile to Prime Communications for $700M, completed January 16, 2019 (~1,284 AT&T stores), which reports said made Prime the largest AT&T authorized dealer; Prime owned ~600 AT&T stores before the deal — https://investor.gamestop.com/news-releases/news-details/2019/GameStop-Completes-Sale-of-Spring-Mobile-Division-01-16-2019/default.aspx ; https://www.retail-insight-network.com/news/gamestop-spring-mobile-prime/ (search-result excerpts, accessed 2026-10-22)
- In Deleon v. Verizon Wireless (Cal. Ct. App. 2012), Verizon's own retail sales representatives received advanced commissions that were not earned until the end of a chargeback period "which ranged from 120 days to one year depending on the service being sold"; the court upheld the chargebacks — Seyfarth Shaw client alert, 31 July 2012, https://www.seyfarth.com/a/web/6609/3G9Bq7/chargebacksofadvancedcommissionsremainvalidincaliforniadespitenovellegalargument.pdf (accessed 2026-10-22)
- A sample dealer agreement defines a 180-day chargeback period from activation, extended by suspension days, with chargebacks netted against amounts owed to the dealer; dealer-side explainers list cancellation inside the window, a missed first payment or recharge, a fraud or duplicate flag, and auto-deactivation of unused lines as triggers, and say spiffs "can vanish with a week of notice" — https://www.lawinsider.com/dictionary/chargeback-period ; https://www.wirelessdealergroup.com/post/residuals-chargebacks-spiffs-how-telecom-commissions-work (accessed 2026-10-22). These are a template and a dealer blog, not a named carrier's current terms.
- A 2024 report on T-Mobile third-party retailers described sources at Arch Telecom saying stores fight chargebacks of several thousand dollars a month and a 27% activation-conversion minimum on all store traffic — https://tmo.report/2024/07/look-at-the-ways-these-shady-t-mobile-stores-were-treating-customers-and-employees/ (anonymous sources, single outlet; accessed 2026-10-22)
- iQmetrix: RQ powered over 20,000 wireless retail stores in the US and Canada in 2019 and "$15.5 billion wireless sales" in 2020 (company milestones); ReconcilePro matches POS sales to carrier commission files with configurable rules and claims a 98% reconciliation rate "when used consistently" with Carrier Activation — https://www.iqmetrix.com/company/about-iqmetrix ; https://www.iqmetrix.com/interconnected-commerce/operate/reconciliation-and-commissions (accessed 2026-10-22)
- An HG Insights sample list of RQ users includes Cricket Wireless, Freedom Mobile, Fido and a Verizon authorized retailer; a third-party estimate puts RQ pricing at ~$2,500 a month for a 5-store retailer to over $7,000 for 50+ stores — https://discovery.hginsights.com/product/rq-retail ; https://www.capterra.com/p/15866/RQ/ (search-result excerpts, accessed 2026-10-22)
- Third-party reconciliation services say most POS commission reconciliation is incomplete and match chargebacks on MDN, IMEI, ESN or SIM; Commission Tracker imports Excel statements but PDF statements are keyed by hand; QCommission and Modero sell telecom commission reconciliation — https://www.rkinfotec.com/wirelessretailsolution/monthly-service-provider-commission.html ; https://help.commission-tracker.com/new_reconcile_carrier_statement/ ; https://www.qcommission.com/industries/industries-four/telecom-reseller-overview.html ; https://mymodero.com/product/commission-management/ (vendor pages, accessed 2026-10-22)
- FCC SIM-swap and port-out fraud order (WC Docket 21-341, adopted November 2023, published December 8, 2023) requires wireless providers to train employees on SIM-swap and port-out fraud and to stop customer-facing employees reaching CPNI before the customer is authenticated; on July 5, 2024 the Wireline Competition Bureau waived compliance until OMB review is complete and a Federal Register notice announces the compliance date — FCC DA 24-649, https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DA-24-649A1.pdf (accessed 2026-10-22). Whether that notice has since issued could not be confirmed.
- The FCC's CPNI rules let a carrier (or its agent) disclose CPNI at a retail location only after the customer presents a valid photo ID matching the account information, a rule in the same form since at least 2009 — 47 CFR 64.2010(d), https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/47/64.2010 ; https://hallikainen.org/org/FCC/FccRules/2015/64/2010 (accessed 2026-10-22)
- SIM-swap order status as written in the vault, per the batch review's standard sentence: the November 2023 order requires authentication before a SIM change or port-out and immediate notice; DA 24-649 (July 2024) deferred compliance with all its rules until OMB approval and a Federal Register compliance-date notice; no such notice had appeared by October 2026 (my own searches found none; see the DA 24-649 entry above).
- Store-employee fraud cases: a former AT&T store employee in Oregon was sentenced to 16 months for SIM-swapping customers for a hacker; the Manhattan DA indicted a SIM-swap ring that included AT&T and T-Mobile retail employees; at Cellular Sales (a Verizon authorized retailer) a regional director noticed irregular phone transfers between two stores, leading to charges that an employee diverted 735 phones worth nearly $600K; in July 2026 a former T-Mobile retailer employee in Florida was charged after opening unauthorized lines on customer accounts — https://www.fbi.gov/contact-us/field-offices/losangeles/news/oregon-man-sentenced-to-16-months-in-federal-prison-for-abusing-his-role-at-mobile-phone-store-to-give-customers-info-to-criminals ; https://manhattanda.org/d-a-bragg-announces-indictment-of-sim-swapping-id-theft-ring-including-att-and-t-mobile-employees/ ; https://www.wftv.com/news/local/employee-stole-735-cellphones-worth-nearly-600k-casselberry-police-say/750497949/ ; https://www.local10.com/news/local/2026/07/08/ex-cellphone-store-employee-facing-serious-charges-over-alleged-theft-fraud-scheme/ (search-result excerpts, accessed 2026-10-22)
- California Regulation 1585 taxes a phone sold with a service contract on its unbundled sales price; CDTFA Publication 120's example taxes a $25 phone sold with a one-year activation on the $110 price charged without one; the Court of Appeal upheld the regulation in Bekkerman v. CDTFA (2024) — https://cdtfa.ca.gov/lawguides/vol1/sutr/1585.html ; https://cdtfa.ca.gov/formspubs/pub120/examples.htm ; https://www.stateandlocaltax.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/525/2024/03/Bekkerman-v-CDTFA-2024.pdf (search-result excerpts, accessed 2026-10-22). One commentary reports the California Supreme Court let the ruling stand; not confirmed from a primary source.
- Prepaid wireless 911 charges are collected by the seller per retail transaction and remitted on state schedules that differ: $0.55 flat in Virginia (dealer keeps 5% if timely), $1.95 in Pennsylvania (1.5% discount), a percentage of the sale in Kansas, 2% of gross receipts in South Dakota, quarterly filing in Delaware (rate rising October 1, 2026 under HB 468), Georgia filings due the 20th with 3% vendor's compensation, North Carolina monthly or semiannual — https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title56/chapter15/section56-484.17:1/ ; https://911.georgia.gov/pages/about-911-fee-remittance ; https://revenue.delaware.gov/business-tax-forms/e-911-prepaid-wireless-surcharge/ ; https://ksrevenue.gov/e911.html ; https://dor.sd.gov/businesses/taxes/telecommunication-emergency-surcharges-fees/ ; https://www.pa.gov/agencies/revenue/resources/tax-types-and-information/sales-use-and-hotel-occupancy-tax/e-911-information (search-result excerpts, accessed 2026-10-22)
- NWIDA (National Wireless Independent Dealer Association) says it supports 40,000+ independent wireless sales and repair businesses, sells member discounts, lobbies on right to repair, and stated in 2020 that Metro dealers "are NOT franchises" and lack franchise-law protection; NWIDA estimated 250–750 stores were affected when Metro by T-Mobile ended non-exclusive dealers in 2020, against Wave7's estimate of 9K+ exclusive Metro doors; T-CETRA joined NWIDA in 2020 — https://nwida.org/ ; https://bestmvno.com/metropcs/metro-by-t-mobile-terminates-non-exclusive-dealers-online-sales/ ; https://nwida.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/08/TCETRA-NWIDA-Press-Release_8.13.20.pdf (accessed 2026-10-22)

# Sources — Court Reporting Firms

## Checked
- NAICS 561492 (Court reporting and stenotype services) in 2022: 2,989 employer firms, 3,161 establishments, receipts $3,070,371K (~$3.07B), annual payroll $742,558K (~$743M), 12,874 employees. State rows: California $610.1M (457 firms), Florida $375.2M (542), Texas $303.4M (182), New Jersey $258.7M (69), New York $203.4M (151), Illinois $179.9M (149), Pennsylvania $170.7M (59) (2022 Economic Census, sector 56 basic file, US and state rows) — https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/economic-census/data/2022/sector56/EC2256BASIC.zip (accessed 2026-10-23)
- NAICS 561492 in 2023: 3,195 employer establishments, 12,440 employees, annual payroll ~$800.7M; 2,843 establishments (89%) have fewer than five employees, 171 have 5-9, 88 have 10-19, 52 have 20-49, 23 have 50-99, 13 have 100-249, 4 have 250-499, none have 500 or more (County Business Patterns 2023, US file) — https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/cbp/datasets/2023/cbp23us.zip (accessed 2026-10-23)
- Court reporters and simultaneous captioners: about 19,900 jobs in 2025; median pay $72,420 (May 2025); employment projected to show little or no change (0%) 2025-2035 with ~1,800 openings a year; 33% self-employed, 24% local government, 20% state government, 19% business support services; many states require a licence; CART demand expected to grow while AI transcription "is expected to dampen employment demand" (BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook) — https://www.bls.gov/ooh/legal/court-reporters.htm (accessed 2026-10-23)
- Ducker Worldwide "Court Reporting Industry Outlook Report" for NCRA (2013-14): ~32,000 stenographic court reporters in 2013; stenographers 96%, voice writers 4%; freelance 72%, officials 28%; fewer than 1,000 work primarily as captioners; median age 51 against 42 for all occupations, 70% aged 46 or older; ~2,500 students enrolled; roughly half of states require qualification exams; 5,000-5,500 retirements and 1,400-1,500 new entrants forecast over five years; 2018 supply 27,700 against demand 33,200, a gap of ~5,500; more than 45 states use some digital recording, with high use in Alaska, Florida, Kentucky, Michigan, Oregon and Utah and low use in California, New York and Texas — https://vernoncourtreporters.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Ducker-report.pdf (accessed 2026-10-23; PDF text extracted locally)
- AAERT-sponsored "Court Reporting Industry 2025 Trends Report" (The Brand Consultancy): as few as ~23,000 stenographers, "up to a 21% drop"; estimated from NCRA annual school reports, program enrollment fell from 3,083 (2015) to 790 (2024), an estimated 74% drop, and schools from 38 to 22, an estimated 42% drop; survey of 500+ end users: stenographers used in 82% of cases, 76% report scheduling difficulties and 55% increased costs from the shortage; claims state certification pass rates are "typically less than 10%" after two years of school and digital reporters can train "in as little as a month" (advocacy source for digital reporting) — https://aaert.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/2025-Court-Reporting-Industry-Trends-Report.pdf (accessed 2026-10-23; PDF text extracted locally)
- Court Reporters Board of California, meeting minutes 9 May 2025: first skills exam since the passing standard was reduced to 95% accuracy; 105 candidates (42 voice writers), 43 first-time candidates (31 voice writers), first-time pass rate 83.7% overall and 90% for voice writers; 219 voice writers licensed; 72 reporters licensed through NVRA reciprocity since 1 January 2025; enrollment at recognised programs up 286% since 2017, voice-writer licensing began late 2022, first-time test takers up 100% since 2021; AB 711 (Chen) would require notice when one side hires a reporter, to stop several reporters being booked for one hearing; AB 1189 (Lackey) to license digital recorders and legal transcriptionists would not move that year; the Board clarified that its regulation on deposition summaries (16 CCR §2474) does not prohibit AI-generated summaries, with members raising neutrality (same product to both sides), privacy and model-training concerns — https://courtreportersboard.ca.gov/about-us/20250509_minutes.pdf (accessed 2026-10-23; PDF text extracted locally)
- California's Transcript Reimbursement Fund, administered by the Court Reporters Board, pays transcript costs for indigent litigants in civil matters, both self-represented and represented pro bono; in fiscal 2024/25 to May 2025 it had paid more than $87,000 for pro bono cases and over $48,000 for self-represented applicants, with ~$60,000 more provisionally approved (same minutes) — https://courtreportersboard.ca.gov/about-us/20250509_minutes.pdf (accessed 2026-10-23)
- The skills-test passing standard change from 97.5% to 95% took effect 1 January 2025 under the Board's sunset bill AB 3252 (CalDRA notice, secondary; the 95% standard itself is confirmed in the minutes above) — https://www.caldra.org (accessed 2026-10-23, via search result)
- California AB 1189 (2025-26): would have created Court Reporters Board certification for digital reporters and legal transcriptionists and allowed depositions to be recorded by a "certified digital recorder"; status Failed, filed with the Chief Clerk under Joint Rule 56 on 2 February 2026 — https://calmatters.digitaldemocracy.org/bills/ca_202520260ab1189 (accessed 2026-10-23)
- Texas: SB 1538 (89th Legislature) directs the Office of Court Administration to study digital court reporting (use, cost, accuracy, other states) with a report due by 1 October 2026; a Texas A&M Public Policy Research Institute final report dated 1 October 2026 cites HB 16 (89th, 2nd Called Session) as its authority (bill text and report via search results; capitol.texas.gov did not resolve, final passage of SB 1538 not confirmed) — https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/89R/billtext/html/SB01538E.htm and https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1463416/20261001-dcr-study-final-report.pdf (accessed 2026-10-23, via search results)
- Texas A&M PPRI project page: over 20 years the number of Certified Shorthand Reporters in Texas fell 14% while trial court benches rose by more than 14%; Bexar County postponed proceedings despite offering salaries exceeding $10,000 a month; nearly half of current CSRs were first certified 30 or more years ago — https://ppri.tamu.edu/portfolio-items/digitalcourtreportingtx (accessed 2026-10-23)
- Federal maximum transcript rates per page set by the Judicial Conference: ordinary (30-day) $4.40, 14-day $5.10, expedited (7-day) $5.85, 3-day $6.55, daily $7.30, hourly $8.70; realtime $3.70 for one feed, $2.55 for two to four, $1.80 for five or more; first copy $1.10-1.45 — https://cand.uscourts.gov/transcripts/rates (accessed 2026-10-23); approved at the Judicial Conference's September 2022 session as roughly 10% increases in each of FY2024 and FY2025 to help courts recruit and retain reporters — https://www.txed.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/goFiles/GO%2023-07%20Adopting%20Transcript%20Rates%20for%20FYs%202024%20and%202025.pdf (accessed 2026-10-23, via search result)
- FRCP 30: the noticing party must state the recording method (30(b)(3)(A)); the officer opens with an on-the-record statement (30(b)(5)); the deponent has 30 days after notice that the transcript is available to review it (30(e)(1)); the officer must certify in writing that the witness was duly sworn (30(f)(1)); a deposition is limited to one day of seven hours unless stipulated or ordered (30(d)(1)) — https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/frcp/rule_30 (accessed 2026-10-23)
- AAA/ICDR reporting schedule with OptimaJuris: 2026 schedule lists realtime at $1.75 per page per party and an in-person surcharge of $100; the 2024 schedule lists a same-day expedite surcharge of 110%, decreasing 10% each following day; an earlier schedule listed a $295 daily appearance fee and $7.50 per page — https://www.adr.org/media/2aclggpo/2026_aaa_icdr_optimajuris_stenographic_court_reporting.pdf and https://WWW.ADR.ORG/sites/default/files/document_repository/2024_AAA_ICDR_OptimaJuris_ServiceOfferings_Stenographic_Court_Reporting.pdf (accessed 2026-10-23, via search results)
- Veritext service terms: expedited transcripts carry an additional percentage of the page rate; rough drafts may not be certified, cited or used as the certified transcript — https://www.veritext.com/?p=60726 (accessed 2026-10-23, via search result)
- Veritext Legal Solutions: founded 1997, headquartered in Livingston, NJ; acquired by Leonard Green & Partners from Pamplona Capital Management in August 2018; network of "over 11,500 independent court reporters" (2018); earlier owners Investcorp (2010) and Pamplona (2016) — https://peprofessional.com/2018/12/leonard-green-adds-veritext/ (accessed 2026-10-23)
- Court reporting M&A tracker: Veritext acquisitions include Richard Lee Reporting (Jan 2025), Carol Nygard & Associates (Mar 2025), Huney-Vaughn (May 2025), Metro Atlanta Reporters (Jun 2025), Scribe Associates and Catana Reporting (Jul 2025), plus four firms on 16 April 2024 alone; Lexitas bought TP.One (Aug 2025); Magna Legal Services bought EveryWord and Basye Santiago (2025) and combined with ERSA Court Reporters (Sept 2024, labelled Magna LS/Odyssey Investment Partners); Esquire bought Hill & Romero (Dec 2023) and TSG Reporting (Jul 2022); U.S. Legal Support bought Wendy Ward Roberts & Associates (Apr 2024) — https://www.jackimwoods.com/category/court-reporting-industry/ (accessed 2026-10-23)

- Stenograph: Case CATalyst Version 22 (April 2024) added Direct Audio recording from web-conferencing platforms and multi-channel audio for in-person jobs; Check It, built on Stenograph's Phoenix speech recognition, highlights areas of concern and suggests edits for the reporter or scopist; Eclipse, Case CATalyst and DigitalCAT named as common CAT systems — https://www.newswire.com/view/content/stenograph-announces-the-release-of-catalyst-version-22-22266655 and https://www.marketscale.com/industries/energy/stenograph-introduces-catalyst-check-it-powered-by-phoenix-a-productivity-tool-for-stenographers (accessed 2026-10-23, via search results)
- Insurer contracting: volume agreements between reporting firms and insurers give preferred rates and extras such as expedited delivery, "deposition databases" for future cases and free deposition summaries; advocates count 16-17 states restricting such arrangements or requiring disclosure (undated, conflicting counts) — https://consumerwatchdog.org/?p=6015 and https://consumerwatchdog.org/privacy/proposed-law-would-regulate-court-reporter-litigant-relationship/ (accessed 2026-10-23, via search results); a Texas bill analysis notes the rule that all parties get the same price when one receives a contract volume discount — https://lrl.texas.gov/scanned/hroBillAnalyses/75-0/HB697.PDF (accessed 2026-10-23, via search result)

## Estimates (not checked)
- ~$0.8-1.2B a year of additional receipts from nonemployer freelance reporters and captioners — BLS puts 33% of the occupation in self-employment; not checked against Census Nonemployer Statistics
- Payroll under a quarter of receipts reflects reporters paid as independent contractors — inference from the Census payroll/receipts ratio, not a published figure
- National agencies (Veritext, Esquire, U.S. Legal Support, Lexitas, Magna and peers) book ~40-50% of US deposition revenue — derived from the size distribution (17 establishments with 100+ employees) and acquisition counts; no public share data
- Niche market sizes (civil deposition coverage ~$1.8B, court and appeal transcripts ~$600M, EUOs ~$150M, administrative and arbitration hearings ~$250M, unreported family and civil hearings ~$120M, CART and live captioning ~$350M, transcript production labour ~$500M with scoping ~$250M and digital transcription ~$200M, read-and-sign and delivery ~$100M) — allocations of the ~$3.07B employer receipts plus the nonemployer estimate; they overlap and are not additive
- Stenographers write at 200-plus words a minute and NCRA's RPR skills test includes 225 wpm testimony — recalled, not re-checked
- Personal steno dictionaries of tens of thousands to 100,000+ entries; one to two hours of editing per hour on the record; scopist rates of roughly $1 a page; freelance original-page rates of ~$3.50-7.50 — trade-forum and vendor norms, recalled
- California's deposition statute requires services offered to one party to be offered to all on the same terms (CCP §2025.320); FRCP 28(c) bars a deposition officer who is a party's relative, employee or attorney or is financially interested — recalled; leginfo and Justia returned 403
- Many California courts do not provide official reporters in civil and family matters; Jameson v. Desta (Cal. 2018) requires a reporter at no cost for fee-waiver litigants on request — recalled
- Deposition cancellation and late-continuance rates (~20-30% of bookings), EUO no-show rates and CART hourly rates (~$100-200) — recalled trade norms
- Expert-testimony databanks exclude transcripts under protective orders or client restrictions at contribution — assumed from the member-sharing model, not a checked policy; the pocket's "no kill switch" call depends on it

# Sources — Greeting Card Publishers

## Checked
- Census Service Annual Survey, total revenue for Greeting Card Publishers (NAICS 511191 under the 2017 codes, now 513191), all establishments, employer firms: $3,552M (2018), $3,004M (2019), $2,931M (2020), $2,927M (2021), $2,601M (2022) (series REVEF511191ALLEST) — https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/REVEF511191ALLEST (accessed 2026-10-22). The hub's "~$2.6B" and "down from ~$3.55B in 2018" rest on this.
- County Business Patterns: 113 establishments in NAICS 511191 at the start of 2022, the same as 2021; establishments with 1–9 employees are 74% of the total (WhatTheyThink analysis of CBP; page returned 403, figures from search-result text) — https://whattheythink.com/data/126506-greeting-card-publishing-establishments20102022/ (accessed 2026-10-22, search result only)
- 2022 NAICS 513191 definition: establishments primarily engaged in publishing greeting cards, in print or electronic form, including exclusively on the Internet (search-result summary of the NAICS definition) — https://www.naics.com/naics-code-description/?v=2022&code=513191 (accessed 2026-10-22, via search result text)
- American Greetings 10-K for fiscal 2011: about 7,400 full-time and 17,400 part-time employees (~16,100 FTE) at 28 February 2011; Walmart ~15% and Target ~14% of revenue; top five customers ~42%; three of the five largest customers on scan-based trading, under which the company owns the product until the retailer sells it and is paid generally within 10–15 days of sale; seasonal cards sold with right of return; Hallmark named as principal competitor; company estimate of ~3,000 US greeting card publishers; licensed character properties Care Bears and Strawberry Shortcake; ~85% of US households buy cards — https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000005133/000095012311041637/l41683e10vk.htm (accessed 2026-10-22)
- American Greetings 10-K filings on retailer agreements: multi-year supply agreements with incentives (cash payments, credits, allowances) recorded as deferred cost assets at inception and earned as the customer buys over the term; a claim for unearned advances if a minimum purchase commitment is missed; a $12.4M special charge for conversion to scan-based trading in fiscal 2002 — https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000005133/000095015203005856/l01173ae10vk.htm and https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000005133/000095015202004592/l94460ae10vkxpdfy.pdf (accessed 2026-10-22, via search result text)
- American Greetings 2006 prospectus supplement: approximately 35% share of the North American greeting card market — https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000005133/000119312506116535/d424b5.htm (accessed 2026-10-22, via search result text)
- Elliott Investment Management closed its acquisition of a majority interest in American Greetings on 3 February 2025 (announced 20 December 2024); CD&R and the Weiss family keep a significant minority; Joe Arcuri remains CEO — https://kyodonewsprwire.jp/release/202502043742 (accessed 2026-10-22, via search result text)
- KeyBanc: July 2026 refinancing for American Greetings of a $250M revolver, $400M Term Loan B and $450M senior secured notes, describing the company as a portfolio company of Elliott and CD&R — https://www.key.com/businesses-institutions/our-transactions/deals/american-greetings.html (accessed 2026-10-22, via search result text)
- Greeting Card Association (US) data as summarised by USPS (2019): ~6.5 billion cards bought a year, $7–8B in annual retail sales; Christmas ~1.6 billion cards; women ~80% of purchases — https://news.usps.com/2019/09/26/facts-on-paper/ (accessed 2026-10-22, via search result text)
- Greeting Card Association tips for artists: flat fees generally $275–$500 with ownership typically passing to the publisher; advance of $150–$300 against a typical 4%–6% royalty; licensing grants use for a set period, use and market while the artist keeps ownership — https://www.greetingcard.org/for-new-companies/tips-for-artists/ (accessed 2026-10-22)
- UK Greeting Card Association guide: Christmas ranges launched to retailers in January, spring seasons in June/July, range development up to six months before launch — https://www.gca.cards/becoming-a-greeting-card-designer-a-guide-for-artists/ (accessed 2026-10-22, via search result text)
- Hallmark (Wikipedia): ~900 artists, designers, stylists, writers, editors and photographers; 19,000+ new or redesigned cards and related products a year; 48,000+ products at any time; ~2,700 staff at Kansas City headquarters; Gold Crown stores independently owned with some corporate stores; revenue $5.0B (2020, flagged "needs update") — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hallmark_Cards (accessed 2026-10-22)
- Card merchandiser job ads: Designer Greetings recruits part-time merchandisers as independent contractors paid a flat monthly rate per assignment to straighten racks, stock, reorder and swap seasonal cards by planogram; Convivial Brands' greeting card merchandiser posting covers setting fixtures to planogram, reordering, and processing credits and returns for out-of-date product — https://www.careerbuilder.com/job-details/greeting-card-merchandiser-kirbyville-tx--b9faa150-c75b-4799-b17a-3305afc12bef and https://jobs.mitalent.org/job-seeker/job-details/9031823 (accessed 2026-10-22, via search result text)
- Scan-based trading: supplier owns inventory until scanned and carries shrink; shrink rates negotiated between retailer and vendor; greeting cards listed among the highest-shrink supermarket categories and Walmart handling cards on SBT; American Greetings cut sales by $65.5M when taking back cards in its SBT conversion (2005-era coverage) — https://www.truecommerce.com/blog/scan-based-trading-basics-why-who-and-how/ and https://www.supermarketnews.com/retail-labor/giant-looks-to-expand-sbt-to-save-on-labor and https://retailwire.com/?p=4186 (accessed 2026-10-22, via search result text)
- NRF/Prosper Insights Valentine's Day 2026 survey (27 January 2026): 41% planned to buy greeting cards, tied with flowers behind candy (56%); record $29.1B total spending — https://nrf.com/media-center/press-releases/valentine-s-day-spending-expected-to-reach-new-records (accessed 2026-10-22, via search result text)
- Flowhaven: Salesforce-based brand licensing platform covering agreements, design approvals and royalty reporting and validation, founded 2016 in Finland — https://www.licenseglobal.com/licensing-resources/flowhaven-offers-solution-licensing-mismanagement (accessed 2026-10-22, via search result text)
- Reese's Law (signed 16 August 2022) covers consumer products containing or designed to use button or coin batteries. CPSC direct final rule (Federal Register, 21 September 2023, FR Doc. 2023-20333) adopts UL 4200A-2023 as the mandatory standard in 16 CFR part 1263; its preamble (Response 3) says UL 4200A's battery-compartment tests cover pliable products "such as shirts and greeting cards that light up or make sound using batteries"; effective 23 October 2023 for products manufactured or imported after that date, with enforcement discretion through 19 March 2024; toys complying with 16 CFR part 1250 are exempt; zinc-air batteries excluded. The separate packaging-label rule (FR Doc. 2023-20334) is effective 21 September 2024 — https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2023-09-21/html/2023-20333.htm and https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2023-09-21/html/2023-20334.htm (accessed 2026-10-22)
- Supermarket card departments of 30 to 220 linear feet and Raley's remodel keeping total pocket capacity constant (1990s trade coverage) — https://www.supermarketnews.com/grocery-operations/price-chopper-s-trump-card and https://www.supermarketnews.com/grocery-operations/survey-helps-raley-s-remerchandise-cards (accessed 2026-10-22, search result only)

## Estimates (not checked)
- Niche market sizes in `_overview.md` and each niche profile — derived from the SUSB rows and chain 10-Ks above: commercial sales at the four public chains (~$26B: AutoZone ~$5.7B domestic commercial, O'Reilly $8.65B professional, Advance ~$4.3B at ~50% of $8.6B, NAPA ~$7.6B at ~80% DIFM of a ~$9.5B North America segment) plus warehouse distributors, jobbers and import specialists give ~$70B of commercial supply; the rest by analogy to the other distributor builds.
- Counter practice: ~20–60 lookups an hour at a busy commercial counter, ~10–30% of lookups returning more than one candidate part, ~15–30 minute delivery promises from store or hub, ~20–50 runs a day per driver, outside reps covering ~60–150 shops each — recalled trade practice; no benchmark found.
- Returns: wrong-part returns at ~5–10% of commercial lines and total returns (including cores and warranty) at ~10–20% of commercial sales — recalled practice plus the vendor claims above.
- Core and warranty economics: core charges of ~$10–$200+ per unit on starters, alternators, calipers, steering racks and batteries; core and warranty credits of ~$4–6B a year passing back through distributors; ~2–5% of credits never recovered — recalled; no published figure.

- Tire wholesale players beyond ATD: TBC/NTW (a Michelin–Sumitomo venture), U.S. AutoForce, Max Finkelstein and manufacturers' own distribution — recalled, not checked.
- Import specialists and large independents: Worldpac (sold by Advance in 2024; buyer recalled as Carlyle), SSF, IMC, Parts Authority, XL Parts — recalled, not checked beyond the Advance 10-K's mention of the Worldpac sale.

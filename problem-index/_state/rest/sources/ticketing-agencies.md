# Sources — Ticketing & Reservation Agencies

## Checked
- NAICS 561599 All other travel arrangement and reservation services in 2022: 1,661 employer firms, 2,996 establishments, receipts $24,988,646K (~$25.0B), annual payroll $7,552,304K (~$7.6B), 76,907 employees. State rows: California 246 firms, $8,577,354K receipts (~$8.6B, ~34% of the US total) and 19,391 employees; Texas 151 firms and ~$2.62B; New York 172 firms and ~$2.27B; Florida 249 firms and ~$2.11B; Nevada 66 firms and ~$0.89B. Washington's row is only ~$188M, so the large online travel agencies are not counted here. For comparison, 561510 travel agencies had $32.3B and 561520 tour operators $9.0B (2022 Economic Census, sector 56 basic file, US and state rows) — https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/economic-census/data/2022/sector56/EC2256BASIC.zip (accessed 2026-10-23)
- NAICS 561599 in 2023: 2,872 employer establishments, 67,902 employees, annual payroll $8,923,448K (~$8.9B). By size: 1,470 establishments (~51%) had fewer than five employees, 495 had 5-9, 418 had 10-19, 273 had 20-49, 111 had 50-99, 60 had 100-249, 27 had 250-499, 10 had 500-999 and 8 had 1,000 or more (45 establishments at 250+). By legal form, C-corporations held 1,517 establishments and 55,973 of the 67,902 employees; 21 establishments were nonprofit (County Business Patterns 2023, US file) — https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/cbp/datasets/2023/cbp23us.zip (accessed 2026-10-23)
- Nonemployer businesses in NAICS 5615 (all travel arrangement and reservation services; no six-digit split published) in 2022: 76,090 establishments with receipts of $3,173,219K (~$3.2B) (Nonemployer Statistics 2022, US file) — https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/nonemployer-statistics/datasets/2022/historical-datasets/nonemp22us.zip (accessed 2026-10-23)
- NAICS 561599 scope: travel arrangement and reservation services other than travel agencies, tour operators and convention and visitors bureaus. Illustrative examples are condominium time-share exchange services; ticket offices and agencies for airlines, buses, cruise ships, sports and theatrical events; reservation services for airlines, car rental, hotels and restaurants; and automobile club road and travel services. Vacation property rental arranging is 531210 and event organising is 561920 — https://www.naics.com/naics-code-description/?code=561599 (accessed 2026-10-23; census.gov's NAICS page returned 403)
- Live Nation FY2025 Form 10-K and Q4 release: Ticketmaster sold 346M fee-bearing tickets in 2025 (340M in 2024); fee-bearing gross transaction value $37.1B, up 6%; Ticketing segment revenue ~$3.1B (up 3%) and Ticketing AOI ~$1.1B; total company revenue $25.2B — https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1335258/000133525826000009/lyv-20251231.htm and https://news.livenationentertainment.com/news/live-nation-entertainment-full-year-and-fourth-quarter-2025-results (accessed 2026-10-23, via search summary of both documents)
- States' antitrust case against Live Nation and Ticketmaster (S.D.N.Y. 1:24-cv-03973): jury verdict for 33 states and the District of Columbia on April 15, 2026 after a five-week trial; the jury found monopolisation of primary ticketing services to major concert venues, an unlawful tie of amphitheatres to promotion, and an overcharge of $1.72 per concert ticket in 22 states. The DOJ settled mid-trial (March 2026): Live Nation keeps Ticketmaster but must open venues to rival ticketing services such as SeatGeek, divest certain amphitheatre arrangements and cap ticket fees at 15%, subject to Tunney Act approval — https://www.thompsoncoburn.com/insights/live-nation-and-ticketmaster-found-liable-for-antitrust-violations-by-federal-jury/ (accessed 2026-10-23)
- Remedies phase in the states' case expected to stretch into 2027; Judge Arun Subramanian said a breakup would not be heard before about February 2027; the DOJ deal included a $280M fund for state claims, divestiture of 13 amphitheatre booking agreements and the 15% fee cap — https://courthousenews.com/penalties-phase-of-live-nation-ticket-monopoly-trial-will-stretch-into-2027/ and https://news.pollstar.com/2026/05/11/live-nation-antitrust-penalties-expected-in-2027/ (accessed 2026-10-23, via search summary)
- FTC and seven states (Colorado, Florida, Illinois, Nebraska, Tennessee, Utah, Virginia) sued Live Nation and Ticketmaster on September 17-18, 2025 under Section 5 of the FTC Act and the BOTS Act: alleged advertised prices that excluded mandatory fees adding 24-44%, and tolerance of brokers circumventing purchase limits, with an internal review cited as showing five brokers controlling over 246,000 tickets through thousands of accounts; Ticketmaster's TradeDesk broker software named — https://www.insideprivacy.com/consumer-protection/ftc-sues-live-nation-and-ticketmaster-for-deceptive-pricing-tactics and https://www.pymnts.com/legal/2025/ftc-sues-ticketmaster-alleging-illegal-coordination-with-brokers-bait-and-switch-pricing/ (accessed 2026-10-23, via search summary of law-firm and press coverage; the complaint itself was not read)
- FTC sued broker Key Investment Group in August 2025 for exceeding online purchase limits under the BOTS Act — same search results as above (accessed 2026-10-23, via search summary)
- Executive Order 14254, "Combating Unfair Practices in the Live Entertainment Market" (March 31, 2025): directs the FTC to enforce the BOTS Act with the states, pursue price transparency and act on unfair secondary-market practices; Treasury and DOJ to check reseller compliance; BOTS Act civil penalties cited at up to $53,088 per violation — https://wiley.law/alert-Executive-Order-on-Ticket-Resale-Market-Calls-for-Greater-FTC-Enforcement and https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/DCPD-202500418/pdf/DCPD-202500418.pdf (accessed 2026-10-23, via search summary)
- FTC maximum civil penalty for violations of a trade regulation rule (FTC Act Section 5(m)(1)(A)) raised from $51,744 to $53,088 per violation, effective January 17, 2025 (16 CFR 1.98); a 2026 adjustment was not checked — https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-A/part-1/subpart-L/section-1.98 and https://search.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/02/ftc-publishes-inflation-adjusted-civil-penalty-amounts-2025 (accessed 2026-10-23, via search summary)
- FTC Rule on Unfair or Deceptive Fees took effect May 12, 2025, limited to live-event tickets and short-term lodging; requires the total price, including mandatory fees, wherever a price is shown; covers resellers, third-party platforms and travel agents; does not cap any fee — https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/rule-unfair-or-deceptive-fees-frequently-asked-questions (accessed 2026-10-23, via search summary)
- Vermont Act 109 (2026, H.512): resale capped at 110 percent of the original ticket price, total price of the original ticket to be disclosed, speculative ticket sales prohibited; takes effect July 1, 2026; the subchapter is repealed on July 1, 2028 — https://legislature.vermont.gov/Documents/2026/Docs/ACTS/ACT109/ACT109%20As%20Enacted.pdf (accessed 2026-10-23, text extracted from the PDF)
- North Carolina enacted ticket-resale provisions (SB 257, Section 5.8) through its 2026 budget, covering resale platforms, brokers, speculative listings, presale inventory and bots from October 1; Maine passed a 110% resale cap in 2025; D.C. and California bills pending as of mid-2026 — https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/07/north-carolina-pushes-ticket-resale-regulations-through-in-budget-bill-sprint/ and https://www.hypebot.com/ca-nc-dc-vt-me-laws-limit-ticket-resale-as-feds-fail-to-act/ (accessed 2026-10-23, via search summary; enacted text not read)

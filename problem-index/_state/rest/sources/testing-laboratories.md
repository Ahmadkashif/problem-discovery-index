# Sources — Testing Laboratories

## Checked
- US testing laboratories (NAICS 54138) employer-firm revenue of $27.5B in 2022 ($27,518 million), up from $24.8B in 2021 and $23.1B in 2019 (Census Service Annual Survey, FRED series REVEF54138ALLEST) — https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/REVEF54138ALLEST (accessed 2026-10-22)
- 7,970 employer establishments in 2022, averaging 20.7 paid employees per establishment and 1.4 establishments per firm, with $27.9B in receipts (2022 Economic Census ECNBASIC, as reported by a third-party summary; not checked on data.census.gov because the Census API needs a key) — https://businessnes.com/business-data/testing-laboratories-and-services/ (accessed 2026-10-22)
- 7,463 establishments, 158,265 employees and $11.9B annual payroll in County Business Patterns 2023 (as reported) — https://plainbizbench.com/industry/54138/ (accessed 2026-10-22)
- NAICS 541380 employment of 164,490 (OEWS May 2023), mean annual wage $73,490; largest occupations: inspectors, testers, sorters, samplers and weighers 21,540 ($52,300 mean); chemical technicians 9,790 ($48,990); chemists 7,500 ($71,920); general and operations managers 7,090; engineering technologists and technicians (other) 5,690; environmental science and protection technicians 4,410 ($48,230); mechanical engineers 4,000 ($108,310); biological technicians 2,730; calibration technologists 2,600; microbiologists 1,740 ($68,740) — https://www.bls.gov/oes/2023/may/naics5_541380.htm (accessed 2026-10-22)
- UL Solutions 2025 revenue $3,053M (Industrial $1,341M, Consumer $1,319M, Software and Advisory $393M); IPO April 2024; segments realigned on 1 January 2026, Software and Advisory renamed Risk and Compliance Software — https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1901440/000190144026000005/uls-20251231.htm and https://www.ul.com/news/ul-solutions-inc-reports-strong-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-2025-results (accessed 2026-10-22)
- Global TIC market: Bureau Veritas puts the total market at more than €200B with roughly half outsourced; SGS sizes the outsourced addressable market at $160B in 2023 rising to $190B by 2027; SGS (~$8.8B), Eurofins (~$8.6B), Bureau Veritas (~$7.9B) and Intertek (~$4.6B LTM) revenues as tabulated by an M&A advisory (not re-checked against each annual report) — https://aventis-advisors.com/tic-industry/ and https://aventis-advisors.com/top-10-largest-testing-inspection-and-certification-companies-globally/ (accessed 2026-10-22)
- A2LA held 4,350 actively accredited certificates across all 50 states and 50+ foreign economies, with 300 contracted assessors (2024 annual report; 4,200+ and 275 in 2022) — https://a2la.org/a2la-2024-annual-report/ and https://a2la.org/a2la-2022-annual-report/ (accessed 2026-10-22)
- AASHTO re:source Proficiency Sample Program serves ~3,000 testing labs and ships 23,000+ samples a year; 2,100+ accredited labs (an older page says 1,800); an AASHTO-accredited lab must take part in all applicable AASHTO re:source and CCRL proficiency programs, and satisfactory means z-scores of 2.00 or less for every test method in its scope — https://aashtoresource.org/psp/overview and https://aashtoresource.org/docs/default-source/publicdocuments/aap-procedures-manual.pdf (accessed 2026-10-22)
- LGC AXIO Proficiency Testing serves 13,000+ laboratories in 160–165+ countries, runs 2,700+ proficiency tests a year from 800+ samples across 30+ open schemes; its food microbiology scheme has 3,000+ participating labs — https://www.lgcgroup.com/what-we-do/reference-materials-research-chemicals-proficiency-testing/ and https://www.lgcstandards.com/GB/en/Resources/TopicInsights/AXIOhub (accessed 2026-10-22)
- TNI-based state programmes require proficiency testing per field of testing: Minnesota (MNELAP) requires two PT studies per calendar year where available and scores a missed PT as failed; New Jersey requires two acceptable results out of the most recent three attempted within 18 months — https://www.health.state.mn.us/communities/environment/mnelap/ptlabs.html and https://dep.nj.gov/dsr/oqa/proficiency-testing/ (accessed 2026-10-22)
- FDA LAAF final rule published 3 December 2021, effective 1 February 2022; A2LA first recognised accreditation body (May 2022); from 1 December 2024 mycotoxin analyses for certain imported foods must use LAAF-accredited labs; further analytes get a compliance date six months after FDA posts sufficient capacity — https://www.fda.gov/food/food-safety-modernization-act-fsma/laboratory-accreditation-analyses-foods-laaf-program-final-rule and https://www.ghy.com/trade-compliance/fda-reminder-use-accredited-labs-for-food-testing-starting-december-1-2024/ (accessed 2026-10-22)
- About 35 LAAF-accredited labs and 8 recognised accreditation bodies roughly two years into the programme (PJLA 2024 presentation) — https://www.pjlabs.com/downloads/webinar_slides/4.30.2024_LAAF.pdf (accessed 2026-10-22, as reported in search results)
- CPSC Certificates of Compliance rule (90 FR 1800, 8 January 2025): importers must eFile certificate data at entry from 8 July 2026 (foreign-trade-zone entries from 8 January 2027); children's product certificates rest on CPSC-accepted third-party lab testing, and the filing names the testing lab and the date and place of the most recent test — https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/01/08/2024-30826/certificates-of-compliance and https://www.akingump.com/en/insights/alerts/cpsc-rule-mandates-efiling-of-certificates-of-compliance-for-imported-consumer-products (accessed 2026-10-22)
- EPA PFAS drinking water rule: PFOA and PFOS MCLs of 4.0 ppt with compliance by April 2029; on 18 May 2026 EPA proposed an optional exemption to April 2031 without changing the MCLs; monitoring timeframes of the April 2024 rule stand — https://www.epa.gov/sdwa/proposed-pfoa-and-pfos-compliance-extension-rule (accessed 2026-10-22)
- Initial PFAS monitoring due by 26 April 2027 and the extension still a proposal as of mid-October 2026 (secondary summaries, not the EPA page) — https://ihmm.org/current-status-of-pfas-regulation-at-the-federal-and-state-levels-august-14-2026/ and https://eelp.law.harvard.edu/tracker/pfas-in-drinking-water/ (accessed 2026-10-22, as reported in search results)
- FCC "bad labs" order (adopted May 2025, Federal Register 7 August 2025) bars test labs, telecommunication certification bodies and accreditation bodies owned or controlled by foreign adversaries from the equipment authorisation programme; FCC 26-28 states 23 such test labs are no longer recognised and proposes limiting recognition to the US and reciprocal economies — https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/08/07/2025-14970/promoting-the-integrity-and-security-of-telecommunications-certification-bodies-measurement and https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/FCC-26-28A1.pdf (accessed 2026-10-22)
- ~75% of new certified devices are tested in labs located in China (stated by a law firm summarising the FCC proceeding; not verified against an FCC source) — https://www.ashurstperkinscoie.com/en/insights/fcc-plugs-national-security-gaps-by-targeting-bad-labs/ (accessed 2026-10-22)
- Construction materials testing field technician: $18–28 an hour, dispatch text the night before, big pours start early, overtime when schedules run long; ACI Concrete Field Testing Grade I, nuclear gauge safety and NICET sponsored; duties include slump, air, temperature, cylinder casting, nuclear density, cure boxes, chain of custody and daily field reports (one Ohio job ad) — https://www.constructionmaterialtesting.com/careers/field-technician/cleveland-oh (accessed 2026-10-22)
- Manual chromatographic integration is a legitimate analyst decision that should be flagged in the CDS audit trail with operator, timestamp and justification; practitioners recommend an experienced senior reviewer wherever manual integration occurs; outright bans are debated — https://casrai.org/guides/chromatography-data-system-audit-trails and https://www.chromatographyonline.com/view/data-integrity-gxp-chromatography-laboratory-part-iii-integration-and-interpretation-data and https://www.technologynetworks.com/analysis/articles/data-integrity-non-compliances-with-chromatography-data-systems-and-how-to-avoid-them-327034 (accessed 2026-10-22)

- NASTF Vehicle Security Professional Registry terms (dated 8 January 2025): the Secure Data Release Model (SDRM) gives pre-approved locksmiths and technicians 24/7 access to key codes, immobilizer reset codes, security PINs and related data from participating automakers; every user must enrol and pass a criminal background check; a VSP ID is valid for two years; the application fee is $100; the VSP must be present with the customer and vehicle, verify ownership (title or registration, US or Canadian driver's licence, VIN match) and complete an electronic Customer Authorization (D1) form within 5 days of the automaker transaction; the information is single-use and may not be stored, databased or transmitted; audit requests must be answered within 7 calendar days; NICB or police auto-theft reports linked to a VSP ID trigger immediate suspension; NASTF requires a locksmith licence to obtain key codes wherever a licence is required; required documents include insurance, licences and fidelity bond — NASTF, https://wp.nastf.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/TCEdit-01082025v14.pdf (accessed 2026-10-23)
- "All locksmith businesses in the US and Canada" must pass Advanced Verification to serve in Google's Local Services unit (garage door services in the US too) — Google Ads Help, https://support.google.com/adwords/answer/7167635?hl=en (accessed 2026-10-23)
- Google and UC San Diego's 2017 study "Pinning Down Abuse on Google Maps" analysed over 100,000 suspended listings; about 2 in 5 fake listings came from people posing as locksmiths, plumbers, electricians and other contractors; 11% of search results for locksmiths were fraudulent; fewer than 0.5% of local searches led to fake listings; improved verification cut fake listings 70% from the June 2015 peak — UCSD, https://today.ucsd.edu/story/pinning_down_abuse_on_google_maps and Google Research, https://research.google/blog/keeping-fake-listings-off-google-maps/ (search-result summaries; accessed 2026-10-23)
- Ohio's attorney general sued an Oregon-based operation (2014) that ran Ohio-sounding locksmith websites, transferred calls to an out-of-state call centre and referred jobs to contracted locksmiths; consumers were quoted $19 for a lock pick and charged far more; Arizona's attorney general (2009) alleged 47 fictitious Tucson Yellow Pages listings routed to one Clearwater, Fla. call centre — Ohio AG, https://www.ohioattorneygeneral.gov/Media/News-Releases/October-2014/Attorney-General-DeWine-Announces-Lawsuit-Agai-(1) and https://www.azag.gov/node/1658 (search-result summaries; accessed 2026-10-23)
- California Business and Professions Code §6980.55: for key work on vehicles and registered property a locksmith records the requester's name, date of birth, driver's licence or ID number and the registration number, with the date and the requester's signature, on a work order; a copy is kept for two years with the locksmith's name and licence number and is open to peace officers and the Bureau (Penal Code §466.6 has a parallel rule) — FindLaw, https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/business-and-professions-code/bpc-sect-6980-55/ and BSIS law index https://www.bsis.ca.gov/about_us/laws/locksmith_law.shtml (search-result summary; accessed 2026-10-23)

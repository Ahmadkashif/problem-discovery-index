# Sources — Title & Settlement Offices

## Checked
- 6,803 US title abstract and settlement firms (NAICS 541191) operating 9,762 establishments with 74,320 employees, $5.23B annual payroll and $12.51B receipts in 2022; 4,072 firms (60%) have fewer than 5 employees and take $1.19B of receipts; 6,110 (90%) have fewer than 20; 64 firms with 500+ employees run 1,810 establishments, employ 20,228 people and take $4.16B (33%) of receipts — Census Bureau, Statistics of U.S. Businesses 2022, U.S. & states 6-digit NAICS table (released 2025-04-10), https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/susb/tables/2022/us_state_6digitnaics_2022.xlsx via https://www.census.gov/data/tables/2022/econ/susb/2022-susb-annual.html (accessed 2026-10-22). Derived: ~$168,000 receipts per employee; ~11 employees per firm.
- US title insurance premium volume $18.5B in 2025, up 13.8% on 2024; Texas $2.7B, Florida $2.01B, California $1.6B, New York $1.2B, Pennsylvania $709M; largest individual underwriters First American Title Insurance Co. 23.1%, Fidelity National Title 14.5%, Old Republic Title 14.0%, Chicago Title 13.1%, Stewart Title 10.9% — ALTA press release, 22 May 2026, https://alta.org/news-and-publications/press-release/ALTA-Reports-2025-Market-Share-and-Title-Insurance-Premium-Volume and ALTA news item, https://alta.org/news-and-publications/news/20260521-Title-Insurance-Premium-Volume-Increased-138-in-2025-ALTA-Reports (accessed 2026-10-22). The agent-versus-direct split sits behind ALTA's member login and was not seen.
- Texas 2024: ~$2.50B gross title premiums, of which ~$2.09B (~84%) was retained by agents and ~$411M remitted to underwriters — Texas Department of Insurance underwriter/agent experience report, https://tdi.texas.gov/reports/pc/documents/2024uwexprpt.pdf (accessed 2026-10-22, figures read from the search-result extract; the direct fetch failed on DNS).
- 48,580 title examiners, abstractors and searchers (SOC 23-2093) employed in May 2025, median annual wage $58,650; 19,260 of them in legal services (NAICS 5411, which contains 541191) and 13,390 in insurance carriers (NAICS 5241, where underwriter-owned direct operations sit) — BLS OEWS via the BLS public API, series OEUN000000000000023209301, OEUN000000000000023209313, OEUN000000054110023209301 and OEUN000000052410023209301, https://api.bls.gov/publicAPI/v1/timeseries/data/OEUN000000000000023209301 (accessed 2026-10-22).
- ALTA Critical Issues Study, "More than pushing a button" (April 2024; 674 responses, Feb–Mar 2024, 48 states + DC): a standard file took 22 hours of work in 2023 (15 median) and a difficult file 45.4 hours (30 median); 36% of files were difficult; 62% of companies that do curative work typically perform at least four curative actions per transaction; the most common curative actions resolve loan payoffs, property taxes and HOA dues; 64% saw curative expenses rise; 71% of respondents had revenue under $1M and 69% closed 75 or fewer files a month; 46% bring a file to clear-to-close in under 30 days and 39% in 31–45 days; about 70 cents of every dollar of title and settlement fees covers upfront research and curative (ALTA analysis of NAIC Form 9) — https://www.alta.org/media/pdf/240506-ALTA-Title-Insurance-Curative-Work-Study-Report.pdf (accessed 2026-10-22).
- TRID: the creditor must ensure the consumer receives the Closing Disclosure no later than three business days before consummation, 12 CFR 1026.19(f)(1)(ii)(A); commentary refers to the settlement agent providing it under 1026.19(f)(1)(v) with the creditor remaining responsible — CFPB Regulation Z §1026.19, https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1026/19/ (accessed 2026-10-22).
- FinCEN Residential Real Estate Rule: reporting duty falls on the settlement, title or escrow agent or attorney for non-financed transfers of residential property to entities and trusts; original effective date 1 December 2025, delayed on 30 September 2025 to 1 March 2026; report due by the later of the last day of the month following the transfer or 30 days after closing — https://www.coleschotz.com/fincen-finalizes-new-residential-real-estate-reporting-rule-effective-march-1-2026/ and https://www.moneylaunderingnews.com/2026/02/status-of-fincens-residential-real-estate-reporting-rule/ (accessed 2026-10-22).
- The rule was vacated on 19 March 2026 by the US District Court for the Eastern District of Texas in Flowers Title Companies, LLC v. Bessent (FinCEN lacked statutory authority); on 20 March 2026 FinCEN said reporting persons are not currently required to file — Burr & Forman, 8 April 2026, https://www.burr.com/newsroom/articles/fincens-residential-real-estate-rule-applicable-to-non-financed-residential-entity-real-estate-transfers-paused (accessed 2026-10-22).
- FBI IC3 2025: real estate fraud losses of $275.1M on 12,368 complaints, up from about $173M in 2024 — NAR, https://www.nar.realtor/news/real-estate-news/online-real-estate-fraud-climbed-to-275m-in-2025-fbi-says, and ALTA release, https://www.alta.org/news-and-publications/press-release/Latest-FBI-Cybercrime-Report-Shows-Real-Estate-Fraud-Losses-Rising-Reinforcing-Need-for-Strong-Consumer-Safeguards (accessed 2026-10-22).
- ALTA 2026 Seller Impersonation Fraud study (released 14 September 2026; 245 respondents, 40 states + DC + USVI): 59% of title firms saw at least one seller impersonation attempt in 2025, up from 28% in 2024; 45% saw one monthly, up from 19%; vacant land named by 82% as the most common target, vacation homes 51%, rentals 48%, agricultural land 36%, primary residences 25% — https://www.housingwire.com/articles/seller-impersonation-fraud-more-than-doubled-since-2024/, https://www.floridarealtors.org/news-media/news-articles/2026/09/seller-impersonation-fraud-attempts-rise and https://www.alta.org/business-operations/research-initiatives-and-resources/critical-issue-studies/seller-impersonation-fraud-study (accessed 2026-10-22, via search summary).

# Sources — Convention & Visitors Bureaus

## Checked
- NAICS 561591 Convention and visitors bureaus in 2022: 863 employer firms, 914 establishments, receipts $2,656,992K (~$2.66B), annual payroll $565,195K (~$0.57B), 7,889 employees. State rows: California 83 firms and ~$461M receipts; Florida 32 firms and ~$385M; Pennsylvania 40 firms and ~$150M; Texas 34 firms and ~$138M (2022 Economic Census, sector 56 basic file, US and state rows) — https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/economic-census/data/2022/sector56/EC2256BASIC.zip (accessed 2026-10-23)
- NAICS 561591 in 2023: 940 employer establishments, 8,898 employees, annual payroll ~$656M. By size: 545 establishments (~58%) had fewer than five employees, 185 had 5-9, 103 had 10-19, 66 had 20-49, 34 had 50-99, 6 had 100-249, and none had 250 or more. By legal form, 823 of the 940 establishments (~88%) and 8,238 of the 8,898 employees were nonprofit (County Business Patterns 2023, US file, lfo = N) — https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/cbp/datasets/2023/cbp23us.zip (accessed 2026-10-23)
- Visit Orlando (Orlando Orange County Convention & Visitors Bureau Inc, EIN 59-2395248) is a 501(c)(6); FY2024 total revenue $113,682,039, expenses $124,661,562, contributions $104,384,475 (FY2023 revenue $114,398,547) — https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/592395248 (accessed 2026-10-23)
- 42% of destination organisation respondents to the 2025 DestinationNEXT Futures Study said their funding is at risk of being reduced or eliminated in the next three years; the survey covered destinations in 36 countries — https://destinationsinternational.org/sites/default/files/2025-07/FINAL_DestinationNEXT%202025%20Futures%20Study%20(July%207%202025).pdf (accessed 2026-10-23, text extracted from the PDF)
- 2015 DMAI/Destinations International DMO Organizational & Financial Profile study: more than 87% of DMOs reported receiving hotel room tax revenue; smaller and mid-sized DMOs got about 90% of funding from public sources (as summarised in search results of the 2015 executive summary) — https://gacvb.memberclicks.net/assets/Resources/DMAI/2015%20dmai%20dmo%20org.%20%20financial%20profile%20study%20exec%20summary_2.pdf (accessed 2026-10-23, via search snippet; the current study is members-only as Destination Organization Performance Reporting: https://destinationsinternational.org/organization-and-financial-profile-study)
- Destinations International / Tourism Economics study "Destination Promotion: A Catalyst for Community Vitality" (2024 executive summary): average spending return on media spending of $85 and average return on taxes of $9 for every $1 invested; Tourism Economics worked with Longwoods International, Clarity of Place and MMGY — https://destinationsinternational.org/sites/default/files/2024-02/Tourism%20Economics-Destinations%20International%20Executive%20Summary-v1.1.pdf (accessed 2026-10-23, text extracted from the PDF)
- Event Impact Calculator: "utilized by over 300 destination organizations worldwide"; models and platform developed by Tourism Economics; draws on ten data sources, updated annually; licensed to Destinations International members only, with fees tiered by the organisation's operating budget — https://destinationsinternational.org/event-impact-calculator (accessed 2026-10-23)
- Standard DMO Performance Reporting handbook (DMAI/Destinations International): a convention lead is an inquiry for at least 10 peak sleeping rooms; booking ratio = bookings / (bookings + lost opportunities); post-event room-night pickup "may not be obtainable" because rooms are increasingly booked outside the block; room-night ratios should not be used to compare DMOs — https://www.nevadacountyca.gov/DocumentCenter/View/13945/Standard-DMO-Performance-Reporting-A-handbook-for-Destination-Marketing-Organizations-PDF (accessed 2026-10-23, via search summary of the handbook)
- 211 tourism improvement districts across 23 states (Civitas-sourced handout, 2024) — https://prla.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/TID-Handout_FINAL.pdf (accessed 2026-10-23, text extracted from the PDF)
- DMAP: nine destination organisations received new accreditation and 24 were reaccredited at the July 22, 2026 Destinations International convention — https://destinationsinternational.org/press-release/33-destinations-receive-dmap-accreditation-destinations-international-2026-annual (accessed 2026-10-23). "Nearly 200 accredited organizations worldwide" per the DMAP list page as shown in search results (the page itself returned 403) — https://destinationsinternational.org/article/dmap-accredited-organizations
- Granicus acquired Simpleview (announced September 5, 2024); Simpleview worked with more than 1,000 destinations and said more than 90% of the top 50 North American meeting destinations used its technology — https://www.govtech.com/biz/granicus-buys-destination-marketing-firm-simpleview and https://granicus.com/blog/granicus-acquires-destination-marketing-leader-simpleview-to-expand-community-experience-capabilities/ (accessed 2026-10-23, via search summary). The product site now brands as Granicus Destinations and lists Simpleview CRM, Simpleview CMS, Data Engine, pace reporting and a Meetings & Groups Database — https://www.simpleviewinc.com/ (accessed 2026-10-23)
- Tempest sells iDSS, a CRM built for destination organisations, plus destination websites and a Sports Tourism Index; vendor pages cite 170+ to 200+ destination clients and native integrations with MINT+, the Event Impact Calculator and Cvent — https://www.tempest.im/technology/idss/ and https://www.tempest.im/ (accessed 2026-10-23, via search summary; vendor-reported)
- Visa's destination product is now "Visa Spend Signals (formerly Visa Destination Insights)", built on "aggregated, depersonalized spend data derived from card-present transactions" — https://www.visa.com/en-us/products/visa-destination-insights (accessed 2026-10-23)
- Zartico launched a Location Intelligence Spatial Archive and Z5 (next version of ZDOS) in August 2025, and marketing performance products in January 2025 — https://www.zartico.com/news/next-gen and https://www.zartico.com/news/zartico-launches-marketing-performance-solutions-redefines-attribution (accessed 2026-10-23, via search summary)
- Placer.ai and Datafy are both sold to DMOs; Placer's visitor figures are panel-extrapolated estimates; a Vernon County, Wisconsin staff report raised privacy and sampling limits — https://www.nexttownai.com/blog/datafy-vs-placer-ai-which-platform-is-right-for-your-dmo and https://citizenportal.ai/articles/11027036/Wisconsin/Vernon-County/County-staff-outlines-Placer-AI-tourism-data-that-estimates-57M-visitor-spend-privacy-and-sampling-limits-raised (accessed 2026-10-23, via search summary)
- FTC finalised orders on January 14, 2025 barring Gravy Analytics/Venntel and Mobilewalla from selling sensitive location data — https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/01/ftc-finalizes-order-prohibiting-gravy-analytics-venntel-selling-sensitive-location-data (accessed 2026-10-23, via search summary)
- Texas municipal hotel occupancy tax (Tax Code ch. 351): every expenditure must directly enhance and promote tourism and the convention and hotel industry and fit one of the statutory categories (the "two-part test") — https://www.tml.org/DocumentCenter/View/281/What-Cities-Need-to-Know-to-Administer-the-Local-Hotel-Occupancy-Tax-PDF (accessed 2026-10-23, via search summary)
- Legends completed its acquisition of ASM Global on August 23, 2024 (combined ~350 venues, later Legends Global); Oak View Group is the other main venue manager — https://news.pollstar.com/2024/08/23/legends-completes-asm-global-acquisition/ and https://meetings.skift.com/2024/08/23/legends-asm-deal-is-done/ (accessed 2026-10-23, via search summary)
- PCMA Convene: the same meeting lead can reach a hotel through a third party, a DMO and the planner directly, creating duplicates; salespeople take 20-30 minutes per RFP response — http://www.pcmaconvene.org/features/is-the-rfp-process-broken/ (accessed 2026-10-23, via search summary)

- Oliver Wyman Global Fleet and MRO Market Forecast 2026–2036 (executive summary distributed by ARSA): global MRO demand $136B in 2025, up 8% from $126B in 2024, forecast near $193B by 2036 (about double 2019); North America 2026 fleet 8,832 commercial aircraft and 2026 MRO demand $31.8B (engine $14.8B, component $6.6B, airframe $6.1B, line $4.4B), rising to $42.0B by 2036 (2.8% a year); world 2026 MRO $139.6B (engine $70.1B, airframe $27.8B, component $24.0B, line $17.7B); "Large component systems have been hit the hardest, particularly the engines that represent the biggest segment of the MRO market"; a "bow wave" of deferred maintenance from the pandemic and the 737 grounding is causing capacity bottlenecks and longer turnaround times — https://arsa.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/ARSA-OW-2026FleetMROMarketReport-ExecSum-2026.pdf (accessed 2026-10-24; PDF text extracted locally). The forecast covers the commercial air transport fleet only, not business or general aviation.
- ATEC/Oliver Wyman Pipeline Report 2025: FAA airmen database lists 337,855 certificated mechanics (+2.6%); 41% are over 60 and the average is 54; more than 45,000 reach retirement age in ten years; repair stations employ about 71% of all technical personnel and almost half of certificated mechanics; Oliver Wyman estimated a North American shortfall of ~17,800 commercial aviation technical personnel in 2025, including 5,338 certificated mechanics, rising to almost 7,000 certificated mechanics (nearly 12% of demand) and a 22,000 total maintenance-worker deficit in 2027 — https://assets.noviams.com/novi-file-uploads/atec/Pipeline_Report_11_07_25pdf.pdf (accessed 2026-10-24; PDF text extracted locally). This is the source of the "~17,000 mechanic shortfall" claim in the placement filter: it is the 2025 total technical-personnel figure, not certificated mechanics alone.
- ATEC/Oliver Wyman Pipeline Report 2026, as reported by AVweb on 25 September 2026: the FAA issued a record 11,103 new mechanic certificates in 2025 (+23.2%); schools produced 7,496 new mechanics (+27%); enrolment at FAA-certificated schools rose 13.5% to 27,740; only 62% of A&P seats were filled; 44% of certificated mechanics are over 60; more than 39,000 retirements expected over the next decade; Oliver Wyman projects a North American shortage of ~9,100 certificated mechanics in 2026 and ~20,000 (12% of demand) by 2030 — https://avweb.com/aviation-news/faa-issues-record-mechanic-certificates/ (accessed 2026-10-24). A search summary of the same report added "approximately 25,000 aviation technical personnel" short in 2026; that figure is marked as search-summary only (generalaviationnews.com returned 403).
- 14 CFR 145.221: a certificated repair station must report to the FAA within 96 hours after it discovers any serious failure, malfunction or defect of an article; 145.211 requires an FAA-acceptable quality control system and manual (incoming inspection, preliminary and hidden-damage inspection, inspector proficiency, current technical data, final inspection and return to service, calibration, corrective action); 145.109(d) requires airworthiness directives, instructions for continued airworthiness, maintenance and overhaul manuals and service bulletins to be current and accessible when the work is done; 145.163 requires an FAA-approved initial and recurrent training programme with records kept two years; 145.219 requires records kept two years — https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/chapter-I/subchapter-H/part-145 (accessed 2026-10-24 via the eCFR versioner API).
- 14 CFR 91.417(a)(2): owners and operators must keep total time in service, the current status of life-limited parts, time since overhaul, current inspection status, and the current status of applicable airworthiness directives including method of compliance, AD number, revision date and next due time for recurring ADs; those records transfer with the aircraft when sold — https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/chapter-I/subchapter-F/part-91/subpart-E/section-91.417 (accessed 2026-10-24 via the eCFR versioner API).
- Airworthiness directives in the Federal Register: 317 final-rule documents titled "Airworthiness Directives" in 2025 (of 553 FAA final rules) and 280 in 2024 (of 611); 249 proposed AD rules in 2025 and 245 in 2024 — Federal Register API, https://www.federalregister.gov/api/v1/documents.json filtered to agency federal-aviation-administration and type RULE/PRORULE by publication year (accessed 2026-10-24; counts computed locally from titles).

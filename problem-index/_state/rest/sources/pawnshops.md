# Sources — Pawnshops

## Checked
- NAICS 522298 All Other Nondepository Credit Intermediation (the NAICS 2017 code that carried pawnshops and was merged into 522299 in NAICS 2022), 2022: 4,700 firms, 9,726 establishments, 67,177 employees, $6.42B annual payroll and $61.3B receipts; 2,972 firms have fewer than 5 employees; 60 firms with 500+ employees run 4,024 establishments and take $23.2B of receipts. The other two codes merged into 522299: 522294 Secondary Market Financing, 123 firms, 17,209 employees, $202.4B receipts (almost all at 5 firms with 500+ employees, i.e. the GSEs); 522293 International Trade Financing, 227 firms, 896 employees, $0.66B receipts — Census Bureau, Statistics of U.S. Businesses 2022, U.S. & states 6-digit NAICS table (released 2025-04-10), https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/susb/tables/2022/us_state_6digitnaics_2022.xlsx via https://www.census.gov/data/tables/2022/econ/susb/2022-susb-annual.html (accessed 2026-10-22). Note: 522298 also holds other residual lenders, so its receipts are not a pawn market size; the establishment count is the best public proxy for pawn store numbers.
- NAICS 522299, 2024 annual average (private ownership): 10,275 establishments, 86,458 employees, average annual pay $121,844 — BLS Quarterly Census of Employment and Wages, https://data.bls.gov/cew/data/api/2024/a/industry/522299.csv (accessed 2026-10-22). The pay average is lifted by the GSEs and is not a pawn wage.
- FirstCash, FY2025 10-K and annual report: 1,207 U.S. pawn stores in 29 states and DC at 31 December 2025; 1,837 Latin America stores; average U.S. pawn loan $312 at year end; U.S. and Latin America terms typically 30–90 days plus a grace period of 14–90 days set by state or local rules; ~22,000 employees, ~8,300 in the U.S.; U.S. pawn segment 2025 revenue $1,753.4M, pawn loan fees $555.0M, retail merchandise sales $1.05B, pre-tax income $452.6M; U.S. pawn loans outstanding $450.5M at year end; AFF lease-to-own segment 15% of consolidated net revenue; the Military Lending Act covers pawn loans and FirstCash began offering an MLA-compliant pawn product in 2025; U.S. stores must follow local law-enforcement "do-not-buy" lists and state hold periods; pawnshops dealing in guns must hold an FFL and keep a permanent record of firearm receipts and dispositions; the company keeps an AML programme under the BSA/USA PATRIOT Act — https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/840489/000084048926000032/fcfs-20251231.htm and https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000840489/000110465926049893/tm2610930d11_ars.pdf (accessed 2026-10-22; segment figures via search extract of the ARS table, store and regulatory text read from the 10-K).
- EZCORP, fiscal 2025 (year to 30 September 2025): 1,360 stores in five countries; 545 U.S. Pawn stores; U.S. Pawn PLO $233.8M (+9%), U.S. pawn service charges $351.5M (+9%); U.S. merchandise margin 37%; U.S. inventory turnover 2.2x versus 2.6x a year earlier; U.S. aged general merchandise 2.2% of general merchandise inventory; U.S. jewelry scrap sales $85.7M (+58%), consolidated scrap $98.9M with gross margin 27%, attributed to higher gold prices; consolidated revenue $1,274.3M; footprint grew by 81 stores in the year, including 52 acquired and 40 de novo — https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/876523/000087652325000095/a2025-q4pressreleaseex991.htm and https://ezcorp.gcs-web.com/news-releases/news-release-details/ezcorp-reports-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-fiscal-2025-results (accessed 2026-10-22; store additions via search summary of the release).
- Florida Pawnbroking Act, s. 539.001: licence from the Department of Agriculture and Consumer Services, $300 a year per shop; transaction form records name, address, date of birth, physical description, right thumbprint, ID details and a complete description of goods including serial number and precious-metal and gemstone details; forms delivered to the appropriate law enforcement official by the end of each business day for the previous day, or electronically if the agency supplies the software; purchased and pledged goods held unaltered for 30 calendar days; maturity 30 days after the pawn, 30-day grace, automatic forfeiture after; pawn service charge capped at 25% of the amount financed per 30-day period (minimum $5); s. 539.003 makes transaction records delivered to law enforcement confidential and usable "only for official law enforcement purposes" — https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/Chapter539/All (accessed 2026-10-22).
- ATF: redemption of a pawned firearm is a disposition requiring an entry in the acquisition and disposition record, a Form 4473 and a NICS check for each redemption; a pawnbroker may not return a firearm to a person NICS denies — https://atf.gov/node/35666 and https://www.atf.gov/firearms/questions-and-answers?page=9 (accessed 2026-10-22, via search summary of the ATF Q&A pages).
- LeadsOnline: agencies can search 1.2+ billion transactions reported by pawn shops, secondhand dealers, scrap yards, ecoATM kiosks and other businesses across all 50 states, with nightly uploads; only law enforcement can search; Tacoma and Fort Wayne ordinances require pawnbrokers and secondhand dealers to report through it — https://leadsonline.com/leads, https://tdocs.tacoma.gov/taxlicense/Regulatory/Leads%20Online_Notice.pdf and https://leadsonline.com/hubfs/CustStory_FortWaynePD.pdf (accessed 2026-10-22). An undated press release reported 5,200+ agencies using it — https://technical.ly/civic-news/leadsonline-pawn-shops-police/ (not used as a current figure).
- Oregon Division of Financial Regulation pawnbroker report 2025: forfeiture rate 33.4% (34.5% the year before; 25.6% in 2022); 174 police pickups, a police pickup rate of 0.094%; 74 licensed pawnbrokers at end of the 2024 report year with 74,158 loans outstanding totalling $18.9M — https://dfr.oregon.gov/business/reg/reports-data/finance/Documents/pawnbrokers/2025-pawnbrokers.pdf and https://dfr.oregon.gov/business/reg/reports-data/finance/Documents/pawnbrokers/2024-pawnbrokers.pdf (accessed 2026-10-22, via search extract; the state site did not resolve for a direct read).
- Texas OCCC requires each licensed pawnshop location to file an annual report by 31 July covering loans made, amounts advanced and loans outstanding; the consolidated 2024 report covers 1,338 reporting companies, ~5.28M loans made for ~$1.15B and ~1.42M loans outstanding for ~$366M at year end — https://occc.texas.gov/sites/default/files/2025-08/pawn-consolidated-2024.pdf and https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/texas/7-Tex-Admin-Code-SS-85-502 (accessed 2026-10-22, via search extract; the table text was garbled and the site did not resolve for a direct read, so the vault writes these with "~").

## Estimates (not checked)
- ~10,000 US pawnshops — derived from the Census establishment count above and older National Pawnbrokers Association figures of 10,000–11,000 (dated 2007–2015 press, not a current count).
- Independents run ~80–85% of shops — derived: FirstCash plus EZCORP run ~1,750 US stores of ~10,000; older press put independents at ~90%.
- US pawn revenue of ~$6–8B a year (loan fees, merchandise and scrap) — derived: the two public chains report ~$2.6B of US pawn revenue from ~1,750 stores; independents assumed to earn roughly a third to half as much per store. IBISWorld's lower figure is noted above.
- Industry pawn loans outstanding of ~$2–3B — derived from the chains' $684M across ~1,750 stores and smaller independents.
- Pawn loans written at ~25–60% of expected resale value — consumer-guide and press figures of low quality (https://www.fox13now.com/2013/04/08/negotiation-skills-worth-a-great-deal-at-pawn-shops quotes 30–60%); not a measured figure.
- Collateral mix (jewellery ~40–50% of value, general merchandise most tickets) — judgement from chain disclosures and trade press; neither chain publishes a U.S. split.
- Repeat use: most pawn customers redeem and re-pawn; the industry association's historical claim that ~80–85% of loans are repaid conflicts with Oregon's 33% forfeiture rate (the association counts customers, Oregon counts loans) — recalled press figures.

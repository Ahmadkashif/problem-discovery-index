# Sources — Pension Funds

- NAICS 525110 in 2024, national level: private ownership 919 establishments, 4,997 average employees, ~$580M total wages, average annual pay $116,076; federal 1 establishment (7 employees), state 3 (130), local 13 (98). The code badly undercounts the activity: public retirement systems report their staff under public administration and corporate plans' staff sit inside the sponsor's own establishments (BLS QCEW annual file) — https://data.bls.gov/cew/data/api/2024/a/industry/525110.csv (accessed 2026-10-22)
- State and local government pension plans, 2025: $6.49T in short- and long-term assets, up 8.46% from $5.98T in 2024; $315.02B of contributions (employees 24.83%, governments 75.17%); $418.25B of benefit payments, up 3.40% from $404.46B; more than 37 million members including inactive members eligible for future benefits (Census Annual Survey of Public Pensions, release of May 14, 2026) — https://www.census.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2026/2025-aspp.html (accessed 2026-10-22)
- 304 state-administered and 4,632 locally administered defined benefit public pension systems (Census ASPP programme page; the page does not state the survey year clearly, so the count is used as approximate) — https://www.census.gov/aspp (accessed 2026-10-22, via search result)
- Private pension plans, plan year 2023 (Form 5500 and 5500-SF, excluding one-participant plans): 46,233 defined benefit plans with 29,347K participants (11,077K active), ~$2,993.7B assets, $78.8B contributions and $260.5B benefits; 6,014 DB plans with 100+ participants hold ~$2,921.5B of that; 1,341 multiemployer DB plans with 10,666K participants and ~$728.6B; 26,764 cash balance plans with ~$1,055B; for comparison 790,610 DC plans with ~$9,407B (DOL EBSA Private Pension Plan Bulletin, Abstract of 2023 Form 5500 Annual Reports, Tables A1, A1(a), A6) — https://www.dol.gov/sites/dolgov/files/ebsa/researchers/statistics/retirement-bulletins/private-pension-plan-bulletins-abstract-2023.pdf (accessed 2026-10-22)
- Milliman 100 Public Pension Funding Index: 88.7% funded at June 30, 2026, down from a record 89.1% at May 31; half of the 100 largest public plans more than 90% funded, 10 below 60%; median assumed return 7.0%. Milliman's corporate Milliman 100 Pension Funding Index read 109.6% in its June 2026 edition — https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260730202469/en/ and https://www.milliman.com/en/insight/pension-funding-index-june-2026 (accessed 2026-10-22, via search results)
- Public pension asset mix: alternatives rose from 27% of total assets in 2019 to 35% in 2022, equities fell from 47% to 42% (Pew Charitable Trusts, April 2025 issue brief) — https://www.pew.org/en/research-and-analysis/issue-briefs/2025/04/increased-risk-complex-investment-landscape-require-prudent-pension-management-practices (accessed 2026-10-22, via search result)
- Plan sponsors hire managers after strong past performance and the hires deliver no positive excess return afterwards; returns of fired managers after termination are indistinguishable from zero, and in matched round trips keeping the fired manager would have done no worse than the new hire (Goyal and Wahal, "The Selection and Termination of Investment Management Firms by Plan Sponsors," Journal of Finance 63(4), 2008, ~3,400 plan sponsors 1994–2003) — https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v63y2008i4p1805-1847.html (accessed 2026-10-22)
- ILPA released an updated Reporting Template and a new Performance Template on January 22, 2025; the Reporting Template applies from Q1 2026 reporting for funds in their investment period or launched on or after January 1, 2026, with more granular fee, expense and internal chargeback lines; Performance Template data capture from Q1 2026, first delivery after March 31, 2027; adoption is voluntary — https://rsmcanada.com/insights/industries/private-equity/essential-insights-on-new-ilpa-reporting-standards.html and https://www.ilpa.org/news/how-we-got-to-new-reporting-standards (accessed 2026-10-22, via search results)
- California Government Code 7514.7 (AB 2833, 2016): public investment funds must obtain, at least annually, each alternative investment vehicle's fees and expenses paid directly, the fund's pro rata share of fees and expenses paid to the manager or related parties, its pro rata share of carried interest, and portfolio-company fees, and present the information at least once a year at a meeting open to the public; applies to contracts and new commitments from January 1, 2017 — https://www.leginfo.ca.gov/pub/15-16/bill/asm/ab_2801-2850/ab_2833_bill_20160914_chaptered.htm and https://www.scers.gov/sites/main/files/file-attachments/20240821_-_item_14.pdf (accessed 2026-10-22, via search results)
- CalPERS disallowed compensation: Government Code 20164.5 (SB 278, Chapter 331, Statutes of 2021), effective January 1, 2022, covers compensation reported by an employer that CalPERS later finds non-compliant with PEPRA, Gov. Code 20636/20636.1 or CalPERS regulations; before it, retirees repaid the overpayment and took a prospective reduction; the law shifts most of that cost to the employer — https://www.calpers.ca.gov/docs/circular-letters/2021/200-076-21.pdf and https://www.westerncity.com/article/three-developments-calpers-city-leaders-need-know-about (accessed 2026-10-22, via search results)
- North Carolina Retirement Systems Division (memo to TSERS/LGERS boards, July 30, 2026): counselors handle eligibility, estimates, applications, refunds, disability, service purchases, taxes and health coverage; five to seven weeks of initial training plus two to five months on calls before a new counselor works most inquiries alone; average monthly calls rose from 22,519 (2024) to 26,282 (2025), written inquiries from 7,690 to 8,358; March 2026 brought 33,149 calls and 10,607 written inquiries, average speed of answer 25 min 19 s, ~30% of callers abandoned; a staffing shortfall of five to eight FTE out of 33 call-centre positions; CEM 2025: cost $33 per member against a peer average of $130, 1.9 front-office FTE per 10,000 members against 3.3, service score 75 against a peer median of 82 — https://files.nc.gov/retirement/documents/2026-07/10.%20Authorization%20to%20Add%20Member%20Counseling%20Positions.pdf (accessed 2026-10-22)
- CEM Benchmarking pension administration benchmarking, 2025 cycle: 81 pension systems participated (34 US, 21 Canada, 23 UK, 3 elsewhere); Washington DRS total cost $98 per member against a peer average of $146, 2.4 front-office FTE per 10,000 members against 3.8, service score 79 against a peer median of 83, call wait time improved to 313 seconds from 553 (DRS committee presentation, April 30, 2026) — https://www.drs.wa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/DAC_CEM_042026.pdf (accessed 2026-10-22)

- Between 94% and 98% of dispatched burglar alarm calls are false, per the DOJ COPS Office guide; Seattle stopped sending officers to unverified alarm calls on 1 October 2024; the Seattle 911 Center fields about 13,000 burglary alarm calls a year, under 4% tied to a confirmed crime in 2023; Dallas, San Jose, Madison and Henderson adopted verified response and later walked it back; Salt Lake City's dispatches to burglar alarms fell about 95% (10,500 to 500 a year) after its 2000 policy; about 2% of one Seattle monitoring company's ~65,000 customers had video verification — AZ Big Media, https://azbigmedia.com/business/why-more-cities-are-following-seattles-lead-on-verified-response-alarm-policies/ (accessed 2026-10-23)

- ANSI/TMA-AVS-01 Alarm Validation Scoring: levels 0 (no call for service) to 4 (confirmed threat to life); level 2 = confirmed or highly probable human presence with unknown intent, level 3 = confirmed threat to property; 2024 edition revised December 2024; IACP ratified a resolution supporting the 2023 edition; scores travel to ECCs/PSAPs on calls for service — The Monitoring Association, https://tma.us/standards/tma-avs-01-alarm-validation-standard/ (accessed 2026-10-23)
- ASAP (Automated Secure Alarm Protocol, developed by TMA and APCO International) is deployed in more than 150 US cities (COPS Monitoring announcement, 20 August 2025) — https://tma.us/cops-monitoring-launches-asap-to-improve-emergency-response-and-reduce-false-alarms/ (accessed 2026-10-23)

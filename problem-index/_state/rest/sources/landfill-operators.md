# Sources — Landfill Operators

## Checked
- 562212 Solid Waste Landfill, 2022: 707 employer firms, 1,592 establishments, 25,635 employees, $12.76B receipts; 8 enterprises with 5,000+ employees ran 748 establishments with 12,881 employees and $8.73B receipts (~68%); 570 firms with fewer than 20 employees took $839M (~7%); 677 firms under 500 employees took $2.47B (~19%) — https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/susb/tables/2022/us_state_naics_detailedsizes_2022.xlsx (Census SUSB 2022, released 10 April 2025; US rows parsed locally) (accessed 2026-10-25)
- 562219 Other Nonhazardous Waste Treatment and Disposal, 2022: 535 firms, 621 establishments, 4,989 employees, $1.70B receipts; 474 firms under 20 employees — same SUSB table (accessed 2026-10-25). The two codes sum to ~$14.46B, ~2,213 establishments and ~30,600 employees; "~1,240 firms" is a sum of code counts. SUSB counts employer firms only, so county- and authority-run landfills are outside it.
- NAICS 2022 index: 562212 covers sanitary, solid waste and sludge disposal landfills, including landfills "combined with collection and/or local hauling"; 562219 covers compost dumps, lawn waste disposal facilities and nonhazardous waste treatment facilities other than combustors, incinerators, landfills and sewage plants — https://www.census.gov/naics/2022NAICS/2022_NAICS_Index_File.xlsx (parsed locally) (accessed 2026-10-25)
- EREF counted 1,412 active landfills in 2025, ~10% fewer than in 2010; ~35% privately owned, a share steady for ~15 years; landfills took 71% of MSW in 2023, up from 67% in 2016 — https://www.wastedive.com/news/eref-msw-flow-data-preview-landfill-tonnage-rcon/832104/ (Waste Dive, 5 October 2026) (accessed 2026-10-25)
- EREF 2024 tipping fee analysis: national average $62.28 a ton from 351 landfills, a 10% increase (largest since 2022); large landfills over $70; private landfills charge ~34% more; Pacific and Midwest rose most (weighted or unweighted not stated) — https://wasteadvantagemag.com/eref-report-shows-10-increase-in-u-s-landfill-tipping-fees-largest-increase-since-2022/ (Waste Advantage, 24 July 2025) (accessed 2026-10-25)
- WM FY2025 10-K: owned or operated 253 solid waste landfills and four hazardous waste landfills at 31 December 2025; 244 sites with remedial activities in closure or post-closure; total landfill capacity 5,242M tons (5,102M permitted, 140M expansion), 5,126M cubic yards; 129M depletable tons received in 2025; expansion permits at 13 landfills in 2025 and 11 in 2024; landfill revenue $5,347M gross, $3,781M after $1,566M intercompany; Renewable Energy segment revenue $478M from 86 landfill beneficial-use projects; 19 third-party power, 17 third-party RNG and nine pipeline projects on its landfills pay royalties; landfill final capping, closure and post-closure asset retirement obligations $3.3B, an audit critical matter alongside landfill depletion rates — https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/823768/000110465926012049/wm-20251231x10k.htm (accessed 2026-10-25)
- Republic Services FY2025 10-K: 207 active landfills; landfill tipping fees charged to third parties ~12% of revenue; 41,158 permitted acres and 5.0B in-place cubic yards of available capacity; responsibility for 124 closed landfills; landfill revenue $3,202M gross, $1,920M net (11.6% of revenue); 77 landfill gas-to-energy projects at its landfills and 84 renewable energy projects in all, more than 30 in development; landfill operating costs rose in 2025 "primarily due to an increase in monitoring and maintenance costs on our gas and leachate extraction systems"; disposal costs include host community fees — https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1060391/000106039126000094/rsg-20251231.htm (accessed 2026-10-25)
- Waste Connections FY2025 10-K: 77 MSW landfills, 20 E&P waste landfills and caverns, 17 non-MSW landfills; Chiquita Canyon Landfill (California) elevated temperature landfill event since 2023, ~410 SCAQMD Rule 402 odour notices of violation since May 2023 plus 26 others, a stipulated abatement order with 107 conditions, increased leachate generation and seeps, active disposal ceased 31 December 2024; guidance on PFAS in leachate "could potentially increase burdens" — https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1318220/000110465926013700/wcn-20251231x10k.htm (accessed 2026-10-25)
- EPA LMOP database: more than 2,600 MSW landfills; as of September 2024, 488 landfills supply 542 operational LFG energy projects and 444 more are "candidate" landfills; updated two to three times a year — https://www.epa.gov/lmop/lmop-landfill-and-project-database (accessed 2026-10-25)
- NSPS Subpart XXX §60.765: quarterly surface methane monitoring along the perimeter and a traverse at 30-metre intervals; 500 ppm above background is an exceedance; re-monitor within 10 calendar days and again at one month; three exceedances in a quarter require a new well or collection device within 120 days; monthly wellhead monitoring of gauge pressure and temperature, with positive pressure or gas above 55 °C corrected within 5 days and a root-cause analysis if not resolved within 15 — https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/40/60.765 (accessed 2026-10-25)
- 40 CFR 258.61: post-closure care for 30 years, which an approved state director may shorten or extend; covers cover maintenance, leachate collection, groundwater monitoring and gas monitoring — https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/40/258.61 (accessed 2026-10-25)
- 40 CFR 258.72: written post-closure cost estimate of hiring a third party, adjusted for inflation every year and increased when plan or unit changes raise maximum costs — https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/40/258.72 (accessed 2026-10-25)

# Sources — Repossession Agencies

## Checked
- NAICS 561491 Repossession Services, 2022: 575 firms, 655 establishments, 6,663 employees, $321.2M annual payroll and $1,150.4M receipts; 339 firms have fewer than 5 employees (621 employees, $149.6M receipts); 494 firms have fewer than 20 employees; 68 firms have 20–99 employees and 8 have 100–499; 5 enterprises with 500+ employees run 53 establishments in the code with 632 employees and $68.8M receipts — Census Bureau, Statistics of U.S. Businesses 2022, U.S. & states 6-digit NAICS table (released 2025-04-10), https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/susb/tables/2022/us_state_6digitnaics_2022.xlsx via https://www.census.gov/data/tables/2022/econ/susb/2022-susb-annual.html (accessed 2026-10-23). Note: agencies that file under towing (488410) or have no employees are outside this count.
- NAICS 561491, 2024 annual average (private ownership): 855 establishments, 7,268 employees, average annual pay $56,179 — BLS Quarterly Census of Employment and Wages, https://data.bls.gov/cew/data/api/2024/a/industry/561491.csv (accessed 2026-10-23).
- Census Service Annual Survey revenue for employer firms in 561491 of ~$1.16B in 2022 (FRED series REVEF561491ALLEST), consistent with the SUSB receipts above — https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/REVEF561491ALLEST (accessed 2026-10-23, via search summary).
- Cox Automotive estimated ~1.73M vehicle repossessions in 2024, the most since 2009 (~1.77M), up ~43% from 2022 — https://www.auto123.com/en/news/vehicle-repossessions-rising-usa/72577 and https://finance.yahoo.com/news/elizabeth-warren-says-car-repossessions-003052531.html (accessed 2026-10-23, via search summary). No Cox full-year 2025 estimate was found.
- RDN assignment data as analysed by CURepossession: 2,422,883 unique repossession assignments and 706,393 repossessions in Q2 2025 (2,571,886 and 735,369 in Q1 2025); a recovery-to-assignment ratio of 29% in Q2 2025, 28% in 2024 and 27% in 2023, against a CFPB-reported 41% for January 2018 to April 2021; Q2 2025 assignments 20% above Q2 2024 — https://curepossession.com/rdn-repossession-volume-report-q2-2025/ (accessed 2026-10-23). A trade blog's reading of platform data; "unique assignments" include reassignments, so the vault writes the ratio as "~29%".
- Motorola Solutions acquired VaaS International Holdings in January 2019 for $445M in cash and equity; VaaS was a holding company for Digital Recognition Network (commercial customers, chiefly repossession) and Vigilant Solutions (law enforcement), with 2019 revenue expected at ~$100M — https://urgentcomm.com/?p=21780 and https://www.securityinfowatch.com/video-surveillance/video-analytics/news/21039990/motorola-acquires-vaas-international-holdings (accessed 2026-10-23, via search summary).
- DRN's own site claims 4.6M+ vehicles recovered, $46B+ value of assets recovered, 1,000+ customers across lending, insurance and recovery, 750K+ monthly lender and provider assignments and coverage of 300+ U.S. markets; product lines include fixed, quick-deploy and mobile LPR cameras and an LPR platform — https://drndata.com/ (accessed 2026-10-23).
- DRN camera kits priced at $12,988 (two cameras) and $15,988 (four cameras) plus tax; RecoveryNet, a PRA Location Services product, offers LPR access for up to 500 plates for $299 a month; a CURepossession editorial argues LPR has captured the easier recoveries and left agencies harder files at 20–30% success with flat fees unchanged — https://www.autoremarketing.com/ar/drn-unveils-new-camera-technology-metrics-of-revenue-potential, https://autoremarketing.com/subprime/recoverynet-offers-lpr-capability-through-subscription-based-platform and https://curepossession.com/why-contingency-pricing-no-longer-works-in-the-repossession-industry/ (accessed 2026-10-23, via search summary; the kit article's date was not seen).
- Repo agents drive vehicles fitted with mobile ALPR cameras, including stealth set-ups, scan plates against hit lists of cars up for repossession, visually confirm a match before marking a car located (one alert in the reporting was rejected because the vehicle did not match), and work the owner's home, workplace and credit-report addresses; every scan goes into DRN's database with time, date and location; DRN has 1,000+ clients, mainly asset protection and repossession — Jalopnik, 9 August 2026, https://jalopnik.com/2231912/repossessions-rise-license-plate-readers-used-for-repo (accessed 2026-10-23).
- OPENLANE, then an independent online auction company, bought Recovery Database Network in 2010 and ran it as a subsidiary; KAR, which then acquired OPENLANE, also owned the forwarder PAR North America, which competes with many RDN agency customers; KAR later combined RDN with its Clearplan agent app in one unit — https://www.autoremarketing.com/ar/openlane-acquires-recovery-database-network, https://www.autoremarketing.com/ar/openlane-ceo-writes-letter-regarding-kar-deal and https://curepossession.com/kar-strengthens-asset-recovery-capabilities-with-consolidated-management-team/ (accessed 2026-10-23, via search summary).
- RDN describes itself as used by 600+ national lenders and 2,500+ service providers to assign cases; Clearplan is the agent field app replacing paper run-lists, integrated with RDN and DRN; Cleardata handles personal property processing, transport scheduling and debtor appointments; RDN's On-Hook Status shows agents green, yellow or red for proceed, hold or cancel — https://recoverydatabase.net/, https://clearplan.io/, https://recoverydatabase.net/cleardata and https://corporate.openlane.com/recovery-database-network-launches-industry-first-all-in-one-solution-for-tracking-repossession-status/ (accessed 2026-10-23, via search summary; the RDN page returned 403 to a direct read).
- OPENLANE, Inc. FY2025 10-K: "We provide end-to-end management of the remarketing process for repossession customers" and "We also operate a proprietary digital platform for repossession management that helps repossession companies"; ~1.5M annual vehicle transactions, $28.8B GMV, ~4,800 employees at 31 December 2025 — https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1395942/000139594226000006/opln-20251231.htm (accessed 2026-10-23; first 100,000 characters read).
- ALS Resolvion and Del Mar Recovery Solutions merged in 2019 into one of the largest repossession management firms, reporting 200+ clients including 40+ of the top 50 auto finance companies and ~1M assignments a year — https://www.autoremarketing.com/subprime/als-resolvion-and-del-mar-recovery-solutions-announce-merger (accessed 2026-10-23, via search summary; self-reported figures).
- DRN terminated its relationships with four national forwarders: Primeritus, Burns National, Victory Recovery Services and Millennium Capital and Recovery — https://curepossession.com/hot-topics/drn-cuts-four-repossession-forwarders/ (accessed 2026-10-23, via search summary; undated). National Finance Adjusters was described in 2013 as the largest industry-owned forwarding cooperative; Relliance is an agency-owned forwarder — https://curepossession.com/largest-industry-owned-forwarding-and-facilitation-cooperative-hires-director-of-business-development/ and https://www.autoremarketing.com/ar/relliance-aims-be-forwarding-company-benefits-lenders-repo-agents (accessed 2026-10-23, via search summary).
- MVTRAC connects lenders with recovery agents through LPR, with fixed and mobile cameras, and integrated its LPR into Consolidated Asset Recovery Systems' IBEAM portal — https://autoremarketing.com/ar/consolidated-asset-recovery-systems-partners-with-mvtrac and https://www.cnbc.com/id/100899311 (accessed 2026-10-23, via search summary; mostly older reporting).
- UCC 9-609: after default a secured party may take possession of collateral "without judicial process, if it proceeds without breach of the peace" — https://www.law.cornell.edu/ucc/9/9-609 (accessed 2026-10-23). Case-law summaries: a debtor's objection can be enough to require the agent to stop (Illinois summary; Indiana Horizon Bank v. Huizar) — https://www.stites.com/resources/creditors-sidebar/knowing-when-to-walk-away/ (accessed 2026-10-23, via search summary).
- California Collateral Recovery Act guidance from the Bureau of Security and Investigative Services: a licensee must notify local law enforcement within one hour of a repossession and in writing within one business day; must inventory personal effects not removed at the scene and store them for at least 60 days; a 2015 bill analysis describes the inventory reaching the debtor within 48 hours of recovery — https://www.bsis.ca.gov/about_us/agendas/repo_guide_draft.pdf, https://www.bsis.ca.gov/about_us/agendas/20160707_8.pdf and https://www.leginfo.ca.gov/pub/15-16/bill/asm/ab_0251-0300/ab_281_bill_20150817_amended_sen_v95.htm (accessed 2026-10-23, via search summary; the current Business and Professions Code text returned 403, so hour and day figures are written as BSIS guidance).
- Florida Chapter 493: anyone performing recovery-agent services needs a Class "E" licence and must work through a Class "R" agency (one location) or Class "RR" branch; the Department of Agriculture and Consumer Services, Division of Licensing regulates; failing to safeguard and inventory personal property is a prohibited act — https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/493.6401 and https://www.fdle.state.fl.us/Media/BPDtraining/PropertyRepossessionProcesses/story_content/external_files/Recovery%20Agent%20Handbook.pdf (accessed 2026-10-23, via search summary).
- The Illinois Commerce Commission enforces the Illinois Collateral Recovery Act and approved RISC's Illinois edition of the Certified Asset Recovery Specialist (CARS) certification; RISC and Hudson Cook update CARS annually; a 2020 RISC release cited a survey finding 60% of agencies hold CARS certification; RISC also runs repossession lot inspections — https://autoremarketing.com/?p=17065, https://www.autoremarketing.com/subprime/3-elements-cars-program-update-risc-and-hudson-cook and https://www.riscus.com/category/news/page/4/ (accessed 2026-10-23, via search summary; RISC's own claims).
- CFPB Supervisory Highlights released 2 May 2022 (exams in the second half of 2021): auto servicers repossessed vehicles "even after consumers took intentional actions to prevent repossessions" — https://www.consumerfinance.gov/archive/newsroom/cfpb-supervisory-report-finds-unlawful-auto-repossessions-breakdowns-in-credit-report-disputes/ (accessed 2026-10-23). Fall 2016 Supervisory Highlights: refusing to return personal property until a fee is paid was unfair — https://www.consumerfinancemonitor.com/2016/11/07/cfpb-issues-fall-2016-supervisory-highlights/ (accessed 2026-10-23, via search summary).
- CFPB order against Wells Fargo, 20 December 2022: $3.7B in total, more than $1.3B of redress on auto lending accounts across 11M+ accounts, including wrongful repossessions; customers whose cars were wrongfully repossessed were to receive a base $4,000 each — https://www.consumerfinance.gov/enforcement/actions/wells-fargo-bank-na-2022/ and https://www.npr.org/2022/12/20/1144331954/wells-fargo-billions-wrongful-fees-settlement (accessed 2026-10-23, via search summary).
- Repo agents shot during recoveries in 2025, including Horn Lake MS (June), Arlington TX (October) and Milwaukee WI (December); no national count of repossession injuries or deaths exists — https://curepossession.com/texas-repo-agent-shot-by-borrower/ and https://curepossession.com/manhunt-underway-for-repo-shooter/ (accessed 2026-10-23, via search summary).
- A CURepossession editorial: a $350 recovery fee in January 2000 would be ~$576 in November 2021 on the BLS CPI calculator, and Florida agency owners were demanding $600 per recovery — https://curepossession.com/?p=22627 (accessed 2026-10-23, via search summary; advocacy piece).
- DRN and Vigilant sued Utah in 2014 over a law restricting private ALPR use (Utah later amended its law to allow private use); their Arkansas challenge was dismissed for lack of standing — https://www.ksl.com/article/news/entertainment/entertainment-news/license-reader-firm-says-new-law-curbs-free-speech/28704509 and https://www.arkansasonline.com/news/2014/jun/08/car-tag-data-law-unjust-suit-says-20140/ (accessed 2026-10-23, via search summary).
- CURepossession notes that RDN covers an estimated ~90% of all repossession assignments (the same estimate the vault's sales-finance niche uses) and that Cox Automotive's figures run ~49% below RDN-derived repossession volume — https://curepossession.com/rdn-repossession-volume-report-q2-2025/ and https://curepossession.com/repossession-assignment-volume-updated-2008-2024/ (accessed 2026-10-23, via search summary; a trade estimate, written with "~").
- DRN says it has the largest LPR database in the US; MVTRAC's site cites access to DRN data — https://www.cnbc.com/id/100899311 and https://autoremarketing.com/ar/consolidated-asset-recovery-systems-partners-with-mvtrac (accessed 2026-10-23, via search summary).
- DRN completed an integration with the agency management system iRepo; a low-cost phone-based LPR service (Up4Repo) is marketed against "$15,000 cameras" — https://www.autoremarketing.com/ar/digital-recognition-network-completes-integration-irepo/ and https://curepossession.com/up4repo-the-new-low-cost-lpr-solution/ (accessed 2026-10-23, via search summary; dates not seen).

## Estimates (not checked)
- ~$1.5–2.5B of total recovery-agent revenue, counting agencies filed under towing (488410) and nonemployer operators — derived: Census receipts of $1.15B for the coded firms, RDN's 2,500+ service providers against 575 coded employer firms, and ~1.7M+ recoveries a year at an assumed ~$400 average recovery fee plus storage, transport, skip and other fees.
- Flat involuntary auto recovery fee of ~$350–450 — derived from the CURepossession fee editorial above and judgement; fees vary by client, market and forwarder and were not surveyed.
- "The fee has barely moved since 2000" — from the CURepossession advocacy editorial above; not an independent measure.
- Camera-car drivers commonly paid per hit or per recovery rather than hourly — judgement, not surveyed; no worker forum source was found.
- Personal property handling time of ~30–45 minutes per car; several hours a night of office update and invoice work at a multi-truck agency; well over a hundred updates a night at a four-truck agency; a few thousand attempts a year at a three-truck agency; hundreds of thousands of scans and a few hundred confirmed hits a month for four camera cars — judgement, not measured.
- Agents can be named alongside the lender in wrongful-repossession suits — recalled from case reporting; not checked for a specific case.
- Most states require notice to the borrower before disposition of repossessed consumer collateral (UCC 9-611 and 9-614 as enacted, plus state retail installment sales acts), and a defective notice can bar or reduce a deficiency — recalled rule; the vault's sales-finance niche states the same.
- CFPB service-provider bulletins (2012, reissued 2016) driving lenders' vendor management of repossession agencies — recalled; described in the vault only as "lenders' vendor-management programmes".
- California SB 34 (2015) privacy-policy and usage requirements for ALPR operators; federal Driver's Privacy Protection Act limits on plate-to-owner lookups — recalled; the vault refers only to "state limits on private plate readers and federal rules on plate-to-owner lookups".
- Vendor landscape not individually checked: SafetyCulture and CompanyCam (inspection apps); ServiceTitan, Salesforce Field Service, Workiz and OptimoRoute (field service and routing); Venminder and ProcessUnity (third-party risk); Samsara and Verizon Connect (fleet telematics); Jobber and Housecall Pro (client portals); PassTime and Spireon (GPS payment-assurance devices; named in the vault's BHPH niche); TOPS and Towbook (towing software; named in the vault's towing hub); Manheim and ACV (auctions).

- Every Pass 2 insight-function headcount (DRN data staff, forwarder analysts, device-vendor and platform data teams) — judgement from firm size and structure, not counted.
- Pass 2 scores rely on judgement against `_direction.md` and comparable pockets: collections-agencies identity-skip-trace-data-providers (54) for the LPR network's Q1 and Q3 (its Q4 of 3 was scored under older rules; the LPR network's on-demand clock is scored 2 per current rest rulings); collections-agencies creditor-recovery-analytics (38) and rmai-certification-programs (34); towing-companies towing-dispatch-software-analytics (36, kill switch) for the platform pockets; pawnshops transaction-reporting-databases (41, kill switch).

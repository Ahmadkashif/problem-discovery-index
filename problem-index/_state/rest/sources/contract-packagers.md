# Sources — Contract Packagers

## Checked
- NAICS 561910 Packaging and Labeling Services, 2022: 1,629 firms, 1,853 establishments, 49,620 employees, $2,623.0M annual payroll and $10,718.9M receipts; 43 enterprises with 500+ employees run 141 establishments in the code with 21,372 employees and $4,057.0M receipts (~38% of receipts); 1,586 firms have fewer than 500 employees ($6,661.9M receipts); 1,231 firms have fewer than 20 employees (~76% of firms) with 5,306 employees and $1,753.0M receipts; 791 firms have fewer than 5 employees; 268 firms have 20–99 employees ($2,237.1M) and 87 have 100–499 ($2,671.7M); California has 329 firms and $1,100.1M receipts — Census Bureau, Statistics of U.S. Businesses 2022, U.S. & states 6-digit NAICS table (released 2025-04-10), https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/susb/tables/2022/us_state_6digitnaics_2022.xlsx via https://www.census.gov/data/tables/2022/econ/susb/2022-susb-annual.html (accessed 2026-10-23). Cells carry Census noise flags (payroll for 500+ enterprises flagged H).
- NAICS 561910, 2024 annual average (private ownership): 3,033 establishments, 63,819 employees, total wages $3,794.6M, average annual pay $59,459 (average weekly wage $1,143); 2023: 3,019 establishments, 64,715 employees, average pay $56,203 (employment −1.4% in 2024). Largest states by 2024 employment: Illinois 9,972, California 7,819, Pennsylvania 6,295, New Jersey 5,100, Ohio 2,977, Tennessee 2,812 — BLS Quarterly Census of Employment and Wages, https://data.bls.gov/cew/data/api/2024/a/industry/561910.csv and https://data.bls.gov/cew/data/api/2023/a/industry/561910.csv (accessed 2026-10-23). QCEW counts establishments and employment differently from SUSB (single-unit and administrative-record scope), so the vault quotes both with their years.
- NAICS 561910 definition: establishments primarily engaged in packaging client-owned materials, which may include labeling or imprinting the package; illustrative activities include kit assembling and packaging, blister and shrink wrapping, and labeling. Cross-references: processing client-owned materials into a different product (e.g. mixing water and concentrate) is Manufacturing; aerosol packaging is 325998; packing and crating incidental to transportation is 488991; warehousing plus packaging or other logistics services is 4931; packing agricultural products is 115114 — https://www.naics.com/naics-code-description/?code=561910 (accessed 2026-10-23; census.gov returned 403).
- PMMI, "Contract Packaging & Manufacturing: Drivers of Machinery Investments" (7 October 2024): 75% of interviewed contract packagers/manufacturers expect year-on-year growth over three years; 87% plan to install new equipment within three years, citing flexibility (78%), expedited changeover (53%) and simpler operation (46%); 67% of brand owners plan to keep or increase use of contract services; 11% use a contract provider as their entire production operation; brand owners outsource for machinery or formats not in-house (59%), testing new products or packaging (48%), customised products and short or limited runs (48%), supplementing capacity during surges (43%) and e-commerce (7%); challenges named as labour shortages, timely supply and machinery delivery, and managing growth, throughput and scheduling — https://www.pmmi.org/news/brand-owners-rely-on-contract-packaging-contract-manufacturing (accessed 2026-10-23). Survey of 157 companies per trade-press coverage — https://www.packagingstrategies.com/articles/105057-pmmi-report-brand-owners-rely-on-contract-packaging-contract-manufacturing (accessed 2026-10-23, via search summary).
- Industrial Packaging (confectionery co-packer) publishes capacity of 1.5 million multipacks, 405,000 kits and 7,000+ retail displays a week; typical prices of $0.75–1.25 per multipack or club pack, $0.40–0.60 per kit or gift set and $30–50 per retail display build, "fully loaded" and held six months; ramp to full production 2.5 weeks after materials arrive and ~10 business days standard turnaround; SQF Level 2 and AIB certified; says Walmart, Costco and Target require food safety certification before approving a co-packer; holiday planning starts 4–6 months ahead (Valentine's Day in October, Easter in December) — https://www.industrialpackaging.com/industries/confectionery-contract-packaging (accessed 2026-10-23). Vendor self-description; the vault quotes it as one firm's published prices.
- Co-packing quotes are usually several pricing buckets presented as one unit price; hourly or line-time pricing is protected with not-to-exceed caps and throughput bands (a rate that assumes so many units per hour, with rules when output falls outside the band) — https://msl-indy.com/co-packing-pricing/ (accessed 2026-10-23, via search summary; vendor guide).
- Nulogy markets Shop Floor as co-pack software digitising co-pack and 3PL production and warehouse workflows, says it has worked with hundreds of contract packaging sites over 20+ years, and names co-packer customers including Summit Packaging Solutions, Bell-Carter Packaging, BCI Packaging and MSI Express; supplier collaboration connects co-packers and brands on shared data — https://nulogy.com/the-complete-guide-to-co-pack-software/ (accessed 2026-10-23).
- Food co-packer ERPs: NutraSoft notes lots belong to the plant's inventory rather than separate client-owned stock pools; Deacom (ECI) is marketed to co-packers and contract manufacturers; MRPeasy supports issuing components to a co-packer and receiving finished goods back — https://www.nutrasoft.ca/industries/co-packers-contract-manufacturing, https://www.ecisolutions.com/industries/manufacturing/erp-software-for-contract-manufacturing/, https://www.mrpeasy.com/blog/co-packing-contract-packing/ (accessed 2026-10-23, via search summary).
- 21 CFR 211.130(e): inspection of packaging and labeling facilities immediately before use to assure all drug products and unsuitable packaging and labeling materials from previous operations have been removed, with results documented in the batch production record; 211.130 also requires written procedures for physical or spatial separation, identification of unlabeled filled containers, lot numbering and examination of packaging and labeling materials before use — https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/21/211.130 (accessed 2026-10-23).
- DSCSA enhanced drug distribution security: FDA's exemption for eligible manufacturers and repackagers ended 27 May 2025, for wholesale distributors 27 August 2025 and for dispensers with 26 or more full-time pharmacists and technicians 27 November 2025 — https://supplychainwizard.com/dscsa-deadline-map-by-trading-partner-type/ (accessed 2026-10-23). The small-dispenser date conflicts across sources (27 November 2026 vs 2027 after an August 2026 FDA letter), so the vault does not cite it.
- PCI Pharma Services: Bain Capital made a strategic investment alongside lead investor Kohlberg, with Mubadala and Partners Group remaining significant minority investors (14 July 2025); PCI describes "integrated end-to-end drug development, manufacturing and packaging capabilities"; terms undisclosed — https://www.kirkland.com/news/press-release/2025/07/kirkland-advises-bain-on-strategic-investment-in-pci-pharma-services (accessed 2026-10-23). A ~$10B valuation is reported by Fierce Pharma citing The Wall Street Journal — https://www.fiercepharma.com/manufacturing/pci-cdmo-growth-journey-nets-sizable-investment-private-equity-heavyweights (search summary only; page returned 403), so the vault writes it as "reported".
- Alcami agreed to acquire Tjoapack, a contract packaging organisation majority-owned by Ampersand Capital Partners, with ~400 staff and 23 packaging lines in Clinton, Tennessee and Etten-Leur, the Netherlands, packaging oral solid dose (blisters, bottles), sterile injectables (vials, prefilled syringes, auto-injectors) and kits; announced April 2026, closing expected Q2 2026 — https://www.alcami.com/news/alcami-to-acquire-contract-packaging-organization-tjoapack/ (accessed 2026-10-23).
- Nonantum Capital Partners bought contract packaging and manufacturing company MSI Express from HCI Equity Partners (March 2025); Prometheus Retail Solutions bought PackagingARTS' value-added packaging and retail display division (August 2025) — https://www.privsource.com/acquisitions/packaging/2025 (accessed 2026-10-23, via search summary).
- The Contract Packaging Association entered a strategic partnership under which PMMI provides administrative, technical and marketing support, effective 1 July 2018 — https://www.pmmi.org/news/contract-packaging-association-announces-new-strategic-partnership-with-pmmi (accessed 2026-10-23, via search summary). - FDA recall records FY2013–FY2025: 24,761 product recall records from 4,649 firms; microbiological hazards 46.2% (11,443) and allergens 27.1% (6,717); at USDA FSIS allergens were 38.6% of recall events (482 of 1,248) against 23.0% microbiological — Foods 15(17):3115 (2026), https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13565260 (accessed 2026-10-23). The paper codes mispackaging under "other/quality", so the vault does not attribute a share of allergen recalls to wrong-package errors.
- Country-of-origin marking: 19 U.S.C. 1304 imposes a 10% marking duty on articles not legally marked unless exported, destroyed or marked under Customs supervision before liquidation; under 19 CFR 134 the port director notifies the importer on CBP Form 4647 to mark, and may demand redelivery of released goods; remarking must be done under Customs supervision to cancel the duty (HQ 735472); defacing an origin mark is a separate offence — https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2016-title19-vol1/pdf/CFR-2016-title19-vol1-part134.pdf, https://rulings.cbp.gov/ruling/735472, https://codes.findlaw.com/us/title-19-customs-duties/19-usc-sect-1304/ (accessed 2026-10-23, via search summary). A 30-day cure window cited by one guide was not confirmed and is not used.
- Walmart OTIF: suppliers are charged ~3% of the cost of goods on non-compliant cases; thresholds were reset on 1 February 2024 (widely reported as 90% on-time and 95% in-full for prepaid, 98% on-time for collect) — https://ziplinelogistics.com/blog/shipping-to-walmart-best-practices-for-cpg-brands/ and https://www.8thandwalton.com/blog/walmart-otif (accessed 2026-10-23, via search summary). Sources disagree on current thresholds, so the vault cites only the ~3% charge.
- Club-store display and kitting co-packers advertise building PDQ and floor displays and club packs to Costco, Sam's Club, BJ's and Walmart specifications (e.g. Cubit Packaging, ActionPak, Assemblies Unlimited, DeLine Box & Display, whose minimum runs start around 300 units) — https://www.cubitpackaging.com/contract-packaging, https://www.actionpakinc.com/contract-packaging-services/club-multi-packaging/, https://www.assemblies.com/club-store-packaging/, https://www.delinebox.com/projects/club-store-packaging/ (accessed 2026-10-23, via search summary).
- TraceLink: a November 2025 release reports 279,800 companies, including 124,000+ dispensers, able to access DSCSA transaction data on its network; company and third-party materials cite ~290,000 network members — https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/tracelink-customers-demonstrate-readiness-as-dscsa-dispenser-deadline-arrives-302610196.html and https://rxalmanac.com/vendor/tracelink/ (accessed 2026-10-23, via search summary).

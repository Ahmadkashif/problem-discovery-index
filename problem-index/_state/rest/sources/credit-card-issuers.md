# Sources — Credit Card Issuers

## Checked
- NAICS 522210 Credit Card Issuing, 2022: 140 firms, 698 establishments, 66,791 employees, $9.02B annual payroll and $142.1B receipts; 29 firms with 500+ employees run 585 establishments, employ 65,641 people and take $140.9B (99%) of receipts; 90 firms have fewer than 5 employees — Census Bureau, Statistics of U.S. Businesses 2022, U.S. & states 6-digit NAICS table (released 2025-04-10), https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/susb/tables/2022/us_state_6digitnaics_2022.xlsx via https://www.census.gov/data/tables/2022/econ/susb/2022-susb-annual.html (accessed 2026-10-22). Delaware alone: 11 firms, 3,026 employees, $18.1B receipts.
- NAICS 522210, 2024 annual average (private ownership): 1,034 establishments, 65,357 employees, average annual pay $140,745 — BLS Quarterly Census of Employment and Wages, https://data.bls.gov/cew/data/api/2024/a/industry/522210.csv (accessed 2026-10-22). Note: most card lending by universal banks (JPMorgan Chase, Citi, Bank of America, Wells Fargo) is booked in banks classified to 522110, so 522210 understates the activity.
- US credit card balances $1.263T in Q2 2026, up $21B on the quarter and $54B on the year; flow into serious (90+ day) card delinquency 6.97% (annualised) versus 6.93% a year earlier — Federal Reserve Bank of New York, Household Debt and Credit Report press release, 11 August 2026, https://www.newyorkfed.org/newsevents/news/research/2026/20260811 (accessed 2026-10-22).
- CFPB seventh biennial Consumer Credit Card Market Report (published 30 December 2025, covering 2024): consumers were assessed $160B in interest in 2024, up from $105B in 2022; average APR 25.2% on general-purpose cards and 31.3% on private-label; cardholders disputed $9.8B of charges, producing $5.9B of chargebacks; total credit lines over $5.7T; new account originations fell 19% to 89M; ~800M accounts; 78% of adults hold a card; about 3,700 institutions reported card balances in 2024, down from 4,700 ten years earlier; large issuers hold ~84% of balances; credit unions $85.7B (~7%); rewards cards are 92% of general-purpose purchase volume; purchase volume of the 14 largest issuers $3.6T; 0% introductory APR promotions carried $899B (one-third) of purchase volume; minimum-only payers 15% (general purpose) and 20% (private label); delinquencies ~3% and charge-offs ~3.8% at year-end 2024; issuer daily call caps of 1–4 per account — https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2026/01/Trends-and-Takeaways-from-the-2025-Credit-CARD-Act-Report and https://public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2026-00081.pdf (accessed 2026-10-22, figures via the Orrick summary and search extracts).
- Reg Z billing-error resolution, 12 CFR 1026.13: consumer notice within 60 days of the first statement showing the error; creditor acknowledgment within 30 days; resolution within two complete billing cycles and no later than 90 days; no collection of, or adverse reporting on, the disputed amount while pending — CFPB, https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1026/13/ (accessed 2026-10-22).
- CFPB's $8 late-fee safe harbour rule (for issuers with 1M+ open accounts, ~95% of balances) was vacated on 15 April 2025 by consent judgment in the Northern District of Texas (Chamber of Commerce et al. v. CFPB), after a May 2024 preliminary injunction — https://www.consumerfinancemonitor.com/2025/04/16/federal-judge-voids-cfpb-credit-card-late-fee-rule/ and https://ktslaw.com/kilpatrick/blog/consumer-financial-services/2025/5/cfpb-credit-card-late-fee-rule-vacated-by-banking-trade-group-lawsuit (accessed 2026-10-22).
- President Trump called for a one-year 10% cap on card APRs from 20 January 2026; Bank of America and Citi were reported weighing 10% cards; no enacted cap or executive order was found in later coverage — https://www.npr.org/2026/01/12/nx-s1-5675151/trump-credit-card-interest-rate-cap and https://www.axios.com/2026/01/22/bank-of-america-credit-card-trump (accessed 2026-10-22).

- Delta's 2025 remuneration from American Express was $8.2B, up 11%; the co-brand agreement runs to 2029; Delta guided to ~$9B for 2026 — https://www.altexsoft.com/travel-industry-news/premium-strategy-works-deltas-2025-revenue-hits-63b/ and https://dc.fortune.com/2026/04/03/how-did-delta-ceo-ed-bastian-build-partnership-with-american-express-10-percent-airlines-revenue (accessed 2026-10-22, via search summary).

- TransUnion: lender exposure to suspected synthetic identities across US auto loans, credit cards, retail cards and unsecured personal loans was $3.3B at end-2024 (H1 2025 State of Omnichannel Fraud report); bankcard exposure ~$1B in H1 2024 — https://www.transunion.com/blog/synthetic-identity-fraud-part-one (accessed 2026-10-22, via search extract).
- Bust-out pattern: a warm-up period of genuine payments, then a burst of charges paid with bad checks or balance-transfer checks, often round-robin; "cardholder bust-out schemes are often not identified as fraud by payment card issuers because in many cases the losses are handled by a financial institution's collections department" — US patent 7,428,509, https://patents.google.com/patent/US7428509; some banks record synthetic-identity losses as credit losses and others as fraud — https://plaid.com/resources/fraud/synthetic-identity-fraud/ (accessed 2026-10-22).

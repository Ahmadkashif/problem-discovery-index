# Sources — Commercial Photographers

## Checked
- US commercial photography (NAICS 541922) employer firms reported ~$2.85B total revenue in 2022 (Census Service Annual Survey, series REVEF541922ALLEST: 2017 2,131; 2018 2,310; 2019 2,250; 2020 1,867; 2021 2,266; 2022 2,849, $M) — https://fred.stlouisfed.org/data/REVEF541922ALLEST (accessed 2026-10-22)
- ~5,021 employer establishments and ~13,488 employees in County Business Patterns 2023, and a ~$2.6B "market size" from the 2022 Economic Census (third-party page citing Census; the vault gives these as approximate) — https://vantainsights.com/industry/541922-commercial-photography (accessed 2026-10-22)
- Census Nonemployer Statistics 2022 for NAICS 54192 (portrait 541921 and commercial 541922 combined): 231,865 nonemployer businesses with $6.81B in receipts; no portrait/commercial split published. The same page gives CBP 2022 employer counts of 11,115 portrait studios and 4,934 commercial photography firms (third-party page citing Census; the same figure is logged in the portrait-photography-studios source log) — https://www.capturedcelebrations.com/photo-booth-business-statistics (accessed 2026-10-22)
- NAICS 541922 covers establishments primarily engaged in commercial photography, generally for advertising agencies, publishers and other business and industrial users — https://www.ibisworld.com/classifications/naics/541922/commercial-photography/ and https://www.worldoftaxonomy.com/system/naics_2022/node/541922 (accessed 2026-10-22)
- BLS: 145,000 photographer jobs in 2025; 67% self-employed; 17% in photographic services; median pay $44,660 ($21.47 an hour, May 2025); photographic-services median $18.84 an hour; employment projected to fall 1% from 2025 to 2035, with ~10,700 openings a year; commercial and industrial photographers shoot buildings, models, merchandise, artifacts and landscapes, usually on location — https://www.bls.gov/ooh/media-and-communication/photographers.htm (accessed 2026-10-22)
- Amazon main images: pure white background (RGB 255,255,255), product filling at least 85% of the frame, no added text, logos or props; 1,000 px or more on the longest side enables zoom (Seller Central forum posts and third-party guides, not Amazon's own guide; sources disagree on recommended size, 1,000 vs 1,600 px) — https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/13af96ea-6b07-4bf9-8dbe-a13292c2e3b1 and https://www.diyphotography.net/?p=308935 (accessed 2026-10-22)
- Copyright Claims Board: awards capped at $30,000 per proceeding, up to $15,000 per timely registered work and $7,500 per unregistered work; "timely" generally means registered before the infringement began or within three months of first publication — https://beancount.io/blog/2026/10/08/copyright-claims-board-small-claims-online-guide and https://petapixel.com/2020/12/28/copyright-small-claims-has-arrived-trump-signs-case-act-into-law (accessed 2026-10-22)
- CCB key statistics through March 2026: pictorial, graphic and sculptural works were ~36% of ~1,920 claims filed (39% of 989 claims in October 2024); most claims were dismissed, often for non-compliance or failure to serve, and only ~43–47 reached a final determination (secondary summaries; the CCB's chart PDF could not be read as text) — https://www.plagiarismtoday.com/2024/10/22/copyright-claims-board-releases-key-statistics/ and https://www.ccb.gov/CCB-Statistics-and-FAQs-April-2026.pdf and https://www.bricker.com/insights/publications/what-does-the-report-card-from-the-copyright-claims-board-tell-us (accessed 2026-10-22)
- Group registration of published photographs is limited to 750 images per application (since February 2018); fee described as ~$55 in 2018 (fee not re-checked against the current schedule) — https://www.asmp.org/strictly-business-blog/point-of-view-small-claims-from-a-photographers-perspective/ (accessed 2026-10-22)
- Pixsy's Licensing Resolution program takes 50% of the total amount recovered, charges nothing unless a case resolves, and may decline matches; litigated US cases are 50% net of costs; Copytrack and ImageRights run the same track-and-pursue model; Copytrack works "no win, no fee" and signed an exclusive enforcement deal over WENN's 10M-image archive; PicRights runs enforcement for news agencies including AAP — https://support.pixsy.com/en/articles/83-what-is-the-resolution-fee-structure-in-pixsy-s-licensing-resolution-program and https://insideimaging.com.au/2019/pixsy-in-australia/ and https://cepic.org/news/copytrack-announces-exclusive-infringement-rights-deal-with-news-content-provider-wenn-media-group and https://www.aap.com.au/legal/ (accessed 2026-10-22)
- Soona lists product photos from $39 each and video clips from $93; Squareshot prices per image (from ~$50 for apparel or beauty, ~$95 for jewellery) including retouching and an unlimited usage licence, with a $300 minimum for non-members (vendor pages; figures vary between pages) — https://soona.co/blog/product-photography-pricing and https://builtin.com/articles/soona-photo-studio-raises-35m-series-b-hiring and https://squareshot.com/post/product-photography-pricing-get-the-best-value-for-your-budget (accessed 2026-10-22)
- Creative Force is a studio workflow platform for high-volume ecommerce production (production planning, sample management, on-set capture, post-production, delivery; integrates with Capture One), founded 2019 in Denmark, customers include Columbia Sportswear, PVH, Bestseller, boohoo and OTTO; listed from ~$2,900 a month plus ~$295 per additional full-time user — https://www.creativeforce.io/about-creative-force and https://trustradius.com/products/creative-force/reviews (accessed 2026-10-22)
- Pixelz retouching: per-image pricing from ~$1.45 (under 150 images a month, 48-hour turnaround) and ~$0.95 (over 150 a month, next-morning turnaround), enterprise turnaround from 3 hours; AI-assisted background removal with human editors, clipping paths, colour matching, ghost-mannequin removal — https://pixelz.com/sem/retouching-services and https://us.fitgap.com/products/049373/pixelz (accessed 2026-10-22)
- Usage fees sit on top of the creative fee and scale with media, territory, duration, print volume and exclusivity — https://fstoppers.com/business/stop-giving-away-your-images-simple-guide-usage-fees-718341 (accessed 2026-10-22)
- Agency triple bids: clients sometimes withhold budgets because of a triple bid; one account describes an agency policy that estimates above ~$25,000 must be compared across three vendors and run past a cost consultant; estimates should state assumptions on image count, media, territory and duration — https://wonderfulmachine.com/article/pricing-calculator-for-commercial-photographers/ and https://fstoppers.com/the-anatomy-of-a-100k-commercial-photography-estimate (accessed 2026-10-22)
- Architectural photography cost-sharing: one photographer licenses a project to several parties (architect, contractor, manufacturers); Esto reportedly adds ~25% per extra party; one firm claims savings up to 60% per party; ownership and use of project photography is described as contentious — https://www.architectmagazine.com/?p=199624 and https://apalmanac.com/business/how-to-make-more-money-with-cost-sharing-6005 and https://aliharperphotography.mypixieset.com/costsharing/ (accessed 2026-10-22)
- Returns tied to images: Royal Mail (2016) 12% of UK online shoppers had returned items that looked different from the pictures; KPMG Germany (2025) 60.4% of online shoppers cite inaccurate descriptions or discrepancies from product images as a return reason (respondent shares, not shares of returns) — https://kpmg.com/de/en/media/press-releases/2025/12/retouren-als-chance-kundenerwartungen-verstehen-huerden-verringern.html and https://channelx.world/2016/05/12-of-consumers-have-made-a-return-as-it-looks-different-to-the-picture/ (accessed 2026-10-22)
- Campbell's used clear glass marbles in soup bowls for print ads (BBDO, late 1960s), the landmark deceptive food-advertising case (exact FTC date and order not retrieved) — https://www.campaignlive.co.uk/article/history-advertising-no-163-campbells-marbles/1384734 and https://www.todayifoundout.com/index.php/2019/07/that-time-campbells-put-marbles-in-their-soup/ (accessed 2026-10-22)
- Getty Images terminated its merger agreement with Shutterstock on 7 July 2026 after declining the UK CMA's required divestiture of Shutterstock's editorial business; the US DOJ had cleared the deal in February 2026 — https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001898496/000162828026055246/gety-20260630.htm (accessed 2026-10-22)
- ASMP membership given variously as 6,500+ (chapter page) and 7,000+ (Wikipedia); members include architectural, food and advertising photographers — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Society_of_Media_Photographers and https://www.asmp.org/colorado/reasons-to-join-asmp/ (accessed 2026-10-22)
- Vizit scores ecommerce product images 0–100 for predicted effectiveness with a chosen audience, using models trained on online behaviour ("Audience Lenses"), and publishes category content-effectiveness rankings built on top Amazon US listings; its scale claims (200,000 brands, 100M images) are self-reported — https://www.vizit.com/blog/audience-lenses-the-key-to-seeing-through-the-eyes-of-the-consumer and https://www.packagingdigest.com/beer-wine-spirits/ai-analyzes-alcohol-products-sold-online (accessed 2026-10-22)
- New York's statewide Freelance Isn't Free Act requires a written contract for freelance work of $800 or more (aggregated over 120 days with the same hiring party) and payment by the contract date or within 30 days of completion; effective 28 August 2024 — https://www.bsk.com/news-events-videos/new-york-enacts-statewide-ldquo-freelance-isn-rsquo-t-free-rdquo-legislation (accessed 2026-10-22)
- New York's Fashion Workers Act (effective summer 2025) requires a separate written agreement covering the creation, use, duration, scope and rate of a model's digital replica, and bars AI alteration of a model's digital replica without it — https://www.polsinelli.com/polsinelli-at-work/new-york-states-fashion-workers-act-effective-summer-2025 (accessed 2026-10-22)
- Marketplace AI imagery: vendor guides describe a hybrid workflow (real product photo as base, AI for secondary and lifestyle images) and say studios remain safer for reflective, transparent, colour-sensitive products; Amazon's main image must be a real photo of the product (vendor blogs, not independent data) — https://salesduo.com/blog/ai-vs-professional-photography-amazon/ and https://rangy.ai/blog/ai-for-ecommerce-product-photos (accessed 2026-10-22)

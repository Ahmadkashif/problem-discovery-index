# Sources — Franchised Car Dealers

## Checked
- 16,990 franchised light-vehicle dealers sold 16.2 million light-duty vehicles in 2025; total franchised light-vehicle dealership sales topped $1.3 trillion; dealerships wrote more than 276 million repair orders, with service and parts sales exceeding $164 billion (NADA Data 2025 full-year report) — https://www.nada.org/NADAData/ (accessed 2026-10-24). Mid-year 2025: 16,972 dealers, ~$645B sales, 137M repair orders, $81B service and parts (search summary of the same page family).
- Total franchised dealership employment rose to 1.13 million in 2024, about 65 employees per store; average weekly earnings $1,897 (NADA 2025 midyear report as reported by CBT News, 2025-10-23) — https://www.cbtnews.com/franchised-dealership-employment-hits-pre-pandemic-levels-in-2024-nada-reports/ (accessed 2026-10-24).
- Car Wars tracked roughly 53 million inbound service calls across its dealership network in 2025; about 19 million were missed opportunities; 53% of missed calls went to voicemail and 29% of callers hung up on hold; only about 4 million service appointments were booked (Car Wars CEO Stephane Ferri, CBT News, 2026-03-12) — https://www.cbtnews.com/?p=237778 (accessed 2026-10-24). The vault writes these as figures for Car Wars' network, not the whole industry. The builder note's source page https://carwars.com/main/resources/ai-service-playbook/ returned 403.
- The Fifth Circuit vacated the FTC's CARS Rule (Combating Auto Retail Scams) on 27 January 2025, 2-1, on procedural grounds: the FTC did not issue an advance notice of proposed rulemaking under Section 18(b) of the FTC Act; petitioners were NADA and the Texas Automobile Dealers Association — https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2025/02/fifth-circuit-strikes-down-ftcs-auto-retail-scam-rule (accessed 2026-10-24). No 2026 federal re-proposal was found (see Searches that found nothing).
- California's CARS Act (SB 766) took effect 1 October 2026 for all licensed dealers retailing or leasing light-duty vehicles under 10,000 lbs: total price in any advertisement naming a specific vehicle or financing terms and in the first written communication with a consumer; no misrepresenting add-on benefits, limits or costs; add-ons disclosed as optional verbally and in writing in at least 12-point type; no "valueless" products (examples: oil changes for EVs, non-beneficial nitrogen fills); an automatic three-business-day cancellation right on used vehicles priced at $50,000 or less, replacing the old optional two-day paid cancellation agreement; two-year retention of advertisements, pricing communications, add-on disclosures and consents and cancellation documents; enforcement through UCL (up to $2,500 per violation), FAL, CLRA, DMV and BAR — California DMV, OLIN 2026-10 — https://www.dmv.ca.gov/portal/file/26olin10-pdf/ (accessed 2026-10-24, PDF text extracted locally).
- CDK Global cyberattack: ransomware first reported 18 June 2024, a second attack 19 June, restoration from 22 June with all dealers targeted to be running by 4 July 2024; about 15,000 dealer locations across North America affected; attributed to the BlackSuit ransomware gang; Bloomberg reported a ransom demand rising from $10M to over $50M; Anderson Economic Group estimated dealership losses at more than $1 billion collectively — https://www.techtarget.com/whatis/feature/The-CDK-Global-outage-Explaining-how-it-happened (accessed 2026-10-24). Other reports put the AEG figure at ~$944M for a three-week outage (search summary, https://gmauthority.com/blog/2024/07/cdk-cyberattack-dealer-losses-estimated-at-1b).
- Kerrigan Advisors Q2 2026 Blue Sky Report: 224 transactions and 351 franchises sold in the first half of 2026; a record 462 transactions representing 724 franchises on a trailing twelve-month basis; the Kerrigan Blue Sky Index remains 78% above its 2019 level; the Hennessy sale to Group 1 Automotive set a $100 million per-dealership blue sky benchmark — https://www.kerriganadvisors.com/the-blue-sky-report/ (accessed 2026-10-24). Public groups' blended blue sky multiple of 6.0–6.4x through 2025, ending at 6.3x (search summary, same site family).
- Virginia Code § 46.2-1571: warranty and recall compensation not less than the dealer's retail rates for nonwarranty parts and labour; claims paid within 30 days of submission or rejected in writing with reasons; chargebacks for warranty parts, service and service incentives, and for sales compensation, limited to the six months after the claim, except for intentionally false or fraudulent claims — https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title46.2/chapter15/section46.2-1571/ (accessed 2026-10-24).
- Armatus Dealer Uplift describes itself as the largest provider of retail warranty reimbursement submission services; "helped over 7,500 dealers achieve 19,500+ successful warranty parts and labor rate increases"; endorsed by 25 state dealer associations; assisted drafting 21 warranty reimbursement statutes — https://www.cncda.org/?p=3240 (accessed 2026-10-24; a sponsor page, so company claims).
- J.D. Power 2025 U.S. Customer Service Index (CSI) Study, released 13 March 2025: 55,210 verified registered owners and lessees of one- to three-year-old vehicles, fielded July–December 2024 — https://media.subaru.com/newsrelease.do?id=2293 (accessed 2026-10-24). About 12% of repairs not completed correctly on the first visit, longer appointment waits than 2018–2022, and a 51-point satisfaction gap between mass-market BEV and ICE owners (search summary only; the J.D. Power and Business Wire pages returned 403).
- Kelley Blue Book September 2026: average new-vehicle transaction price $50,562; incentive spending averaged 6.6% of ATP, an average estimated incentive of $3,359 — https://www.coxautoinc.com/insights/sept-2026-atp-report/ (accessed 2026-10-24).
- J.D. Power merged with Autodata Solutions on completion of Thoma Bravo's acquisition of J.D. Power (16 December 2019); Autodata's Chrome-branded solutions include rebates and incentives, VIN decoding and description, and vehicle configuration and comparisons — https://www.thomabravo.com/press-releases/j.d.-power-to-merge-with-autodata-solutions-creating-a-leading-source-of-automotive-data-analytics-and-software-solutions (accessed 2026-10-24, fix round).
- J.D. Power's Power Information Network (PIN): dealership transaction data with ~250 data points per transaction from ~16,000 dealerships in North America, updated daily (search summary of https://jdpower.com/business/data-and-analytics, which returned 403); a 2017 release said PIN represented more than 40% of franchised retail transactions and fed J.D. Power's residual value product (search summary of https://www.jdpower.com/business/press-releases/jd-power-residual-values, 403). Agreement terms not seen.
- Urban Science is a Detroit-based automotive consultancy serving nearly every OEM, rooted in dealer network planning (Wikipedia, via search summary) — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_Science (accessed 2026-10-24).

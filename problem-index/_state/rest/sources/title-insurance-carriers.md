# Sources — Title Insurance Carriers

## Checked
- US title insurance premium volume $18.5B in 2025, up 13.8% on 2024; Q4 2025 premiums up 14.5% year over year; claims paid more than $667M in 2025 against $676M in 2024; largest individual underwriters First American Title Insurance Co. 23.1%, Fidelity National Title 14.5%, Old Republic Title 14.0%, Chicago Title 13.1%, Stewart Title 10.9%, Westcor Land Title 4.2%, Title Resources Guaranty 4.0%, Commonwealth Land Title 3.3%, WFG National Title 2.6%, First American Title Guaranty 1.5%; top states Texas $2.7B, Florida $2.01B, California $1.6B, New York $1.2B, Pennsylvania $709M; "more than 17,000 title insurance companies" counting agents — ALTA press release, 22 May 2026, https://alta.org/news-and-publications/press-release/ALTA-Reports-2025-Market-Share-and-Title-Insurance-Premium-Volume (accessed 2026-10-23). Derived: the top five individual underwriters hold 75.6%; adding the family affiliates in the top ten (Commonwealth to Fidelity, First American Title Guaranty to First American) puts the four families at ~80%.
- NAIC 2025 full-year title results: direct premiums written $18.6B (+13.5%); non-affiliated agency operations 62.7% of DPW; net retention 99.5%; 13.6M policies issued (+8.0%), 12.5M residential (92.1% of policies, 71.6% of DPW, average $1,063) and 1.1M non-residential (average $4,918, +22.1%); amounts paid to or retained by title agents 72.6% of operating expenses incurred, personnel costs 16.1%; losses and LAE incurred $807.7M (−17.4%); net loss ratio 4.4%, expense ratio 98.5%, combined ratio 102.8% (third consecutive year above 100%); net income $1.3B; policyholders' surplus $5.8B after $1.4B of dividends; per-policy direct known claim reserves $155 for direct operations, $56 for non-affiliated agents and $35 for affiliated agents; per-policy losses and LAE incurred $133 direct, $51 non-affiliated, $42 affiliated; Fitch cited commercial activity and refinances as the premium drivers — NAIC, "U.S. Property & Casualty and Title Insurance Industries – 2025 Full Year Results", pp. 13–16, https://content.naic.org/sites/default/files/2025-annual-property-and-casualty-and-title-insurance-industries-analysis-report.pdf (accessed 2026-10-23, read from the PDF text).
- NAICS 524127 Direct Title Insurance Carriers, 2022: 1,004 firms, 3,999 establishments, 46,518 employees, $5.34B annual payroll and $22.36B receipts; 24 firms with 500+ employees run 2,808 establishments, employ 38,809 people (83%) and take $20.54B (92%) of receipts; 888 firms (88%) have fewer than 20 employees and take $678M — Census Bureau, Statistics of U.S. Businesses 2022, U.S. & states 6-digit NAICS table (released 2025-04-10), https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/susb/tables/2022/us_state_6digitnaics_2022.xlsx via https://www.census.gov/data/tables/2022/econ/susb/2022-susb-annual.html (accessed 2026-10-23). The small-firm tail is mostly underwriter-affiliated and direct agencies classified as carriers, not underwriters.
- 13,390 title examiners, abstractors and searchers (SOC 23-2093) employed in insurance carriers (NAICS 5241) in May 2025 — BLS OEWS public API, series OEUN000000052410023209301, https://api.bls.gov/publicAPI/v1/timeseries/data/OEUN000000052410023209301 (accessed 2026-10-23).
- First American Financial FY2025 Form 10-K: title loss provision rate 3.0% of title premiums and escrow fees in 2025 and 2024, 3.25% in 2023; current-policy-year ultimate loss rate 3.75%; favourable prior-year development of $39.8M in 2025; "Generally, 65% to 75% of claim amounts become known in the first six years of the policy life"; title claims reserves $1,150.5M at year-end 2025, of which IBNR $1,095.9M and known claims $54.6M; a 50 basis point change in loss rates for the last six policy years moves IBNR by $162.7M; agent premiums $2,959.4M and direct premiums and escrow fees $2,347.5M; 19,102 employees (12,143 in the US); risk factors name defalcations attributable to the use of title agents, errors and fraud in funds transfers, and the McGirt decision; underwriting refers to "automated decisioning tools"; title plants described as "one of our principal assets" — SEC EDGAR, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1472787/000119312526055516/faf-20251231.htm (accessed 2026-10-23).
- Stewart: title loss expense 3.4% of title operating revenues in Q4 2025 against 3.7% a year earlier (Q3 3.0%, Q2 3.6%), with management guiding 3.5–4% for 2026 — Stewart Q4 and full-year 2025 results, https://s27.q4cdn.com/679580685/files/doc_news/Stewart-Reports-Fourth-Quarter-and-Full-Year-2025-Results-2026.pdf and earnings-call transcript https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/04/21/stewart-stc-q4-2025-earnings-call-transcript/ (accessed 2026-10-23, via search summary).
- Milliman analysis for ALTA (28 May 2024): over 127,000 claims on policies issued 2013–2022; fraud and forgery 21% of claims expense and losses, average claim over $143,000 against over $26,000 for other claims; nearly 30% of losses and claims expense came from problems not discoverable in a public-records search — ALTA, https://alta.org/news-and-publications/news/20240528-Average-Title-Insurance-Claim-Cost-for-Fraud-and-Forgery-is-143000 (accessed 2026-10-23).
- A later Milliman study for ALTA of nearly 162,000 claims (policies 2014–2023) put the average refinance fraud and forgery claim at about $207,000 including defence costs — https://www.mpamag.com/us/mortgage-industry/industry-trends/refinance-fraud-and-forgery-claims-soar-driving-up-title-insurance-losses/557031 and https://newslink.mba.org/?p=266103 (accessed 2026-10-23, via search summary).
- ALTA seller impersonation study (September 2026): of firms that saw at least one attempt in 2025, 1 in 4 paid a claim, and half of those disclosing the amount said the average payout exceeded $100,000; 59% of firms saw an attempt — NAR, 14 September 2026, https://www.nar.realtor/news/real-estate-news/6-in-10-title-insurance-firms-experienced-seller-impersonation-fraud-attempts-last-year-study-finds (accessed 2026-10-23).
- ALTA announced two endorsements offering post-policy protection against forged deeds and mortgages in August 2025 — https://alta.org/news-and-publications/press-release/American-Land-Title-Association-Announces-New-Innovations-to-Raise-the-Bar-on-Fraud-Protection (accessed 2026-10-23, via search summary).
- Texas promulgated rates: in February 2025 the commissioner ordered a 10% cut in title premiums to take effect 1 July 2025, citing a 26.6% industry profit ratio; TLTA recommended no change, OPIC 8.9–19% and TDI staff 4.9–15.4% — National Mortgage News, 21 February 2025, https://www.nationalmortgagenews.com/news/texas-mandates-the-reduction-of-title-insurance-costs (accessed 2026-10-23); TLTA appealed, arguing TDI used data only through 2022 — https://www.housingwire.com/articles/texas-orders-cut-to-title-insurance-premiums/ (accessed 2026-10-23, via search summary).
- Texas Commissioner's Order No. 2025-9697 (19 December 2025, Docket 2858) reduced basic premium rates by 6.2% effective 1 March 2026, a figure both OPIC and TLTA recommended; TDI's September 2025 rate report gave indications of −6.2% to −14.9% on experience through 2024; the hearing was held 12 December 2025 — https://www.tdi.texas.gov//orders/documents/20259697.pdf and https://tdi.texas.gov/bulletins/2025/documents/2025dechearingstdireport.pdf (accessed 2026-10-23, via search summary; direct fetch failed on DNS), corroborated by https://docsdirect.com/texas-title-insurance-premiums-decreasing-march-1-2026/ (search summary). Whether the February 2025 cut ever took effect was not established, so the vault does not say.
- TIRSA is a rate service organisation licensed by the New York Department of Financial Services under Article 23 of the Insurance Law; its manual binds members and subscribers unless the Superintendent approves a deviation; the seventh revision took effect 1 October 2024 and changed forms, not rate brackets — https://ratecalculator.fnf.com/Documents/TIRSA%20Rate%20Manual%20Appvd%20June%2010%202024%20Effective%20Oct%201%202024.pdf (accessed 2026-10-23, via search summary). TIRBOP is licensed under Section 741 of Pennsylvania's Insurance Company Law of 1921; its current manual took effect 1 April 2023 — https://www.stewart.com/content/dam/stewart/Microsites/pennsylvania/pdfs/2023-04-01-pa-rate-manual-stewart.pdf (accessed 2026-10-23, via search summary).
- Fannie Mae title acceptance pilot: on 10 July 2025 FHFA Director Pulte announced Westcor (working with X1 Analytics) as a second vendor after Doma; eligibility is certain refinances under 80% LTV from a limited group of lenders in select geographies; an automated title review decides whether title insurance or an attorney opinion letter is needed, and lenders pay Fannie a fee where risk is judged low; Fannie estimates $500–1,500 saved per loan; shares fell ~6% (First American), ~7% (FNF), ~7% (Stewart) and 2%+ (Old Republic) that day; Doma was taken private and is part of Title Resources Group — National Mortgage News, 10 July 2025, https://www.nationalmortgagenews.com/news/fannie-mae-adding-vendor-to-title-waiver-pilot-pulte (accessed 2026-10-23).
- 14 state attorneys general urged FHFA to end the pilot in July 2024 — https://alta.org/news-and-publications/news/20240723-14-Attorneys-General-Urge-FHFA-to-Terminate-Title-Waiver-Pilot (accessed 2026-10-23, via search summary).
- Fannie Mae followed Freddie Mac in allowing limited use of attorney opinion letters in place of title insurance (April 2022) — https://alta.org/news-and-publications/news/20220412-Fannie-Mae-Follows-Freddie-Mac-Guidance-to-Allow-Limited-Use-of-Title-Insurance-Alternative (accessed 2026-10-23, via search summary); Fannie later widened eligibility to condos and HOA-restricted properties and reported buying more than 10,000 loans with an AOL — https://www.biggerpockets.com/blog/?p=165286 (accessed 2026-10-23, via search summary).
- Instant underwriting: States Title (later Doma) claimed clear-to-close decisions in minutes on ~80% of refinance properties; Doma reported average premiums on instantly underwritten files of 63% (2019) to 76% (2021) of traditional premiums; WFG's DecisionPoint produces touchless refinance commitments; ANTIC's REalSearch is an automated decision engine — https://www.housingwire.com/articles/50037-states-title-transforms-closings-with-machine-intelligence/, https://doma.com/refi-loss-profiles-and-ami/, https://www.housingwire.com/articles/wfg-expands-decisionpoint-with-automated-refi-title-commitments/, https://www.mannpublications.com/mannreport/2020/06/22/antic-launches-realsearch-automated-decision-engine-for-title-agents-and-lenders/ (accessed 2026-10-23, via search summary; vendor claims).
- Underwriter liability for agent defalcation: some states' defalcation statutes make the title insurer directly liable for misdirected escrow funds; closing protection letters cover losses from the closing agent's dishonesty or failure to follow closing instructions; underwriters audit agents' escrow accounts, watching for even-dollar withdrawals and overdrafts — https://www.carltonfields.com/insights/publications/2016/defalcation-is-a-dirty-word,-but-not-the-end-of-th, https://alta.org/news-and-publications/news/20120410-Finding-Solutions-to-Protect-Escrow-Funds, https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/us-7th-circuit/1030125.html (accessed 2026-10-23, via search summary).

- Carried from the Title & Settlement Offices source log (checked there 2026-10-22, not re-fetched here): First American launched AgentNet Assist: Title Intelligence on 29 April 2026, AI review of title search packages with a human making the final determination — https://www.firstam.com/news/2026/first-am-agentnet-assist-title-intelligence-20260429.html; First American's DataTree sells ownership-and-encumbrance and chain-and-lien reports drawing on its title plant — https://dna.firstam.com/property-research.

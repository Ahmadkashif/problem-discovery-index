# Sources — Specialty Design Studios

## Checked
- Total revenue of employer firms in NAICS 54149 (Other Specialized Design Services) was $2,890M in 2022, up from $2,256M in 2021 and $1,829M in 2020 (Census Service Annual Survey, FRED series REVEF54149ALLEST, millions of dollars, not seasonally adjusted) — https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/REVEF54149ALLEST and https://fred.stlouisfed.org/graph/fredgraph.csv?id=REVEF54149ALLEST (accessed 2026-10-22)
- Fashion designers held about 32,700 jobs in 2025; 2025 median pay $80,960; job outlook 2025–35 0% (little or no change); largest employers: self-employed workers 36%, retail trade 20%, apparel, piece goods and notions merchant wholesalers 16%, management of companies 11%, apparel manufacturing 7%; designers make prototypes in cheaper fabric and then samples in production materials, and give manufacturers instructions on how to make the product (BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook) — https://www.bls.gov/ooh/arts-and-design/fashion-designers.htm (accessed 2026-10-22). Note: a search snippet of an earlier version of the same page gave 25,700 jobs in 2024; the live page fetched twice gives 32,700 for 2025, which is what the vault uses.
- NAICS 541490 covers professional design services other than architectural, landscape, engineering, interior, industrial, graphic and computer systems design; illustrative examples include fashion, textile, jewelry, shoe, fur, float and costume design (except independent theatrical costume designers). Exhibit design is not among the official illustrative examples; it is classified here by the queue boundary and by practice — https://www.ibisworld.com/classifications/naics/541490/other-specialized-design-services/ and https://www.sweetspot.so/markets/federal/naics/other-specialized-design-services-541490/ (accessed 2026-10-22)
- Apparel development averages three to five physical sample rounds per style (second proto, sealing and production samples); the first proto arrives four to six weeks after the tech pack goes to the supplier; Browzwear claims 50–80% fewer sample rounds with production-accurate 3D (vendor claim) — https://browzwear.com/blog/how-much-does-your-sampling-cost-you (article dated 2026-03-31, accessed 2026-10-22)
- An extra sample round costs $90–$300 per sample in courier, re-cut labour and materials and adds two to four weeks; "fit subjectivity is the most common cause of Round 3 and Round 4"; incomplete tech packs (missing measurements and tolerances, ambiguous construction notes, unspecified trims, prior-round comments not passed in structured form) are a top cause of re-samples; brands moving off spreadsheets report 25–40% fewer rounds (PLM vendor blog, vendor claims) — https://blog.waveplm.com/how-to-reduce-sample-rounds-in-apparel-development/ (article dated 2026-05-01, accessed 2026-10-22)
- Style3D reports manufacturers with mature 3D sampling workflows commonly cutting physical samples by around half, 70%+ in aggressive programmes (vendor claim) — https://www.style3d.com/blog/?p=17137 (accessed 2026-10-22)
- Backbone PLM lists from $199 per user per month (Capterra); positioned as lightweight PLM for growing DTC brands, with a Shopify-focused Backbone Lite tier; Centric PLM positioned for large multi-region brands; Techpacker, Onbrand, Delogue and WFX positioned for small teams — https://www.capterra.com/p/191336/backbone/ and https://style.clo-set.com/en/resources/article/d3a24e2976254e4ab816812436a4e780 and https://www.onbrandplm.com/blog/techpacker-alternatives (accessed 2026-10-22)
- CEIR survey (fielded late 2025, report "How the Exhibit Dollar Is Spent"): exhibit space accounts for 40.5% of total exhibitor spend; 52% of exhibitors plan to use their booths as is in 2026, the rest refurbishing, buying new or renting — https://www.tsnn.com/exhibitors/brand-marketer-direct-spending-in-the-b2b-exhibition-channel-part-i (published 2026-04-21, accessed 2026-10-22)
- A custom exhibit for a 30x50 booth costs $150,000+ and lasts about 20 shows or three to four years (IPVM report on manufacturer trade show costs) — https://ipvm.com/reports/trade-show-costs (accessed 2026-10-22)
- Show rules require booth fabrics to pass NFPA 701 and other construction and decoration materials NFPA 703 / UL 1975; exhibitors are told to carry flame-retardancy certificates, which exhibit houses supply, or face on-site testing by the fire marshal; McCormick Place classifies booths into formats by size and number of levels, with covered areas requiring smoke detection or sprinklers at some venues — https://www.wef.org/globalassets/assets-weftec/3---exhibit/exhibitor-service-manual/display-guidelines/mccormick-place-fire-safety-regulations.pdf and https://www.exhibitoronline.com/topics/article.asp?ID=2471 (accessed 2026-10-22)
- FTC rules require most textile and wool products to carry fiber content, country of origin and the identity of the manufacturer or responsible business (RN), with care labels required under a separate FTC rule; Textile Rules amended effective 5 May 2014 — https://business.ftc.gov/documents/bus21-threading-your-way-through-labeling-requirements-under-textile-and-wool-acts (accessed 2026-10-22)
- Fit diagnosis from drag lines is described by pattern professionals as a skill built over years; diagonal drag lines point to where more room is needed, horizontal folds to excess length, vertical folds to excess width, and the visible wrinkle can be caused by imbalance elsewhere; technical designer job ads ask for "accurate fit direction with specific pattern corrections to factories" through PLM — https://minervapatterns.com/blog/diagnosing-fit-problems-reading-the-wrinkles and https://minervapatterns.com/blog/fit-sessions-and-sample-reviews-getting-fit-right (accessed 2026-10-22)
- Color Solutions International (CSI) is a division of DyStar that creates, manages and distributes colour standards for retailers and brands; lab-dip approval is the review of a lab-dyed sample against the buyer's standard before bulk, with best practice using a third-party colour standards provider and visual assessment under multiple light sources alongside instrument data — https://ecolorworld.com/wiki/about-us/ and https://cottonworks.com/en/topics/sourcing-manufacturing/dyeing/the-lab-dip-process (accessed 2026-10-22)
- Heuritech (Paris, founded 2013) applies image recognition to social media images across 2,000+ garment components, analyses ~3 million images and videos a day, trains on ~4,000 trends over five years of history, forecasts up to a year ahead and sells on subscription to brands including Louis Vuitton, Dior, Adidas and Moncler — https://heuritech.com/articles/how-heuritech-forecasts-fashion-trends-thanks-to-artificial-intelligence/ (accessed 2026-10-22)
- Textile print designs are sold at shows "for around $400 on up" for exclusive use, with large print houses holding a design exclusive to a label for a season against a yardage commitment and the client paying screen charges (practitioner forum, anecdotal) — https://fashion-incubator.com/freelance_fabric_design_question/ (accessed 2026-10-22)

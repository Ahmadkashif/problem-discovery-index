# The 24-Hour Drill Nobody Has Run

**Niche:** [[niches/grocery-stores/store-food-safety-traceability-records/profile|Store Food-Safety & Traceability Records]]
**Industry:** [[industries/grocery-stores|Grocery Stores]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Chains are building traceability systems for a rule that asks for records within 24 hours, and few have timed whether they could produce them.
**Tags:** #evaluation-metrics #compliance #quick-win #workflow-orchestration #data-integration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep a store's temperature, sanitation and traceability records complete without clipboards, and whoever produces the highest share of complete key-data-element records for Food Traceability List receipts takes the account.

## The Problem
Records must be made available to FDA within 24 hours of a request (FDA). With enforcement deferred to 20 July 2028, chains are buying tools and running pilots. The record set touches the warehouse, wholesalers, DSD vendors and each store's receiving. Nobody owns the end-to-end test.

## Why It's Still Broken
There is no penalty before 2028. Each vendor tests its own piece, and a full drill means pulling people from stores and suppliers for a day.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A quarterly mock request: pick a listed food and a date range, start a clock, and assemble the sortable spreadsheet across sources. Record which data elements were missing, which partners were slow and which store records were on paper. Fix the top three gaps before the next drill.

## Who Feels the Pain
Food-safety directors who will answer the first real request; receivers whose paper records will be the gap; and suppliers who will be asked for lot data at short notice.

## Impact If Fixed
A drill that finds a chain at ~40% complete key data elements a year before the deadline, rather than during an outbreak, gives it four quarters to reach ~95% (estimate).

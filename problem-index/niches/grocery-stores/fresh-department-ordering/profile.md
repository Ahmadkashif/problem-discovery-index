# Fresh Department Ordering

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/grocery-stores|Grocery Stores]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to set each fresh item's store order before the truck is cut, and whoever cuts the most shrink at an unchanged in-stock rate takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$900M a year of US spend on fresh forecasting, computer-assisted ordering and related services at supermarkets (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~0.1% of grocery sales (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — the largest chains have deployed AI ordering in some fresh departments; many still order from handhelds against last week's sales
**Target Buyer:** VP of fresh merchandising or replenishment at a chain
**Automation Potential:** High — the order is a daily, structured decision with a fast, measurable outcome

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The contest is decided before the product arrives. The vendor's forecast and the manager's override meet in one number per item per delivery, and the outcome shows within days as shrink and empty shelves. Chains buy by running a pilot: a set of test stores on the vendor's orders, matched control stores on the old process, and a comparison of shrink and in-stock after eight to twelve weeks (estimate). Vendors win on that comparison, and the hard part is proving the cut did not come from emptier shelves.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Afresh (which says it is live in over 10% of the US grocery market and was hiring forward deployed engineers in October 2026), RELEX, Crisp with Shelf Engine, Upshop, Invafresh and Blue Yonder. Gaps: overrides carry no reason, stockout days censor demand, and product condition never enters the forecast.

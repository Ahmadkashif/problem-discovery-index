# Fresh Markdown & Expiry Pricing

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/grocery-stores|Grocery Stores]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to price or divert short-dated fresh product before it is culled, and whoever recovers the most margin per marked-down lot net of cannibalised full-price sales takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$600M a year of US spend on markdown pricing, date-checking, surplus marketplaces and electronic shelf labels used for fresh pricing at supermarkets (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~0.07% of grocery sales (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low-Medium — most markdowns are still a clerk with a sticker gun and a fixed rule (30% off on the sell-by date); electronic shelf labels and dynamic pricing are in a minority of stores
**Target Buyer:** VP of fresh merchandising; director of sustainability or food waste at a chain
**Automation Potential:** High — markdown depth and timing follow from days-to-expiry, stock and sales rate, all recordable

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
This contest starts after the order is wrong or the week is slow. The product is already in the store and losing value daily, so the choice is between a deeper markdown, an earlier one, a transfer to the deli, a surplus app, a donation, or the dumpster. The winner is judged on recovered margin per lot, net of the full-price sales the markdown stole. That needs causal measurement the ordering vendors do not do. The buyers are often different, with sustainability teams owning donation targets.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Wasteless (dynamic expiry pricing), electronic shelf labels from Vusion and Hanshow, surplus marketplaces Flashfood and Too Good To Go, date-checking tools, and food-bank pickup programmes. Gaps: markdowns are rule-based rather than lot-based, cannibalisation is not measured, and markdown, donation and cull outcomes sit in separate systems.

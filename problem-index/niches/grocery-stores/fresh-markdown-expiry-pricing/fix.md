# The Thirty-Percent Sticker on Every Item

**Niche:** [[niches/grocery-stores/fresh-markdown-expiry-pricing/profile|Fresh Markdown & Expiry Pricing]]
**Industry:** [[industries/grocery-stores|Grocery Stores]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** One markdown rule for every fresh item gives away margin on items that would have sold and fails to move the ones that needed a deeper cut sooner.
**Tags:** #linear-regression #hypothesis-testing #evaluation-metrics #quick-win #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to price or divert short-dated fresh product before it is culled, and whoever recovers the most margin per marked-down lot net of cannibalised full-price sales takes the account.

## The Problem
The clerk walks the case with a list of items at their date and a sticker gun loaded with "30% off". It is simple and auditable, and it treats a pack of steaks and a bag of salad as if shoppers valued them the same way.

## Why It's Still Broken
Store systems record that a markdown label was scanned but not when it was applied or what else was on the shelf. No one has measured what different depths would have done, so the rule cannot be challenged with evidence.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Run a simple test: vary depth (20%, 30%, 50%) and timing (one or two days out) by item group across matched stores for eight weeks, record sell-through and full-price sales beside the markdown, and set a rule per item group from the results. Repeat quarterly.

## Who Feels the Pain
Clerks applying stickers to product they know will not move; meat managers watching marked-down steaks sit beside full-price ones; and finance teams booking markdown and shrink as separate losses.

## Impact If Fixed
Moving from one rule to five tested item-group rules typically recovers several points of markdown margin (estimate); at ~$200,000 of fresh markdowns per store a year, 5 points is ~$10,000 a store.

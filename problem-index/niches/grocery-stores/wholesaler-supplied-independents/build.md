# Deal Entitlement Reconciliation for Independents

**Niche:** [[niches/grocery-stores/wholesaler-supplied-independents/profile|Wholesaler-Supplied Independent Supermarkets]]
**Industry:** [[industries/grocery-stores|Grocery Stores]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** An independent grocer cannot see which vendor and wholesaler allowances it was entitled to, which it claimed, and which arrived on its invoices and credit memos.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #gradient-boosting #data-integration #automation #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to run the independent's buying off the wholesaler's weekly deals and price file, and whoever recovers the most unclaimed wholesaler and vendor allowances per store takes the account.

## The Problem
Each week the wholesaler publishes deals: off-invoice discounts in a buying window, ad allowances for stores that feature the item, and billbacks paid on scan data after the fact. An owner running two stores with no buyer reads some of the book, orders on habit and checks the invoice total. Allowances missed, billbacks never filed and fees charged in error are invisible.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The deal data, invoices and credit memos come from the wholesaler in its own formats, and the wholesaler's tools are built to sell, not audit. Independent POS vendors stop at the store's own data. Each independent is a small account.

## What to Build
Read the deal books, price files, invoices, credit memos and scan-based billback statements. Build an entitlement ledger per store and week: what the store qualified for given what it bought and sold, what it claimed, and what was paid. Flag gaps with the evidence, draft the claim, and, before the next order, show which deals the store's own movement justifies buying.

## Target Customer
Owners and buyers at 1- to 20-store independents, and retail accounting firms serving them.

## Impact If Built
If a store doing ~$15M a year buys ~$11M through its wholesaler and misses ~0.5% of that in allowances (estimate), recovering half is ~$27,000 a year, against net profit of ~$255,000 at a 1.7% margin.

# Accounts Payable Audit Tools Adapted to Grocery Allowances

**Niche:** [[niches/grocery-stores/wholesaler-supplied-independents/profile|Wholesaler-Supplied Independent Supermarkets]]
**Industry:** [[industries/grocery-stores|Grocery Stores]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Recovery audit firms already find overpayments in big retailers' payables; an independent grocer needs the same audit for wholesaler deals, at a fraction of the cost.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #decision-trees #data-integration #automation #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to run the independent's buying off the wholesaler's weekly deals and price file, and whoever recovers the most unclaimed wholesaler and vendor allowances per store takes the account.

## The Problem
Large chains pay recovery auditors to comb their payables and vendor agreements for missed allowances. Independents have no equivalent, though their deal structure is just as complex.

## What Already Exists
Accounts payable recovery audit firms (PRGX and similar), which work on contingency for large retailers, and AP automation tools (Bill.com-type invoice capture) that read invoices but not deal terms.

## The Customization Gap
The independent version must parse wholesaler deal books and price files rather than direct vendor contracts. It must understand cost-plus billing, upcharges and fees, and the ad-group and buying-window rules that decide entitlement. It must also read scan-based billback statements against the store's own POS. The economics must work at a few thousand dollars of recovery per store, so the audit has to run itself.

## Target Customer
Retail accounting services and independent grocers' associations offering audits to members.

## Impact If Solved
An automated audit at ~20% contingency on ~$27,000 of yearly recovery per store (estimate) leaves the store ~$22,000 a year it currently never sees.

# Wholesaler-Supplied Independent Supermarkets

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/grocery-stores|Grocery Stores]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to run the independent's buying off the wholesaler's weekly deals and price file, and whoever recovers the most unclaimed wholesaler and vendor allowances per store takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$700M a year of US spend by independent supermarkets on retail software, wholesaler retail-services fees and buying and accounting services (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** Independents had $353.5B of sales in 2024 by NGA's count (Grocery Dive), whose definition includes regional private chains; single- and few-store operators are a much smaller share (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low-Medium — scanning POS is universal, but buying runs on the wholesaler's portal, PDF deal sheets and the owner's memory
**Target Buyer:** Owner-operator or buyer at a 1- to 20-store independent supermarket
**Automation Potential:** High — deal sheets, price files, invoices and POS movement are all documents or data

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A chain negotiates with vendors itself; an independent buys most of its goods through a wholesaler such as UNFI, C&S (which completed its purchase of SpartanNash in September 2025 and serves close to 10,000 independent locations, search summary) or Associated Wholesale Grocers. The wholesaler sells at cost plus a fee or upcharge. It passes through vendor deals, including off-invoice allowances, ad allowances, billbacks and scan-downs, but only if the store buys in the window, joins the ad group and claims them. Each week's deal book runs to hundreds of items. Owners with no buyer on staff miss allowances they were entitled to and are billed for fees they did not expect. The software market serves chains, and the wholesaler's own tools serve the wholesaler.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Wholesalers' ordering portals and retail-services programmes, independent-grocery POS (LOC Software, ECRS Catapult), Vori, and accounting services. Gaps: no tool reconciles the deals offered with the deals claimed and received, and the store's own movement data is rarely used to size the buy.

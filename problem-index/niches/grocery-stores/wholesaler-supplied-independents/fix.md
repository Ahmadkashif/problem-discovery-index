# The Deal Book Read on Sunday Night

**Niche:** [[niches/grocery-stores/wholesaler-supplied-independents/profile|Wholesaler-Supplied Independent Supermarkets]]
**Industry:** [[industries/grocery-stores|Grocery Stores]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The independent owner reads hundreds of pages of weekly deals at home after closing, and buys on the items they happen to notice.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #gradient-boosting #quick-win #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to run the independent's buying off the wholesaler's weekly deals and price file, and whoever recovers the most unclaimed wholesaler and vendor allowances per store takes the account.

## The Problem
The wholesaler's deal book arrives weekly as a PDF or a portal list. An owner who is also the store manager, the produce buyer and the person who fixes the cooler reads it late at night and places the order. Good deals on items the store sells well are missed; deep deals on items it does not sell lead to overbuying.

## Why It's Still Broken
The deal book is written for the wholesaler's whole customer base, not for one store's movement. Nobody has joined the two for the owner.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Each week, rank the deal book against the store's own sales: the twenty deals worth most to this store, with a suggested quantity from its movement and shelf life, and the items it should skip. One page, read in five minutes, with the order drafted.

## Who Feels the Pain
Owner-operators doing the buying alone; their families, who see the Sunday nights; and the store's margin, which depends on deals being bought in the window.

## Impact If Fixed
Cutting the weekly deal review from ~3 hours to ~30 minutes (estimate) gives the owner back ~130 hours a year, and buying the top deals consistently adds margin the store was already entitled to.

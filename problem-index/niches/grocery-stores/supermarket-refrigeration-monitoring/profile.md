# Supermarket Refrigeration Monitoring & Refrigerant Management

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/grocery-stores|Grocery Stores]]
**Category:** Insight Layer
**Value-Chain Position:** Suppliers selling into the industry

## What They Do
Equipment makers and service firms that watch supermarket refrigeration remotely: rack systems, display cases and walk-ins. Their monitoring centres take alarms, diagnose faults, dispatch technicians and track refrigerant leaks for the store's compliance records. Copeland and Danfoss run connected-services and monitoring centres, and newer firms such as Axiom Cloud sell analytics on the same controller data (recalled). Grocers pay monitoring subscriptions, often bundled with equipment or service contracts.

## Insight Function
**Size:** ~10-40 analysts and refrigeration engineers above the monitoring floor at the largest providers (estimate); alarm operators and technicians are production
**Output:** Alarm triage, fault diagnoses, energy and leak reports, refrigerant compliance records
**Proprietary data:** Controller, temperature and alarm histories from thousands of client stores, plus leak and repair records
**Clock:** Leak-repair deadlines under EPA refrigerant rules that bind the grocer's repair work (recalled); product-loss risk measured in hours
**Buyer:** Head of connected services at the equipment maker

**Kill switches:** **data isn't theirs to use**, live on the monitoring-service half, which carries the verdict. Equipment makers' monitoring centres work each grocer's controller, alarm and leak records under monitoring and service agreements with no checked product rights, and that service is most of the pocket's revenue and headcount. The analytics-software half (Axiom-style SaaS that the grocer licenses to analyse its own controller data) is undetermined (terms unread): whether those vendors may pool data across clients was not checked.
**Verdict:** Logged — kill switch. The insight is bundled into a service, and the regulatory clock binds repairs, not analysis.

# Retrans Negotiation & Blackout Leverage

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/broadcast-stations|Broadcast TV & Radio Stations]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche (distribution consultants, economic advisers and the groups' own distribution teams) is fighting to estimate, distributor by distributor, what a blackout would cost each side in lost subscribers, fees and ratings, and whoever estimates that blackout cost most accurately takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$200M a year in negotiation staff, counsel, economic advice and modelling across TV groups (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~0.5% of TV station revenue spent on it (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — leverage is argued from experience, past blackouts and spreadsheets
**Target Buyer:** Group EVP of distribution
**Automation Potential:** Low for the negotiation; Medium for the leverage analysis behind it

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Every renewal is a bilateral negotiation in which each side's leverage is what it loses if the signal goes dark. The distributor risks subscribers who cancel or switch. The group loses fees, advertising reach and, with the network, goodwill. Cable operators must give a station 30 days' written notice before deleting or repositioning it (47 CFR 76.1601), and blackouts now often play out in public. The clock is the contract expiry, typically at year-end, inside the cycle fixed by the 1 October election. The winners are the negotiators who know the numbers best on both sides.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Experience, outside counsel, past blackout anecdotes, industry per-subscriber estimates and internal spreadsheets. Gaps: no systematic estimate of subscriber loss by distributor and market from past blackouts, and no view of the station's own audience share by distributor footprint.

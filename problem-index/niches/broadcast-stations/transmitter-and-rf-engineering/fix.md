# The Site Log in a Binder at the Transmitter

**Niche:** [[niches/broadcast-stations/transmitter-and-rf-engineering/profile|Transmitter, Tower & RF Engineering]]
**Industry:** [[industries/broadcast-stations|Broadcast TV & Radio Stations]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The history of every repair at a transmitter site is written in a binder at the site, and the engineer who wrote it is the only one who can read it.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #quick-win #worker-facing #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche (contract engineering firms, remote-control and transmitter-monitoring vendors, and the groups' own engineering departments) is fighting to keep each licensed transmitter on the air and within its FCC parameters with fewer engineer hours per site, and whoever keeps the most sites legal and on air per engineer takes the account.

## The Problem
Maintenance logs, wiring changes and fault histories live on paper at the site or in personal notes. A backup engineer arriving at night starts from nothing.

## Why It's Still Broken
Engineers write for themselves, under time pressure, and stations have never funded documentation as a task.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Photograph the binders and transcribe them into a searchable site history. Then capture each new visit as a short voice note after the work, transcribed and tagged to the equipment.

## Who Feels the Pain
Backup and successor engineers, group engineering directors after an acquisition, and the retiring engineer who cannot take a holiday.

## Impact If Fixed
A searchable history per site at the cost of a few minutes per visit, which cuts the time a stand-in engineer needs to diagnose a fault.

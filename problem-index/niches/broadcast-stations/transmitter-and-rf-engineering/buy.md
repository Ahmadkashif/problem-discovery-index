# Maintenance Management Adapted for Transmitter Sites

**Niche:** [[niches/broadcast-stations/transmitter-and-rf-engineering/profile|Transmitter, Tower & RF Engineering]]
**Industry:** [[industries/broadcast-stations|Broadcast TV & Radio Stations]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Maintenance management systems run work orders and preventive schedules for any plant; a transmitter site also carries FCC and FAA obligations that the systems do not know exist.
**Tags:** #survival-analysis #time-series-forecasting #feature-engineering #compliance #data-integration #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche (contract engineering firms, remote-control and transmitter-monitoring vendors, and the groups' own engineering departments) is fighting to keep each licensed transmitter on the air and within its FCC parameters with fewer engineer hours per site, and whoever keeps the most sites legal and on air per engineer takes the account.

## The Problem
Preventive maintenance, spares and regulatory checks are tracked per engineer, often on paper. They include tower light observations, EAS weekly and monthly tests, generator runs and transmission-line pressure. A group cannot see the state of its fleet.

## What Already Exists
CMMS platforms such as IBM Maximo, Fiix and UpKeep, used across utilities and manufacturing.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs asset models for transmitters, PA modules, combiners, lines, antennas, STLs and generators, with telemetry fed in from remote-control systems. It needs FCC and FAA obligations as scheduled tasks with evidence: EAS logs, tower lighting checks and outage notifications. Spares should be pooled across a group's sites by transmitter family, and component life should be forecast from run hours and fault history.

## Target Customer
Group engineering directors standardising maintenance across many stations.

## Impact If Solved
A fleet view of risk and compliance, fewer missed checks, and spares where they will be needed, without building a new system.

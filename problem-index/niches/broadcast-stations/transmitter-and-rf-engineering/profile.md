# Transmitter, Tower & RF Engineering

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/broadcast-stations|Broadcast TV & Radio Stations]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche (contract engineering firms, remote-control and transmitter-monitoring vendors, and the groups' own engineering departments) is fighting to keep each licensed transmitter on the air and within its FCC parameters with fewer engineer hours per site, and whoever keeps the most sites legal and on air per engineer takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$2B a year in engineering labour, contract engineering, maintenance, monitoring and site services across TV and radio (estimate; 9,380 broadcast technicians in BLS OEWS May 2023 plus engineers and contractors)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~4% of station revenue (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — transmitters report telemetry, but diagnosis, maintenance records and site knowledge live on paper and in the engineer's head
**Target Buyer:** Group VP or director of engineering; owner of a contract engineering firm
**Automation Potential:** Medium for monitoring and diagnosis; Low for the physical work

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Engineering is the part of a station that keeps the licence alive: authorised power and frequency, AM directional patterns, EAS equipment, tower lighting reported to the FAA, and a transmitter site often hours from the studio. It is physical, regulated and staffed by an ageing workforce, with one projection of 6,200 retirements against 5,100 needed hires over a decade. The FCC's October 2025 NextGen TV proposal, which would let each broadcaster choose when to end ATSC 1.0 simulcasting, adds transition planning on top of maintenance.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Remote-control systems (Burk, Davicom) and transmitter makers' monitoring (GatesAir, Nautel, Rohde & Schwarz), generator and HVAC alarms, and paper or spreadsheet site logs. Gaps: alarms judged without site history, no cross-vendor view of a group's fleet, and maintenance knowledge that cannot be handed to a backup.

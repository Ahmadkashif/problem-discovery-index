# Compliance Calendars Taught the FCC's Rules

**Niche:** [[niches/broadcast-stations/public-file-and-fcc-compliance/profile|Public File & FCC Compliance Reporting]]
**Industry:** [[industries/broadcast-stations|Broadcast TV & Radio Stations]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Governance, risk and compliance platforms track obligations and evidence for any regulated business; none knows the FCC public file's folders, timing rules or content expectations.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #bert #compliance #workflow-orchestration #data-integration #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche (broadcast law firms, compliance software vendors and state associations' inspection programmes) is fighting to keep every licence's public file complete and on time (issues/programs lists, political file, EEO and children's reports, carriage elections) with content that survives a petition to deny at renewal, and whoever does that for the most licences with the least station staff time takes the account.

## The Problem
Group compliance teams track hundreds of licences' obligations in spreadsheets and email reminders, while station staff upload to the FCC file by hand.

## What Already Exists
GRC and compliance-calendar platforms, and document management with retention rules.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs the FCC public-file taxonomy by service (full-power TV, Class A, AM, FM), per-item timing rules and licence-specific dates such as renewal anniversaries and election cycles. It needs upload verification against the FCC's file and content checks for issues/programs lists: number of issues, programme detail, local sourcing. Political-file items must link to traffic orders.

## Target Customer
Compliance officers at station groups.

## Impact If Solved
One dashboard of every licence's obligations and evidence, built on an existing compliance platform rather than spreadsheets.

# Document AI Taught to Read Copy Instructions

**Niche:** [[niches/broadcast-stations/traffic-and-log-operations/profile|Traffic, Log Building & Continuity]]
**Industry:** [[industries/broadcast-stations|Broadcast TV & Radio Stations]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Document extraction services already read invoices and forms; copy instructions are short, inconsistent documents whose meaning depends on the order they refer to.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #seq2seq #data-integration #automation #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche (traffic-system vendors, order-exchange and document-extraction services, and hubbed traffic centres) is fighting to turn every order, copy instruction and late change into an on-air log that runs without a missed spot, wrong copy or separation conflict, and whoever drives discrepancies toward zero with the fewest traffic staff takes the account.

## The Problem
Agencies and advertisers send rotation instructions as email bodies, PDFs and spreadsheets in their own formats, and traffic staff key them into each order.

## What Already Exists
Document AI services from Google, Microsoft and Amazon, and email-parsing tools.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs a schema for rotations (Ad-ID or ISCI, flight dates, weights, daypart and program restrictions, tags), matching to the right order and advertiser in the traffic system, and validation against the copy library. Unclear "rotate evenly" instructions should be routed for confirmation rather than guessed, and every applied rotation logged.

## Target Customer
Traffic directors and hub managers.

## Impact If Solved
Copy instructions applied in minutes, with fewer wrong-copy errors, using commodity extraction plus traffic-specific rules.

# The Renewal Lost to a Dashboard

**Niche:** [[niches/broadcast-stations/local-direct-sales/profile|Local Direct Sales]]
**Industry:** [[industries/broadcast-stations|Broadcast TV & Radio Stations]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A local advertiser cancels because the search and social rep shows a dashboard every month and the station's AE shows up with a ratings sheet once a year.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #descriptive-statistics #time-series-forecasting #quick-win #automation #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche (broadcast CRM vendors, attribution and call-tracking services, and the groups' own sales research teams) is fighting to show a local business owner, in the owner's own terms (calls, leads, booked jobs, showroom visits), that the station's schedule produced customers before the annual renewal, and whoever proves that most convincingly takes the account.

## The Problem
Between contract signing and renewal, the advertiser hears from the station mostly through invoices and makegood notices. Results conversations happen only when the AE has time to build a recap, and most do not.

## Why It's Still Broken
The data for a basic recap (what aired, when, against what audience, and what the advertiser's own call and web trend did) sits in three systems. Assembling it takes ~1 hour per account (estimate), which no AE with ~100 accounts can spare. Sales compensation also rewards new orders, not retained ones.

## What a Fix Looks Like
An automatic monthly recap per local account: spots aired by daypart, estimated audience, the advertiser's call and web trend around airtimes where data is shared, and a plain-language note drafted for the AE to edit and send. Accounts whose results or engagement are slipping are flagged to the sales manager.

## Who Feels the Pain
Local AEs losing accounts they could have kept, sales managers missing budget, and small advertisers who cannot tell whether their money worked.

## Impact If Fixed
A recap that takes minutes instead of an hour turns retention from a once-a-year scramble into a monthly habit, at almost no cost.

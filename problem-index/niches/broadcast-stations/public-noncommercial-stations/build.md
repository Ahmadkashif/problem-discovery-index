# Giving-Ladder Propensity from Membership History

**Niche:** [[niches/broadcast-stations/public-noncommercial-stations/profile|Public & Noncommercial Stations After CPB]]
**Industry:** [[industries/broadcast-stations|Broadcast TV & Radio Stations]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A public station holds years of member giving history and does not know which of its sustaining members could give ten times more if asked.
**Tags:** #gradient-boosting #survival-analysis #logistic-regression #feature-engineering #evaluation-metrics #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche (public-media membership CRMs, fundraising consultancies and donor-analytics vendors) is fighting to move a station's members up the giving ladder, from occasional donor to sustaining member to major donor, fast enough to replace lost Corporation for Public Broadcasting grant money, and whoever moves members up that ladder fastest takes the account.

## The Problem
With federal money gone, a station must raise more from the same audience. Its development director, often one of two or three development staff (estimate), has to choose whom to call for an upgrade, whom to ask for a major gift and whom to steward, and chooses by memory and gift size.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Large universities and hospitals buy wealth screening and donor models. Public stations have been too small and too grant-funded to pay for them. Membership data sits in systems built to process pledges, not to predict giving.

## What to Build
Models of upgrade and major-gift propensity and of lapse risk for sustaining members, trained on the station's own giving history. Signals include tenure, frequency, response to drives, event attendance, program affinity and email engagement. Pooled across stations with permission, the models also work for small ones. The output is a weekly call list for the development staff with the reason for each name and an expected gift range.

## Target Customer
General managers and chief development officers at public radio and TV stations, especially small and rural licensees.

## Impact If Built
Development staff spend their hours on the members most likely to give more, which is the fastest controllable path to replacing a lost grant.

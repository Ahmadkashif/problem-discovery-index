# A Time-Period Ratings Forecast for Every Deal

**Niche:** [[niches/broadcast-stations/syndicated-programming-acquisition/profile|Syndicated & Local Programming Acquisition]]
**Industry:** [[industries/broadcast-stations|Broadcast TV & Radio Stations]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Groups license shows for several seasons across dozens of markets on comparables and the syndicator's pitch, without a forecast of what each show will rate in each slot or deliver to the news that follows it.
**Tags:** #gradient-boosting #bayesian-inference #causal-inference #time-series-forecasting #feature-engineering #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche (ratings vendors' analytics tools, programming consultants and the groups' own research teams) is fighting to predict what a syndicated show or local programme will deliver in a specific market's time period, and as a lead-in to the news, before a multi-year licence is signed, and whoever forecasts those time-period ratings most accurately takes the account.

## The Problem
A show that rates well in one market fails in another because of competition, audience mix and the lead-in it inherits. Programming VPs carry a mental model of these effects built over decades. It is never written down or tested, and it is a large part of why some groups program better than others.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Ratings vendors sell history, not forecasts. Syndicators' research is advocacy. Each group's own record of past acquisitions, slots and outcomes is scattered across deal memos and old ratings books.

## What to Build
A model of time-period rating for any show in any market slot, using the show's performance elsewhere, the market's audience composition, competing programmes, lead-in and lead-out, and seasonality, with credible intervals. Add an estimate of the show's effect on the following newscast's audience, priced at the news's unit rates. Grade it against every past acquisition the group made.

## Target Customer
Group VPs of programming at multi-station TV groups.

## Impact If Built
Licence fees are negotiated against a forecast of revenue delivered, the wrong slot is avoided, and the programming VP's judgement is checked and kept.

# Renewals Priced on Last Year's Book

**Niche:** [[niches/broadcast-stations/syndicated-programming-acquisition/profile|Syndicated & Local Programming Acquisition]]
**Industry:** [[industries/broadcast-stations|Broadcast TV & Radio Stations]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A show's renewal is judged on its last ratings period, though the period reflected a lead-in, a competitor's schedule and a measurement change that will not repeat.
**Tags:** #causal-inference #descriptive-statistics #hypothesis-testing #quick-win #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche (ratings vendors' analytics tools, programming consultants and the groups' own research teams) is fighting to predict what a syndicated show or local programme will deliver in a specific market's time period, and as a lead-in to the news, before a multi-year licence is signed, and whoever forecasts those time-period ratings most accurately takes the account.

## The Problem
Renewal decisions come up on the syndicator's timetable, and the latest book is the easiest evidence to use. Nielsen's January 2025 switch of local TV currency to Big Data + Panel made period-to-period comparisons harder still (estimate).

## Why It's Still Broken
Adjusting for lead-in and competition takes analysis that small programming staffs do not have time to do for every show.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A one-page renewal note per show, with the latest rating adjusted for lead-in, competition and measurement change, its trend, and its share of the following news's audience. Draft it automatically from ratings data for the programmer to review.

## Who Feels the Pain
Program directors and group programming VPs who have to justify renewals, and GMs who pay for shows that are fading.

## Impact If Fixed
A renewal decision based on adjusted evidence, at the cost of a few minutes per show.

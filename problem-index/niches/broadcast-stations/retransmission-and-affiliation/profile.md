# Retransmission & Network Affiliation Economics

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/broadcast-stations|Broadcast TV & Radio Stations]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is that every serious competitor in this niche (distribution advisers, contract audit firms, and contract and royalty software vendors) is fighting to raise the station's net distribution revenue per subscriber after reverse compensation, but that splits into two contests with different winners: estimating what a blackout would cost each side in negotiations with each distributor, and testing distributors' self-reported subscriber counts after the agreement is signed. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$300M a year in distribution teams, outside counsel, consultants, audits and modelling spent to manage ~$14–15B in retransmission fees (estimate on BIA's $14.3B for 2024)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~30–40% of TV station revenue governed by this function (estimate); well under 1% spent on it
**Digital Adoption:** Low-Medium — agreements, rate schedules and subscriber reports live in contract files and spreadsheets
**Target Buyer:** Group EVP of distribution or chief revenue officer; group CFO
**Automation Potential:** Medium — collection and modelling automate; the negotiation does not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Retransmission consent is the TV station's second business: permission for cable, satellite, telco and virtual distributors to carry its signal, priced per subscriber per month. Commercial stations elected retrans or must-carry by 1 October 2026 for 2027–2029. A large share of what they collect is then owed to their network as reverse compensation under the affiliation agreement (estimate). Radio has no equivalent. The economics are set at group level, by a few people, for hundreds of agreements.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Spreadsheets, contract files, outside communications counsel, occasional audit firms and industry estimates from S&P Global and BIA. Gaps: no model of net distribution margin (retrans in, reverse comp out) by station across contract expiries, and no systematic check of reported subscribers.

# Reverse Comp Paid on One Count, Retrans Collected on Another

**Niche:** [[niches/broadcast-stations/retransmission-and-affiliation/profile|Retransmission & Network Affiliation Economics]]
**Industry:** [[industries/broadcast-stations|Broadcast TV & Radio Stations]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The station pays its network on one subscriber figure and collects from distributors on another, and the difference is found, if ever, in an annual true-up.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #hypothesis-testing #data-integration #quick-win #automation #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is that every serious competitor in this niche (distribution advisers, contract audit firms, and contract and royalty software vendors) is fighting to raise the station's net distribution revenue per subscriber after reverse compensation, but that splits into two contests with different winners: estimating what a blackout would cost each side in negotiations with each distributor, and testing distributors' self-reported subscriber counts after the agreement is signed. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## The Problem
Where reverse comp is computed per subscriber, the count the network uses may come from its own data or industry estimates rather than the distributor reports the station receives (estimate). Mismatches persist for years.

## Why It's Still Broken
Retrans collection and network payments sit with different people, sometimes in different departments, and both are reconciled in spreadsheets on different cycles.

## What a Fix Looks Like
One monthly reconciliation per station that sets the subscriber counts used for network payments beside the counts collected from distributors, flags gaps above a threshold, and documents the source for each figure ahead of the true-up.

## Who Feels the Pain
Group finance and distribution teams, and station GMs whose margin absorbs the error.

## Impact If Fixed
A monthly check catches overpayments and underpayments within the period rather than after the contract year, with little new tooling.

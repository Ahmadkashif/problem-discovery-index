# Niche Analysis — Broadcast TV & Radio Stations

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/broadcast-stations|Broadcast TV & Radio Stations]]

## Niche Selection

A station owns a licence, a transmitter and a perishable inventory of airtime in one market. It earns from local and national spot advertising and, for TV, from retransmission fees, part of which it passes to its network. The work splits into how that money is earned (local direct selling, distribution economics), what keeps the licence alive (engineering, the public file), what fills the airtime (programming acquisition, music programming), what turns orders into air (traffic), and one segment whose money has changed shape (public stations after the CPB rescission). National spot selling, order exchange with agencies and political rate quoting are done by reps and analysed under [[industries/media-rep-firms|Media Representative Firms]]. Local newsroom production is analysed under [[industries/news-media-local|Local News Media]]. Neither is claimed again here. Market sizes are estimates of tooling, services and labour spent on each function, not of station revenue.

## Why These Niches

Local direct sales and retransmission are where the money is. Local direct is roughly half of a station's core advertising (estimate) and is renewed on whether a local owner believes the schedule worked. Retransmission brought local TV ~$14.3B in 2024 (BIA) on terms set by a few people per group. The two low-digitized niches are where memory still runs the work. Transmitter care depends on what an ageing engineering workforce knows about each site. Programming acquisition is decided by phone, comparables and a programmer's sense of what a show will do in a slot. The two underserved audiences are those the commercial tools ignore. Public stations lost ~$1.1B in CPB funding in 2025 and must replace it from donors their systems were not built to cultivate. The cluster program director now programmes several stations without the music research the job used to rely on. The two automatable niches are the most mechanical: compiling the public file against fixed FCC clocks, and turning orders and copy instructions into a conflict-free log.

## Filter Notes

Seven of the eight level-1 niches are terminal. Retransmission & Network Affiliation Economics is not. Writing its contested sentence produced two sentences with different winners. Negotiation is a leverage contest among distribution consultants, economic advisers and in-house teams. It is won by whoever best estimates what a blackout costs each side, distributor by distributor, under contract expiries inside the cycle the FCC's 1 October 2026 election fixed for 2027–2029. Collection is a reconciliation contest among audit firms, royalty-management vendors and distribution finance teams. It is won by whoever tests every distributor's self-reported subscriber counts against an independent expectation and finds shortfalls earliest and most completely, before audit windows close. The people (negotiators against distribution finance), the data (blackout history against system-level reports) and the clocks (expiry dates against audit clauses) all differ. So the niche decomposes into **Retrans Negotiation & Blackout Leverage** and **Retrans Subscriber Reporting & Fee Audit**.

Three terminal calls were close. **Local Direct Sales** could be split into TV and radio, but both are renewed by the same owner on the same question of whether the schedule produced customers, so one contest governs. **Transmitter, Tower & RF Engineering** could be split into daily maintenance and the ATSC 3.0 conversion the FCC's October 2025 proposal would leave to each broadcaster's timing. The scarce resource in both is engineer time per site, so one contest governs, and conversion is treated as load on the same people. **Public File & FCC Compliance Reporting** overlaps with traffic on the political file. It stays separate because its contest is the completeness of the licence record, not the accuracy of the log.

Five candidates were considered and rejected. **Local TV news production** is a large share of station headcount, but it is already a niche under local news as [[niches/news-media-local/local-tv-stations/profile|🔵 Local TV News Stations]] and is not claimed twice. **Political advertising sales** is decomposed under media reps into [[niches/media-rep-firms/candidate-orders-luc-compliance/profile|🎯 Candidate Orders & Lowest-Unit-Charge Quoting]] and [[niches/media-rep-firms/issue-and-pac-inventory-pricing/profile|🎯 Issue & PAC Inventory Pricing]]. The station's own political work (holds, preemptions and the political file) is carried here by the high-impact problem and the compliance niche. **Small-market and AM owner-operators** were considered as an underserved audience. Their contest split into proving audience to local advertisers, which is Local Direct Sales, and running a station with three staff as cheaply as possible, which is a generic cost contest and fails the filter. **Station digital and streaming extensions** (apps, FAST channels, local connected-TV inventory) compete on streaming distribution and programmatic yield. That contest belongs to [[industries/streaming-video-platforms|Streaming Video Platforms]] and the exchanges already logged under local news. **Multicast and low-power spectrum leasing** (filling diginet streams on 1,772 LPTV and 397 Class A stations) has a real contest, but its terms depend on ATSC 3.0 rules the FCC had not finalised in the sources checked, so it is noted rather than built.

## Why These Pockets

No new pocket qualifies, and the stations themselves are the reason. A station group holds rich, owned data (traffic, ratings licences, retrans terms) and employs real research teams (pocket 13), but its analysis serves its own selling and programming and is never invoiced. Distributors on the other side of retrans (pocket 14) have the same shape. The firms that do invoice analysis to stations are small. The news and radio consultancies (pockets 9 and 11) have the invoice and a cross-market archive. Their kill switch is judged not live: each market study belongs to the station that commissioned it, but the norms and methods are the firm's own, as the vault rules for public-affairs pollsters. They fall short on bespoke work, client-set clocks and a buyer field of a handful of firms.

The insight businesses that matter most to a station are already profiled elsewhere and are not profiled twice. Even-year revenue rides on political spending, tracked by [[niches/news-media-local/political-ad-tracking/profile|🔍 Political Advertising Tracking]] (51, indexed). Every ratings-priced unit settles on [[niches/news-media-local/local-audience-measurement/profile|🔍 Local Audience Measurement]] (49) for TV and [[niches/podcasting-networks/audio-audience-measurement/profile|🔍 Audio Audience Measurement & Ratings]] (52, indexed) for radio. Radio stations are among the largest licensees of [[niches/music-publishers/collecting-societies-distribution/profile|🔍 Performing Rights Organisations & Collecting Societies]] (51, indexed), whose plays are detected by [[niches/music-publishers/broadcast-usage-monitoring/profile|🔍 Broadcast & Usage Monitoring Vendors]] (40). Market share comes from [[niches/media-rep-firms/market-revenue-pooling/profile|🔍 Market Revenue Pooling & Share Reporting]] (48), prospecting from [[niches/media-rep-firms/ad-spend-occurrence-intelligence/profile|🔍 Ad Spend & Occurrence Intelligence]] (45) and price benchmarks from [[niches/media-rep-firms/media-cost-benchmarks/profile|🔍 Media Cost Benchmark Providers]] (47).

Station combinations such as Nexstar–TEGNA, approved in March 2026, are argued by the economists logged under investment banking boutiques as [[niches/investment-banking-boutiques/antitrust-economic-consulting/profile|🔍 Antitrust Economic Consulting]] (46, kill switch: privilege and client data). The groups' leveraged debt is rated by the agencies logged under financial data vendors as [[niches/financial-data-vendors/credit-rating-agency-analytics/profile|🔍 Credit Rating Agency Analytics]] (47, kill switch: confidential issuer information). The regulator's files are logged as [[niches/news-media-local/broadcast-regulatory-filings/profile|🔍 Broadcast Regulatory & Political File Data]] (37, kill switch), and station brokerage as [[niches/news-media-local/media-brokerage-valuation/profile|🔍 Media Brokerage & Valuation]] (fails gate). The rules counsel and the traffic vendors are logged under media reps as [[niches/media-rep-firms/political-broadcasting-counsel/profile|🔍 Communications Law & Political Broadcasting Counsel]] and [[niches/media-rep-firms/broadcast-sales-traffic-software/profile|🔍 Broadcast Sales, Traffic & Order Exchange Software]], both kill switches. The second is now sharper, with Lumine Group owning both WideOrbit and Imagine. The measurement accreditor is [[niches/media-rep-firms/ratings-accreditation-bodies/profile|🔍 Ratings Accreditation Bodies]] (30).

The clean near-miss among the new pockets is retransmission and station economics research (40). It sells estimates of the station industry's second revenue line to lenders, investors and groups, and could grade its own forecasts against the fees groups later disclose. But it models public inputs rather than observing contracts, its broadcast team is small, and two firms make up the market. It is the same Kagan unit as the subscriber trackers logged under wired broadband, scored on the same basis. For an insight engine, broadcasting is a market to sell around, through measurement, tracking and licensing businesses already indexed, rather than into. The station groups are buyers of the operator tools Pass 1 describes: yield, retrans audit, public-file and transmitter monitoring.

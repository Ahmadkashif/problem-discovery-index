# One PD, Five Formats, No Notes

**Niche:** [[niches/broadcast-stations/cluster-radio-program-director/profile|The Cluster Radio Program Director]]
**Industry:** [[industries/broadcast-stations|Broadcast TV & Radio Stations]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A cluster PD makes hundreds of music and clock decisions a month across several stations and records the reasons for none of them, so nobody can learn from what worked.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #descriptive-statistics #quick-win #worker-facing #workflow-orchestration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche (music research firms, music-scheduling vendors and listening-data analytics services) is fighting to tell a program director which songs to add, rotate and rest, and where listeners are tuning away, from signals measured between ratings releases and without a callout budget, and whoever gives the PD the music read they trust most takes the account.

## The Problem
When a station's ratings move, nobody can trace which programming changes preceded it, and a new PD inherits the stations with no history of decisions.

## Why It's Still Broken
PDs are stretched across formats and have no tool that asks for a reason at the moment of change.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A one-line reason prompt when a song is added, rested or re-categorised, or a clock changed, stored with the date and station. Later, set beside each monthly ratings release.

## Who Feels the Pain
Cluster PDs, their successors, and group programming heads trying to tell good programming from luck.

## Impact If Fixed
A decision history that turns monthly ratings into feedback, at seconds per change.

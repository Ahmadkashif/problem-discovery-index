# Music Scheduling Software Fed With Research Signals

**Niche:** [[niches/broadcast-stations/cluster-radio-program-director/profile|The Cluster Radio Program Director]]
**Industry:** [[industries/broadcast-stations|Broadcast TV & Radio Stations]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Music schedulers place songs perfectly by the rules they are given; the rules themselves (categories, rotations, rest dates) are still set by hand from instinct.
**Tags:** #feature-engineering #time-series-forecasting #data-integration #workflow-orchestration #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche (music research firms, music-scheduling vendors and listening-data analytics services) is fighting to tell a program director which songs to add, rotate and rest, and where listeners are tuning away, from signals measured between ratings releases and without a callout budget, and whoever gives the PD the music read they trust most takes the account.

## The Problem
A PD moves songs between categories, sets rotations and rest dates, and checks the schedule for every station in the cluster, each week, inside the scheduler.

## What Already Exists
RCS Selector and MusicMaster, which schedule music to rules on separation, tempo, era and rotation.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs research signals on each song card: fatigue and tune-out from stream data, chart and airplay trends, and any test scores. It needs suggested category moves with reasons, and cross-station checks so sister stations in a cluster do not burn the same song.

## Target Customer
Cluster PDs and music directors.

## Impact If Solved
Category decisions made with evidence in the tool where they are executed, saving the PD hours each week across stations.

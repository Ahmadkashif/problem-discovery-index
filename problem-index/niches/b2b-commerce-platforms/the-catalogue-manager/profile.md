# The Catalogue Manager

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/b2b-commerce-platforms|B2B Commerce Platforms]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to tell the catalogue manager which gaps are costing sales — and whoever ranks the work by revenue at risk turns an endless backlog into a finite prioritised queue.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$800M US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~6% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Low — spreadsheets and manual keying
**Target Buyer:** Ecommerce and merchandising management
**Automation Potential:** Very High — extraction and prioritisation are both mechanisable

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A catalogue manager keys technical attributes from manufacturer PDFs, prioritising by intuition, with no way to know which gaps are costing sales. The work is unbounded — a distributor carries hundreds of thousands of parts, each with dozens of attributes, from thousands of manufacturers, all of whom publish differently and revise without notice. No amount of staffing finishes it. What makes this a distinct problem rather than a data entry problem is that the catalogue manager has no basis for choosing what to work on next, and the platform holds the exact evidence — failed searches, filtered-out results, abandoned sessions — that would tell them.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Product information management systems, spreadsheets, manufacturer portals and PDFs, and manual keying. The gaps: no prioritisation by commercial impact; no measurement of catalogue completeness against what customers actually search on; no detection of manufacturer data revisions; no feedback loop from search behaviour to enrichment queue; and no way to show the work's value, which is why the function is chronically understaffed.

# The Highest-Value Orders Never Touch the Platform

**Niche:** [[niches/b2b-commerce-platforms/quote-to-cash/profile|Quote to Cash]]
**Industry:** [[industries/b2b-commerce-platforms|B2B Commerce Platforms]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Quote-to-cash sits in the resource planning system or in email, which means the largest and most negotiated orders are the ones the commerce platform never sees.
**Tags:** #workflow-orchestration #revenue-impact #data-integration #evaluation-metrics #automation #confidence-intervals #convex-optimization #compliance
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to bring the quote inside the commerce platform rather than leaving it in a resource planning system and an inbox — and whoever does that captures the highest-value orders, which currently never touch the storefront.

## The Problem
A customer needs a hundred and twenty lines for a project, some configured, several requiring a negotiated price. The rep builds it in a spreadsheet, prices some lines from the system and some from judgement, emails it for approval, revises it twice, and the customer accepts by replying. The order is keyed into the resource planning system. None of it touched the storefront. The distributor's digital channel reports thirty percent of orders and a much smaller share of value, because the transactions that carry the value are the ones the platform was never designed to handle, and the quote — where the margin was actually decided — is a document in an inbox.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Commerce platforms came from a transactional model where the price is known and the customer self-serves, and a negotiated quote does not fit it. Quoting lived in the resource planning system before the storefront existed and stayed there. Configure-price-quote tools address it for large firms at a price and complexity most distributors will not take. And the orders arrive regardless, so the gap appears as a channel mix rather than as a failure.

## What to Build
Bring the quote into the commerce platform. Make the quote a first-class object alongside the order, created from a cart or by a rep, priced from the same entitlement and pricing resolution, and convertible into an order with one action — which closes the loop and makes the highest-value transactions visible and analysable for the first time. Let the customer see, comment on, partially accept and convert their quote digitally, since the email thread is currently the only interface and it serves neither party. Route margin approval through a defined workflow with the margin calculated automatically, rather than through a chain of emails, which is where quote cycle time actually goes. Feed accepted negotiated prices back into the customer's pricing where they should persist, since the same negotiation is otherwise repeated. Track every quote's outcome — won, lost, expired, revised — which is the data the sales organisation does not have and which the fix note develops. Support configuration where products require it, using the same catalogue and constraint data the part selection work builds. Set expiry and revision handling explicitly, since quotes go stale and the resulting disputes are avoidable. And report digital share by value rather than by order count, because the count-based figure is flattering and the value-based one is the honest measure of the channel.

## Target Customer
Sales operations and finance at distributors and manufacturers, the reps producing quotes, and the customers receiving spreadsheets.

## Impact If Built
The transactions carrying the value are the ones the platform was never designed for, and the quote where margin is decided is a document in an inbox. Making the quote a first-class object priced from the same resolution makes the highest-value business visible and analysable for the first time.

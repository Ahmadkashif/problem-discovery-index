# Part Search & Selection

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/b2b-commerce-platforms|B2B Commerce Platforms]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this sub-niche is fighting to identify the one correct item in three hundred thousand from whatever the buyer happens to have — and whoever does that takes the account, because the alternative is calling a specialist and the wrong part stops a machine.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.5B US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~11% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Low — a known weak point
**Target Buyer:** Technical buyers, maintenance staff and engineers
**Automation Potential:** Very High — identification from technical data is learnable

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Search in industrial catalogues is a known weak point where part numbers, cross-references and technical specifications matter more than relevance ranking. The buyer arrives with a competitor's part number, a specification from a drawing, a dimension and a thread pitch, a photograph of a broken component, or a description relayed from a technician. They need to be told which single item is correct, and being told an approximately relevant one is worse than useless because fitting the wrong part costs far more than the part. Consumer search returns a ranked list of plausible matches, which is exactly the wrong output for a question with one right answer, and the platforms deploy it because that is what they have.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Keyword search with relevance ranking, faceted filters on whatever attributes are populated, and part number lookup where the data exists. The gaps: cross-reference from competitor and manufacturer part numbers is patchy; specification-based selection is not supported because the attributes are incomplete; no interactive narrowing by technical criteria; compatibility and fitment relationships are absent; and the output is a ranked list where the buyer needs an identification.

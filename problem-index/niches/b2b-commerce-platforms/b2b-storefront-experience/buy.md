## The Problem
Designing for somebody who uses a system every day to do a repeated task is a different discipline from designing for a visitor, and enterprise software knows it: dense information display, keyboard efficiency, saved views, bulk operations, role-based interfaces and a deliberate absence of decoration. Procurement buyers are exactly that user. They are given a shopping experience designed to be attractive to a first-time visitor, with large images, promotional content and one item added at a time.

## What Already Exists
Enterprise interaction design patterns for high-frequency users; bulk entry and grid-based ordering interfaces; keyboard-driven workflows; role-based interface configuration; saved views and personal defaults; and the accessibility and efficiency practice that enterprise procurement demands.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a purchaser buying from an external supplier rather than an employee using an internal system. It requires: (1) efficiency patterns applied to ordering — paste a list of part numbers and quantities, upload a requisition file, order from history in a grid — which is how these buyers actually want to work and which every consumer-derived storefront makes slow; (2) role-based views for the distinct people involved, since the requisitioner, the approver and the receiver have different jobs and are given one interface; (3) the supplier's merchandising ambitions reconciled with the buyer's efficiency needs, which is a genuine tension and is currently resolved entirely in the supplier's favour; (4) accessibility to a standard enterprise procurement will require, since a large customer's own policies frequently apply to the suppliers they buy from; and (5) two interfaces rather than one, since the reorder buyer wants density and the part searcher wants guidance.

## Target Customer
Platform vendors, distributor digital teams, the procurement buyers using these systems, and the enterprise design community.

## Impact If Solved
These buyers are daily high-frequency task users given an interface designed for an attractive first visit. Bulk paste, file upload and grid ordering from history are how they want to work and are slow in every consumer-derived storefront.

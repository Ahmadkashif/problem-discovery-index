# B2B Storefront Experience

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/b2b-commerce-platforms|B2B Commerce Platforms]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Not terminal — the contest differs by whether the buyer already knows what they want, and the decomposition is recorded below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$3.6B US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~26% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** High
**Target Buyer:** Repeat account buyers; separately, part searchers
**Automation Potential:** High

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
This is the buying surface: the storefront a business customer uses instead of phoning. It is the largest product line in the category and the one every vendor competes on.

It is **not terminal**, because two buyers with opposite problems use it. The account buyer is a repeat purchaser who knows exactly what they want, has ordered it fifty times, and needs it reordered accurately at their negotiated price within their organisation's approval rules — a workflow contest with essentially no discovery in it, lost to a phone call or to the buyer's own procurement system. The part searcher does not know what they want in the catalogue's terms: they have a competitor's part number, a specification from a drawing, a photograph of a broken component or a description from a maintenance technician, and they need the platform to identify which of three hundred thousand items is correct — a retrieval and technical-data contest, lost to a phone call to a product specialist. The data that matters, the techniques and the definition of a good result share nothing. Filter Notes in the overview records the two rejected alternatives; the sub-niches below are the split.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Account portals, order history, requisition lists, search and faceted navigation inherited from consumer commerce. The gaps are specific to each buyer and are stated in the sub-niches.

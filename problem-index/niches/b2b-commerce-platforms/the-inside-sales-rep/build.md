# Three Systems and a Memory

**Niche:** [[niches/b2b-commerce-platforms/the-inside-sales-rep/profile|The Inside Sales Rep]]
**Industry:** [[industries/b2b-commerce-platforms|B2B Commerce Platforms]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Inside sales reps assemble quotes by hand from a resource planning system, a spreadsheet and their memory, and no product in the category was designed for them.
**Tags:** #worker-facing #workflow-orchestration #matrix-decompositions #data-integration #automation #k-nearest-neighbors #quick-win #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to give the inside sales rep a working surface rather than three systems and a memory — and whoever does that gets the rep to keep the platform open, which is the only path to the data that every other feature needs.

## The Problem
A rep takes a call. The customer wants the same as last time plus a few changes. The rep opens the resource planning system to find last time, a spreadsheet template to build the quote, the storefront to check a part number, and remembers that this customer takes the other manufacturer's fitting because they once complained about the first. Forty minutes later the quote goes out. They do this nine times a day. Every element of it exists in a system — the history, the entitlement, the catalogue, the availability — and none of them are in the same place, so the rep is the integration layer.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The category sells to the executive buying digital transformation, and the deliverable is a customer-facing storefront. The rep is not a buyer, is often seen as the thing the storefront will replace, and is measured on output rather than tooling. The resource planning vendor owns the rep's screen by default and has no reason to improve it. And the data integration required to build a unified surface is the same work the platform avoided by keeping the storefront separate.

## What to Build
A working surface for the rep. Put the customer's entitlement, price resolution, order history, open orders, backorders, quotes and credit position on one screen, which removes most of the system-switching and is the whole of the immediate gain. Build the quote from history — start from the last order, the last quote or a stated pattern, since most quotes are variations rather than novel and starting from blank is the largest single waste in the day. Surface availability and lead time at the line, because the rep's most common interruption is finding out something is not there after the quote went out. Show what the customer did on the storefront — what they searched for and did not order, what sits in an abandoned cart — which is information the rep has never had and which converts. Capture substitution and preference knowledge as the rep works, by asking one question when they deviate, which is how the fix note's knowledge problem is solved without a data entry project. Suggest lines from purchasing pattern intelligence, which makes the rep's calls more complete rather than replacing them. Handle the phone-to-quote path in a few clicks rather than forty minutes. And measure the rep's day honestly — time to quote, lines per quote, conversion — which is the evidence sales management currently lacks.

## Target Customer
Inside sales reps at distributors and manufacturers, sales management, and B2B commerce platforms that need rep adoption for their data to be complete.

## Impact If Built
Every element of the rep's day exists in a system and none are in the same place, so the rep is the integration layer. One surface with quote-from-history removes most of the switching, and capturing substitution knowledge as they work solves the knowledge problem without a data entry project.

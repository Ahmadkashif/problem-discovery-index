# The Knowledge That Leaves With the Rep

**Niche:** [[niches/b2b-commerce-platforms/the-inside-sales-rep/profile|The Inside Sales Rep]]
**Industry:** [[industries/b2b-commerce-platforms|B2B Commerce Platforms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A twenty-year rep knows which substitutions each customer accepts, which prices move and which parts actually fit, and when they retire the distributor loses it entirely.
**Tags:** #tacit-knowledge-ml #worker-facing #matrix-decompositions #workflow-orchestration #quick-win #k-nearest-neighbors #data-integration #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to give the inside sales rep a working surface rather than three systems and a memory — and whoever does that gets the rep to keep the platform open, which is the only path to the data that every other feature needs.

## The Problem
The best rep in the branch knows that this customer will take the alternative brand but that one will not, that this fitting supersedes that one despite what the catalogue says, that this account will always ask for a price break and always buy anyway, and which three parts are always forgotten on this kind of job. None of it is written down anywhere. It is the most valuable commercial asset the distributor has and it is stored in one person's memory. When they retire, a decade of accumulated account knowledge walks out and the replacement spends three years rebuilding a fraction of it.

## Why It's Still Broken
There is no field for it. The resource planning system records transactions, not the reasoning behind them. Asking reps to document their knowledge in the abstract fails reliably — it is large, unstructured and only surfaces when a specific situation arises. And the knowledge is a source of the rep's standing, which makes explicit extraction projects socially awkward.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Capture it in the moment rather than in the abstract. When a rep substitutes a part, ask one question — why, and does this customer always take it — which is the single highest-yield capture point and costs three seconds. Record customer-level preferences as structured flags on the account: accepts substitutions, requires original manufacturer, always wants the price checked, ships to the second address. Infer what can be inferred from the transaction record rather than asking, since consistent substitution patterns are visible in the data and only the reasons are not. Build the supersession and compatibility record from actual rep behaviour, which is more current than the manufacturer's and is what the part selection work needs. Flag frequently-forgotten companion lines by job type, which is the most transferable piece of the knowledge. Make the captured knowledge visible to every rep covering the account, which is the point and is what makes coverage during absence work. Run a structured handover when a rep leaves, drawing on what was captured rather than on a farewell conversation. And credit the rep whose knowledge is being used, because the social dynamic determines whether capture happens at all.

## Who Feels the Pain
Distributors losing decades of account knowledge to retirement; new reps rebuilding it slowly and expensively; customers getting worse service after a handover; and the rep whose expertise has no durable form.

## Impact If Fixed
The distributor's most valuable commercial asset is stored in one person's memory and leaves when they retire. Asking one question at the moment of substitution is the highest-yield capture point and costs three seconds, and most of the rest is inferable from the transaction record.

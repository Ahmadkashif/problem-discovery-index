# Attribute Extraction

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/b2b-commerce-platforms|B2B Commerce Platforms]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn manufacturer documents into catalogue attributes at an accuracy the distributor will publish without review — and whoever reaches that threshold replaces an entire manual function.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.3B US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~9% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Low — largely manual keying
**Target Buyer:** Ecommerce and catalogue operations
**Automation Potential:** Very High — the task is document understanding

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Product information management is mature and industrial catalogues remain incomplete because attributes arrive as manufacturer PDFs. The systems for storing, governing and syndicating attributes are excellent; the bottleneck is entirely upstream, in getting a thread pitch out of a table on page four of a datasheet and into a field. This is a document understanding problem wearing a catalogue problem's clothes, and it is distinct from the catalogue manager's prioritisation problem: one is about what to work on, this one is about the work itself disappearing.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Manual keying, offshore data entry, supplier data feeds where they exist, industry data pools, and early document extraction tools. The gaps: extraction accuracy below the publish-without-review threshold; no unit and format normalisation across manufacturers; no handling of tabular and diagram-embedded specifications; no confidence signal to route the uncertain cases; and no continuous reprocessing when documents are revised.

# Property Catastrophe Treaty Underwriting

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/reinsurers|Reinsurers]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn a cedent's submission into a quoted own view of risk, with every load against the vendor model explained, before the broker's firm-order deadline, and whoever's loads still look right after the next large event takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.5B a year of reinsurer spend on catastrophe model licences, pricing and portfolio platforms, and cat modelling and underwriting staff time for property treaty business (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~30% of reinsurers' property-casualty analytics and tooling spend (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** High for modelling, low for the adjustment — every programme runs through licensed models, but the loads that turn model output into a price sit in workbooks and memos
**Target Buyer:** Chief Underwriting Officer, Property / Head of Catastrophe Pricing
**Automation Potential:** High for model runs and pricing packs; low for the lead underwriter's adjustment, which can be supported and graded but not replaced

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Property catastrophe excess-of-loss is the largest single block of reinsurance demand and the one most tightly bound to the renewal calendar: 1 January, 1 April, 1 June and 1 July. Global reinsurer capital reached a record $785B at end-2025 (Aon) and the Guy Carpenter US property cat rate-on-line index fell 16% in 2026, so the contest in this niche is now about defending price, not finding capacity. The underwriter's work is judged twice: by whether the broker gives the reinsurer its line, and by whether the loads applied held up when a large event struck the cedent.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Verisk Touchstone Re, Moody's RMS Intelligent Risk Platform and KCC for modelling; portfolio roll-up and marginal-impact tools such as Verisk's Analyze Re (recalled) or in-house Python; broker analytics for market views; Excel for the final price. The gaps: loads recorded as one blended factor with no reason; no standing join between a cedent's loads and its later experience against the model; and pricing packs rebuilt by hand for each programme.

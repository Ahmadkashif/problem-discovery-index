# Casualty Excess-of-Loss Trend & Development Pricing

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/reinsurers|Reinsurers]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to put trend and development selections on long-tail excess layers that survive the next several years of ceded development, and whoever's selections still hold when the adverse development arrives takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$700M a year of reinsurer spend on casualty pricing actuaries, large-loss data assembly, trend studies and litigation analytics for excess-of-loss treaties (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~15% of reinsurers' property-casualty analytics and tooling spend (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — triangles are digital, large-loss listings and claim narratives are cedent spreadsheets and PDFs
**Target Buyer:** Head of Casualty Pricing / Chief Casualty Actuary
**Automation Potential:** High for data assembly and change-point monitoring; low for the final selection, which the pricing committee owns

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
An excess-of-loss layer attaches above the cedent's retention, so its cost depends on the severity tail: the few claims that develop past the attachment years later. Trend compounds into that tail faster than into ground-up losses, which is why social inflation hits excess layers hardest. The contest is the selection itself: which trend and development pattern to apply to each cedent's large losses. The winner is the reinsurer whose selections, tested against later development, prove right.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Experience and exposure rating in in-house models; development benchmarks such as the RAA study; verdict and venue data from analytics vendors and [[niches/personal-injury-law/jury-verdict-settlement-reporters/profile|🔍 Jury Verdict & Settlement Reporters]]. The gaps: large losses re-keyed per renewal; no cross-cedent severity trend by venue; and no record of each selection against later development.

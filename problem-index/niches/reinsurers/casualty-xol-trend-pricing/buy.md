# Litigation Analytics Mapped to Cedent Venues

**Niche:** [[niches/reinsurers/casualty-xol-trend-pricing/profile|Casualty Excess-of-Loss Trend & Development Pricing]]
**Industry:** [[industries/reinsurers|Reinsurers]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Docket and verdict analytics already score venues and plaintiff firms for carriers' claims teams; reinsurers need the same signal translated into each cedent's exposure and layer.
**Tags:** #gradient-boosting #feature-engineering #evaluation-metrics #data-integration #revenue-impact #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to put trend and development selections on long-tail excess layers that survive the next several years of ceded development, and whoever's selections still hold when the adverse development arrives takes the account.

## The Problem
A reinsurer knows that some venues produce far larger verdicts than others but cannot easily say how much of a cedent's exposure sits in them, or what that does to an excess layer's expected loss.

## What Already Exists
Litigation and verdict analytics (Trellis, Lex Machina, verdict reporters) and emerging-liability models sold to carriers, profiled as [[niches/property-casualty-insurers/casualty-litigation-emerging-risk-analytics/profile|🔍 Casualty Litigation & Emerging-Liability Analytics]].

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs: mapping of venue scores to each cedent's exposure distribution by state and county; conversion of venue severity into excess-layer loss cost through the severity curve; time lags from filing to verdict to settlement; and a feedback loop from the cedent's later large losses to the venue scores.

## Target Customer
Casualty pricing teams at reinsurers already buying litigation data for claims or research.

## Impact If Solved
Venue risk priced into each layer explicitly, rather than absorbed into a single trend load.

# Reinsurance Claims, Technical Accounting & Collateral

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/reinsurers|Reinsurers]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to agree each cedent's ceded balance, loss and collateral position against the contract terms before the quarter-end, and whoever keeps the fewest balances past 90 days takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$600M a year of reinsurer spend on treaty administration systems, technical accounting and claims staff, claims audit and collateral management (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~12% of reinsurers' property-casualty analytics and tooling spend (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — statements of account, loss advices and cash calls arrive as spreadsheets, PDFs and emails through brokers
**Target Buyer:** Head of Reinsurance Operations / Chief Claims Officer, Reinsurance
**Automation Potential:** High for statement reading and reconciliation; medium for claims review; low for disputed losses and commutations

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Behind every treaty is a stream of money in both directions: premium, commission, reinstatement premium, paid losses, cash calls, collateral postings and releases. Since the early-1990s Schedule F revisions, US statutory accounting has charged a cedent's surplus 20% of paid-loss reinsurance recoverables more than 90 days overdue, and collateral for unauthorised reinsurers (Reg 114 trusts, letters of credit, funds withheld) must match reserves. Since 2022, all 56 NAIC jurisdictions have removed collateral for qualifying reciprocal-jurisdiction reinsurers (NAIC), which shifted but did not end the reconciliation. Claims review, technical accounting and collateral are one contest here because all three end in the same agreed balance on the same quarter-end clock.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Treaty administration platforms such as Sapiens' reinsurance system and in-house builds, broker accounting portals, and spreadsheets for everything that arrives outside them (recalled). The gaps: statements read by hand; differences labelled in email; cash calls lost in the broker chain; collateral requirements recomputed manually each quarter.

# Retrocession Buyers & Sellers

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/reinsurers|Reinsurers]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to present a reinsurer's whole treaty portfolio as one loss distribution that a retrocession writer can price without seeing the underlying cedents, and whoever's roll-up the retro panel accepts with the fewest data requests takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$150M a year of analytics, modelling and broking-support spend on retrocession by reinsurers and retro writers (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~3% of reinsurers' property-casualty analytics and tooling spend (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — the inwards portfolio is modelled, but the retro submission is assembled by hand from roll-ups and summaries
**Target Buyer:** Head of Retrocession / Chief Risk Officer (buyer side); Portfolio Manager at a retro writer (seller side)
**Automation Potential:** High for portfolio roll-ups and submission assembly; medium for pricing

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Retrocession is reinsurance for reinsurers. The buyer is a reinsurer protecting its own catastrophe portfolio; the sellers are other reinsurers and, increasingly, ILS funds and collateralised vehicles. Catastrophe tools are built for location-level exposure. A retro portfolio is a stack of hundreds of treaties, each with its own attachment, limit, reinstatements and share, written on cedents the retro writer will never see. The buyer must also buy retro before its 1 January inwards book is final. Both sides are served by tools designed for someone else.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Portfolio roll-up in vendor platforms and engines such as Analyze Re (recalled), in-house aggregation, and broker presentations. The gaps: no standard retro submission format; uncertainty from the unfinished inwards book not quantified; and the retro writer's view of the buyer's underwriting quality left to reputation.

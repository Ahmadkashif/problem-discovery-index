# Submission & Exposure Data Preparation

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/reinsurers|Reinsurers]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn a cedent's exposure submission into a model-ready file with a data-quality score within hours of arrival, and whoever's cleaned files need the fewest manual fixes in December takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$300M a year of reinsurer and broker spend on exposure analysts, offshore data teams, geocoding and data-preparation tools for treaty submissions (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~6% of reinsurers' property-casualty analytics and tooling spend (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — the files are digital, but the mapping, recoding and checking is manual
**Target Buyer:** Head of Exposure Management / Head of Catastrophe Modelling
**Automation Potential:** Very high — mapping, recoding and plausibility checks are repeatable rules plus pattern recognition

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Every property programme starts as a cedent spreadsheet that must become a model-ready file before anyone can price it. The work is the same at every reinsurer on the panel and is redone at each. Supercede described the submission process at its 2021 launch as typically taking several months of spreadsheets passed between cedents, brokers and reinsurers. It is the most mechanical step in the renewal and the one that consumes the most analyst hours in November and December.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Vendor import tools in Verisk Touchstone and Moody's RMS, the Oasis Open Exposure Data format, broker analytics teams logged as [[niches/independent-insurance-agents/reinsurance-broking-analytics/profile|🔍 Reinsurance Broking Analytics]], Supercede's Packs, and offshore data-preparation teams. The gaps: construction and occupancy recoded by hand; insured values unchecked against rebuild cost; and no data-quality score carried into pricing.

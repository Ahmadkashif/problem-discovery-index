# Slip Agreed, Wording Signed Months Later, Never Compared

**Niche:** [[niches/reinsurers/treaty-wording-contract-certainty/profile|Treaty Wording & Contract Certainty]]
**Industry:** [[industries/reinsurers|Reinsurers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Terms are agreed on a slip at the renewal, and the full wording that arrives later is signed without anyone checking it says the same thing.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #compliance #quick-win #workflow-orchestration #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to compare each renewal wording with the expiring contract, the agreed slip and the market model clauses clause by clause before signing, and whoever catches the material deviations before a loss tests them takes the account.

## The Problem
The slip records the agreed terms; the wording is drafted afterwards by the broker or cedent's counsel. Small inconsistencies — a different reinstatement basis, an extra exclusion, a changed territorial scope — slip through because the wording arrives during the next busy period.

## Why It's Still Broken
Nobody owns the comparison, and the wording arrives long after the underwriter has moved on.

## What a Fix Looks Like
An automatic slip-to-wording check on arrival, listing every term that differs, routed to the underwriter who agreed the slip for a yes or no.

## Who Feels the Pain
Underwriters surprised by claims positions; claims teams arguing over terms; brokers whose drafting is questioned after a loss.

## Impact If Fixed
A known source of post-loss disputes closed at almost no cost.

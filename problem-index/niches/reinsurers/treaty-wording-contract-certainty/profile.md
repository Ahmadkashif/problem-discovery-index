# Treaty Wording & Contract Certainty

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/reinsurers|Reinsurers]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to compare each renewal wording with the expiring contract, the agreed slip and the market model clauses clause by clause before signing, and whoever catches the material deviations before a loss tests them takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$150M a year of reinsurer and broker spend on wording specialists, contract-certainty checks and legal review of treaty documents (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~3% of reinsurers' property-casualty analytics and tooling spend (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — wordings are Word documents and PDFs, compared by reading and redlines
**Target Buyer:** Head of Wordings / Chief Underwriting Officer
**Automation Potential:** High — clause classification and version comparison are well suited to language models; judging materiality needs specialists

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A treaty's wording decides what counts as one loss occurrence, how ultimate net loss is calculated, which reinstatements are paid and what is excluded. It is often issued weeks after the slip, drifts year to year through small edits, and may differ between reinsurers on the same programme. The market rewrote cyber and communicable disease exclusions after 2020. The niche is narrow and mechanical, and its errors surface only after a loss, months after anyone could have fixed them.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Redlining in word processors, legal contract AI (Kira, Luminance, Harvey), model clause libraries from the Lloyd's Market Association and others, and contract-certainty processes in the London market. The gaps: no clause-level history per cedent; no materiality view of what a change does to past losses; slip-to-wording mismatches caught by chance.

# An Event-Definition Simulator for Every Wording Change

**Niche:** [[niches/reinsurers/treaty-wording-contract-certainty/profile|Treaty Wording & Contract Certainty]]
**Industry:** [[industries/reinsurers|Reinsurers]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A change to an hours clause or an event definition can add a reinstatement to a large loss, and no tool shows what a wording change would have done to past events.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #monte-carlo-methods #evaluation-metrics #compliance #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to compare each renewal wording with the expiring contract, the agreed slip and the market model clauses clause by clause before signing, and whoever catches the material deviations before a loss tests them takes the account.

## The Problem
Wording specialists see that an hours clause moved from 72 to 96 hours or that an event definition now groups wildfire differently. They cannot quickly say what that means in money, so the change is accepted or argued on principle.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Clause extraction and catastrophe event data live in different teams and systems, and contract AI vendors do not hold event footprints or loss histories.

## What to Build
Clause extraction that turns event definitions, hours clauses and reinstatement provisions into parameters, joined to historical event footprints and the cedent's past losses. Each proposed wording is re-run over past events to show how many occurrences, reinstatements and recoveries it would have produced against the expiring wording.

## Target Customer
Heads of wordings and chief underwriting officers at reinsurers with large property catastrophe books.

## Impact If Built
Wording changes negotiated on their cost, not on principle, before the loss rather than after it.

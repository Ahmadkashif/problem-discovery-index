# Casualty Treaty Reinsurance

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/reinsurers|Reinsurers]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is that every serious competitor is fighting to price long-tail casualty ahead of social inflation, but writing it produces two contests with different winners: on excess-of-loss layers, setting trend and development selections from cedents' ceded triangles and large-loss listings that survive years of adverse development; on quota shares of MGA and programme business, spotting underwriting drift in monthly bordereaux early enough to reprice or exit. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.2B a year of reinsurer spend on casualty pricing and reserving actuaries, loss-trend analytics, litigation data and programme audit for general liability, commercial auto, professional, workers' compensation and umbrella treaties (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~25% of reinsurers' property-casualty analytics and tooling spend (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — triangles and pricing models are digital, while large-loss listings, claim narratives and bordereaux arrive as cedent spreadsheets in their own layouts
**Target Buyer:** Chief Underwriting Officer, Casualty / Chief Reserving Actuary
**Automation Potential:** High for data assembly and monitoring; medium for trend selection, which stays a committee judgement

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Casualty is the other half of the treaty market and runs on a different clock from property: the price is set at the renewal, but whether it was right is known only as claims develop over five to ten years. Swiss Re estimated that social inflation raised US liability claims by 57% over a decade, and US liability lines exposed to bodily injury lost $43B over five years. Reinsurers see more cedents' development than any carrier does. That cross-cedent view is the niche's asset, used in two different ways.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Reserving and pricing in WTW ResQ, Milliman Arius and in-house R or Python; benchmarks such as the RAA loss development study; litigation analytics bought from vendors logged in [[niches/property-casualty-insurers/casualty-litigation-emerging-risk-analytics/profile|🔍 Casualty Litigation & Emerging-Liability Analytics]]; bordereaux processed in spreadsheets. The gaps: ceded triangles not pooled across cedents; large-loss listings re-keyed every renewal; and programme drift seen only in the loss ratio.

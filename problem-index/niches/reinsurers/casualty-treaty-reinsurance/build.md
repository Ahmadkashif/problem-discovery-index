## The Problem
Every casualty submission brings triangles, large-loss listings and exposure bases in the cedent's own layout. Pricing actuaries fit each cedent separately, borrowing from benchmarks by judgement. The reinsurer's widest view of liability development, across all its cedents, is never assembled.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Data arrive in incompatible formats at renewal pace, and each treaty's data is treated as belonging to that placement. Reserving tools assume one company's data, not hundreds of cedents' partial views.

## What to Build
A normalised store of every ceded triangle, large loss and bordereau by cedent, line, venue and accident year. On top of it sit a hierarchical development model that borrows strength across cedents and a severity-trend monitor by line and venue. The same store serves the excess-of-loss pricing team and the programme oversight team, and drives the contested sub-niches below.

## Target Customer
Chief casualty underwriters and chief reserving actuaries at reinsurers with large US casualty books.

## Impact If Built
Earlier sight of adverse trends across the book, used at the renewal rather than in the next reserve strengthening.

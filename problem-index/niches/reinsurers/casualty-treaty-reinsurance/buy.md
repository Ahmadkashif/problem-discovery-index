**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is that every serious competitor is fighting to price long-tail casualty ahead of social inflation, but writing it produces two contests with different winners: on excess-of-loss layers, setting trend and development selections from cedents' ceded triangles and large-loss listings that survive years of adverse development; on quota shares of MGA and programme business, spotting underwriting drift in monthly bordereaux early enough to reprice or exit. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## The Problem
Ceded data are reported in arrears, on the cedent's definitions, net or gross of other reinsurance, and often only above a reporting threshold. Reserving tools treat them as if they were a carrier's complete ground-up triangles.

## What Already Exists
WTW ResQ, Milliman Arius and similar reserving platforms, with chain-ladder, Bornhuetter-Ferguson and stochastic methods, plus in-house actuarial code.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs: reporting-lag and threshold adjustments for ceded data; mapping of each cedent's line and accident-year definitions to the reinsurer's; layer-aware development for excess business; a cross-cedent benchmark built from the reinsurer's own book; and links from each pricing assumption to the reserve review that later tests it.

## Target Customer
Reserving and casualty pricing actuaries at reinsurers already licensing a reserving platform.

## Impact If Solved
Pricing and reserving use the same adjusted data, so the gap between the renewal assumption and the reserve review becomes visible within a year rather than a cycle.

# Social Inflation Found in the Reserve Review, Not the Pricing

**Niche:** [[niches/reinsurers/casualty-treaty-reinsurance/profile|Casualty Treaty Reinsurance]]
**Industry:** [[industries/reinsurers|Reinsurers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Casualty pricing and casualty reserving sit in different teams with different data, so a deteriorating trend is usually discovered by the reserving committee after the renewal has been priced on the old one.

**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is that every serious competitor is fighting to price long-tail casualty ahead of social inflation, but writing it produces two contests with different winners: on excess-of-loss layers, setting trend and development selections from cedents' ceded triangles and large-loss listings that survive years of adverse development; on quota shares of MGA and programme business, spotting underwriting drift in monthly bordereaux early enough to reprice or exit. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## The Problem
The reserving team sees development emerging across accident years in quarterly reviews. The pricing team selects trend at the renewal from cedent submissions and benchmarks. The two rarely compare notes until a reserve charge makes them.

## Why It's Still Broken
The teams report to different executives, run different tools and work to different calendars: reserves to the quarter-end, pricing to the renewal.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A shared, quarterly trend dashboard by line and venue built from the reserving data, with the trend each renewal was priced at shown alongside. Any line whose emerging trend runs ahead of the priced trend is flagged before the next renewal.

## Who Feels the Pain
Casualty underwriters repricing books after a charge; reserving actuaries explaining adverse development; chief financial officers facing analysts after another reserve strengthening.

## Impact If Fixed
The reserving signal reaches the renewal in time to change the price, which is the cheapest place to respond to social inflation.

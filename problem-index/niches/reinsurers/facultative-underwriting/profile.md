# Facultative Reinsurance Underwriting

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/reinsurers|Reinsurers]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to read an individual-risk submission (engineering survey, statement of values, loss runs) and return a priced line inside the broker's turnaround, and whoever answers fastest without missing the risk's worst feature takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$400M a year of reinsurer spend on facultative underwriters' time, risk engineering review and underwriting tools for property and casualty fac (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~8% of reinsurers' property-casualty analytics and tooling spend (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — submissions arrive by email with PDF engineering reports, statements of values and loss runs, read one at a time
**Target Buyer:** Head of Facultative / Chief Underwriting Officer, Fac
**Automation Potential:** High for document reading and similar-risk retrieval; low for the decision on a large single risk

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Facultative reinsurance covers one risk at a time: a refinery, a hospital campus, a high-rise, a large casualty account the cedent cannot keep net. Unlike treaty, there is no portfolio to average over; the underwriter decides on the risk in front of them, usually within days, from documents written for someone else. Experienced fac underwriters say they read an engineering survey for what it does not say. The volume is in mid-sized risks where speed decides who gets the line.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Email, PDF readers and underwriting workbenches adapted from primary commercial lines (recalled); cat models for location exposure; in-house risk engineering. The gaps: survey findings not extracted into structured form; no retrieval of similar risks the reinsurer has seen or declined; declinations not logged with reasons.

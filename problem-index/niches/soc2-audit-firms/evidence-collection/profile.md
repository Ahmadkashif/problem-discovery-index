# Evidence Collection & Request Management

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/soc2-audit-firms|SOC 2 & Attestation Audit Firms]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Whether evidence is pulled from connected systems or requested, chased and received as files.

## Profile

**Market Size:** ~$120M
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~4%
**Digital Adoption:** Moderate — portals and request lists
**Target Buyer:** Audit operations, client compliance functions
**Automation Potential:** Very high — the evidence is already in systems

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche

The request list is the engagement's spine. Dozens to hundreds of items, sent to the client, tracked, chased, received in whatever form the client produced, and organised into the workpapers.

It is where the elapsed time goes. An engagement's calendar length is determined far more by how long evidence takes to arrive than by how long testing takes, and a large share of both the associate's and the client compliance manager's time is spent on the logistics rather than on anything substantive.

It is also the most automatable part of the engagement. Most of the evidence exists in systems — the compliance platform, the cloud accounts, the identity provider, the ticketing system — and could be pulled rather than requested. Where an integration exists, it is frequently used to retrieve the items for a sample rather than to remove the request cycle.

The remaining friction is the same at every firm: requests phrased in audit language the client must interpret, evidence rejected and re-requested because it did not show what was needed, and a chase cycle that runs for weeks.

## Current Tools & Gaps

Request list generation from the audit programme. Client portals for upload and tracking. Automated reminders. Some compliance platform integrations retrieving evidence directly. Standard evidence templates by control.

The gaps are the same ones that make engagements slow. Requests are phrased in control language rather than in terms of the specific system and artefact, so the client interprets before they can act. Nothing states what would satisfy the request, so evidence is produced, rejected and reproduced. Requests arrive in bulk rather than progressively. The same items are requested every period with no memory of what was supplied last time. And where integrations exist they retrieve rather than replace the request cycle.

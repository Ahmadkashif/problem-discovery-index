# Fieldwork Delivery

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/soc2-audit-firms|SOC 2 & Attestation Audit Firms]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Whether fieldwork is spread across the period or compressed into a window where junior staff at high utilisation check evidence against a fixed fee.

## Profile

**Market Size:** ~$750M
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~25%
**Digital Adoption:** Moderate — workpapers and workflow
**Target Buyer:** Firm operations, audit managers, practice leadership
**Automation Potential:** High — most of the work is evidence checking

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche

Fieldwork is where the engagement's economics land. Fixed-fee engagements, junior staff at high utilisation, concentrated into periods when many clients' audit windows coincide, performing work that is largely evidence checking.

The concentration is the structural problem. Audit periods cluster around calendar and fiscal year ends, so a firm's capacity is oversubscribed for a few months and underused for others. Fixed fees mean any overrun is absorbed by the firm, which lands on the associates as hours. And the work itself — requesting evidence, waiting for it, checking it against a control description, documenting the test — is mostly mechanical.

Every competitor is fighting the same battle: delivering an engagement at a price the market has pushed down, with staff who are scarce, in a window when everyone needs them. The firms that win on cost do so by compressing further, which lands on the same people.

What would change it is testing continuously rather than at a point, which is available now and is not how the engagement is structured.

## Current Tools & Gaps

Workpaper platforms with test documentation, review workflow and sign-off. Evidence request lists and tracking. Client portals for evidence upload. Scheduling and resourcing tools. Standard programmes per framework.

The gaps are about when and how the work happens. Fieldwork is concentrated because the engagement is structured around a point-in-time examination rather than a continuous one, though most controls could be tested throughout the period. Evidence requests are sent in bulk and chased, so a large share of elapsed time is waiting. Testing is manual even where the data is available through an integration. Review is a queue landing on managers who are themselves on engagements. And nothing measures the actual hours against the fee, so the overrun that lands on associates is invisible in the firm's own reporting.

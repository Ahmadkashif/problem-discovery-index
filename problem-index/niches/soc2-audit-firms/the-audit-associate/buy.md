# Buy: Apprenticeship Practice From Other Professions

**Niche:** The Audit Associate
**Industry:** [[industries/soc2-audit-firms|SOC 2 & Attestation Audit Firms]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Medicine, law and the trades all structure early-career learning deliberately with graduated responsibility and supervision, and audit assigns associates to whatever engagement needs capacity.
**Tags:** #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #worker-facing #workflow-orchestration #compliance #tacit-knowledge-ml
**Contested on:** Whether the entry-level role is professional training or evidence checking at high utilisation.

## The Problem

Professions that develop practitioners over years have thought carefully about how early-career learning is structured, and the common elements are recognisable.

Medical training has defined rotations ensuring exposure across domains, graduated responsibility as competence develops, supervision ratios, and a curriculum specifying what must be experienced. Legal training has supervised practice periods with required exposure to specified areas. The trades have apprenticeship frameworks with progression tied to demonstrated capability rather than to time served.

Audit has a professional qualification with examinations, and the practical experience component is largely whatever engagements the associate was staffed to. Exposure across control domains is accidental. Progression is by tenure and utilisation. Supervision is by whoever is running the engagement. And the curriculum is what the season required.

The examination side is rigorous. The experience side, which is where the judgement actually develops, is unstructured.

## What Already Exists

Medical training: structured rotations, competency frameworks, graduated responsibility, supervision requirements and formal assessment of practical capability.

Legal training: supervised practice with required areas of exposure and periodic review.

Apprenticeship frameworks: competency-based progression in the trades and increasingly in professional services, with defined skill milestones.

Professional qualification: the examination and experience requirements for audit qualifications, which specify hours and areas at a coarse level.

Firm training programmes: technical training and methodology courses, which are real and are separate from the engagement experience.

## The Customization Gap

**Experience is not curriculum-driven.** Medical rotations ensure exposure. Audit staffing ensures engagements are covered, and the associate's exposure is a by-product.

**Graduated responsibility is not designed.** Medicine explicitly increases responsibility as competence develops. Audit progression is largely by tenure and by who is available for the next role.

**Supervision ratios are not managed.** Medicine specifies them. Audit supervision depends on the manager's engagement load, which during the peak is high.

**Competency assessment is examination-based.** The qualification tests knowledge. Whether an associate can assess a control's design adequately is assessed informally by whoever worked with them.

**The seasonal peak overrides everything.** Even a firm with a good development framework will staff by availability during the peak, which is when most of the experience accumulates.

**The judgement is not taught because it is not delegated.** Competence develops by doing the judgement work under supervision, and the current role design gives associates very little of it.

## Target Customer

Firm practice leadership, for whom the entry-level pipeline is the binding capacity constraint and structured development is the available response.

Professional bodies, who set the experience requirements for qualification and could specify exposure and competency milestones rather than hours.

Larger firms, who have the scale to run structured rotation and who lose the most from attrition at the associate level.

## Impact If Solved

Professions with a similar development problem structure the experience deliberately, and audit structures the examination and leaves the experience to staffing.

Defined rotation across control domains would ensure exposure that is currently accidental, and is an administrative change within a firm's control.

And graduated delegation of the judgement work — under supervision, as competence develops — is how the judgement is actually learned, and is what the current role design most conspicuously lacks.

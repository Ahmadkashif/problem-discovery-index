# Buy: Governance Mechanisms From Financial Audit

**Niche:** Auditor Independence & Selection
**Industry:** [[industries/soc2-audit-firms|SOC 2 & Attestation Audit Firms]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Financial audit responded to the same structural conflict with audit committees, mandatory rotation and public inspection, and attestation has the professional rules and none of the structure.
**Tags:** #compliance #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #descriptive-statistics #revenue-impact #workflow-orchestration
**Contested on:** Whether the party selecting and paying the auditor can be the party the audit is about.

## The Problem

The conflict is identical and financial audit built a response.

Audit committees, composed of independent directors, own the auditor relationship — selection, fee negotiation and dismissal — rather than management, which separates the party being audited from the party choosing the auditor. Mandatory rotation of the audit firm or the engagement partner caps relationship length in several jurisdictions. Independent inspection examines firms' work and publishes deficiency findings. Non-audit service restrictions limit the commercial relationship beyond the audit itself. And auditor changes are publicly disclosed.

None of it removes the conflict. All of it constrains the conflict's expression, and the apparatus was built after a series of failures demonstrated that professional rules alone were insufficient.

Attestation has the professional rules. It has no audit committee equivalent, no rotation requirement, no published inspection, and no change disclosure. It is where financial audit was before the reforms, serving a market that has grown to substantial size.

## What Already Exists

Audit committee governance: independent director oversight of the auditor relationship, now standard for listed companies in most major jurisdictions.

Mandatory rotation: firm or partner rotation requirements, varying by jurisdiction, with a substantial body of argument about their effects.

Public inspection: independent oversight bodies inspecting registered firms and publishing findings.

Change disclosure: public reporting of auditor changes and the reasons, which is used by the market as a signal.

Attestation: professional independence rules, engagement acceptance procedures and peer review.

## The Customization Gap

**No audit committee equivalent exists, and one could.** Most companies buying SOC 2 are private and have no audit committee. A board committee or an independent director owning the relationship is achievable and essentially never done.

**Rotation is voluntary and rarely adopted.** Partner rotation on a defined cycle is available to any firm as a policy and is not a market expectation.

**Inspection results are not published.** Peer review exists for attestation practice and its outcomes are not published comparably — the most directly transferable element and it requires a professional decision rather than new institutions.

**Change disclosure has no mechanism.** Financial audit changes are disclosed through securities filings. Attestation has no public reporting channel, which means the disclosure would have to be in the report itself.

**The relying party could take the audit committee's role.** The structural insight is that the party relying should influence selection. In attestation the relying parties are enterprise customers with real commercial leverage, which is an available and unused route.

**The reforms have known limitations.** Financial audit's apparatus improved matters and did not resolve the conflict, which is worth knowing before treating it as a complete answer.

## Target Customer

Enterprise relying parties, who could collectively require rotation, disclosure or approved-firm lists of their suppliers — the audit committee role played by the customer rather than by the board.

Professional bodies, who could publish peer review outcomes comparably, which is the most available import and requires their decision alone.

Boards and investors of audited companies, who could take ownership of the auditor relationship in the way financial audit governance requires, which is available to any company that chooses it.

## Impact If Solved

An apparatus built in response to the identical structural conflict exists next door, is well documented, and has not been carried across to a market that now has substantial economic weight.

Publishing peer review outcomes comparably is the single most available import and would give the market its first quality signal.

And relying parties taking the audit committee's structural role — influencing selection because they are the ones relying — is the adaptation that fits this market's actual shape, and it requires only that a few large buyers decide to.

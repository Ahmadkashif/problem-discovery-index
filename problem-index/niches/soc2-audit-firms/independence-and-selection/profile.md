# Auditor Independence & Selection

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/soc2-audit-firms|SOC 2 & Attestation Audit Firms]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Whether the party selecting and paying the auditor can be the party the audit is about, without the selection determining the result.

## Profile

**Market Size:** ~$300M
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~10%
**Digital Adoption:** Very low — a procurement decision
**Target Buyer:** Standards bodies, relying parties, professional bodies
**Automation Potential:** Low — this is a market-structure problem

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche

The company being audited selects the auditor, negotiates the fee, and can change firms. The auditor's continued engagement depends on the audited party's satisfaction. And the report the audited party wants is a clean one, issued quickly.

The profession's independence rules address the obvious conflicts — financial interests, employment relationships, prohibited services — and do not address the structural one, because it is inherent to the model. Financial statement audit has the same structure and responded with mandatory rotation in some jurisdictions, audit committee ownership of the relationship, and independent inspection. Attestation has the professional rules and almost none of the structural apparatus.

The visible consequence is opinion shopping. A company that receives pushback can change firms, and the next firm's engagement begins with knowledge of why the last one ended. Whether this happens often is unmeasured; that it is available is structural.

Every serious firm in this market navigates the same tension: the client is paying, needs a clean report for a deal closing this month, and the auditor has found a control that did not operate.

## Current Tools & Gaps

Professional independence rules covering financial interests, employment, and prohibited non-audit services. Engagement acceptance and continuance procedures. Peer review of firm quality systems. Predecessor auditor communication requirements in some contexts. Rotation policies at some firms voluntarily.

The gaps are the structural ones. No mandatory rotation, so a relationship can continue indefinitely. No requirement for the relying parties to have any input into selection, though they are the beneficiaries. Auditor changes are not disclosed in the report, so a reader cannot see that the previous firm was replaced. Predecessor communication exists in principle and is weak in practice for this work. And no inspection results are published by firm, so the market has no quality signal at all.

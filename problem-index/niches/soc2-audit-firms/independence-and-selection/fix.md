# Fix: The Deal Closes This Month

**Niche:** Auditor Independence & Selection
**Industry:** [[industries/soc2-audit-firms|SOC 2 & Attestation Audit Firms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The auditor has found a control that did not operate, the client needs the report for a contract signing in three weeks, and the conversation that follows is the one this whole market turns on.
**Tags:** #compliance #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #worker-facing #revenue-impact #hypothesis-testing
**Contested on:** Whether the party selecting and paying the auditor can be the party the audit is about.

## The Problem

The associate finds it during fieldwork. Access reviews were not performed in two quarters. The control did not operate.

The client is not a bad actor. They know it happened, they know why — a reorganisation, a departure, a period when nobody owned it — and they have since fixed it. What they also have is a contract worth a substantial share of next year's revenue, contingent on a clean SOC 2, signing in three weeks.

The conversation that follows involves a client explaining the context, a partner weighing the professional position, and a commercial relationship in the room. The technically correct answer may be a qualification, a modified opinion or a shortened period, and each has a real consequence for a client who will select an auditor again next year.

Most firms handle this professionally and it is the moment where the market's structure bears directly on an individual judgement. The partner making it knows the client pays, knows they can change firms, and knows that a competitor would be pleased to take the engagement.

Nothing in the report will show that the conversation happened or how it went.

## Why It's Still Broken

**The structure puts the pressure on an individual.** The professional standard is clear and the commercial context is present in the room, and the resolution rests on one partner's judgement with no external support.

**The client can change firms.** The option exists, both parties know it exists, and it does not need to be mentioned to be operative.

**There is no record of the decision.** How a contested exception was resolved is in the workpapers at best and is invisible to anyone outside.

**Timing pressure is real and often genuine.** A deal contingent on a report is a real commercial fact, not a manipulation, which makes the pressure harder to resist than an improper request would be.

**The alternative outcomes are poorly understood by clients.** A qualified report is frequently survivable commercially, and clients and their customers both tend to treat it as fatal — which raises the stakes unnecessarily.

**Nobody supports the partner.** A firm's technical standards function exists and consultation is not always structural, so the partner may be deciding alone.

## What a Fix Looks Like

**Make consultation mandatory on contested exceptions.** Any exception where the client is disputing treatment goes to technical standards, automatically, removing it from a single partner's judgement under commercial pressure. This is a firm policy change and is the most directly available fix.

**Record the resolution.** What was found, what the client argued, how it was resolved and by whom. Internal at minimum, and it makes the decision reviewable rather than invisible.

**Normalise the qualified report.** Much of the pressure comes from both parties believing a qualification is catastrophic. Relying parties who understood that a qualification with context is frequently more informative than an unqualified opinion would reduce the stakes considerably.

**Separate the fee conversation from the findings.** Fee negotiation and exception resolution should not be handled by the same person at the same time, which is a structural separation firms can make internally.

**Give the partner institutional backing.** A firm that visibly supports partners who take the correct position in difficult engagements, including where the client leaves, is making the position sustainable. Where it does not, the outcome is predictable.

**Advise the client early.** A control failure identified during the period, rather than at the end, can be remediated. Continuous testing turns this conversation from an unavoidable exception into a fixable finding, which is the strongest argument for it.

**Tell clients what a qualification actually means.** Most have never been told, assume the worst, and would be substantially less pressured if they understood their customers' likely reaction.

## Who Feels the Pain

The partner, holding a professional judgement under real commercial pressure, alone, with the client's revenue and the firm's relationship in the room.

The associate, who found the exception and watches the resolution, learning something about how the profession works.

The relying party, who receives an opinion without knowing that anything was contested.

And the client, who is in a genuinely difficult position through circumstances that were frequently not their fault.

## Impact If Fixed

Mandatory consultation on contested exceptions removes the decision from an individual under commercial pressure and is a firm policy change available immediately.

Continuous testing would move most of these conversations months earlier, when the failure can still be remediated — which converts the hardest moment in the engagement into an ordinary one.

And normalising the qualified report would lower the stakes for both parties, because most of the pressure in that room comes from a shared belief that a qualification is fatal, which is frequently not true and which nobody has told the client.

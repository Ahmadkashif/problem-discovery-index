# Buy: Quality Signalling From Other Assurance Markets

**Niche:** Rigour Expression
**Industry:** [[industries/soc2-audit-firms|SOC 2 & Attestation Audit Firms]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Financial audit faced the same unobservable-quality problem and built inspection regimes, published findings and firm-level quality indicators, and attestation has none of it.
**Tags:** #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #compliance #descriptive-statistics #hypothesis-testing #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Whether a report can convey how searching the audit was.

## The Problem

Financial statement audit has exactly this structure — the audited company selects and pays the auditor, investors rely on the opinion, and the opinion is binary — and it built an apparatus in response.

Independent inspection regimes examine a sample of each firm's audits and publish deficiency rates by firm. Audit quality indicators have been developed and published in several jurisdictions, covering experience levels, hours per engagement, partner workload and training. Firm transparency reports disclose governance, quality control and inspection results. And regulators can sanction firms whose work falls below standard.

None of it is perfect and all of it makes quality partially observable, which is the thing attestation lacks entirely.

SOC 2 and related attestation work is performed under professional standards with peer review, and it has no equivalent inspection publication, no quality indicators and no firm-level comparison a relying party can see. The market that buys it therefore competes on price, which is exactly what financial audit did before the inspection regimes.

## What Already Exists

Audit inspection: the independent oversight bodies that inspect registered audit firms and publish deficiency findings by firm, in several major jurisdictions.

Audit quality indicators: developed and published by regulators and firms, covering resourcing, experience, workload and quality control measures.

Transparency reporting: firm-level disclosure of governance and quality systems, required in several jurisdictions.

Peer review: the profession's own quality review mechanism, which applies to attestation practice and whose results are not published in a comparable form.

Attestation: professional standards, peer review, and no published quality comparison.

## The Customization Gap

**No published inspection results.** Peer review exists and its outcomes are not published in a form a relying party can compare. Publishing them is the most directly transferable element and requires a decision by the profession rather than new machinery.

**Quality indicators have not been defined for attestation.** The financial audit indicators — hours, experience mix, partner workload — transfer almost directly and nobody has specified them for this work.

**No regulator owns attestation quality.** Financial audit has statutory oversight. Attestation is governed by professional standards with peer review, which is a weaker mechanism and is the structural difference.

**The relying party is a customer, not an investor.** Investor protection drove the financial audit reforms. Enterprise buyers relying on attestation have commercial leverage instead, which is a different and possibly faster route.

**Deficiency rates would be uncomfortable.** Publishing per-firm findings would show variation the market currently cannot see, which is the point and is resisted for the same reason.

**The financial audit experience is instructive about limits.** Inspection regimes improved observability and did not eliminate the underlying incentive problem, which is worth knowing before treating them as a complete answer.

## Target Customer

The professional bodies governing attestation work, who could publish peer review outcomes in a comparable form — the most directly available import.

Large enterprise buyers, who could demand quality indicators from their suppliers' auditors and whose commercial leverage is the faster route than regulation.

Audit firms with strong quality records, for whom published indicators convert an invisible investment into a visible one and who are the natural supply-side constituency.

## Impact If Solved

An assurance market with the identical structural problem built partial answers over decades, and the elements transfer directly to a market that has none of them.

Publishing peer review outcomes in a comparable form is the most available step and requires a professional decision rather than new institutions.

And defining quality indicators for attestation work — resourcing, experience mix, testing depth — would give relying parties the first basis they have ever had for distinguishing one firm from another.

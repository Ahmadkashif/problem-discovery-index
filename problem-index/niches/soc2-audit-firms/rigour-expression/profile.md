# Rigour Expression

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/soc2-audit-firms|SOC 2 & Attestation Audit Firms]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Whether a report can convey how searching the audit was, so that a rigorous one and a permissive one stop looking identical.

## Profile

**Market Size:** ~$360M
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~12%
**Digital Adoption:** Very low — the output is binary
**Target Buyer:** Standards bodies, relying parties, enterprise vendor risk teams
**Automation Potential:** Low — this is a standards and market-structure problem

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche

A SOC 2 report from a firm that tested exhaustively, examined the awkward systems and pushed back on the scope, and one from a firm that sampled minimally and accepted the client's boundary, are the same artefact. Both say the controls operated effectively. The reader cannot distinguish them.

That is the market failure. Rigour cannot be priced because the deliverable does not express it, so competition runs to speed and price, and a firm investing in better work captures none of the return.

It is a completely different problem from its sibling. [[niches/soc2-audit-firms/full-population-testing/profile|🎯 Full-Population Testing]] is a capability a firm can build alone, this year, with an immediate cost benefit and nobody's permission required. Rigour expression requires the attestation report format to change, which requires standards bodies to act; it requires relying parties to ask for something they currently do not; and it delivers nothing to the first firm that does it, because the party paying for the audit does not want rigour expressed and the party who would value it is not in the transaction.

A firm can test everything and issue an identical binary report. Most would.

## Current Tools & Gaps

The attestation report format, which contains the opinion, the system description, the controls and the tests performed at a descriptive level, and the results. Exception reporting where exceptions were found. Firm reputation as the only available rigour signal. Some enterprise vendor risk programmes maintain informal views of which audit firms are thorough.

The gaps are structural. The report does not state testing depth, population sizes or sample sizes. It does not indicate what was excluded from scope or why. It does not convey how much the auditor challenged the client's description. There is no comparable measure of audit quality anywhere. Firm reputation is the only proxy and it is unevidenced. And no mechanism exists by which a relying party could reward a rigorous auditor, because they cannot identify one.

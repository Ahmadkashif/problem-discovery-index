# Report Production & Distribution

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/soc2-audit-firms|SOC 2 & Attestation Audit Firms]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Whether the report is generated from the engagement record or assembled by hand from last year's document.

## Profile

**Market Size:** ~$60M
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~2%
**Digital Adoption:** High — templates and workflow
**Target Buyer:** Firm operations, quality reviewers
**Automation Potential:** Very high — it is assembly from structured data

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche

The report is the deliverable and its production is largely assembly. The opinion, the management assertion, the system description, the control matrix, the tests performed and the results — most of it exists in the engagement record and is transcribed into a document.

The mechanics are more mature here than elsewhere in this industry. Templates are standard, the format is prescribed, and the larger firms generate substantial parts of the report from their workpaper platforms.

The residue is where the time goes. The control matrix is reconciled by hand against the workpapers. The system description is the client's document, edited through several rounds. Exception descriptions are written from workpaper notes. Quality review comments are applied and re-checked. And distribution — who receives the report, under what restrictions, with what tracking — is handled as an afterthought in a market where the report circulates to hundreds of the client's customers.

## Current Tools & Gaps

Report templates per framework and per report type. Workpaper platforms with partial report generation. Quality review workflow with comment tracking. Client review cycles on the system description. PDF production and delivery, frequently by email.

The gaps are assembly and distribution. The control matrix is reconciled manually against test records, which is a mechanical check performed by a person. Exception descriptions are rewritten rather than generated from the structured test result. Multiple report types — a Type 1, a Type 2, a bridge letter — are produced separately rather than rendered from one record. Distribution is uncontrolled, so a firm has no view of where its report went. And no machine-readable companion is issued, so every recipient extracts the content by hand.

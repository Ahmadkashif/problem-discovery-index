# Broker Portals Turned Into a Duty Ledger

**Niche:** [[niches/consumer-goods-wholesalers/small-importer-tariff-whiplash/profile|Small Importer-Wholesalers Under Tariff Whiplash]]
**Industry:** [[industries/consumer-goods-wholesalers|Consumer Goods Wholesalers]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Brokers' client portals show shipments and invoices; a small importer needs them to show money owed back and deadlines.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #feature-engineering #compliance #data-integration #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep a small importer's duty bill as low as the law allows through each tariff change, and whoever leaves it paying the lowest net duty per dollar of imports after refunds takes the account.

## The Problem
Small importers see their imports through the broker's portal or emailed PDFs: entry numbers, values, duty paid. That is a filing record, not a decision tool.

## What Already Exists
Digital forwarders' and brokers' client portals, ACE reports for importers who register, and trade-content databases indexed at [[niches/customs-brokers/global-trade-content-databases/profile|🔍 Global Trade Content Databases]] (55; ~47–49 under current rulings, pending Stage E).

## The Customization Gap
The portal needs duty split by tariff authority per entry, refund and protest status with dates, SKU mapping so the owner sees duty by item and customer, and alerts when a pending tariff action will hit open purchase orders.

## Target Customer
Brokers serving small importers, as a client feature, or the importers themselves.

## Impact If Solved
A broker that adds a duty ledger could hold clients through the next tariff change; for a client it can catch refunds worth ~5–10% of IEEPA duty paid that would otherwise be missed (estimate).

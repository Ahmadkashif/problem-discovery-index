# Refunds Waiting on a Bank Form

**Niche:** [[niches/consumer-goods-wholesalers/small-importer-tariff-whiplash/profile|Small Importer-Wholesalers Under Tariff Whiplash]]
**Industry:** [[industries/consumer-goods-wholesalers|Consumer Goods Wholesalers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** IEEPA refunds are owed but not sent, because the importer never enrolled for electronic refunds or let a liquidated entry pass its protest window.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #workflow-orchestration #compliance #automation #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep a small importer's duty bill as low as the law allows through each tariff change, and whoever leaves it paying the lowest net duty per dollar of imports after refunds takes the account.

## The Problem
CBP pays IEEPA refunds only to importers enrolled for electronic refunds through ACE. As of 2 October 2026, ~20,362 refunds worth ~$1.1B were held for missing bank details (search summary). Entries liquidated long ago now need a court case or the class action to recover.

## Why It's Still Broken
Small importers never registered in ACE, the broker files entries but does not chase refunds, and nobody tracks each entry's liquidation date against the deadlines.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A checklist service that confirms ACE enrolment and refund registration, lists every entry with IEEPA duty and its status, flags entries near a deadline, and prepares the paperwork for broker or counsel.

## Who Feels the Pain
Owners of small importers, their bookkeepers, and brokers fielding the questions.

## Impact If Fixed
The ~$1.1B held for missing bank details averages ~$54K per refund (derived from the search-summary figures); releasing even one held refund pays for the service many times over.

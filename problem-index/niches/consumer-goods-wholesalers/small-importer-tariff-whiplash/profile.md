# Small Importer-Wholesalers Under Tariff Whiplash

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/consumer-goods-wholesalers|Consumer Goods Wholesalers]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep a small importer's duty bill as low as the law allows through each tariff change, and whoever leaves it paying the lowest net duty per dollar of imports after refunds takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$64.6B of 2022 receipts at the 25,921 firm counts under 20 employees in the seven codes (Census SUSB 2022), a large share of them importing directly (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~16%
**Digital Adoption:** Low — entries filed by a broker, duty seen as a line on the broker's invoice, refunds and classifications rarely reviewed
**Target Buyer:** Owner-CEO
**Automation Potential:** High for refund tracking and classification review; medium for sourcing decisions

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A ten-person toy, gift or sporting goods importer has no trade compliance staff. Between February 2025 and July 2026 it paid IEEPA duties, then a 10% Section 122 tariff, then Section 301 forced-labour tariffs of 10% or 12.5% from 24 July 2026. After *Learning Resources, Inc. v. Trump* it is owed IEEPA refunds, but they are not automatic. Importers had to enrol for refunds in CBP's ACE portal, ~$1.1B across 20,362 refunds was held for missing bank details as of 2 October 2026 (search summary), and CAPE Phase 3, for finally liquidated entries, opened on 6 October 2026, initially to nearly 7,000 importers that had filed their own suits. A class of non-litigant importers was certified on 8 October, with no refund order yet. Larger importers have counsel and a trade team; small ones have a broker who files entries. Competitors for the small importer's duty savings are customs brokers, trade-law firms, refund-claim services, drawback specialists and software. The broker-side view of these importers is at [[niches/customs-brokers/small-importer-ecommerce/profile|Small Importers & E-Commerce Sellers]], drawback at [[niches/customs-brokers/duty-drawback-specialists/profile|🔍 Duty Drawback Specialists]] and legal planning at [[niches/customs-brokers/customs-trade-law-practices/profile|🔍 Customs & Trade Law Practices]].

## Current Tools & Gaps
Brokers' portals and entry reports, ACE reports for those who register, and the occasional consultant. The gaps: no per-entry duty position by tariff authority, no tracking of refund eligibility and liquidation dates, and no classification review on the importer's own items.

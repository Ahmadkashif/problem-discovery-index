# Supply Planning Fed by Retailer Product Activity

**Niche:** [[niches/consumer-goods-wholesalers/replenishment-programmes/profile|Replenishment Programmes]]
**Industry:** [[industries/consumer-goods-wholesalers|Consumer Goods Wholesalers]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Mid-market supply planning plans from the importer's own shipments and needs the retailer's store-level signal and ocean lead times layered in.
**Tags:** #time-series-forecasting #exponential-smoothing #feature-engineering #data-integration #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep a retailer's everyday items in stock through its automatic replenishment orders, and whoever ships the fewest of the retailer's ordered units short takes the account.

## The Problem
Planners at importers plan from their own shipment history and the retailer's latest purchase orders, so they see demand changes only when the retailer's orders change, months after a container should have been booked.

## What Already Exists
Netstock, Inventory Planner, Logility and the forecasting add-ons of POS harmonisers such as Crisp and Alloy.ai.

## The Customization Gap
The planner needs the retailer's 852 product-activity data as a leading input, retailer order rules as constraints, ocean and port lead-time distributions per lane, and units shipped short at each retailer as the objective rather than generic fill.

## Target Customer
Supply chain leads at mid-size importers already licensing a planning tool.

## Impact If Solved
Booking containers on store-level signals rather than on purchase orders shortens the effective planning lag by ~4–6 weeks (estimate) and cuts stockout weeks by a third (estimate).

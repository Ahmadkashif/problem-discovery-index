# Replenishment Programmes

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/consumer-goods-wholesalers|Consumer Goods Wholesalers]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep a retailer's everyday items in stock through its automatic replenishment orders, and whoever ships the fewest of the retailer's ordered units short takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$75B a year of everyday, auto-replenished items sold by importers and wholesalers to big-box retailers (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~19% (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium-High — orders, ship notices and product-activity data arrive by EDI; planning against them is manual
**Target Buyer:** VP of Supply Chain or the account's demand planner
**Automation Potential:** High — the retailer's ordering follows rules that its own data reveals

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Basic housewares, small appliances, sporting goods staples, pet supplies and everyday toys sit on retailer shelves for years and are reordered by the retailer's replenishment system, week after week, to its distribution centres. The importer holds the US stock and is judged on how many ordered units it ships short. A short shipment draws a fine (Walmart charges 3% of the cost of goods on cases that miss its on-time or in-full targets, search summary), and repeated misses send the item to a competitor or to the retailer's own direct import at the next line review. The winners have the stock in the right US warehouse before the order arrives, which takes forecasting from the retailer's own store data, since ocean lead times are longer than the order cycle. The routing-guide, label and ship-notice fines that the same retailer programmes impose are a different contest, fought by EDI networks, warehouse software and 3PLs and profiled at [[niches/consumer-products-makers/otif-routing-edi-compliance/profile|OTIF, Routing-Guide & EDI Compliance]]; this niche is stock positioning, so that the units exist in the right warehouse to ship at all.

## Current Tools & Gaps
EDI networks, POS harmonisers with forecast add-ons, generic supply planning and the retailer's own collaborative forecasting portals. The gaps: forecasts of the retailer's orders rather than its store sales, and no tie from an inbound container's delay to the purchase orders it will fail.

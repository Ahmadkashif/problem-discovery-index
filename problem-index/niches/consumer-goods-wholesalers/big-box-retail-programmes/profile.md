# Big-Box Retail Programmes

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/consumer-goods-wholesalers|Consumer Goods Wholesalers]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is that every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep its items through each retailer's annual line review. But the review is won on two different measures with different winners: everyday items on automatic replenishment are kept on how few of the retailer's ordered units the importer ships short, and one-shot seasonal and promotional buys on how little markdown money the retailer claims back. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$120B a year of sales by importers and wholesalers to mass, club, home-centre, specialty chains and Amazon's first-party business (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~30% (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium-High — EDI, portals and scorecards everywhere; the decisions behind them on spreadsheets
**Target Buyer:** VP of Sales for big-box accounts
**Automation Potential:** High for replenishment and compliance; low for item selection

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Housewares, toy, sporting goods, gift and seasonal importers sell to the large chains on two kinds of terms. Domestic programmes leave the importer holding US inventory and shipping to the retailer's distribution centres. Direct-import programmes have the retailer take title at an Asian port, with the importer acting as vendor of record for the factory. Either way the retailer runs an annual line review, issues programme letters it can revise, scores the supplier on its own scorecard and deducts what it believes it is owed. Importers compete with each other, with the retailer's own sourcing offices and with brands selling direct. Retailer own-brand programmes made by domestic plants are profiled at [[niches/consumer-products-makers/private-label-retail-programmes/profile|Private-Label Programmes for Mass Retailers]].

## Current Tools & Gaps
EDI networks, POS harmonisers (Crisp, Alloy.ai, SPS Analytics), deduction platforms and Bentonville agencies. The gap: nobody joins an item's sell-through, scorecard results and every claim against it into a net margin the importer can take into the line review.

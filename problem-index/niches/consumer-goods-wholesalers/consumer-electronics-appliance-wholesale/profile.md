# Consumer Electronics, Appliance & Housewares Wholesale

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/consumer-goods-wholesalers|Consumer Goods Wholesalers]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to have the exact model a dealer or regional retailer just sold sitting in a nearby warehouse when the order lands, and whoever fills the highest share of dealer order lines from stock takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$134.3B of 2022 receipts in NAICS 423620 (Census SUSB 2022), of which 56 enterprises with 500+ employees, mostly brand-owned US sales arms and the largest distributors, took $102.8B
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~34%
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — dealer portals and EDI are common, but allocation, model changeovers and price-protection claims run on spreadsheets
**Target Buyer:** VP of Sales or COO at an independent distributor
**Automation Potential:** Medium-High — demand positioning and claims are data problems; delivery and install stay physical

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Independent appliance and electronics dealers sell from a showroom floor model and promise the boxed unit within days, because Lowe's, Home Depot and Best Buy promise the same. Few dealers hold deep stock, so they buy from whoever has the unit nearby: independent distributors such as Almo (2,000+ independent appliance dealers, nine regional facilities, next-day delivery to almost anywhere, search summary), D&H, Petra and regional houses, the brands selling direct, and buying-group warehouses. Models turn over yearly and brands cut prices mid-cycle, so a distributor that stocks deep carries model-transition and price-drop risk, and one that stocks thin loses the order. The retailer-owned buying groups that many dealers belong to are already profiled at [[niches/independent-retailers/retail-cooperative-buying-groups/profile|🔍 Retail Cooperative Buying Groups]]. Appliance and electronics retail itself belongs to the queued appliance and electronics stores build.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Distribution ERPs, dealer B2B portals, brand allocation systems and generic demand planning. The gaps: no model-level demand by dealer cluster across a fragmented dealer base, no forecast through model changeovers, and price-protection exposure on inventory counted by hand.

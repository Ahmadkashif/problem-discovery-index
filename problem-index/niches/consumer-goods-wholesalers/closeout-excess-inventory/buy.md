# Resale Price Data Fitted to Wholesale Lots

**Niche:** [[niches/consumer-goods-wholesalers/closeout-excess-inventory/profile|Closeout & Excess-Inventory Wholesale]]
**Industry:** [[industries/consumer-goods-wholesalers|Consumer Goods Wholesalers]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Marketplace price trackers tell a reseller what one unit sells for online; a closeout buyer needs what a pallet clears for at an off-price chain.
**Tags:** #k-nearest-neighbors #gradient-boosting #feature-engineering #data-integration #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to price a closeout lot from a manifest, a few photos and a deadline, and whoever earns the highest realised margin on the lots it wins takes the account.

## The Problem
Buyers look up items one at a time on marketplaces and price trackers built for single-unit online resale, then discount by instinct for volume and outlet.

## What Already Exists
Marketplace price-history tools such as Keepa for Amazon and sold-listing searches on eBay, plus B2B auction results visible to registered buyers on liquidation marketplaces.

## The Customization Gap
A wholesale buyer needs prices at lot and case volume, by outlet (off-price chain, dollar store, export, online), adjusted for condition grade and how many units the market absorbs before the price falls, linked to the house's own sell-through history.

## Target Customer
Buyers at mid-size closeout houses who already use online price lookups.

## Impact If Solved
Cutting lot pricing time from ~4 hours to ~30 minutes (estimate) lets one buyer bid on roughly twice as many lots a year.

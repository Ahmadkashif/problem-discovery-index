# Markdown Analytics Turned Around for the Supplier

**Niche:** [[niches/consumer-goods-wholesalers/seasonal-promotional-buys/profile|Seasonal & Promotional Buys]]
**Industry:** [[industries/consumer-goods-wholesalers|Consumer Goods Wholesalers]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Retailers run markdown optimisation to decide what to cut and when; the supplier funding the markdown has nothing to forecast or contest the claim.
**Tags:** #time-series-forecasting #gradient-boosting #causal-inference #revenue-impact #data-integration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to pick and size one-shot seasonal and promotional items that sell through at full price, and whoever leaves the retailer claiming the least markdown money per dollar shipped takes the account.

## The Problem
Late in the season the retailer asks for markdown support on slow items. The importer agrees a number without knowing what sell-through the remaining weeks would have brought or what other suppliers paid.

## What Already Exists
Retailer-side markdown and price optimisation in suites such as Blue Yonder, Oracle Retail and Aptos's Revionics, built for the retailer's own pricing decisions.

## The Customization Gap
The supplier needs a forecast of remaining-season sell-through by store from portal data, an estimate of how much of the slowdown is the retailer's own placement and pricing, and a record of past markdown claims by retailer, buyer and category to negotiate from.

## Target Customer
Account managers and finance leads at seasonal importers with several big-box accounts.

## Impact If Solved
Negotiating markdown support from a forecast rather than from the buyer's number could trim claims by ~10–15% (estimate), ~$250K a year on $2M of markdown money.

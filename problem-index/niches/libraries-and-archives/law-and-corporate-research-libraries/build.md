# Content Usage Mapped to Matters and Renewals

**Niche:** [[niches/libraries-and-archives/law-and-corporate-research-libraries/profile|Law Firm & Corporate Research Libraries]]
**Industry:** [[industries/libraries-and-archives|Libraries & Archives]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A firm spends millions on research content and cannot say which practice, matter or person each dollar served.
**Tags:** #feature-engineering #time-series-forecasting #evaluation-metrics #data-integration #revenue-impact #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to show, subscription by subscription and practice group by practice group, which licensed research content is used and recoverable before each renewal, and whoever makes the content budget defensible to the CFO takes the account.

## The Problem
Each research platform reports usage in its own format, by user ID, sometimes with a client-matter code the lawyer typed in and often without. Seats are reassigned informally. At renewal the library negotiates with partial evidence, and cost recovery from clients — where engagement terms allow it — depends on codes nobody checks.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Content vendors have no interest in exposing low usage, and the library teams that care are small. Usage-tracking tools capture logins but stop short of joining them to the firm's time and billing records and to contract terms.

## What to Build
A service that ingests each platform's usage logs, maps users to practice groups and timekeepers, matches sessions to matters through the time-entry system, flags unused and shared seats, models renewal scenarios against contract terms, and produces a cost-recovery feed where clients permit it. Firm and client data stay inside the firm's tenancy.

## Target Customer
Directors of research and knowledge services at law firms with more than about 100 lawyers; heads of information services at corporations with large content budgets.

## Impact If Built
Renewals negotiated on evidence, unused seats cut, and recovered costs that make the library's budget visible as value rather than overhead.

# Small & Rural Public Libraries

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/libraries-and-archives|Libraries & Archives]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to be the shared back office — catalogue, e-content, selection and reporting — that a library with four staff and no systems librarian can run, and whoever sells it once through the state library or regional consortium rather than library by library takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$250M a year in software, shared e-content, cataloguing, IT and consulting services bought by or for public libraries with fewer than ten paid staff (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~2% of public library operating spend (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium-Low — most run an ILS through a consortium or a low-cost hosted system, but statistics, selection and planning are done on paper and in spreadsheets by the director
**Target Buyer:** Library director (often the only professional librarian); state library development consultant; regional system or consortium director
**Automation Potential:** Medium — shared back-office services automate well; the director's community work does not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The typical US public library is small. In FY 2022 the median library system had 4.0 paid FTE, 1.9 of them librarians, and a median operating revenue of about $289,000 (IMLS survey). The director selects books, catalogues, writes grants, runs programmes, fixes the printer and files the annual state report. Products built for a 40-branch system assume a selector, a systems librarian and an analyst these libraries do not have. They buy through their state library, regional system or consortium, which is where any vendor has to sell.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Shared ILSs (Evergreen and Koha consortia, regional Polaris or Symphony systems), low-cost hosted systems such as Biblionix Apollo, consortium OverDrive collections, state-funded databases, and state library consultants. Gaps: no selection support; collection analysis nobody has time to run; annual reporting by hand; and the shock when a supplier fails, as when Baker & Taylor's October 2025 closure forced libraries to rebuild standing orders mid-year.

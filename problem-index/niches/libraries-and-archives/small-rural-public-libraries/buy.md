# Shared Catalogue Software Without Shared Staff

**Niche:** [[niches/libraries-and-archives/small-rural-public-libraries/profile|Small & Rural Public Libraries]]
**Industry:** [[industries/libraries-and-archives|Libraries & Archives]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Open-source consortium ILSs give small libraries a shared catalogue; they do not give them the shared cataloguing, reporting and e-content management that the software assumes someone else does.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #descriptive-statistics #data-integration #automation #workflow-orchestration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to be the shared back office — catalogue, e-content, selection and reporting — that a library with four staff and no systems librarian can run, and whoever sells it once through the state library or regional consortium rather than library by library takes the account.

## The Problem
Joining a consortium solves the system cost but not the work. Members still catalogue their own local items, configure their own reports and manage their share of a joint e-content collection.

## What Already Exists
Evergreen and Koha with hosting and support from Equinox and ByWater; commercial ILSs offered regionally; OverDrive's shared consortium collections with member "Advantage" accounts.

## The Customization Gap
Small members need the consortium layer as a service: central copy cataloguing and record clean-up for local items, preset reports that answer the state survey and the board's questions, shared e-content purchasing rules that are fair to small members, and plain-language help inside the staff client.

## Target Customer
Consortium directors and state library staff serving small members.

## Impact If Solved
Hours a week returned to each small library's director, and consistent data across the consortium.

# The State Report Typed From Paper Tallies

**Niche:** [[niches/libraries-and-archives/small-rural-public-libraries/profile|Small & Rural Public Libraries]]
**Industry:** [[industries/libraries-and-archives|Libraries & Archives]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Once a year the director rebuilds visits, programme attendance and circulation from tally sheets and three systems to fill in the state's Public Libraries Survey return.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #large-language-models #data-integration #quick-win #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to be the shared back office — catalogue, e-content, selection and reporting — that a library with four staff and no systems librarian can run, and whoever sells it once through the state library or regional consortium rather than library by library takes the account.

## The Problem
State libraries collect the annual data that feeds the federal Public Libraries Survey and often state aid formulas. Small libraries assemble it by hand from door-counter readings, programme sign-in sheets, the ILS, e-content platforms and the budget.

## Why It's Still Broken
Each source has a different export, definitions change between survey years, and the work happens once a year so no one builds a process.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Connectors that pull ILS, e-content and programme data monthly into the survey's definitions, with a short form for the counts only staff can supply, so the annual return is a review rather than a reconstruction.

## Who Feels the Pain
Small library directors; state library data coordinators chasing late or inconsistent returns.

## Impact If Fixed
Days of annual work reduced to an hour of review, and more reliable national data on small libraries.

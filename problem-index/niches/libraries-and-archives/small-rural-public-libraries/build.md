# A Selector for Libraries That Have None

**Niche:** [[niches/libraries-and-archives/small-rural-public-libraries/profile|Small & Rural Public Libraries]]
**Industry:** [[industries/libraries-and-archives|Libraries & Archives]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A one-librarian library picks its books from bestseller lists and patron requests because no one has time to select, and its shared consortium holds the demand data to do better.
**Tags:** #tacit-knowledge-ml #gradient-boosting #k-nearest-neighbors #time-series-forecasting #evaluation-metrics #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to be the shared back office — catalogue, e-content, selection and reporting — that a library with four staff and no systems librarian can run, and whoever sells it once through the state library or regional consortium rather than library by library takes the account.

## The Problem
In a small library the director orders new books in spare moments, usually from wholesaler bestseller lists, review roundups and patron requests. Selection and weeding are the work a big system's specialists do full time; here they are squeezed between story time and the circulation desk. The library's own circulation history is too small to learn from.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Vendors' selection services (curated lists, standing orders) are generic and tuned to sell copies. Consortia hold pooled title-level circulation across dozens of small members but have no product that turns it into per-library recommendations.

## What to Build
A selection service run at consortium or state level: learn from pooled title-level circulation of similar small libraries (community size, demographics, branch type) and from experienced selectors at larger member systems, and propose a monthly order list per library within its budget, plus a weeding list, each with reasons tied to the library's collection policy. The director approves or edits in minutes; edits are recorded and graded.

## Target Customer
State library development offices and consortia serving dozens to hundreds of small libraries; the libraries themselves through those channels.

## Impact If Built
Small libraries get specialist-quality selection without a specialist, and a documented rationale for every purchase if a title is challenged.

# Academic Unified Resource Management

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/libraries-and-archives|Libraries & Archives]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep one record of what a university library has paid for, is entitled to and can actually deliver across print and every publisher platform, and whoever keeps entitlements, links and access correct for the most publishers with the least staff effort takes the platform renewal.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$450M a year in US academic library services platform, discovery and knowledge-base spend (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~2% (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** High — cloud platforms with shared knowledge bases are the norm at research and mid-size universities
**Target Buyer:** Associate university librarian for technical services or collections; consortium systems director
**Automation Potential:** High for entitlement reconciliation and access monitoring; low for licence negotiation

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
An academic library's collection is mostly licensed, not owned: journal packages, databases and ebook collections whose title lists change monthly, plus perpetual-access rights to back years of cancelled titles. The platform has to know which titles, years and platforms the library is entitled to, activate them in the knowledge base and discovery layer, and route patrons through proxy authentication. Ex Libris Alma leads (2,745 libraries in the 2025 Library Systems Report), with FOLIO and OCLC WorldShare competing. The winner is decided by how rarely a patron hits a paywall for something the library paid for.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Alma's Community Zone and the EBSCO and OCLC knowledge bases supply KBART-based title lists; link resolvers and proxy servers (OCLC's EZproxy) handle access. Gaps: entitlements and activations drift apart as publishers change packages; broken access is found by patrons, not monitoring; and perpetual-access rights live in licence PDFs rather than the platform.

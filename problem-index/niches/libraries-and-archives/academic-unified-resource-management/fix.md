# The Paywall the Library Already Paid For

**Niche:** [[niches/libraries-and-archives/academic-unified-resource-management/profile|Academic Unified Resource Management]]
**Industry:** [[industries/libraries-and-archives|Libraries & Archives]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A student clicks a licensed article, meets a publisher's paywall, and pays or gives up, and the library never learns the access was broken.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #evaluation-metrics #data-integration #worker-facing #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep one record of what a university library has paid for, is entitled to and can actually deliver across print and every publisher platform, and whoever keeps entitlements, links and access correct for the most publishers with the least staff effort takes the platform renewal.

## The Problem
Access problems reach the library only when a patron reports them, and most do not. Turnaway and denial reports exist in COUNTER data but are not read as access-failure signals.

## Why It's Still Broken
Troubleshooting spans the proxy, the link resolver, the knowledge base and the publisher platform, each run by a different party, and the e-resources team handles tickets one at a time.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Add a one-click "this didn't work" report on every full-text link that captures the URL chain and the landing page, classify the failure automatically (proxy, activation, platform change, entitlement), and route it with the evidence attached. Read COUNTER denials weekly for titles the library believes it licenses.

## Who Feels the Pain
Students and faculty off campus; electronic resources librarians fielding vague tickets.

## Impact If Fixed
Failures reported with diagnosis attached cut troubleshooting from an hour to minutes per ticket and show the library how often its paid access fails.

# Genealogy & Archival Records Platforms

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/libraries-and-archives|Libraries & Archives]]
**Category:** Insight Layer
**Value-Chain Position:** Data & benchmark vendors

## What They Do
Ancestry, FamilySearch (run by the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints), MyHeritage and Findmypast digitise and index records held by archives and libraries — census schedules, vital records, newspapers, military files — often in exchange for free imaging and a period of exclusive access, and sell or give subscriptions to individuals. Public libraries license institutional editions so patrons can search them on site.

## Insight Function
**Size:** ~50-300 content acquisition, record-research and professional-genealogy staff at the largest platform, excluding indexers (estimate)
**Output:** Indexed record collections, family-tree matching, paid professional research
**Proprietary data:** Record indexes, user-built family trees and, at some, DNA match networks; the underlying records remain the archives'
**Clock:** Rare and occasional — NARA releases census schedules 72 years after enumeration (the 1950 census came out on 1 April 2022), a race every decade
**Buyer:** Head of content acquisition / chief content officer

**Verdict:** Logged — below threshold. A subscription business built on access to records rather than analysis, with indexing as production labour, a once-a-decade clock and a handful of platforms.

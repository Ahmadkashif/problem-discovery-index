# Scholarly Abstracting & Indexing Databases

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/libraries-and-archives|Libraries & Archives]]
**Category:** Insight Layer
**Value-Chain Position:** Data & benchmark vendors

## What They Do
Subject abstracting-and-indexing databases — CINAHL for nursing (EBSCO), APA PsycInfo, Inspec for engineering (IET), the MLA International Bibliography, EconLit and similar — read the literature of a discipline, write or edit abstracts, assign terms from a controlled thesaurus and sell the resulting index to academic, hospital and special libraries, usually through platforms such as EBSCOhost, Ovid or ProQuest. Many producers are scholarly societies; a few are commercial.

Scope note: the full-text aggregation business of EBSCO, ProQuest (part of Clarivate) and Gale is outside this pocket. That full text is licensed from publishers and archives under terms that restrict reuse, including text-and-data-mining and AI-use clauses. Those clauses bind the full text, not the producer's own abstracts and indexing, which is what this pocket covers.

## Insight Function
**Size:** ~20-150 subject indexers, abstractors and thesaurus editors per producer (estimate)
**Output:** Indexed and abstracted bibliographic records, discipline thesauri, curated subject databases
**Proprietary data:** Decades of expert controlled-vocabulary indexing in each discipline — not rebuildable at the same depth from open bibliographic data, although open metadata (Crossref, OpenAlex) and free discovery erode its exclusivity
**Clock:** None external; currency of indexing competes, but no third party sets a deadline
**Buyer:** Director or publisher of the database

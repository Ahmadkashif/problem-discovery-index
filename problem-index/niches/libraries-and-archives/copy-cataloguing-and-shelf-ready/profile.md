# Copy Cataloguing & Shelf-Ready Records

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/libraries-and-archives|Libraries & Archives]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to deliver a catalogue record a library can load without touching — matched to the exact edition and format and arriving with the item — and whoever does it right for the most formats and languages takes the library's record supply.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$300M a year in US spend on bibliographic record supply, cataloguing subscriptions, shelf-ready processing and cataloguing outsourcing (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~1% (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** High — records move as MARC files and APIs; checking and fixing them is still manual
**Target Buyer:** Head of technical services or cataloguing; collection services manager at a public library system
**Automation Potential:** Very high — matching, validation and record enrichment are repetitive and rule-governed

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Most items a library acquires already have a catalogue record somewhere. OCLC's WorldCat added 25.3 million new records in FY2025 and OCLC reports improving the quality of 62% of WorldCat's bibliographic records in the year. Public libraries mostly buy "shelf-ready" books from wholesalers — catalogued, labelled, barcoded and covered to the library's specifications — so the record and the item arrive together. The work left is matching each item to the right edition and format, loading the record, and catching the bad ones. It is the most mechanical part of a library's technical services and the part most exposed to automation.

## Current Tools & Gaps
OCLC (WorldCat, Connexion, Record Manager, its cataloguing partner services), wholesaler shelf-ready programmes (Ingram, Follett, Mackin and others after Baker & Taylor's 2025 closure), outsourcers such as Backstage Library Works, Alma's Community Zone, and MarcEdit for batch editing. Gaps: wrong-edition matches; vendor records missing subjects or call numbers; and processing specifications that live in a vendor's profile rather than the library's own systems.

# Shelf-Ready Profiles Rebuilt After a Wholesaler Closes

**Niche:** [[niches/libraries-and-archives/copy-cataloguing-and-shelf-ready/profile|Copy Cataloguing & Shelf-Ready Records]]
**Industry:** [[industries/libraries-and-archives|Libraries & Archives]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** When Baker & Taylor announced its closure in October 2025, libraries found their cataloguing, labelling and standing-order specifications lived in the vendor's system and had to be rebuilt from memory.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #data-integration #workflow-orchestration #quick-win #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to deliver a catalogue record a library can load without touching — matched to the exact edition and format and arriving with the item — and whoever does it right for the most formats and languages takes the library's record supply.

## The Problem
Shelf-ready profiles — which record source, call number rules, spine label layout, barcode placement, standing orders by author — are set up once with a vendor and held there. Baker & Taylor supplied over 5,000 public libraries; its closure forced them to recreate profiles with Ingram and others under time pressure while new releases arrived late.

## Why It's Still Broken
The specifications are configured in vendor portals and old emails, not documented in the library's own systems.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Export and keep a vendor-neutral processing and standing-order specification in the library's systems, drafted from past invoices, record files and the vendor profile, so switching suppliers is a configuration job.

## Who Feels the Pain
Technical services managers and selectors in a supplier switch; patrons waiting for new titles.

## Impact If Fixed
A supplier change measured in days rather than months.

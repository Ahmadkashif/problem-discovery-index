# Public Library ILS & Consortium Circulation

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/libraries-and-archives|Libraries & Archives]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to fill a patron's hold from any shelf in a shared consortium catalogue in the fewest days, without losing a hold or a patron record when the system changes, and whoever makes daily holds delivery and the migration least risky takes the consortium's shared contract.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$350M a year in US public library ILS, consortium hosting, delivery logistics and patron-facing catalogue spend (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~2% (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** High for circulation and holds; low for the physical delivery logistics that move items between branches
**Target Buyer:** Consortium executive director; public library system's IT or systems manager
**Automation Potential:** High for holds routing and delivery planning; medium for migrations

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Public libraries share. Many US public libraries belong to a consortium or regional system that runs one catalogue, one patron database and one holds queue across dozens of members, with vans moving items between branches every weekday. The IMLS survey counted 17,329 public library outlets in FY 2022; a large share of them fill holds from another library's shelf. The platform contest here is about circulation at consortium scale: holds that fill fastest, delivery that does not jam, migrations that do not drop a single patron's holds. Polaris and Sierra (Clarivate), SirsiDynix Symphony, and the open-source Evergreen and Koha (hosted by Equinox and ByWater) are the contenders.

## Current Tools & Gaps
The ILS assigns holds by simple rules — nearest available copy, or rotating lender lists — and generates paging slips; delivery runs on a fixed van schedule. Gaps: holds routing ignores transit time and van capacity; floating collections pile up at popular branches; and migrations between ILSs remain the moment consortia fear most.

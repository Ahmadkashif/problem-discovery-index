**Industry:** [[industries/libraries-and-archives|Libraries & Archives]]

**One-liner:** Litigation review platforms already sort millions of documents by topic and privilege; archives need the same engine for arrangement, description and restriction.
**Tags:** #bert #transformers #k-means-clustering #evaluation-metrics #compliance #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn unprocessed boxes into a searchable, privacy-screened finding aid at a cost per linear foot a grant or budget line can carry, and whoever makes processing fastest without exposing restricted material takes the archive's processing budget.

## The Problem
Born-digital collections — a retired faculty member's email, a nonprofit's shared drive — arrive as hundreds of thousands of files. Reading them is impossible; leaving them unprocessed makes them invisible.

## What Already Exists
E-discovery platforms (Relativity, Everlaw) with clustering, near-duplicate detection, email threading, technology-assisted review and PII detection; ePADD, a free tool from Stanford for appraising and processing email archives.

## The Customization Gap
Archives need the output in archival form: series and folder structures rather than review sets, DACS descriptions, restriction categories tied to donor agreements and lapse dates, and export to ArchivesSpace and the repository. Pricing must fit a grant, not a litigation budget, and the data often cannot leave the institution.

## Target Customer
Digital archivists at universities and government archives receiving born-digital collections.

## Impact If Solved
Born-digital collections processed at the scale they arrive, with screening documented, instead of sitting closed on a server.

# The Accession That Never Got a Record

**Industry:** [[industries/libraries-and-archives|Libraries & Archives]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A donation is accepted with a signed deed of gift, the boxes go to the stacks, and years later nobody can find the restriction the donor asked for.

## The Problem
Accessioning is supposed to record what arrived, from whom, under what terms. In practice deeds of gift sit in paper files, box counts in spreadsheets, and the donor's conditions in a curator's memory.

## Why It's Still Broken
Accession records are a low-visibility task done in a hurry when a collection arrives, and older collections predate any system.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Scan and extract deeds of gift and accession correspondence into structured accession records — donor, date, extent, restrictions, lapse dates, copyright transfer — linked to the location and the eventual finding aid, with a list of collections whose terms are unknown.

## Who Feels the Pain
Archivists preparing a collection for digitisation; reference staff answering researchers; donors' families.

## Impact If Fixed
Restrictions honoured and copyright status known before a collection is processed or published.

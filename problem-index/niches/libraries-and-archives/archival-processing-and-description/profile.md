# Archival Processing & Finding Aids

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/libraries-and-archives|Libraries & Archives]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn unprocessed boxes into a searchable, privacy-screened finding aid at a cost per linear foot a grant or budget line can carry, and whoever makes processing fastest without exposing restricted material takes the archive's processing budget.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$400M a year in archival processing labour, contract processing, description software and processing grants across US special collections and archives (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~2% (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — collections arrive as boxes of paper, photographs and obsolete media; arrangement and appraisal happen by hand, and many accession records are still paper deeds of gift
**Target Buyer:** Head of special collections, university archivist, state archivist, director of a historical society
**Automation Potential:** Medium — description, inventory and sensitive-content screening automate well; appraisal and donor relations do not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Processing is what turns an accession into something a researcher can find: appraising what to keep, arranging it into series and folders, describing it in a finding aid under DACS and encoding it in EAD, and setting access restrictions. It is skilled, slow and chronically behind; OCLC's 2010 survey of 275 research libraries found about half of archival collections had no online presence. The "More Product, Less Process" approach cut processing depth to clear backlogs, trading description quality for throughput. Funding comes in grants with fixed end dates and processing budgets that rarely grow.

## Current Tools & Gaps
ArchivesSpace (Lyrasis) is the common description system, with Preservica, Archivematica and repository platforms for digital objects; contract archivists and student workers do much of the labour. Gaps: no first-pass description from the boxes themselves; screening for personal data by reading; and accession records, donor restrictions and processing priorities kept in paper files and memory.

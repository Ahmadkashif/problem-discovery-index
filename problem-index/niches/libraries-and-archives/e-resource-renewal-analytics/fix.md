# Usage Reports That Don't Add Up Across Platforms

**Niche:** [[niches/libraries-and-archives/e-resource-renewal-analytics/profile|E-Resource Renewal & Usage Analytics]]
**Industry:** [[industries/libraries-and-archives|Libraries & Archives]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The cost-per-use figure in the renewal memo depends on which COUNTER report, which metric and which months the librarian happened to download.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #data-integration #quick-win #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to hand an academic library a renew, cancel or renegotiate number for each package — usage, open-access substitution and post-cancellation demand joined — before the publisher's renewal deadline, and whoever's number the provost and the consortium both accept takes the analytics budget.

## The Problem
Usage harvests fail silently, platforms change, COUNTER releases change metrics, and titles move between publishers mid-year. The resulting numbers differ from the publisher's own presentation at renewal time.

## Why It's Still Broken
Harvest monitoring is nobody's job, and gaps are discovered only when the report is needed.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Monitor every SUSHI harvest monthly, flag gaps and metric changes, normalise titles across platforms and releases, and keep a single, versioned usage history per licence that the memo cites.

## Who Feels the Pain
Electronic resources librarians defending numbers to a committee or a publisher's sales team.

## Impact If Fixed
A usage figure the library, the consortium and the publisher can agree on before negotiations start.

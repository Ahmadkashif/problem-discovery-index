# Contract Lifecycle Software for Publisher Licences

**Niche:** [[niches/libraries-and-archives/e-resource-renewal-analytics/profile|E-Resource Renewal & Usage Analytics]]
**Industry:** [[industries/libraries-and-archives|Libraries & Archives]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Contract lifecycle platforms extract terms and track renewals for any contract; a publisher licence's critical terms are ones they have no model for.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #evaluation-metrics #compliance #data-integration #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to hand an academic library a renew, cancel or renegotiate number for each package — usage, open-access substitution and post-cancellation demand joined — before the publisher's renewal deadline, and whoever's number the provost and the consortium both accept takes the analytics budget.

## The Problem
Licence terms that decide renewals — price caps, cancellation allowances, perpetual access, interlibrary loan rights, text-and-data-mining and AI-use clauses — are read once at signing and forgotten.

## What Already Exists
Contract lifecycle management platforms (Ironclad, DocuSign CLM, Icertis) with AI term extraction and renewal alerts; the ERM licence fields in library platforms; model licences from library organisations.

## The Customization Gap
The library needs an extraction model for licence-specific clauses, a mapping of entitlements to KBART title lists and years, renewal alerts aligned to consortium deadlines that fall weeks before the publisher's, and output written into the ERM so access and interlibrary loan systems obey the terms. University procurement's CLM, if one exists, knows none of this.

## Target Customer
Electronic resources librarians and licensing staff at universities and consortia.

## Impact If Solved
Every licence's decisive terms visible before renewal, and perpetual-access and AI-use rights enforced rather than forgotten.

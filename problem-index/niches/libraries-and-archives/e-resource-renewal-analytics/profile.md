# E-Resource Renewal & Usage Analytics

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/libraries-and-archives|Libraries & Archives]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to hand an academic library a renew, cancel or renegotiate number for each package — usage, open-access substitution and post-cancellation demand joined — before the publisher's renewal deadline, and whoever's number the provost and the consortium both accept takes the analytics budget.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$150M a year in electronic resource management, usage analytics, subscription-agent services and consortium analysis serving US academic and research libraries (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~1% (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — usage arrives digitally through COUNTER and SUSHI, but the renewal analysis is assembled in spreadsheets each year
**Target Buyer:** Head of collections or electronic resources librarian at a university library; consortium licensing director
**Automation Potential:** High — harvesting, reconciliation and scenario modelling are repetitive; negotiation is not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Academic libraries spend most of their materials budget on licensed e-resources (estimate), and every licence comes up for renewal on the publisher's date. The analysis behind each renew, cancel or renegotiate decision has the same shape every year: usage, cost per use, overlap, open-access substitution, interlibrary loan after cancellation, and contract terms. Funder mandates shift the inputs: NIH's public access policy removed the embargo on NIH-funded manuscripts from 1 July 2025. This is a decision-support contest, distinct from the platform that stores the licence, covered under [[niches/libraries-and-archives/academic-unified-resource-management/profile|Academic Unified Resource Management]].

## Current Tools & Gaps
ERM and analytics modules in Alma and FOLIO, COUNTER harvesting services, Unsub for journal-cancellation scenarios, subscription agents' spend reports (EBSCO, Harrassowitz), and consortium analysts. Gaps: no post-cancellation demand estimate grounded in real cancellations; usage reports that disagree across platforms and COUNTER releases; licence terms (perpetual access, text-and-data-mining and AI clauses) kept in PDFs.

# Library Services Platforms

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/libraries-and-archives|Libraries & Archives]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is: every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to become the system of record a library will not migrate off — but academic libraries choose a platform on how well it manages licensed electronic resources alongside print, and public libraries choose on circulation, holds and patron service across a shared consortium catalogue, and the two contests have different winners. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$900M a year in US spend on integrated library systems, library services platforms, discovery layers and resource-sharing software (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~4% of combined public and academic library spending (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** High — every library above the smallest runs one, mostly hosted, but contracts commonly last an estimated seven to fifteen years and migrations are rare and feared
**Target Buyer:** Library director, associate university librarian for technical services, consortium executive director
**Automation Potential:** Medium — transactions are automated; migration, configuration and data quality are still hand work

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
This is the largest software line in a library's budget and the one it changes least often. The market is concentrated: Clarivate owns Ex Libris (Alma, at 2,745 libraries in the 2025 Library Systems Report) and Innovative (Sierra, Polaris); OCLC runs WorldShare Management Services; EBSCO backs the open-source FOLIO; SirsiDynix and Koha and Evergreen hosts hold much of the public market. A platform decision is made by RFP, often by a consortium on behalf of dozens of members, and lives for a decade.

## Current Tools & Gaps
The platforms are mature at the transaction. The gaps are at the edges of a contract: migrations that drop order notes, weeding history and licence annotations; analytics modules that report but do not recommend; and a decision record — why the collection looks as it does — that no platform models.

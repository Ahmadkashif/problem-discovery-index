# Licences That Expire Without a Decision

**Niche:** [[niches/libraries-and-archives/public-digital-lending/profile|Public Library Digital Lending]]
**Industry:** [[industries/libraries-and-archives|Libraries & Archives]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Hundreds of ebook licences lapse every month, and whether each is renewed depends on an auto-buy rule or a selector's free afternoon.
**Tags:** #survival-analysis #gradient-boosting #evaluation-metrics #worker-facing #quick-win #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to deliver the most patron loans per licence dollar under publishers' expiring and pay-per-use terms, and whoever proves cost per loan on the library's own data and helps the selector act on it takes the library's digital content budget.

## The Problem
Expiring licences appear in a report the selector may or may not open. Some libraries auto-repurchase anything with holds; others let everything lapse unless someone intervenes. Either way the decision is a default.

## Why It's Still Broken
The volume is large, the price differs by title and term, and nothing in the platform estimates whether the next licence would be used.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A monthly expiry list ranked by expected loans per dollar over the next licence term, with a renew, lapse or replace-with-cheaper-format recommendation and the reason, approved in bulk by the selector.

## Who Feels the Pain
Selectors handling expiries on top of new orders; patrons who find a backlist title has disappeared.

## Impact If Fixed
Renewal money spent on titles that will circulate rather than on defaults, and lapses that are chosen rather than accidental.

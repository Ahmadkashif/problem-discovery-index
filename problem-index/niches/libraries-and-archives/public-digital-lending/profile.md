# Public Library Digital Lending

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/libraries-and-archives|Libraries & Archives]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to deliver the most patron loans per licence dollar under publishers' expiring and pay-per-use terms, and whoever proves cost per loan on the library's own data and helps the selector act on it takes the library's digital content budget.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$650M a year in US public library spend on ebook, e-audiobook, digital magazine and streaming licences (estimate; public libraries spent ~$619M on electronic materials in FY 2022, derived from the IMLS survey)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~4% of public library operating spend (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** High — lending is fully digital; the decisions about what to license and renew are made by hand in vendor carts
**Target Buyer:** Collection development manager or digital services librarian at a public library system; e-content manager at a state or regional consortium
**Automation Potential:** High for licence and holds management; low for publisher terms

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Digital lending is the fastest-growing part of public library circulation: readers borrowed over 820 million titles through OverDrive's Libby and Sora in 2025, up 10.9% (Sora is OverDrive's K-12 school app, so the figure includes school lending), and electronic items were about 27% of public library circulation in FY 2022 (derived). Unlike print, the library rarely owns what it lends. Publishers sell library ebooks and audiobooks on licences that expire after a term or a number of loans, at prices well above retail, or on pay-per-use terms through hoopla. Every title is a recurring cost decision about copies, holds and renewals.

## Current Tools & Gaps
OverDrive Marketplace dominates, with hoopla, cloudLibrary (bibliotheca) and the Palace Project (Lyrasis) competing; each offers purchase suggestions and reports on its own platform. Gaps: no cross-platform view of cost per loan; holds-to-copy ratios set by rule of thumb; expiring licences renewed or dropped by default; and pay-per-use budgets exhausted mid-month.

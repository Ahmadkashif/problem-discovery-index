# A Rights Map for Every Local Newspaper Run

**Niche:** [[niches/libraries-and-archives/local-history-digitisation/profile|Local History & Newspaper Digitisation]]
**Industry:** [[industries/libraries-and-archives|Libraries & Archives]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A small library cannot digitise and publish its newspaper run until it knows which years are in the public domain and what any past partner contract allows, and nobody has mapped either.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #feature-engineering #evaluation-metrics #compliance #data-integration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn a small institution's newspapers, photographs and records into searchable online collections at a per-page cost a grant can carry, and whoever does it with the fewest strings on the library's rights to its own content takes the collection.

## The Problem
A town newspaper's run spans a century. Issues published 95 or more years ago are in the US public domain; later issues may be too if the publisher never registered or renewed copyright, which was common for local papers, but checking means searching registration and renewal records title by title. Some runs were already filmed or scanned under a genealogy platform's partnership whose exclusivity window the library has lost track of. Uncertainty means the library either publishes nothing after the 1920s or takes a risk it cannot assess.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Rights research is slow, legalistic and specific to each title, and the buyers are small institutions with grant budgets. Commercial platforms have the research capacity but an interest in keeping exclusive content.

## What to Build
A shared rights service: for each newspaper title and year range, assemble registration and renewal evidence from digitised Copyright Office records, publisher ownership history, and any known digitisation agreements, and output a status per year — public domain, likely public domain with evidence, in copyright, or under contract — with the documentation a library board or grant officer needs.

## Target Customer
State libraries and state newspaper projects, which could license it for every library in the state; local history librarians directly.

## Impact If Built
Decades of local newspaper pages published with documented rights status instead of left offline out of caution.

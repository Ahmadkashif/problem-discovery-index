# Inventory & Demand Planning

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/d2c-brand-operators|D2C Brand Operators]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to tell a brand how much to buy six months ahead without a stockout or a markdown — and whoever does that takes the account, because that decision commits more cash than any other the brand makes and is currently made in a spreadsheet.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$3.8B US in working capital and margin destroyed
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~13% of category operating spend
**Digital Adoption:** Very Low — six months of cash committed in a spreadsheet
**Target Buyer:** Merchandising and finance functions
**Automation Potential:** Very High — forecasting and buy optimisation are mature

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Demand planning software is a mature enterprise category and the direct-to-consumer brand buying six months of stock is doing it in a spreadsheet against a forecast that is last year plus a growth assumption. The decision commits more cash than anything else the brand does, it is made once per season with a long lead time, and both failure modes destroy margin: stock out and the demand goes to a competitor and the marketing spend that generated it is wasted, overbuy and the excess is discounted to clear at a loss. The brand has its own sales history, its marketing calendar, its return rates and its supplier lead times — every input a forecast needs — and the decision is made by a person looking at last season's numbers.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Spreadsheets, platform inventory reports, reorder point rules, and a small number of planning apps. The gaps: no statistical forecast, so seasonality and trend are eyeballed; marketing plans are not an input despite driving the demand; uncertainty is not quantified, so safety stock is a guess; the cost of a stockout is never compared to the cost of excess; and new products, which have no history, are the largest source of error and get the least method.

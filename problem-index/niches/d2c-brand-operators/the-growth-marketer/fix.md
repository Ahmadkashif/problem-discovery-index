# Explaining the Same Discrepancy Every Week

**Niche:** [[niches/d2c-brand-operators/the-growth-marketer/profile|The Growth Marketer]]
**Industry:** [[industries/d2c-brand-operators|D2C Brand Operators]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The gap between platform-claimed conversions and actual orders is re-explained verbally in every weekly meeting, and it is a number that could be tracked, decomposed and monitored like any other.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #change-point-detection #confidence-intervals #hypothesis-testing #data-integration #quick-win #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to stop a skilled marketer spending their week assembling a report they do not believe — and whoever does that takes the account, because that week is the largest recurring waste in the function.

## The Problem
Every weekly review reaches the same moment: somebody points out that the platforms claim more conversions than the brand had orders, the marketer explains attribution windows, view-through conversions and modelled estimates, everybody nods, and the meeting proceeds on the numbers anyway. The same explanation is given every week to the same people. It is never written down, never quantified, never tracked, and never used for anything — even though the gap itself is a measurable quantity whose movements carry real information about tracking health and channel behaviour.

## Why It's Still Broken
The gap is treated as a known annoyance rather than as a metric, so nobody thought to track it. Explaining it is easier than quantifying it, and the explanation satisfies the room. The marketer has no time to build the tracking and no mandate to. And a number that shows the platforms over-claiming by a stable factor is awkward to put in front of a board that approved the spend.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Make the gap a tracked metric. Compute the ratio of claimed conversions to actual orders per channel per week and chart it, which is one arithmetic step on data already assembled and converts a recurring verbal caveat into a monitored quantity — this is the fix and it takes an afternoon. Decompose the gap into its known causes where possible: overlap between channels, view-through windows, modelled conversions, tracking loss, and genuine attribution ambiguity. Alert when the ratio moves, since a sudden change is almost always a tracking break and is currently discovered weeks later. Report the gap alongside the headline figures so the caveat is in the document rather than in somebody's memory. Use the stable component as a deflator, so the reported channel figures are at least internally consistent even before the incrementality work is done. Write the explanation down once, so a new team member does not require a verbal briefing to read the report. Track it per channel, since the channels differ enormously and the aggregate hides which one is over-claiming most. And present it to leadership deliberately, because a board that understands the gap makes better decisions than one that has been protected from it.

## Who Feels the Pain
Marketers repeating the same caveat weekly; leadership making decisions on numbers whose caveats live in someone else's head; and new joiners who read the report without the verbal briefing and believe it.

## Impact If Fixed
The gap is a computable ratio, it moves, and its movements are informative — and it is currently a verbal footnote. Alerting on a change catches tracking breaks that are otherwise found weeks later, and writing the explanation down once ends the weekly repetition.

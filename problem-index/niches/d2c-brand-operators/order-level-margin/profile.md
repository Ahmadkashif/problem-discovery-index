# Order-Level Margin

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/d2c-brand-operators|D2C Brand Operators]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to tell a brand what each order actually made after everything — and whoever does that takes the account, because every decision in the business is currently made on revenue while the margin varies from healthy to negative order by order.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.6B US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~6% of category operating spend
**Digital Adoption:** Low — revenue is known, margin is not
**Target Buyer:** Finance and growth functions jointly
**Automation Potential:** Very High — every component is in a system somewhere

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A brand knows its revenue to the penny and its margin approximately, once a month, in aggregate. Order by order it is unknown: the cost of goods, the discount applied, the shipping cost actually incurred, the payment fees, the return probability and cost, the pick and pack cost, and the advertising cost attributable to that customer all sit in different systems and are never joined. The result is that every operational decision — which products to promote, which channels to scale, which customers to pursue, whether free shipping is working, whether a discount code is profitable — is made on revenue proxies while the actual margin ranges from healthy to negative across the order book. Every component exists in a system the brand already runs.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Monthly management accounts, platform revenue reporting, and a cost of goods field. The gaps: no per-order margin; returns are accounted at a blended rate rather than per product; shipping cost is a subsidy of unknown size per order; discount codes are measured on redemption rather than on incremental margin; and no product, channel or customer is ranked by contribution.

## Why These Niches

The measurement foundation of this industry collapsed and was not rebuilt. Platform-reported conversions, the analytics platform and the brand's own order data disagree, sometimes by large factors, and the discrepancy is not reconcilable — so budget is allocated by people who know their numbers are wrong and have nothing better. Establishing what marketing actually caused, as opposed to what each channel claims, is therefore the largest contested surface in the category and the one every other decision rests on.

The growth stack **failed the filter as one niche**. Paid acquisition is fought over cost per incremental new customer in an auction whose prices rise structurally, run by performance marketers whose alternative is another channel or another brand outbidding them. Owned retention is fought over repeat purchase rate and lifetime value from customers already acquired, run by lifecycle marketers whose alternative is spending more on acquisition instead, with a near-zero marginal cost per message and a completely different constraint — audience fatigue rather than auction price. The teams, the economics, the vendors and the failure modes are unrelated, and the tension between them is the category's defining strategic argument. Decomposed below.

The two underdigitised areas are both places where money is committed on a guess. A brand commits six months of cash to stock against a forecast that is last year plus a growth assumption, in a spreadsheet, while mature demand planning software exists. And creative — the dominant performance variable in paid social — is produced at volume with no basis for deciding what to make, because the tools made production cheap and did nothing about the decision.

The two underserved constituencies are the growth marketer who spends a large share of every week reconciling platforms that disagree to produce a report whose central figures they do not believe, and the customer experience agent whose day is spent reading back a tracking number the customer already has.

The automation niches are order-level margin, which every brand could compute and few do, and the first-party customer corpus — the one dataset the advertising platforms do not have and most brands do not use.

## Filter Notes

Seven of the eight level-1 niches are terminal. **Growth & Retention Stack** is not: it names the marketing function rather than a contest, and the two halves are run by different people on opposite economics. Paid acquisition buys attention in an auction at a rising price, is measured in cost per new customer, and competes against every other bidder for the same impression. Owned retention sends messages at near-zero marginal cost to people who already bought, is measured in repeat rate and lifetime value, and is constrained by audience fatigue rather than by price. A brand's central strategic question is how to split between them, which only makes sense as a question because they are two different contests. Decomposed into two contested sub-niches.

Two candidates were rejected. *Storefront and commerce platform software* was rejected because its contest belongs to the commerce platform industries covered separately in this vault, and the brand here is its customer. *Fulfilment and delivery* was rejected because it belongs to the logistics industries covered separately, and the brand's role is selection and integration rather than competition.

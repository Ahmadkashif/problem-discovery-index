# Niche Analysis — Technical Content Agencies

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/technical-content-agencies|Technical Content Agencies]]

## Niche Selection

Technical documentation exists so that someone with a task completes it, and the field measures traffic. It also has the cleanest available signal of failure — searches that returned nothing, rephrasings, the ticket opened straight after a page — sitting unused in every documentation site's logs. Meanwhile the reader is increasingly a machine synthesising an answer without arriving at all. The eight niches below follow both: what the corpus does for a person, what it does for an assistant, and what keeps it true.

## Why These Niches

Effectiveness measurement and assistant readiness are the two questions that determine whether the work is worth anything: whether a person with a task finished it, and whether a machine answering on the corpus's behalf answers correctly. Content drift and the engineering review dependency are the two conditions that make documentation untrue — code that ships weekly against prose that does not, and a writer who cannot ship until an engineer with other commitments confirms the detail. The two underserved audiences are the person keeping the build, search index and versioning working in time carved out of writing, and the freelance writer with no access to the engineers or the outcomes. The last two are mechanical: generating reference material, and keeping the corpus consistent.

## Filter Notes

Seven of the eight are terminal — each names one contest that every serious competitor is fighting over, and decomposing further would produce features rather than markets.

**Assistant readiness** is not. It names a property, and achieving it requires two things that are different work done by different people at different points. Corpus structure and disambiguation asks whether the content is written and structured so that a machine reading it arrives at the right answer — unambiguous references, explicit prerequisites, versioned statements, semantic structure rather than visual formatting — which is an authoring and information architecture problem sold into the documentation practice, and improves the corpus for human readers regardless of what any assistant does with it. Synthesis verification asks what assistants actually answer when asked about the product — a measurement problem against third-party systems nobody controls, run continuously, where the finding may be that a correct corpus is being synthesised into a wrong answer for reasons the writer cannot fix by writing better. The first is craft the field is unusually well-equipped for; the second is an outward-facing measurement it has never attempted, and a corpus can be excellent at one and fail the other.

Two adjacent candidates were rejected as belonging elsewhere: **translating the documentation** belongs to [[industries/localization-services|Localization Services]], and **the developer relations programme the content supports** sits with [[industries/developer-relations-agencies|Developer Relations Agencies]].

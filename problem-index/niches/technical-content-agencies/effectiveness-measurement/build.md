# The Record of Where Readers Failed

**Niche:** [[niches/technical-content-agencies/effectiveness-measurement/profile|Effectiveness Measurement]]
**Industry:** [[industries/technical-content-agencies|Technical Content Agencies]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** The cleanest failure signal in professional services sits in every documentation site's logs and nobody reads it.
**Tags:** #data-integration #evaluation-metrics #word-embeddings #descriptive-statistics #k-means-clustering #automation #confidence-intervals #large-language-models
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to establish whether a reader with a task completed it, when every documentation site holds the exact record of where readers failed and reports pageviews — and whoever uses that record takes the account.

## The Problem
Documentation's purpose is task completion and its measurement is attention. Traffic, time on page and ranking describe whether someone arrived, not whether they left with an answer. Meanwhile the failures are recorded precisely: a search returning nothing, a query rephrased three times, a page visited immediately before a support ticket, a sequence of pages that reads as someone lost. None of it is analysed.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The metrics were inherited from marketing, where attention is the outcome. Site search logs and support tickets sit in different systems from analytics. Nobody owns the joined question. And pageviews are easy to report and hard to argue with.

## What to Build
Read the failure signals that already exist and act on them. Analyse failed and repeated site searches as the primary signal, which is the core — a search that returns nothing is a reader telling you exactly what is missing, in their own words. Detect rephrasing sequences, since a reader reformulating a query three times is a stronger failure signal than any bounce rate. Link page visits to subsequent support tickets, which identifies the pages that look like answers and are not. Identify tickets whose answer exists in the documentation, as those are pure findability failures and a large share of the total. Measure whether the reader's session ended in a resolution behaviour — copying a code sample, completing a flow, no ticket — rather than in a duration. Rank documentation work from the signals rather than from stakeholder requests, which is what turns measurement into a practice. Report the failure list to writers in their own terms rather than as analytics. Feed the missing-content list into the editorial plan continuously. Establish a baseline so improvement is demonstrable. And report on resolution rather than traffic to the client, which is the change that makes the rest matter commercially.

## Target Customer
Documentation teams and technical content agencies, developer experience leadership, documentation platform vendors, and support operations.

## Impact If Built
A search that returns nothing is a reader telling you exactly what is missing, in their own words, and nobody reads the log. Failure signals already in the logs turn a traffic report into a work list.

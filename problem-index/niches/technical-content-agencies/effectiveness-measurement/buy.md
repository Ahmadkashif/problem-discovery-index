# Deflection Measurement From Support Operations

**Niche:** [[niches/technical-content-agencies/effectiveness-measurement/profile|Effectiveness Measurement]]
**Industry:** [[industries/technical-content-agencies|Technical Content Agencies]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Support operations measures whether an article resolved the question, and documentation measures whether it was visited.
**Tags:** #evaluation-metrics #data-integration #descriptive-statistics #confidence-intervals #causal-inference #automation #word-embeddings #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to establish whether a reader with a task completed it, when every documentation site holds the exact record of where readers failed and reports pageviews — and whoever uses that record takes the account.

## The Problem
Support operations built the measurement that documentation lacks. Deflection tracks whether a self-service article prevented a contact; article effectiveness is measured by whether the reader went on to open a ticket; failed searches drive the content backlog; and the knowledge base's success metric is resolution rather than traffic. The practice is standard because support has a cost per contact that makes the question unavoidable. Documentation has the same content and the marketing metric.

## What Already Exists
Deflection and resolution measurement; article effectiveness by subsequent contact; failed search analysis driving content creation; content gap identification from ticket themes; and resolution as the governing metric.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a reader who is a developer completing a build task rather than a customer with a problem. It requires: (1) success being task completion in a separate environment — the reader's own code — rather than the absence of a ticket, since developers frequently succeed or fail without contacting anyone, which is the substantive difference; (2) an audience with high tolerance for searching elsewhere, so absence of a ticket is weak evidence; (3) content that is reference as well as procedural, where lookup is the intended use; (4) documentation frequently public with no identified reader; and (5) a growing share of readers who never arrive because an assistant answered first.

## Target Customer
Documentation teams and agencies, developer experience leadership, documentation platform vendors, and support operations providers.

## Impact If Solved
Support made resolution the governing metric because a cost per contact made the question unavoidable. A reader whose success happens in their own code, and who may never contact anyone, is what documentation has to measure differently.

# Nobody Has Ever Asked

**Niche:** [[niches/technical-content-agencies/synthesis-verification/profile|Synthesis Verification]]
**Industry:** [[industries/technical-content-agencies|Technical Content Agencies]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** No one at the company has ever asked an assistant a basic question about their own product and checked the answer.
**Tags:** #quick-win #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #large-language-models #automation #confidence-intervals #compliance #data-integration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to find out what assistants are actually telling developers about a product, from systems nobody controls — and whoever monitors that takes the account.

## The Problem
The simplest version of the whole problem is that nobody has looked. Asking an assistant twenty common questions about a product and checking the answers against reality takes an hour and has not been done at most companies. The results are frequently alarming — deprecated methods recommended, versions confused, parameters invented — and the fact that nobody knows is a straightforward oversight rather than a hard problem.

## Why It's Still Broken
It belongs to no team — a question about what a third-party system says about the product sits between documentation, product and marketing, and therefore with nobody. The metrics do not cover it. It is nobody's target. And the first hour of looking has not happened.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Spend the hour, then decide whether it warrants more. Ask the major assistants twenty common questions about the product and record the answers, which is the fix and takes an afternoon. Check each against what is actually true, which the product team can do quickly. Note the type of each error, since the pattern is usually informative immediately. Show the results to product, documentation and support, as the reaction is what determines whether anything follows. Trace two or three wrong answers to their source, which demonstrates that the corpus is fixable. Repeat after a documentation change to see whether it moved. Add the questions your support team receives most, which is where the commercial consequence is. Check the questions a prospective customer would ask, since those affect acquisition rather than support. Keep the results, so a trend exists. And assign it to somebody, because the reason it has not happened is that it belongs to nobody.

## Who Feels the Pain
Developers acting on wrong answers; support teams receiving the consequences; product teams whose product is misdescribed at scale; and companies who would want to know and have never asked.

## Impact If Fixed
A question about what a third-party system says about the product sits between three teams and therefore with nobody. An afternoon asking twenty questions is the whole first step.

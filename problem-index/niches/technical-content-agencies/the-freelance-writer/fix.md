# Asked to Document a Product Nobody Gave Them

**Niche:** [[niches/technical-content-agencies/the-freelance-writer/profile|The Freelance Technical Writer]]
**Industry:** [[industries/technical-content-agencies|Technical Content Agencies]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The brief asks for a tutorial and the writer has never been able to log in.
**Tags:** #worker-facing #quick-win #workflow-orchestration #compliance #evaluation-metrics #data-integration #automation #descriptive-statistics
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to let a freelance writer produce accurate technical content without access to the engineers, the product or the outcome — and whoever equips them takes the account.

## The Problem
The commonest failure in freelance technical content is writing about something the writer cannot use. A tutorial written from the public documentation of a product the writer has never run will contain plausible errors — a step in the wrong order, an omitted prerequisite, an interface that has changed. The client receives content that reads well and does not work, and attributes it to the writer's competence rather than to the access they were not given.

## Why It's Still Broken
Access provisioning is nobody's step — a short engagement with a contractor falls outside the onboarding process, so the request either takes longer than the engagement or is never made. Security approval is slow. The agency does not have access to pass on. And the writer proceeds rather than losing the work.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Provision access before the brief, or scope the work to what is possible without it. Issue product access as the first step of every commission rather than as a request, which is the fix and requires a standing process rather than a per-engagement negotiation. Use a sandbox or demo environment where production access is impossible, which is usually sufficient and always faster. Scope the deliverable to what the available access supports, and say so explicitly, which is honest and prevents the failure. Give the writer a recorded walkthrough where no access can be provided, which is far better than nothing. Provide a named contact for factual questions with a response expectation. Let the writer mark statements they could not verify, which is a professional practice the current arrangement suppresses. Review the content against the real product before publishing, which catches what the writer could not. Build a standing access process for regular contributors rather than repeating it. Ask contractors what access they need at briefing rather than assuming. And treat a piece written without access as unverified rather than as finished.

## Who Feels the Pain
Writers producing content they know they cannot verify; clients publishing tutorials that do not work; readers following steps that fail; and the freelancer's reputation, damaged by a constraint they did not set.

## Impact If Fixed
A short engagement with a contractor falls outside onboarding, so access either takes longer than the engagement or is never requested. A standing provisioning step is what separates verified content from plausible content.

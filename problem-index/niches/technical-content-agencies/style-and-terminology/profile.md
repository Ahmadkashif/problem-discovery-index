# Style & Terminology Consistency

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/technical-content-agencies|Technical Content Agencies]]
**Category:** ⚡ Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep one term meaning one thing across a corpus written by many people over many years, and whoever enforces that mechanically takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$90M US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~3% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — linting
**Target Buyer:** Editorial leadership
**Automation Potential:** Very high — linting and terminology enforcement

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A documentation corpus written by many people over years drifts terminologically: the same concept has three names, the same word means two things, capitalisation and product naming vary, and deprecated terms persist. It confuses readers, breaks search, and degrades machine synthesis badly. Style linting exists and is adopted patchily, and terminology consistency — the part that actually affects comprehension — is usually enforced by editors reading.

## Current Tools & Gaps
A style guide, a linter with some rules and an editor. The gaps: no enforced terminology; no detection of the same concept named differently; no detection of one term used for two concepts; no terminology change propagation; and no coverage of the older corpus.

## Why These Niches

Catering fragments along event type (corporate vs. wedding vs. social), production model (commissary kitchen vs. on-site), and business function (sales vs. production vs. staffing). These 8 niches cover the two largest revenue segments (corporate and wedding, together representing 65% of the market), the two most digitally neglected (small social caterers and mobile production operations that exist entirely on paper), the two most underserved populations (multi-event kitchens and dietary-complex events that no tool handles well), and the two highest-ROI automation targets (the proposal-to-invoice pipeline and temp crew management, both rule-heavy and currently manual). Excluded: institutional catering (hospitals, schools — different contract model), food truck catering (covered under food trucks), and restaurant private dining (a different workflow).

## Why These Pockets

Catering itself is a project business run by chefs, and nothing at that altitude survives the gate. What the sweep finds above it are two very different insight businesses, both of which sell judgment as the product.

Price reporting agencies are the more striking of the two, and structurally the closest thing in the vault to the tax research publishers found in accounting. In markets with no exchange — protein, produce, dairy — a reporter spends the day on the phone, assesses where trade actually occurred, and publishes a number that contracts then settle against by name. The clock is daily and absolute, the judgment is the invoice, and the assessment record behind every published figure is kept nowhere. That is a remarkable exposure for a business whose product is credibility, and it is becoming a regulatory one as benchmark governance expectations migrate from energy and financial price reporting into food.

Foodservice market intelligence is the second, and its gap is a measurement one. The whole product is a longitudinal menu database coded by hand against a proprietary taxonomy, sold on the strength of trend claims — and nobody has ever measured whether two coders reading the same dish description code it the same way. In a product whose value is change over time, coding drift is indistinguishable from a real trend, which makes the unmeasured consistency a liability sitting directly underneath the flagship.

Eleven pockets logged without qualifying, and this industry produces the sharpest instance yet of the sweep's recurring pattern. The two largest insight workforces found — food safety auditors at up to 1,500 and distributor category teams at up to 500 — score 48 and 43, because one sells a scored inspection and the other gives away the best operator purchase data in the industry to sell food. The GPO position holds line-item purchase data across billions in spend and earns a rebate on purchasing. And the purest insight-as-invoice shape in the industry, the annual food trend forecast that every hospitality operator cites, is produced by studios of one or two people.

# The Search Path Nobody Saved

**Niche:** [[niches/legal-support-services/patent-search-prior-art/profile|Patent Search & Prior-Art Analysis]]
**Industry:** [[industries/legal-support-services|Legal Support Services]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The queries, classes and dead ends of a two-week search vanish when the report is sent, so the next search on the same family starts from zero.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #feature-engineering #data-integration #quick-win #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to find the closest prior art — including non-patent and foreign-language literature — before the examiner, the PTAB petitioner or the opposing expert does, and whoever's searches are least often beaten by art someone else finds later takes the account.

## The Problem
Clients come back: a continuation, an opposition, a new defendant on the same patent. The analyst who did the first search may have left; the report lists references found, not the strategy that found them or the documents rejected.

## Why It's Still Broken
Search platforms keep session histories in their own format and expire them; houses use several platforms per search; and nobody is paid to archive strategy.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Capture every query, class and screened document across platforms into one search record attached to the matter, with reasons for exclusion in a word or two, searchable when the family returns.

## Who Feels the Pain
Search analysts repeating work; clients paying for it twice; houses losing know-how when analysts leave.

## Impact If Fixed
Repeat searches on known families in an estimated fraction of the original time (estimate), and the strategy record that grading a search requires.

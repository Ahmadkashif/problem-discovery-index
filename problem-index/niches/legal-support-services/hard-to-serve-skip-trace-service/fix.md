# The Diligence Declaration Written From Memory

**Niche:** [[niches/legal-support-services/hard-to-serve-skip-trace-service/profile|Hard-to-Serve & Skip-Trace Service]]
**Industry:** [[industries/legal-support-services|Legal Support Services]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** When an attorney asks for substituted service, the server writes the diligence declaration days later from texts and memory, and a thin declaration gets the motion denied and the window keeps running.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #seq2seq #compliance #quick-win #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to locate and serve the defendant who does not want to be found inside the service window, and whoever converts the most dead-address and evasive files into valid service before the court's deadline takes the litigator's account.

## The Problem
Judges grant alternative service only on a showing of reasonable diligence. The record of attempts, stakeouts and inquiries lives in the server's phone, notes and texts with the attorney. Declarations come out vague ("multiple attempts at various times"), and courts deny them.

## Why It's Still Broken
Attempt logging tools are built for the routine serve, not for a week of investigation, and nobody owns turning the record into the document the judge reads.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Every attempt, stakeout and inquiry logged with one tap as it happens, and a declaration drafted from the log on demand, itemised with times, places and evidence, ready for the server to review and sign.

## Who Feels the Pain
Litigators watching the service window close; servers rewriting declarations; plaintiffs whose cases stall.

## Impact If Fixed
Stronger declarations filed days sooner, and fewer denied motions for alternative service.

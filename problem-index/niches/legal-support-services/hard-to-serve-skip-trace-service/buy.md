# Investigator Case Management Fitted to the Service Window

**Niche:** [[niches/legal-support-services/hard-to-serve-skip-trace-service/profile|Hard-to-Serve & Skip-Trace Service]]
**Industry:** [[industries/legal-support-services|Legal Support Services]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Private-investigator case-management software already tracks subjects, surveillance hours and evidence; it does not count down a service deadline or build the diligence record a judge needs before allowing substituted service.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #feature-engineering #workflow-orchestration #compliance #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to locate and serve the defendant who does not want to be found inside the service window, and whoever converts the most dead-address and evasive files into valid service before the court's deadline takes the litigator's account.

## The Problem
Hard-serve work looks like a small investigation: subject profile, candidate locations, surveillance windows, evidence of evasion. Servers track it in notes and spreadsheets, and reconstruct it when the attorney needs a declaration of diligence.

## What Already Exists
Investigator platforms such as Trackops and CaseFleet manage subjects, surveillance logs, hours and evidence, with client reporting.

## The Customization Gap
Hard-serve work needs: a service-window clock per case tied to the court's rules; attempt logging that maps directly to each state's diligence standard for substituted service or publication; a declaration generator that cites every attempt with time, place and evidence; integration with skip-trace vendors and server-management software; and per-job rather than hourly billing options.

## Target Customer
Hard-serve specialists and server firms that also hold investigator licences.

## Impact If Solved
Diligence declarations produced from the record instead of memory, faster court approval of alternative service, and one system instead of three.

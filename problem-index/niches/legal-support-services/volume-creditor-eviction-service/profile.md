# Volume Creditor & Eviction Service

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/legal-support-services|Legal Support Services]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to make every affidavit at volume provably true — GPS, photo and time evidence that survives a traverse hearing and a regulator's audit — and whoever can show a volume filer the lowest rate of vacated defaults per thousand serves takes the filer's docket.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.1B a year in serves for consumer-debt, eviction and other high-volume filers (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~15% (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium-High — volume filers send jobs by API or bulk upload and national networks geotag every attempt
**Target Buyer:** Operations head at a national service network; managing partner or litigation operations lead at a creditor-rights or landlord law firm
**Automation Potential:** High for records, routing and affidavits; the attempt itself stays physical

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Debt buyers' law firms and landlords' attorneys file tens of thousands of near-identical cases and buy service by the batch at a low flat fee. Defendants rarely answer — about 17% did in New York City consumer-credit cases in 2019–2023 — so the affidavit almost always becomes the basis for a default judgment. That makes the evidence behind each affidavit the whole risk: sewer-service class actions (a $59M settlement in 2015 that vacated nearly 200,000 judgments), attorney-general actions and NYC's licensing, GPS and electronic-log rules all target this segment. The filer is buying defensibility at volume, not heroics on any one serve.

## Current Tools & Gaps
ABC Legal, Proof and regional networks with photo and GPS records; ServeManager at local firms; NYC DCWP monthly recordkeeping reports for licensed agencies. Gaps: evidence is captured but not checked for plausibility before filing; nobody reports vacated-default rates by network or server; and the GPS record proves presence at an address, not service on a person.

# Transaction Monitoring Pointed at Affidavits

**Niche:** [[niches/legal-support-services/volume-creditor-eviction-service/profile|Volume Creditor & Eviction Service]]
**Industry:** [[industries/legal-support-services|Legal Support Services]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Banks already score millions of transactions a day for patterns no honest customer would produce; serve affidavits have the same structure and no one monitors them.
**Tags:** #gaussian-mixture-models #dbscan #feature-engineering #hypothesis-testing #compliance #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to make every affidavit at volume provably true — GPS, photo and time evidence that survives a traverse hearing and a regulator's audit — and whoever can show a volume filer the lowest rate of vacated defaults per thousand serves takes the filer's docket.

## The Problem
A network handling a large volume of filer work cannot review every affidavit against the GPS trail. Fabrication shows up as patterns — a server who "served" two addresses forty miles apart ten minutes apart, identical descriptions across unrelated defendants, every serve at 9:00 p.m. — visible only in aggregate.

## What Already Exists
Transaction-monitoring and fraud platforms (NICE Actimize, Featurespace, SAS fraud tools) score event streams for anomalies, with case management for investigators.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs: spatio-temporal features from attempt trails (travel feasibility, dwell time, clustering of attempt times); text features from affidavits (description reuse, boilerplate); jurisdiction rules as hard checks; a pre-filing hold so flagged affidavits are reviewed before they reach court; and investigator case management sized for a network's quality team rather than a bank's fraud unit.

## Target Customer
Quality and compliance heads at national service networks; vendor-management teams at debt buyers and large landlord firms.

## Impact If Solved
Fabricated or careless affidavits are caught before filing rather than after a default is challenged, cutting class-action and regulatory exposure for networks and filers alike.

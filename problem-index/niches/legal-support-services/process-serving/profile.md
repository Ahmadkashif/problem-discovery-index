# Process Serving

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/legal-support-services|Legal Support Services]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is that every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to prove valid service of process — but the volume creditor and eviction market and the hard-to-serve litigation market reward different winners on different evidence, so it is decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$2.0B a year in US process-serving fees, employer and sole-proprietor firms combined (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~27% (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — national networks geotag and photograph every attempt; most local servers run on a phone, ServeManager or paper logs
**Target Buyer:** Owner of a multi-server firm; operations head at a national service network; litigation support manager at a law firm
**Automation Potential:** Medium — routing, records and affidavits automate; the knock on the door does not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Service of process is the one step in civil procedure that requires a person at a physical place, and its proof is a sworn affidavit the court takes largely on trust. It is the largest activity in this industry by establishment count. State rules decide who may serve (California county registration with a $2,000 bond, New York City licensing with GPS and tamper-proof logs, court certification in other states), how many attempts justify substituted service, and what the return must say. Fees are mostly flat per serve, with surcharges for rush, stakeouts and skip traces.

## Current Tools & Gaps
ServeManager and similar job-management software; national networks (ABC Legal, Proof) that dispatch to thousands of independent servers; skip-trace data vendors. The gaps: attempt data used as compliance proof rather than to plan attempts; returns drafted from templates that do not know each court's requirements; and no shared record of which affidavits were later challenged. The two buyer groups want opposite things from the same server — low-cost defensible volume, or a hard target found before the deadline — which is why the contest splits.

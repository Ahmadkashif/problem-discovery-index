# The Search Report Written in Examiner Language

**Niche:** [[niches/legal-support-services/independent-inventors-startups/profile|Independent Inventors & Early-Stage Startups]]
**Industry:** [[industries/legal-support-services|Legal Support Services]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** An inventor pays for a search and receives ten patent numbers with "see col. 4, ll. 12–30" against each, and decides whether to file without understanding any of it.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #seq2seq #transformers #worker-facing #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to give a first-time inventor a trustworthy answer to "is there anything left to patent, and is it worth the cost?" for a few hundred dollars, and whoever's answer most often matches what the examiner later finds takes the inventor.

## The Problem
Search reports are written for patent professionals. An inventor needs to know what each reference shows and whether it covers their idea.

## Why It's Still Broken
Search providers' report templates were built for law-firm clients, and writing a lay explanation costs analyst time the inventor's fee does not cover.

## What a Fix Looks Like
An auto-drafted plain-language explanation of each reference against the inventor's own feature list, with the key figure shown, reviewed by the analyst before delivery.

## Who Feels the Pain
Independent inventors; agents who spend unpaid hours explaining reports by phone.

## Impact If Fixed
Better filing decisions, fewer follow-up calls, at a few minutes of review per report.

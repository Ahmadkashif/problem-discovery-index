# Independent Inventors & Early-Stage Startups

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/legal-support-services|Legal Support Services]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to give a first-time inventor a trustworthy answer to "is there anything left to patent, and is it worth the cost?" for a few hundred dollars, and whoever's answer most often matches what the examiner later finds takes the inventor.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$150M a year in patentability searches and independent patent agent services bought by individual inventors and pre-seed startups (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~2% (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — searches are digital, but the buyer arrives with a sketch, a prototype and no idea what a claim is
**Target Buyer:** The inventor or founder; independent registered patent agents and small search firms who sell to them
**Automation Potential:** High for first-pass search and explanation; medium for the judgement of what is claimable

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Corporate filers buy searches through counsel on standing contracts; the individual inventor buys once, with little money and much hope. They are the market that invention-promotion firms have long exploited, which is why federal law requires those firms to make disclosures and why inventor forums are full of warnings. The honest providers — independent patent agents (the USPTO lists 14,873 active agents) and small search firms — sell a patentability search for a few hundred to a couple of thousand dollars (estimate), and the inventor cannot judge its quality until an examiner rejects the claims two years and several thousand dollars later. Tools are built for professional searchers; the inventor needs the answer explained.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Free databases (Google Patents, Espacenet, USPTO PPUBS), USPTO inventor resources and pro bono programmes, low-cost search providers and agent practices. Gaps: search reports written for patent professionals; no estimate of what claim scope would survive; and no track record an inventor can check.

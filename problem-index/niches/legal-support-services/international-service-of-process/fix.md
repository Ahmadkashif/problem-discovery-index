# The Request Rejected Three Months Later

**Niche:** [[niches/legal-support-services/international-service-of-process/profile|International Service of Process]]
**Industry:** [[industries/legal-support-services|Legal Support Services]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A Hague request disappears into a foreign ministry and comes back by post a quarter later with a rejection for a missing copy or form field that a checklist would have caught.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #decision-trees #compliance #quick-win #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get a service request through each foreign Central Authority or court on the first submission — right form, translation, copies and fee — and whoever knows each country's current practice best returns proof of service months sooner and takes the account.

## The Problem
Once sent, a request is invisible until the authority replies. A rejection restarts the clock; the litigator explains another delay to the judge.

## Why It's Still Broken
Country requirements are scattered across HCCH declarations, practical handbooks and the specialist's memory; nobody maintains a pre-submission checklist per country.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A per-country pre-flight check of the request package — form version, copies, translations, summary of documents, fee or reimbursement — before it ships, plus a follow-up schedule that chases acknowledgement.

## Who Feels the Pain
Litigators with stalled cases; specialists absorbing re-work; clients paying for months of delay.

## Impact If Fixed
Rejections avoided at submission, saving months per affected case.

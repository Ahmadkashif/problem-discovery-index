# Translation Management Fitted to Service Requests

**Niche:** [[niches/legal-support-services/international-service-of-process/profile|International Service of Process]]
**Industry:** [[industries/legal-support-services|Legal Support Services]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Translation management systems already run multilingual projects with vendors, glossaries and quality checks; they do not know which documents a given Central Authority requires translated, into which language, certified how.
**Tags:** #transformers #large-language-models #evaluation-metrics #workflow-orchestration #compliance
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get a service request through each foreign Central Authority or court on the first submission — right form, translation, copies and fee — and whoever knows each country's current practice best returns proof of service months sooner and takes the account.

## The Problem
Translation is usually the longest and most expensive step before a request can be sent: complaint, summons and exhibits into the official language, sometimes a regional one, often certified. Errors or omissions are a common rejection reason.

## What Already Exists
Translation management platforms (Phrase, Smartling, XTM) and legal translation agencies, with machine translation and post-editing.

## The Customization Gap
Service work needs: country rules on which documents must be translated (summons, complaint, exhibits) and into which language; certification and apostille requirements; the request form and summary of documents generated in the target language; cost and time estimates per country before the client commits; and versions tied to the exact filed documents so an amended complaint triggers retranslation.

## Target Customer
International service specialists and the litigation support teams that commission translations for service abroad.

## Impact If Solved
Translations right the first time and sized before the request is filed, removing a leading cause of rejected requests.

# Country Practice Learned From Every Request

**Niche:** [[niches/legal-support-services/international-service-of-process/profile|International Service of Process]]
**Industry:** [[industries/legal-support-services|Legal Support Services]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Each foreign Central Authority has habits — forms it insists on, translations it rejects, months it really takes — that a specialist learns one request at a time and never writes down.
**Tags:** #survival-analysis #bayesian-inference #large-language-models #feature-engineering #workflow-orchestration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get a service request through each foreign Central Authority or court on the first submission — right form, translation, copies and fee — and whoever knows each country's current practice best returns proof of service months sooner and takes the account.

## The Problem
The published rules for a country say one thing; the authority's practice says another. One wants two copies with a certified translation; another sends back requests whose summary of documents is incomplete; a third takes four months in spring and nine in autumn. A specialist firm knows this from its last requests to that country, which may be a handful a year.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The volume per country is small, the knowledge sits in a few specialists' files, and the official country profiles are maintained by governments that rarely update them.

## What to Build
A request-level history across a specialist's or a consortium's filings: country, document types, translation route, submission date, acknowledgement, rejection reasons, certificate of service date. Bayesian time-to-completion estimates per country that sharpen as requests accumulate, a rejection-risk check against each country's observed reasons, and an expected-completion date the litigator can put in a case-management statement.

## Target Customer
Specialist international service firms; large litigation firms with cross-border dockets.

## Impact If Built
Fewer rejected requests, honest timelines for courts and clients, and country knowledge that survives the specialist who learned it.

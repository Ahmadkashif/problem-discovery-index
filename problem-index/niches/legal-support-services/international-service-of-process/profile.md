# International Service of Process

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/legal-support-services|Legal Support Services]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get a service request through each foreign Central Authority or court on the first submission — right form, translation, copies and fee — and whoever knows each country's current practice best returns proof of service months sooner and takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$150M a year in outbound and inbound international service, translation and letters rogatory handled by US specialists (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~2% (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — requests travel on paper forms, by courier, to government offices that reply by post months later
**Target Buyer:** Litigation paralegal manager or partner at a firm suing a foreign defendant; in-house counsel in cross-border disputes
**Automation Potential:** Medium — packaging, translation management and tracking automate; foreign authorities' timing does not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Serving a defendant abroad follows treaty, not state law. The Hague Service Convention has 84 contracting parties, each with a Central Authority, its own declarations on translation and alternative channels, and its own pace: requests typically take two to four months, against six months to a year by letters rogatory through diplomatic channels for non-member countries. FRCP 4(m)'s 90-day limit does not apply to foreign service, but cases stall, defaults cannot be taken, and courts lose patience. A handful of US specialist firms handle most of the volume, maintaining country-by-country knowledge of what each authority rejects.

## Current Tools & Gaps
The HCCH status table and country profiles, translation agencies, courier services, and specialist firms' own files and spreadsheets. Gaps: no tracking of requests once they reach a foreign authority; country practice known only from the specialist's last few requests; and translations commissioned separately from the request that needs them.

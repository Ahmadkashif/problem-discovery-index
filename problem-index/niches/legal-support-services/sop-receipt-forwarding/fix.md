# The Garnishment Routed as a Lawsuit

**Niche:** [[niches/legal-support-services/sop-receipt-forwarding/profile|Service-of-Process Receipt & Forwarding]]
**Industry:** [[industries/legal-support-services|Legal Support Services]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A wage garnishment served on the employer goes to the litigation team instead of payroll, and the employer's short window to answer passes while two departments argue about whose it is.
**Tags:** #bert #large-language-models #workflow-orchestration #quick-win #compliance
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to read every served document correctly — entity, court, case type, answer date — and put it in front of the right lawyer the same day, and whoever never lets a served suit become a default takes the account.

## The Problem
Garnishments, levies and subpoenas for employee records arrive through the same registered agent as lawsuits but belong to payroll, HR or records custodians, each with their own deadlines and liability if missed.

## Why It's Still Broken
Intake taxonomies were built around litigation, and routing rules on the client side are set once and rarely updated.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Document-type classification that separates garnishments, levies and records subpoenas from suits, with client-maintained routing to the right function and an acknowledgement requirement.

## Who Feels the Pain
Payroll and HR teams; employers facing liability for unanswered garnishments; agents fielding complaints.

## Impact If Fixed
Non-litigation documents reach the right desk the day they are served.

# Freelance & Contract Paralegal Services

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/legal-support-services|Legal Support Services]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn a supervising attorney's two-line instruction into a filing-ready draft in that court's form and that attorney's style, and whoever's drafts need the fewest minutes of attorney review takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.0B a year in freelance and contract paralegal work sold to law firms by independent paralegals and small paralegal service firms (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~13% (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — instructions arrive by email and text, drafts move as Word attachments, and preferences live in the paralegal's memory
**Target Buyer:** Solo and small-firm attorneys; the freelance paralegal as owner of their own practice
**Automation Potential:** Medium — first drafts and formatting automate; matching an attorney's judgement and a local court's habits is the craft

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Paralegals may draft nearly anything an attorney files, provided an attorney supervises and takes responsibility; unauthorised-practice rules forbid them to advise clients or appear for them. That makes the business one of supervision economics: an attorney outsources a task only if checking the result takes much less time than doing it. BLS counts 376,200 paralegal and legal assistant jobs in 2024, almost all in-house; the freelance slice serves the solo and small firms that cannot keep one busy. Typical work is discovery, deposition summaries, chronologies, probate and family-law disclosures and bankruptcy schedules, priced by the hour or the task.

## Current Tools & Gaps
The attorney's Clio or MyCase seat, Word templates, email, and freelancer directories run by paralegal associations. Gaps: no record of each attorney's corrections to learn from, conflicts tracked in spreadsheets, and nothing that documents supervision.

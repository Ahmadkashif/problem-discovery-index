# Freelance Marketplaces Taught the Supervision Rule

**Niche:** [[niches/legal-support-services/freelance-contract-paralegals/profile|Freelance & Contract Paralegal Services]]
**Industry:** [[industries/legal-support-services|Legal Support Services]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** General freelance marketplaces already match buyers to vetted freelancers, hold payment in escrow and rate the work; they know nothing about supervision, conflicts or unauthorised practice.
**Tags:** #k-nearest-neighbors #logistic-regression #compliance #workflow-orchestration #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn a supervising attorney's two-line instruction into a filing-ready draft in that court's form and that attorney's style, and whoever's drafts need the fewest minutes of attorney review takes the account.

## The Problem
Solo attorneys find freelance paralegals through referrals, association directories or general marketplaces. Vetting skill, jurisdiction knowledge and conflicts is done by phone, and the marketplace cannot tell whether a "paralegal" is offering advice directly to the public.

## What Already Exists
General freelance marketplaces (Upwork, Fiverr Pro) and legal-specific marketplaces for contract attorneys, with matching, escrow, contracts and ratings.

## The Customization Gap
Supervised legal work needs: buyers verified as licensed attorneys and sellers barred from contracting with the public; conflict screening across the freelancer's prior matters before a match; jurisdiction and court experience as matching features; engagement terms that document attorney supervision and confidentiality; and ratings measured in review time and corrections rather than stars.

## Target Customer
Marketplace operators and paralegal associations; solo attorneys as the paying side.

## Impact If Solved
Faster, safer matching for attorneys who need a task done this week, and a market where good freelancers are found by evidence rather than by luck.

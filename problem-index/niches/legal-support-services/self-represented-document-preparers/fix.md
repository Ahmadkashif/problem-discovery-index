# The Customer Who Misses the Hearing

**Niche:** [[niches/legal-support-services/self-represented-document-preparers/profile|Self-Represented Litigants & Legal Document Preparers]]
**Industry:** [[industries/legal-support-services|Legal Support Services]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The preparer gets the papers filed; the customer, alone afterwards, misses the hearing notice, the service deadline or the creditors' meeting, and the case collapses.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #time-series-forecasting #worker-facing #quick-win #workflow-orchestration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get a self-represented customer's papers accepted and the case moving without crossing into legal advice, and whoever gets the most filings through first time while staying on the right side of the unauthorised-practice line takes the customer.

## The Problem
Self-represented parties face deadlines they do not recognise: serving the other side, filing proof of service, appearing at a hearing, attending a meeting of creditors. Cases are dismissed or defaulted for missed steps rather than on the merits.

## Why It's Still Broken
The preparer's engagement ends at filing, and court notices go by post to addresses that change.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Text-message reminders, in the customer's language, of every date generated by the filing, with plain explanations of what each step requires and a link to the court's self-help resources — information, not advice.

## Who Feels the Pain
Tenants, debtors and family-law parties; preparers whose customers come back angry; courts handling avoidable dismissals.

## Impact If Fixed
Fewer cases lost to procedure, at the cost of a reminder service.

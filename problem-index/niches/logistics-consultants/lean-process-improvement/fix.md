# The Value Stream Map That Lives on a Conference Room Wall

**Niche:** [[niches/logistics-consultants/lean-process-improvement/profile|Lean & Process Improvement Engagements]]
**Industry:** [[industries/logistics-consultants|Logistics Consultants]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The week's most valuable artefact is a brown-paper map covered in sticky notes, photographed once, and gone when the room is booked for something else.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #object-detection #workflow-orchestration #quick-win #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to show, from the plant's own MES, ERP or WMS records, that the targeted measure — units per labour hour, changeover minutes or dock-to-stock time — is still holding its promised gain over the pre-engagement baseline twelve months after handover, and whoever can evidence that twelve-month hold takes the client's next site.

## The Problem
A value-stream mapping workshop produces a current-state and future-state map: process boxes, inventory triangles, cycle and changeover times, the kaizen bursts that become the action plan. It is drawn by hand because drawing it together is the point. Afterwards it is photographed, perhaps redrawn in Visio by a junior consultant over a weekend, and the data on it — times, inventories, owners of each action — never enters any system the plant uses.

## Why It's Still Broken
Digitising the map takes hours of tedious work nobody budgets, and digital mapping tools used in the room break the collaborative drawing that makes the workshop work.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Keep the paper. Photograph the map at the end of each day and convert it automatically into a structured record: process steps, the times and inventories written on them, and each kaizen burst as an action with an owner and due date, loaded into the plant's action tracker and linked to the system-recorded measure it is meant to move. The current-state times become the documented pre-engagement baseline, checked against the plant's own records, so the twelve-month holding check has something to measure against. The facilitator corrects the extraction in minutes.

## Who Feels the Pain
Junior consultants redrawing maps at weekends; plant managers who cannot find the plan three months later; operators whose ideas on the sticky notes are never followed up.

## Impact If Fixed
The workshop's output becomes a live action plan with owners and measures the day it is drawn, rather than a photograph in a project folder.

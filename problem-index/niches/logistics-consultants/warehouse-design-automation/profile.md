# Warehouse Design & Automation Advisory

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/logistics-consultants|Logistics Consultants]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to predict the throughput a redesigned or automated building will actually reach on the client's own order profile, and whoever's projections hold at go-live wins the next site.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$3.5B US consulting fees for distribution centre design, automation feasibility, vendor selection and project oversight (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~12% of US logistics and process consulting fees (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — layouts are drawn in CAD and systems are simulated, but order profiling, vendor bid comparison and ramp-up assumptions live in spreadsheets
**Target Buyer:** VP Distribution, Director of DC Engineering
**Automation Potential:** Medium — profiling, simulation runs and bid normalisation automate; the design concept and the vendor decision do not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
An owner planning a new distribution centre or an automation retrofit — goods-to-person, autonomous mobile robots, shuttle systems, sortation — faces a capital decision of ~$10M–$100M+ per site (estimate) and vendor proposals each claiming a throughput number. The consultant profiles the client's orders, designs the concept, sizes the system, writes the specification, normalises the vendor bids and often oversees the project to go-live. The deliverable is a throughput and cost projection for a building or system that does not yet exist, and its accuracy is revealed months later at ramp-up.

## Current Tools & Gaps
AutoCAD for layouts; FlexSim, AnyLogic and Simio for discrete-event simulation; Excel for order profiles, labour models and bid comparison; the vendors' own simulation results, analysed under [[niches/warehouse-3pl/warehouse-automation-analytics/profile|Warehouse Automation Vendor Analytics]]. The gaps: vendors simulate their proposals on order profiles they choose; consultants rarely replay one common profile through every proposal; and no firm keeps a record comparing projected with achieved throughput across its projects.

# The Projected Saving Nobody Checks

**Niche:** [[niches/logistics-consultants/network-design-strategy/profile|Distribution Network Design & Strategy]]
**Industry:** [[industries/logistics-consultants|Logistics Consultants]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Every network study ends with a projected saving, and almost none is ever compared with what the client actually saved once the new footprint was running.
**Tags:** #causal-inference #linear-regression #confidence-intervals #evaluation-metrics #revenue-impact #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn a client's raw shipment, order and invoice data into a baseline the CFO accepts as the real cost of today's network, and whoever closes that reconciliation fastest gets to run the scenarios that decide the footprint.

## The Problem
The readout says the recommended network saves a given share of logistics cost. The client implements part of it over 12 to 36 months, in a freight market that has moved, with volumes and channel mix that have shifted. Nobody restates the projection at the conditions that actually occurred, so nobody — client or consultant — knows whether the model was right, which assumptions failed, or whether the firm's next projection deserves trust.

## Why It's Still Broken
The consultant rolls off at the readout; implementation is the client's. Fixed-fee engagement letters do not provide for follow-up data access. And a projection compared naively with realised cost looks wrong for reasons that have nothing to do with the model, which makes firms reluctant to start.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A short follow-up clause in the engagement letter granting access to a defined set of post-implementation data at 12 and 24 months; a restatement method that re-runs the baseline and the recommended network at realised volumes and market rates, so the comparison isolates the model; and a one-page reconciliation per engagement, logged against the assumptions that drove the projection.

## Who Feels the Pain
CFOs who approved a footprint on a projection they cannot verify; partners who cannot prove their track record; modellers who never learn which of their assumptions were wrong.

## Impact If Fixed
A firm with a restated record of projected against realised savings across even a dozen engagements has evidence no competitor can match, and its modellers learn from outcomes rather than from readouts.

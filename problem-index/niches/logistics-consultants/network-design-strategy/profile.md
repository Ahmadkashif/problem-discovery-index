# Distribution Network Design & Strategy

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/logistics-consultants|Logistics Consultants]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn a client's raw shipment, order and invoice data into a baseline the CFO accepts as the real cost of today's network, and whoever closes that reconciliation fastest gets to run the scenarios that decide the footprint.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$3.0B US consulting fees for network design and footprint strategy studies, including strategy firms' supply chain practices (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~10% of US logistics and process consulting fees (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — the optimisation is done in mature solvers, while the data preparation that feeds them is done by hand in Excel and SQL
**Target Buyer:** Chief Supply Chain Officer, VP Logistics
**Automation Potential:** High for data preparation and baseline reconciliation, low for the footprint recommendation itself

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A network study answers how many distribution centres a shipper should run, where, serving which customers, fed by which flows. Large shippers buy one every few years and whenever something forces the question: a lease expiry, an acquisition, a change in channel mix, or a tariff shift like 2025's that moves sourcing and therefore inbound flows. The study runs three to four months, and one to two of them go on data (Miebach Consulting, quoted by Optilogic). Nothing is decided until the baseline — the model of today's network — ties to the ledger closely enough that the CFO believes the scenarios built on it.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Coupa Supply Chain Design & Planning (the former LLamasoft), Optilogic Cosmic Frog and AIMMS SC Navigator do the optimisation well; Alteryx, SQL and Excel do the cleansing; PowerPoint carries the readout. The gaps: no tool attributes a baseline variance to its likely cause; past engagements' cleansing precedents sit unlinked on shared drives; and no one records the projected saving in a form that can be checked against what the client later realised.

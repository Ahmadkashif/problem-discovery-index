# Transportation Procurement & Bid Management

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/logistics-consultants|Logistics Consultants]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is that every serious competitor is fighting to lower a shipper's contracted freight cost, but truckload is won by designing awards carriers keep honouring when the market turns, and parcel and LTL by knowing what comparable shippers have been conceded. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.5B US consulting and managed-bid fees, including contingency fees on parcel and LTL negotiations (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~5% of US logistics and process consulting fees (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** High — bids run on sourcing platforms and rate benchmarks; the analysis around them still runs in Excel
**Target Buyer:** Director of Transportation, Logistics Procurement Lead
**Automation Potential:** High — bid logistics, normalisation and re-rating are mechanical; award judgement and negotiation are not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A shipper's freight contracts are re-bid on fixed cycles: truckload lanes annually, usually timed to contract expirations, with mini-bids when the market moves; parcel and LTL agreements renegotiated around carrier rate changes — UPS and FedEx both raised list rates 5.9% on average for 2026 — and, for LTL, around NMFC dockets such as the 19 July 2025 move to density-based classes. The consultant designs the event, normalises responses, models awards and, for parcel and LTL, negotiates. It is the most repeatable analytical work in the industry and the most automated, which is why the remaining contest splits by mode.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Keelvar, Coupa and Transporeon sourcing modules run the event; DAT, Freightos and Xeneta supply benchmarks; TMS platforms hold tender history. Gaps: no cross-mode view of which freight belongs in which mode, and savings reported at award rather than at payment.

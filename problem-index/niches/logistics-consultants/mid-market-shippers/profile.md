# Mid-Market Shippers Without Logistics Engineers

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/logistics-consultants|Logistics Consultants]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to reconstruct shipment-level history for a client with no TMS — freight invoices in PDFs, carrier portals, ERP order lines — and whoever can do it in days rather than weeks can deliver a study at a fee a mid-market CFO will approve.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$900M US consulting fees from shippers with ~$50M–$500M revenue (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~3% of US logistics and process consulting fees (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — the client has an ERP and a pile of carrier invoices, but no TMS, no shipment table and nobody whose job is logistics data
**Target Buyer:** CFO or COO of a $50M–$500M manufacturer or distributor
**Automation Potential:** High for history reconstruction, medium for the recommendation

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A mid-market manufacturer or distributor spends a meaningful share of revenue on freight and has no logistics engineer. Transportation is run by a customer service manager and a shipping supervisor; parcel and LTL are booked on carrier websites; truckload goes to two or three brokers by phone. When the CFO asks whether the company should open a second warehouse, switch carriers or bid its freight, there is no shipment history to model — only invoices. The enterprise network study, priced at ~$75K–$500K (estimate) and built on a clean TMS extract, does not fit. The work here is mostly solo and boutique consultants, many ex-shipper practitioners, from the ~75% of firms in the code with fewer than five employees.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Network design platforms (Coupa Supply Chain Design & Planning, Optilogic Cosmic Frog) assume a shipment table exists. Freight audit and payment providers build one, but only going forward and only for clients who sign up for continuous audit. Carrier portals export a few months at a time in inconsistent formats. The gap is turning twelve months of PDFs, portal exports and ERP order lines into a shipment-level history cheaply enough that the study itself stays affordable.

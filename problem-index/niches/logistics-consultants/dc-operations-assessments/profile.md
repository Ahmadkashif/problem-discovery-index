# DC Operations Assessments

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/logistics-consultants|Logistics Consultants]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to find, in a two-to-five-day visit, the few constraints that actually cap a building's throughput, and whoever turns a walk-through into a quantified, defensible list fastest wins the implementation work that follows.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$800M US consulting fees for operational assessments, productivity diagnostics and labour standards work in existing distribution centres (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~3% of US logistics and process consulting fees (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — the assessment runs on a notebook, a phone camera, a stopwatch and the assessor's memory
**Target Buyer:** VP Operations, DC General Manager
**Automation Potential:** Medium — quantification from WMS task data automates; spotting the constraint on the floor does not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
An operating building is missing its numbers: overtime is up, peak was late, cost per unit has crept. A senior assessor walks it for a few days — receiving, put-away, replenishment, picking, packing, shipping — watches a shift, interviews supervisors and pulls a WMS extract. Experienced assessors see the constraint quickly: replenishment starving the pick face, a pack station layout forcing double handling, a wave plan that idles pickers for an hour. That judgement is tacit. The deliverable is a prioritised findings list with estimated savings, and its real purpose is to win the redesign, slotting or labour-standards implementation that follows.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Paper and phone notes, spreadsheets for time studies, the client's WMS reports, and benchmarks from WERC's DC Measures survey (36 metrics in 2026). Engineered labour standards work, logged as [[niches/warehouse-3pl/engineered-labour-standards/profile|Engineered Labour Standards & Productivity Consulting]], still relies on manual time study. The gaps: observations are not linked to the task data that would quantify them; findings are written from memory after the visit; and nothing preserves what the senior assessor noticed.

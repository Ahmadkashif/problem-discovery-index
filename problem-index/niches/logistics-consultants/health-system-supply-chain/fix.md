# Shadow Stock in Every Nursing Unit

**Niche:** [[niches/logistics-consultants/health-system-supply-chain/profile|Health-System Supply Chains]]
**Industry:** [[industries/logistics-consultants|Logistics Consultants]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Nurses keep private stashes of the supplies they have run out of before, and every inventory study models the official stock while the real buffer sits in drawers.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #hypothesis-testing #evaluation-metrics #worker-facing #quick-win #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to set par levels and replenishment that clinical units trust enough to stop keeping shadow stock, and whoever wins that trust captures the inventory savings everyone else only models.

## The Problem
After one stockout during a procedure, a unit starts keeping extra in carts, drawers and patient rooms. The stock is invisible to the materials system, expires unused, and inflates restock volume, which raises par levels at the next review. A consultant's inventory reduction plan removes official stock while the shadow stock grows.

## Why It's Still Broken
Nobody counts it, because counting it reads as an accusation, and nurses are acting rationally: a stockout harms a patient, excess costs a budget they do not own. The incentive gap is structural — supply chain carries the inventory cost, nursing carries the stockout risk.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Periodic sweeps of non-official locations recorded as data rather than discipline, joined to stockout history by item, so the hoarding pattern shows which items are distrusted and why. Fix those items first: guaranteed service levels, visible restock status on the unit, and a fast emergency path, before cutting any par.

## Who Feels the Pain
Nurses who waste time hunting supplies; materials managers whose par reviews ratchet upward; supply chain leaders whose projected savings never appear.

## Impact If Fixed
Measuring shadow stock turns the industry's most common savings shortfall into a list of specific items to fix.

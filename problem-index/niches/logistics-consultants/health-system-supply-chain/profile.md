# Health-System Supply Chains

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/logistics-consultants|Logistics Consultants]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to set par levels and replenishment that clinical units trust enough to stop keeping shadow stock, and whoever wins that trust captures the inventory savings everyone else only models.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$400M US consulting fees for hospital and health-system logistics: consolidated service centres, par-level programmes, case-cart and point-of-use replenishment design (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~1% of US logistics and process consulting fees (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — par levels are set by periodic counts and a materials manager's judgement; usage is inferred from what was restocked, not what was used on patients
**Target Buyer:** VP Supply Chain at a health system
**Automation Potential:** Medium — the computation automates; adoption depends on clinicians

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A health system's logistics network looks like a distributor's — a consolidated service centre feeding hospitals, clinics and surgery centres — but the last fifty feet run through nursing units and operating rooms, where the customer is a clinician who will not tolerate a stockout and will hoard to prevent one. Demand follows procedure schedules and census rather than orders; products expire, are recalled by lot, and carry unique device identifiers; and the people who count and restock supply rooms are not the people who use the product. Specialist firms with healthcare practices, St. Onge among them, design these networks, but the tools that set replenishment were built for warehouses or retail.

## Current Tools & Gaps
ERP materials modules (Workday, Infor, Oracle) hold par levels and two-bin kanban settings; point-of-use cabinets and RFID systems track some high-value items; case-cart systems pick supplies for scheduled procedures. Gaps: par levels set from restock history rather than clinical usage; no link between procedure schedules and unit demand; and no measure of the shadow stock clinicians keep because they do not trust the official supply.

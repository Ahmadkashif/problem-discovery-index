# Parcel Rate Modelling Adapted to Surcharge-Driven Pricing

**Niche:** [[niches/logistics-consultants/parcel-ltl-contract-optimisation/profile|Parcel & LTL Contract Optimisation]]
**Industry:** [[industries/logistics-consultants|Logistics Consultants]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Parcel rate modelling tools reprice a shipper's history against new discounts; the cost now moves through surcharges the headline rate increase does not include.
**Tags:** #monte-carlo-methods #feature-engineering #descriptive-statistics #data-integration #revenue-impact #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to know — from contracts and invoices across many comparable shippers — what discount tiers, minimums and surcharge terms a carrier will concede to a shipper of this profile, and whoever holds the deepest comparable-contract record negotiates the best agreement.

## The Problem
UPS and FedEx both announced 5.9% average list increases for 2026, but a shipper's paid increase depends on its mix: dimensional weight, residential and delivery-area surcharges, additional handling, large-package and peak charges, each with its own rate change and each partly negotiable.

## What Already Exists
Parcel audit firms and rating tools reprice a year of invoices under proposed discounts and incentive tiers, and carriers supply their own modelling to shippers during negotiation.

## The Customization Gap
Negotiation needs: surcharge exposure modelled per shipment, not as a percentage uplift; packaging and carton-size scenarios that change dimensional weight; residential and delivery-area mix projected from customer growth rather than last year; peak surcharges by week; and each proposal's total cost compared across carriers and regional alternatives with the surcharge concessions valued explicitly.

## Target Customer
Parcel-heavy ecommerce and distribution shippers, and their negotiation advisers.

## Impact If Solved
Negotiation effort lands on the surcharges that drive paid cost rather than on headline discounts.

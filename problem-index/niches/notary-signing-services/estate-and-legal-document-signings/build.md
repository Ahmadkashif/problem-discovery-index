# A Capacity and Free-Will Record Made at the Signing

**Niche:** [[niches/notary-signing-services/estate-and-legal-document-signings/profile|Estate & Legal Document Signings]]
**Industry:** [[industries/notary-signing-services|Notary & Signing Services]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** An experienced notary can tell when a frail signer is following someone else's lead, and records nothing about it beyond an ID number.

**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to execute a will, trust or power of attorney with the witnesses the state requires and a contemporaneous record of the signer's identity, awareness and free will, and whoever's record holds up in a later probate or elder-abuse challenge takes the estate attorney's repeat work.

## The Problem
A notary at a care-home bedside must decide whether the signer understands the document and is signing willingly. Experienced notaries ask open questions, send the beneficiary out of the room, and watch who the signer looks at before answering. Years later, a contested probate asks what happened, and the notary's memory and a journal line are all there is.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Notaries are told not to judge legal capacity, which is a lawyer's or doctor's call, so no one has defined what a notary should record. The outcomes that would test a record — will contests and elder-abuse findings — are rare, slow and scattered across courts.

## What to Build
A short structured observation captured at the signing: who was present and who left the room, the open questions asked and the signer's answers in their own words, whether the signer read or had the document read, any prompting, and the notary's proceed/decline decision with reasons. A language model turns the notary's spoken notes into those fields and flags missing ones. Where the attorney and state law permit, an audio or video record attaches. Over time, linked to contest outcomes the firm learns about, the record shows which observations matter.

## Target Customer
Estate planning attorneys who send mobile notaries to clients; mobile notary businesses specialising in estate work.

## Impact If Built
The firm's documents arrive with a defensible record of the ceremony, and the notary gains a protocol for the hardest judgement in the job.

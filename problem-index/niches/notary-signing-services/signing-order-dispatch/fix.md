# The Order Confirmed by Seven Phone Calls

**Niche:** [[niches/notary-signing-services/signing-order-dispatch/profile|Signing Order Dispatch]]
**Industry:** [[industries/notary-signing-services|Notary & Signing Services]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Confirming one signing — agent, borrower, documents, time, signers — takes a coordinator a string of calls and texts that nobody logs.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #workflow-orchestration #automation #quick-win #data-integration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to fill each signing order, within minutes of receipt, with the agent most likely to arrive on time and finish clean, and whoever predicts agent reliability best takes the title company's overflow orders.

## The Problem
Between acceptance and appointment, the coordinator confirms the agent, the borrower's time and address, who will sign, the ID each will bring, and when documents will arrive, then relays changes among the title company, the agent and the borrower. Most of this is routine, and missed messages cause no-shows.

## Why It's Still Broken
Every party uses a different channel, and the service's tools do not talk to the title company's or to the agent's phone.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Automated confirmation sequences by text and email with structured replies (time confirmed, signers, IDs), a language model that reads free-text replies and updates the order, and escalation to a coordinator only when something changes or nobody answers.

## Who Feels the Pain
Coordinators at month end; agents who arrive to an empty house; title companies asking for status.

## Impact If Fixed
Routine confirmations run themselves and coordinator time goes to exceptions.

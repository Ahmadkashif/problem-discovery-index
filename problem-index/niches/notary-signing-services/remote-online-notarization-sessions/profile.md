# Remote Online Notarization Sessions

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/notary-signing-services|Notary & Signing Services]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to clear genuine signers through remote identity proofing on the first attempt while refusing impostors, with a session record that survives a later forgery claim, and whoever has the best trade-off between false rejects and false accepts takes the platforms' and title companies' session volume.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$300M a year in RON session fees and notary pay on RON platforms (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~12% (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** High — the whole act happens on a screen
**Target Buyer:** RON platform operations and trust leads; title companies and lenders running RON closings; RON notary businesses
**Automation Potential:** High — identity proofing, recording and journaling automate; the notary's live observation and decision remain

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Remote online notarization was in effect in 42 states plus DC by late 2025, with three more states' laws not yet in effect and California's set for 2030 (Proof). Identity proofing typically follows the MISMO standard: credential analysis plus knowledge-based authentication of five questions, four correct within two minutes, with some states such as Missouri allowing biometrics as one of the methods. This niche is the notary side of the session — the notaries staffing platforms and the businesses selling sessions — whose craft is watching a signer over video for coaching, off-camera prompting or a mismatched face. Whether the destination accepts the finished act is analysed in [[niches/esignature-document-workflow/notarization-and-witnessing/profile|Notarization & Witnessing]] under E-Signature & Document Workflow.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Proof, NotaryCam, BlueNotary, OneNotary, NotaryLive and DocuSign Notary for sessions; KBA and credential-analysis providers underneath. Gaps: KBA fails thin-file and elderly signers and is weak against breached data; nobody measures false-reject and false-accept rates by signer group; and the notary's in-session observations are not captured.

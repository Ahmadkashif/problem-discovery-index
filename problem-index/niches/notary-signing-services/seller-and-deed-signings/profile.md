# Seller & Deed Signings

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/notary-signing-services|Notary & Signing Services]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this sub-niche is fighting to prove that the person signing the deed is the owner of record before it records and the proceeds are wired, and whoever stops impostors without delaying genuine sellers becomes the title company's approved notary.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$300M a year in seller-side and stand-alone deed signing fees, plus the fraud tooling bought to protect them (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~12% (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — the seller signs in person or by RON; identity checks are a glance at a licence
**Target Buyer:** Title agency owners and underwriters' fraud and agency-risk leads
**Automation Potential:** Medium — context checks and credential reading automate; the refusal decision stays with the notary

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Here the signer has not been through underwriting, and the property may be vacant land owned by someone who lives elsewhere or has died. ALTA's 2026 survey found ~59% of title firms saw a seller impersonation attempt in 2025, up from 28% in its 2024 survey, with vacant land the most common target and a seller-chosen notary or remote signing among the top red flags. The skill that wins is identity judgement under adversarial pressure, which is a different skill from flawless package handling, and title companies increasingly route seller signings only to an approved panel.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Seller-verification and wire-fraud tools sold to title offices (profiled as [[niches/title-and-settlement-offices/wire-identity-verification-vendors/profile|Wire & Seller Identity Verification Vendors]] under [[industries/title-and-settlement-offices|Title & Settlement Offices]]); identity verification apps with document and liveness checks; approved-notary panels; owner-of-record call-backs. The gaps on the notary's side are that their in-person observations are never captured, the title office's risk flags never reach them before the appointment, and outcomes are never joined back to the act.

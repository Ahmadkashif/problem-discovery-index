# The Seller-Chosen Notary

**Niche:** [[niches/notary-signing-services/seller-and-deed-signings/profile|Seller & Deed Signings]]
**Industry:** [[industries/notary-signing-services|Notary & Signing Services]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A seller who cannot meet in person sends back a notarised deed from a notary the title company has never heard of, and the stamp may be as fake as the seller.
**Tags:** #object-detection #cnns #k-nearest-neighbors #compliance #quick-win #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this sub-niche is fighting to prove that the person signing the deed is the owner of record before it records and the proceeds are wired, and whoever stops impostors without delaying genuine sellers becomes the title company's approved notary.

## The Problem
Out-of-state sellers often sign with a local notary and mail the deed back. ALTA's survey lists a request for a seller-chosen notary among the most common red flags. Title staff check that a seal is present and the certificate looks complete; they rarely confirm that the notary exists, holds a current commission, or performed the act.

## Why It's Still Broken
Verifying a notary means finding the right state's commission database, then finding a phone number not printed on the document, during a busy closing week. Nobody's workflow requires it.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Read the seal and certificate from the returned document, look up the commission automatically, contact the notary through details from the state record (not from the document) to confirm the act and journal entry, and route seller signings by default to an approved notary, with the seller-chosen path allowed only after verification.

## Who Feels the Pain
Title closers and agency owners who pay the claims; defrauded owners; genuine notaries whose seals are forged.

## Impact If Fixed
A ten-minute verification closes one of the commonest routes for forged deeds.

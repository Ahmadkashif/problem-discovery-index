# State-Rule-Aware Electronic Journals

**Niche:** [[niches/notary-signing-services/notary-journals-and-records/profile|Notary Journals & Records]]
**Industry:** [[industries/notary-signing-services|Notary & Signing Services]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Generic record-keeping apps and today's e-journals store entries; they do not stop a California notary from closing a deed entry without a thumbprint.
**Tags:** #decision-trees #large-language-models #evaluation-metrics #compliance #automation #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to make the journal entry the evidence that settles a later challenge — who appeared, how they were identified, what they signed — complete under each state's rules and retrievable in minutes years later, and whoever does that without slowing the signing takes the notary's record-keeping.

## The Problem
A notary working across act types and, for remote acts, across signer locations must remember which fields each entry needs. Missed fields are found only when the entry is needed.

## What Already Exists
E-journal apps (Notary eJournal, NotaryAssist and others), RON platforms' built-in journals, and general form apps that capture signatures and photos.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs a maintained rule base per state and act: required fields, permitted identity evidence and credible-witness counts, thumbprint triggers, whether an electronic journal is permitted for in-person acts, retention and disposal, and copy-request rules; blocking of incomplete entries; and automatic updates when a legislature or Secretary of State changes the rules.

## Target Customer
Signing agents and mobile notaries; journal and supply vendors who want a compliance layer for their apps.

## Impact If Solved
Every entry is complete under the notary's state law at the moment it is made, which is the only time completeness is cheap.

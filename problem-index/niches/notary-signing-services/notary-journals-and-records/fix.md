# Records Requests Answered by Flipping Pages

**Niche:** [[niches/notary-signing-services/notary-journals-and-records/profile|Notary Journals & Records]]
**Industry:** [[industries/notary-signing-services|Notary & Signing Services]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A title company, an attorney or a court asks for the journal line for a 2021 signing, and the notary spends an evening searching boxes.
**Tags:** #cnns #transformers #data-integration #automation #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to make the journal entry the evidence that settles a later challenge — who appeared, how they were identified, what they signed — complete under each state's rules and retrievable in minutes years later, and whoever does that without slowing the signing takes the notary's record-keeping.

## The Problem
Statutes let the public request copies of journal entries — California caps the charge at 30 cents a page — and courts can subpoena the journal for inspection in the notary's presence. Paper journals are indexed by date at best, while requests arrive by party name or property address.

## Why It's Still Broken
Notaries keep journals for years in personal storage, the requests are infrequent, and nobody has made the retrieval step easy enough to bother with.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Photograph each completed journal page and index it by name, date, document type and property with handwriting recognition, so a request is answered by search. Produce the excerpt with the other entries on the page redacted, as privacy requires.

## Who Feels the Pain
Notaries answering subpoenas and records requests; attorneys and title companies waiting on them.

## Impact If Fixed
Minutes instead of evenings per request, and fewer cases where a notary cannot produce the entry that would have cleared them.

# The Wasted Trip to the Jail

**Niche:** [[niches/notary-signing-services/facility-bound-signers/profile|Hospital, Care-Facility & Jail Signers]]
**Industry:** [[industries/notary-signing-services|Notary & Signing Services]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The notary drives an hour, clears security and learns the detainee was moved, the ID is in property, or the document cannot come through the screen.
**Tags:** #decision-trees #automation #quick-win #worker-facing #workflow-orchestration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to complete a valid act for a signer who cannot leave a hospital, care home or jail and often has no ID in hand — securing access within the facility's windows and identity evidence the state accepts — and whoever completes on the first visit takes the facility's and families' referrals.

## The Problem
Detention and hospital signings fail for reasons that could have been checked by phone: transfer or discharge, a lockdown, an ID that must be retrieved from property in advance, witnesses not on the visitor list, or a signer sedated at the appointed hour. The family pays for a second trip or gives up.

## Why It's Still Broken
Each facility has its own rules and nobody collects them; families do not know what to ask; notaries price the risk in by refusing the work.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A pre-visit checklist per facility type, completed with the family and the facility the day before: signer location confirmed, ID retrieval arranged, witnesses cleared, documents pre-screened, a lucid time window agreed. A shared per-facility notes file captures what each visit taught.

## Who Feels the Pain
Detainees and patients who need a document signed; families paying for failed trips; the few notaries who take this work.

## Impact If Fixed
Most failed trips become first-visit completions at the cost of a phone call.

# Hospital, Care-Facility & Jail Signers

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/notary-signing-services|Notary & Signing Services]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to complete a valid act for a signer who cannot leave a hospital, care home or jail and often has no ID in hand — securing access within the facility's windows and identity evidence the state accepts — and whoever completes on the first visit takes the facility's and families' referrals.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$120M a year in mobile notary fees for hospital, nursing-home, hospice and detention signings (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~5% (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Very low — in-person by necessity; remote acts often impossible because the signer cannot pass identity proofing or has no device
**Target Buyer:** Families, hospital social workers and case managers, criminal defence and family lawyers, facility administrators
**Automation Potential:** Low — access, identity and observation are physical; preparation and scheduling automate

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
These signers need powers of attorney, healthcare directives, guardianship papers, vehicle transfers, custody consents and property documents, often urgently. They are the people least able to travel, least likely to have a current photo ID with them (licences expire in a drawer; detainees' IDs sit in property), and most at risk of undue influence. Most notaries avoid the work: visiting windows, security screening, rescheduled appointments and bedside capacity doubts make it slow and uncertain, and remote identity proofing by knowledge-based authentication is often unpassable for elderly or detained signers.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Mobile notary directories, phone calls to facility front desks, jail professional-visit booking systems, and notaries' own lists of which facilities are workable. Gaps: no per-state guide to identity alternatives (credible witnesses, facility-issued identification where a state allows it), no facility access profiles, and no protocol for capacity doubts at the bedside.

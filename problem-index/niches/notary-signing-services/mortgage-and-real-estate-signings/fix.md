## The Problem
Notary commissions run for fixed terms set by each state, and E&O policies and background screenings renew annually. An act performed after a commission expires is defective, and a deed with a defective acknowledgment may be rejected by the recorder or attacked later. Coordinators check eligibility when an agent joins the panel and rarely afterwards.

## Why It's Still Broken
Commission data sits in state databases that do not push updates; renewals are the agent's responsibility; and the order is assigned in minutes by whoever is available, so nobody re-checks.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Check commission status, expiry, bond and screening date automatically at the moment of assignment, block ineligible agents, and remind agents sixty days before each expiry. Store the eligibility snapshot with the order so the file shows the notary was valid on the signing date.

## Who Feels the Pain
Closing coordinators who assign under time pressure; title companies facing recorder rejections and underwriter audits; borrowers whose closings slip when a package has to be re-signed.

## Impact If Fixed
A near-zero-cost check removes a class of defects that otherwise surface at the worst moment and are expensive to cure.

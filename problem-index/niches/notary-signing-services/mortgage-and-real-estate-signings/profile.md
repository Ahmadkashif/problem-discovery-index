# Mortgage & Real Estate Signings

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/notary-signing-services|Notary & Signing Services]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is that every serious competitor is fighting for title and escrow order flow, but borrower loan signings are won by returning a clean, fully executed package before the funding cutoff, while seller and deed signings are won by proving the signer is the owner of record before the deed records — two contests with different winners. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.4B a year in signing fees paid to agents and signing services for purchase, refinance, HELOC, reverse-mortgage and seller signings (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~56% (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — orders, documents and scanbacks move digitally; the signing is wet ink at a table
**Target Buyer:** Title and escrow operations managers, lender closing departments, signing-service owners
**Automation Potential:** Medium — order handling and package checks automate; the appointment does not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
This is where the money is: ~5.4 million mortgage loans in 2025 and ~5.8 million forecast for 2026 (MBA), most of them with at least one notarised signing, plus seller deeds on purchases. Work flows from lenders and title companies through signing services and marketplaces such as Snapdocs to independent agents paid a flat fee. Title companies screen agents through NNA certification, annual background checks and E&O requirements, then judge them on two very different things depending on who sits at the table.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Snapdocs and signing-service portals for orders; closing platforms for hybrid and digital closings; NNA certification and screening; title production systems that hold the file. Snapdocs' 2025 survey found 90% of lenders offer digital closings but only 14% of those with the technology close more than 80% of loans digitally, so wet-ink signings remain the norm. The gap is a shared record of how each agent actually performs — on redraws and on identity incidents — which no title company, signing service or marketplace holds across the market.

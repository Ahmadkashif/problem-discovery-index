# Niche Analysis — Optometry Practices

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/optometry-practices|Optometry Practices]]

## Niche Selection

An optometry practice earns its money in two currencies: fixed vision-plan fees for routine exams and eyewear, and medical billing for eye disease. VSP alone paid $4.8B to private practices in 2024 (VSP Vision). The binding constraints are doctor time (24,950 optometrists in 621320 offices, BLS 2023), payer rules that differ by plan, and a growing set of external duties: the FTC Contact Lens Rule's eight-business-hour verification clock, the diabetic eye exam Star measure, and scope laws that now allow lasers in 14 states. Ownership is consolidating: MyEyeDr. agreed in September 2026 to buy EyeCare Partners' 300-plus optometry offices. The eight niches below split the industry by what vendors and practice teams actually compete over. Market sizes are estimates of tooling, services and staff spend within each niche, not practice revenue.

## Why These Niches

The two market-share niches are where the practice's revenue is decided. Every exam carries the routine-versus-medical billing decision, and the medical eye care programmes are the practice's growth line beyond fixed vision fees. The two low-digitized niches run on fax, paper and phone: contact lens prescription release and seller verification under the FTC rule, and the letters and codes that get diabetic eye exams credited by health plans. The two underserved audiences are children. One group is in myopia control, a new self-pay programme the software ignores. The other is low-income children on Medicaid, whose failed school screenings rarely become exams. The two automatable niches are the most mechanical work in the office: checking benefits across vision and medical plans before every visit, and the refraction itself, which automated and remote systems are starting to take off the doctor.

## Filter Notes

Seven of the eight level-1 niches are terminal. **Medical Eye Care Programmes** is not. Writing its sentence produced two contests with different winners. Glaucoma is won by catching progression at the first visit it shows, across devices and years and in co-management with ophthalmology. The players are device review platforms (Zeiss FORUM, Topcon Harmony, Heidelberg) and OCT analytics such as Altris AI. Dry eye is won by turning diagnoses into completed, mostly cash-paid treatment series. There the players are device makers' practice programmes (Lumenis, Johnson & Johnson Vision's LipiFlow, Sight Sciences' TearCare), financing and package billing. A missed progression costs permanent field loss. An abandoned dry-eye course costs revenue and the patient's relief. It decomposes into **Glaucoma Monitoring & Progression Detection** and **Dry Eye Treatment Courses**.

Four terminal calls were close. **Routine-vs-Medical Visit Billing** and **Benefits Verification** both deal with payers. They stay separate because one is the after-visit routing decision, scored on collected revenue that survives audit, and the other is the before-visit estimate, scored on the gap to the adjudicated claim. The staff, the tools (claim scrubbers versus eligibility agents) and the failure (lost medical revenue versus a checkout dispute) differ. **Contact Lens Prescriptions** could split into rule compliance and annual-supply capture. It stays one because the moments the rule governs, the release at the fitting and the seller's verification request, are the moments the sale is won or lost, and the same staff and vendors handle both. **Diabetic Eye Exam Report-Back** sits beside the plan-side [[niches/health-insurance-carriers/hedis-hybrid-chart-review/profile|⚡ HEDIS Hybrid Chart Review & Stars Measurement]]. It stays here because the buyer and the work are the eye practice's (codes, letters, gap reconciliation), not the plan's chart chase. **Medicaid Children's Vision** touches [[industries/community-health-centers|Community Health Centers]], which run vision services inside health centres. This niche covers private practices that accept Medicaid and the school-referral loop into them.

Six candidates were considered and rejected. **Exam documentation and AI scribing** is now a free EHR feature (RevolutionEHR's AI Scribe since April 2026, Eyefinity's EncompassScribe). Its contest repeats [[niches/physician-practices/ambient-documentation-visit-coding/profile|🔵 Ambient Documentation & Office Visit Coding]], and the device image-to-code chain is already [[niches/healthcare-practice-software/ophthalmology-ehr-imaging-chain/profile|🎯 Ophthalmology EHR — the Imaging-to-Code Chain]]. **Frame inventory, optical lab turnaround and eyewear materials billing** belong to the queued optical retailers industry, whose boundary claims them, including vision-plan billing for eyewear sold by optical shops. Niches 1 and 7 cover the exam, the medical crossover and the combined benefit check inside OD practices. **Recall and no-shows** are [[niches/scheduling-booking-platforms/no-show-and-attendance/profile|🔵 No-Show & Attendance]]; the medical-recall gap appears in niche 2's fix. **Credentialing with vision and medical panels** repeats [[niches/behavioral-health-clinics/credentialing-operations/profile|⚡ Credentialing & Payer Enrollment Operations]]. **Rollup integration** splits into EHR conversion, already [[niches/healthcare-practice-software/ehr-data-migration-services/profile|⚡ EHR Data Migration & Conversion]], and analytics, which is Pass 2 pocket 11. **Autonomous diabetic retinopathy screening** happens mostly in primary care offices, outside this industry's work. Its vendors are pocket 13, and the optometry side is niche 4.

## Why These Pockets

No pocket qualifies. The practices themselves fail the gate: nearly half of the 18,708 firms have fewer than five employees, and even the 45 largest spend their analysis on their own margins. The organisations around them split into two kinds. The data-rich ones are fenced. The vision plans (42) hold claims for most insured Americans behind HIPAA. The ophthalmic CROs and reading centres (46) sell their analysis outright but on sponsor-owned trial data. The rollups (36) and the diabetic screening AI vendors (32) work on identified patient records. The clean ones give their insight away to sell something else, or sell it to a market of one. The lens makers (32) and imaging makers (30) use their data to sell lenses and devices, and NBEO (36) is a single national body on its own calendar. Much of the insight layer around optometry is already profiled under other industries and scores the same way:

- The ophthalmologists' IRIS Registry and Verana Health are [[niches/physician-practices/specialty-society-office-registries/profile|🔍 Office-Based Specialty Registries & QCDRs]] (43, kill switch); optometry's own registry is pocket 16. Specialty platforms that include ophthalmology are [[niches/physician-practices/pe-specialty-platform-analytics/profile|🔍 Private-Equity Specialty Platforms & Physician MSO Analytics]] (36, kill switch).
- Claims plumbing and practice software data are [[niches/medical-billing/healthcare-clearinghouses/profile|🔍 Healthcare Claims Clearinghouses]] (52, kill switch) and [[niches/medical-billing/practice-management-ehr-data/profile|🔍 Practice Management & EHR Data Teams]] (45, kill switch). Code sets are [[niches/medical-billing/medical-coding-content-publishers/profile|🔍 Medical Coding Content Publishers]] (54, indexed), and audits [[niches/medical-billing/coding-audit-compliance-firms/profile|🔍 Coding Audit & Compliance Firms]] (46, kill switch).
- The diabetic eye exam measure lives with the plans' quality layer. That covers [[niches/health-insurance-carriers/stars-forecasting-consultancies/profile|🔍 Star Ratings Forecasting & Quality Strategy Consultancies]] (43) and [[niches/health-insurance-carriers/ncqa-measurement-accreditation/profile|🔍 NCQA Health Plan Measurement & Accreditation]] (41).
- Medicare's local coverage policies for eye testing come from the contractors at [[niches/physician-practices/mac-medical-directors-lcds/profile|🔍 MAC Medical Directors & Local Coverage Policy]] (fails gate).
- Imaging AI and trial imaging elsewhere score as the eye-care pockets do: [[niches/diagnostic-imaging-centers/radiology-ai-vendor-data-science/profile|🔍 Radiology AI & Reporting Vendors' Clinical Data Science Teams]] (32, kill switch) and [[niches/diagnostic-imaging-centers/imaging-core-labs/profile|🔍 Clinical Trial Imaging Core Labs]] (46, kill switch).

The coordinator asked for eight angles to be tested, and each lands on a pocket that fails. Vision-plan versus medical billing and its claim-rule intelligence meet the vision plans (42, HIPAA). No third party sells the rules; they sit in provider manuals behind network portals, and VSP owns the largest practice software vendor, Eyefinity. Medical-model optometry and the diabetic exam Star measure meet AOA MORE (fails gate) and the plans' quality pockets linked above (41–43). Autonomous diabetic retinopathy AI scores 32 with a HIPAA switch, and retinal imaging makers 30. Myopia management meets the lens makers (32), whose programme registries would carry a HIPAA switch. Contact lenses and the FTC rule meet the regulators (fails gate). Online contact lens sellers such as 1-800 Contacts are retail businesses whose verification data serves their own sales, so they are left to the queued optical retailers build. Online refraction (Visibly and similar) sells prescriptions straight to consumers, and no insight function was found behind it; the in-practice side is niche 8. Rollups score 36 with HIPAA, and supplier data 30–32. Optometry EHR and practice management vendors' data teams are the medical billing pocket above (45, kill switch).

The nearest miss is the ophthalmic CROs and reading centres (46). They are the only scored pocket whose clients pay for the analysis itself (Q1 5), with sizeable scientific teams and a cross-trial view of site and grading performance no sponsor has. Even without the data-ownership switch they would sit four points short, because the trial clock belongs to the sponsor. The vision plans (42) are second: the richest dataset in the industry, but sold as a benefit, held by two dominant firms and behind HIPAA. The practical lesson for placement matches the hub. The buyers worth approaching are the vendors hiring engineers to sell into practices and plans, and on 25 October 2026 those were few: VSP Vision (one AI platform director, remote US), Weave (speech and GenAI engineers, remote US and India), 1-800 Contacts (Draper, Utah), Optos (Dunfermline, Scotland) and AEYE Health (Tel Aviv).

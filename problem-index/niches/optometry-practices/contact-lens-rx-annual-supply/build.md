# Run-Out Predictor and Practice Subscription

**Niche:** [[niches/optometry-practices/contact-lens-rx-annual-supply/profile|Contact Lens Prescriptions, Verification & Annual Supply]]
**Industry:** [[industries/optometry-practices|Optometry Practices]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A service that predicts when each wearer will run out of lenses and offers a practice-branded annual supply or subscription, priced with plan allowances and rebates, before an online seller reaches them.
**Tags:** #survival-analysis #gradient-boosting #large-language-models #automation #revenue-impact #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep fitted contact lens wearers buying their lenses through the prescribing practice, and whoever puts the highest share of fitted wearers on an annual supply or subscription takes the account.

## The Problem
Practices fit lenses and then lose the resupply to online sellers. The patient leaves with a prescription (as the rule requires), buys three months of lenses, and reorders wherever is easiest. The practice learns of it only when a verification request arrives.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Practices see the fitting and the first order, not the wearer's usage. Manufacturers and distributors run their own ship-to-patient programmes, each covering one brand. Vision-plan contact lens allowances and manufacturer rebates change every year and are applied by hand.

## What to Build
A run-out model from order history, lens modality and wearing schedule. An offer engine that prices the annual supply net of the patient's plan allowance and the current manufacturer rebate. Reorder prompts by text, with one-tap ordering for direct shipping. And capture of the wearer's purchase channel when a verification request shows they bought elsewhere.

## Target Customer
Independent practices and groups with large contact lens panels.

## Impact If Built
A practice with ~1,500 wearers that moves 10% more of them to an annual supply at ~$100 of margin each would add ~$15,000 a year (estimate).

# The Verification Request That Lapses at Hour Eight

**Niche:** [[niches/optometry-practices/contact-lens-rx-annual-supply/profile|Contact Lens Prescriptions, Verification & Annual Supply]]
**Industry:** [[industries/optometry-practices|Optometry Practices]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A seller's verification request sits unread in a fax queue past eight business hours, the prescription is verified by default, and the patient may receive lenses the doctor would not have approved.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #bert #compliance #automation #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep fitted contact lens wearers buying their lenses through the prescribing practice, and whoever puts the highest share of fitted wearers on an annual supply or subscription takes the account.

## The Problem
Under the Contact Lens Rule, a prescriber who does not respond within eight business hours has verified the prescription (FTC guidance). Requests for expired prescriptions, wrong parameters or a different brand go through if nobody reads them in time.

## Why It's Still Broken
Nobody owns the queue. Requests arrive between patients, robocalls are hard to understand, and the staff member who knows the contact lens patients may be off that day.

## What a Fix Looks Like
One inbox for all verification requests, with the clock shown on each. Automatic matching to the patient and prescription, with a drafted response. Escalation to a named person at hour six.

## Who Feels the Pain
Patients who receive the wrong lenses, ODs whose prescription is effectively overridden, and owners exposed to complaints.

## Impact If Fixed
If ~10% of ~750 yearly requests at a busy practice currently lapse, answering them in time prevents ~75 unreviewed verifications a year (estimate), some of them for expired or wrong prescriptions.

# E-Fax and Call Tools Adapted to the Contact Lens Rule

**Niche:** [[niches/optometry-practices/contact-lens-rx-annual-supply/profile|Contact Lens Prescriptions, Verification & Annual Supply]]
**Industry:** [[industries/optometry-practices|Optometry Practices]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Practices already have e-fax, call recording and patient messaging; adapting them to the rule's release confirmations and eight-business-hour verification clock would make compliance automatic.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #naive-bayes #compliance #automation #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep fitted contact lens wearers buying their lenses through the prescribing practice, and whoever puts the highest share of fitted wearers on an annual supply or subscription takes the account.

## The Problem
Verification requests arrive by fax, email and automated phone call and land in general inboxes. Release confirmations are signed on paper and scanned, if at all. The practice cannot show an FTC investigator that it complied.

## What Already Exists
E-fax services, phone systems with call recording, Weave and similar patient messaging, and EHR document storage. Each handles the medium, not the rule.

## The Customization Gap
The tools need to recognise a verification request in any medium, start the eight-business-hour clock (with the practice's business hours), match the patient and current prescription, and draft the response. They also need to send each prescription at the end of the fitting, with a confirmation request, and store both for three years in a form an investigator would accept.

## Target Customer
Patient-communication vendors and EHR vendors serving optometry, reselling to practices.

## Impact If Solved
For a practice receiving ~15 requests a week, automatic matching and drafting saves ~2 staff hours a week (estimate) and leaves an auditable record behind every release and response.

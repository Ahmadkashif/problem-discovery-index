# The Second IPL Session Nobody Books

**Niche:** [[niches/optometry-practices/dry-eye-treatment-courses/profile|Dry Eye Treatment Courses]]
**Industry:** [[industries/optometry-practices|Optometry Practices]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Patients leave the first treatment session without the next one booked, and the course quietly ends there.
**Tags:** #logistic-regression #survival-analysis #automation #worker-facing #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this sub-niche is fighting to turn dry-eye diagnoses into completed in-office treatment series, and whoever completes the most full series per diagnosed patient takes the account.

## The Problem
IPL courses typically need three or four sessions a few weeks apart (estimate). Front desks book one at a time. If the patient leaves without the next date, or cancels it, nobody follows up until the annual exam.

## Why It's Still Broken
Dry eye is often run by one enthusiastic doctor without a coordinator. Recall tools don't know a course is in progress, and device makers don't see the schedule.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Book every session at the start, and track the course as one object. Send a reminder that explains why the next session matters. List incomplete courses weekly for a staff call.

## Who Feels the Pain
Patients who pay for one session and get little relief, and doctors who conclude the treatment does not work.

## Impact If Fixed
Recovering ~2 stalled courses a week at ~$600 of remaining sessions each (estimate) is ~$60,000 a year for a busy dry-eye clinic.

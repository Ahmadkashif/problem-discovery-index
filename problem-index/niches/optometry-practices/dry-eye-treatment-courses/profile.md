# Dry Eye Treatment Courses

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/optometry-practices|Optometry Practices]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this sub-niche is fighting to turn dry-eye diagnoses into completed in-office treatment series, and whoever completes the most full series per diagnosed patient takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$800M a year of in-office dry-eye devices, consumables, diagnostics and staff time in optometry practices (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~55% of the medical eye care niche by tooling and services spend (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low-Medium — diagnostics are digital, but treatment plans, packages and follow-up are run on paper and memory
**Target Buyer:** Owner optometrist running a dry-eye clinic
**Automation Potential:** Medium — scheduling, package billing and adherence follow-up automate; diagnosis and procedures stay with the OD and staff

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Dry eye is mostly a self-pay service line in optometry. Intense pulsed light, thermal pulsation, meibomian gland expression, punctal plugs and newer prescription drugs are sold as courses of several sessions. Medical insurance covers parts of the workup and some drugs, and patients pay cash for most procedures (estimate). Success is completion. A patient who stops after one IPL session gets little benefit and is unlikely to return. This sub-niche contests completion and cash conversion, not progression detection.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Lumenis (OptiLight IPL), Johnson & Johnson Vision (LipiFlow), Sight Sciences (TearCare) and diagnostic makers run practice-building programmes. Patient financing (CareCredit), EHR templates and recall tools sit alongside. None predicts which patients will complete a course, tracks completion by doctor and package, or ties symptom scores to treatment outcomes.

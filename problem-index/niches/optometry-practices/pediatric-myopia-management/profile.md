# Pediatric Myopia Management Programmes

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/optometry-practices|Optometry Practices]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to slow eye growth in children enrolled in myopia control, and whoever shows the lowest measured axial elongation per child per year across a practice's programme takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$400M a year of myopia-control fitting and programme fees, biometers and tracking tools in optometry practices (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~2% of practice revenue today, growing (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low-Medium — axial length is measured on digital biometers in some practices, but progression is tracked in spreadsheets and paper charts
**Target Buyer:** Owner optometrist running a myopia clinic; paediatric-focused practices
**Automation Potential:** Medium — forecasting, tracking and family communication automate; treatment choice stays with the OD

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Children with progressing myopia are an audience the tools ignore. The products are new: CooperVision's MiSight 1 day contact lens (approved 2019, recalled) and Essilor's Stellest spectacle lens, authorised by FDA in September 2025 with 53% less axial growth in its pivotal trial (search summary). Orthokeratology is used off-label for control, and a low-dose atropine candidate received an FDA complete response letter in October 2025 (search summary). Families pay out of pocket for a multi-year programme. Success is measured in tenths of a millimetre of eye length a year, a number most practice software does not store. The child's eye keeps growing whether or not the family keeps coming.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Optical biometers (Topcon Myah, Oculus Myopia Master, Zeiss IOLMaster) come with growth-curve viewers. Manufacturers run practice programmes such as CooperVision's Brilliant Futures, Treehouse Eyes franchises myopia clinics, and education comes from Myopia Profile. None compares a child's growth with the expected curve for their treatment, aggregates outcomes across a practice's programme, or tells the OD when to switch or combine treatments.

# Automated & Remote Refraction

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/optometry-practices|Optometry Practices]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to produce final spectacle prescriptions without the optometrist's full manual refraction, and whoever has the fewest prescription-related remakes per hundred refractions takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$500M a year of digital phoropters, automated refraction systems, remote-optometrist services and the technician time around them (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~2–3% of practice spend, with refraction itself taking a large share of every routine exam's doctor time (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — autorefractors and digital phoropters are common, but the final prescription is still refined by the OD's subjective "one or two" sequence
**Target Buyer:** Owner optometrist facing a doctor shortage; operations leaders at rollups
**Automation Potential:** High — objective measurements plus a guided subjective sequence can be run by a technician or the patient, with OD sign-off

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Refraction is the most repeated task in optometry and the one vision plans pay for. It is also where the optometrist's craft shows: how much astigmatism to give a first-time wearer, when to cut back a child's or a presbyope's prescription, how to balance the two eyes. Remakes for "can't see right" are the industry's quality signal. Automated refraction systems (Topcon's Chronos, Visionix's VX-series, digital phoropters), remote refraction services (Digital Optometrics) and online vision tests promise to take that time off the doctor. Optometrists in 621320 offices earned a mean of $131,590 in 2023 (BLS OEWS), so each minute of OD time freed is worth real money. The contest is whether the automated prescription is as wearable as the doctor's.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Autorefractors and wavefront aberrometers give objective starting points. Digital phoropters let technicians run subjective sequences under remote OD supervision, and remote refraction services supply licensed ODs by video. Online tests sell prescription renewals direct to consumers. None learns from remakes which objective-to-final adjustments an experienced OD makes for which patients, so automated prescriptions stay generic.

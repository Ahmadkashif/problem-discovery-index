# Optometry Rollup Corporate & Clinical Analytics

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/optometry-practices|Optometry Practices]]
**Category:** Insight Layer
**Value-Chain Position:** Aggregator / rollup platforms

## What They Do
Private-equity-backed platforms buy independent optometry practices and run them behind a shared services company. MyEyeDr. (~800–1,000 offices, search summary) announced on 29 September 2026 that it will buy EyeCare Partners' optometry division of more than 300 offices, which EyeCare Partners is selling to cut debt. AEG Vision, Keplr Vision and Acuity Eyecare Group are other optometry platforms (recalled). What is distinct to optometry is the mix their analysts manage: vision-plan versus medical revenue, optical capture and eyewear margin, and OD productivity. Their corporate development and analytics teams screen acquisitions, track integration, model OD productivity, payer mix, optical capture and medical revenue per exam, and report to lenders and boards. The same function at specialty physician platforms, including ophthalmology, is [[niches/physician-practices/pe-specialty-platform-analytics/profile|🔍 Private-Equity Specialty Platforms & Physician MSO Analytics]] (36, kill switch), and at dental service organisations [[niches/dental-practices/dso-corp-dev-analytics/profile|🔍 DSO Corporate Development & Clinical Analytics]] (41, kill switch).

## Insight Function
**Size:** ~20-100 analysts, data staff and corporate development staff per large platform (estimate)
**Output:** Acquisition models, OD productivity and payer-mix analytics, optical capture and margin reporting, integration tracking, lender reporting; none of it sold
**Proprietary data:** Encounter, claims, optical sales and financial records across hundreds of acquired offices
**Clock:** Deal timelines and lender covenant reporting; regulatory approval of the MyEyeDr.–EyeCare Partners deal
**Buyer:** Chief strategy officer or head of analytics at the platform

**Kill switches:** **HIPAA**, on the analytics half, which is most of the work and carries the verdict: productivity, payer-mix and clinical analytics run on identified encounter and claims data. The deal-screening half runs on practice financials and has no switch, but would fail Q1 anyway, since it is never invoiced.
**Verdict:** Logged — kill switch. An internal function that spends its insight on the platform's own deals and margins, but the most likely buyer of clinical and billing automation across hundreds of offices at once.

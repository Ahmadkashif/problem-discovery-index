# The Allowance Quoted in the Morning

**Niche:** [[niches/optometry-practices/benefits-verification-estimates/profile|Vision & Medical Benefits Verification and Patient Estimates]]
**Industry:** [[industries/optometry-practices|Optometry Practices]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The frame allowance and lens coverage quoted at check-in turn out wrong at checkout, and the patient pays more than they were told.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #gradient-boosting #worker-facing #quick-win #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to tell each patient before the visit what their plans will pay for the exam, glasses and contacts, and whoever leaves the smallest gap between the pre-visit estimate and the adjudicated claim takes the account.

## The Problem
A patient is told their plan covers a ~$150 frame (estimate). They choose a frame in that range, add anti-reflective coating and progressives, and the bill is far higher than expected because the plan pays those options differently. Or the benefit had already been used at another provider.

## Why It's Still Broken
Morning checks capture headline allowances, not lens options, overage discounts or recent use elsewhere. Nobody compares the estimate with the adjudicated claim afterwards, so the same mistakes repeat.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Re-price at the optical counter from the full benefit card, with option-level coverage. Compare every adjudicated claim with its estimate, and feed the differences back into the rules.

## Who Feels the Pain
Patients surprised at checkout, opticians who absorb the complaint, and front-desk staff blamed for the original quote.

## Impact If Fixed
Halving estimate errors on a practice's ~2,500 yearly eyewear sales would avoid ~250 checkout disputes or write-offs a year (estimate).

# Medicaid Children's Vision & School-Screening Follow-Up

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/optometry-practices|Optometry Practices]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get children who fail a vision screening through a full eye exam, and whoever completes exams for the highest share of referred children takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$600M a year of Medicaid and CHIP children's eye exams in optometry practices, plus the outreach, scheduling and benefit administration around them (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~3% of practice revenue, concentrated in practices that accept Medicaid (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — screening results come home on paper, referrals are rarely tracked, and Medicaid vision benefits differ by state and managed-care plan
**Target Buyer:** Owner optometrist at Medicaid-accepting practices; Medicaid managed-care vision benefit administrators; school-vision programmes
**Automation Potential:** Medium — referral tracking, benefit checks and scheduling automate; the exam and dispensing stay in person

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Low-income children are an audience the industry's tools ignore. Medicaid's EPSDT benefit entitles children to eye exams and glasses, but the path from a failed school or paediatric screening to a completed exam breaks at every handoff. A note goes home in a backpack, the family has to find a Medicaid-accepting optometrist, the benefit is administered by a different vision vendor in each state or plan, and fees are low. Medicare-age disparities in eye exams are documented: Black and Hispanic beneficiaries with diabetes were less likely to have one (CDC, 2017). The paediatric picture is similar but less measured (estimate). The dental equivalent is analysed at [[niches/dental-practices/medicaid-dental/profile|🟣 Medicaid Dental Providers]].

## Current Tools & Gaps
Screening devices (photoscreeners in paediatric offices and schools), school nurses' spreadsheets, nonprofit mobile clinics such as Vision To Learn and OneSight, Medicaid vision benefit administrators (Centene's Envolve Vision, March Vision Care, Avesis, Superior Vision, recalled) and practice EHRs. Nobody follows a referred child from failed screening to completed exam across the school, the family, the plan and the practice.

# The Failed Screening That Never Becomes an Exam

**Niche:** [[niches/optometry-practices/medicaid-childrens-vision/profile|Medicaid Children's Vision & School-Screening Follow-Up]]
**Industry:** [[industries/optometry-practices|Optometry Practices]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A child fails a school vision screening, a note goes home, and nothing happens, often for years.
**Tags:** #logistic-regression #workflow-orchestration #data-integration #worker-facing #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get children who fail a vision screening through a full eye exam, and whoever completes exams for the highest share of referred children takes the account.

## The Problem
The school nurse records the failed screening and sends a letter home. The family may not read it, may not know where to go, or may not know Medicaid covers the exam. The nurse has no way to know whether the child was seen.

## Why It's Still Broken
No one is paid to close the loop. Schools screen, practices examine, plans pay, and the gap between them belongs to nobody.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A shared referral record with a named owner, a booked appointment before the family leaves the screening conversation where possible, and a completion check after 60 days that triggers a call.

## Who Feels the Pain
Children struggling to see the board, families who did not understand the letter, and school nurses who screen the same children year after year.

## Impact If Fixed
If a school nurse's caseload of ~150 failed screenings a year moves from ~40% to ~55% examined, ~22 more children a year get glasses or treatment (estimate).

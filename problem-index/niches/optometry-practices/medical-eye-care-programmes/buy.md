# EHR Disease Templates Adapted to Optometric Co-Management

**Industry:** [[industries/optometry-practices|Optometry Practices]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Optometry EHRs already have disease templates; adapting them to the co-management relationship with ophthalmology would stop patients disappearing after referral.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #workflow-orchestration #data-integration #automation #compliance
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is "Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep medically managed eye patients in the optometry practice", but glaucoma and dry eye are won by different things. Glaucoma is won by catching progression at the visit it first shows, with imaging and field analysis across devices and co-management with ophthalmology. Dry eye is won by turning a diagnosis into a completed, mostly cash-paid series of in-office treatments. Vendors, buyers' metrics and the cost of failure differ. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## The Problem
Optometrists refer patients to ophthalmologists for surgery, injections and advanced glaucoma, then often share their post-operative or ongoing care. The referral and the return are handled by letter and fax, and patients who are referred often never come back to the optometrist.

## What Already Exists
EHR templates for glaucoma, cataract and dry eye; referral letter generators; Direct messaging between practices; co-management agreements that set out who does what and how post-operative fees are split under Medicare's global surgery rules (recalled).

## The Customization Gap
Templates need the co-management workflow: a tracked referral with the question asked, the ophthalmologist's finding returned in structured form, the agreed split of care, and the date the patient is due back. The EHR should flag a referred patient with no return letter after a set number of weeks.

## Target Customer
EHR vendors serving optometry, and multi-OD groups with referral relationships to ophthalmology groups.

## Impact If Solved
If a practice referring ~300 patients a year gets 15% more back into its own follow-up, it keeps ~45 recurring medical patients a year (estimate).

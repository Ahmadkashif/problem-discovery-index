# Medical Eye Care Programmes (Glaucoma & Dry Eye)

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/optometry-practices|Optometry Practices]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is "Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep medically managed eye patients in the optometry practice", but glaucoma and dry eye are won by different things. Glaucoma is won by catching progression at the visit it first shows, with imaging and field analysis across devices and co-management with ophthalmology. Dry eye is won by turning a diagnosis into a completed, mostly cash-paid series of in-office treatments. Vendors, buyers' metrics and the cost of failure differ. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.5B a year of imaging and testing devices, review software, technician time and dry-eye treatment equipment tied to medical eye care in optometry practices (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~20% of practice revenue comes from medical eye care at medically oriented practices, far less at retail-style ones (estimate; no current survey found)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — devices are digital and every practice keeps an EHR, but disease tracking lives in device viewers and recall lists
**Target Buyer:** Owner optometrist or clinical director at multi-OD groups
**Automation Potential:** Medium — testing analysis and recall automate; the clinical call stays with the licensed OD

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Medical eye care is how an optometry practice grows beyond the fixed-fee vision exam. Optometrists treat glaucoma in every state, and 14 states now let them perform lasers such as SLT and YAG capsulotomy (Optometric Management, 2026). Dry eye has become a self-pay service line built on in-office devices and new prescription drugs. Both create a recurring patient who returns several times a year, billed to medical insurance or paid in cash, instead of once a year to a vision plan. Platforms such as MyEyeDr. now describe centres of excellence for common eye diseases as part of their growth plans.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Device makers' review software (Zeiss FORUM, Topcon Harmony, Heidelberg's viewer), EHR disease templates, recall tools such as Weave, and dry-eye device makers' practice programmes cover pieces. None keeps one record of each medical patient's disease course, treatment and adherence across devices and visits, and none tells the owner how many medical patients the practice is losing.

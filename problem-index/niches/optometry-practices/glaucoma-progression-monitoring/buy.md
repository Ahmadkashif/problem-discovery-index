# Device Review Software Adapted to Mixed Optometry Lanes

**Niche:** [[niches/optometry-practices/glaucoma-progression-monitoring/profile|Glaucoma Monitoring & Progression Detection]]
**Industry:** [[industries/optometry-practices|Optometry Practices]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Device makers' review platforms already do progression analysis well on their own scans; adapting them to import other makers' data would serve the typical mixed optometry lane.
**Tags:** #cnns #transfer-learning #data-integration #evaluation-metrics #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this sub-niche is fighting to catch glaucoma progression at the first visit it shows, and whoever leaves the smallest share of progressing eyes flagged a visit late takes the account.

## The Problem
A typical optometry practice owns an OCT from one maker, a field analyser from another and a widefield camera from a third. Each comes with its own viewer. The doctor flips between three screens to judge one eye.

## What Already Exists
Zeiss FORUM and Topcon Harmony are vendor-neutral image management platforms to a degree, storing DICOM from other devices. Their progression analytics work fully only on the maker's own scans.

## The Customization Gap
The platforms need cross-device progression for the device mixes optometry practices actually own. That means harmonised thickness scales, imported field series, and a one-screen glaucoma summary built for a fifteen-minute exam rather than a glaucoma subspecialty clinic.

## Target Customer
Device makers and their optometry sales channels, or a third party selling into practices that already own the platforms.

## Impact If Solved
Cutting the time an OD spends assembling a glaucoma review from ~3 minutes to under one, across ~15 glaucoma visits a day, saves ~30 minutes of doctor time daily (estimate).

# Hour-Bank Eligibility & Self-Pay at Health and Welfare Funds

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/self-insured-benefit-pools|Self-Insured Benefit & Risk Pools]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to decide every member's coverage for next month from hours reported late and by hand, and whoever pays the fewest claims for people who were not eligible while cutting off nobody who was takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.5B a year of eligibility, contribution-processing and member-service spend at US multiemployer health and welfare funds, mostly TPA fees and fund-office staff (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~10% of self-insured funds' and pools' non-claim spend (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low-Medium — larger contractors remit online; small ones send spreadsheets, PDFs and paper, and rules live in trustee resolutions
**Target Buyer:** Fund Administrator / TPA account executive
**Automation Potential:** High for posting, eligibility runs and notices; low for appeals and hardship decisions

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Coverage in a Taft-Hartley health fund is earned month by month from hours. TeamCare grants coverage in the second month after the month worked; the Northern California Laborers grant initial eligibility after 440 banked hours and deduct 110 a month; the Southern California IBEW-NECA plan credits contributions more than three months late to the month received, because its vendor contracts forbid retroactive eligibility. The same remittance that feeds a fund office's pension credit (the contest of [[niches/pension-funds/multiemployer-fund-offices/profile|🟠 Multiemployer Pension Fund Offices]]) feeds health eligibility here, but the health contest differs: it is decided every month, and errors become paid claims or uninsured families rather than a pension dispute decades later.

## Current Tools & Gaps
TPAs serving multiemployer funds (Zenith American, BeneSys, Wilson-McShane and regional firms), fund-office systems and employer remittance portals, with COBRA and self-pay administration attached. Gaps: lapses discovered at the pharmacy counter; retro-terminations after payroll audits that leave claims to recover; reciprocity transfers handled by fax and email; rules nobody has written down in one place.

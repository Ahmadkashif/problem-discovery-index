# Member Liability Rating & Contribution Allocation

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/self-insured-benefit-pools|Self-Insured Benefit & Risk Pools]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to price each member's liability coverage close to its own risk (sworn officers, jail beds, payroll, road miles, past losses), and whoever keeps its low-loss members from leaving for commercial quotes at renewal takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$300M a year of underwriting staff, actuarial rating studies and pool-system rating modules spent on allocating liability contributions among members (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~2% of self-insured funds' and pools' non-claim spend (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low-Medium — rating formulas run in pool systems or spreadsheets; exposure data is self-reported each spring
**Target Buyer:** Pool Underwriting Director / Finance Director
**Automation Potential:** High for the calculation; medium for the judgement about loads and credits

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A pool's liability contribution is an allocation of a fixed budget (the projected loss fund, reserve-fund contributions, reinsurance and administration) among members. CIRSA's method multiplies each coverage's rate by exposure, a deductible factor and an experience modifier. Over-charge a well-run town and a commercial carrier will quote it lower; under-charge a city with a troubled police department and every other member subsidises it. The contest is accuracy at the member level with thin, lumpy experience. Commercial insurers, brokers' alternative programmes and competing pools are the competitors, and the prize is the low-loss member's renewal.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Pool management systems with rating modules, actuarial rate studies, exposure questionnaires. Gaps: exposure bases (payroll, population, officers) say little about how a police department is run; experience modifiers swing on one claim; and risk-management quality (policies, training, early-warning systems) is not rated.

# Body-Camera Footage Reviewed When Counsel Gets to It

**Niche:** [[niches/self-insured-benefit-pools/police-civil-rights-claims/profile|Law Enforcement & Civil Rights Claims Management]]
**Industry:** [[industries/self-insured-benefit-pools|Self-Insured Benefit & Risk Pools]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The evidence that most often decides a police claim's value sits unwatched for weeks after notice.
**Tags:** #cnns #object-detection #transformers #large-language-models #workflow-orchestration #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to tell, within weeks of notice, which use-of-force, false-arrest and employment claims will pierce the pool's retention and to settle or harden the defence accordingly, and whoever closes those claims at the lowest ultimate cost per claim takes the account.

## The Problem
Body-worn and dash camera video now exists for most police encounters. It reaches the pool late, through the member's records unit or defence counsel, and is watched in full only when counsel prepares a response. By then the early-settlement window may have closed, or the reserve has sat too low for months.

## Why It's Still Broken
Video is large, held in the department's evidence system under chain-of-custody rules, and slow to release. Pool adjusters lack time and tools to review hours of footage on every claim, so it waits for counsel, who bills for review.

## What a Fix Looks Like
An intake protocol that requests video at first notice, plus assisted review: transcription, event detection (weapon displayed, force used, restraint, commands given) and a timeline of the encounter linked to the complaint's allegations, for the adjuster to confirm. High-risk footage goes to senior review in days.

## Who Feels the Pain
Liability adjusters setting reserves blind; claims managers who learn a claim's value from counsel months later; members whose contributions absorb the cost of late settlements.

## Impact If Fixed
The decisive evidence informs the first reserve and the settle-or-defend call, rather than arriving after the cheap options are gone.

# Lasers Discovered at Filing

**Niche:** [[niches/self-insured-benefit-pools/stop-loss-reimbursement/profile|Stop-Loss Reimbursement Claims & Large-Claimant Notices]]
**Industry:** [[industries/self-insured-benefit-pools|Self-Insured Benefit & Risk Pools]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The fund learns that a claimant was lasered at renewal, or that its plan document does not match the carrier's terms, only when the reimbursement is denied.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #compliance #quick-win #automation #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to recover every reimbursable dollar of a large claim from the stop-loss carrier inside the contract's paid-claim and filing windows, and whoever files complete packets that are paid without a records request or denial takes the account.

## The Problem
At renewal the carrier may set a higher deductible for a known large claimant, exclude a condition, or require plan-document language. Those terms sit in the policy and the broker's renewal summary. Months later, the TPA files as usual and is denied.

## Why It's Still Broken
Renewal terms are negotiated by the broker and trustees, administered by the TPA, and enforced by the carrier. Nobody translates them into the TPA's claim tracking.

## What a Fix Looks Like
At each renewal, extract lasers, exclusions and conformity requirements into a register shared with the TPA and fund office; check the plan document against them; and flag affected claimants as soon as claims start.

## Who Feels the Pain
Fund administrators explaining a shortfall to trustees; TPAs absorbing blame; trustees facing an unbudgeted loss.

## Impact If Fixed
No surprise denials from terms everyone signed but nobody tracked.

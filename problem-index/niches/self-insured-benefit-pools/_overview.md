# Niche Analysis — Self-Insured Benefit & Risk Pools

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/self-insured-benefit-pools|Self-Insured Benefit & Risk Pools]]

## Niche Selection

A self-insured fund or pool bears its members' risk itself and buys protection only above a retention. It sets each member's contribution on experience too thin to trust, and learns years later whether it was right. The industry has two halves on different clocks. Health and welfare funds (Taft-Hartley funds, retiree trusts, public employee health pools) are financed by hours or appropriations and governed by trustees, with HIPAA over every claim. Public entity property, liability and workers' compensation pools are owned by the cities, counties and districts they cover. The eight niches below split the industry by what vendors, advisers and internal teams compete over. Market sizes are estimates of administration, claims handling, consulting, actuarial, audit, reinsurance placement and systems spend within each niche, not of benefits or claims paid. The parent denominator for "share" is self-insured funds' and pools' non-claim spend, estimated at ~$15B a year.

## Why These Niches

Cost projection at health and welfare funds leads on market share because it governs the largest pot of money in the industry: ~1,220 multiemployer funds covering ~4.8M members (Milliman, 2023 data). Each one commits a share of every hour's wage package to a projection that holds for a whole bargaining agreement. Public entity liability pools are second because liability is the line that created pooling, and the police-verdict cycle (CIRSA's 150% law enforcement increase for 2024) now drives its cost and its reinsurance. The two low-digitized niches are where the work still runs on spreadsheets and paper: members' property schedules collected each spring for reinsurers, and monthly health eligibility decided from hours that small contractors report by hand. The two underserved audiences are the small member with no risk manager, typical of the 80% of public entities that pool (AGRiP), and the trustees of closed retiree medical trusts, whom tools built for active plans ignore. The two automatable niches are the most rule-bound, deadline-driven work in health funds: recovering large claims from stop-loss carriers inside the contract window, and assembling federal filings from four or five vendors' data before statutory dates.

## Filter Notes

Seven of the eight level-1 niches are terminal. Public Entity Liability Pools is not: its contested sentence produced two contests with different winners. One is claims: telling early which use-of-force, false-arrest and employment claims will pierce the retention, and settling or defending accordingly. Claims managers, TPAs and defence panels compete on that, on complaints, video and dockets, against litigation milestones. The other is rating: pricing each member close to its own risk so low-loss members do not leave for commercial quotes. Underwriters, actuaries and commercial carriers compete on that, on exposures and loss histories, against the July 1 or January 1 renewal. The niche decomposes into **Law Enforcement & Civil Rights Claims Management** and **Member Liability Rating & Contribution Allocation**. The early claim read, the industry's high-impact problem, sits in the first.

Three terminal calls were close. **Health & Welfare Fund Cost Projection** could have split off vendor procurement (PBM, network and stop-loss RFPs), but the projection governs both: trustees buy vendors to hit the cost per hour the projection sets. The comparative view of PBM contracts is already logged as [[niches/pharmacy-independents/pharmacy-benefit-consulting-audit/profile|🔍 Pharmacy Benefit Consulting & Contract Audit]]. **Hour-Bank Eligibility** shares its input, the employer remittance, with [[niches/pension-funds/multiemployer-fund-offices/profile|🟠 Multiemployer Pension Fund Offices]]. It stays separate because the health contest is decided every month and its errors become paid claims or uninsured families, while the pension contest is hours-to-credit, settled at retirement. **Federal Health Plan Reporting** could split RxDC from parity and ACA work, but one contest governs all of them: assembling vendors' data before a statutory date. The parity analysis itself is logged as [[niches/behavioral-health-clinics/parity-compliance-analysis/profile|🔍 Mental Health Parity Compliance Analysis]].

Four candidates were considered and rejected. **Single-employer self-insured health programmes** belong to the employer's HR and finance staff and are already analysed as [[niches/insurance-tpa/self-insured-employers/profile|🟠 Self-Insured Employer Programs]]; this industry covers the trustee- or board-run funds. **Workers' compensation group self-insurance funds** were considered as a level-1 niche. Their claims contest is the TPA's ([[niches/insurance-tpa/workers-comp-claims/profile|🔵 Workers' Comp Claims TPA]]), their rating rests on bureau experience mods ([[niches/insurance-tpa/workers-comp-rating-bureaus/profile|🔍 Workers' Compensation Rating Bureaus]]), and their solvency contest belongs to regulators, so nothing distinct was left. **MEWAs and association health plans** fail the filter. Their contest is regulatory status (state licensing and DOL Form M-1) rather than a workflow, and the operating contests repeat niches 1, 4 and 7. **Retiree health benefit tracking for public employers** is covered by [[niches/insurance-tpa/municipal-benefits/profile|🟠 Municipal & Public Entity Benefits]], so niche 6 is scoped to the trusts that hold the money. Labor Unions (a sibling industry not yet built) will cover the unions that bargain the contribution, not the jointly trusteed funds.

## Why These Pockets

No pocket qualifies, and the split runs along the industry's two halves. On the health side, every firm that sees across many funds works on protected health information that belongs to the funds. That covers the repricing vendors (47), the consultants (45), the stop-loss underwriters who hold the label for every large-claim prediction (43), the analytics platforms (41), the captive programmes (38) and the captive managers (38). The plan auditors fail the gate before the question arises: their headcount is audit testing, and the technical group behind it is a handful of people per firm. The neighbours already in the vault show the same fence and are linked, not re-profiled: [[niches/insurance-tpa/medical-bill-review-networks/profile|🔍 Medical Bill Review & Repricing Networks]], [[niches/hr-consultants/benefits-brokerage-analytics/profile|🔍 Employee Benefits Brokerage Analytics]], [[niches/pharmacy-independents/pharmacy-benefit-consulting-audit/profile|🔍 Pharmacy Benefit Consulting & Contract Audit]], [[niches/behavioral-health-clinics/parity-compliance-analysis/profile|🔍 Mental Health Parity Compliance Analysis]] and [[niches/insurance-tpa/wc-pharmacy-benefit-management/profile|🔍 Workers' Compensation Pharmacy Benefit Management]]. The actuaries who sign pools' and captives' reserve opinions are already logged as [[niches/insurance-tpa/self-insured-actuarial-consulting/profile|🔍 Self-Insured Actuarial Consulting]], held back by client-owned loss data, and the multiemployer pension actuaries as [[niches/pension-funds/multiemployer-plan-actuaries/profile|🔍 Multiemployer Plan Actuaries & Zone Certification]]. The one clean health-side data business, normalised price-transparency files, sits under [[niches/medical-billing/price-transparency-data-vendors/profile|🔍 Healthcare Price Transparency Data]] (a second copy is logged under [[niches/urgent-care/price-transparency-data-vendors/profile|🔍 Healthcare Price Transparency Data Vendors]]).

On the public-entity side the data is cleaner but the buyers are few. Excess and reinsurance underwriters (39) hold the only cross-pool view of large police and property losses, but price it into insurance, and only a handful of markets write it. The largest pools (36) own the richest clean corpus in the industry and spend it on their own rates. Pool associations fail the gate outright: AGRiP lists a staff of seven, which is why the latest national count of pools dates from 2014. The claims comparables pool adjusters value against come from [[niches/personal-injury-law/jury-verdict-settlement-reporters/profile|🔍 Jury Verdict & Settlement Reporters]] (45), and workers' compensation pools draw on [[niches/insurance-tpa/workers-comp-rating-bureaus/profile|🔍 Workers' Compensation Rating Bureaus]] (56, indexed) for class codes and mods.

The strongest clean near-miss is the public safety policy publisher (44). Lexipol's attorneys turn each state's new laws and appellate decisions into policy manuals before their effective dates, some pools discount premiums for members that subscribe, and the content is the invoice. The market is essentially one firm, and its inputs are public law. The vault's favourite shape, a decision record whose outcome the producer holds and does not grade, appears twice here, both times out of reach. The stop-loss underwriter sees every disclosure and pays every large claim, behind HIPAA. The pool sees every early police claim read and its closed value, but has no research function to sell. A consortium of pools pooling that record, the build in [[niches/self-insured-benefit-pools/public-entity-liability-pools/build|A Consortium Loss Database for Police and Civil Rights Claims]], is the nearest thing to an insight business this industry could grow.

# A Recommendation List Small Members Actually Finish

**Niche:** [[niches/self-insured-benefit-pools/small-public-entity-members/profile|Small Public Entity Members Without a Risk Manager]]
**Industry:** [[industries/self-insured-benefit-pools|Self-Insured Benefit & Risk Pools]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A village clerk receives a twelve-item loss-control letter once a year and fixes the two cheapest items, not the two that prevent claims.
**Tags:** #causal-inference #gradient-boosting #large-language-models #evaluation-metrics #worker-facing #workflow-orchestration #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get members with no risk manager to fix what the loss-control visit found (the playground surface, the holding-cell door, the untrained part-time officer), and whoever gets the most recommendations completed before a claim takes the account.

## The Problem
Loss-control letters list every finding with equal weight. Small members have little time and less budget, so they choose by cost and convenience. The pool cannot say which recommendations reduce claims, so it cannot tell them either.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Recommendation completion is rarely recorded in structured form, the evidence linking fixes to claims has never been assembled, and loss-control software is designed for inspectors, not for the person who must act.

## What to Build
A member-facing list of at most three priorities per quarter, ranked by estimated claim reduction per dollar from the pool's own history (causal estimates where data allow, consultant judgement where not). Each item carries a photo from the visit, a plain-language reason, a template or vendor contact, and a one-tap way to mark it done with a photo. Completion feeds the member's rating credit.

## Target Customer
Risk control directors at municipal, special district and school pools with many small members.

## Impact If Built
More of the fixes that matter get done, and the pool gets the completion data it needs to prove loss control works.

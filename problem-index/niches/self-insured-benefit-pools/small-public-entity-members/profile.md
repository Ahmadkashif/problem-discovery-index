# Small Public Entity Members Without a Risk Manager

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/self-insured-benefit-pools|Self-Insured Benefit & Risk Pools]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get members with no risk manager to fix what the loss-control visit found (the playground surface, the holding-cell door, the untrained part-time officer), and whoever gets the most recommendations completed before a claim takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$600M a year of loss-control, training and member risk-management services that pools and their competitors direct at small members (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~4% of self-insured funds' and pools' non-claim spend (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — visits recorded on paper or templates; recommendations sent as letters; follow-up by email
**Target Buyer:** Pool Risk Control Director (the audience is the member's clerk, manager or superintendent)
**Automation Potential:** Medium — reporting, prioritising and follow-up automate; the visit and the persuasion do not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
AGRiP estimates at least 80% of the 90,000+ US public entities belong to a pool, and pooling is most common among small and mid-sized entities. Most of them are villages, townships, fire and water districts and small school districts with no risk manager. The person responsible is a clerk-treasurer, a town manager or a superintendent who also runs payroll, elections or a budget. Risk tools are built for corporate EHS departments and large cities. These members generate a disproportionate share of preventable claims (playground falls, sidewalk trips, small-department police claims) and are the members commercial carriers court when their loss record is clean.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Pool loss-control consultants, online training libraries, model policy subscriptions (pools sometimes discount premiums for members that subscribe to services such as Lexipol), and pool member portals. Gaps: recommendations not prioritised for someone with two hours a month; completion not tracked; no evidence shown to members of which fixes matter.

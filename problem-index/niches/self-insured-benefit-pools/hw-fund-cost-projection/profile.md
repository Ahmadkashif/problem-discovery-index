# Health & Welfare Fund Cost Projection & Bargaining Allocation

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/self-insured-benefit-pools|Self-Insured Benefit & Risk Pools]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to project a health fund's cost per hour worked across a three-to-five-year bargaining agreement, and whoever's projection keeps the fund's reserve inside the trustees' target band without a mid-contract benefit cut or emergency reallocation takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.2B a year of consulting, actuarial and analytics spend by US multiemployer health and welfare funds (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~8% of self-insured funds' and pools' non-claim spend (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — claims and hours are digital at the TPA, but the projection that sets the contribution is a consultant's spreadsheet
**Target Buyer:** Fund Administrator and the union and employer co-chairs of the board of trustees
**Automation Potential:** High for the projection and scenario work; low for the advice to the bargaining parties

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A Taft-Hartley health fund does not set a premium. The union and the contractors' association bargain a total wage-and-benefit package per hour, typically fixed for three to five years (estimate), and the trustees tell the bargaining parties how much of each hour must go to health. If the projection is low, the reserve drains and trustees must cut benefits or ask for a mid-contract reallocation from wages. If it is high, members lose take-home pay for no reason. Milliman counted 1,220 such funds covering ~4.8M members in 2023, holding on average about sixteen months of benefits and expenses in reserve. Medical trend is the hardest input to call: Segal projects a 9.9% median for 2027, with trends approaching their highest levels in 15 years. Hours are the other: a construction downturn cuts contributions while claims keep coming.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Consulting actuaries (Segal, Horizon Actuarial, Milliman and regional firms) build the projection from the TPA's claims and hours, trend surveys and plan-design pricing. Analytics vendors report experience. The gaps: projections are not graded against what emerged; hours are forecast by assumption rather than from dispatch, permit or project data; and each fund's projection is rebuilt from scratch at every bargaining cycle.

# Medicare Plan Comparison Engines Adapted to Trust Subsidies

**Niche:** [[niches/self-insured-benefit-pools/retiree-medical-trusts/profile|Retiree Medical VEBAs & OPEB Trusts]]
**Industry:** [[industries/self-insured-benefit-pools|Self-Insured Benefit & Risk Pools]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Medicare exchanges compare individual plans well; they do not check whether each retiree keeps the benefit the trust promised.
**Tags:** #k-nearest-neighbors #feature-engineering #data-integration #worker-facing #automation #compliance
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to cut a closed retiree trust's cost per retiree without cutting benefits, and whoever moves the most Medicare-eligible retirees onto group Medicare Advantage or subsidised exchange coverage with no loss of benefit takes the account.

## The Problem
Trusts move Medicare-eligible retirees to group Medicare Advantage or to individual plans funded through an HRA. Each move must not reduce benefits the trust is bound to provide, and retirees need help choosing.

## What Already Exists
Individual Medicare exchanges with plan comparison and enrolment, and group Medicare Advantage proposals from national carriers.

## The Customization Gap
The trust needs comparison against its own legacy plan design, with each retiree's drugs and providers; the trust's subsidy and HRA rules; flags where a retiree would lose coverage the trust must keep; and reporting to trustees on cost per retiree before and after.

## Target Customer
Retiree trusts and the consultants and exchanges that serve them.

## Impact If Solved
Moves that cut cost per retiree can proceed with evidence that no one was made worse off, which is the trustees' fiduciary question.

# Retirees Lost Between the Trust and the Exchange

**Niche:** [[niches/self-insured-benefit-pools/retiree-medical-trusts/profile|Retiree Medical VEBAs & OPEB Trusts]]
**Industry:** [[industries/self-insured-benefit-pools|Self-Insured Benefit & Risk Pools]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A retiree who moved to an exchange plan funded by the trust's HRA stops claiming reimbursements and never tells anyone why.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #logistic-regression #worker-facing #workflow-orchestration #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to cut a closed retiree trust's cost per retiree without cutting benefits, and whoever moves the most Medicare-eligible retirees onto group Medicare Advantage or subsidised exchange coverage with no loss of benefit takes the account.

## The Problem
After a move to individual coverage funded through an HRA, retirees must submit premium and expense reimbursements. Some never do, some miss deadlines, some lose coverage when a premium fails. The trust sees only that claims stopped.

## Why It's Still Broken
Responsibility is split between the trust, the exchange and the HRA administrator, and no one owns the retiree's experience end to end.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A shared view of each retiree's enrolment, premium and reimbursement status, with outreach when reimbursements stop or premiums lapse, and a plain-language helpline drafted from the trust's own rules.

## Who Feels the Pain
Elderly retirees and surviving spouses; trust staff fielding calls; trustees who promised the move would be seamless.

## Impact If Fixed
Fewer retirees without coverage or unreimbursed, and a smoother path for the cost savings the move was meant to deliver.

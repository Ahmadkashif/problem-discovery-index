# Retiree Medical VEBAs & OPEB Trusts

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/self-insured-benefit-pools|Self-Insured Benefit & Risk Pools]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to cut a closed retiree trust's cost per retiree without cutting benefits, and whoever moves the most Medicare-eligible retirees onto group Medicare Advantage or subsidised exchange coverage with no loss of benefit takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$400M a year of consulting, actuarial, exchange and administration fees paid by retiree medical trusts (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~3% of self-insured funds' and pools' non-claim spend (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — claims and exchange enrolment are digital; projections and retiree communication are not
**Target Buyer:** Trust Executive Director / board of trustees
**Automation Potential:** Medium — projection and enrolment support automate; benefit-change decisions stay with trustees

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Retiree medical trusts pay for a closing population with a fixed pot. Voluntary employees' beneficiary associations (VEBAs) were created when employers or unions settled retiree health liabilities, and public employers prefund OPEB in trusts reported under GASB 74 and 75. The UAW Retiree Medical Benefits Trust alone held ~$62.9B of assets and spent ~$3.3B at end-2024 (Form 990), and reported covering over 535,000 members in 2024. Most trusts are far smaller and have no in-house actuaries. Tools serve active employer plans; retiree trusts face a different question: how to make the money last as long as the people. The retiree-liability tracking work of public employers' TPAs sits under [[niches/insurance-tpa/municipal-benefits/profile|🟠 Municipal & Public Entity Benefits]].

## Current Tools & Gaps
Consulting actuaries' valuations, group Medicare Advantage and employer group waiver plans from national carriers, individual Medicare exchanges funded through HRAs, and TPA claims administration. Gaps: depletion dates projected once a year in a valuation; benefit equivalence between the legacy plan and replacement options checked by hand; retirees confused by HRA reimbursements.

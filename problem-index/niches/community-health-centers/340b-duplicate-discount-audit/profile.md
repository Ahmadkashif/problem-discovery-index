# Medicaid Duplicate-Discount Prevention & HRSA 340B Audit

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/community-health-centers|Community Health Centers]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep every 340B purchase defensible against diversion and Medicaid duplicate-discount findings, and whoever has the fewest adverse findings per HRSA 340B audit takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$0.1B a year of self-audit software, independent audits and 340B compliance consulting for health centers (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~25% of the parent 340B niche's spend (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — data comes from administrators, but self-audits are sampled by hand into spreadsheets
**Target Buyer:** Compliance officer or 340B programme manager
**Automation Potential:** High — patient definition, provider eligibility, Medicaid Exclusion File and carve-in rules apply to every prescription

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
HRSA audits covered entities for diversion (drugs to non-patients) and duplicate discounts (a Medicaid rebate claimed on a drug bought at 340B). Each adverse finding means repayment to manufacturers and a corrective action plan, so the measure is findings per audit, not pass or fail. For a health center, the hard part is Medicaid managed care. Whether a center carves 340B drugs in or out differs by state and sometimes by plan. Its listing on the Medicaid Exclusion File must match its billing, and contract pharmacies must apply the same logic. The pharmacy's own audit readiness is the fix in [[niches/pharmacy-independents/340b-contract-pharmacy/profile|🟠 340B Contract Pharmacy]].

## Current Tools & Gaps
Administrator compliance modules, independent audit firms, 340B counsel and HRSA's published audit results. Gaps: self-audits sample a few dozen prescriptions; carve-in and carve-out status is held in a spreadsheet by plan and state; contract pharmacy billing is not checked against the center's election.

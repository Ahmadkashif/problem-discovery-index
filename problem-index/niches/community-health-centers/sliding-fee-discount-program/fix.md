# Nominal Charges Nobody Tests as a Barrier

**Niche:** [[niches/community-health-centers/sliding-fee-discount-program/profile|Sliding Fee Discount Programme & Income Documentation]]
**Industry:** [[industries/community-health-centers|Community Health Centers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** HRSA expects a center to evaluate whether its sliding fees deter patients, but most centers have no data linking the fee charged to whether the patient came back.
**Tags:** #causal-inference #survival-analysis #descriptive-statistics #compliance #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to make every sliding-fee discount traceable to documented household income and size under the center's board-approved schedule, and whoever leaves the fewest sliding-fee conditions at HRSA's operational site visit takes the account.

## The Problem
A $20 nominal fee may be trivial for one patient and enough to make another skip follow-up. The board reviews the schedule with a patient survey or none at all, and the site visitor asks what the evaluation found.

## Why It's Still Broken
Fees, collections and return visits sit in different reports. Centers lack analysts. A survey asks patients who came, not those who stopped.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A recurring analysis linking each patient's tier and amount charged to unpaid balances and return-visit intervals. It compares patients just above and below each band threshold, and produces a board-ready summary with the evidence site visitors ask for.

## Who Feels the Pain
CFOs and boards setting fees, compliance officers preparing for site visits, and patients who quietly stop coming.

## Impact If Fixed
If a fee change brought back even 2% of ~5,000 sliding-fee patients (estimate), that is ~100 patients and ~300 kept visits a year.

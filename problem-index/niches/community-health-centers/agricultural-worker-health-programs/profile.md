# Agricultural Worker Health Programmes

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/community-health-centers|Community Health Centers]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep a mobile agricultural worker's chronic care continuous as the worker moves between seasons, farms and states, and whoever keeps the largest share of returning workers with hypertension or diabetes still controlled at their first visit of the season takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$0.2B a year of care coordination, mobile clinic operations and records work for agricultural worker patients (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** 1,028,890 agricultural workers and family members, ~3% of health center patients (UDS 2025)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — paper care cards, phone calls between clinics, and records that stay in the state where they were made
**Target Buyer:** Migrant health programme director; health center CMO
**Automation Potential:** Medium — record exchange and scheduling automate; reaching workers in camps and fields does not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Section 330(g) funds health centers to serve migratory and seasonal agricultural workers. In 2025, health centers saw 1,028,890 of them and their families, including 260,650 migratory workers at 330(g) awardees (UDS Table 4). A worker with diabetes may spend spring in Florida, summer in Michigan and autumn in Washington. Each clinic starts over, without the last HbA1c, the medication list or the pending referral, and a worker whose blood pressure or diabetes was controlled in one state can arrive uncontrolled in the next. Care is delivered by mobile units and evening clinics around harvest schedules, in Spanish and Indigenous languages such as Mixteco, and alongside heat, pesticide and injury risks that clinicians in other settings rarely see.

## Current Tools & Gaps
EHRs at each center, state HIEs that rarely cross state lines, national exchange networks the small clinics seldom use, and the patient-carried record programmes run by migrant clinician networks (named from recall). Gaps: no reliable hand-off of records between seasons; no worker-held summary; seasonal scheduling run on whiteboards.

## Why These Niches

The determination is the product and it is an assertion. Payroll executes it across dozens of regimes maintained by hand. Benefits and termination are where local law is least forgiving and most often wrong. The two underserved people are the worker with two employers and nobody to ask, and the specialist holding a dozen jurisdictions in their head. Monitoring and onboarding are mechanical, and the first is growing faster than any evidence that it works.

## Filter Notes

Seven of the eight are terminal — each names one contest, and dividing further yields features rather than markets.

**Classification and compliance determination** is not, and its halves separate on the rule versus the facts it is applied to. Rule application asks whether the platform has the right legal rule for this jurisdiction, current as of today, traceable to its source, applied consistently. It is internal knowledge work — versioning the rule base, timestamping verification, detecting staleness, making the same facts produce the same answer in every case — and every party benefits: the specialist stops holding a dozen jurisdictions in their head, the platform's own exposure falls, and the customer gets a better answer without anyone having to concede anything. Fact verification asks whether the facts the rule was applied to are the real ones. Classification is a legal test applied to what the person actually does, how they are controlled and whether they work exclusively — and the platform takes the client's description of the engagement at face value, because the client is the customer. Checking means asking the worker how they are managed, examining exclusivity and direction, and being willing to tell a paying customer that the arrangement they described is not the arrangement that exists — which may void the service, cost the account, and produce a written finding that the engagement was non-compliant. A platform can process a contractor payment correctly and cannot make the underlying relationship compliant; improving the rule base does the first, and only fact verification touches the second. So the rules get better and the facts stay unexamined, which is why misclassification remains the sharpest exposure in this industry and why it lands on the client years later.

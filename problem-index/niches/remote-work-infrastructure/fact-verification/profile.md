# Fact Verification

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/remote-work-infrastructure|Remote Work Infrastructure]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Whether the engagement described in the questionnaire is the engagement that actually exists.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$864M — 40% of the classification and determination niche
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~11% of US remote work infrastructure spend
**Digital Adoption:** None — the client's description is taken at face value
**Target Buyer:** Clients' risk functions; platforms willing to check; tax and labour authorities
**Automation Potential:** Moderate — the signals exist; the willingness does not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche

Classification is a legal test applied to facts: what the person actually does, how they are controlled, whether they work exclusively, who provides the equipment, whether they can send a substitute, how integrated they are into the client's organisation.

The platform learns those facts from a questionnaire completed by the client at engagement setup. It does not check them. It cannot know that the contractor attends the client's daily stand-up, uses the client's laptop, has a manager, works exclusively for them and has done for three years — all of which would change the determination in most jurisdictions.

This is the half that is not built, and the reasons are entirely about who the customer is. Checking means asking the worker how they are actually managed, examining exclusivity and direction, and being prepared to tell a paying client that the arrangement they described is not the one that exists — which may void the service, cost the account, and produce a written finding that the engagement was non-compliant.

## Current Tools & Gaps

Classification questionnaires at setup, completed by the client. Contract templates specifying contractor terms, which describe the intended arrangement rather than the actual one. Periodic re-attestation at some platforms. Risk flagging on obvious indicators such as long tenure or a single client.

The gaps are everything on the worker's side and everything continuous. The worker is never asked. Exclusivity is never checked against the worker's other engagements. Integration into the client's organisation is not observed, though invoicing patterns, engagement duration, payment regularity and the platform's own tooling usage all indicate it. And nothing re-evaluates as an engagement drifts, which is how most misclassification arises.

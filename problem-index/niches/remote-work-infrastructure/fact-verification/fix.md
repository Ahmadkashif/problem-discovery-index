# Fix: Nobody Ever Asks the Worker

**Niche:** [[niches/remote-work-infrastructure/fact-verification/profile|Fact Verification]]
**Industry:** [[industries/remote-work-infrastructure|Remote Work Infrastructure]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The classification test turns on how the person is actually managed, and the only party who knows is the one nobody consults.
**Tags:** #compliance #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #workflow-orchestration #worker-facing #quick-win #hypothesis-testing
**Contested on:** Whether the worker will be asked six questions about how they actually work.

## The Problem

A classification determination rests on facts about the working relationship: who sets the hours, who directs the work, whose equipment, whether there are other clients, whether a substitute could be sent, how integrated the person is into the client's team.

Every one of those is known precisely by the worker. The platform asks the client.

The client answers from the arrangement as they conceived it, or as their contract describes it, or as they would prefer it to be characterised — not usually dishonestly, but from a position with an obvious interest and imperfect visibility. The worker, who attends the daily stand-up on the client's calendar invite, using the client's laptop, reporting to a named manager, would answer differently.

Asking them is six questions.

## Why It's Still Broken

The worker's answer might be inconvenient, and the platform's customer is the client. A process that generates evidence against the client's stated position is a process the client did not buy.

There is also a genuine concern about the worker's position: someone who answers honestly and triggers a review may lose the engagement, because the most likely client response to a classification problem is to end the relationship rather than convert it. That concern is real and is a reason to design the protection rather than to avoid the question.

And the worker is not in the platform's contractual chain in the way the client is, so asking them things is outside the established relationship shape.

## What a Fix Looks Like

Ask, protect, and route the answer to a review rather than to a consequence.

Ask six questions, annually. Do you have set working hours and who sets them. Who directs how you do the work. Do you work for other clients. Whose equipment do you use. Could you send someone else to do the work. Are you part of a team at the client with a manager. Two minutes, plain language, in the worker's own language.

Frame it truthfully as protecting them. Misclassification costs the worker their entitlements — leave, notice, statutory contributions, protection from unfair termination — and they have the largest personal stake in the answer. Most will answer honestly if told why.

Protect the answer. The response goes to the platform's compliance function, not to the client. A finding is expressed to the client as a compliance requirement about the arrangement, never as a report of what the worker said. Retaliation is a contractual breach with a stated consequence.

Compare the two accounts. The worker's answers against the client's questionnaire, with the divergences flagged. Where they agree, the determination is far better evidenced than before. Where they diverge, that is the finding, and it is the only one anyone has ever generated in this industry.

Route divergence into a review, not a termination. A conversation with the client about the arrangement, a recommended change, a conversion path. The point is to correct the relationship while it is correctable.

And report the aggregate divergence rate. What share of engagements show a material difference between the client's account and the worker's, by jurisdiction. That number does not exist anywhere and it is the size of the industry's central exposure.

## Who Feels the Pain

Workers, misclassified and losing entitlements they do not know they should have, who could say so in two minutes and are never asked. Clients, carrying an exposure they may not know exists because nobody checked the arrangement they described. And the platform, selling a determination made from one interested party's account of facts the other party knows exactly.

## Impact If Fixed

The facts that classification turns on get established from the person who knows them, in six questions once a year. Divergence between the client's account and the worker's becomes visible — the single most informative signal in this industry and currently generated nowhere. And drift gets caught while a conversation can still fix it.

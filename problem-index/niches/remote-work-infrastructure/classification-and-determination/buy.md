**Contested on:** Whether regulatory intelligence products can support a determination rather than an alert.

## The Problem

Regulatory intelligence is a real category. Change monitoring services track legislative and regulatory developments across jurisdictions, legal research platforms provide primary sources and commentary, and horizon-scanning products alert compliance teams to what is coming.

They deliver awareness. The product here is a determination: given these facts, in this country, is this engagement employment or contracting, does it create a permanent establishment, are these terms valid. That requires the rule identified, the facts structured and the judgement made — and regulatory intelligence delivers the first input to the first step.

## What Already Exists

Regulatory change monitoring services. Legal research platforms with multi-jurisdiction coverage. Global employment law guides from the major firms. Tax treaty databases. Local counsel networks. Contract lifecycle and template management. Generative legal research tooling, arriving quickly.

## The Customization Gap

**Awareness is not a rule base.** An alert that a country changed its contractor test is the beginning. Turning it into a versioned, structured, applicable rule — what the test now is, which facts it turns on, effective from when — is the work, and it is where the specialist's time goes.

**The determination needs facts in a schema the rules can consume.** Legal research assumes a lawyer who knows the facts. Here the facts arrive as a client's questionnaire answers and must be structured consistently enough that the same engagement produces the same determination twice. That schema is domain work no research product supplies.

**Coverage is dozens of jurisdictions at once and depth is uneven.** Research products are strong in major markets and thin in the smaller ones, which are frequently where these platforms' risk concentrates. Knowing where your own coverage is weak — and saying so in the determination's confidence — is the honest handling and is not something a vendor provides.

**Currency has to be tracked per rule, not per subscription.** A monitoring service tells you something changed somewhere. The determination needs to know whether this specific rule, relied on in these specific determinations, is current. That mapping is the platform's to maintain.

**The validation evidence is the platform's own dispute history and no vendor has it.** Which determinations held up when an authority looked. That corpus exists only inside the platforms and is the only empirical check on any of this.

## Target Customer

Platform compliance leadership, who license regulatory intelligence and still maintain the rule base by hand. Also the legal research and regulatory intelligence vendors, for whom structured, applicable rule bases for employment classification across jurisdictions are a natural product extension with a clear buyer.

## Impact If Solved

The change monitoring, primary sources and research tooling get used as inputs, and the versioned rule base, fact schema, coverage honesty, per-rule currency tracking and dispute-outcome validation get built. Concretely: a determination that can cite its rule and say when that rule was last checked.

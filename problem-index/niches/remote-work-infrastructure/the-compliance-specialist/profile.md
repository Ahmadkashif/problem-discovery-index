# The Compliance Specialist

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/remote-work-infrastructure|Remote Work Infrastructure]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Whether the person holding a dozen jurisdictions in their head has any tooling at all.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$560M — 7% of US remote work infrastructure spend
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~7%
**Digital Adoption:** Low — a wiki, an inbox and a great deal of reading
**Target Buyer:** Platform compliance leadership
**Automation Potential:** High — most of the role is retrieval, drafting and tracking

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche

One team maintains the employment, tax and benefit rules for every country the platform operates in, by reading, and a green tick in the product depends on them having read the right thing in time.

The specialist's week is reading statutes and guidance, answering determination queries, writing knowledge base pages, briefing local counsel, reviewing contracts, and being the escalation point for anything unusual. They hold a set of jurisdictions personally, and the depth of the platform's coverage in any country is substantially the depth of one person's knowledge of it.

The niche is distinct because it is the industry's key-person risk concentrated in a role with no instruments. The specialist has no way to know whether their guidance is current, no way to see whether their determinations are consistent with a colleague's, no way to track what changed while they were on leave, and no way to hand over a jurisdiction except by conversation.

## Current Tools & Gaps

An internal wiki. A shared inbox or ticket queue for determination requests. Subscriptions to regulatory change services of varying coverage. Local counsel relationships. Country guides from law firms. Personal notes.

The gaps are everything that would make the role sustainable. No structured rule base, so knowledge lives in prose and in memory. No change monitoring routed to the person who owns the jurisdiction. No drafting assistance for the determinations and guidance that consume the week. No consistency measurement. And no handover artefact, so a departure takes a jurisdiction with it.

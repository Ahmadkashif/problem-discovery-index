**Contested on:** Whether research-oriented legal assistants suit repeated application rather than open enquiry.

## The Problem

Generative legal research tooling is arriving quickly and getting better. Assistants that search primary sources, summarise positions across jurisdictions, draft memoranda and answer legal questions with citations are available from the major legal research vendors and from a growing set of entrants.

They are built for research: an open question, a lawyer, a memorandum. The compliance specialist's work is different in shape — a known rule applied repeatedly to structured facts, at volume, with consistency mattering more than depth, and the output being a determination record rather than a memo.

## What Already Exists

Generative legal research assistants from the major research vendors. Document drafting tools. Multi-jurisdiction country guides. Regulatory change monitoring with summarisation. Contract review assistants. Legal knowledge management with generative retrieval.

## The Customization Gap

**Application, not research.** The specialist is not asking what the law is; they know. They are applying it to a specific engagement and recording the result. The tooling should take structured facts plus a rule version and produce a reasoned determination, which is a different interaction from a research query.

**Consistency across instances is the requirement.** A research assistant answering the same question twice may reason differently both times, which is acceptable for research and unacceptable when two engagements with identical facts must receive the same determination. Deterministic rule application with generative reasoning over it, rather than generative reasoning from scratch, is the structure that achieves it.

**Grounding must be the platform's own versioned rules.** A general assistant grounded in public sources will produce answers inconsistent with the platform's own guidance, which is what the determinations must be based on. Retrieval must be over the internal rule base with its versions and dates, not over the web.

**Coverage in small jurisdictions is thin and confidence must reflect it.** These assistants are strongest where legal publishing is strongest. The platform's risk concentrates in smaller markets where the sources are thin, and an assistant that answers with equal confidence everywhere is dangerous precisely where it matters most.

**The output is a record, not a memo.** Structured facts, rule version, reasoning, confidence, specialist acceptance — designed for retention and later examination rather than for reading once.

## Target Customer

Platform compliance leadership evaluating generative legal tooling for a determination queue. Also the legal research vendors, for whom high-volume applied determination is a distinct use case from research and one with a clearly identifiable buyer.

## Impact If Solved

The retrieval, summarisation, citation and drafting capability gets used, and the applied-determination interaction, cross-instance consistency, internal rule grounding, coverage-aware confidence and record output get built. Concretely: an assistant that drafts the fortieth determination this week consistently with the thirty-nine before it.

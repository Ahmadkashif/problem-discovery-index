# Workforce Monitoring

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/remote-work-infrastructure|Remote Work Infrastructure]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Whether measuring presence and motion tells anyone anything about work.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$720M — 9% of US remote work infrastructure spend
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~9%
**Digital Adoption:** High — deployment has outrun the evidence
**Target Buyer:** Employers; monitoring vendors; the workers subject to it
**Automation Potential:** Very high — the capture is trivial and the interpretation is not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche

Keystroke counts, periodic screenshots and idle detection measure whether someone is moving, which is not the same as whether they are working, and the deployment has grown faster than the evidence for it.

Activity tracking became widespread with remote work and is now a substantial tier of this industry. The evidence that it improves performance is thin; the evidence that it damages trust and correlates with attrition is better established. Meanwhile the measures themselves are trivially defeated by anyone who wishes to — a mouse jiggler costs a few dollars — so the tool most reliably measures compliance with being measured.

The niche is distinct because it sits inside an industry otherwise about legal compliance, and because it raises a different kind of problem: not whether something is lawful but whether it works, combined with jurisdictional limits on monitoring that are considerably stricter than the platforms' US-shaped defaults assume.

## Current Tools & Gaps

Activity trackers with keystroke and mouse counts, application and URL logging, periodic screenshots, idle detection and productivity scores derived from application categorisation. Time tracking with automated timesheets. Some products offer aggregated and anonymised modes.

The gaps are validation, legality and the alternative. No vendor publishes evidence that their score predicts output. Deployment defaults assume permissive US practice and are unlawful in several jurisdictions without consent, notification, works council consultation or a documented necessity assessment. And almost nobody offers the thing employers actually want, which is a measure of output rather than of motion.

# Build: An Employment Record and an Advocate

**Niche:** [[niches/remote-work-infrastructure/the-eor-worker/profile|The EOR-Employed Worker]]
**Industry:** [[industries/remote-work-infrastructure|Remote Work Infrastructure]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Give the worker a complete, explained picture of their own employment position and a route to someone inside the structure who is on their side.
**Tags:** #compliance #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #large-language-models #workflow-orchestration #worker-facing #data-integration
**Contested on:** Whether a platform paid by the client will fund a function that represents the worker.

## The Problem

A worker in this arrangement has an employment relationship they cannot see the shape of. They do not know their notice period, their statutory severance, whether pension contributions are being made and where, what happens if the client cancels the contract, whether their leave entitlement meets the local minimum, or who to ask.

The platform holds every one of those answers. It must — it is the legal employer and the obligations are its own. It shows the worker a payslip and a leave balance.

The second gap is representational. When the client instructs something that conflicts with the worker's legal position — a termination date that is too soon, a leave refusal, an entitlement the policy does not cover — there is nobody in the structure whose job is the worker's interest. The platform's compliance function acts in the platform's interest, which overlaps with the worker's and is not the same thing.

## Why Nobody Has Built This

The client pays. A worker-facing function costs money and produces no revenue, and a worker advocate inside the platform will sometimes tell the client no, which is commercially uncomfortable.

The worker also has no exit. They cannot change platforms; they can only leave the job. So the platform experiences no market pressure from them at all, which is why the experience has never been designed.

And the platform's own legal position is slightly awkward: it is the employer, so an advocate for the employee inside the employer is a structure that requires thought rather than a feature.

## What to Build

A complete explained record and a defined route.

**Show the full employment position, generated per worker.** Legal employer and what that means. Contract terms with the statutory floor alongside. Notice period at current tenure and how it grows. Severance entitlement if terminated. Pension or social contributions being made, to which scheme, and how to check. Sick pay, parental leave and eligibility. Public holidays. What happens if the client ends the arrangement. Each item with its legal basis and in the worker's language.

**Explain the structure once, properly, at onboarding.** Who employs you, who directs your work, who is responsible for what, and who to ask about which. Fifteen minutes of explanation at the start prevents most of the confusion that follows and essentially nobody provides it.

**Provide a worker route that is not the client's support queue.** A contact for employment questions, staffed by people who know the worker's jurisdiction, with a stated response time. This is the single most valuable thing to build and it is an operating cost.

**Define the advocacy function deliberately.** Where a client instruction conflicts with the worker's statutory position, someone whose role is to establish and assert the worker's entitlement. The platform is the employer and is legally obliged to do this; naming the function and resourcing it is what makes the obligation real rather than notional.

**Warn on the fragility.** A worker should know, at onboarding and not at termination, that the arrangement depends on a client contract, what notice applies if it ends, and what their entitlements would be. This is uncomfortable and it is the most important thing they are not told.

**Give them a portable record.** Employment history, tenure, role, jurisdiction and contributions made — theirs, exportable, for their own future employment and for their own pension and tax position.

## Target Customer

Platforms competing on worker experience, where the argument is retention and quality of the workforce clients are hiring, and where the worker population's public account of the arrangement is increasingly visible. Also worker advocacy bodies and regulators in the jurisdictions where this employment model is growing fastest.

## Impact If Built

The worker can see the shape of their own employment, with the legal basis, in their own language — from information the platform is already obliged to hold. They have someone to ask who knows their jurisdiction. And the fragility of the arrangement is explained at the start rather than discovered at the end.

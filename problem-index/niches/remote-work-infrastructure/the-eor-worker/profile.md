# The EOR-Employed Worker

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/remote-work-infrastructure|Remote Work Infrastructure]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Whether the person with two employers has anyone to ask about their own employment.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.04B — 13% of US remote work infrastructure spend
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~13%
**Digital Adoption:** Very low — a payslip portal and a support address
**Target Buyer:** Workers; platforms competing on experience; worker advocacy bodies
**Automation Potential:** High — the information they need is all held

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche

Legally employed by a local entity, actually working for a company abroad, and unsure who to ask about notice periods, benefits or what happens if the contract ends.

The worker's situation is genuinely confusing. Their legal employer is an entity in their own country they have never interacted with beyond a contract. Their actual work, management and colleagues are at a company abroad that is not their employer. Their payslip comes from one, their performance review from the other, and their questions about notice, pension, sick pay or termination fall between them.

The niche is distinct because the arrangement was designed around the client's needs and the worker's experience of it was never designed at all. It is also growing quickly, and the population is dispersed, individually powerless and mostly unaware of what they are entitled to.

## Current Tools & Gaps

A worker portal with payslips, leave balance and documents. A support address, usually shared with client-side queries. The employment contract with the local entity. Benefit enrolment through whatever local arrangement exists.

The gaps are explanation, advocacy and continuity. Nothing explains the arrangement — who employs them, what that means, who is responsible for what. There is no advocate: when the client's instruction and the worker's legal position conflict, the worker has nobody on their side inside the structure. And when the client ends the arrangement, the worker's employment ends with it regardless of their own performance, which is a fragility nobody explained at the start.

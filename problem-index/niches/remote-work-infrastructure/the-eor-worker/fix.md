# Fix: Nobody Explained Who Their Employer Is

**Niche:** [[niches/remote-work-infrastructure/the-eor-worker/profile|The EOR-Employed Worker]]
**Industry:** [[industries/remote-work-infrastructure|Remote Work Infrastructure]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The worker signs a contract with an entity they have never heard of, works for a company that is not their employer, and nobody spends fifteen minutes explaining the arrangement.
**Tags:** #compliance #workflow-orchestration #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #large-language-models #worker-facing #quick-win #confidence-intervals
**Contested on:** Whether fifteen minutes will be spent explaining the structure to the person inside it.

## The Problem

A person accepts a job with a company abroad. What they sign is an employment contract with a local entity whose name they have not seen before. Their manager, their team and their work are at the company they applied to, which is not their employer.

Nobody explains this. The contract is signed, the onboarding covers systems and the team, and the worker forms a mental model that is approximately wrong: they believe they work for the company abroad, or they believe the local entity is a payroll agent, or they do not think about it until something happens.

Then something happens. They are ill and do not know whose sick pay rules apply. They want to know their notice period and ask their manager, who does not know. The client restructures and the arrangement ends, and the worker discovers their employment was contingent on a contract between two companies.

## Why It's Still Broken

Onboarding is designed by the client, who covers their own systems and culture, and by the platform, which covers document collection and payroll setup. The explanation of the structure belongs to neither and is therefore in neither.

The explanation is also slightly awkward. Telling a new employee that their employment depends on a commercial contract between two companies and could end if that contract does is not an encouraging first-day message, so it is omitted.

And the worker does not ask, because they do not know there is anything to ask about.

## What a Fix Looks Like

Explain it, once, properly, at the start.

Fifteen minutes at onboarding — a document, a short video, or both, in the worker's language. Who your legal employer is and why. Who directs your work. What each is responsible for. Where your employment terms come from — the contract, the client's policies, and your own country's law — and which wins where they conflict. Who to ask about what, with actual contact routes.

Say what happens if the client contract ends. Plainly, at the start. It is the most important structural fact about the arrangement and the one the worker will otherwise discover at the worst moment. Stated alongside the notice and severance that would apply, it is a manageable fact rather than a shock.

Give them a one-page summary of their own position to keep. Employer, notice period at current tenure, statutory entitlements, pension arrangement, who to contact. Generated per worker and worth more than the contract they signed, which they will not re-read.

Prompt the questions they do not know to ask. A short list — what is my notice period, what is my severance entitlement, where are my pension contributions going, what happens if I am ill for a month — with the answers filled in. Most workers in this arrangement have never asked any of them.

Repeat at the anniversary. Entitlements change with tenure and a worker's notice period and severance at year three are different from year one. A brief annual statement of their current position is cheap and is the kind of thing that builds the trust this arrangement badly needs.

## Who Feels the Pain

Workers, operating for years with an incorrect understanding of who employs them and what they are entitled to, and discovering the structure at the moment it matters most. Their managers at the client, asked employment questions they cannot answer. Platform support, receiving confused queries that a fifteen-minute explanation would have prevented. And the platform, whose worker relationships start from a misunderstanding.

## Impact If Fixed

The worker understands the arrangement they are in from the first week rather than from the crisis. The structural fragility is a known fact rather than a discovery. And a one-page summary of their own position, generated per worker and refreshed annually, gives them something they can actually use — which is more than the contract does.

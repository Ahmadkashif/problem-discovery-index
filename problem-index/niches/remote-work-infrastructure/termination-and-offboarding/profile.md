# Termination & Offboarding

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/remote-work-infrastructure|Remote Work Infrastructure]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Whether an ending that is routine in the client's country is lawful in the worker's.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$720M — 9% of US remote work infrastructure spend
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~9%
**Digital Adoption:** Very low — the most legally exposed moment, handled by email
**Target Buyer:** Clients' legal functions; platform legal and operations; workers
**Automation Potential:** Moderate — the process automates; the judgement does not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche

Termination is where employment law is least forgiving and where the gap between the client's expectations and the worker's jurisdiction is widest. A US client accustomed to at-will employment instructs an ending; the worker is in a country with statutory notice, mandatory severance, required consultation, protected categories, a specified process and a tribunal that will examine whether it was followed.

The employer of record is the legal employer and carries the obligation. The client wants the person gone by the end of the month. The worker frequently does not know their own protections. And the process that would make the termination lawful — notice, documented reasons, consultation where required, correct final pay, statutory severance — is executed under time pressure by people working from a template.

The niche is distinct because it is where the industry's exposure concentrates, where errors are least reversible, and where the worker's interest and the client's are most directly opposed.

## Current Tools & Gaps

Country-specific termination guidance in the internal knowledge base. Notice period calculation from the payroll rules. Final pay calculation including accrued leave. Local counsel engagement for contested cases. Templates for termination letters per jurisdiction.

The gaps are process enforcement, timeline management and the worker's position. Statutory processes with required steps and waiting periods are guided rather than enforced, so a step gets skipped under client pressure. Termination timelines are driven by the client's date rather than by the jurisdiction's requirements. And the worker is told they are being terminated without being told what their own law entitles them to.

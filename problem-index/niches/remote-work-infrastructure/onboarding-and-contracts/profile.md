# Onboarding, Contracts & Documentation

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/remote-work-infrastructure|Remote Work Infrastructure]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Whether the contract a worker signs reflects the determination that was made and the jurisdiction they are in.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$400M — 5% of US remote work infrastructure spend
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~5%
**Digital Adoption:** High — this is the part that works
**Target Buyer:** Platform operations; clients; workers
**Automation Potential:** Very high, and largely realised

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche

Onboarding is the industry's strongest capability. A client can engage a worker in a new country in days — contract generated, identity and right-to-work verified, tax registrations handled, payroll set up, benefits enrolled, equipment arranged. It is genuinely impressive and it is what the platforms are bought for on the demo.

The niche is contested on whether the documentation produced actually reflects the determination and the jurisdiction. Contract templates are maintained per country and are the artefact a tribunal or authority examines first. A template that lags a legislative change, or that describes a contracting arrangement while the determination concluded employment, or that contains a clause unenforceable in that jurisdiction, is a defect embedded in every engagement created from it.

## Current Tools & Gaps

Contract template libraries per jurisdiction with variable clauses. Document generation and e-signature. Identity and right-to-work verification. Tax registration handling. Benefit enrolment. Equipment provisioning partners. Onboarding workflow with task tracking.

The gaps are template currency and the link to the determination. Templates are maintained by the same reading process as the rules and go stale the same way, with no version tracking of which engagement used which template. And the contract is generated from a country template rather than from the determination's conclusions, so a determination's reasoning and the contract's terms can diverge silently.

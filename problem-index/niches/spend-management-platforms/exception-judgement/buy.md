# Decision Automation From Claims and Credit

**Industry:** [[industries/spend-management-platforms|Spend Management Platforms]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Insurance claims and credit operations automated routine human decisions with straight-through processing and referral tiers, and spend exceptions are all referred.

**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn thousands of recorded approve-or-deny judgements a month into automated judgement that a controller trusts — and whoever does it takes the approval queue out of the product entirely.

## The Problem
Insurance claims handling and credit operations both faced queues of routine decisions made by humans and both built straight-through processing: rules and models that settle the clear cases automatically, referral tiers for the uncertain, quality assurance sampling on the automated decisions, and governance that satisfies auditors and regulators. The practice is mature. Spend exceptions have the same shape, better labels, and lower stakes per decision, and none of it has been applied.

## What Already Exists
Straight-through processing architectures; referral tiering and escalation design; quality assurance sampling on automated decisions; decision model governance; and the audit frameworks that make automation acceptable.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a decision owned by the customer rather than by the operator. It requires: (1) the automation threshold set by each customer rather than by the platform, which is the substantive difference and turns a model into a configurable control; (2) policy meaning that varies per company, so a single model cannot be validated once and deployed everywhere; (3) far lower stakes per decision but far higher volume, which changes where automation pays; (4) labels generated by the customer's own staff rather than by trained adjusters, so consistency is lower and must be measured; and (5) an audit expectation about control effectiveness rather than a regulatory model risk regime, which is lighter but still binding.

## Target Customer
Product and risk leadership, customer controllers and auditors, and decision automation vendors for whom spend is unentered.

## Impact If Solved
Straight-through processing with referral tiers is a solved architecture. The spend version has better labels and lower stakes, and the only real adaptation is that each customer sets its own threshold.

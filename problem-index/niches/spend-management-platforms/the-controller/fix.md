# Everything Lands in the Last Week

**Niche:** [[niches/spend-management-platforms/the-controller/profile|The Controller]]
**Industry:** [[industries/spend-management-platforms|Spend Management Platforms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Three weeks of approvals and receipt chasing, then the entire close compressed into five days.
**Tags:** #worker-facing #quick-win #workflow-orchestration #automation #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #confidence-intervals #compliance
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to give the controller back the three weeks a month they spend approving what they were always going to approve — and whoever does it sells to the person who actually chooses the platform.

## The Problem
The pattern repeats every month. Early weeks go to approvals and receipt chasing. Nothing forces resolution until the close approaches, so the unresolved items accumulate. In the final week the controller is simultaneously chasing the last receipts, resolving the last exceptions, coding the backlog, reconciling and producing reports. The workload was always going to be the same total; it arrives all at once because nothing in the process levels it.

## Why It's Still Broken
The deadline is the only forcing function, so everything gravitates to it — a process with one due date will always be back-loaded. The platform does not show what is outstanding against the close. Employees respond to escalation rather than to reminders, and escalation happens late. And nobody has treated workload distribution as a design problem.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Move the deadlines earlier and level the load. Set intermediate cut-offs during the month rather than one at the end, which is the fix and is the standard remedy for a back-loaded process. Show a running close-readiness view so the remaining work is visible from day five rather than day twenty-five. Escalate receipts and exceptions on a rolling basis, since escalation is what actually produces action and it currently all fires at once. Resolve what can be resolved immediately rather than queuing it, because much of the backlog is items that were never going to be contested. Report the workload profile across the month, which will show the shape clearly and is not currently visible to anyone. Set a weekly review rhythm, as a small regular pass is far cheaper than one large one. Flag the items that will definitely block the close early, since those are known well in advance. Give employees a deadline that is before the controller's, because the current arrangement makes the controller the buffer. Track days to close and the pattern of when work arrives, which is the measurement the fix needs. And ask controllers what lands last, since they know exactly and have never been asked.

## Who Feels the Pain
Controllers working a compressed week every month; accounting teams whose quality suffers under time pressure; CFOs whose reporting is late; and employees escalated to all at once.

## Impact If Fixed
A process with one due date is always back-loaded, and the close deadline is the only forcing function in the month. Intermediate cut-offs with rolling escalation level a workload whose total was never the problem.

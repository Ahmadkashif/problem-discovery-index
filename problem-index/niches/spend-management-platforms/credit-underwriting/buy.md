# Commercial Credit Risk Management

**Niche:** [[niches/spend-management-platforms/credit-underwriting/profile|Credit Underwriting]]
**Industry:** [[industries/spend-management-platforms|Spend Management Platforms]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Commercial banking built a full credit risk discipline around financial statements and covenants, and these platforms lend to companies that have neither.
**Tags:** #compliance #gradient-boosting #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #survival-analysis #time-series-forecasting #revenue-impact #hypothesis-testing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to set a corporate card limit for a company with twelve months of history and no credit file — and whoever joins the limit decision to what happened afterwards underwrites a book everyone else is guessing at.

## The Problem
Commercial credit risk management is thorough: financial statement analysis, covenant setting and monitoring, risk rating with migration tracking, portfolio concentration limits, loss forecasting and stress testing, and independent review. Every element assumes audited statements, a lending relationship with periodic review, and a borrower with history. These platforms lend to companies with twelve months of operation, no statements and no credit file, and they underwrite in minutes.

## What Already Exists
Risk rating frameworks with migration analysis; covenant design and monitoring; portfolio loss forecasting and stress testing; concentration limit frameworks; and independent credit review structures.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a borrower with no statements and a lender with continuous behavioural data. It requires: (1) risk assessment from transaction behaviour and bank data rather than from statements, which is the substantive adaptation and produces a richer and more frequent signal than the original ever had; (2) continuous rather than annual review, since the data updates daily and a covenant-style monitoring cadence is obsolete here; (3) business failure rather than default as the dominant loss mode, which changes the modelling target entirely; (4) limits adjusted dynamically rather than renegotiated, which is operationally possible and rarely done; and (5) a young portfolio with no downturn history, so stress testing must borrow external evidence.

## Target Customer
Credit and risk leadership, capital providers and securitisation buyers, and commercial credit platform vendors entering the segment.

## Impact If Solved
The discipline assumes statements, covenants and annual review. Rebuilding risk rating and monitoring around continuous transaction behaviour is the adaptation, and the resulting signal is better than what the original discipline was designed for.

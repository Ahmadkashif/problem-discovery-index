# Never Finding Out

**Niche:** [[niches/spend-management-platforms/the-credit-analyst/profile|The Credit Analyst]]
**Industry:** [[industries/spend-management-platforms|Spend Management Platforms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The analyst approved four hundred limits last year and could not tell you how any of them performed.
**Tags:** #worker-facing #quick-win #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #automation #confidence-intervals #workflow-orchestration #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to tell the analyst what happened to the last five hundred companies that looked like this one — and whoever closes that loop turns a judgement made in the dark into one made on evidence.

## The Problem
An analyst makes a judgement, the file closes, and the outcome — repayment, delinquency, shutdown — is recorded in a different system attached to a different team. The analyst never sees it. They develop a sense of the market from anecdotes that reach them informally, which is the worst possible sample because the cases that get talked about are the spectacular ones. Years into the role, their calibration is based on stories.

## Why It's Still Broken
Outcomes belong to collections, so the reporting was built for recovery operations rather than for the decision makers — the data exists and was simply never routed back. No one owns analyst development. Throughput is the measured metric. And nobody asked the analysts what they were missing.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Send the outcomes back. Report each analyst's own decisions with their outcomes on a regular cycle, which is the fix and is a join between two systems the company already runs. Show base rates rather than individual cases, since anecdotes are what they have and distributions are what they need. Flag the decisions that went materially wrong in both directions, because a limit that was far too low is a lost customer and is never counted as an error. Hold a regular review of outcomes as a team, as shared calibration is how a desk improves and there is currently no forum. Compare analysts to each other on similar files, which will reveal systematic differences nobody knows about. Show the portfolio's overall performance, since the analysts do not know it. Attach the outcome to the original file, so a decision can be reviewed with its reasoning intact. Report the time between decision and outcome, because knowing the feedback delay is itself informative about what can be learned. Include the customers who churned or never used the limit, as those are outcomes too and are entirely absent from a collections-centred view. And ask the analysts what would help, since the role has never been consulted about its own information.

## Who Feels the Pain
Analysts calibrating on anecdotes; credit leadership with no view of decision quality; the portfolio, whose errors repeat; and companies given the wrong limit in both directions.

## Impact If Fixed
The outcome reporting was built for recovery operations, so the data exists and was never routed back to the decision makers. Returning each analyst's own outcomes on a cycle is a join between two existing systems and is the first feedback the role has ever had.

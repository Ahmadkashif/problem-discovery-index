## The Problem
Auditing has a developed concept of evidence: sufficiency, appropriateness, reliability by source, and the principle that evidence from an independent external source is stronger than evidence provided by the entity. By that standard, a card network authorisation record is a stronger form of evidence than a photograph of a receipt supplied by the employee being reimbursed. Expense policy has never been designed against these standards and simply requires the photograph.

## What Already Exists
Audit evidence standards and hierarchies of reliability; substantive analytical procedures; sampling methodologies; tax substantiation requirements by expense type; and continuous auditing approaches using full population data.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a full population of machine-generated evidence. It requires: (1) transaction data recognised as independent external evidence, which is the substantive argument and is stronger than the artefact currently required — making it explicitly is the contribution; (2) full population testing rather than sampling, since the data supports it and auditors increasingly want it; (3) tax substantiation rules modelled per category, as they are specific and are currently approximated by a single threshold; (4) evidence requirements that differ by auditor and jurisdiction, so the policy must be configurable rather than global; and (5) the platform positioned to make the argument to auditors on the customer's behalf, which no individual company can do.

## Target Customer
Product and accounting leadership, customer audit committees, external auditors, and GRC vendors whose evidence models predate machine-generated populations.

## Impact If Solved
Audit's own evidence hierarchy rates an independent external record above an entity-supplied photograph. Making that argument with the transaction data would reframe the whole requirement, and only the platform is positioned to make it.

# How Businesses Actually Spend

**Industry:** [[industries/spend-management-platforms|Spend Management Platforms]]

**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn line-item purchasing across thousands of companies joined to their bank balances into answers nobody else can produce — and whoever organises it holds a real-time read on how businesses spend and how that relates to their survival.

## The Problem
Two facts sit next to each other in the platform's database and have never been introduced. The first is what thousands of companies buy, from whom, in what quantity, month by month. The second is each company's bank balance, revenue and payment behaviour. Together they answer questions nobody can currently answer: what a spending pattern predicts about survival, how software and services adoption is actually moving, what companies at each stage spend on what, and which vendors are gaining and losing.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The data was collected to code transactions and render dashboards, so its analytical value was never the point of holding it — nobody was asked what else it could answer. Using customer data across customers raises governance questions that require a deliberate answer. The commercial model is interchange, where data products are a distraction. And the credit and product functions that would use it sit apart from the data that would feed it.

## What to Build
Model the portfolio and answer the two questions the category avoids. Link spending patterns to survival, which is the core and is the question with the most value to the credit book, to customers and to the ecosystem — and it is answerable today. Build spend benchmarks by company size, stage and sector, since every customer asks for them and only the platform can produce them. Characterise vendor-level spend across the portfolio, because software and services vendors would pay for it and no other source has line-item business purchasing at this granularity. Detect category-level shifts early, as changes in aggregate business spending are visible here weeks before they appear in official statistics. Feed distress signals to the credit function, which is the highest-value internal use and closes the underwriting loop. Establish governance and consent explicitly, because the asset's legitimacy is the binding constraint and building on an unexamined basis is how this goes wrong. Aggregate and anonymise so no customer's specifics are exposed, which is what makes the whole thing defensible. Deliver benchmarks back to customers first, since that is the most acceptable form and builds the case for everything else. Validate against external data where it exists, so the picture is anchored. And treat the macroeconomic content seriously, because a credible real-time read on business spending is a genuinely valuable position.

## Target Customer
Data and commercial leadership, credit teams needing distress signals, customers asking for benchmarks, software vendors with no view of their own market, and economic researchers.

## Impact If Built
The data was collected to code transactions and nobody asked what else it could answer. Line-item purchasing joined to bank balances across thousands of companies answers what spending predicts about survival — the question the credit book most needs.

**Automation Potential:** Medium — screening, measurement and reporting automate; the diagnosis stays with the pathologist

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Every GLP toxicology study ends at the microscope. A board-certified veterinary pathologist examines dozens of tissues from every animal, records findings in standard nomenclature with severity grades, and decides which are related to the test article. That decision sets the no-observed-adverse-effect level and therefore the clinical starting dose. Pathologists are scarce (~1,000 active in industry worldwide, estimate), increasingly work remotely, and are booked by both large CROs and specialist pathology groups. A second pathologist then peer-reviews the read. The niche is distinct from the wider study because it is a separate labour pool, workflow and bottleneck.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Glass microscopes; pathology modules of Provantis and Pristima for recording findings; whole-slide scanners (Leica Aperio and others) and image analysis (Visiopharm, Indica Labs HALO, Aiforia). The 2026 Toxicologic Pathology Forum noted that no formal standards exist yet for judging AI slide analysis as an aid to postmortem evaluation. Gaps: no pre-screening in routine use, peer review reconciled by spreadsheet, and severity grading that varies by pathologist.

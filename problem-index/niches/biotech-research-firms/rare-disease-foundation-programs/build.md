# A Rights-of-Reference Exchange Brokered by the Foundation

**Niche:** [[niches/biotech-research-firms/rare-disease-foundation-programs/profile|Rare-Disease & Foundation-Funded Programmes]]
**Industry:** [[industries/biotech-research-firms|Biotech Research Firms]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Two programmes using the same antisense chemistry or viral capsid each pay for similar toxicology, because the first sponsor's studies sit in its own files and nobody brokers the right for the second to cite them.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #k-nearest-neighbors #feature-engineering #data-integration #compliance #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to make the evidence a patient foundation owns or can broker — natural-history data, characterised disease models, and earlier sponsors' toxicology under rights of reference — citable by the next small sponsor's IND, and whoever makes one programme's evidence reusable by the next takes the account.

## The Problem
A foundation funds an academic antisense programme that completes GLP toxicology on a backbone chemistry. Three years later a parent-founded company targets the same disease with the same chemistry and a different sequence. Much of the class toxicology is already known, but it belongs to the first sponsor, which may have been acquired, closed or moved on. The second sponsor cannot cite studies it cannot reference, so it repeats them on a budget that may not survive the repetition.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Toxicology data belongs to whoever paid for it, and small sponsors lack the leverage to negotiate access. Nobody's business is organising evidence for programmes too small to buy much. Foundations hold the relationships and the mission to do it, but not the tools or the legal templates.

## What to Build
An exchange run by or for foundations. It keeps an inventory of every study the foundation funded or knows of, by modality, species, duration and finding, with who holds the rights. Standard licence and letter-of-authorisation terms are written into funding agreements from the start, so rights pass to the foundation if a sponsor stops. A matching tool shows a new programme which existing studies cover its chemistry or vector and which it must still run. Public precedent on what FDA accepted is linked from the regulatory consultancies' work, not rebuilt.

## Target Customer
Patient foundations and rare-disease consortia that fund several programmes in one disease or modality; academic translational centres.

## Impact If Built
Each new programme starts from the evidence the foundation already paid for, which can remove a repeat toxicology study costing a large share of a small sponsor's budget (estimate).

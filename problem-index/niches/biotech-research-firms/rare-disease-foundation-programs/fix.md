# Disease Models Locked in Academic Labs

**Niche:** [[niches/biotech-research-firms/rare-disease-foundation-programs/profile|Rare-Disease & Foundation-Funded Programmes]]
**Industry:** [[industries/biotech-research-firms|Biotech Research Firms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The animal model that best represents a rare disease often lives in one academic lab, characterised in papers but not documented well enough for the next sponsor to rely on.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #data-integration #compliance #quick-win #workflow-orchestration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to make the evidence a patient foundation owns or can broker — natural-history data, characterised disease models, and earlier sponsors' toxicology under rights of reference — citable by the next small sponsor's IND, and whoever makes one programme's evidence reusable by the next takes the account.

## The Problem
A rare-disease package can lean on a disease model to show both efficacy and safety in the relevant biology, but only if the model's genetics, phenotype, variability and husbandry are documented. Academic labs record this in theses, lab notebooks and methods sections. Each new sponsor that wants the model re-characterises it at a CRO.

## Why It's Still Broken
Academic incentives reward publication, not regulatory documentation. Foundations fund experiments rather than characterisation dossiers, and rarely own the result.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A model characterisation dossier per disease model, assembled from publications and the lab's records with gaps listed, owned by the foundation under its funding terms, and transferable to whichever sponsor and CRO use the model next.

## Who Feels the Pain
Foundation research directors; academic investigators asked for data they never organised; sponsors paying to re-characterise a model someone already funded.

## Impact If Fixed
A documented disease model becomes a foundation asset that every later programme can cite, which is how one programme's evidence carries over to the next.

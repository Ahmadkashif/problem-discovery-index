# Rare-Disease & Foundation-Funded Programmes

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/biotech-research-firms|Biotech Research Firms]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to make the evidence a patient foundation owns or can broker — natural-history data, characterised disease models, and earlier sponsors' toxicology under rights of reference — citable by the next small sponsor's IND, and whoever makes one programme's evidence reusable by the next takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$2B a year of preclinical and IND-enabling spend on rare-disease programmes run by small biotechs, patient foundations and academic spinouts (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~2% of NAICS 541714 receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — academic data, foundation spreadsheets, consultants' documents
**Target Buyer:** Foundation Chief Scientific Officer or research director; founder of a rare-disease biotech
**Automation Potential:** Medium — evidence inventories, licensing records and dossiers are document work; studies are not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Rare-disease programmes are run by people the industry's tools ignore: patient foundations that fund drug development themselves, parent-founded companies, and academic teams developing antisense or gene therapies for a handful of patients. Their budgets are a fraction of a venture-backed biotech's. What sets them apart is that the foundation, not the sponsor, is the permanent party. It funds the natural-history study, the patient registry and the mouse model, and it sees one sponsor after another try the same disease with the same modality. A venture-backed biotech guards its data. A foundation's mission is served when one programme's evidence shortens the next one's path. Today that reuse rarely happens, because the evidence is not owned, documented or licensed in a form a new sponsor can cite.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Patient registries on general-purpose platforms, academic collaborators' lab records, CRO quotes, and data-sharing platforms built for clinical data. What FDA has accepted for comparable programmes is the territory of [[niches/biotech-research-firms/ind-regulatory-strategy-consultancies/profile|Nonclinical & IND Regulatory Strategy Consultancies]]; this niche is about the foundation's own evidence. Gaps: no inventory of what the foundation funded and who holds the rights, toxicology repeated because an earlier sponsor's studies cannot be referenced, and disease models documented in papers rather than dossiers.

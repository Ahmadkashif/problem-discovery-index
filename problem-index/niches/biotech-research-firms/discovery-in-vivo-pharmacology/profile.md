# Discovery In Vivo Pharmacology

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/biotech-research-firms|Biotech Research Firms]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to make a non-GLP efficacy study trustworthy enough to move a programme — randomised, blinded and completely recorded — and whoever makes the efficacy signal reproduce in the next study takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$3B global spend on non-GLP in vivo efficacy and PK/PD studies, in-house and outsourced (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~3% of NAICS 541714 receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — cage cards, paper observation sheets and caliper readings transcribed into spreadsheets
**Target Buyer:** VP Pharmacology or Head of In Vivo Sciences; discovery services lead at the CRO
**Automation Potential:** Medium — measurement capture, randomisation and monitoring automate; handling and dosing do not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Before a programme earns a GLP package, it must show efficacy in animal models: tumour growth inhibition in xenograft or syngeneic mice, disease scores in inflammation models, behaviour in neuroscience models. These studies sit outside GLP, run in sponsors' own vivaria or at discovery CROs such as Charles River, Crown Bioscience and The Jackson Laboratory, and decide whether a programme advances. Their weakness is reproducibility: unblinded scoring, unrecorded randomisation and small groups produce effects that shrink in the next study.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Paper cage cards and observation sheets, digital calipers and spreadsheets, some study-management software, digital ventilated cages and home-cage monitoring systems (Tecniplast, The Jackson Laboratory's Envision) in a minority of facilities. Gaps: randomisation and blinding rarely documented, measurements transcribed by hand, and continuous behavioural data available only where special caging exists.

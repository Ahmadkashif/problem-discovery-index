# Tumour Volumes Written on Cage Cards

**Niche:** [[niches/biotech-research-firms/discovery-in-vivo-pharmacology/profile|Discovery In Vivo Pharmacology]]
**Industry:** [[industries/biotech-research-firms|Biotech Research Firms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Caliper measurements are read aloud, written on paper and typed into a spreadsheet later, and each transcription step is a chance to move a tumour from one group to another.
**Tags:** #evaluation-metrics #data-integration #quick-win #automation #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to make a non-GLP efficacy study trustworthy enough to move a programme — randomised, blinded and completely recorded — and whoever makes the efficacy signal reproduce in the next study takes the account.

## The Problem
Two or three times a week a technician measures each tumour's length and width, a second person writes the numbers down, and someone enters them into Excel to compute volumes and growth curves. Errors and unblinded re-measurements are invisible in the final graph.

## Why It's Still Broken
Digital calipers and 3D tumour imagers exist but are not wired to the study record in many facilities, and paper is the default when two people work in a hood.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Measurements captured directly from a connected caliper or imager against a scanned cage and animal ID, with coded group labels so the measurer stays blind, automatic volume calculation and outlier flags at the moment of measurement.

## Who Feels the Pain
In vivo technicians doing repetitive transcription; scientists defending results to partners; programmes advanced on noise.

## Impact If Fixed
Cleaner data at the source and a blinding record that holds up in diligence.

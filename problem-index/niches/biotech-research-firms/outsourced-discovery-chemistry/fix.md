# The Dead Route Reported on Friday

**Niche:** [[niches/biotech-research-firms/outsourced-discovery-chemistry/profile|Outsourced Discovery Chemistry & DMTA Services]]
**Industry:** [[industries/biotech-research-firms|Biotech Research Firms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A CRO chemist knows on Tuesday that a route has failed, and the sponsor finds out in Friday's status deck, after the replacement design could already have started.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #workflow-orchestration #data-integration #quick-win #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to shorten the sponsor's design–make–test cycle — designs in on Monday, compounds made, purified and assayed within days — from a location the sponsor's partners and acquirers will accept once the BIOSECURE list is published, and whoever turns cycles fastest from an acceptable jurisdiction takes the account.

## The Problem
Progress is reported in a weekly slide deck and call across time zones. Failed reactions, stalled purifications and late reagents are batched into that report, so the sponsor's decision to drop or replace a design waits days.

## Why It's Still Broken
CRO chemists record reactions in their own ELN, which the sponsor cannot see, and the deck is assembled by a project manager from conversations. Contracts specify weekly reporting because nothing better was available.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Per-compound status pushed daily from the CRO's ELN to a shared view, with failures summarised automatically ("step 3 amide coupling, two attempts, no product") and a prompt to the sponsor chemist to swap or persist.

## Who Feels the Pain
Sponsor project chemists; CRO project managers writing decks; programme leads watching the cycle stretch.

## Impact If Fixed
Several days recovered on every failed design, multiplied across every cycle a programme runs.

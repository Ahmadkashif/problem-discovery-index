# Retrosynthesis Software Fitted to an FTE Chemistry Contract

**Niche:** [[niches/biotech-research-firms/outsourced-discovery-chemistry/profile|Outsourced Discovery Chemistry & DMTA Services]]
**Industry:** [[industries/biotech-research-firms|Biotech Research Firms]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Computer-aided retrosynthesis already proposes routes to target molecules; it does not know what this CRO's chemists can make this week.
**Tags:** #graph-neural-networks #transformers #transfer-learning #feature-engineering #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to shorten the sponsor's design–make–test cycle — designs in on Monday, compounds made, purified and assayed within days — from a location the sponsor's partners and acquirers will accept once the BIOSECURE list is published, and whoever turns cycles fastest from an acceptable jurisdiction takes the account.

## The Problem
A route that looks short in software fails at the bench because a building block takes three weeks to arrive, a reaction class has a poor record at this site, or the FTE team is committed to another project's priority compounds.

## What Already Exists
Synthia (Merck KGaA), CAS SciFinder retrosynthesis, Iktos Spaya and the open-source ASKCOS propose routes from reaction databases and models.

## The Customization Gap
The FTE context needs: the CRO's own reaction success history as a fine-tuning signal; live building-block inventory and supplier lead times in the CRO's country (which change when supply moves out of China); the contract's priority rules and team capacity; and a route score in days to compound, not steps.

## Target Customer
Chemistry CRO site heads and sponsor project chemists planning each batch.

## Impact If Solved
Fewer failed routes per cycle and batches planned to what can actually be made, which is how the cycle shortens.

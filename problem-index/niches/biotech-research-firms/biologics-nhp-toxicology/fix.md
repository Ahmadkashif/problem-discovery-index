# The Species-Relevance Argument Rebuilt for Every Antibody

**Niche:** [[niches/biotech-research-firms/biologics-nhp-toxicology/profile|Biologics & NHP-Dependent Toxicology]]
**Industry:** [[industries/biotech-research-firms|Biotech Research Firms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Every biologics package must justify which species is pharmacologically relevant, and toxicologists rebuild the sequence, binding and expression comparison from scratch each time.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #k-nearest-neighbors #compliance #quick-win #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to deliver a biologics safety package FDA will accept using the fewest non-human primates — purpose-bred animals secured where they are unavoidable, validated non-animal evidence substituted where the 2025 roadmap allows — and whoever can show FDA-accepted primate-sparing designs takes the account.

## The Problem
ICH S6 requires testing in a pharmacologically relevant species. For each antibody the team compares target sequence homology, binding affinity and functional activity across human, monkey, dog and rodent, and tissue expression. The same comparisons are repeated across programmes hitting related targets, in Word documents, with no reuse.

## Why It's Still Broken
The data comes from public databases, literature and the sponsor's own binding assays, and it is assembled by whoever writes the section. There is no standard record format, so nothing accumulates.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A species-relevance record per target: homology pulled from public sequence databases, expression from public atlases, the sponsor's binding and functional data attached, and a templated conclusion. Prior records for related targets are retrieved automatically.

## Who Feels the Pain
Nonclinical toxicologists writing packages; reviewers reading inconsistent justifications; sponsors who default to monkeys because the argument for anything else takes too long.

## Impact If Fixed
A faster, consistent justification, which is the first step in every primate-sparing argument.

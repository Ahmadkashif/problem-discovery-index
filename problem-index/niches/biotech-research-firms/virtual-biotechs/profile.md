# Virtual Biotechs Running on Outsourced Labs

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/biotech-research-firms|Biotech Research Firms]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to let a team of two to fifteen people with no lab of its own run a portfolio of outsourced studies and make go/no-go calls on data it never generated, and whoever turns every CRO's output into one decision record takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.5B a year spent by virtual and semi-virtual biotechs on programme management, data handling and outsourcing overhead around their CRO work (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~2% of NAICS 541714 receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — shared drives, spreadsheets, email and CRO portals
**Target Buyer:** CEO or Chief Scientific Officer of a two-to-fifteen-person company; venture firm platform lead
**Automation Potential:** High — intake, tracking and reporting are document work

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A virtual biotech owns a molecule or a licence, a plan and a few experienced people — often a CSO, a development lead and a part-time CFO — and buys every experiment. Scarce capital pushes more companies into this shape: global biopharma venture funding fell to $26B across 435 rounds in 2025 (DealForma), and venture creators run several such companies at once. The tools of the industry assume a lab and staff. This company has neither, but it carries the same diligence burden when it raises money or partners.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Benchling or a shared drive, Smartsheet for timelines, CRO client portals, outsourced project managers and fractional consultants. Gaps: no single record across CROs, results re-keyed by the CSO, quotes compared by hand, and a data room built from scratch before each financing.

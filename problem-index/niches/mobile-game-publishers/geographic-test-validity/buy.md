**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to know whether a number measured in a soft-launch market will hold when the game scales globally — and whoever can predict the transfer takes the account.

## The Problem
Consumer packaged goods spent decades on exactly this: launch a product in selected markets, measure, and project the national result. The discipline that emerged covers representativeness analysis, matched market selection, projection factors calibrated on past launches, and explicit accounting for why a test market over- or under-reads. It exists because getting it wrong meant a national rollout of a product nobody wanted. Mobile publishing has the same structure, better data and none of the method.

## What Already Exists
Test market representativeness scoring; matched market selection; projection factors calibrated on historical launches; control market design; and read-rate adjustment by category.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to markets that differ in monetisation behaviour rather than in demographics, and to a product with no distribution constraint. It requires: (1) markets selected for monetisation and retention similarity rather than demographic representativeness — a market can match on population and diverge entirely on willingness to spend, which is the substantive difference; (2) genre-specific transfer rather than a category read rate, since the same market reads differently for puzzle and for strategy; (3) acquisition cost as a distinct varying factor, with no packaged goods equivalent; (4) instant global distribution, so the test exists only to inform spend rather than to stage production; and (5) a platform and device mix that shifts the result independently of the market's consumers.

## Target Customer
Mobile publishers, UA and publishing leads, market intelligence vendors, and consumer research firms with test market practices.

## Impact If Solved
Packaged goods built projection factors because a wrong national rollout was ruinous. Selecting markets on monetisation similarity rather than demographic representativeness is the adaptation, and genre-specific read rates are what the borrowed method lacks.

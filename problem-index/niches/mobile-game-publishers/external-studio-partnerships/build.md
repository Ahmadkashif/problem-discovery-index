# The Signing Decision With a Record Behind It

**Niche:** [[niches/mobile-game-publishers/external-studio-partnerships/profile|External Studio Partnerships]]
**Industry:** [[industries/mobile-game-publishers|Mobile Game Publishers]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A publisher signs external studios on judgement about people and has never checked which parts of that judgement were right.
**Tags:** #causal-inference #logistic-regression #confidence-intervals #evaluation-metrics #gradient-boosting #revenue-impact #data-integration #descriptive-statistics
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to decide which external studio and which deal terms will produce a hit, using judgement about people that nobody has ever converted into evidence — and whoever does takes the account.

## The Problem
A partner manager sees hundreds of pitches a year and signs a handful. The decision rests on the concept, the playable, the team's track record and an assessment of whether they can execute. Every element of that is recorded informally or not at all. Two years later the outcome is known and nothing connects it back — so the publisher's most experienced judgement never becomes institutional knowledge, and leaves when the person does.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The pipeline lives in a spreadsheet and a mailbox. Partner managers regard their judgement as craft rather than as a model's input. The outcome arrives years after the decision, past most attention spans. And the sample is small enough that any analysis is easy to dismiss.

## What to Build
Capture the decision, then close the loop on it. Record the signing decision in structured form — the concept, the team, the build state, the terms, the manager's stated reasoning and confidence — which is the core and is worthless in year one and decisive in year five. Join outcomes back to those records automatically, since the reason nobody learns from this is that the join is manual. Analyse which deal structures produced better realised returns, as advance and revenue share are set by negotiation habit rather than by evidence. Track each studio's delivery history across milestones, which partner managers hold in their heads. Score portfolio concentration by genre, platform and studio, because the risk is correlated and nobody views it together. Surface comparable past deals at negotiation time, which is the moment the record pays for itself. Calibrate the partner manager's own confidence against outcomes, which is the most valuable and most sensitive output. Model the milestone schedule against what the studio has actually delivered before rather than against the plan. Keep the pipeline's rejections with reasons, since the rejected pitches are the same censored population as the killed prototypes. And design it so a partner manager fills it in during the deal rather than afterwards.

## Target Customer
Mobile publishers with third-party programmes, publishing groups, platform first-party publishing arms, and games investment firms.

## Impact If Built
The publisher's most experienced judgement never becomes institutional knowledge and leaves when the person does. Recording the decision in structured form and joining outcomes back automatically is what converts craft into a record.

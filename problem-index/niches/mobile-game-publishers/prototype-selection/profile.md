# Prototype Selection

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/mobile-game-publishers|Mobile Game Publishers]]
**Category:** 🔵 High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to decide which of dozens of prototypes a month deserves to continue, on evidence that is three days long and drawn from a population that was itself filtered by the same rule — and whoever gets the selection right takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$5B US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~20% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** High but unexamined
**Target Buyer:** Publishing leadership
**Automation Potential:** High — prediction and experimental design

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The publishing model is a kill funnel: build many cheap prototypes, run small install campaigns, measure early retention against a threshold, and terminate the overwhelming majority within weeks. Everything downstream — which games get made, which teams survive, what the portfolio looks like in two years — is determined here, by a number with enormous uncertainty compared against a threshold that is largely inherited folklore. It is the highest-leverage decision in the business and the least evidenced.

### Contested sub-niches
This niche is not terminal. It names a decision, and beneath it sit two different businesses:
- [[niches/mobile-game-publishers/early-signal-modelling/profile|🎯 Early Signal Modelling]] — what a concept's first days imply about its eventual performance
- [[niches/mobile-game-publishers/survivorship-correction/profile|🎯 Survivorship Correction]] — how to learn when the evidence base is only the games that passed

## Current Tools & Gaps
A test campaign, a cohort dashboard, a day-one retention figure and a threshold in a document. The gaps: thresholds not derived from the publisher's own outcomes; no uncertainty attached to a decision made on a few thousand installs; no genre or mechanic conditioning; no record of what killed concepts would have done; and no accounting for the acquisition conditions the test ran under.

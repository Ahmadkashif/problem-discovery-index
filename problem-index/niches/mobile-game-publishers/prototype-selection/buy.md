# Stage-Gate Portfolio Selection From Pharma and Venture

**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Drug development and venture investing built formal machinery for killing most candidates early under uncertainty, and mobile publishing uses a number in a document.

**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to decide which of dozens of prototypes a month deserves to continue, on evidence that is three days long and drawn from a population that was itself filtered by the same rule — and whoever gets the selection right takes the account.

## The Problem
Pharmaceutical development and venture investing face the same structure: many candidates, most of which must be terminated, early signals that correlate weakly with eventual outcomes, and a portfolio whose value comes from a small number of successes. Both built formal machinery for it — stage-gate criteria derived from historical outcomes, explicit probability of success by stage, expected value under uncertainty, and deliberate attention to what the process rejects. Mobile publishing runs the same funnel at higher volume and faster cadence with none of it.

## What Already Exists
Stage-gate criteria with defined decision rules; probability-of-success estimation by stage and category; portfolio expected value modelling; formal go/no-go review; and post-hoc analysis of terminated candidates.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a funnel that runs weekly rather than over years, where the signal is behavioural rather than clinical. It requires: (1) a three-day signal and a weekly cadence rather than multi-year phases, so the decision must be automated rather than reviewed by committee — this is the substantive difference; (2) behavioural telemetry as the evidence, which is abundant and noisy where clinical data is scarce and structured; (3) genre and mechanic as the conditioning variable, with no equivalent of a therapeutic area's established base rates; (4) an outcome measured in revenue over months rather than in approval; and (5) a kill rate so high that the terminated population is the overwhelming majority of the evidence and cannot be ignored the way pharma can afford to.

## Target Customer
Mobile publishers, publishing arms of larger groups, third-party concept evaluators, and portfolio decision tooling vendors.

## Impact If Solved
Pharma and venture formalised selection under uncertainty because the cost of getting it wrong was visible. A weekly cadence on behavioural telemetry is the adaptation, and the far higher kill rate makes the terminated population impossible to ignore.

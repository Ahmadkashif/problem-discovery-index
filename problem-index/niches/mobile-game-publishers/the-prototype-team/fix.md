# Thirty Kills and No Idea Whether You Are Improving

**Niche:** [[niches/mobile-game-publishers/the-prototype-team/profile|The Prototype Team]]
**Industry:** [[industries/mobile-game-publishers|Mobile Game Publishers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A designer finishes a year with thirty killed concepts and cannot answer whether they are better at this than they were in January.
**Tags:** #worker-facing #quick-win #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #confidence-intervals #survival-analysis #automation #tacit-knowledge-ml
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to give a team that is killed dozens of times a year some way to know whether any of the work was good — and whoever provides it takes the account.

## The Problem
At a performance review a prototype designer has nothing to show. Everything they made was killed. They cannot demonstrate improvement, cannot point to a body of work, and cannot argue that their near-misses were closer than last year's because nobody recorded the numbers in a comparable way. The honest answer — that thirty attempts at a low base rate produce almost no signal about an individual — is not said, and the person concludes they are bad at their job.

## Why It's Still Broken
Nothing keeps a per-person record across attempts — a career measured only by outcomes that almost never occur produces no evidence of skill either way, and the person fills the silence with self-assessment. Test conditions vary so figures are not comparable. Near-misses are not distinguished from clear failures. And nobody says out loud how much of this is noise.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Keep the record and state the noise. Maintain a per-designer history of concepts with their test numbers, conditions and margins, which is the fix and is a table that does not currently exist. Normalise for test conditions so figures across a year are comparable, since audience and cost differences otherwise dominate. Distinguish near-misses from clear failures explicitly, as that distinction is most of the available signal. Show the trend in margin rather than only the count of kills, which is the closest thing to a progress measure. State the base rate and the noise plainly, because a designer comparing themselves against an unstated standard assumes the worst. Record concepts that were killed and later validated by a similar title elsewhere. Aggregate to the team so leads can see whether the group is improving. Bring this into performance reviews, which is where the absence currently does its damage. Keep the record portable to the designer, since it is their body of work. And present it as evidence rather than as a ranking, which determines whether people trust it.

## Who Feels the Pain
Designers with nothing to show for a year's work; team leads with no basis for a review; publishers losing experienced prototypers; and the craft that never accumulates.

## Impact If Fixed
A career measured only by outcomes that almost never occur produces no evidence of skill either way, and the person fills the silence with self-assessment. A normalised per-designer record of margins makes a year of work legible.

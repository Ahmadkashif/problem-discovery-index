# Niche Analysis — Mobile Game Publishers

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/mobile-game-publishers|Mobile Game Publishers]]

## Niche Selection

Mobile publishing is a funnel with a measurement problem at every stage. Concepts are built cheaply and killed on a three-day signal against a threshold nobody derived; the games that survive earn from advertising and purchases that compete for the same attention and are tested one at a time; the validation happens in a handful of markets and the scaling happens everywhere. The eight niches below follow the money through that funnel and stop wherever the contest becomes a single question every competitor is answering differently.

## Why These Niches

The first two carry most of the revenue and most of the unexamined decision-making: which concepts continue, and how the survivors earn. Geographic test validity and external studio partnerships are the two places where a publisher's judgement is applied at scale with no instrumentation behind it — a test market chosen by convention, a third-party deal negotiated on relationship. The two underserved audiences are people inside the building: a prototype team killed dozens of times a year with no way to know whether any of the work was good, and a monetisation manager who can see where the revenue comes from and has no instrument telling them whether the people generating it are all right. The last two are mechanical: producing prototypes at volume, and configuring the offers that convert.

## Filter Notes

Seven of the eight are terminal — each states a single contest that every serious competitor is fighting over, and decomposing further would produce features rather than markets.

**Prototype selection** is not. It names a decision rather than a contest, and underneath it are two problems that are genuinely different businesses. Early signal modelling asks what a concept's first days of behaviour imply about its eventual performance — a modelling problem over the publisher's own telemetry, sold as a tool to a publisher who already has the data. Survivorship correction asks how to learn anything when the evidence base consists entirely of the games that passed the threshold — an experimental design and data acquisition problem that requires deliberately funding some failures, and whose value comes from the corpus rather than the model. One improves a prediction; the other fixes the data the prediction is trained on. A publisher can buy either without the other, and the second is the harder sell precisely because it costs money visibly and saves it invisibly.

Two adjacent candidates were rejected as belonging elsewhere: **buying the installs and testing the ad creative** is its own industry and sits at [[industries/game-user-acquisition-firms|Game User Acquisition Firms]], and **running a shipped game as an ongoing service** belongs to [[industries/game-liveops-services|Game LiveOps Services]].

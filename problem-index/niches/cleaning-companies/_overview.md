## Why These Niches

Commercial janitorial and residential recurring represent the two dominant revenue engines — together they account for over 70% of industry revenue and face distinct operational challenges (contract bidding vs. consumer scheduling). Post-construction and carpet/floor care are digitally neglected segments still running on paper work orders and phone scheduling despite having complex job costing needs. Green cleaning and medical facility cleaning serve underserved audiences with specialized compliance and certification requirements that generic cleaning software ignores. Franchise operations and quality inspection represent the highest automation ROI — franchise owners manage dozens of identical workflows across locations, and quality inspection is still done by walk-throughs that could be standardized and sensor-augmented.

## Why These Pockets

The industry's central skill, per the Pass 1 analysis, is that an experienced estimator walks a facility and knows the labour hours. The sweep finds that somebody sells exactly that knowledge as a database, one layer up, and it is the only pocket here that qualifies.

Facility and construction cost data publishers maintain hundreds of thousands of unit cost line items — crew composition, productivity, material cost, localized to hundreds of cities — collected and verified by cost engineers over decades. Estimators license it because it is the defensible basis for a bid. Two gaps compound in the same direction. The database is validated only by the collection method that produced it: subscribers estimate against it and then find out what the work actually cost, and none of that returns, so the weakest entries are precisely the infrequent tasks and secondary markets where no researcher has looked recently and no feedback exists. And each figure is stored as a number with its derivation discarded, so a challenged line item is defended from a cost engineer's recollection, and a retiring engineer takes the basis for several thousand entries with them.

Eleven pockets logged without qualifying, and the two largest research organizations in the sweep are among them. Cleaning chemical manufacturers run formulation and microbiology groups of several hundred and hold efficacy data across millions of customer sites, all of it a reason to buy chemicals. Facility management firms hold cost data across billions of square feet under management and deliver benchmarking inside a management contract. Two carry live kill switches from data ownership rather than scale — GLP efficacy studies are sponsor-owned under regulatory audit, and product certification submissions are confidential business information. And the industry association publishes the cleaning time standards the entire sector workloads against, as a membership benefit rather than a licensed product.

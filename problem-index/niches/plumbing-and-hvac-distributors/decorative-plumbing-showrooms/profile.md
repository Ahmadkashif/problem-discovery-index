# Decorative Plumbing & Kitchen-Bath Showrooms

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/plumbing-and-hvac-distributors|Plumbing & HVAC Distributors]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn a homeowner's or designer's showroom selections into a complete, compatible order — rough valves matched to trims, finishes matched across brands, lead times known — that reaches the plumber's rough-in without a wrong or missing piece, and whoever delivers the most selection packages complete and compatible takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$10B a year of decorative faucets, fixtures, tubs, showers and accessories sold through distributor showrooms (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~5% of the three codes' ~$192.7B receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — selections live in binders, spreadsheets and ERP quotes keyed from notes; manufacturers' configurators don't talk to each other
**Target Buyer:** Showroom manager or director of showrooms; the plumbing contractor or builder who refers the client
**Automation Potential:** Medium — compatibility checking, bills of material and order tracking are mechanical; the selection conversation is not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Plumbing distributors run showrooms (Ferguson Bath, Kitchen & Lighting Galleries, Hajoca's and many regional houses', recalled) where homeowners and designers choose what the plumber will install. The buyer of record is usually the contractor or builder, who gets a trade price and sends clients in; the showroom wins the builder's referrals when selections arrive complete and on time and loses them when the plumber finds the wrong rough valve in a framed wall. It is low digitized because every brand's compatibility rules (valve bodies, cartridges, rough-ins, finishes) live in its own spec sheets, and a selection crosses brands. It sits between [[niches/plumbing-contractors/residential-service-repair/profile|Residential Service & Repair]] on the installer side and the homeowner's own retail purchases, and the queued plumbing products makers build covers the manufacturers.

## Current Tools & Gaps
ERP quotes, showroom selection software and spreadsheets, manufacturers' configurators and dealer portals, and email. The gaps: no cross-brand compatibility check, no live lead times on a selection, and no record of what the homeowner chose versus what was ordered.

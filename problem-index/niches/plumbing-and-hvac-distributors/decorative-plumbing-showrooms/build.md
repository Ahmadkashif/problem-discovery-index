# A Compatibility Graph Across Brands, Valves and Finishes

**Niche:** [[niches/plumbing-and-hvac-distributors/decorative-plumbing-showrooms/profile|Decorative Plumbing & Kitchen-Bath Showrooms]]
**Industry:** [[industries/plumbing-and-hvac-distributors|Plumbing & HVAC Distributors]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Each manufacturer publishes what fits its own products; no one publishes what fits across a whole bathroom.
**Tags:** #graph-neural-networks #graph-theory #large-language-models #k-nearest-neighbors #data-integration #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn a homeowner's or designer's showroom selections into a complete, compatible order — rough valves matched to trims, finishes matched across brands, lead times known — that reaches the plumber's rough-in without a wrong or missing piece, and whoever delivers the most selection packages complete and compatible takes the account.

## The Problem
A shower system needs a valve body that fits the trim, a diverter for the hand shower, a supply elbow and a slide bar in the same finish; a tub filler needs a floor rough; a lavatory faucet needs a drain in a finish that matches. Experienced consultants know the traps; newer ones learn them from angry plumbers.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Compatibility is buried in spec-sheet PDFs and changes with model years; manufacturers have no reason to map competitors' products; showrooms never pooled their error histories.

## What to Build
A graph of products and their required companions (rough valves, cartridges, drains, mounting kits) extracted from spec sheets by language models and checked against the house's order and return history; finish families mapped across brands from images and names; and a selection checker that completes the bill of materials and flags conflicts as the consultant builds the selection. A demo can start with three major faucet brands' public spec sheets.

## Target Customer
Showroom directors at plumbing distributors with several showrooms.

## Impact If Built
If ~5–10% of showroom projects now hit a wrong or missing rough-in part (estimate), catching them at selection saves a return trip and a reorder per incident and protects the builder referrals that feed the showroom.

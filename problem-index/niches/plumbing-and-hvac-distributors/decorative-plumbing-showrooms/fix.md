# The Rough Valve That Ships With the Finish Package

**Niche:** [[niches/plumbing-and-hvac-distributors/decorative-plumbing-showrooms/profile|Decorative Plumbing & Kitchen-Bath Showrooms]]
**Industry:** [[industries/plumbing-and-hvac-distributors|Plumbing & HVAC Distributors]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The plumber needs the valve bodies at rough-in, weeks before the trims; when they ship together, the walls wait or get opened.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #data-integration #workflow-orchestration #quick-win #automation #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn a homeowner's or designer's showroom selections into a complete, compatible order — rough valves matched to trims, finishes matched across brands, lead times known — that reaches the plumber's rough-in without a wrong or missing piece, and whoever delivers the most selection packages complete and compatible takes the account.

## The Problem
A showroom order is often entered as one order and held until every item arrives, because a homeowner's twelve-week tub filler holds the rest. The plumber reaches rough-in with no valve bodies, roughs in a generic valve, or waits; when trims arrive that don't fit what was roughed, the tile comes off.

## Why It's Still Broken
ERPs don't know which lines are rough-in and which are finish, consultants don't know the plumber's schedule, and builders call only when it's already late.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Classify every showroom line as rough or finish from the product data, split the order automatically, release rough parts against the plumber's rough-in date and finish parts against trim-out, and alert the consultant when a rough part's lead time threatens the rough-in date.

## Who Feels the Pain
Plumbers on site, builders on schedule, homeowners paying for opened walls, and consultants absorbing the blame.

## Impact If Fixed
If late or wrong rough parts delay ~5–10% of showroom projects by days (estimate), splitting and timing the release removes most of those delays at almost no cost.

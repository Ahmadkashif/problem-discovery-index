# Commercial Plumbing & Mechanical Project Supply

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/plumbing-and-hvac-distributors|Plumbing & HVAC Distributors]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is that every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to win the mechanical or plumbing contractor's material package on a commercial job, but contractors routinely split that package, and the two halves are won differently: the equipment and fixtures go to whoever re-selects the engineer's schedule in its own lines and gets the substitutions approved, and the pipe, valves and fittings go to whoever holds the lowest firm price across a release schedule that runs for months while copper and PVC move. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$50B a year of distributor sales to commercial plumbing and mechanical contractors (estimate; Ferguson says non-residential is about half of its $29.8B US revenue, and waterworks sits outside this niche)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~26% of the three codes' ~$192.7B receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low-Medium — ERPs and EDI handle stock orders; schedules, selections, submittals, price holds and releases run on PDFs, spreadsheets, selection software and phone calls
**Target Buyer:** VP of commercial or mechanical sales; branch and regional managers; quotation and PVF managers
**Automation Potential:** Medium — high for schedule extraction, quote assembly and repricing; low for engineer relationships and contractor credit

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Commercial work is bid from plans and specs and supplied over months. A mechanical contractor bidding a school sends the package to several supply houses, and often buys the plumbing fixtures and water heaters from one, the light-commercial HVAC equipment from another and the PVF from whoever is cheapest that month (estimate). Before the bid, the contest for equipment and fixtures is selection and submittal approval, which [[problems/plumbing-and-hvac-distributors/high-impact|the high-impact problem]] describes; for PVF it is a firm price held across the job's releases, which the [[industries/electrical-distributors|electrical]] build sees as copper-indexed wire. Different people, measures and winners, so the niche decomposes. Contractor-side estimating is [[niches/plumbing-contractors/commercial-new-construction/profile|Commercial New Construction]] and [[niches/hvac-contractors/commercial-installation/profile|Commercial Installation]].

## Current Tools & Gaps
Epicor Eclipse quote modules, OEM selection programs, fixture catalogues, Rebar's computer-vision HVAC quoting, contractor-side takeoff (Trimble estimating, QuoteSoft-type tools, recalled), manufacturers' price sheets and project pricing. The gaps: no record joining the schedule, the selection, the submittal response and the outcome, and price holds tracked in memory.

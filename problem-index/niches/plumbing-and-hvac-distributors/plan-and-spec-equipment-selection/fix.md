# The Submittal Rejection That Never Comes Back to the Quote

**Niche:** [[niches/plumbing-and-hvac-distributors/plan-and-spec-equipment-selection/profile|Plan-and-Spec Equipment & Fixture Selection]]
**Industry:** [[industries/plumbing-and-hvac-distributors|Plumbing & HVAC Distributors]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Engineers' submittal responses are the only honest label on a substitution, and they land in the contractor's project platform, not the supply house's records.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #logistic-regression #data-integration #quick-win #evaluation-metrics
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn an engineer's equipment and fixture schedules into a priced package of the supply house's own lines that helps the contractor win the bid and clears submittal, and whoever posts the most awarded packages approved on the first submittal takes the account.

## The Problem
After award, the house prepares submittals; the engineer returns them marked approved, approved as noted, revise and resubmit, or rejected, with comments on a line or two. The response goes to the contractor through Procore or email, the specialist fixes the line, and the comment is never stored against the selection that caused it.

## Why It's Still Broken
Submittal review happens in the contractor's system, the distributor sees PDFs forwarded by email, and nobody owns the job of tagging the response back to the quote line.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Read forwarded submittal responses automatically, extract the stamp and line-level comments, match them to the job and quote lines, and store the outcome with the engineer's name and the reason. Show the specialist the engineer's history the next time that engineer's schedule arrives.

## Who Feels the Pain
Applications specialists repeating rejected substitutions, contractors losing weeks to resubmittals, and outside salespeople whose engineer relationships rest on memory.

## Impact If Fixed
If each resubmittal costs ~1–3 weeks on the contractor's schedule (estimate), knowing an engineer's rejection patterns before quoting avoids a share of them, and creates the approval labels the selection model needs.

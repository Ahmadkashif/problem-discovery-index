# The Price Hold Written Only in an Email

**Niche:** [[niches/plumbing-and-hvac-distributors/pvf-package-price-holds/profile|PVF Package Pricing & Price Holds]]
**Industry:** [[industries/plumbing-and-hvac-distributors|Plumbing & HVAC Distributors]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The terms of a six-month price hold often exist only in the email that promised it, so the releases are priced by whoever keys the order.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #data-integration #quick-win #automation #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to hold a firm price on a mechanical contractor's pipe, valve and fitting package across a release schedule that runs for months while copper and PVC move, and whoever quotes the lowest price held firm through the job's last release takes the account.

## The Problem
The PVF manager emails the contractor: "pricing held through June 30 on quoted quantities." Months later, a release arrives at a branch; the inside salesperson either prices it at today's sheet and angers the contractor, or honours a hold that expired or quantities that were exceeded. Disputes are settled by searching inboxes.

## Why It's Still Broken
ERPs store quote prices and expiry dates but not hold terms, quantity caps or the commodities a hold excluded, and the person who promised the hold is rarely the one keying the release.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Extract hold terms from the quote email or PDF automatically (dates, quantities, excluded items, escalation clauses), attach them to the job and quote lines in the ERP, and apply them when a release is keyed: honour within terms, flag overruns and expiries, and show the margin consequence before the order is released.

## Who Feels the Pain
Inside salespeople caught between the contractor and the margin, PVF managers fielding disputes, and controllers finding below-cost releases at month end.

## Impact If Fixed
If hold disputes or mispriced releases touch ~10–20% of commercial PVF jobs (estimate), recording the terms ends most of them and recovers margin on releases priced below a hold's actual terms.

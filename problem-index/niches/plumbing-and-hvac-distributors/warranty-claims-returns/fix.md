# The Failed Part Scrapped Before the Credit Arrived

**Niche:** [[niches/plumbing-and-hvac-distributors/warranty-claims-returns/profile|Warranty Claims & Defective-Part Returns]]
**Industry:** [[industries/plumbing-and-hvac-distributors|Plumbing & HVAC Distributors]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Manufacturers sometimes ask for the failed part back weeks after the claim; by then the branch has thrown it away and the credit is reversed.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #data-integration #workflow-orchestration #quick-win #automation #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn each warranty replacement handed across the counter into a claim the manufacturer pays on first submission, and whoever posts the highest first-pass paid-claim rate takes the account.

## The Problem
Tagged failed parts pile up in a branch's back room. Some manufacturers want specific parts returned for analysis, others authorise scrap, and instructions arrive by email or portal at different times. A branch clearing space in August scraps a compressor the manufacturer then requests, and the claim is charged back.

## Why It's Still Broken
Return instructions aren't linked to the physical tag on the shelf, and branch staff can't see which claims are still open.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Print a code on every failed-part tag linked to the claim, pull each manufacturer's return-or-scrap instruction into the same record, and tell the branch weekly which parts to ship, which to scrap and which to hold.

## Who Feels the Pain
Warranty clerks reversing credits, branch staff with full back rooms, and controllers writing off charge-backs.

## Impact If Fixed
If charge-backs for missing failed parts cost a mid-size house ~$20,000–$100,000 a year (estimate), linking tags to instructions removes most of them at the cost of a label printer.

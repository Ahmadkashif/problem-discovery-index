# The Texted Nameplate Photo Nobody Answers Until Noon

**Niche:** [[niches/plumbing-and-hvac-distributors/hvac-parts-counter-will-call/profile|HVAC Repair Parts Counter & Will-Call]]
**Industry:** [[industries/plumbing-and-hvac-distributors|Plumbing & HVAC Distributors]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Technicians text photos and part requests to the branch at 6 a.m.; in peak season the queue waits behind the counter line.
**Tags:** #cnns #transformers #large-language-models #workflow-orchestration #quick-win #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to hand a service technician the right replacement part — OEM or approved universal — from a model and serial number, a nameplate photo or a description while the homeowner waits, and whoever sends the most technicians back to the job with the right part on the first trip takes the account.

## The Problem
Texting moved counter traffic to phones, but the texts land on the same people serving the counter. A photo of a rusted nameplate, "need this board," and "is the 3-ton in stock at Eastside" sit unread while the line moves, and the technician calls, drives over, or buys elsewhere.

## Why It's Still Broken
Text platforms route messages but don't identify parts; branches staff the counter for the line, not the inbox; and nobody measures response time on texts.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Read every incoming text and photo as it arrives, parse the nameplate, identify the likely part with confidence, check stock, draft the reply and reserve the part for will-call, leaving the counter person to approve with one tap or take over when confidence is low. Measure time to first answer per branch.

## Who Feels the Pain
Technicians waiting in a driveway, counter staff juggling the line and the phone, and branch managers losing texted orders they never see.

## Impact If Fixed
If peak-hour text answers drop from ~30–60 minutes to a few (estimate), more texted orders convert at the branch instead of a competitor's app, and the counter line moves faster because fewer technicians come in to ask.

# Parts Databases Wired Into the Branch's Own Stock and Prices

**Niche:** [[niches/plumbing-and-hvac-distributors/hvac-parts-counter-will-call/profile|HVAC Repair Parts Counter & Will-Call]]
**Industry:** [[industries/plumbing-and-hvac-distributors|Plumbing & HVAC Distributors]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Model-number and cross-reference databases now exist commercially; the counter needs them answering in the branch's SKUs, stock, contractor prices and warranty rules.
**Tags:** #k-nearest-neighbors #large-language-models #cnns #data-integration #worker-facing #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to hand a service technician the right replacement part — OEM or approved universal — from a model and serial number, a nameplate photo or a description while the homeowner waits, and whoever sends the most technicians back to the job with the right part on the first trip takes the account.

## The Problem
A counter person can look a model up in Bluon or a manufacturer's site, then has to find the house's SKU for the OEM part or its preferred universal, check stock at this and nearby branches, apply the contractor's price and note whether the part is under warranty.

## What Already Exists
Bluon's brand-agnostic database and PartsConnect, OEM parts portals, universal-parts makers' cross-reference tools and the ERP's item master.

## The Customization Gap
A mapping layer from any model and part number to the house's SKUs and preferred universals, live multi-branch stock, contractor-specific pricing, warranty and serial-date checks, Section 608 certification checks for refrigerant, and one-tap sale into Eclipse or Infor CSD. It must keep each branch's preferences ("we stock this universal, not that one") and learn from returns.

## Target Customer
Independent and OEM-aligned HVAC distributors that want counter speed without building Watsco-scale software.

## Impact If Solved
If a lookup falls from ~2–5 minutes to under 30 seconds (estimate), a counter serving ~200 peak-day transactions saves hours a day and keeps technicians who would otherwise order from their phones elsewhere.

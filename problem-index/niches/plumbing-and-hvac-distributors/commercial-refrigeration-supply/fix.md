# Refrigerant Sold Without Knowing the System It Goes Into

**Niche:** [[niches/plumbing-and-hvac-distributors/commercial-refrigeration-supply/profile|Commercial Refrigeration Supply & the Refrigeration Technician]]
**Industry:** [[industries/plumbing-and-hvac-distributors|Plumbing & HVAC Distributors]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The counter checks the technician's certification card, but not whether the refrigerant suits the system or the system now falls under the leak-repair rule.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #logistic-regression #data-integration #compliance #quick-win #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to tell a refrigeration technician which compressor, controls and refrigerant will keep a walk-in, reach-in or rack running and legal under the AIM Act's refrigerant limits and leak-repair rules, and whoever specifies the most retrofits and replacements that work and comply on the first attempt takes the account.

## The Problem
A technician buys three cylinders of R-404A for the same store every month. The counter sells them; nobody notices that the repeat purchases point to a leaking rack that now owes repairs and records under EPA's rule, or that a lower-GWP option would serve better as virgin supply tightens.

## Why It's Still Broken
Refrigerant sales are recorded per cylinder and customer, not per system or site, and distributors have no role in the store's compliance.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Ask for the site and system at the counter or in the app when refrigerant is sold to a refrigeration account, flag repeat purchases for the same system, and offer the technician the leak-repair obligations, retrofit options and reclaimed-refrigerant alternatives that apply — a service that also sells parts.

## Who Feels the Pain
Refrigeration technicians caught between store owners and the rule, store owners facing leak-repair obligations, and distributors selling refrigerant that will be harder to source.

## Impact If Fixed
If ~10–20% of the cylinders sold to refrigeration accounts are repeat top-ups on the same system (estimate), flagging them turns a commodity sale into retrofit and repair business worth several times the refrigerant.

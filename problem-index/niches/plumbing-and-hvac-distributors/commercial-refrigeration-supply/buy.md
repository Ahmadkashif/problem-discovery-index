# Component Selection Tools Joined Across Manufacturers

**Niche:** [[niches/plumbing-and-hvac-distributors/commercial-refrigeration-supply/profile|Commercial Refrigeration Supply & the Refrigeration Technician]]
**Industry:** [[industries/plumbing-and-hvac-distributors|Plumbing & HVAC Distributors]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Compressor, valve and controls makers each ship a good selection tool for their own products; a refrigeration system is built from all of them.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #k-nearest-neighbors #data-integration #compliance #automation #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to tell a refrigeration technician which compressor, controls and refrigerant will keep a walk-in, reach-in or rack running and legal under the AIM Act's refrigerant limits and leak-repair rules, and whoever specifies the most retrofits and replacements that work and comply on the first attempt takes the account.

## The Problem
A distributor's refrigeration specialist opens three manufacturers' tools to size a compressor, an expansion valve and a condensing unit for one walk-in, then checks the refrigerant's status and the house's stock separately.

## What Already Exists
Manufacturers' selection software and mobile apps, refrigerant producers' retrofit charts, ERP item masters and general parts-lookup tools.

## The Customization Gap
A distributor-side layer that calls or mirrors each manufacturer's selection data, assembles a system from the house's carried brands, applies refrigerant compatibility and legal status by application, adds the parts a retrofit needs (oil, driers, valve elements, labels), prices from stock and records the configuration for the job and any later warranty claim.

## Target Customer
HVACR distributors with refrigeration counters and specialist staff.

## Impact If Solved
If a system selection falls from ~30–60 minutes to ~5–10 (estimate), a refrigeration specialist serves several times more calls in a heat wave, when walk-ins fail most.

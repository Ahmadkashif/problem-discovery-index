# A Retrofit Checker That Knows the Rules by Application and Date

**Industry:** [[industries/plumbing-and-hvac-distributors|Plumbing & HVAC Distributors]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A refrigeration retrofit is a compatibility problem and a legal problem at once, and the counter specialist solves both from memory.

**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to tell a refrigeration technician which compressor, controls and refrigerant will keep a walk-in, reach-in or rack running and legal under the AIM Act's refrigerant limits and leak-repair rules, and whoever specifies the most retrofits and replacements that work and comply on the first attempt takes the account.

## The Problem
A technician calls about a failed compressor on an R-404A walk-in freezer. Replace like for like, retrofit to a lower-GWP blend, or replace the condensing unit? The answer depends on the compressor's compatibility with the blend, oil, expansion valve, the system's charge, the store's other appliances under the leak rule, and refrigerant availability and price as allowances tighten. The veteran knows; the technician is on a clock while the food warms.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Each compressor, valve and refrigerant maker publishes guidance for its own products; the regulatory status moves (EPA changed supermarket deadlines in May 2026, search summary); and nobody records which retrofits worked.

## What to Build
A checker that takes the nameplate, application and charge, proposes like-for-like, retrofit and replacement options from manufacturers' data with each refrigerant's legal status by application and date, prices them from the house's stock, and records the outcome the technician reports. A demo can use published retrofit guides and EPA's rule tables.

## Target Customer
Refrigeration product managers at HVACR distributors.

## Impact If Built
If ~10–20% of retrofit attempts now need a second visit (estimate), first-time-right selection saves a technician trip and hours of product loss per failure.

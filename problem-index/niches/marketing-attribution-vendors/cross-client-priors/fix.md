# The Benchmark Nobody Can Source

**Niche:** [[niches/marketing-attribution-vendors/cross-client-priors/profile|Cross-Client Priors]]
**Industry:** [[industries/marketing-attribution-vendors|Marketing Attribution Vendors]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The prior came from an industry benchmark that appears in every deck, and nobody can say what data produced it or when.
**Tags:** #compliance #descriptive-statistics #confidence-intervals #evaluation-metrics #hypothesis-testing #quick-win #bayesian-inference #probability-distributions
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn a portfolio of clients into estimated priors that replace an analyst's judgement — and whoever does that makes every new engagement start from evidence instead of from an assumption.

## The Problem
A typical channel return figure circulates through the industry. It appears in decks, in priors, in business cases and in benchmark comparisons. Trace it back and it leads to a report citing a survey citing an earlier report, with no accessible underlying data, no stated sample, no date and no methodology. It may have been reasonable when it was produced, for a set of businesses that may not resemble this one, some years ago. It is being used as a prior in models whose output determines budget allocation, and it is functionally folklore with a citation.

## Why It's Still Broken
A cited number is treated as evidence regardless of whether the citation leads anywhere, and nobody checks because checking is tedious and the number is convenient — provenance is assumed rather than verified. Benchmarks are published as marketing rather than as research and are not expected to withstand scrutiny. Analysts need a prior and the alternative is inventing one, which feels worse. And the field has no norm of sourcing.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Require provenance. State the source, sample, date and method for every benchmark used as a prior, which is the fix, is one line, and eliminates most circulating figures immediately because they cannot survive it. Prefer priors estimated from the vendor's own portfolio, which have known provenance by construction and are the build note's subject. Attach uncertainty to any benchmark, since a point figure used as a prior is a far stronger assumption than the underlying evidence supports. Check applicability explicitly — business model, scale, geography, period — because a benchmark from a different context is worse than an honest wide prior. Report which priors materially affected the result, connecting to the mix modelling work, so the reliance is visible. Maintain an internal benchmark library with sources, replacing recollection with a reference. Retire benchmarks that cannot be sourced, rather than continuing to use them because everyone does. Publish the vendor's own benchmarks with full methodology, which is both a contribution and a competitive position in a field where nobody does. Teach clients to ask where a number came from, since a single question changes what can be presented. And report the share of a model's result that rests on unsourced priors, because a business case built substantially on folklore should be labelled.

## Who Feels the Pain
Analysts using priors they cannot defend; clients whose budgets rest partly on a circulating number; and the field, whose empirical base is a set of uncheckable citations.

## Impact If Fixed
A cited number is treated as evidence regardless of whether the citation leads anywhere, and most circulating benchmarks cannot survive a one-line provenance requirement. Portfolio-estimated priors have known provenance by construction and are the durable replacement.

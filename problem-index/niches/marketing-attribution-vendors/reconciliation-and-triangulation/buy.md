## The Problem
Combining evidence of differing quality into a defensible conclusion is a developed discipline. Systematic review grades evidence by design, evidence hierarchies place randomised trials above observational studies for causal questions, network meta-analysis combines indirect comparisons, and formal frameworks exist for expressing confidence in a body of evidence. Medicine and public policy built this because decisions matter and evidence is mixed. Marketing measurement faces exactly this and combines by eye.

## What Already Exists
Systematic review and evidence grading frameworks; evidence hierarchies by study design; network and Bayesian meta-analysis; formal confidence assessment in bodies of evidence; and bias assessment tools for individual studies.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a continuously updating commercial decision with a tiny evidence base. It requires: (1) a handful of estimates rather than dozens of studies, so formal meta-analytic pooling is over-specified and a simpler weighted combination with explicit reasoning is the right instrument — the framework transfers better than the machinery; (2) estimates from the same underlying data, which makes them correlated in ways independent studies are not and which is the specific reason naive combination overstates confidence; (3) an evidence hierarchy that must be established empirically here rather than assumed, since which method is more reliable varies by channel and context; (4) weekly decisions rather than periodic reviews, requiring the synthesis to be continuous and automated; and (5) evidence produced by interested parties, which medicine handles through disclosure and registration and marketing handles not at all.

## Target Customer
Client measurement leadership, independent measurement advisors, and evidence synthesis practitioners for whom commercial measurement is an unclaimed application.

## Impact If Solved
Medicine built evidence synthesis because decisions matter and evidence is mixed, and marketing combines by eye. The estimates here share underlying data, which makes naive combination overstate confidence in a way independent-study methods do not anticipate.

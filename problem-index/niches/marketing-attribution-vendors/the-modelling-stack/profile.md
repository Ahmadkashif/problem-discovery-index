# The Modelling Stack

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/marketing-attribution-vendors|Marketing Attribution Vendors]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** This niche is not terminal — path-based attribution and mix modelling are different disciplines with different failure modes and different winners, and they are stated separately in the sub-niches below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$750M US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~25% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** High — sophisticated and unresolved
**Target Buyer:** Vendor science leadership
**Automation Potential:** Very High — modelling is the product

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The models are what customers believe they are buying. Three methods compete and none is sufficient alone, the modelling is often genuinely good, and the category's difficulty is that it sells answers to a causal question from observational data. This is the second-largest concentration of contested value because the model is the deliverable and because vendor differentiation, such as it is, is claimed here.

It is also **not terminal**. The label names the product category rather than a contest. Path-based attribution assigns fractional credit along observed journeys and depends entirely on identity resolution and path completeness — it was undermined first by its own correlational logic and then by the collapse of cross-site identity, and its contest is a data and identity fight. Mix modelling works on aggregate time series and depends on identification — channel spends move together, adstock and saturation are specification choices, and priors frequently do more work than the data — and its contest is an econometric fight. The data, the failure modes, the expertise and the vendors are different. The two contests are stated in the sub-niches.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Multi-touch attribution products, mix modelling platforms including open-source implementations, and hybrid offerings. The gaps: two methods sold as one category with incompatible assumptions; no shared basis for comparing their outputs; identity collapse unaddressed in the first; identification weakness understated in the second; and neither validated against experiment.

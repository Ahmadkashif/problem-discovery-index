# The Quarter Spent Defending a Number

**Niche:** [[niches/marketing-attribution-vendors/the-marketing-scientist/profile|The Marketing Scientist]]
**Industry:** [[industries/marketing-attribution-vendors|Marketing Attribution Vendors]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The same five objections are raised by channel owners every quarter, the scientist answers them from memory each time, and none of the answers is written down.
**Tags:** #worker-facing #tacit-knowledge-ml #evaluation-metrics #workflow-orchestration #quick-win #descriptive-statistics #hypothesis-testing #confidence-intervals
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to let the scientist communicate the uncertainty they actually have — and whoever makes an honest interval presentable changes what this profession is able to say.

## The Problem
Every quarterly review produces the same challenges. The model does not capture brand effects. The window is too short. Our channel is different. The competitor's activity is not controlled for. You have not accounted for the promotion. Each is a reasonable question, each has a considered answer, and the scientist constructs that answer live, from memory, under pressure, in front of an audience that wants a different result. They do this across every client, every quarter, for years. Nothing is written down, so a new scientist starts from nothing and the answers vary between people in ways clients notice.

## Why It's Still Broken
The objections are treated as conversation rather than as a recurring analytical requirement, so nobody prepares for them systematically — the framing as a meeting dynamic rather than a work product is why the preparation never happens. Answering well requires analysis that takes longer than the meeting. Scientists are measured on delivery rather than on preparation. And the knowledge is individual and leaves with people.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Prepare the answers in advance and keep them. Maintain a library of the standard objections with their analytical answers, which is the fix, takes a day to assemble from what people already know, and removes most of the live improvisation. Run the relevant sensitivity analysis before the meeting rather than promising to look into it afterwards, since the objections are predictable and the analysis is the answer. Include the pre-emptive analysis in the deliverable, which is more persuasive than any response and changes the meeting's dynamic entirely. Distinguish objections that are valid from those that are not, plainly, because treating all challenges as equally reasonable is how a good analysis loses to a persistent channel owner. Record which objections were raised and how they were answered, so the library improves and a new scientist inherits it. Standardise the answers across the firm, since inconsistency between scientists is noticed and damages credibility. Give clients the objection library too, which turns an adversarial exchange into a shared understanding of the method's limits. Track which objections recur for which client, since a repeated objection usually points at a genuine gap in the analysis or in the relationship. Build the pre-emptive analysis into the standard deliverable template so it happens by default. And measure the meeting's outcome rather than its comfort, because a review that ends in agreement on a wrong number is worse than one that ends in a scheduled experiment.

## Who Feels the Pain
Scientists improvising defences quarterly; clients receiving different answers from different people; and firms whose most valuable delivery knowledge is held individually.

## Impact If Fixed
The objections are framed as meeting dynamics rather than as a recurring work product, which is why nobody prepares. An objection library with pre-run sensitivity analysis takes a day to assemble and changes the meeting from an improvised defence into a prepared disclosure.

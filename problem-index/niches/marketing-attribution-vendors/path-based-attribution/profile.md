# Path-Based Attribution

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/marketing-attribution-vendors|Marketing Attribution Vendors]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to say something defensible about journeys that are now mostly unobservable — and whoever handles the missing paths honestly replaces a method that survives on familiarity.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$350M US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~12% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** High — widely deployed, structurally undermined
**Target Buyer:** Client analytics teams and vendor product leadership
**Automation Potential:** High — the modelling is tractable, the data is not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Multi-touch attribution assigns fractional credit along observed paths and was undermined first by its own correlational logic and then by the collapse of cross-site identity; it survives mostly because clients are used to it. Its contest is fundamentally about data: how much of a journey can be observed, how the unobserved portion is handled, and whether anything defensible can be said from a path that is now mostly missing. That is a data and identity fight, not an econometric one, which is what separates it from the sibling niche.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Rule-based and algorithmic credit assignment, identity stitching across available signals, platform-modelled conversions accepted as observations, and journey reporting. The gaps: paths that are majority unobserved; no representation of the missing portion; credit assigned along observed touchpoints as though they were the whole journey; platform-modelled data treated as observed; and correlational logic presented as contribution.

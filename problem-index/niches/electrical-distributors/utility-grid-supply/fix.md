# The Transformer Order Placed Without the Work Plan

**Niche:** [[niches/electrical-distributors/utility-grid-supply/profile|Utility & Grid Supply]]
**Industry:** [[industries/electrical-distributors|Electrical Distributors]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The distributor orders transformers on last year's usage while the utility's new-service backlog is in a system nobody shares.
**Tags:** #time-series-forecasting #linear-regression #data-integration #quick-win #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep a utility's or co-op's transformers, cable, meters and line hardware on the shelf under an alliance agreement through storms and multi-year lead times, and whoever posts the highest fill rate when the storm hits takes the account.

## The Problem
With pad-mount distribution transformers at ~75 weeks after order in mid-2024 and still lengthening in 2025 (Wood Mackenzie), orders must be placed long before need. The utility knows its new-service and capital pipeline by kVA class; the distributor orders on history.

## Why It's Still Broken
Utilities treat work plans as internal, and alliance agreements rarely require sharing them.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A monthly work-plan extract by transformer class and region, shared under the alliance, turned into a rolling order plan with the manufacturer's lead time applied.

## Who Feels the Pain
Utility materials managers facing new-service delays, distributors holding the wrong ratings, and developers waiting for power.

## Impact If Fixed
Transformer orders match the jobs coming. If ~10–20% of new-service delays trace to the wrong kVA classes on the shelf (estimate), sharing the work plan removes most of them at no software cost, the cheapest fill-rate gain available.

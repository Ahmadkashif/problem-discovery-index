# Contractor Project Supply

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/electrical-distributors|Electrical Distributors]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is that every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to win and keep the electrical contractor's project business, but that splits into two contests with different winners: putting the complete, winning quote in the contractor's hands by bid day, and getting the long-lead gear to the jobsite on the date the schedule needs after award. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$87B a year of distributor sales to electrical contractors (Electrical Wholesaling's 2026 Market Planning Guide estimate, search summary), most of it on construction projects quoted from plans and specs
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~58% of EW's ~$151.2B distributor sales estimate (search summary) (derived)
**Digital Adoption:** Low-Medium — ERPs, EDI and e-commerce handle stock orders; project quotes, SPA requests, submittals and release tracking still run on PDFs, spreadsheets, phone calls and portals
**Target Buyer:** VP of Construction Sales or project business at the distributor; branch and regional managers
**Automation Potential:** Medium — high for drawing extraction, quote assembly and status tracking; low for pricing judgement and contractor relationships

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Electrical contractors buy most of what an electrical distributor sells, and they buy it job by job. A commercial, healthcare, industrial or data center project goes through two phases with different people and clocks. Before the bid, the distributor's quotation desk competes with two to five other distributors to give the contractor a complete, compliant, SPA-priced package (~3–6 distributors per contractor bid, estimate). After award, a project manager has to deliver switchgear, panelboards, transformers, lighting and thousands of commodity lines against a schedule that keeps moving, while manufacturers' lead times move too. The contractor stays with a distributor that wins it bids and keeps its job on schedule, but those are different capabilities, so the niche is decomposed.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Distributor ERPs (Epicor Eclipse, SAP), quoting tools such as Parspec, contractor-side estimating software (Trimble Accubid, ConEst, McCormick), construction lead services, manufacturer portals and the new IDEA ProjectNexus order-status platform. The gaps: no record linking a quote's inputs, edits and SPA requests to the job's outcome; win and loss rarely captured; and order status scattered across manufacturers.

# Wire & Cable Pricing Against COMEX Copper

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/electrical-distributors|Electrical Distributors]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to price every wire and cable quote and order to the day's copper with hold terms the contractor accepts, and whoever keeps wire margin flat through a copper swing without losing the bid takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$30B a year of building wire, cable and cord sold through US electrical distributors (estimate, ~15–25% of distributor sales)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~20% of EW's ~$151.2B distributor sales estimate (search summary) (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low-Medium — manufacturer price sheets arrive electronically, but repricing of open quotes and hold-period decisions are manual
**Target Buyer:** Wire and cable product manager or pricing manager at the distributor; the CFO when margin slips
**Automation Potential:** Very High — the cost reference is public and the rules are arithmetic

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Wire is the commodity inside electrical distribution. Its cost follows copper, which moves daily on COMEX; manufacturers reprice their sheets as often as daily; and contractors buy wire for jobs priced weeks earlier. The distributor's margin depends on the gap between the cost at which its reels were bought, the cost at which it quoted, and the cost when the order releases. A 50% Section 232 tariff on semi-finished copper and derivatives such as wire and cable took effect on 1 August 2025 and moved to full value from 6 April 2026, and COMEX copper set a record settlement of ~$6.9/lb in September 2026 (search summary). The contest is mechanical and measurable, which is why it is terminal. Cable manufacturing is the queued wire and cable manufacturers build; hedging advisory for firms large enough to hedge is logged at [[niches/commodity-brokers/end-user-hedge-advisory/profile|Commodity Hedge Programme Advisory for End Users]].

## Current Tools & Gaps
Manufacturer copper-based price sheets, ERP cost updates and matrix pricing in Epicor Eclipse and others, price optimisation software (Zilliant, PROS, Vendavo), and a few people who watch copper. The gaps: hold periods unrecorded, open quotes never swept when copper moves, escalation clauses inconsistent, and reel versus cut pricing handled by habit.

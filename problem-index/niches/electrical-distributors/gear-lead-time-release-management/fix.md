# The Acknowledgement That Doesn't Match the Submittal

**Niche:** [[niches/electrical-distributors/gear-lead-time-release-management/profile|Gear Lead Times & Release Management]]
**Industry:** [[industries/electrical-distributors|Electrical Distributors]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A switchboard ships with the wrong option because nobody compared the factory acknowledgement with the approved drawings.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #quick-win #workflow-orchestration #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get switchgear, transformers and other long-lead gear to the jobsite on the date the contractor's schedule needs, and whoever warns the contractor of a slip earliest takes the account.

## The Problem
Configured gear is ordered from an approved submittal, and the manufacturer's acknowledgement lists catalogue numbers and options back. Differences — a breaker frame, a voltage, a NEMA enclosure type — are easy to miss in a long PDF and show up at the jobsite, after a lead time measured in months.

## Why It's Still Broken
Project managers have no time to compare documents line by line, and the two documents use different formats and numbering.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Extract both documents, align lines and options, and flag every difference for the project manager before the order is released to production.

## Who Feels the Pain
Project managers, contractors waiting on gear, and distributors paying for returns and expedites.

## Impact If Fixed
A wrong option caught at acknowledgement costs an email; caught at the jobsite it costs a second lead time of ~20–60 weeks on configured gear (estimate) and the contractor's schedule with it.

# Branch Counter Sales to Contractors

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/electrical-distributors|Electrical Distributors]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get an electrician out of the branch with the right part during the early-morning rush, and whoever clears the 6:30 a.m. line with the fewest wrong-part return trips takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$25B a year of counter, will-call and walk-in sales at US electrical distributor branches (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~11% of 423610 receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — point-of-sale runs in the ERP, but identification, phone and text orders and will-call staging run on memory and paper tickets
**Target Buyer:** Branch manager; regional operations manager
**Automation Potential:** Medium — photo and text identification and will-call staging automate; the counter relationship does not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The counter is where residential, service and small commercial electricians buy daily. Between about 6:00 and 8:00 a.m. a busy branch serves dozens to more than a hundred walk-ins (~30–150, estimate) while its staff answer phones and texts from crews on site. The hard part is identification: breakers for old panels (Eaton's classified CL breakers are approved for nearly 750 other makers' loadcenters, each only in specific models and only up to 120/240 V and 10 kA, per Eaton's literature via search summary), lamps and ballasts for discontinued fixtures, fittings for a field change. The electrician values speed and a right first answer above price, and moves daily buying to the branch that delivers both. The contest differs from project quoting (planned, priced, bid-driven) and from emailed order intake (a document, not a person at the counter).

## Current Tools & Gaps
ERP point-of-sale and counter screens (Epicor Eclipse and others), manufacturer cross-reference apps, contractor texting inboxes such as Prokeep, e-commerce with branch pickup, and paper will-call tickets. The gaps: identification knowledge held by a few veterans, no photo lookup, field texts retyped, and no record of what was asked versus what was sold.

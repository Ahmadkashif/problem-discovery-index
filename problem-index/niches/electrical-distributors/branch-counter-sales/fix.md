# The Breaker That Fits the Bus but Not the Listing

**Niche:** [[niches/electrical-distributors/branch-counter-sales/profile|Branch Counter Sales to Contractors]]
**Industry:** [[industries/electrical-distributors|Electrical Distributors]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A breaker that physically snaps onto a panel is not necessarily listed or classified for it, and the counter is where that mistake starts.
**Tags:** #k-nearest-neighbors #large-language-models #compliance #quick-win #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get an electrician out of the branch with the right part during the early-morning rush, and whoever clears the 6:30 a.m. line with the fewest wrong-part return trips takes the account.

## The Problem
Electricians replacing breakers in old panels often ask for "whatever fits." Classified breakers are approved only for specific panel models and ratings, and a breaker that fits mechanically but isn't listed for the panel can fail inspection or worse.

## Why It's Still Broken
Compatibility tables are long PDFs; the counter has seconds.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A compatibility lookup by panel label or photo that returns only listed or classified options, with the limits shown, and refuses a match when the panel model is unknown.

## Who Feels the Pain
Electricians failing inspection, counter staff giving answers they can't check, and distributors exposed to returns and liability.

## Impact If Fixed
Correct, defensible breaker answers at counter speed. If ~1 in 20 old-panel breaker sales today is a fit without a listing (estimate), the lookup removes that failed-inspection and liability exposure.

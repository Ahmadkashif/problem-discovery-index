# The Controls the Energy Code Required and the Package Left Out

**Niche:** [[niches/electrical-distributors/lighting-desk-quoting-submittals/profile|The Lighting Desk: Package Quoting & Submittals]]
**Industry:** [[industries/electrical-distributors|Electrical Distributors]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The lighting package is quoted fixture by fixture, and the occupancy sensors and daylight controls the energy code requires show up only when the submittal is rejected.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #compliance #quick-win #workflow-orchestration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn a lighting fixture schedule into a priced package and a submittal the engineer approves on first review, and whoever gets the most fixture types approved without a resubmittal takes the account.

## The Problem
Commercial energy codes adopted by states (IECC or ASHRAE 90.1 editions, or California's Title 24) require lighting controls such as occupancy sensing, daylight-responsive dimming and automatic shutoff. The controls are often on a separate sheet or in the specification rather than the fixture schedule, so packages are quoted without them or with incompatible ones, and the gap surfaces at submittal review.

## Why It's Still Broken
The lighting desk prices what the schedule lists; reading the controls narrative and the adopted code edition takes time nobody has on bid day.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A check that reads the controls sections and sequence of operations, notes the jurisdiction's adopted energy code, and lists the control devices and fixture options the package must include, flagging driver and protocol incompatibilities before the quote goes out.

## Who Feels the Pain
Lighting specialists redoing packages, contractors absorbing unquoted controls, and engineers reviewing the same omission repeatedly.

## Impact If Fixed
Controls are priced in the bid rather than added after award; on a typical commercial job they run ~10–20% of the lighting package (estimate), money that otherwise comes out of the distributor's or contractor's margin.

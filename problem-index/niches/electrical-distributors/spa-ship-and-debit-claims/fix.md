# The Job That Slipped Past Its SPA Expiry

**Niche:** [[niches/electrical-distributors/spa-ship-and-debit-claims/profile|Manufacturer SPAs & Ship-and-Debit Claims]]
**Industry:** [[industries/electrical-distributors|Electrical Distributors]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The SPA was granted for a job that was supposed to release in March; the gear shipped in September and the claim was rejected as expired.
**Tags:** #survival-analysis #logistic-regression #quick-win #revenue-impact #compliance
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn every job-specific SPA the distributor quoted into a paid ship-and-debit credit — the 844 claim accepted on the manufacturer's 849 — before the agreement or claim window closes, and whoever gets the most SPA dollars paid on first submission takes the account.

## The Problem
Project schedules slip; SPA expiry dates don't move unless someone asks. When the job finally releases, the distributor sells at the SPA price and the claim is rejected, leaving a sale below cost.

## Why It's Still Broken
SPA expiries live in the ERP or a portal, job schedules live with the project manager, and nobody compares them.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A weekly check of open SPAs against unreleased orders and the job's expected release, with extension requests drafted to the manufacturer before expiry for any job still open.

## Who Feels the Pain
Controllers absorbing write-offs, project managers blamed for timing they don't control, and branch margins.

## Impact If Fixed
Extensions requested in advance are usually granted (estimate); recovering even ~1% of SPA credits now lost to expiry is ~$200K a year on ~$20M of credits (estimate).

## Why These Niches

Federal grants represent the largest funding pool but involve the most complex application requirements (grants.gov, SF-424 forms, CFDA compliance), while foundation grants require entirely different proposal formats and relationship management. Small nonprofit grant writers are the most digitally neglected — they serve organizations with $500K-5M budgets using Word documents and spreadsheet calendars. SBIR/STTR specialists serve a growing but underserved market where technical founders need grant writing help but can't find writers who understand deep tech. Rural and tribal communities face acute grant access inequality — they need the most funding but have the least grant-writing capacity. The automation niches target the two most time-intensive non-writing activities: finding eligible grants (currently a manual research process consuming 30-40% of a grant writer's time) and post-award compliance reporting (which most grant writers don't offer, leaving clients scrambling).

## Why These Pockets

Grant writing is the sharpest illustration in this sweep of why the gate exists. The operator layer has the ideal shape on every axis that matters — the written analysis *is* the invoice, the deadline is absolute and externally set, the work is repeatable, and the outcome is cleanly observable because you either win the grant or you do not. It fails only on size: a grant consulting firm is two to ten writers, and the research is done by whoever is on the proposal. There is no research function to sell to. The identical shape at fifty times the headcount is what the sweep is looking for, and in this industry it sits one position up, in the platforms that tell the operator layer where to apply.

One pocket qualified. Funder research platforms match nonprofits to opportunities using eligibility criteria and keyword overlap, and never learn which recommendations were won — a 40-80 hour investment made at a 10-25% success rate against a recommendation nobody has ever scored. The outcome data exists in published awards and in the users themselves; the platform simply never built the loop, because subscriptions renew on coverage rather than accuracy.

The structural finding is where the answer actually lives. Federal peer review administration holds every proposal, every reviewer score, and every summary statement — the definitive record of what gets funded and why — and is prohibited by confidentiality policy from letting anyone near it. Foundation programme offices hold the same thing at portfolio scale, and are grantmakers rather than buyers. The entire industry is trying to predict a decision whose complete history sits with two parties who cannot and will not sell. What the qualifying pocket has instead is the observable half — who applied and who won — which is enough, and is being ignored.

Two further positions came up thin. Association research arms exist but are small and philanthropically funded. The aggregator position is effectively absent: grant consulting has not consolidated, because there is nothing to consolidate but writers.

# Forecast Evaluation From Supply Chain Planning

**Industry:** [[industries/revops-consultancies|RevOps Consultancies]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Demand planning scores every forecast against actuals as routine practice, and revenue forecasting keeps nothing.

**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to improve a forecast whose accuracy nobody can measure, because the series required to score it is destroyed every week — and whoever makes it scoreable takes the account.

## The Problem
Demand planning has scored forecasts for decades because inventory decisions depend on them. Forecast versions are retained by horizon, accuracy is measured with standard metrics, bias is separated from error, forecast value added quantifies whether human adjustment improved the statistical baseline, and planners' track records are known. The discipline is mature and taught. Revenue forecasting asks the same question of the same kind of quantity and retains nothing.

## What Already Exists
Retained forecast versions by horizon; standard accuracy and bias metrics; forecast value added measurement of human adjustment; planner-level accuracy tracking; and consensus forecasting with documented overrides.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a forecast built from individual judgements about named deals. It requires: (1) a forecast composed of opportunity-level judgements by salespeople with a direct interest in the number, rather than a statistical baseline adjusted by planners — this is the substantive difference and makes the bias behavioural rather than methodological; (2) a quarterly rhythm with a hard close rather than rolling periods; (3) the forecast being a management instrument as well as a prediction, so scoring it changes behaviour; (4) far fewer, larger units than a demand forecast's many items; and (5) no retained history at all, so the practice must start from zero.

## Target Customer
Revenue operations leadership, RevOps consultancies, forecasting platform vendors, and planning and analytics providers.

## Impact If Solved
Demand planning scores every forecast because inventory depends on it, with mature metrics and forecast value added. A forecast composed of interested individual judgements about named deals is what makes the bias behavioural rather than methodological.

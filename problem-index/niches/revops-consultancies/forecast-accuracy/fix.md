# Nobody Kept Last Quarter's Forecast

**Niche:** [[niches/revops-consultancies/forecast-accuracy/profile|Forecast Accuracy]]
**Industry:** [[industries/revops-consultancies|RevOps Consultancies]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Asked how accurate the forecast was in week four last quarter, nobody can answer, because the number no longer exists.
**Tags:** #quick-win #data-integration #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #automation #confidence-intervals #revenue-impact #workflow-orchestration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to improve a forecast whose accuracy nobody can measure, because the series required to score it is destroyed every week — and whoever makes it scoreable takes the account.

## The Problem
The most basic question about a forecast — how wrong was it, and when — cannot be answered in most revenue organisations. The weekly numbers were in a spreadsheet that was overwritten, or a CRM field that was updated, or a slide in a deck nobody kept. There is no series. The discipline's entire improvement agenda rests on a measurement that has never been possible because the data was thrown away every seven days.

## Why It's Still Broken
The forecast is a field, not a record — a value updated in place leaves no history, and nobody thought to keep one because nobody was asked to score it. Spreadsheets are overwritten. Slides are archived unstructured. And the absence is only noticed when somebody asks the question.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Start keeping it this week, before designing anything. Snapshot the forecast fields on a schedule into a table, which is the fix and is an afternoon's work on any CRM. Capture at every level — representative, manager, segment, company — since the aggregate alone cannot locate bias. Include the pipeline alongside the forecast so movements can be explained later. Recover whatever history exists from archived spreadsheets, decks and emails, which usually yields a few quarters. Record the methodology in use at each point, so a later change can be evaluated. Keep the commit and best-case numbers separately rather than a single figure. Store it outside the CRM so it survives administration changes. Start scoring after one quarter rather than waiting for a long series, since even a few points are informative. Publish the first accuracy figures internally, which is what creates the demand for everything else. And keep it forever, because it costs nothing and the value compounds.

## Who Feels the Pain
Revenue leaders who cannot tell whether forecasting improved; consultancies unable to demonstrate their methodology; analysts asked questions the data cannot answer; and every methodology change adopted on conviction.

## Impact If Fixed
A value updated in place leaves no history, and nobody thought to keep one because nobody was asked to score it. An afternoon's snapshotting starts the series the entire discipline depends on.

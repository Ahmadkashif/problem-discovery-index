# Two Days a Month and No Memory Between Them

**Industry:** [[industries/revops-consultancies|RevOps Consultancies]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The first hours of every client day are spent remembering where everything was left.

## The Problem
A fractional practitioner arrives at a client after three weeks away. What was in progress, what was decided, what was promised, what broke while they were gone — all of it must be reconstructed from email, messages and memory before any work happens. Multiply by three clients and the reconstruction is a meaningful share of the billable time, and the client experiences it as a slow start rather than as a structural feature of the arrangement.

## Why It's Still Broken
Nothing captures the state at the end of a visit — context that exists only in the practitioner's head has to be reloaded every time, and the gap between visits guarantees it has faded. Notes are scattered. Clients do not maintain a record. And the reconstruction is billed, so it never surfaces as waste.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Close each visit by writing the state down. Keep a per-client working record — open items, decisions, promises, watch items — updated at the end of each visit, which is the fix and takes ten minutes. Note what should be checked on return, so the next visit opens with a list rather than a reconstruction. Set up automated monitoring of the client's key metrics between visits, so a problem is known rather than discovered. Ask the client to maintain a running list of items for the next visit, which most will do gladly. Review the record before arriving rather than on arrival, which moves the reconstruction out of billable time. Keep the record in a form the client can also see, which builds trust and reduces duplication. Capture decisions and their reasons, since those are what fade fastest and matter most. Standardise the record across clients so the habit holds. Use it in the engagement's reporting, which makes maintaining it obviously worthwhile. And treat the first hour of each visit as the review rather than the reconstruction.

## Who Feels the Pain
Practitioners rebuilding context three times a month; clients paying for it; things that broke between visits and were not noticed; and the quality of decisions made from partial memory.

## Impact If Fixed
Context that exists only in the practitioner's head has to be reloaded every time, and the gap between visits guarantees it has faded. Ten minutes of state capture at the end of each visit converts reconstruction into review.

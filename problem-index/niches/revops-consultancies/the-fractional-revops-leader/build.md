**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to let one person run an entire revenue operations function part-time across several companies — and whoever equips them takes the account.

## The Problem
A fractional RevOps leader is responsible for everything a revenue operations function does — systems, reporting, forecasting, process, enablement — for several companies at once, on a few days each per month. Every tool they use is built for a full-time person in one company. They rebuild the same reports, playbooks and processes for each client, carry the context in their head between visits, and have nobody to check their judgement.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The fractional model grew fast and no vendor has recognised it as a market. Tooling licences and access are per company. The practitioner is an individual buyer with a small budget. And the fragmentation is invisible to the clients, who each see a competent person.

## What to Build
Design for one person across several stacks. Provide a working environment spanning several clients' systems with fast switching and per-client context retained, which is the core and addresses the defining condition of the role. Maintain reusable templates — reporting packs, process documents, playbooks, system configurations — that deploy into any client's stack, since rebuilding them per client is most of the recoverable time. Keep a per-client state record so a visit resumes rather than restarts. Automate the recurring work — reporting, hygiene checks, forecast assembly — so the days on site are spent on judgement. Provide benchmarks across the practitioner's own clients, which is a perspective no full-time person has and is genuinely valuable to each client. Connect fractional practitioners to one another for peer review, which is the missing professional structure. Handle the access and licensing awkwardness of one person across several tenants. Let them demonstrate results across clients in a form that wins the next engagement. Support the commercial side — scoping, pricing, contracting — which is a second job they also do alone. And price it for an individual practitioner, which is the binding constraint.

## Target Customer
Fractional RevOps practitioners, small and mid-sized companies using them, RevOps consultancies with fractional offerings, and practitioner tooling vendors.

## Impact If Built
Every tool assumes a full-time person in one company, and the role is one person across three. A multi-client environment with deployable templates addresses the defining condition rather than working around it.

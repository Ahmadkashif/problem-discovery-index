# The Representative Who Is Always Optimistic

**Niche:** [[niches/revops-consultancies/methodology-and-scoring/profile|Forecast Methodology & Scoring]]
**Industry:** [[industries/revops-consultancies|RevOps Consultancies]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Everyone knows which representatives overcall their number and nobody has quantified it or adjusted for it.
**Tags:** #quick-win #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #hypothesis-testing #revenue-impact #time-series-forecasting #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to establish which forecasting method actually predicts best, and where the bias sits, using a retained history — and whoever scores it takes the account.

## The Problem
Forecast bias is personal and consistent. Some representatives systematically overcall, some undercall, and their managers know it informally and adjust by instinct in the commit call. The adjustment is inconsistent, undocumented and frequently wrong in magnitude. A measured bias per person would let the adjustment be made once, correctly, and would improve the company forecast immediately without changing anyone's methodology.

## Why It's Still Broken
The bias is known and never measured — an adjustment made by instinct in a meeting produces no record, so it is neither consistent nor improvable, and the underlying bias is never quantified. There is no retained history. Measuring individual bias is politically sensitive. And the manager's instinct works well enough to avoid the conversation.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Compute the bias per person and apply it consistently. Measure each representative's historical forecast against their actual, by horizon, which is the fix and needs only a few quarters of retained data. Express it as a multiplier rather than a judgement, so the adjustment is consistent. Apply it to the rolled-up forecast rather than asking anyone to change their behaviour, which is faster and less contentious. Separate bias from noise, since a consistently optimistic representative is correctable and an erratic one is not. Look at manager-level bias too, as the adjustment layer has its own. Break it out by deal size and stage, where the pattern usually differs. Share it privately with each person, which is coaching rather than accusation and is where the behaviour actually changes. Recompute each quarter, as bias shifts with experience and territory. Report the improvement the adjustment delivered, which justifies the whole practice. And be explicit that the purpose is a better company forecast rather than a performance judgement, which determines whether it is accepted.

## Who Feels the Pain
Revenue leaders committing to a number adjusted by instinct; managers making the same correction every week without a figure; representatives judged informally on a bias nobody told them about; and the board, receiving a forecast with a known and uncorrected error.

## Impact If Fixed
An adjustment made by instinct in a meeting produces no record, so it is neither consistent nor improvable. A measured multiplier per representative improves the company forecast without changing anyone's behaviour.

# The Territory Nobody Could Have Made Quota In

**Niche:** [[niches/revops-consultancies/territory-and-quota/profile|Territory & Quota Design]]
**Industry:** [[industries/revops-consultancies|RevOps Consultancies]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The representative missed quota, left, and their replacement missed it too, in the same territory, for the same reason.
**Tags:** #quick-win #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #revenue-impact #hypothesis-testing #worker-facing #optimization-fundamentals
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to set territories and quotas that are actually attainable across a sales team, when the method is splitting last year's number in a spreadsheet — and whoever models it takes the account.

## The Problem
Some territories cannot produce their quota. The representative in one misses, is managed out or leaves, and the replacement — hired, onboarded and ramped at considerable cost — misses the same number for the same reason. The signal is unmistakable in the data: consecutive occupants of the same territory failing while others consistently exceed. Nobody looks at attainment by territory across occupants, so the territory is never suspected and the people are.

## Why It's Still Broken
Attainment is reported per person — a number attributed to whoever holds the territory can never reveal that the territory is the problem, because the territory is not the unit of analysis anywhere. Turnover masks the pattern. Managers attribute to capability. And the design is not revisited mid-year.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Report attainment by territory across occupants, not by person. Plot attainment by territory over several years and several occupants, which is the fix and is a query that names the problem outright. Flag territories where consecutive occupants missed, since that is the clearest possible evidence about the territory rather than the person. Compare territory attainment against a rough potential estimate — account count, segment, installed base — even a crude one. Calculate the replacement cost incurred in the flagged territories, which is the number that gets attention. Rebalance mid-year where a design is clearly wrong rather than waiting for the annual exercise. Adjust the quota rather than the person where the evidence points that way. Record the territory's history so a new manager inherits the context. Look at the top performers' territories too, as systematic over-attainment is the same design failure in the other direction. Share the analysis with sales leadership before the next planning cycle. And separate territory quality from representative performance in every review, which is the durable change.

## Who Feels the Pain
Representatives managed out of unwinnable territories; managers replacing people repeatedly for a structural reason; finance paying recruitment costs for a design problem; and the organisation, losing capable people to arithmetic.

## Impact If Fixed
A number attributed to whoever holds the territory can never reveal that the territory is the problem, because the territory is not the unit of analysis anywhere. Plotting attainment by territory across occupants names it in one chart.

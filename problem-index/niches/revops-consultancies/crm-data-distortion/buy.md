**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to quantify the specific ways CRM data is distorted by the people entering it, because every model built on it inherits those distortions silently — and whoever measures them takes the account.

## The Problem
Survey methodology developed a detailed science of response bias: social desirability, acquiescence, satisficing, anchoring, and the systematic ways in which the conditions of answering distort the answer. It also built detection and correction techniques and a professional norm that an instrument's biases must be understood before its results are used. CRM data is a survey completed under incentive pressure by people whose compensation depends on the answers, analysed as though it were observation.

## What Already Exists
Catalogued response bias mechanisms; detection through response pattern analysis; correction and weighting techniques; instrument design to reduce bias; and a norm of characterising bias before analysis.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to an instrument completed continuously by people with direct financial stakes in the values. It requires: (1) respondents whose compensation depends on the recorded values, which is a stronger and more systematic incentive than any survey context — this is the substantive difference and makes the distortion strategic rather than psychological; (2) an instrument completed over months with values revised repeatedly, so the revision pattern itself is evidence; (3) distortions that are organisationally sanctioned, since a manager asking for a stage change is management rather than fraud; (4) no possibility of an unbiased comparison sample; and (5) findings that implicate the incentive design, which is a leadership conversation rather than a methodological one.

## Target Customer
Revenue operations leadership, RevOps consultancies, CRM vendors, and survey methodology consultancies.

## Impact If Solved
Survey methodology catalogued how the conditions of answering distort the answer and made characterising bias a norm. Respondents whose compensation depends on the values makes the distortion strategic rather than psychological, which is what has to be modelled.

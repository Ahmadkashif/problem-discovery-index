# Customer Attrition Estimated From a Single Spreadsheet

**Niche:** [[niches/investment-banking-boutiques/financial-reporting-valuation/profile|Financial Reporting Valuation (Purchase Price Allocation & Impairment)]]
**Industry:** [[industries/investment-banking-boutiques|Investment Banking Boutiques]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The most sensitive assumption in many purchase price allocations is estimated from a few years of customer revenue in one spreadsheet.
**Tags:** #survival-analysis #confidence-intervals #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious valuation firm in this pocket is fighting to deliver assumptions the acquirer's auditor accepts on the first review before the reporting deadline — and whoever can evidence each assumption from its own engagement record takes the recurring work.

## The Problem
Customer relationship value is driven by an attrition curve fitted, often crudely, to limited data.

## Why It's Still Broken
Time pressure and thin data; methods default to a single rate.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Standard survival analysis on the acquired customer data with cohort curves and intervals, and the firm's sector priors used where history is short.

## Who Feels the Pain
Valuers defending the number; auditors reviewing it.

## Impact If Fixed
A more defensible intangible value with less review back-and-forth.

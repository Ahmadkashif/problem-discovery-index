# Assumption Ranges Defended One Engagement at a Time

**Industry:** [[industries/investment-banking-boutiques|Investment Banking Boutiques]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A valuation practice has selected customer attrition, royalty and discount rates on thousands of engagements and still defends each new one to the auditor from scratch.
**Tags:** #survival-analysis #linear-regression #k-nearest-neighbors #confidence-intervals #evaluation-metrics #compliance #tacit-knowledge-ml
**Contested on:** Every serious valuation firm in this pocket is fighting to deliver assumptions the acquirer's auditor accepts on the first review before the reporting deadline — and whoever can evidence each assumption from its own engagement record takes the recurring work.

## The Problem
A purchase price allocation values customer relationships, technology and trade names using assumptions that are partly judgment: an attrition rate, a royalty rate, an obsolescence curve. Senior valuers carry a feel for the defensible range in each sector. Auditors' valuation specialists challenge the same assumptions on every engagement.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Engagement files are archived per client; the assumption and the auditor's challenge sit in reports and emails, not in a database.

## What to Build
A de-identified ledger of every assumption, its rationale and the auditor's response, with nearest-neighbour retrieval of comparable engagements and observed ranges, and — where acquired customer data permits — attrition estimated from the target's own history with survival methods rather than asserted.

## Target Customer
Valuation practice leaders at independent valuation and accounting firms.

## Impact If Built
Fewer review cycles before the filing deadline and more consistent conclusions across teams.

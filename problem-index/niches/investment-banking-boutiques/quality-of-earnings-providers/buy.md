# Audit Analytics Built for a Deal Clock

**Niche:** [[niches/investment-banking-boutiques/quality-of-earnings-providers/profile|Quality of Earnings & Financial Due Diligence Providers]]
**Industry:** [[industries/investment-banking-boutiques|Investment Banking Boutiques]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Audit data analytics ingests general ledgers and tests populations; a QoE team needs the same ingestion in days, on a target whose books were never built for scrutiny.
**Tags:** #gradient-boosting #change-point-detection #feature-engineering #data-integration #evaluation-metrics #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious QoE provider is fighting to deliver an adjusted EBITDA that survives the other side's diligence inside the banker's timetable — and whoever can show, from its own engagement record, which adjustments hold up takes the next mandate from the same sponsors and bankers.

## The Problem
The first week of a QoE is spent getting the target's general ledger, trial balances and sub-ledgers into a usable state, mapping accounts and building monthly trends.

## What Already Exists
Audit analytics platforms (MindBridge, the Big Four's proprietary tools, Caseware IDEA) for ledger ingestion, anomaly scoring and journal-entry testing.

## The Customization Gap
QoE needs monthly EBITDA bridges, revenue by customer and product, working capital seasonality and run-rate analysis, not audit assertions; it must map messy owner-operated charts of accounts to a standard deal chart in hours; and it must flag non-recurring candidates and trend breaks as adjustment candidates rather than as audit exceptions.

## Target Customer
QoE teams at accounting firms and specialists.

## Impact If Solved
Compresses the data-preparation week, which is the single most common cause of QoE reports arriving after the banker's deadline.

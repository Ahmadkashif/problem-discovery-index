# Sell-Side M&A Advisory

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/investment-banking-boutiques|Investment Banking Boutiques]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Not terminal as stated — every competitor is fighting to deliver the highest certain price, but a sponsor selling its fifth portfolio company and a founder selling the only company they will ever own buy that outcome on different evidence, from different banks, and the two contests are stated in the sub-niches below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$10B a year in mid-market and independent sell-side fees, as an estimate
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~45% of category fees
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — licensed data and virtual data rooms, hand-built deliverables
**Target Buyer:** Head of M&A / managing partner
**Automation Potential:** High — the process is repeatable in shape across every mandate

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Running a company sale is the core product of almost every boutique: a teaser, a CIM, a buyer universe, a two-round auction, and a negotiated purchase agreement, typically over four to nine months, paid largely as a success fee. It is the largest fee pool in the category and the one in which the firm's process history accumulates.

It is **not terminal**. The client changes the contest. A private equity sponsor exiting a portfolio company has sold businesses before, knows the buyers, measures banks on speed, certainty and the price achieved against its own expectations, and hires repeatedly. A founder or family selling an owner-operated company sells once, chooses the bank on trust and the valuation it is promised, and needs the bank to prepare a business that has never been diligenced. Different buyers of the service, different evidence, different winners.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Capital IQ, PitchBook and FactSet for screens and comps; DealCloud or Salesforce for pipeline; Datasite or Intralinks for the data room; Excel trackers for buyers. The gaps: no cross-process buyer memory, no measure of how well a buyer list performed, and diligence workflow treated as tickets rather than as evidence.

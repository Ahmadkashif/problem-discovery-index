# Which Sponsors Will Actually Bid

**Niche:** [[niches/investment-banking-boutiques/sponsor-exit-processes/profile|Sponsor Exit Processes]]
**Industry:** [[industries/investment-banking-boutiques|Investment Banking Boutiques]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** The same few hundred sponsors appear in every mid-market exit, and the bank that has watched them all can predict who bids — if it ever assembled what it watched.
**Tags:** #tacit-knowledge-ml #gradient-boosting #survival-analysis #graph-theory #evaluation-metrics #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to tell a sponsor, before the process launches, which buyers will actually bid and at what price — and whoever proves that from their own process record wins the next exit from the same fund.

## The Problem
In a sponsor-to-sponsor process the buyer list is mostly other sponsors. Some take every meeting and rarely bid; some bid aggressively in sectors where they have an existing platform; some are constrained by fund life or concentration limits the bank can infer but rarely does. Senior bankers carry this as instinct.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The evidence is spread across hundreds of deal trackers in inconsistent formats; sponsor, fund and platform-company identities are tangled; and bankers' buyer knowledge is their personal franchise.

## What to Build
A sponsor-behaviour model trained on the firm's own process history, enriched with fund vintage, dry powder, platform hold periods and add-on activity: for a new exit, each sponsor's probability of reaching LOI and bidding in the final round, with the firm's interaction history shown beside every name. Track model-suggested names the MD would not have added, and contact a few deliberately, so the model can learn what the expert does not already know.

## Target Customer
Sponsor-coverage groups at mid-market boutiques running repeated exits for the same funds.

## Impact If Built
A buyer list the sponsor could not have written itself is the decisive argument in a bake-off, and it is built from data only this bank owns.

# Price Guidance Nobody Checks

**Niche:** [[niches/investment-banking-boutiques/sponsor-exit-processes/profile|Sponsor Exit Processes]]
**Industry:** [[industries/investment-banking-boutiques|Investment Banking Boutiques]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Every pitch to a sponsor includes a valuation range, and no bank keeps a record of how its ranges compared with the prices it eventually achieved.
**Tags:** #confidence-intervals #linear-regression #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #quick-win #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to tell a sponsor, before the process launches, which buyers will actually bid and at what price — and whoever proves that from their own process record wins the next exit from the same fund.

## The Problem
The pitch's valuation page is the number the sponsor remembers. Banks are tempted to pitch high to win, and sponsors know it, so guidance is discounted by both sides without either having data.

## Why It's Still Broken
Pitch ranges are in pitch books; outcomes are in closing files; nobody joins them, and a bank's guidance accuracy is not something it wants to be held to.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Record every pitched range and the eventual clearing price, by sector and MD, and report calibration internally: how often outcomes fell inside, above or below the range. Use it to anchor guidance and, where the record is good, to show sponsors.

## Who Feels the Pain
Sponsors setting exit expectations with their investment committees; MDs whose credibility rides on the number.

## Impact If Fixed
Honest, calibrated guidance becomes a differentiator in a bake-off where every bank claims it will achieve the highest price.

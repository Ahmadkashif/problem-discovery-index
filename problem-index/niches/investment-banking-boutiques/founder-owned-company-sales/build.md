# The Re-Trade Predictor

**Niche:** [[niches/investment-banking-boutiques/founder-owned-company-sales/profile|Founder-Owned Company Sales]]
**Industry:** [[industries/investment-banking-boutiques|Investment Banking Boutiques]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A bank that has sold two hundred founder-owned companies has watched which issues caused buyers to cut price after the LOI, and does not use that record to prepare the next one.
**Tags:** #gradient-boosting #logistic-regression #large-language-models #evaluation-metrics #feature-engineering #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to make an undiligenced owner-operated business survive a buyer's diligence at the price it was marketed at — and whoever closes at the headline number instead of re-trading wins the referrals that source the next founder.

## The Problem
Re-trades follow recognisable causes — customer concentration understated, add-backs that do not survive scrutiny, working capital peg disputes, pending litigation, key-person dependence. Experienced bankers sense them early; less experienced teams find them when the buyer's QoE lands.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Re-trade histories sit in emails and negotiation notes; boutiques rarely record the price bridge from LOI to close with reasons.

## What to Build
A record of every LOI-to-close price bridge with its stated causes, joined to the pre-launch profile of each company, and a model that flags the issues most likely to cause a re-trade in a new engagement before the CIM is written — so they are fixed, disclosed or priced in at launch.

## Target Customer
Lower and middle-market boutiques for which founder sales are the core business.

## Impact If Built
Closing at the marketed price is the outcome founders refer others for; pre-empting re-trades protects both the client's proceeds and the bank's success fee.

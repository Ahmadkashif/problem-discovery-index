# Founder-Owned Company Sales

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/investment-banking-boutiques|Investment Banking Boutiques]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to make an undiligenced owner-operated business survive a buyer's diligence at the price it was marketed at — and whoever closes at the headline number instead of re-trading wins the referrals that source the next founder.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$4.5B a year in sell-side fees on founder- and family-owned company sales, as an estimate
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~20% of category fees
**Digital Adoption:** Low — the client's own financial records are often the weakest link
**Target Buyer:** Managing partner of a lower- and middle-market boutique
**Automation Potential:** Medium — preparation work is repeatable, the client relationship is not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A founder sells once. They choose a bank on trust, referral and the valuation it promises, and they arrive with financials prepared for tax rather than for a buyer: add-backs undocumented, revenue recognition inconsistent, customer contracts in a drawer. The bank's real work is preparation — sell-side quality of earnings, normalised EBITDA, a defensible story — and the failure mode is the re-trade, where a buyer signs an LOI at the marketed price and cuts it after diligence finds what preparation missed. The federal M&A broker exemption that took effect in 2023 has made it easier for smaller intermediaries to run these sales, widening the competitive field at the bottom.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Client accounting in QuickBooks or a mid-market ERP; sell-side QoE from an accounting firm; Excel normalisation; generic data rooms. The gap is the bank's own record of which adjustments buyers accepted and which caused re-trades.

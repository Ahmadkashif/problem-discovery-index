# Add-Backs Defended From Memory

**Niche:** [[niches/investment-banking-boutiques/founder-owned-company-sales/profile|Founder-Owned Company Sales]]
**Industry:** [[industries/investment-banking-boutiques|Investment Banking Boutiques]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Every founder sale argues for adjusted EBITDA through a list of add-backs, and the bank has no record of which kinds of add-back buyers accepted last time.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #logistic-regression #large-language-models #evaluation-metrics #quick-win #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to make an undiligenced owner-operated business survive a buyer's diligence at the price it was marketed at — and whoever closes at the headline number instead of re-trading wins the referrals that source the next founder.

## The Problem
Owner compensation, one-off legal costs, a discontinued product line, pro-forma pricing — each add-back raises EBITDA and therefore price, and each is argued afresh with every buyer.

## Why It's Still Broken
Acceptance outcomes are buried in negotiation and the buyer's QoE; the bank never codifies them.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A taxonomy of add-back types and a record, per closed deal, of which were accepted, haircut or rejected by the eventual buyer — used to set an evidence-based adjusted EBITDA at launch and to prepare documentation for the contested items.

## Who Feels the Pain
Founders whose proceeds depend on it; deal teams negotiating from instinct.

## Impact If Fixed
A marketed EBITDA that survives diligence is the single biggest protection against a re-trade.

# The Banking Analyst

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/investment-banking-boutiques|Investment Banking Boutiques]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor serving the analyst is fighting to turn an MD's markup into a correct, on-template page before morning — and whoever does that from the firm's own archive rather than from a generic template becomes the tool the analyst class refuses to work without.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$600M addressable annual spend on analyst productivity tooling and outsourced production support across boutiques and middle-market banks, as an estimate
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~3% of category fees, against a far larger share of category labour cost
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — power users of Excel and PowerPoint plug-ins, little else
**Target Buyer:** COO / head of the analyst programme
**Automation Potential:** High — page production is repetitive and template-bound

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The analyst is the production layer of the industry: comps, models, pitch pages, CIM drafts, buyer lists, tie-outs. Their tools are bought by someone else, their hours are treated as free, and their work is mostly reconstruction of pages that already exist somewhere in the firm. They are underserved because tooling has been sold to MDs and to the firm, while the analyst's actual bottleneck — finding and adapting prior work, and absorbing rounds of markup — has no product.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Macabacus, UpSlide, FactSet and Capital IQ Office plug-ins; shared drives; newer generative tools such as Rogo under pilot. The gap is firm-archive retrieval and markup learning.

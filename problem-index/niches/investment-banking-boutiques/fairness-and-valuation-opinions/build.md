# The Opinion Consistency Ledger

**Niche:** [[niches/investment-banking-boutiques/fairness-and-valuation-opinions/profile|Fairness & Valuation Opinions]]
**Industry:** [[industries/investment-banking-boutiques|Investment Banking Boutiques]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A firm that has issued hundreds of fairness opinions cannot readily show which discount rates, peer sets and premiums it used in each, and that is exactly what a plaintiff will ask.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #k-nearest-neighbors #large-language-models #confidence-intervals #compliance #evaluation-metrics
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to deliver an opinion whose assumptions survive a Delaware plaintiff's cross-examination — and whoever can show its analysis is consistent with its own prior opinions takes the special-committee engagement.

## The Problem
Each opinion selects a WACC range, terminal multiple, peer group and control premium. Over years these choices drift with the team and the market. In litigation, inconsistency with the firm's own prior opinions is a line of attack.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Past opinions exist as PDF committee books and Excel files; extracting assumptions retroactively is tedious and nobody owns it.

## What to Build
Extract every assumption from past committee books and models into a structured ledger; when a new opinion is drafted, show the nearest prior opinions by sector, size and date and flag assumptions outside the firm's own historical range, requiring a written rationale.

## Target Customer
Opinion practices at independent valuation firms and boutiques.

## Impact If Built
Makes every opinion defensible against the firm's own record and shortens committee review.

# Fairness & Valuation Opinions

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/investment-banking-boutiques|Investment Banking Boutiques]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to deliver an opinion whose assumptions survive a Delaware plaintiff's cross-examination — and whoever can show its analysis is consistent with its own prior opinions takes the special-committee engagement.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1B a year in fairness, solvency and independent valuation opinion fees, as an estimate
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~5% of category fees
**Digital Adoption:** Low — committee review of Excel analyses and printed books
**Target Buyer:** Head of opinions practice / fairness committee chair
**Automation Potential:** Medium — analyses are standardised, judgment and liability are not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A fairness opinion tells a board or special committee that the consideration in a transaction is fair from a financial point of view. Independent providers — Houlihan Lokey, Kroll, Stout and many boutiques — are hired precisely because they have no success fee in the deal. Every opinion passes an internal committee — FINRA Rule 5150 requires member firms to disclose whether it did — and is summarised in the proxy, where plaintiffs' lawyers read it. Delaware litigation over adviser conflicts and analyses (the Rural/Metro decisions are the standard reference) shapes how carefully every assumption is documented.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Capital IQ and FactSet data; Kroll's cost of capital data; Excel DCF and comps; Word and PowerPoint committee books. The gap is consistency: discount rates, peer sets and premiums chosen across hundreds of past opinions are not queryable, so the firm cannot easily show that this opinion is consistent with its last ten in the sector.

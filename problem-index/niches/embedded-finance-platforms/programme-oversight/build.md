# Accountable for What You Cannot See

**Niche:** [[niches/embedded-finance-platforms/programme-oversight/profile|Programme Oversight]]
**Industry:** [[industries/embedded-finance-platforms|Embedded Finance Platforms]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** The platform is now accountable for how dozens of fintech programmes treat their customers, and can see only the API calls those programmes make.
**Tags:** #compliance #gradient-boosting #change-point-detection #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #workflow-orchestration #graph-theory #automation
**Contested on:** This niche is not terminal — detecting what a programme is doing and demonstrating that it was overseen are different contests with different winners, and they are stated separately in the sub-niches.

## The Problem
A programme markets an account to consumers, designs the onboarding, writes the terms, sets the fees and handles the support. The platform provides the accounts, cards and payments through an interface, and sees a stream of calls. If the programme's marketing is misleading, its fee disclosure inadequate, its onboarding discriminatory or its customer treatment poor, the platform is now expected to know — and its entire window onto the programme is a sequence of account creations, transfers and card authorisations. The obligation is real and the visibility is structurally partial, which is the defining condition of this layer.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The three-party structure separated responsibility, visibility and control at its inception and nobody engineered the oversight because until the consent orders nobody was required to — the arrangement was designed for developer velocity and the compliance model was assumed rather than built. The platform's instinct is to treat programmes as customers rather than as supervised entities. Monitoring tooling is built for a bank's own retail customers and does not fit. And the obligation arrived faster than the capability.

## What to Build
Build oversight that works from the outside. Infer conduct from behaviour rather than from documents, which is the first sub-niche's contest and is the only route when the app and the marketing are invisible. Produce the evidence once and satisfy every partner from it, which is the second sub-niche and is what makes the obligation operable at scale. Use the cross-programme comparison, since a programme whose abandonment, dispute or fee patterns differ from every comparable programme on the same rails is the strongest available signal and only the platform can see it. Establish a risk-based oversight model rather than uniform monitoring, since dozens of programmes with different products cannot receive identical attention and pretending otherwise means none get enough. Obtain the things that cannot be inferred — marketing, terms, disclosures, complaint data — contractually, since some of the obligation genuinely requires artefacts the API does not carry. Define what good looks like for each programme archetype, which is the standard against which anything can be judged and which the category has not written down. Intervene proportionately, with graduated responses short of termination, because the only current lever is ending a programme and that harms its customers most. Give the bank partner a live view rather than a report, which reduces their work and is a genuine relationship improvement. Handle the programme's own obligations to its customers, since the end customer's protection depends on the weakest party in the chain. And report oversight coverage and findings honestly, because a platform that can evidence real findings is in a far better position than one evidencing a process.

## Target Customer
Platform compliance and risk leadership, sponsor banks whose obligation flows down, and the regulators whose expectations of this layer are forming.

## Impact If Built
The arrangement was designed for developer velocity and the compliance model was assumed rather than built, then an obligation arrived for it. Inferring conduct from cross-programme behavioural comparison is the only route when the app and the marketing are invisible, and it is a view only the platform has.

# Programme Oversight

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/embedded-finance-platforms|Embedded Finance Platforms]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** This niche is not terminal — detecting what a programme is doing and demonstrating that it was overseen are different contests with different winners, and they are stated separately in the sub-niches below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$2.1B US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~26% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Low — an obligation without an apparatus
**Target Buyer:** Platform compliance and risk leadership
**Automation Potential:** High — inference and evidence are both automatable

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The platform is now accountable for how dozens of fintech programmes treat their customers, and can see only the API calls those programmes make. That obligation arrived through supervisory action against the banks above it and was pushed down a layer that has neither the customer relationship nor the charter. It is the largest concentration of contested value in the category because a platform that cannot demonstrate oversight cannot keep bank partners, and a platform that cannot keep bank partners has no product.

It is also **not terminal**. Two problems live here. Detecting what a programme is doing to its customers — from transaction patterns, API sequences and outcomes, without ever seeing the app, the marketing or the conversation — is an inference problem with a detection objective. Demonstrating that oversight occurred, to banks who each specify it differently and to examiners who will judge it, is an evidence generation and partner management problem where the winner is whoever makes one substantive record satisfy every partner. Detection and documentation need different skills and are frequently owned by different teams. The two contests are stated in the sub-niches.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Transaction monitoring built for a bank's own customers, programme-level rules, periodic reviews, and bespoke reporting per bank partner. The gaps: conduct inferred from API calls with no view of the customer experience; alerts calibrated for one product applied to all; evidence rebuilt per partner; oversight demonstrated by process documentation rather than by findings; and the platform accountable for what it cannot see.

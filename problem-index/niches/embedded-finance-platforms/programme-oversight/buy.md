## The Problem
Overseeing an entity you do not control is a developed banking discipline. Third-party risk management frameworks cover due diligence before engagement, risk tiering, ongoing monitoring proportionate to risk, contractual right-to-audit, issue tracking to closure, and documented evidence of the whole cycle. Regulators require it and examine it. Embedded finance platforms have acquired an equivalent obligation over their programmes and are largely building the practice from first principles under time pressure.

## What Already Exists
Third-party risk management frameworks; risk tiering and proportionate monitoring; due diligence and onboarding assessment; contractual audit rights and issue management; and examination-ready evidence practice.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a third party whose conduct is observable only through an interface. It requires: (1) monitoring by behavioural inference rather than by attestation and audit, since the practice assumes questionnaires and site visits and here the evidence is a transaction stream — this inference layer is the substantive addition and is where the platform's advantage lies; (2) dozens or hundreds of programmes rather than a manageable vendor list, which forces automation where the practice assumes analyst attention per relationship; (3) a three-party structure where the platform oversees on behalf of a bank that is itself overseen, so the evidence must satisfy two levels; (4) programmes that are customers, which makes the commercial relationship and the supervisory one the same and creates a conflict the practice does not contemplate; and (5) the end customer's protection depending on the chain, which raises the stakes above vendor risk.

## Target Customer
Platform compliance leadership, sponsor bank oversight teams, and third-party risk vendors for whom the embedded finance structure is an unserved case.

## Impact If Solved
Banks built the practice because regulators required oversight of parties they do not control, and this layer inherited the obligation without it. Monitoring by behavioural inference rather than by attestation is the addition, and it is what the platform's transaction view uniquely enables.

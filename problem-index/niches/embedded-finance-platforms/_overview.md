# Niche Analysis — Embedded Finance Platforms

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/embedded-finance-platforms|Embedded Finance Platforms]]

## Niche Selection

These platforms expose accounts, cards, ledgers and payments as clean developer primitives on top of a compliance model that was never engineered, sitting between a programme that owns the customer and a bank that owns the regulatory obligation. The supervisory conclusion of 2023 and 2024 was that banks must oversee programmes they had not been overseeing, and that obligation has landed on a layer that can observe programme conduct only as API calls. Meanwhile the platform sees the full behaviour of dozens of distinct financial products built on identical infrastructure — a comparison set no bank possesses — and uses it for billing and incident response. The eight niches below split the category by what competitors actually fight over.

## Why These Niches

Programme oversight takes the largest share because it is the obligation the supervisory action created and the one the platform is least structurally equipped to meet. Ledger integrity is second because the failure mode is people losing access to their own deposits, which is the event that reshaped the category. The two low-digitized niches are the ones rebuilt by hand every time: a programme configuration assembled bespoke across twenty recurring archetypes, and a bank relationship that is now the binding constraint on whether a programme can exist. The two underserved audiences are the handful of analysts responsible for dozens of independently run financial products and the solutions engineer carrying bank-specific knowledge in their head. The two automatable niches are the cross-programme comparison set nobody exploits and the end customer whose protection depends on a chain of three parties.

## Filter Notes

Seven of the eight level-1 niches are terminal. Programme Oversight is not: the label names a responsibility rather than a contest, and writing the contested statement produces two sentences describing different winners. Conduct monitoring asks what a programme is actually doing to its customers, inferred from API calls and transaction patterns by a party that never sees the marketing, the app or the conversation — an inference problem with a detection objective. Compliance evidence asks how to demonstrate that oversight happened, to banks who each specify it differently and examiners who will judge it — an evidence generation and partner management problem where the winner is whoever makes one substantive record satisfy every partner. One is detection and the other is documentation; they need different skills and are frequently owned by different teams. It therefore decomposes into **Conduct Monitoring** and **Compliance Evidence**.

Two candidates were considered and rejected. **Card network and payment rails** underlie the product but the contest over them belongs to [[industries/payment-processors|Payment Processors]]. **The fintech programmes themselves** — the consumer-facing products built on these platforms — are the contest of [[industries/neobanks|Neobanks]], where they are analysed on their own terms.

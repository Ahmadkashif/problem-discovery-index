# Protections as a Platform Property

**Niche:** [[niches/embedded-finance-platforms/end-customer-protection/profile|End-Customer Protection]]
**Industry:** [[industries/embedded-finance-platforms|Embedded Finance Platforms]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** The end customer's protections depend on how their particular programme implemented them, when the platform could guarantee them for everyone.
**Tags:** #compliance #workflow-orchestration #automation #evaluation-metrics #change-point-detection #descriptive-statistics #data-integration #gradient-boosting
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to guarantee the end customer's baseline protections — access to funds, disputes, disclosures, error resolution — regardless of which programme they signed up with, and whoever makes those protections a property of the platform rather than of the programme takes the risk the whole category carries.

## The Problem
A customer's account is frozen. The programme's support is a form that is not answered. The bank does not know the customer exists as anything but a sub-ledger line. The platform can see the freeze, the balance, and the fact that nothing has happened for three weeks, and has built no mechanism to act on any of it. Multiply by every programme that implemented dispute handling as a backlog, error resolution as an intention, and closure notice as an afterthought, and the category has an exposure whose size nobody has measured.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Protections were contracted to the programme operator, so enforcement looked like a legal matter rather than a product one — the obligation was assigned and the mechanism was never built. Platforms position themselves as infrastructure and enforcement feels like overreach into a customer's product. There is no route from the end customer to the platform by design. And the exposure has been realised episodically rather than continuously, which keeps it off the roadmap.

## What to Build
Make the baseline enforceable in the platform. Define the protections the platform will guarantee regardless of programme — resolution timeframes for frozen funds, dispute acknowledgement and decision windows, disclosure of fees before assessment, notice and fund return on closure — which is the core and is a decision the category has avoided making. Instrument each protection from the data the platform already has, since freezes, disputes, fee postings and closures are all visible as events and their timeliness is computable. Enforce timeframes automatically, escalating or acting when a programme does not, because an obligation with no consequence is an intention. Measure protection performance per programme, which turns a contractual clause into a monitored metric and feeds conduct monitoring directly. Provide a route for the end customer to reach the platform, since the absence of one is the single clearest failure and is a product decision rather than a technical constraint. Handle the programme that goes dark, because the customers of a failed programme are the worst case and nobody has a plan. Return funds on a defined timeline when a programme cannot or will not, which is the protection that matters most. Report protection metrics to sponsor banks, since they carry the exposure and cannot see it. Make the guarantees public, because a stated baseline is what makes the category trustworthy and is a competitive asset. And track outcomes rather than compliance with process, since the customer's experience is the only real measure.

## Target Customer
Platform risk and product leadership, sponsor banks carrying the exposure, programme operators who would benefit from an assured baseline, and regulators examining the category.

## Impact If Built
The obligation was assigned contractually and the mechanism was never built. Freezes, disputes, fees and closures are already visible as events, which makes a guaranteed baseline enforceable rather than aspirational.

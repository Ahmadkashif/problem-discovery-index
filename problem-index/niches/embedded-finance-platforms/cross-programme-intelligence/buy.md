## The Problem
Consortium models are established practice: card networks score fraud across every issuer, fraud consortia pool signals across institutions, credit bureaus aggregate across lenders. Each solved the governance, the contribution incentives, the abstraction that makes contribution safe, and the regulatory framing. The embedded finance platform has the pool already sitting inside one company and has adopted none of the practice.

## What Already Exists
Network-level fraud scoring; consortium fraud databases with contribution rules; bureau aggregation and furnishing frameworks; benchmark reporting services; and the contractual and privacy architecture underneath all of them.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a pool held by one party rather than contributed by many. It requires: (1) governance for data the platform already holds under operating agreements rather than under contribution agreements, which is legally different and is the binding question — resolving it is the substantive work; (2) products rather than customers as the unit of comparison, since the interesting variation is in programme design rather than in individual behaviour; (3) no contribution incentive to engineer, because the data arrives regardless, which removes the usual consortium problem and replaces it with a consent one; (4) heterogeneous products rather than one product type across institutions, so abstraction must handle archetypes; and (5) the platform as both pooler and beneficiary, which raises a conflict the consortium structure was designed to avoid.

## Target Customer
Platform data leadership and counsel, sponsor banks, programme operators receiving benchmarks, and consortium operators for whom this pool is both familiar and out of reach.

## Impact If Solved
Consortia solved contribution and governance for pools assembled across firms. A pool that already sits inside one company inverts the hard part — the question is permission rather than participation, and nobody has answered it.

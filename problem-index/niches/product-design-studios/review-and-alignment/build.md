# A Review Process That Converges

**Niche:** [[niches/product-design-studios/review-and-alignment/profile|Review & Stakeholder Alignment]]
**Industry:** [[industries/product-design-studios|Product Design Studios]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Six rounds of review, no evidence on either side, and the loudest preference wins.
**Tags:** #workflow-orchestration #evaluation-metrics #large-language-models #descriptive-statistics #worker-facing #data-integration #compliance #tacit-knowledge-ml
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get a design decision made when the objections are preferences and neither side has evidence — and whoever makes that process work takes the account.

## The Problem
A design is reviewed repeatedly by a shifting set of stakeholders. Objections arrive as preferences — this feels cluttered, I would not click that, our customers are different — and are answered with the designer's own judgement, because no evidence is available to either party. Decisions are made and then reopened by someone who was not in the room. The rounds consume the schedule and the result is a design assembled from concessions rather than decided.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Review is treated as a meeting rather than as a process with a design. Decision rights are not defined because doing so would require the client to organise itself. The studio has no standing to impose structure. And each round feels reasonable in isolation.

## What to Build
Define who decides, distinguish the kinds of objection, and put evidence in the room. Establish decision rights explicitly at kickoff — who decides, who advises, who is informed — which is the core and prevents most of the reopening outright. Classify each piece of feedback as a requirement, a preference or a question, since conflating them is what makes review unresolvable. Surface relevant evidence at the point of disagreement, whether from the client's own analytics, prior research or the studio's accumulated record, which changes an argument about taste into one about a finding. Record every decision with its reason and the person who made it, so reopening requires new information rather than a new attendee. Bound the number of rounds in the contract and hold it, which is commercially routine and rarely enforced. Bring late stakeholders into the record rather than into the debate. Batch feedback and resolve it as a set rather than responding individually, which prevents contradictory instructions. Show the cost of each change in schedule terms at the moment it is requested, which changes many requests. Separate what is testable from what is not, since the testable questions can be settled rather than argued. And give the client the process as part of the engagement, because their organisation is where the disorder originates.

## Target Customer
Design studios and consultancy design arms, client product leadership, in-house design organisations, and collaboration and workflow vendors.

## Impact If Built
Objections arrive as preferences, are answered with judgement, and are reopened by whoever was absent, which is why rounds multiply. Defined decision rights plus classified feedback makes the process converge.

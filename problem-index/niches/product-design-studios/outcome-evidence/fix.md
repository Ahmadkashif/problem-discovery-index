# Twenty Engagements and No Baseline for Any of Them

**Niche:** [[niches/product-design-studios/outcome-evidence/profile|Outcome Evidence]]
**Industry:** [[industries/product-design-studios|Product Design Studios]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Nobody wrote down what the number was before the redesign, so nothing after it can be interpreted.
**Tags:** #quick-win #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #confidence-intervals #workflow-orchestration #data-integration #causal-inference #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to demonstrate that the design it sold produced the outcome it claimed, when the evidence lands in the client's analytics after the contract has closed — and whoever demonstrates it takes the account.

## The Problem
Even studios that want to demonstrate results cannot, because no baseline exists. The conversion rate before the redesign was never recorded, the support volume was never captured, the retention curve was never looked at. Afterwards the client's numbers have moved for many reasons and there is nothing to compare against. The opportunity to capture it cost ten minutes at kickoff and passed unnoticed on every engagement.

## Why It's Still Broken
Nobody asks at kickoff — a baseline can only be captured before the work and nothing in the kickoff process prompts anyone to capture it, so the opportunity passes on every engagement. The studio does not have analytics access. Clients are not asked. And the absence is only noticed at the end, when it cannot be fixed.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Ask for the numbers at kickoff and write them down. Capture the current value of the metrics the work is meant to move, at kickoff, as a standard step — which is the fix and takes one conversation. Ask the client which numbers they will judge this by, since that is the measure that matters and is often not the one the studio assumed. Record the studio's own claim about what will change and by roughly how much, which makes the check meaningful later. Note what else is planned in the same period, as that determines whether any comparison will be readable. Request a screenshot or export rather than access if access is difficult, which is a far smaller ask and usually granted. Agree to a follow-up conversation at a defined interval, which costs nothing and creates the occasion. Do it on every engagement rather than the important ones, since the corpus is the point. Store the baselines in one place so they accumulate. Use the existence of a baseline as a selling point, which several clients will value in itself. And accept an approximate baseline over none, because an imperfect comparison is infinitely better than an impossible one.

## Who Feels the Pain
Studios unable to prove work they believe in; clients who cannot justify the spend internally; designers whose good decisions are indistinguishable from their bad ones; and the next proposal, argued on taste.

## Impact If Fixed
A baseline can only be captured before the work, and nothing in the kickoff process prompts anyone to capture it. One conversation at kickoff is the difference between an engagement that can be graded and one that cannot.

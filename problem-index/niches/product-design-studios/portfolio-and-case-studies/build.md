# Capturing the Work While It Happens

**Niche:** [[niches/product-design-studios/portfolio-and-case-studies/profile|Portfolio & Case Study Production]]
**Industry:** [[industries/product-design-studios|Product Design Studios]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** The material that wins the next engagement is assembled months later by whoever is free.
**Tags:** #automation #workflow-orchestration #large-language-models #data-integration #evaluation-metrics #revenue-impact #descriptive-statistics #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn finished engagements into the material that wins the next one, when the work is done by whoever is between projects — and whoever automates that takes the account.

## The Problem
Case studies are produced retrospectively, badly, and often not at all. The engagement ends, the team disperses, and months later someone assembles a case study from a folder of files without the context that made the work good. Client approval is requested long after the relationship has cooled and is frequently declined. The studio's best recent work is therefore missing from the material it sells with.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Nobody is paid for the case study, so it happens between projects or not at all. Capture during the engagement competes with delivery. Approval is awkward to request and easy to defer. And new business works with whatever exists rather than asking for what it needs.

## What to Build
Capture during the engagement and secure approval while the relationship is warm. Capture the material as the work happens — the problem, the reasoning, the artefacts, the outcome — which is the core and is the only moment the context exists. Ask for publication consent at contracting rather than after delivery, since it is granted at the start and refused at the end. Generate a first draft of the case study from the engagement's own record rather than from memory. Structure case studies so they can be matched to a new opportunity by problem type rather than browsed. Include the measured outcome where one exists, which is what separates a case study from a screenshot. Produce redacted versions for confidential clients, which recovers work that is currently unusable entirely. Keep the assets organised during the project so assembly is not archaeology. Track which case studies appear in won and lost proposals, which tells the studio what to produce more of. Assign capture to the producer as a standing task rather than leaving it unowned. And make the whole thing take an hour at project close rather than a week months afterwards.

## Target Customer
Design studios and consultancy design arms, new business and marketing functions, in-house design teams reporting internally, and content automation vendors.

## Impact If Built
The context that makes the work legible exists only during the engagement, and the case study is written months later without it. Capture at the time and consent at contracting recovers the studio's best work into its own material.

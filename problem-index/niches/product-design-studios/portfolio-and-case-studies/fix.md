# Asking for Approval Eight Months Later

**Niche:** [[niches/product-design-studios/portfolio-and-case-studies/profile|Portfolio & Case Study Production]]
**Industry:** [[industries/product-design-studios|Product Design Studios]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The studio emails a former client to ask whether it can publish the work, and hears nothing.
**Tags:** #quick-win #workflow-orchestration #automation #compliance #revenue-impact #evaluation-metrics #data-integration #descriptive-statistics
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn finished engagements into the material that wins the next one, when the work is done by whoever is between projects — and whoever automates that takes the account.

## The Problem
Publication consent is requested after the fact. By then the contact may have moved on, the product may have changed, legal may have become involved, and the request lands as an unexpected favour rather than an agreed step. Consent is refused or ignored, and work the studio is proud of cannot be shown. The same request made at contracting would almost always have been granted.

## Why It's Still Broken
The request is made at the wrong moment — consent asked for after delivery arrives as an imposition on a relationship that has ended, where the same question at contracting is a routine clause. Nobody owns it. The contract does not mention publication. And by the time it is wanted, the easy moment has gone.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Move the request to contracting and make it conditional rather than absolute. Include a publication clause in the standard contract with conditions the client can set, which is the fix and is a paragraph. Offer tiered consent — full, anonymised, or internal use only — since a client who will not agree to one will often agree to another. Agree a timing condition tied to the product's public release, which resolves the commonest objection. Name the approver in the contract so the later request has somewhere to go. Capture a short client quote at the end of the engagement while the relationship is warm. Produce the case study in draft before asking, as approving something concrete is far easier than agreeing in principle. Keep a record of which clients consented to what, so nobody asks twice or publishes wrongly. Follow up once at a defined point rather than sporadically. Produce the anonymised version for everything, which is usable regardless. And treat the consent as part of closing the engagement rather than as a marketing task later.

## Who Feels the Pain
Studios unable to show their best work; new business pitching with outdated material; designers whose work is invisible; and the next proposal, which competes on a weaker portfolio.

## Impact If Fixed
Consent asked for after delivery arrives as an imposition on a relationship that has ended, where the same question at contracting is a routine clause. Moving it to the contract recovers the work the studio cannot currently show.

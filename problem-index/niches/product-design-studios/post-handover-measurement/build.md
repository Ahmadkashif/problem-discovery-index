# Getting the Result Back

**Niche:** [[niches/product-design-studios/post-handover-measurement/profile|Post-Handover Measurement]]
**Industry:** [[industries/product-design-studios|Product Design Studios]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** The number that grades the work is in the client's warehouse and nobody has asked for it.
**Tags:** #causal-inference #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #hypothesis-testing #data-integration #compliance #workflow-orchestration #descriptive-statistics
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get one engagement's outcome back from a client whose contract has closed — and whoever makes that routine takes the account.

## The Problem
Measuring a single engagement's outcome is not technically hard — the client has the data, the intervention has a date, and the metric is usually obvious. It fails on access and on timing. Nobody agreed a measure, nobody captured a baseline, and nobody arranged to look afterwards. The studio's relationship with the client is good and the request is never made, because there is no point in the process at which making it is anyone's job.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The contract ends at delivery and the follow-up is unpaid. Studios treat client analytics access as an imposition. There is no standard clause to point at. And the result might not flatter the work.

## What to Build
Make the measurement part of the engagement's shape rather than a favour afterwards. Write a measurement addendum into the standard contract covering the metric, the baseline and post-launch access, which is the core and is a commercial change rather than a technical one. Agree the metric with the client at kickoff, since it must be theirs to be persuasive. Capture the baseline before any work begins, which is the irreversible step. Design the comparison to account for the client's other releases, because a redesign shipping alongside four other changes cannot be read naively. Accept aggregate rather than record-level data, which removes most of the objection. Offer the measurement as a paid follow-on so it is funded rather than absorbed. Set the measurement window at contracting so nobody argues about timing later. Report the result to the client as a service, which is what makes them willing to participate. Handle the null or negative result with a stated method rather than quietly, since that is what makes the positive results credible. And keep the whole thing light enough to happen on a six-week engagement, which is most of them.

## Target Customer
Design studios and consultancy design arms, clients commissioning design, in-house design leadership, and measurement vendors.

## Impact If Built
There is no point in the process at which asking for the outcome is anyone's job, so a request every client would grant is never made. A measurement addendum and a baseline at kickoff makes one engagement gradeable.

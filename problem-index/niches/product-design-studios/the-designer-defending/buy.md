## The Problem
Clinical practice confronted the gap between a body of evidence and a decision being made now, and built point-of-care tools: summarised evidence retrievable in seconds, graded by strength, written for the moment of the decision rather than for a literature review. Practitioners consult them during consultations. The effect was to move practice from individual recollection toward the accumulated record. Design has a smaller and messier evidence base and no retrieval layer at all.

## What Already Exists
Point-of-care evidence summaries; strength-graded recommendations; second-scale retrieval during a decision; synthesis written for practitioners rather than researchers; and integration into the workflow where the decision happens.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a field whose evidence is thin, commercial and contextual. It requires: (1) an evidence base that is mostly proprietary engagement records rather than published literature, so retrieval must span a studio's own corpus and whatever public work exists — this is the substantive difference; (2) findings that are heavily context-dependent, where a result from one product may not transfer; (3) a decision made in a meeting with non-practitioners present, so the output must persuade a stakeholder rather than inform a professional; (4) no grading framework for design evidence, which must be built; and (5) a large share of questions where the honest answer is that nothing is established.

## Target Customer
Design studios and in-house teams, design leadership, design professional bodies, and knowledge retrieval vendors.

## Impact If Solved
Clinical practice moved decisions from recollection toward the record with second-scale retrieval. An evidence base that is mostly proprietary and contextual, consulted in front of non-practitioners, is what has to be built differently.

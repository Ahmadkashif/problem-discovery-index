# Something Other Than Judgement in the Room

**Niche:** [[niches/product-design-studios/the-designer-defending/profile|The Designer Defending a Decision]]
**Industry:** [[industries/product-design-studios|Product Design Studios]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** The design is decided by whoever argues best, which is not the same as whoever is right.
**Tags:** #worker-facing #large-language-models #tacit-knowledge-ml #evaluation-metrics #data-integration #descriptive-statistics #workflow-orchestration #confidence-intervals
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to give the designer something other than their own judgement to bring to the sixth round of the same argument — and whoever supplies that takes the account.

## The Problem
A designer makes a decision for reasons they can articulate, and is challenged by stakeholders whose objections are preferences. Neither party has evidence. The designer's options are to assert their expertise, which reads as arrogance, or to concede, which degrades the work. They do this several times a week, for months, on every engagement. The outcome depends on rhetorical skill, and the profession loses people over it.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The evidence that would settle these questions is exactly what the studio does not accumulate. Retrieving relevant findings mid-meeting is not something any tool does. Persuasion is classified as a soft skill. And the designer is the person absorbing it, not the person buying tools.

## What to Build
Put retrievable evidence in the designer's hands at the moment of the argument. Surface relevant findings — from the client's own analytics, prior research, the studio's record or published work — at the point a specific decision is questioned, which is the core and changes the character of the conversation entirely. Make retrieval work from a design question in plain language rather than requiring a search strategy, since the moment is short. Distinguish what is established from what is the designer's judgement, because conflating them is what makes the designer's position brittle. Provide framing for the honest answer when there is no evidence, which is a real and currently unsupported position. Record what the studio itself has found, so its own history becomes retrievable. Suggest the testable version of a disputed question, which converts an argument into an experiment. Show comparable products' approaches as context rather than as authority, which is how designers actually argue and is currently done from memory. Give the designer language for preference versus requirement that does not sound dismissive. Support the design lead in backing their designer, which is the organisational half. And keep the design decision with the designer rather than with the tool, since an automated recommendation would be worse than the current situation.

## Target Customer
Design studios and in-house design teams, design leadership, design education, and knowledge retrieval tooling vendors.

## Impact If Built
The design is decided by whoever argues best, which is not the same as whoever is right, and neither party has anything but assertion. Retrievable evidence at the moment of the question changes what the conversation is about.

# Relitigating the Same Question Every Project

**Niche:** [[niches/product-design-studios/cross-engagement-evidence/profile|Cross-Engagement Design Evidence]]
**Industry:** [[industries/product-design-studios|Product Design Studios]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The team is arguing about how many steps the flow should have, for the ninth time, from first principles.
**Tags:** #quick-win #tacit-knowledge-ml #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #workflow-orchestration #data-integration #confidence-intervals #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn a decade of engagements into evidence about what interface decisions actually do — and whoever accumulates that takes the account.

## The Problem
The same design questions recur on every project: how many steps, how much onboarding, progressive disclosure or everything at once, defaults or choices. The studio has faced each of them repeatedly, with different clients, and reached conclusions each time. None of those conclusions is retrievable, so each project argues from first principles, and the argument is settled by whoever is most senior or most articulate in the room.

## Why It's Still Broken
Nothing is written down — a conclusion reached in a project and recorded only in the delivered design is not retrievable by anyone later, so the firm has no memory of its own reasoning. Portfolios show outcomes rather than decisions. Nobody owns the practice's knowledge. And the argument feels productive each time.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Write down what was decided and why, before worrying about outcomes. Record the recurring design decisions and the reasoning behind them at the end of each project, which is the fix and takes half an hour per engagement. Tag by the kind of problem rather than by the client, so retrieval is possible. Note what the client reported afterwards where anything came back, even informally. Make the record searchable by the designers on the next project. Include the decisions that turned out badly, since those are the most useful and the least recorded. Surface the relevant past decisions at the start of a project rather than expecting anyone to search. Hold a short practice review on the recurring questions, which produces a position the firm can hold rather than relitigate. Separate the firm's position from an individual's preference explicitly, which is what makes the review worth having. Revisit positions as evidence accumulates rather than treating them as doctrine. And use the recorded reasoning in client conversations, which is immediately more persuasive than assertion.

## Who Feels the Pain
Designers arguing the same case repeatedly; clients paying for reasoning the studio already did; junior designers with no way to learn the firm's accumulated judgement; and every decision settled by seniority.

## Impact If Fixed
A conclusion recorded only in the delivered design is not retrievable by anyone later, so the firm has no memory of its own reasoning. Half an hour per engagement recording decisions and why makes the ninth argument the first informed one.

# Niche Analysis — Product Design Studios

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/product-design-studios|Product Design Studios]]

## Niche Selection

A design studio's product is a causal claim — this arrangement of an interface will produce that behaviour — sold to clients who can verify it and by a firm that never asks. The craft tooling is excellent and there is no measurement layer anywhere. The eight niches below follow that: what happens to the claim after handover, what happens to the artefact, how the work is scoped and argued in the absence of evidence, and who absorbs the consequences.

## Why These Niches

Outcome evidence and design system durability carry the money and the two ways a studio's work is judged to have failed: nobody ever establishes whether the redesign worked, and the artefact starts drifting from production the week the studio leaves. Discovery scoping and review are where the absence of evidence becomes operational — a fixed fee for work that by definition does not know what it will find, and six rounds of review settled by seniority because neither side has anything better. The two underserved audiences are the designer defending the same decision repeatedly against preferences, and the producer who sees the scope creeping three weeks before anyone else and has to be the one who says so. The last two are mechanical: producing the deliverables, and producing the case studies that win the next engagement.

## Filter Notes

Seven of the eight are terminal — each names one contest that every serious competitor is fighting over, and decomposing further would produce features rather than markets.

**Outcome evidence** is not. It names a capability, and underneath it are two problems that are bought separately and solve different things. Post-handover measurement asks what happened to this engagement's work — a contractual and instrumentation problem, negotiated per client, requiring access to analytics the studio does not own, and valuable immediately because a studio that can show one client's result has something no testimonial provides. Cross-engagement design evidence asks what twenty engagements a year across a decade have established about interface design generally — a corpus problem, requiring comparable measurement across clients and a structure for accumulating it, and valuable to the studio's practice rather than to any single client. The first is sold into an engagement; the second is what changes the business from selling taste to selling evidence. A studio can do the first for one client this quarter without ever building the second, and most that have closed the loop at all stopped there.

Two adjacent candidates were rejected as belonging elsewhere: **the qualitative user research that informs the design** is its own industry at [[industries/ux-research-agencies|UX Research Agencies]], and **testing and optimising a live funnel continuously** belongs to [[industries/conversion-optimization-firms|Conversion Optimization Firms]].

# Being the Person Who Has to Say It

**Niche:** [[niches/product-design-studios/the-producer/profile|The Producer Holding the Budget]]
**Industry:** [[industries/product-design-studios|Product Design Studios]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Every engagement has a moment when someone must tell the client the requests have added up, and it is always the same person.
**Tags:** #worker-facing #quick-win #workflow-orchestration #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #revenue-impact #automation #compliance
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to let the person who sees scope creep three weeks before anyone else raise it with evidence rather than as an accusation — and whoever supplies that takes the account.

## The Problem
The scope conversation falls to the producer by default. They are the one tracking the budget, so they are the one who notices, so they are the one who raises it — with the client, with their own team, repeatedly, on every project. It is conflict work with no authority behind it, it is not in the job description, and it is a leading reason producers leave the role. The studio treats it as part of production rather than as a structural gap.

## Why It's Still Broken
Nobody else is watching the number — a conversation that only one person has the information to start will always be started by that person, regardless of whether it should be their job. There is no defined trigger. The commercial lead is not in the weekly detail. And the producer absorbing it is invisible.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Make the trigger a threshold rather than a judgement, and put someone else in the room. Define a burn threshold that automatically triggers a scope review, which is the fix and removes the decision to speak from the producer entirely. Have the commercial lead rather than the producer raise it with the client, which is where the conversation properly belongs. Share the burn position with the client weekly as routine, so the conversation is continuous rather than an event. Record each request's impact when it arrives rather than at the threshold, which is what makes the case unarguable later. Put the contracted revision count in front of both parties, since it is frequently forgotten by everyone. Support the producer explicitly rather than treating this as their temperament. Track how often each client triggers a review, which is commercially informative for the next engagement. Rotate who raises it rather than defaulting to one person. Write the change process into the contract so raising it is procedural rather than personal. And measure producer turnover against this specifically, which is the evidence that would make the studio act.

## Who Feels the Pain
Producers doing conflict work with no authority; design teams told to stop mid-flow; clients surprised by an accumulation they created; and the studio, which loses producers and does not connect it.

## Impact If Fixed
A conversation that only one person has the information to start will always be started by that person, regardless of whether it should be their job. A threshold trigger and a commercial lead in the room moves it off them.

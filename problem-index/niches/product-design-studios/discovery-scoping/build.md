# Pricing Work You Cannot Yet Specify

**Niche:** [[niches/product-design-studios/discovery-scoping/profile|Discovery Scoping & Pricing]]
**Industry:** [[industries/product-design-studios|Product Design Studios]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** The fee is fixed before anyone knows what the work is.
**Tags:** #confidence-intervals #evaluation-metrics #revenue-impact #descriptive-statistics #gradient-boosting #hypothesis-testing #workflow-orchestration #probability-distributions
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to price fixed-fee discovery work that by definition does not know what it will find — and whoever prices it honestly and still wins takes the account.

## The Problem
Discovery engagements are commissioned to establish what the problem is. Their scope therefore depends on what they establish. Clients want a fixed fee and a date, so the studio produces one from recollection of similar projects, and then either absorbs an overrun or opens a scope conversation that damages the relationship. The studio has run dozens of these and has never compared what it estimated against what the work actually took.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The history exists as invoices and timesheets and has never been assembled. Pricing is done by whoever is most senior, from memory. Proposing a staged structure feels like a weaker offer against a competitor's confident fixed price. And the losses appear as margin variation.

## What to Build
Estimate from the firm's own record and restructure the commercial shape. Build a history of estimates against actuals across past engagements, which is the core and is the evidence the whole practice currently lacks. Model effort against the characteristics of the engagement — product maturity, stakeholder count, research depth, existing evidence — rather than against a subjective size. Produce a range with stated assumptions rather than a point, since a point on discovery work is a fiction both sides then plan against. Price the uncertainty explicitly rather than burying it in margin, which is what makes an honest quote survivable. Offer a staged commercial structure — a short paid framing phase before a scoped engagement — which is the correct answer to the information problem and is rarely proposed. Identify the characteristics that historically predict overruns and check for them at proposal time. Track scope change frequency and cause, which tells the studio where its estimating is systematically wrong. Record the assumptions in the proposal so a change is a variation rather than an argument. Report estimate accuracy as a business metric. And give the commercial lead the numbers rather than the argument, since the numbers make the staged structure defensible.

## Target Customer
Design studios and consultancy design arms, commercial and delivery leadership, clients commissioning discovery, and professional services estimation vendors.

## Impact If Built
A fixed point estimate on work that does not know what it will find is a fiction both sides plan against. A range from the studio's own history, plus a paid framing phase, prices the uncertainty instead of absorbing it.

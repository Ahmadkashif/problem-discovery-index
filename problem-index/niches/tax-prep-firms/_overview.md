## Why These Niches

Schedule C and multi-state returns represent the two fastest-growing complexity categories in individual tax preparation, driven by gig economy growth and remote work respectively. Spanish-language firms and seasonal storefronts are digitally underserved — both serve populations with lower digital literacy and rely on in-person interactions that mainstream tax tech does not optimize for. K-1 partnership specialists and military/expat preparers serve populations with genuinely different filing requirements that mainstream software handles poorly. Document intake and amendment processing are the two highest-volume repetitive workflows with the clearest automation ROI. Excluded: large CPA firms (enterprise buyers), corporate tax departments (different workflow), and H&R Block/Jackson Hewitt franchises (captive technology).

## Why These Pockets

No new qualifiers, and the two pockets that would qualify are both already indexed under SMB accounting firms — tax research content publishing at 58, which is the joint highest-scoring pocket in this index, and tax software content teams at 47. That second one deserves a note on method: the filing season clock bearing on consumer preparation is harder than anything in professional accounting, and the score has deliberately not been revised upward for it. It is the same organisation doing the same work, and re-scoring a pocket because a different industry looks at it from a better angle would corrupt the comparison the index exists to support.

What this sweep records instead is an industry organised entirely around a single immovable date, with several parties holding information that the deadline makes valuable and nobody selling it.

Refund transfer banks hold the only large dataset on when refunds actually arrive — which returns get delayed, which get offset, and by how long — assembled from millions of returns across many seasons. That is information every taxpayer and every preparer wants during the ten weeks it matters, it is not published by the tax authority, and it is used to price a cash advance.

Tax resolution firms hold the acceptance rates and settlement patterns of an authority that publishes almost nothing about how it actually settles, and use them to set expectations one client at a time. E-file transmitters see exactly which return constructions are rejected and why, at national scale, and return a rejection code. And the preparer oversight function holds preparer-level accuracy joined to examination outcomes — the single piece of information a taxpayer choosing a preparer would most want — and is barred by taxpayer confidentiality from publishing any of it.

The tax administration research function is the extreme case in this whole sweep: it holds every return filed in the country joined to examination and collection outcomes, which is the largest and best-labelled compliance dataset that exists, behind both government procurement and a statutory confidentiality regime stricter than any privacy wall recorded elsewhere in this index.

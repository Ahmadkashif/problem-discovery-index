# The Expert Call Notes That Live in One Consultant's Inbox

**Niche:** [[niches/private-equity-firms/commercial-diligence-consultancies/profile|Commercial Due Diligence Consultancies]]
**Industry:** [[industries/private-equity-firms|Private Equity Firms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A CDD team runs dozens of expert and customer calls per study, and the notes are typed into personal documents that the next team in the same market will never find.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #bert #word-embeddings #k-nearest-neighbors #evaluation-metrics #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to deliver, inside a sponsor's exclusivity window, the market and customer evidence that decides whether the deal team believes the revenue plan — and whoever arrives with the sharpest answer fastest, on a market they already know, wins the next mandate from that sponsor.

## The Problem
Interview notes are written by consultants under deadline, in their own format, saved to the engagement folder and forgotten. When the firm returns to the market a year later, it pays again for expert calls on questions it has already answered.

## Why It's Still Broken
Notes mix market-level and target-specific content, which makes reuse feel risky; nobody owns tagging; and expert networks' own transcript libraries cover only calls run through them.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Structured note capture at the moment of the call (or transcript ingestion where recording is permitted), automatic separation of market-level from target-specific statements, tagging by market and theme, and retrieval of prior relevant statements when a new study opens in the same market.

## Who Feels the Pain
Consultants repeating calls; managers rebuilding interview guides; partners whose market knowledge never compounds across the team.

## Impact If Fixed
Capturing and separating call notes is cheap and makes every future study in that market start ahead.

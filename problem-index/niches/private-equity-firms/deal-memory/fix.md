# The Company That Came Back

**Niche:** [[niches/private-equity-firms/deal-memory/profile|Deal Memory & Judgement Reuse]]
**Industry:** [[industries/private-equity-firms|Private Equity Firms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A company the firm looked at four years ago comes back to market, and the new deal team starts from scratch because nobody can find what the firm thought last time.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #k-nearest-neighbors #word-embeddings #evaluation-metrics #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to make a sponsor's past deal judgements — what it saw, why it passed or invested, and what happened next — retrievable at the moment a similar deal arrives, within what the firm's NDAs let it keep.

## The Problem
Mid-market companies come to market repeatedly — a failed process, a sponsor-to-sponsor sale, a recap. The firm's prior screening memo, model and partner comments exist somewhere, under an old company name or a project codename.

## Why It's Still Broken
Project codenames hide the company; staff turnover removes the memory; and CRM records are keyed by the banker's project, not by the company.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Resolve every opportunity to a persistent company identity at entry, map codenames to companies, and surface the prior record automatically when the company or its close competitors reappear.

## Who Feels the Pain
Deal teams repeating work; partners who could have moved faster with their own prior view.

## Impact If Fixed
A small entity-resolution step turns repeat opportunities into a head start instead of a repeat.

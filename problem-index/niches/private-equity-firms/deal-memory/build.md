# Ten Years of Passes, None of Them Graded

**Niche:** [[niches/private-equity-firms/deal-memory/profile|Deal Memory & Judgement Reuse]]
**Industry:** [[industries/private-equity-firms|Private Equity Firms]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A sponsor's largest research output is its record of declined deals, and nobody has ever checked how those companies turned out.
**Tags:** #tacit-knowledge-ml #large-language-models #survival-analysis #k-nearest-neighbors #evaluation-metrics #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to make a sponsor's past deal judgements — what it saw, why it passed or invested, and what happened next — retrievable at the moment a similar deal arrives, within what the firm's NDAs let it keep.

## The Problem
Partners decline most opportunities within an hour of reading them, for reasons that are mostly tacit. The decision record is a CRM stage and a reason code. The outcome of each declined company becomes visible in transaction databases over the following years and is never joined back.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Capturing reasons requires partners to say something at the moment they are moving fastest. CIMs often must be returned or destroyed. Outcomes arrive years later and are censored. And grading passes is uncomfortable for the people who made them.

## What to Build
A deal memory with three layers. Capture: extract retainable facts from each CIM on arrival and record the partner's reason by email reply or voice note. Join: resolve each company against PitchBook or Capital IQ and record subsequent sales, financings and failures, treating pending outcomes as censored. Use: on each new opportunity, retrieve the closest prior deals with the firm's view and the outcome, and produce periodic reports of where the firm's passes have aged worst. Measure agreement between partners on a blind re-screen sample so the firm knows how consistent its own judgement is.

## Target Customer
Managing partners and COOs at sponsors with five or more years of CRM history.

## Impact If Built
The firm's tacit screening judgement becomes teachable and auditable, and its systematic blind spots become visible for the first time.

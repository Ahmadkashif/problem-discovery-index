# The One-Time Cost That Happens Every Year

**Niche:** [[niches/private-equity-firms/quality-of-earnings-review/profile|Quality of Earnings Review]]
**Industry:** [[industries/private-equity-firms|Private Equity Firms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Sponsors accept add-backs as non-recurring and discover in the first year of ownership that many of them recur.
**Tags:** #survival-analysis #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #quick-win #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to establish a defensible adjusted EBITDA — which add-backs are real and which are recurring costs relabelled — inside the exclusivity window, because that number multiplied by the multiple is the price.

## The Problem
Post-close, the portfolio company's actual EBITDA comes in below the underwritten adjusted figure. Some of the gap is performance; some of it is add-backs — recruiting fees, consulting, "non-recurring" legal — that recur. Neither the sponsor nor the QoE provider measures how often each add-back category recurs across their deals.

## Why It's Still Broken
The diligence team and the post-close finance team never compare notes. The variance is explained at board level as "integration costs" or "investment in growth". And measuring add-back recurrence would expose underwriting to the lenders and LPs who relied on it.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Tag every accepted add-back at close by category and amount. Each year, check the portfolio company's ledger for recurrence of the same categories. Publish internally a recurrence rate per category across the firm's portfolio, and use it as a haircut in the next deal's underwriting.

## Who Feels the Pain
Deal partners whose underwriting erodes; portfolio CFOs blamed for missing a number built on optimistic add-backs; lenders whose leverage was sized on it.

## Impact If Fixed
A per-category recurrence rate is a small table that directly improves the most consequential number in every future deal.

# Quality of Earnings Review

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/private-equity-firms|Private Equity Firms]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to establish a defensible adjusted EBITDA — which add-backs are real and which are recurring costs relabelled — inside the exclusivity window, because that number multiplied by the multiple is the price.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$2.5B US (buy-side and sell-side QoE fees on sponsor transactions)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~8% of sponsor deal-related spend
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — trial balances and GL exports in Excel databooks
**Target Buyer:** Deal partners; transaction advisory practice leaders on the provider side
**Automation Potential:** Very High — ledger-level analysis is computation on the target's own data

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Adjusted EBITDA is the number the purchase price is multiplied from, and sellers' bankers present it with a list of add-backs — owner compensation, one-time legal costs, pro forma savings, run-rate adjustments for new contracts. The QoE provider's job is to test each one against the general ledger, normalise working capital and identify what the seller left out. The contest is precise: whose adjusted EBITDA survives the lender's review, IC and the first year of ownership. It is fought on the target's ledger, by accounting firms' transaction advisory practices (RSM, BDO, Grant Thornton, Baker Tilly and many boutiques), in a few weeks.

## Current Tools & Gaps
GL and trial balance exports, Excel databooks, proof-of-cash procedures, and provider-built analytics templates; some providers use data analytics platforms on full GL detail. The gaps: add-back testing still largely sampled rather than full-population; no reference distribution for how often each add-back type recurs after close; and the buyer's model rebuilt by hand from the databook.

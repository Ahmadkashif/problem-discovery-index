# Marks Never Compared With the Exit Price

**Niche:** [[niches/private-equity-firms/portfolio-valuation-advisory/profile|Portfolio Valuation Advisory]]
**Industry:** [[industries/private-equity-firms|Private Equity Firms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Every portfolio company is eventually sold at a real price, and the valuation practice that marked it for five years rarely measures how far its last marks were from that price.
**Tags:** #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #hypothesis-testing #descriptive-statistics #survival-analysis #compliance
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to produce a quarterly fair value mark the sponsor, its auditor and its LPs all accept — defensible against observable market evidence, consistent across hundreds of companies and delivered before the reporting deadline — and whoever does that most credibly at volume takes the valuation mandate.

## The Problem
Fair value is meant to be an exit price. The exit eventually happens and the price is known. Academic and regulatory attention to whether private marks are stale or smoothed is long-standing, and LPs increasingly ask about it. The valuation practice holds every mark and, for most companies, learns the exit value — and the comparison is done ad hoc, if at all.

## Why It's Still Broken
A systematic comparison could show marks lag or smooth reality, which is commercially awkward for the practice and its sponsor clients. Exit prices are reported by the sponsor in varying forms. And there is a genuine methodological point that fair value at a quarter-end is not a forecast of a later exit price.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Record for every exited company the marks in the four quarters before exit and the realised exit value. Measure the gap by sector, size, method weighting and quarter, separating market movement between mark date and exit from mark error. Report calibration — how often the exit fell within the practice's implied range. Use the result to adjust method weights and to show auditors and LPs measured accuracy rather than asserted process.

## Who Feels the Pain
Valuation practice leaders defending methodology without evidence; sponsors whose marks are questioned by LPs; LPs relying on smoothed NAVs for allocation decisions.

## Impact If Fixed
Measured mark-to-exit accuracy is the strongest possible defence of a valuation methodology and the clearest guide to improving it, and the data is already in the practice's files.

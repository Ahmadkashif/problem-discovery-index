# Organic Growth That Was Really an Add-On

**Niche:** [[niches/private-equity-firms/value-creation-attribution/profile|Value Creation Attribution]]
**Industry:** [[industries/private-equity-firms|Private Equity Firms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Platform revenue growth is reported as organic when part of it is acquired revenue that crossed its anniversary.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #data-integration #quick-win #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to show which value creation initiatives actually moved EBITDA and which growth came from add-ons, the market or multiple expansion — because that evidence is what the next fundraise is sold on.

## The Problem
In buy-and-build strategies, add-ons are folded in and, after twelve months, their growth counts as organic, while pricing and cross-selling across acquired units blur the line further. LPs increasingly ask for organic growth rates, and the definitions vary by deal team.

## Why It's Still Broken
Unit-level data is lost when add-ons are integrated into the platform ledger; and a strict definition lowers the headline number.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Keep acquired units tagged in the data model after integration, compute same-store organic growth on a consistent definition across the portfolio, and report acquired, organic and price components separately.

## Who Feels the Pain
IR and fund finance answering LP questions; operating partners whose genuine organic work is indistinguishable from acquisitions.

## Impact If Fixed
A consistent organic-growth definition protects credibility with LPs and shows which platforms are actually growing.

# The Proprietary Deal That Wasn't

**Niche:** [[niches/private-equity-firms/deal-sourcing/profile|Deal Sourcing & Origination]]
**Industry:** [[industries/private-equity-firms|Private Equity Firms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Fundraising decks claim a high share of "proprietary" deals, and nobody at the firm can say which sourcing activity actually produced the deals it closed.
**Tags:** #causal-inference #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #data-integration #quick-win #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to know that a founder-owned company is becoming sellable — and to be the trusted buyer in the room — before a banker is hired and the price is set by auction.

## The Problem
Sponsors tell LPs that a large share of their deals were proprietary or "limited process". Internally, the label is assigned by the deal team after the fact, often to any deal where a banker was involved but the firm had a prior relationship. Sourcing spend — conferences, data, business development salaries — is justified by this label, and the firm has no record linking the first touch to the eventual deal or to the price paid relative to auctioned comparables.

## Why It's Still Broken
Attribution is unflattering and politically awkward: the honest record usually shows most closed deals came through bankers and the relationship mattered less than the bid. The data to check it is scattered — first-touch dates in the CRM, process type in deal files, entry multiples in the model — and nobody owns joining it. And the label is useful for fundraising as it is.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Record the sourcing path as a fact at first contact rather than a label at close: who first touched the company, when, through whom, and whether a banker was engaged when the process began. Join that to entry multiple and to comparable auctioned deals in the same sector and size band. Report, per sourcing channel, conversion rates and the entry-multiple discount actually achieved. Use the result to reallocate sourcing spend and to write the LP narrative from evidence.

## Who Feels the Pain
Managing partners allocating sourcing budget blind; business development professionals whose contribution is unmeasured; LPs evaluating a claimed edge that has never been tested.

## Impact If Fixed
Sourcing is one of the few edges a mid-market sponsor can credibly claim, and it is currently asserted rather than measured. A first-touch record joined to entry pricing turns it into a defensible, improvable number.

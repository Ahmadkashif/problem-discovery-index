# Deal Sourcing & Origination

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/private-equity-firms|Private Equity Firms]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to know that a founder-owned company is becoming sellable — and to be the trusted buyer in the room — before a banker is hired and the price is set by auction.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$5B US (business development headcount, private-company data subscriptions, conferences and intermediary relationship spend)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~17% of sponsor operating spend
**Digital Adoption:** High — data subscriptions and CRMs everywhere, used mainly as lookup
**Target Buyer:** Head of business development / managing partner
**Automation Potential:** High — signal aggregation and prioritisation are machine work; the relationship is not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Mid-market sponsors compete for a finite pool of companies and the price of a company falls sharply the earlier the sponsor arrives: a banker-run auction prices it at the clearing multiple, while a founder approached two years before a sale may transact bilaterally or give the sponsor a last look. Sourcing is therefore a forecasting contest — which of tens of thousands of founder-owned companies will become sellable, and when — combined with a relationship contest over who the founder trusts. The data vendors (PitchBook, Grata, SourceScrub, Inven) sell the same universe to everyone; the differentiation is in what the sponsor layers on top from its own history.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Private-company databases with firmographics, headcount and web signals; CRM relationship intelligence (DealCloud, Affinity) from email metadata; banker coverage lists; conference attendance. The gaps: no model of sale timing using the firm's own touchpoint history; outreach prioritised by size and sector filters rather than readiness; no feedback from which sourced relationships ever converted; and intermediated deals logged separately from proprietary ones so the firm cannot tell what its sourcing spend actually bought.

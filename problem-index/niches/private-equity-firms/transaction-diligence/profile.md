# Transaction Diligence

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/private-equity-firms|Private Equity Firms]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** This niche is not terminal — establishing what the earnings really are and establishing whether the revenue will persist are different contests with different providers, evidence and winners, and they are stated separately in the sub-niches below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$7B US (third-party diligence fees across quality of earnings, commercial, legal, insurance, IT and environmental workstreams, plus deal-team time)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~23% of sponsor deal-related spend
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — virtual data rooms and research tools, with the analysis still in Excel and Word
**Target Buyer:** Deal partners and the sponsor's head of diligence or COO
**Automation Potential:** High — document reading, spreading and reconciliation are mechanical; the judgement on what matters is not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Once a sponsor signs an LOI it has four to eight weeks of exclusivity to confirm the price it offered. Diligence is the largest and most time-critical research effort in the deal cycle, and it is where most third-party research spend goes. It is also **not terminal**. Two separate contests live inside it. Financial diligence asks whether the earnings are what the seller says — which add-backs are real, whether working capital is normal, whether revenue recognition is aggressive — and is fought out by accounting firms' transaction advisory practices on the target's ledger. Commercial diligence asks whether the revenue will persist and grow — market size and growth, share, pricing power, customer stickiness — and is fought out by strategy consultancies and specialist CDD boutiques on surveys, interviews and market data. Different evidence, different providers, different failure modes.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Datasite, Intralinks and Firmex for document exchange; Excel databooks from QoE providers; consultancy reports; AlphaSense, Tegus and expert networks; LLM research tools such as Hebbia reading the data room. The gaps: workstreams that never reconcile with each other; findings that do not flow into the model automatically; no record of which diligence findings mattered after close; and a single "diligence" budget line that obscures two different contests.

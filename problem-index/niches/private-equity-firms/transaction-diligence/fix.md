# Diligence Findings Nobody Revisits After Close

**Niche:** [[niches/private-equity-firms/transaction-diligence/profile|Transaction Diligence]]
**Industry:** [[industries/private-equity-firms|Private Equity Firms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The firm pays for a stack of diligence reports on every deal and never checks which findings turned out to matter.

## The Problem
After close, the diligence reports are filed. Two years later a customer churns, a key add-back proves to have been recurring, or a regulatory issue surfaces, and nobody checks whether diligence flagged it, rated it low, or missed it. Over dozens of deals the firm accumulates the evidence to know which providers find what and which categories of finding predict trouble — and never looks.

## Why It's Still Broken
Post-close the deal team moves on and the operating team inherits the company, not the diligence file. Grading providers is awkward because the firm hires them again. And no structured record of findings exists to grade.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Keep the findings ledger alive after close. At each annual review, tag post-close events (material variances, churn of named customers, disputes, write-downs) and match them to diligence findings. Report by provider and by finding category: flagged and materialised, flagged and benign, missed. Feed the result into provider selection and into how IC weighs specific finding types.

## Who Feels the Pain
Operating partners surprised by issues that were in the file; deal teams who cannot tell which diligence spend is valuable; and the providers whose good calls are never credited.

## Impact If Fixed
A sponsor running a dozen deals a year builds within a fund cycle the only dataset that says which diligence actually protects returns, at the cost of a tagging exercise once a year.

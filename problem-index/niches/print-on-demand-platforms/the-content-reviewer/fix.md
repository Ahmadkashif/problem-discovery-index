# A Removal With No Explanation

**Niche:** [[niches/print-on-demand-platforms/the-content-reviewer/profile|The Content Reviewer]]
**Industry:** [[industries/print-on-demand-platforms|Print on Demand Platforms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A creator's design is removed with a generic policy reference, they cannot tell what was wrong or how to fix it, and the appeal goes to a form that produces the same generic response.
**Tags:** #worker-facing #compliance #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #confidence-intervals #quick-win #automation #large-language-models
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to support a reviewer deciding genuinely contested legal questions at volume — and whoever does that manages the liability, because the platform carries it and the decision is made in seconds by somebody with no legal support.

## The Problem
A creator's design is removed for intellectual property. The notice cites a policy section. They do not know which element was the problem — a word, a silhouette, a colour combination, a stylistic reference — so they cannot fix it, and they have twenty other designs that may contain the same element and may be removed next week without warning. They appeal; the form asks them to explain why the removal was incorrect, which they cannot do without knowing what it was for; the appeal is declined with the same policy reference. A legitimate creator has lost income and learned nothing, and the platform has lost a supplier.

## Why It's Still Broken
The notice is templated because writing a specific one takes reviewer time the queue does not allow. There is a genuine concern that explaining the exact trigger helps bad actors evade it, which is applied uniformly rather than case by case. The appeal is handled by the same process that made the decision. And the creator's loss is invisible to everybody except them.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Tell the creator what to change. Name the specific element that triggered the removal — this word, this logo element, this character — which the reviewer has already identified in order to decide and which costs a click to record, and is the fix; a creator told what to change fixes it and one told nothing appeals or leaves. Distinguish the cases where specificity genuinely aids evasion from the many where it does not, since applying that concern uniformly is why nothing is explained. Offer a modification path rather than only removal, since many designs become compliant with a small change and the platform would rather have the modified version than nothing. Flag the creator's other designs containing the same element proactively, which prevents a sequence of removals and is a service rather than an enforcement action. Route appeals to a different reviewer with the original reasoning attached, since an appeal reviewed by the same process is not an appeal. Track appeal overturn rates by reviewer and by pattern, which is the only measurement of how often removals are wrong. Write the notice in plain language rather than in policy references. And measure creator retention after a removal, since it is much lower than after no removal and the gap is the true cost of handling this badly.

## Who Feels the Pain
Creators who lost income and cannot learn what to change; reviewers whose considered decision is delivered as a template; and platforms losing legitimate suppliers through a notification design choice.

## Impact If Fixed
The reviewer already identified the triggering element in order to decide, and recording it costs a click. Flagging the creator's other designs containing the same element prevents a sequence of removals and turns enforcement into a service.

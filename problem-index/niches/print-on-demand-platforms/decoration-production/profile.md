## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
This is the physical half of the business: putting a design onto an item, at unit volumes of one, across a distributed network. It is where the capital, the labour and the failure live.

It is **not terminal**, because the two dominant decoration families are unrelated contests. Digital printing — direct-to-garment, direct-to-film, sublimation, UV — is a colour reproduction and material interaction problem: the input is an image, the contest is getting its colours onto a specific substrate through a specific ink and cure process within an achievable gamut, and the failures are colour shift, washout, cracking and hand feel. Embroidery is a mechanical stitch problem: the input must be converted into a stitch program, the contest is density, underlay, pull compensation and thread behaviour on a given fabric, and the failures are puckering, registration, thread breaks and lost detail. An operation excellent at one is routinely poor at the other, the file preparation has nothing in common, and neither the preflight nor the prediction transfers. Filter Notes in the overview records the two rejected alternatives; the sub-niches below are the split.

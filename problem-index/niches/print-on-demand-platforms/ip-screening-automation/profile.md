# IP Screening Automation

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/print-on-demand-platforms|Print on Demand Platforms]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to catch the infringing uploads that exact matching misses — and whoever does that manages the exposure, because the obvious cases are already handled and the liability lives entirely in what gets through.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$460M US in screening and liability
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~8% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Moderate — obvious cases handled, grey area missed
**Target Buyer:** Platforms carrying the liability
**Automation Potential:** Very High — detection and triage are both mechanical

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Automated content screening handles obvious infringement and misses the substantial grey area. Exact logo matches and literal brand names are caught; a recognisable character redrawn in a different style, a distinctive trade dress reproduced without a mark, a stylised text treatment that is the brand's identity, a slogan with one word changed, or a copyrighted image with a filter applied are not. Those are where the enforcement notices come from, where the liability sits, and where the human review queue becomes unmanageable. Creators upload at a rate no team can inspect, which makes the screening's recall the variable that determines how much reaches a reviewer and how much reaches a customer.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Text matching against brand lists, image hashing for exact and near-duplicate matches, and keyword rules. The gaps: stylistic and structural similarity is not detected; character and trade dress recognition is absent; the rights-holder corpus is assembled ad hoc rather than maintained; screening precision is not measured, so the reviewer queue is full of false positives; and enforcement notices received are not fed back as training data.

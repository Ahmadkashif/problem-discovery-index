## Why These Niches

The business prints a unique file on a physical item once, with no proof and no sample, and eats the cost if it is wrong. Reprints and refunds are the margin, and they are driven by artwork that was never going to print well — insufficient resolution, colours outside the achievable gamut, transparency handled unexpectedly, a design positioned where a seam runs. Predicting the physical outcome before printing is therefore the largest contested surface in the category and the one that decides whether the unit economics work.

Decoration production **failed the filter as one niche**. Digital printing onto a substrate is a colour reproduction and material interaction problem: the contest is getting a file's colours onto a specific fabric through a specific process within an achievable gamut, and the failures are colour shift, washout, cracking and hand feel. Embroidery is a stitch generation problem: the contest is converting artwork into a stitch file whose density, underlay and pull compensation produce a clean result on a given fabric, and the failures are puckering, thread breaks, registration and detail loss. The file preparation, the equipment, the operator skill and the entire failure vocabulary are unrelated. Decomposed below.

The two underdigitised areas are both decisions made on the wrong variables. Routing assigns orders to facilities by distance and capacity, and the variable that determines whether the customer is happy — which facility prints this kind of work well — is not in the decision. And preflight, a mature discipline in commercial printing, ships here as a resolution and dimension check, which is not what causes customers to complain.

The two underserved constituencies are the production operator who can frequently tell a job will fail and has no route to say so, and the content reviewer deciding whether a design is infringement or legitimate parody at hundreds a day, on questions that occupy courts.

The automation niches are intellectual property screening, where the obvious cases are handled and the grey area — which is where the liability lives — is not, and the corpus mapping digital artwork to physical outcome at a scale that makes the relationship learnable and which is used for billing.

## Filter Notes

Seven of the eight level-1 niches are terminal. **Decoration Production** is not: it names the physical operation rather than a contest, and the two dominant methods inside it fail for unrelated reasons and require unrelated capabilities. Digital printing is won on colour reproduction and ink-substrate interaction, with a file that is an image and failures that are colour and durability. Embroidery is won on stitch file generation and thread-fabric behaviour, with a file that is a stitch program and failures that are mechanical. An operation excellent at one is frequently poor at the other, and the preflight, the prediction and the quality vocabulary do not transfer between them. Decomposed into two contested sub-niches.

Two candidates were rejected. *Storefront integration* was rejected because although it is the primary commercial surface it is a mature, commoditised connector layer whose remaining contest belongs to the commerce platform industries covered separately in this vault. *Creator marketplace discovery* was rejected because its contest — surfacing designs to buyers — belongs to the marketplace industries covered separately.

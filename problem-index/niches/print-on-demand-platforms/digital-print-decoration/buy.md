# Colour Management and Device Characterisation

**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Colour management is a standardised, instrumented discipline with decades of practice and available tooling, and this industry applies it inconsistently to the thing every complaint is about.
**Tags:** #numerical-methods #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #descriptive-statistics #optimal-transport #hypothesis-testing #compliance #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this sub-niche is fighting to get a screen colour onto a specific fabric so that it survives washing and matches what the customer saw — and whoever does that keeps the margin, because colour and durability are what every complaint is about.

## The Problem
Predicting what a colour will become when printed on a given device and substrate is solved. Device characterisation produces a profile; gamut mapping decides what to do with colours outside it; standardised colour differences quantify how far off a result is; and instrumentation to measure it costs less than a day of reprints. Commercial printing, packaging and photographic output all run on this. Print-on-demand handles the most substrate-variable printing in existence with profiles that are frequently absent.

## What Already Exists
Device profiling and characterisation workflows; gamut mapping with selectable rendering intents; standardised colour difference metrics with published tolerance conventions; spectrophotometry and printed characterisation targets; standardised printing condition definitions; and colour management modules in every raster processor.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to hundreds of substrate and machine combinations across facilities somebody else owns. It requires: (1) profiling at a scale and cost that suits a partner network, which means simplified characterisation targets, automated measurement and a workflow a partner will actually perform — designing that is the practical barrier rather than the colour science; (2) substrate colour as a first-class variable, since printing on a coloured garment with an underbase behaves unlike printing on white and the classical profile assumes a fixed substrate; (3) tolerances defined against consumer acceptance rather than against a brand standard, since nobody here is matching a specified colour and the question is whether a customer notices; (4) drift monitoring across facilities the platform does not control, which is a contractual and operational design problem; and (5) profiles exposed into the merchant experience rather than kept in prepress, since the merchant's colour choice is where the problem originates.

## Target Customer
Platform prepress engineering, partner facilities, colour management vendors for whom distributed textile printing is an unserved market, and merchants.

## Impact If Solved
The discipline is standardised and instrumented and is applied inconsistently to the most substrate-variable printing there is. A characterisation workflow a partner facility will actually perform is the practical barrier, not the colour science.

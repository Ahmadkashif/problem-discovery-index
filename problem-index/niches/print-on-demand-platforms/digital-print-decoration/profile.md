# Digital Print Decoration

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/print-on-demand-platforms|Print on Demand Platforms]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this sub-niche is fighting to get a screen colour onto a specific fabric so that it survives washing and matches what the customer saw — and whoever does that keeps the margin, because colour and durability are what every complaint is about.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.1B US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~18% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Moderate — colour management applied inconsistently
**Target Buyer:** Digital print production operations
**Automation Potential:** Very High — colour is a measurable, modellable relationship

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Direct-to-garment, direct-to-film, sublimation and ultraviolet printing all put ink onto a substrate, and the contest is the same across them: getting a colour that was chosen on a screen to appear on a specific fabric, through a specific ink and cure process, within a gamut that is narrower than the screen's, and to stay there through washing. The failure vocabulary is colour shift, dullness, banding, cracking, washout and hand feel. Colour management is an established discipline that solves precisely this and is applied inconsistently here, which is why the same design printed at two facilities produces two different results and why the customer's complaint is almost always about how it looks rather than about whether it printed.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Raster image processors with basic colour handling, substrate pretreatment, cure profiles, and operator adjustment. The gaps: device and substrate profiles are incomplete or absent across a partner network; gamut is not checked against the artwork before printing; the merchant's screen and the printed result are never reconciled; wash durability is not predicted; and cure parameters are not tied to outcomes.

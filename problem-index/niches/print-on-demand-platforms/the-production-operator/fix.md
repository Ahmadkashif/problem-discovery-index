# The Reprint With No Explanation

**Niche:** [[niches/print-on-demand-platforms/the-production-operator/profile|The Production Operator]]
**Industry:** [[industries/print-on-demand-platforms|Print on Demand Platforms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A reprint arrives in the queue looking like a new job, with no indication of what was wrong the first time, so the operator repeats whatever produced the failure.
**Tags:** #worker-facing #workflow-orchestration #evaluation-metrics #automation #descriptive-statistics #quick-win #data-integration #confidence-intervals
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to let the person at the press stop a job that will fail — and whoever does that saves the margin, because the operator can usually see it coming and the workflow gives them nowhere to say so.

## The Problem
A job failed inspection for colour on the first run. The reprint enters the queue as an ordinary job with a new number. The operator has no idea it is a reprint, no idea what was wrong, and no reason to do anything differently. They run it with the same settings on the same machine and it fails the same way. The item is reprinted a third time, this time at a different facility because somebody noticed, and finally ships eight days late. Two of the three runs were pure waste caused by a missing sentence on a job ticket.

## Why It's Still Broken
Reprints are generated by the order system as replacement orders, which is correct from an order management perspective and destroys the connection to the original. The inspection reason is recorded in a quality system and does not travel with the job. Nobody designed the reprint path as an information flow. And the second failure is recorded as a separate defect rather than as a repeat.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Carry the reason forward. Mark reprints as reprints and attach the original failure reason and inspection image to the job, which is a data join and is the fix — the operator can then do something different, which is the entire point. Route reprints away from the machine and settings that failed, or to a different facility where the failure suggests it, rather than back into the same queue. Require an acknowledged change before a reprint runs, so nobody repeats the first attempt by default. Report repeat failure rates as their own metric, since a reprint that fails again is a much worse event than a first failure and is currently pooled. Show the operator the inspection image, since a photograph of what was wrong is immediately actionable where a reason code is not. Escalate the second failure automatically to somebody who can change the approach, since a third attempt at the same thing is almost never the right answer. Track reprint chains end to end, which reveals the items consuming three and four runs and is invisible when each is a separate order. And feed repeat failures into the prediction and routing work, since they are the strongest evidence that a combination does not work.

## Who Feels the Pain
Operators repeating a failure they were never told about; customers waiting eight days for an item that took three attempts; and platforms paying for runs that a sentence on the ticket would have prevented.

## Impact If Fixed
The reprint arrives as a new job with the reason stripped, so the operator repeats the failure by default. Attaching the original reason and inspection image is a data join, and a required acknowledged change before rerunning is what stops the second attempt being identical to the first.

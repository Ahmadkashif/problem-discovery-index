# Live Checkout & Drops

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/live-commerce-platforms|Live Commerce Platforms]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to settle hundreds of simultaneous claims on one unit in the seconds the host is still holding it — and whoever does that without overselling or stalling captures the moment the category's revenue actually happens.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$2.3B US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~18% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — borrowed mechanics, improvised edges
**Target Buyer:** Platform commerce engineering
**Automation Potential:** High — allocation and settlement are mechanical

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A host announces a limited item and hundreds of people attempt to buy the same unit within seconds. That is not a retail checkout under load — it is a contention problem closer to ticketing, and it happens thousands of times a day across independent micro-drops rather than once for a scheduled event. The distinct contest is settling contention fairly and instantly while the host is still on camera, because the resolution is public: everyone watching sees who got it, and the seller's next hour depends on the room still believing the mechanism is fair.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Queuing and inventory reservation borrowed from ticketing, optimistic reservation with cancellation, first-tap-wins allocation, and standard payment authorisation. The gaps: allocation rules that reward connection speed rather than anything defensible; reservations that expire silently mid-show; no handling of the auction and giveaway formats hosts actually run; payment failures surfacing after the item is gone; and no reconciliation between what the host said and what the system recorded.

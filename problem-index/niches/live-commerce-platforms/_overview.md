# Niche Analysis — Live Commerce Platforms

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/live-commerce-platforms|Live Commerce Platforms]]

## Niche Selection

Live commerce is the only commerce format where supply is perishable by the hour. A stream exists for two hours, has no history, carries inventory that vanishes with it, and its value to a viewer depends entirely on what is happening in it right now. Every contest in the category derives from that: discovery has to work with no data and a deadline, checkout has to survive a burst rather than a load, moderation has to act while the violation is still airing, and the host has to do six jobs at once with a camera and a product list. The eight niches below split that surface by what competitors are actually fighting over, not by feature area.

## Why These Niches

Discovery takes the largest share because the industry's own account of itself names it as the binding constraint, and because an unwatched stream is not a deferred sale — it is a seller who does not come back. Checkout sits beside it because the live drop is closer to ticketing than to retail and is where the format's revenue is either captured or dropped. The two low-digitized niches are the parts of live selling that happen off camera and have no software at all: getting a show's worth of orders packed and shipped, and getting the seller's stock into the platform in the first place. The two underserved-audience niches are the people the format runs on, the host and the moderator, both of whom absorb the consequences of everything the platform has not built. The two automatable niches are the ones where the platform already holds the data and does not model it — the second-by-second record of what was shown, said and bought, and the audio-visual stream a violation is airing on right now.

## Filter Notes

Seven of the eight level-1 niches are terminal — a single contested statement can be written for each without qualification. Stream Discovery is not. Written as one niche it collapses into two incompatible fights: a stream that has existed for four minutes with no history has to be ranked against streams with hours of signal, which is an item cold-start problem; and the ranking objective inherited from short-form video is watch time, which is not what a commerce platform is trying to produce, which is an objective problem. A competitor can lead decisively on either while being ordinary at the other, which is the test. It therefore decomposes into **Cold-Start Stream Ranking** and **Purchase-Intent Matching**, each carrying its own contested statement.

Two candidates were considered and rejected. **Video delivery infrastructure** is a solved commodity in this category by the industry's own account, and the contest that does exist over it belongs to [[industries/edge-cdn-providers|Edge & CDN Providers]] rather than here. **Payment fraud under live velocity** is a genuine contest, but it is the contest of [[industries/payment-fraud-vendors|Payment Fraud Vendors]] as an industry, and siting it here would duplicate that analysis rather than add to it.

## The Problem
Retail merchandising analytics is a deep practice: assortment planning, placement and adjacency effects, price elasticity estimation, promotional lift measurement, and conversion analysis by department and hour. Physical retailers have built it despite terrible data — footfall estimates, no view of what a shopper considered, attribution inferred from aggregates. Live commerce has vastly better data, the sequence and the stimulus fully observed, and reports less than a supermarket does.

## What Already Exists
Assortment and space planning analytics; price elasticity and promotional lift measurement; conversion funnel analysis by segment and time; adjacency and placement effect modelling; and store benchmarking against comparable locations.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is from a spatial layout to a temporal one, with far better observation. It requires: (1) sequence replacing placement, since the analogue of shelf adjacency is what was shown before and after — an easier problem than the spatial one because the order is fully observed and controllable, which is the opportunity retail analytics never had; (2) the pitch as a measurable treatment, which has no retail equivalent at all and is the genuinely new capability; (3) elasticity estimated within a session from a live room rather than across weeks of price changes, which is faster and noisier and needs different technique; (4) the host as a persistent factor confounding everything, so comparisons must control for who is presenting — a factor retail does not have; and (5) output delivered to an individual seller in a form they will act on tonight, rather than to a merchandising team planning a season.

## Target Customer
Live commerce platforms, live sellers, brands selling live, and retail analytics vendors for whom live commerce is a better-observed version of a problem they already solve.

## Impact If Solved
Retail built this practice on far worse data — inferred footfall and aggregate attribution — while live commerce observes the entire sequence and the pitch. Sequence effects are easier than spatial adjacency because the order is fully observed and controllable.

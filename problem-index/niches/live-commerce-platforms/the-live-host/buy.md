# Broadcast Production Practice

**Niche:** [[niches/live-commerce-platforms/the-live-host/profile|The Live Host]]
**Industry:** [[industries/live-commerce-platforms|Live Commerce Platforms]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Live television solved the problem of keeping a presenter supported on air with a gallery, a producer and an earpiece, and live commerce gives one person all of those roles at once.
**Tags:** #worker-facing #workflow-orchestration #large-language-models #automation #evaluation-metrics #transformers #revenue-impact #confidence-intervals
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to take jobs off the host while they are on camera — and whoever removes enough of them decides how long and how often a seller can stream, which is the platform's supply.

## The Problem
Television shopping channels ran this format profitably for decades and never asked the presenter to do more than present. Behind them sat a producer, a director, a gallery, a rundown, an autocue, a call screener and real-time sales figures fed into an earpiece so the presenter knew when to push an item and when to move on. The entire discipline of supporting a live presenter is mature. Live commerce reproduced the format, removed the whole production apparatus, and handed the presenter a phone.

## What Already Exists
Rundown and run-of-show systems; talent support through earpiece cueing; live audience response feeding presenter direction; call and message screening; and real-time sales telemetry driving on-air decisions.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is from a crew to software, for a host with no crew at all. It requires: (1) every gallery role performed automatically and delivered to one person, which is a synthesis problem rather than a tooling problem and is why buying broadcast software does not help; (2) delivery that does not break the performance — glanceable, ambient, never demanding a read at the wrong moment, which is a far harder interface constraint than any dashboard; (3) a rundown that is adaptive rather than fixed, since live sellers legitimately reorder based on the room and a rigid run-of-show is the wrong model; (4) chat as the audience channel rather than phone calls, which is higher volume, lower quality and needs screening the broadcast tools never contemplated; and (5) costs that work for a seller turning over a few thousand dollars a night rather than for a broadcaster.

## Target Customer
Live sellers, live commerce platforms, host agencies, and broadcast tooling vendors for whom the crewless presenter is an unserved case.

## Impact If Solved
Television never asked the presenter to do more than present, and live commerce removed the entire gallery. Delivering every crew role to one person without breaking the performance is the synthesis problem, and it is harder than any of the individual tools.

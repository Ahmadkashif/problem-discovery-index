# Post-Show Fulfilment

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/live-commerce-platforms|Live Commerce Platforms]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get a show's worth of one-off orders packed, combined and shipped without the host doing it by hand at midnight — and whoever does that determines how many shows a seller can run.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.6B US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~12% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Very Low — manual, physical, unsupported
**Target Buyer:** Seller operations
**Automation Potential:** High — batching and combination are mechanical

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Everything after the stream ends is a separate business from everything during it, and the platforms have built almost nothing for it. A three-hour show generates hundreds of individual orders, many from the same buyer across different moments, for items that are frequently unique and were never catalogued. Somebody has to find each item, combine the buyer's purchases into one parcel, weigh it, buy postage and ship it — and that somebody is usually the host who has just finished performing for three hours. This is the constraint on how often a seller can go live, which makes it a constraint on the platform's supply.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Order exports to spreadsheets, consumer shipping label tools, manual combination of orders, and handwritten packing lists. The gaps: no combination of a buyer's multiple wins into one shipment; no link from an order to a physical item that was never catalogued; no batch label purchase priced for hundreds of small parcels; no packing sequence; and no handling of the buyer who keeps bidding across three consecutive shows.

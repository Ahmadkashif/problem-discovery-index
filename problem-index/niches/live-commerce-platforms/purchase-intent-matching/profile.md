# Purchase-Intent Matching

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/live-commerce-platforms|Live Commerce Platforms]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to rank streams for what a viewer will buy rather than for how long they will watch — and whoever changes the objective successfully converts an entertainment feed into a commerce channel.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.6B US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~12% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** High — optimising the wrong thing well
**Target Buyer:** Platform discovery and commerce leadership
**Automation Potential:** Very High — the objective is the product

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The ranking objective was inherited from short-form video, where watch time is the business, and live commerce never changed it. The result is a system that is excellent at keeping people watching and indifferent to whether they buy, which is a different failure from the cold-start one: the model has plenty of signal and is pointed at the wrong target. The contest is over the objective, the labels and the evaluation together — purchase is rare, delayed, correlated with price, and confounded with entertainment value, so simply swapping the label produces a worse system. A competitor can be excellent at cold start and lose entirely here.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Engagement-optimised ranking, watch-time and session-length objectives, occasional conversion reweighting, and purchase events used for reporting rather than training. The gaps: no intent signal captured distinct from attention; sparse and delayed purchase labels handled naively; no separation of browsing from buying sessions; and no accounting for price and category in the conversion target.

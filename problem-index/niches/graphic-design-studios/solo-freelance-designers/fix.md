# Source Files and Rights That Transfer Before the Invoice Is Paid

**Niche:** [[niches/graphic-design-studios/solo-freelance-designers/profile|Solo Freelance Designers]]
**Industry:** [[industries/graphic-design-studios|Graphic Design Studios]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The client asks for "the final files to review", gets the editable source and full-resolution logo, and the invoice goes unpaid because the client already has everything.
**Tags:** #workflow-orchestration #automation #revenue-impact #quick-win #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to hold the solo designer's scope and payment terms for them — counting revision rounds against the quote, holding source files until the invoice clears and transferring the licence only on payment — and whoever enforces the quoted job without the designer having the awkward conversation takes the designer.

## The Problem
Final payment is the freelancer's only leverage, and it disappears the moment the source files are sent. Clients ask for finals before paying for plausible reasons — a printer needs them, the board wants to see them — and the designer complies to avoid seeming difficult. Then payment drifts past 30, 60, 90 days.

## Why It's Still Broken
File delivery and payment run through separate tools — a file-sharing link and an invoice — so nothing links release to payment. Asking a client to pay before receiving files feels confrontational, and the protections that exist, such as New York's statute, require the freelancer to file a complaint.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Delivery through a handoff page where proofs at full visual quality are available immediately, and the editable source files, production-ready exports and signed rights assignment unlock automatically when the final invoice is paid. The printer can be given a time-limited production file directly if needed.

## Who Feels the Pain
Solo designers waiting on overdue invoices; clients' printers who need files urgently and get caught in the middle.

## Impact If Fixed
Payment and transfer happen together by default, overdue final invoices fall sharply, and the designer never has to have the awkward conversation.

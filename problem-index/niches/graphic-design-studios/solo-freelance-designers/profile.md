# Solo Freelance Designers

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/graphic-design-studios|Graphic Design Studios]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to hold the solo designer's scope and payment terms for them — counting revision rounds against the quote, holding source files until the invoice clears and transferring the licence only on payment — and whoever enforces the quoted job without the designer having the awkward conversation takes the designer.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$5B in US freelance graphic design receipts, overlapping the deliverable niches above (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~28% of combined employer and nonemployer receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — the design tools are professional-grade, but the business runs from an inbox, a spreadsheet, a PayPal account and memory
**Target Buyer:** The freelancer, who is also the creative, the account manager, the producer and the collections department
**Automation Potential:** Medium — admin, feedback consolidation, contracts and invoicing automate; winning clients and doing the work do not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
One in five US graphic designers is self-employed (BLS), and many more freelance alongside a job. They do the same deliverables as studios — identities, packaging, layouts — without a creative director to check the work, a producer to defend scope or an account manager to chase payment. Their clients arrive either directly (referrals, a portfolio on Behance or Dribbble, Instagram) or through marketplaces that take a cut and push prices down. Every tool sold to them is fighting over the same thing: where the next paying client comes from and how the money flows.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Marketplaces and lead sources: Upwork, Fiverr, 99designs by Vista (logo contests from roughly $249–$1,499 by tier), Dribbble, Contra. Back office: Bonsai, HoneyBook and Dubsado for proposals, contracts and invoices. Protection: New York's Freelance Isn't Free Act ($800 contract threshold, payment within 30 days). Gaps: marketplaces commoditise and take fees; back-office suites are generic across creative trades and don't understand revision rounds, deliverable-based scope or IP that should transfer only on payment.

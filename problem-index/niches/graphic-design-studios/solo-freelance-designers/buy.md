# Freelancer Back-Office Suites With Design-Specific Scope

**Niche:** [[niches/graphic-design-studios/solo-freelance-designers/profile|Solo Freelance Designers]]
**Industry:** [[industries/graphic-design-studios|Graphic Design Studios]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Freelancer back-office suites handle proposals, contracts and invoices for photographers, coaches and designers alike, and know nothing about revision rounds, deliverable files or licence transfer.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #workflow-orchestration #automation #revenue-impact #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to hold the solo designer's scope and payment terms for them — counting revision rounds against the quote, holding source files until the invoice clears and transferring the licence only on payment — and whoever enforces the quoted job without the designer having the awkward conversation takes the designer.

## The Problem
A designer's commercial terms are specific: a number of initial concepts, a number of revision rounds, defined deliverable files, usage rights that transfer on final payment, and fonts or stock the client must license. Generic suites turn all of this into free-text line items that nothing enforces.

## What Already Exists
Bonsai, HoneyBook and Dubsado provide proposals, contract templates, e-signature, invoicing and payment scheduling across creative trades.

## The Customization Gap
The designer version needs: (1) proposals built from deliverable types with concepts and rounds as counted units; (2) a revision counter tied to the proofing tool so both sides see the round number; (3) milestone payments linked to concept, revision and final-file stages; (4) an IP and usage schedule that becomes effective on final payment; and (5) a licence purchase list for fonts and stock generated at handoff.

## Target Customer
Freelance designers already paying for a back-office suite who keep their real terms in a separate document.

## Impact If Solved
Contract terms the designer already writes become terms the system enforces, with fewer free rounds and cleaner handoffs.

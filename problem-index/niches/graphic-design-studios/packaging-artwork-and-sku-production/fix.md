# The Copy Deck That Lives in Email

**Niche:** [[niches/graphic-design-studios/packaging-artwork-and-sku-production/profile|Packaging Artwork & SKU Production]]
**Industry:** [[industries/graphic-design-studios|Graphic Design Studios]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The copy deck exists as `v3_final_legal-approved_v2.xlsx` in three inboxes, and the studio builds forty SKUs from the wrong one.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #data-integration #compliance #quick-win #workflow-orchestration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to push every SKU's artwork through each copy or regulatory change to press-ready files right first time, and whoever turns a change around in the fewest days with zero label errors takes the account.

## The Problem
Regulatory, marketing and the brand manager each edit the copy deck and email it on. The studio receives version three while legal has approved version four. The error is found at client proof — or at the printer, or on shelf.

## Why It's Still Broken
The client's regulatory team owns the content and the studio owns the artwork, and there is no shared system between them below enterprise scale. A spreadsheet is the lowest common denominator everyone can open, so it wins.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A hosted copy deck with field-level history: each SKU's statements as structured fields, changes tracked by who and when, legal approval recorded per field, and the studio notified with a diff of exactly what changed since the version its artwork was built from. Uploaded spreadsheets are parsed into the same structure so clients don't have to change habits on day one.

## Who Feels the Pain
Production artists rebuilding files; regulatory reviewers re-checking proofs they already approved; brand managers explaining a reprint.

## Impact If Fixed
One whole class of packaging artwork error — building from the wrong version — disappears, along with the reprints and delays it causes, and the regulatory reviewer's approval finally attaches to the exact text that was printed.

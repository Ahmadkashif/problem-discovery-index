# Sign Location and Message Schedule From the Floor Plan

## The Problem
Most signs in a building are predictable: a room-identification sign at every door, a restroom sign meeting ADA rules, egress and stair signs required by code, directional signs at decision points. A designer still works through the plans sheet by sheet, placing each sign type, reading the room name and number off the plan, typing the message into Excel and checking latch side and mounting height. On a large project that is weeks of work before any design judgement is applied.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Architectural drawings arrive as PDFs more often than models, room data is inconsistent, and the market of environmental graphic design studios is small. Sign fabricators' software starts after the schedule exists.

## What to Build
Read the floor plans (PDF or model export): detect doors, swing direction, room tags, stairs, elevators and corridors; propose room-identification, regulatory and code-required signs at the right locations with latch-side placement and §703 mounting rules; pull messages from the room tags; and generate the message schedule and location plan. The designer adds the wayfinding strategy — directional and identity signs at decision points — and reviews the rest.

## Target Customer
Environmental graphic design studios working on healthcare, higher-education, airport and large commercial projects.

## Impact If Built
Weeks of mechanical placement become days of review, ADA errors are caught before bid, and designers spend their time on wayfinding logic instead of transcription.

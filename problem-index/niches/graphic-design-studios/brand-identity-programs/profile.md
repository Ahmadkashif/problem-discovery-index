# Brand Identity Programs

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/graphic-design-studios|Graphic Design Studios]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get a new identity used intact across every touchpoint after launch — through the templates, guidelines and rollout support the client's own staff and suppliers actually use — and whoever owns the rollout owns the retained relationship.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$2.5B US identity and brand-system design fees, plus rollout, template and guideline tooling (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~15% (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — the design work is fully digital, but guidelines still ship as a PDF and the studio has no view of how the identity is used after launch
**Target Buyer:** Studio founder or creative director; on the client side, the head of brand or marketing director
**Automation Potential:** Medium — rollout templates and guideline checks automate well; strategy and concept work do not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
An identity program is the studio's flagship deliverable: logo and wordmark, type system, colour, imagery rules, core applications and a guidelines document, sold as a fixed-fee project from roughly $10,000 for a small business to six figures for a mid-sized company (estimate). It is judged twice — at the reveal, and a year later by how intact the identity still is once the client's marketing team, franchisees, printers and agencies have been using it. The fee is one-off; the recurring revenue sits in the rollout: templates, new applications, guardianship. That is what separates it from the low-end logo kit (different buyer and price) and from packaging (regulated, SKU-driven production).

## Current Tools & Gaps
Brand portals such as Frontify, Bynder and Brandfolder are quote-priced for corporate brand teams. Canva's Brand Kit and templates are where client staff actually make things, often set up without the studio. Figma libraries hold digital components. The gaps: guidelines describe the identity but nothing checks the client's output against them; the studio cannot see drift until the client complains the identity "isn't working"; and the templates that determine day-to-day use are usually built by someone else.

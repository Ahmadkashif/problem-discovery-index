# Technical, Medical & Scientific Illustration

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/graphic-design-studios|Graphic Design Studios]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn CAD models, specimens and source literature into figures that a subject expert — a technical publications manager, a clinician, a journal art editor — signs off without a correction round, and whoever gets expert sign-off first time at volume takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$350M US fees for technical and assembly illustration, service-manual figures, and medical and scientific illustration (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~2–3% (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low-Medium — drawing is digital, but source material arrives as STEP files, engineering change orders, specimens, photos and marked-up PDFs, and accuracy review runs by email between illustrator and expert
**Target Buyer:** Technical publications manager at a manufacturer; medical communications director or journal art editor
**Automation Potential:** Medium — line art from CAD and change tracking automate; the expert accuracy judgement does not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
NAICS 541430 explicitly includes commercial artists producing drawings that require technical accuracy or interpretative skill, and this group's tools and buyers differ from everyone else in the industry. An assembly or service illustration must match the product revision exactly, because a technician will act on it. A medical or scientific illustration must satisfy a clinician or a journal's reviewers. Success is accuracy confirmed by an expert, not taste, and the design tools the rest of the industry uses were built for marketing designers. Patent figures, which follow the USPTO's drawing rules, belong to [[niches/drafting-services/patent-drawing-bureaus/profile|Patent Drawing Bureaus]] under drafting services and are not counted here.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Illustrator and Affinity for drawing; CAD-to-illustration tools such as SOLIDWORKS Composer and Creo Illustrate, built for in-house technical publications teams with CAD seats; 3D tools (Blender, Cinema 4D, ZBrush) for medical and scientific work. Gaps: outside illustrators receive CAD exports without the tools to use them well; engineering changes reach the illustrator as PDFs, so manual figures go stale; and expert corrections arrive scattered across email, annotated PDFs and calls.

# Expert Corrections That Arrive in Five Places

**Niche:** [[niches/graphic-design-studios/technical-and-scientific-illustration/profile|Technical, Medical & Scientific Illustration]]
**Industry:** [[industries/graphic-design-studios|Graphic Design Studios]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A medical or scientific figure goes out for expert review and comes back as an annotated PDF from one reviewer, an email from another and a phone call from a third, sometimes contradicting each other.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #bert #workflow-orchestration #worker-facing #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn CAD models, specimens and source literature into figures that a subject expert — a technical publications manager, a clinician, a journal art editor — signs off without a correction round, and whoever gets expert sign-off first time at volume takes the account.

## The Problem
Medical and scientific illustrators send each draft to the subject experts who must approve it: the surgeon who described the procedure, the researcher whose mechanism it depicts, the journal's art editor. Corrections come back in whatever channel each reviewer prefers. Some conflict, some repeat a correction already made, and some refer to an earlier draft. The illustrator reconciles them by hand, and a missed correction resurfaces in the next round or after publication.

## Why It's Still Broken
General review tools (Filestage, Ziflow, Acrobat comments) collect markup on one file but do not ask reviewers to cite the anatomy or source behind a correction, do not flag conflicts between reviewers, and are rarely used by busy clinicians, who reply by email.

## What a Fix Looks Like
One review link per figure that accepts markup, email replies and dictated notes, anchors each correction to a region of the figure, asks for a source when a correction changes content, groups duplicates, flags conflicting corrections for the commissioning editor to settle, and carries resolved items forward so the next draft shows what changed.

## Who Feels the Pain
Freelance and studio medical illustrators, scientific illustrators working for journals and research groups, and the editors who coordinate reviewers.

## Impact If Fixed
Fewer correction rounds per figure, fewer accuracy errors surviving to publication, and less unpaid reconciliation time for the illustrator.

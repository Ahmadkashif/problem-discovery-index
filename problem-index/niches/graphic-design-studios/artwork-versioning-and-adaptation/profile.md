# Artwork Versioning & Adaptation

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/graphic-design-studios|Graphic Design Studios]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to produce every size, language and channel variant from an approved master with no manual touch and no brand or legal-copy error, and whoever delivers the most compliant variants per hour takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.5B US adaptation and versioning work done in design studios and production houses (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~9% (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** High — masters, templates and delivery specs are all digital, though in most studios the re-layout itself is still done by hand, one file at a time
**Target Buyer:** Studio production director; on the client side, the marketing operations or brand production lead
**Automation Potential:** Very high — the work is rule-bound adaptation of an approved design

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Once a master layout is approved, the rest of a launch is adaptation: social formats, display ad sizes, print ads to publication specs, point-of-sale, email, out-of-home, and the whole set again in each language. The creative decision has been made; what remains is fitting it into dozens of canvases without breaking the brand or legal rules. It is the most mechanical, repeatable work a studio sells, priced per variant or per hour, and it is where junior production artists spend much of their time. Regulated pack copy belongs to the packaging artwork sub-niche; this niche is marketing and channel adaptation.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Canva's resize feature and Adobe Express and Firefly bulk-create tools for simple cases; Figma plugins and InDesign data merge; creative automation platforms such as Celtra, Bannerflow and Storyteq, built for brands' in-house teams and ad operations. Gaps: platforms are priced and set up per brand, not per studio job; print and digital formats sit in different tools; text expansion in translation breaks templates; and nothing learns the studio's own adaptation habits.

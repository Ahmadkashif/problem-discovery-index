# Packaging Design

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/graphic-design-studios|Graphic Design Studios]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is that every competitor is fighting to put a pack on shelf that sells — but that splits into two contests with different winners: proving before the retailer's shelf reset that a redesign will outsell the pack it replaces, and pushing every SKU's artwork through each copy change to press-ready files right first time. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$3B US packaging design, adaptation and artwork fees billed by design studios (estimate; global prepress groups such as SGS & Co are larger and sit partly outside NAICS 541430)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~18% (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — design and artwork are digital, but verification is by eye at small studios, mock-ups are physical, and dielines travel by email
**Target Buyer:** Packaging studio principal; on the client side, the brand manager or packaging manager at a consumer-goods company
**Automation Potential:** Mixed — high for artwork production and verification, low for brand and shelf strategy

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Packaging is the largest slice of graphic design tied to a physical, regulated product. A pack has to sell at shelf, carry legally required copy, fit a converter's structural dieline, survive a specific print process (flexo, offset, gravure or digital) on a specific substrate, and do all of this across dozens of SKUs that change every time a formula, claim or retailer requirement changes. Retailer shelf resets, regulatory compliance dates and print plate deadlines set the clock, not the studio. The converters who print the result are covered under [[industries/printing-shops|Printing Shops]].

## Current Tools & Gaps
Illustrator with Esko's DeskPack and Studio plug-ins for dielines and 3D mock-ups, and ArtPro+ at prepress houses; GlobalVision for inspection; pack testing from firms such as Behaviorally, Ipsos and Kantar; physical comps from a sample-making shop. The gaps fall into two groups with different owners and clocks — strategic evidence that a design will win at shelf, and production certainty that every file is right — which is why this niche decomposes. One gap spans both: neither the brand designer nor the artwork team can see how a design will actually print until the press proof.

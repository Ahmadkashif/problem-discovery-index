# The Dieline That Changed After the Artwork Was Built

**Industry:** [[industries/graphic-design-studios|Graphic Design Studios]]

**One-liner:** The converter revises the structural dieline by a few millimetres, emails a new PDF, and the studio finds out when the printer's preflight rejects forty files.

## The Problem
Dielines change: a new filling line, a revised glue flap, a carton board of different caliper. The converter or structural designer sends a revised PDF to whoever they think needs it. The studio may be building artwork on the old one, and nothing compares them. Copy moves into a fold or out of the safe area on every SKU in the range.

## Why It's Still Broken
Three parties own pieces of one object — the converter owns the structure, the studio owns the artwork, the brand owns approval — and the dieline travels between them as an email attachment with no version control. Each party checks its own part.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A shared dieline register per pack format, with every revision diffed automatically against the previous one (cut, crease, bleed and safe-area changes highlighted) and every artwork file built on that format checked against the new geometry, listing exactly which SKUs and panels are now wrong.

## Who Feels the Pain
Production artists rebuilding forty files on deadline; studio principals absorbing unbilled rework; brand managers whose launch slips a week.

## Impact If Fixed
Dieline changes become a known, scoped change order instead of a surprise at preflight, saving a day or more of rework per change and a missed print slot in the worst cases.

# The Contest Logo That Looks Like Someone Else's

**Niche:** [[niches/graphic-design-studios/small-business-logo-and-brand-kits/profile|Small-Business Logo & Brand Kits]]
**Industry:** [[industries/graphic-design-studios|Graphic Design Studios]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A contest draws eighty entries in a week, and the winner turns out to be adapted from a stock icon or another company's mark.
**Tags:** #contrastive-learning #cnns #k-nearest-neighbors #compliance #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to deliver a logo a small business can actually register and own — distinct from existing marks and free of unownable AI output — at a sub-$500 price, and whoever makes the mark clearable takes the customer.

## The Problem
Contest economics reward volume: designers submit speculative entries quickly for a chance at one fee, and the temptation to adapt a stock vector or a mark seen elsewhere is real. Platforms have originality policies and handle reports after the fact, but the customer has often already chosen and paid.

## Why It's Still Broken
Checking every entry by eye is impossible at contest volume, and originality is only investigated once someone complains. The customer is the person least able to recognise a borrowed mark.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A similarity check on every entry at submission against stock icon libraries, registered marks and the platform's past winners, with close matches held for review before the customer sees them and the winning design re-checked before files and rights transfer.

## Who Feels the Pain
Small business owners who build on a borrowed mark; honest contest designers undercut by copied entries; platforms handling disputes and refunds.

## Impact If Fixed
Borrowed entries are stopped before they can win, disputes and refunds fall, and the platform can stand behind the originality of what it sells.

# Small-Business Logo & Brand Kits

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/graphic-design-studios|Graphic Design Studios]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to deliver a logo a small business can actually register and own — distinct from existing marks and free of unownable AI output — at a sub-$500 price, and whoever makes the mark clearable takes the customer.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$700M US spend on low-end logos and starter brand kits through contests, marketplaces, AI logo makers and budget freelancers (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~4% (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** High — bought online, delivered as files, increasingly generated rather than drawn
**Target Buyer:** Small business owner launching or rebranding; on the supply side, the product lead at a logo platform or the budget freelancer
**Automation Potential:** Very high — generation is already automated; clearance and ownership are not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The low end of identity work has become a product. Tailor Brands claimed 3.4 million business users as early as 2017; Looka, Canva's logo templates and Wix's logo maker generate marks in minutes; 99designs by Vista sells contests in fixed tiers from roughly $249 to $1,499 that draw 30 to 100+ entries. Price and speed are solved. What none of them solves is whether the owner can register the result: template marks are reused by thousands of businesses, contest entries sometimes borrow from existing marks, the USPTO's search system cannot search by image, and under the Copyright Office's January 2025 guidance a purely prompted mark may not be the owner's to protect.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Looka, Tailor Brands, Canva, Wix and Fiverr for generation and low-cost design; 99designs by Vista contests; trademark filing services such as LegalZoom for the filing itself; USPTO design search codes for anyone willing to learn them. Gaps: no generator checks its output against registered marks or against its own other customers' logos; clearance searches from specialist providers are priced for trademark counsel; and owners discover the problem when their filing is refused or a cease-and-desist arrives.

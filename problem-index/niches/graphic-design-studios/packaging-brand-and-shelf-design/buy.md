# Predictive Attention Tools Tuned to the Shelf

**Niche:** [[niches/graphic-design-studios/packaging-brand-and-shelf-design/profile|Packaging Brand & Shelf Design]]
**Industry:** [[industries/graphic-design-studios|Graphic Design Studios]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Predictive attention tools already produce heatmaps of where eyes land; they were trained on web pages and ads, not on a pack two metres away in a bay of thirty competitors.
**Tags:** #cnns #transfer-learning #semantic-segmentation #evaluation-metrics #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to prove, before the retailer's shelf reset, that a redesign will be found faster and chosen more often than the pack it replaces, and whoever brings shelf evidence the brand manager can defend takes the account.

## The Problem
Studios use attention heatmaps in pitches because they are cheap and persuasive. On a single pack image they mostly confirm that faces, high contrast and large text attract the eye. They say little about whether the pack stands out in its category, where every competitor uses the same colour codes.

## What Already Exists
3M Visual Attention Software, Dragonfly AI and similar tools predict saliency on any image and report areas of interest; consumer research firms run real eye-tracking on shelves.

## The Customization Gap
A shelf-grade version needs: (1) evaluation in a competitive bay rather than in isolation; (2) viewing distance and angle modelled, since packs are seen small and at the periphery; (3) category colour-blocking norms, so "distinct" means distinct from this category; (4) retailer lighting and shelf-edge clutter; and (5) calibration against real shelf eye-tracking studies, with the error reported, so the heatmap is evidence rather than decoration.

## Target Customer
Packaging studios and brand managers who already buy attention tools and want results they can defend to a sceptical marketing director.

## Impact If Solved
A tool studios already pay for becomes credible shelf evidence at a fraction of the cost of a full pack test.

# The Redesign Approved on a Monitor, Judged on a Shelf

**Niche:** [[niches/graphic-design-studios/packaging-brand-and-shelf-design/profile|Packaging Brand & Shelf Design]]
**Industry:** [[industries/graphic-design-studios|Graphic Design Studios]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Routes are reviewed one pack at a time, full-screen, at a size no shopper ever sees, and chosen on details that vanish at shelf distance.
**Tags:** #diffusion-models #cnns #object-detection #automation #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to prove, before the retailer's shelf reset, that a redesign will be found faster and chosen more often than the pack it replaces, and whoever brings shelf evidence the brand manager can defend takes the account.

## The Problem
In the review meeting the pack fills a monitor. Stakeholders debate a flourish on the logo and the weight of a claim. On the shelf, the same pack is a few centimetres wide, beside the market leader and the retailer's own label, and the debated details are invisible while the colour block and silhouette decide everything.

## Why It's Still Broken
Realistic shelf comps take time to build in Photoshop from category photos, so studios make one for the final presentation, if at all. Physical shelf mock-ups cost more. The review habit follows the cheapest format.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Automatic shelf renders for every route at every review: category photographs segmented into individual facings, the candidate packs placed at true scale and shelf distance, the current pack shown in the same bay for comparison, and a squint view at the size a shopper actually sees.

## Who Feels the Pain
Brand managers approving a redesign they can't picture in store; designers whose route is killed over a detail no shopper would notice.

## Impact If Fixed
Reviews move to the decisions that matter at shelf, and weak routes die in the first meeting rather than after a pack test.

# Every Event Starts From a Blank Page

**Niche:** [[niches/procurement-spend-platforms/sourcing-category-management/profile|Sourcing & Category Management]]
**Industry:** [[industries/procurement-spend-platforms|Procurement & Spend Platforms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** An organisation sources the same categories repeatedly and retains nothing transferable between events, so the specification, the supplier list, the questions asked and the lessons learned are reconstructed by whoever runs it this time.
**Tags:** #bert #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #workflow-orchestration #automation #tacit-knowledge-ml #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in sourcing software is fighting to remove the assembly work from a sourcing event so the category manager spends the time on negotiation — and whoever cuts specification and bid normalisation effort most takes the account.

## The Problem
A category manager runs a logistics sourcing event. Three years earlier her predecessor ran one. The specification from that event exists in a folder, the supplier list is in an email, the bid analysis is in a spreadsheet on a shared drive, and the reasons the incumbent was selected and the three things that went wrong afterwards exist in nobody's record. She reconstructs most of it, repeats one of the mistakes, and leaves behind the same artefacts in the same scattered form for her successor. The organisation has sourced this category four times and has learned nothing cumulative.

## Why It's Still Broken
Sourcing events are treated as projects with deliverables rather than as a repeating process with an institutional memory, so the artefacts are outputs rather than inputs to the next one. The platforms store events and do not relate them — an event is a record, not a version of a recurring activity. And the most valuable content is the part that was never written: why a supplier was selected over another, what the negotiation actually turned on, and what went wrong in delivery, which is knowledge that exists only in the category manager's head and leaves with them.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Treat the category rather than the event as the durable object. Every event, specification, supplier list, bid response and award attaches to a category with its history, so the next event opens with the previous one rather than with a template. Capture the judgement at the moment it is made and not in a retrospective: why this supplier, what the negotiation turned on, what was conceded and what was held — three questions at award, answered in a minute, which is the difference between a record and an archive. Link delivery outcomes back to the award decision, since supplier performance after the event is the only evidence about whether the award was right and is currently held by a different team in a different system. And surface the history when the next event is created, including the uncomfortable parts — this supplier was selected last time and delivered poorly, and here is who said so — because the most valuable institutional memory is the kind that prevents a repeat.

## Who Feels the Pain
Category managers reconstructing work their predecessors did; organisations repeating sourcing mistakes at three-year intervals; and suppliers who performed badly and are re-invited because nobody recorded it.

## Impact If Fixed
Category-level continuity turns each event into a revision rather than a reconstruction, which compounds — the fourth event in a category should be substantially better than the first and currently is not. The three questions at award are the cheapest element and capture the judgement that is otherwise lost entirely when a category manager moves on.

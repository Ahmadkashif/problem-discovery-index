# Source-to-Pay Suites

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/procurement-spend-platforms|Procurement & Spend Platforms]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is that every competitor is fighting to get more of an organisation's spend through a controlled process — but running a sourcing event and routing an employee's request are different products for different users. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$3.4B US source-to-pay suite software
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~38% of procurement software revenue
**Digital Adoption:** High — the suites are deployed widely and used partially
**Target Buyer:** Chief procurement officers and procurement operations leaders
**Automation Potential:** High, and split between two entirely different workloads

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Source-to-pay is the category's dominant product form and its dominant disappointment. Implementations run for years, adoption is partial, and a substantial share of spend routes around the system entirely — which is how card-based spend management took a large share of tail spend by making the employee experience better than the requisition process. The reason the suites disappoint is visible in what replaced them: the fastest-growing entrants in recent years did not build better sourcing or better purchase orders, they built intake — the front door where an employee asks for something — and orchestration across the systems behind it. That is a different problem from the one the suites were designed around, with a different user, and treating both as one product is precisely what left the opening.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Coupa and SAP Ariba anchor the enterprise market; Jaggaer serves specific verticals; Oracle and SAP's own procurement modules serve their installed bases; Zip and Oro grew rapidly on intake and orchestration. The gap that spans the whole block is adoption: the suites measure spend under management and the number is lower than any procurement organisation would like, because the process is harder than the alternatives available to an employee with a card. What the two halves need to do about that differs completely — one by removing assembly work from category managers, the other by making the front door work for people who have never used it — which is the substance of the decomposition.

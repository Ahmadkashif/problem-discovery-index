# Contract Price Compliance

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/procurement-spend-platforms|Procurement & Spend Platforms]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in procurement controls is fighting to check the invoiced price against the contracted price at the moment of payment — and whoever closes that gap takes the savings the organisation already negotiated.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$780M US spend across contract compliance, price auditing and recovery audit services
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~9% of procurement software revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Low — three-way match is universal and price compliance is not performed
**Target Buyer:** Procurement compliance, finance and internal audit leaders
**Automation Potential:** Very High — the contract price and the invoice line are both structured and are never compared

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Procurement's central activity is negotiating prices, and the control that would confirm those prices are actually charged does not exist in most organisations. Three-way match — the universal accounts payable control — verifies that an invoice matches a purchase order and a receipt on quantity and amount. It says nothing about whether the amount reflects the contracted rate. A supplier who invoices at list rather than at the negotiated price passes three-way match perfectly, provided the purchase order carried the same wrong price, which it will if the price was never loaded into the ordering system. The result is that organisations negotiate savings carefully and then fail to realise a meaningful share of them, which is why recovery audit exists as a profession — firms that examine historical spend and take a share of what they find, on a contingent basis, because the amounts are large enough to support that model.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Three-way match is implemented everywhere. Contract lifecycle management systems hold contracts, usually as documents with metadata. Recovery audit firms find historical overpayments after the fact. The gaps are the join: contracted prices and terms live in a contract repository as a document and not as structured price data, so the ordering and invoicing systems do not know them; price changes, tier and volume breaks, rebates and indexed adjustments are administered manually; the check that does exist is retrospective and contingent, which means the profession that finds the money is paid from it rather than the leakage being prevented; and nobody measures compliance as a rate, so an organisation does not know what share of its spend is at the price it negotiated.

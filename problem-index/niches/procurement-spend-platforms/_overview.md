## Why These Niches

Spend analytics is the category's central promise and it rests on two layers that are wrong nearly everywhere: transactions classified by an engine reading a supplier name, and a supplier master containing the same vendor eleven times. Every negotiation position, consolidation opportunity, tail spend analysis and savings claim in procurement inherits both errors, which makes classification and entity resolution the contested capability beneath everything else.

The source-to-pay suites are the larger block and **failed the filter as one niche**. Running a sourcing event is a category manager's problem of specification, bid normalisation and negotiation, where the contest is how much of the event is assembly rather than judgement. Intake and orchestration is a different problem entirely — routing a request from someone who does not know what procurement needs from them — and the vendors that grew fastest in this market grew on exactly that distinction. Decomposed below.

Direct materials and public sector are the two underdigitised areas. The suites were built for indirect spend and fit direct materials badly, where the requirement flows from engineering rather than from a requisition. Public procurement runs on solicitation rules, protest exposure and transparency obligations that no commercial suite models. Suppliers and diverse suppliers are the two underserved constituencies: every product in this category serves the buyer, and the supplier absorbs the process cost of each buyer's portal, while diversity spend is reported in percentages that nobody verifies.

The automation niches are the two controls the category asserts and does not perform: checking that an invoiced price matches the contracted one, and knowing which single supplier failure would actually stop production.

## Filter Notes

**Niche 2 failed the filter as stated and was decomposed.** "Source-to-pay" is a product suite rather than a contest. Sourcing and category management is bought by category managers whose measure is how much of a sourcing event is negotiation rather than spreadsheet assembly — a specification, bid normalisation and analysis problem. Intake and orchestration is bought because employees do not know how to ask procurement for things, and its measure is whether a request routes itself correctly without a human help desk — a classification and workflow problem against an entirely different user. The market's own recent history confirms the split: the fastest-growing entrants took the second and left the first to the incumbents. Both sub-niches are terminal.

**Niches 1, 3–8 are terminal as stated.** Each names something testable: line-item classification accuracy and supplier resolution correctness, whether an engineering change reaches sourcing before production, whether a compliant solicitation runs without a specialist, what a buyer's process costs its small suppliers, whether a diversity spend claim can be verified, invoiced price against contracted price, and which single supplier failure stops the line.

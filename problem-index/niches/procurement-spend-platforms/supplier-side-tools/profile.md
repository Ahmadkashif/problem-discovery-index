# Supplier-Side Tools

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/procurement-spend-platforms|Procurement & Spend Platforms]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor building for suppliers is fighting to let a small supplier transact with a large buyer without absorbing the buyer's process cost — and whoever reduces the supplier's cost of being a supplier takes the network.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$640M US spend attributable to supplier-facing portals, networks and onboarding tooling
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~7% of procurement software revenue, paid almost entirely by buyers
**Digital Adoption:** Low from the supplier's perspective — many portals, no consolidation
**Target Buyer:** Buyers purchase; suppliers use; small suppliers bear the cost disproportionately
**Automation Potential:** High — onboarding, catalogue maintenance and invoice submission are repetitive across buyers

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Every procurement product is built for the buyer, and the supplier is modelled as a party who must comply with the buyer's process. A supplier selling to forty enterprise customers maintains forty portal logins, completes forty onboarding processes with overlapping but non-identical documentation, uploads catalogues in forty formats, submits invoices through forty mechanisms, responds to forty diversity and sustainability questionnaires, and chases payment through forty accounts payable functions. For a large supplier this is a cost of doing business absorbed by a dedicated team. For a small supplier it is a substantial share of their administrative capacity, and it is a genuine barrier to selling to large organisations — which is the same barrier that supplier diversity and small business programmes exist to lower, applied by the buyers running those programmes. Nobody builds for the supplier because the supplier is not the customer.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Supplier networks — Ariba Network above all — provide a shared identity and transaction channel and charge suppliers for it, which is a longstanding source of friction. Buyer portals are per-buyer. Invoice submission standards exist and are inconsistently honoured. The gaps: onboarding documentation is collected repeatedly by every buyer with no reuse, despite being substantially the same set; catalogue maintenance is per-buyer and per-format; payment status is opaque, so a small supplier chasing an invoice calls an accounts payable line; questionnaire responses — diversity, sustainability, security, financial — are re-answered per buyer per year; and the supplier's own view of a buyer relationship, including what was ordered against a contract and whether the price is right, does not exist.

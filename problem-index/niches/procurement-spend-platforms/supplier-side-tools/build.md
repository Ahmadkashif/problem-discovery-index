# The Supplier's Own Record Across Buyers

**Niche:** [[niches/procurement-spend-platforms/supplier-side-tools/profile|Supplier-Side Tools]]
**Industry:** [[industries/procurement-spend-platforms|Procurement & Spend Platforms]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A supplier's documentation, certifications, catalogue and transaction history are substantially the same for every buyer, and they are held separately in every buyer's system with the supplier maintaining all of them.
**Tags:** #data-integration #graph-theory #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #compliance #workflow-orchestration #automation #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor building for suppliers is fighting to let a small supplier transact with a large buyer without absorbing the buyer's process cost — and whoever reduces the supplier's cost of being a supplier takes the network.

## The Problem
A twelve-person manufacturer sells to eighteen large customers. Their office manager maintains eighteen portal accounts, uploads the same insurance certificate eighteen times a year with eighteen different expiry-tracking systems chasing her, completes eighteen annual supplier questionnaires with overlapping questions, updates pricing in eighteen catalogue formats, and tracks eighteen payment processes. The work is a meaningful fraction of one person's job at a company with twelve people, and every element of it is duplication. The buyers who imposed it each consider their own process reasonable, and none of them sees the aggregate.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The supplier is nobody's customer. Supplier networks that attempted a shared identity monetised the supplier side, which converted a solution into a grievance and limited adoption. Buyers have no incentive to accept another buyer's onboarding, partly from genuine due diligence obligations and partly from institutional habit. And the suppliers who feel it most — small ones — have the least influence over any buyer's process and no collective mechanism at all.

## What to Build
A supplier-held record that buyers can consume. The supplier maintains one profile: legal and tax identity, insurance certificates with expiries, certifications, banking details, diversity and small business status, financial statements where required, security and sustainability questionnaire responses, and a catalogue. Buyers subscribe to the parts they need, with the supplier controlling what each buyer sees, and receive updates automatically — so an insurance renewal is uploaded once and propagates to eighteen buyers rather than being chased by eighteen. Questionnaire responses are held once with buyer-specific supplements, since the overlap between buyers' questionnaires is large and the differences are few. Transaction history across buyers is the supplier's own, which gives a small supplier something it has never had: a consolidated view of its own business with its largest customers, including payment performance by buyer. The commercial model must not charge the supplier, since that is what broke the previous generation — buyers benefit from accurate current supplier data and reduced onboarding cost, and they are the party that should pay.

## Target Customer
Buyers with large small-supplier populations and supplier diversity commitments, procurement platform vendors, and the supplier communities and trade associations who could drive adoption from the other side.

## Impact If Built
The administrative burden of being a supplier to large organisations is a real barrier to small firms selling into enterprise and public sector markets, and it is imposed by the same organisations whose programmes aim to include them. A supplier-held, buyer-consumed record removes most of the duplication, and the buyers get better and more current data as a by-product than their own chasing produces.

# Intake & Orchestration

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/procurement-spend-platforms|Procurement & Spend Platforms]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in procurement intake is fighting to take a request from someone who does not know what procurement needs and route it correctly without a human help desk — and whoever routes most requests unassisted takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.6B US procurement intake, orchestration and workflow software
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~18% of procurement software revenue, and its fastest-growing segment
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — a young product category with rapid adoption
**Target Buyer:** Procurement operations leaders; the users are employees across the whole organisation
**Automation Potential:** Very High — request classification and routing are well-posed and are currently a person

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Intake is the front door: the point at which somebody in the business needs to buy something and has to engage with procurement. It turned out to be where the friction actually lived, which is why the vendors that built it well grew quickly against incumbents with far broader suites. The user is an employee who buys something twice a year, does not know what a commodity code is, does not know whether this purchase needs legal review or a security assessment or a data processing agreement, and does not know which of the eleven approvals apply. The traditional answer is a form with every field, which the employee cannot complete, and a procurement analyst who functions as a help desk answering the same questions and manually routing requests into the right processes. That analyst role exists in every procurement organisation and is almost entirely a consequence of the intake design.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Zip and Oro built the category; the suites have added intake layers in response; workflow and low-code platforms are used to build bespoke versions. Routing to the correct downstream processes — legal, security, privacy, finance, supplier onboarding — is the core function and is configured as rules. The gaps: the request is classified by asking the requester questions they cannot answer rather than by inferring from what they can describe; routing rules accumulate in the same way as the CRM routing tree and become unmaintainable; the parallel reviews that a request triggers are orchestrated sequentially in many implementations, which is a large and avoidable share of cycle time; and nobody measures whether a request was routed correctly, so misrouting is discovered when a review team receives something irrelevant.

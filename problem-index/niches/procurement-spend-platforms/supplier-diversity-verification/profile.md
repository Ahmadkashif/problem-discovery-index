# Supplier Diversity Verification

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/procurement-spend-platforms|Procurement & Spend Platforms]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in supplier diversity software is fighting to make a diverse spend claim verifiable rather than self-reported — and whoever can evidence what actually reached diverse suppliers takes the programme.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$410M US spend across supplier diversity management, certification verification and tier-two reporting
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~5% of procurement software revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Low — certification status is a PDF and tier-two spend is a supplier's own assertion
**Target Buyer:** Supplier diversity leads and procurement leadership; the diverse suppliers are the population the programme exists for
**Automation Potential:** High — certification verification and spend attribution are both mechanical and both are assertions today

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Large organisations publish supplier diversity figures — spend with minority-owned, women-owned, veteran-owned, disability-owned and small businesses — that are reported to customers, regulators and the public, and that influence contract awards in both the private and public sectors. The figures rest on two things that are rarely verified. Certification status is supplied by the supplier as a document, checked at onboarding and frequently not rechecked, so a business that lost its certification or was acquired continues to count. Tier-two spend — the diverse spend a company's own suppliers make on its behalf — is reported by those suppliers on a self-assessed basis and is included in headline numbers with no independent basis at all. The result is a published metric with real consequences for real businesses, computed from assertions. That is a measurement problem, and it is one where the parties with the strongest interest in accuracy are the diverse suppliers who are actually doing the work.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Supplier diversity management products track certifications and compute spend; certifying organisations maintain registries of certified businesses; tier-two reporting is collected through surveys and portals. The gaps: certification status is a captured document rather than a live check against the certifying body's registry, so lapses go undetected; the certifying registries are separate and are queried manually; spend attribution relies on the supplier master, so the resolution failures described in this industry's first niche flow straight into the diversity numbers; tier-two is self-reported with no audit and no methodology standard, which makes cross-company comparison meaningless; and nobody measures what diverse suppliers actually experience — win rates, contract sizes, payment terms and retention — which is the outcome the programme exists to change and is far more informative than a spend percentage.

# Evaluation Scoring With the Controls Any Assessment Process Uses

**Niche:** [[niches/procurement-spend-platforms/public-sector-procurement/profile|Public Sector Procurement]]
**Industry:** [[industries/procurement-spend-platforms|Procurement & Spend Platforms]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Structured multi-evaluator assessment with rubrics, blinding, consensus and inter-rater reliability is standard practice in grant review, academic admissions and clinical assessment, and public bid evaluation is performed in a spreadsheet.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #hypothesis-testing #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #compliance #workflow-orchestration #automation #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in public procurement software is fighting to let an agency run a compliant, protest-resistant solicitation without a specialist assembling it from precedent — and whoever makes compliance a property of the process takes the account.

## The Problem
Five evaluators score seven proposals against published criteria with stated weights. They score in a spreadsheet, in a room, frequently after discussion. One evaluator scores consistently high across everything, which shifts the ranking. Another has a relationship with a bidder that was disclosed verbally. The consensus process is a conversation. The resulting award is documented as a scoring table. If a losing bidder protests, the agency must demonstrate that the published criteria were applied as published, consistently, without bias — and the evidence is the spreadsheet.

## What Already Exists
Structured assessment with these exact controls is mature practice in grant peer review, academic admissions, clinical trial adjudication and competition judging: rubric-based scoring, independent scoring before discussion, blinding where feasible, conflict declaration, inter-rater reliability measurement, and documented consensus procedures. Software supporting it exists in each of those domains. The methodology is published and the statistical checks are elementary.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a process whose output must survive legal challenge. It requires: (1) independent scoring recorded before any discussion, with the individual scores retained — which is both better practice and the evidence a protest response needs, and which a shared spreadsheet destroys by construction; (2) rubric anchoring so that a score has a stated meaning rather than being a number on a scale, since "4 out of 5 on technical approach" is indefensible and "meets all stated requirements and demonstrates X" is defensible; (3) evaluator calibration statistics — leniency, dispersion, agreement with the panel — computed and surfaced, since a systematically lenient evaluator shifts an award and nobody currently detects it; (4) conflict declaration as a structured, recorded step rather than a verbal disclosure, which is the most common protest ground and the easiest to prevent; and (5) the consensus process recorded with its reasoning, since the transition from individual scores to a panel position is precisely what a protest examines and is currently a conversation.

## Target Customer
Public procurement platforms, agencies running evaluated solicitations, and the cooperative and state systems that could mandate the practice across many buyers.

## Impact If Solved
The controls are borrowed wholesale from assessment domains that have used them for decades, and they simultaneously improve the decision and produce the defensibility the process actually requires. Independent scoring before discussion is the single highest-value change and costs nothing but sequencing.

# Public Sector Procurement

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/procurement-spend-platforms|Procurement & Spend Platforms]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in public procurement software is fighting to let an agency run a compliant, protest-resistant solicitation without a specialist assembling it from precedent — and whoever makes compliance a property of the process takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$980M US software spend across state, local, education and federal procurement
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~11% of procurement software revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Low — solicitation documents are assembled in word processors from prior solicitations
**Target Buyer:** Procurement officers at agencies, school districts, municipalities and higher education
**Automation Potential:** High — solicitation assembly, compliance checking and bid evaluation are rule-governed and manual

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Public procurement is governed rather than optimised. A solicitation must follow a prescribed process, include required clauses, provide adequate notice, apply the stated evaluation criteria exactly as published, document the basis of award, and withstand a protest from a losing bidder — which is a real and consequential risk that shapes every decision. The rules come from statute, regulation, agency policy and funding source conditions simultaneously, and a federally funded project run by a school district carries all four. The people doing the work are procurement officers who are frequently sole practitioners in their agency, assembling solicitations from prior ones, applying evaluation criteria in spreadsheets, and documenting for a file that may be examined. The commercial suites optimise for savings and cycle time, which are secondary here; what matters is defensibility, and no commercial product is designed around it.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Specialist public procurement platforms — Bonfire, OpenGov, Periscope and the state-run e-procurement systems — handle solicitation posting, bid receipt and evaluation workflow; larger agencies run ERP procurement modules. Bid notification services reach suppliers. The gaps: solicitation documents are assembled from precedent, so an error or an outdated clause propagates through every subsequent solicitation that copied it; required clause sets vary by funding source and are tracked in checklists; evaluation scoring is performed in spreadsheets, which is the most protest-exposed step in the process and the least controlled; the award documentation that would defend a protest is assembled after the fact; and cooperative purchasing agreements — which let an agency buy off another's competitively awarded contract — are underused because finding an applicable one is a manual search.

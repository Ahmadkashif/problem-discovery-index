# Books Rejected for Noise Floor After Mastering

**Niche:** [[niches/recording-studios/backlist-volume-narration/profile|Backlist & Independent Volume Narration]]
**Industry:** [[industries/recording-studios|Recording Studios]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A home-booth narrator finds out the noise floor was too high only when the finished book fails the retailer's check, and every chapter has to be reprocessed or re-recorded.
**Tags:** #cnns #evaluation-metrics #automation #quick-win #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to cut the edit, proof and pickup hours behind each finished hour without letting misreads through publisher QC, and whoever gets closest to one clean pass per finished hour keeps backlist programmes from moving to AI narration.

## The Problem
ACX requires a noise floor no higher than -60 dB RMS, loudness between -23 and -18 dB RMS and peaks below -3 dB. A home booth that is fine in winter picks up an air conditioner in summer; a new computer fan adds hum; a neighbour starts building work. The narrator records for days, masters at the end, and only then learns that files fail, or that aggressive noise reduction to pass the check has left the voice sounding processed.

## Why It's Still Broken
Spec checks are done at the end because that is where the tools sit: a mastering plug-in or the retailer's upload checker. Nothing measures the room at the start of each session, and narrators don't hear low-level noise once they're used to their booth.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A session-start check that records ten seconds of room tone and a test read, measures noise floor and spectrum against the narrator's own best sessions and the retailer's spec, names the likely source (hum, broadband, intermittent), and blocks recording until it passes. Per-chapter checks run automatically as files are saved, so a problem is caught after one chapter, not twenty.

## Who Feels the Pain
Home-booth narrators producing their own books; production houses receiving files from remote narrators.

## Impact If Fixed
Whole-book reprocessing and re-records for technical failure largely disappear, and narrators stop applying heavy noise reduction that makes their audio worse.

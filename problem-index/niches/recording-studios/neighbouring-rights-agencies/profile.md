# Neighbouring Rights & Session Royalty Agencies

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/recording-studios|Recording Studios]]
**Category:** Insight Layer
**Value-Chain Position:** Payer & intermediary layer

## What They Do
Private agencies register artists' and labels' recordings, with full performer line-ups, at collecting societies around the world and claim the performance royalties those societies pay for sound recordings (neighbouring rights), keeping a commission on what they collect. The work is research: establishing who played and sang on each recording, matching recordings across societies' databases, and chasing claims before they lapse. Featured artists, session musicians, producers and labels pay through the commission.

## Insight Function
**Size:** ~10–60 registration, research and claims staff at the larger agencies (estimate)
**Output:** Registrations, line-up determinations, claims and collected royalties
**Proprietary data:** Line-up and claims histories across many clients' catalogues, and records of which societies pay what for which uses
**Clock:** The distribution windows of the societies they claim from: EU societies must distribute within nine months of the end of the collection year and treat money still unidentified three years after that year as non-distributable (Directive 2014/26/EU, Article 13), so a claim has to land before then
**Buyer:** Head of neighbouring rights operations

**Kill switches:** none live. The repertoire belongs to clients, but line-ups and claims are factual records the agency builds as its service, not confidential client data reused elsewhere.
**Verdict:** Logged — below threshold. The closest this value chain comes to a research product, since collections depend on the line-up research. It stops short on Q3 and Q5. Much of the matching data sits with the societies the agencies claim from, which are profiled as [[niches/recording-studios/soundexchange-royalty-collective/profile|🔍 Sound-Recording & Performer Collecting Societies]] (51), so the agencies' own corpus is thinner than it looks. And the field is a few dozen agencies whose heads are often their founders.

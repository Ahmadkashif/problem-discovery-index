# Title Management Systems Extended to Audio Production

**Industry:** [[industries/recording-studios|Recording Studios]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Publishers track every print title's schedule and metadata in title management software, then run audio production for the same title in a spreadsheet.

Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## The Problem
An audio producer at a publisher juggles dozens of titles in flight: narrator casting and contracts, studio bookings, recording progress, proofing status, pickups outstanding, retailer delivery and metadata. The print side of the same title lives in a title management system; the audio side lives in spreadsheets and email, and the two drift apart on on-sale dates, ISBNs and contributor credits.

## What Already Exists
Title management and rights systems used by publishers (Firebrand, Klopotek and similar) that hold schedules, metadata, contributors and rights for each title and feed ONIX metadata to retailers. General project tools such as Airtable and Monday.

## The Customization Gap
Audio needs fields and logic print doesn't: estimated and actual finished hours; narrator, director, engineer and proofer assignments with per-finished-hour rates; union terms where the narration is under a SAG-AFTRA agreement; recording progress by chapter; pickup counts and status; retailer-specific technical specs and delivery status; and audio rights windows, which often differ from print. Narrator and contributor credits must flow into the same ONIX record as the print edition.

## Target Customer
Directors of audio production at trade publishers and the production houses that work for them.

## Impact If Solved
One record per title across print and audio removes double entry and the missed on-sale dates and credit errors that come from it, and gives the publisher, for the first time, a view of cost per finished hour by narrator and studio.

# Audiobook Production

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/recording-studios|Recording Studios]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is that every competitor is fighting to deliver finished hours a publisher accepts, but on a lead title the account goes to whoever casts and directs the performance listeners single out, and on backlist and independent volume it goes to whoever needs the fewest edit, proof and pickup hours per finished hour. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$400M US spend on audiobook narration, direction, engineering and proofing (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~12% of receipts including the solo layer (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium-High — recording, editing and delivery are digital; casting, pronunciation research, pickup tracking and series continuity run on spreadsheets, email and the narrator's memory
**Target Buyer:** Director of audio production at a publisher; owner of an audiobook production house
**Automation Potential:** High for proofing, editing and spec compliance; low for performance and casting

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
It is the largest line of 512290 work and the only one where correctness is defined by a text: the manuscript says what the narrator should have said. It is paid per finished hour, delivered to a retailer's technical spec (ACX: -23 to -18 dB RMS, peaks below -3 dB, noise floor below -60 dB), and faces direct substitution, since Audible opened AI narration to publishers in May 2025. US audiobook sales reached $2.43B in 2025 across 750,000+ active titles, and AI-narrated titles were 0.03% of that revenue. The work is done by production houses, publishers' in-house studios and independent narrators recording at home.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Pro Tools, Adobe Audition and Reaper for recording and editing; Source-Connect for remote direction; Pozotron for script-aligned proofing; ACX and Findaway Voices for distribution; spreadsheets for casting, pronunciations and pickups. The gaps that both sub-niches share: pronunciation research done title by title from scratch, production status tracked outside the publisher's title systems, and character voices in long series kept in the narrator's head.

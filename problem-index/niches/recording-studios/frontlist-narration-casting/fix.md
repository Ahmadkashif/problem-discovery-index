# Pickups Chased After the Narrator Has Left Town

**Industry:** [[industries/recording-studios|Recording Studios]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Proofing starts after the sessions end, so the misreads it finds on a lead title have to be fixed by booking a busy narrator again, sometimes days before the on-sale date.

**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to cast and direct the narration that listeners and reviewers single out on a publisher's lead title, ready for the print on-sale date, and whoever delivers performances that lift a title's audio sales keeps the publisher's frontlist.

## The Problem
A name narrator or an author records a lead title over several days. Proofing against the manuscript happens afterwards, often by a freelancer, and turns up missed lines, misreads and mispronunciations. Getting the narrator back means matching schedules, mic setup and vocal tone weeks later, while the release date doesn't move.

## Why It's Still Broken
Proofing is organised as a separate step done by a separate person at a separate time, a structure inherited from print proofreading. Directors catch some errors live, but no one can follow the manuscript word for word while also directing performance.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Live proofing during the session: speech recognition aligned to the manuscript flags missed, added and misread words within seconds of the take, so the director can ask for the pickup while the narrator is still in the booth and warmed up. A pickup queue at the end of each day closes the session with nothing outstanding.

## Who Feels the Pain
Directors and producers on lead titles; narrators recalled for short pickup sessions; publishers whose on-sale date is at risk.

## Impact If Fixed
Most frontlist pickup sessions disappear, along with the tone-matching problems that make pickups audible to listeners.

# Frontlist Casting & Directed Narration

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/recording-studios|Recording Studios]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to cast and direct the narration that listeners and reviewers single out on a publisher's lead title, ready for the print on-sale date, and whoever delivers performances that lift a title's audio sales keeps the publisher's frontlist.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$150M US spend on frontlist narration, casting and direction (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~4% (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — sessions are recorded digitally and often directed remotely; casting runs on demo reels, agent relationships and the director's memory
**Target Buyer:** Executive producer or director of audio production at a trade publisher
**Automation Potential:** Low for casting and performance; medium for session logistics and live proofing

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Lead titles are recorded under time pressure to release with the print edition, often with name narrators, full casts or the author reading, and frequently under SAG-AFTRA terms. The publisher pays for performance, not throughput: a narrator listeners love sells the series. The decision that matters is casting, then direction in the booth, and both rest on a director's ear for which voice suits which book.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Demo reels from agents and narrator directories; Source-Connect and similar tools for remote-directed sessions; studio time at production houses; Pozotron for proofing after the session. The gaps: casting decisions are never compared with what the audio later sold or how reviewers responded; directors mark scripts on paper or PDF; pickups are found after the narrator has left.

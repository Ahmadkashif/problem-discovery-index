# Pitch-Correction Settings Turned Into an Artist Profile

**Niche:** [[niches/recording-studios/vocal-editing-and-comping/profile|Vocal Editing, Tuning & Comping]]
**Industry:** [[industries/recording-studios|Recording Studios]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Pitch-correction tools correct notes brilliantly but start every session from zero, though each artist has a tuning style their editor learned the hard way.
**Tags:** #feature-engineering #gradient-boosting #transfer-learning #automation #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn a night of raw vocal takes into a tuned, timed comp the artist hears as their own performance by the next morning, and whoever's comps need the fewest artist overrides gets the producer's next session.

## The Problem
One singer's scoops into notes are part of their sound and must be left alone; another's vibrato must be preserved while the centre pitch is tightened; a third wants hard, audible correction as an effect. A vocal editor learns this over several songs and redoes it every session, and a new editor on the project gets it wrong until corrected.

## What Already Exists
Melodyne and Auto-Tune, with note-level editing, macros for pitch centre and drift, and presets; DAW templates for routing.

## The Customization Gap
What's missing is a profile per artist learned from approved edits: which deviations are style and which are errors, how much pitch drift and vibrato to keep by section, how transitions between notes are handled, and how hard to correct in which genre context. Applied as a first pass in the pitch tool, it would produce edits the editor reviews rather than performs, and it would let a project change editors without losing the artist's tuning.

## Target Customer
Freelance vocal editors and producers working repeatedly with the same artists; the pitch-correction vendors.

## Impact If Solved
Less editing time per song and fewer artist complaints that a vocal sounds "tuned", the most common reason comps are reopened.

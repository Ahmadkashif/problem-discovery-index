# Vocal Editing, Tuning & Comping

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/recording-studios|Recording Studios]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn a night of raw vocal takes into a tuned, timed comp the artist hears as their own performance by the next morning, and whoever's comps need the fewest artist overrides gets the producer's next session.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$100M US spend on freelance and in-house vocal comping, tuning and alignment (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~3% (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** High — entirely in the DAW, with comp choices recorded in playlists and comp lanes
**Target Buyer:** Producer; freelance vocal editor; assistant engineer at a production studio
**Automation Potential:** High — the work repeats song after song and the past choices are stored in the session

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
In modern pop, hip-hop and country production, the lead vocal is built rather than sung once: eight to twenty takes are comped phrase by phrase, pitch-corrected in Melodyne or Auto-Tune, timing-tightened, and doubles and harmonies aligned to the lead with VocAlign or Revoice Pro. Freelance vocal editors do it overnight, paid per song (~$50–150, estimate), and assistants do it in-house. It is the most mechanical, repeatable work in music production, with a clear test: does the artist accept the comp as their performance, or reopen it phrase by phrase?

## Current Tools & Gaps
Pro Tools playlists and Logic comp lanes; Melodyne and Auto-Tune for pitch; VocAlign and Revoice Pro for alignment; iZotope RX for breaths and noise. The gaps: no tool learns a producer's comp preferences from past songs, no per-artist tuning profile carries over between sessions, and aligning stacked vocals still fails on breaths and consonants and needs manual repair.

# Mastering & Delivery for Streaming and Spatial Formats

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/recording-studios|Recording Studios]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to deliver masters, stereo and Dolby Atmos, that pass every platform's and label's delivery checks first time and still sound right on earbuds, cars and club systems, and whoever has the fewest rejections and re-requests keeps the label's catalogue work.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$250M US spend on mastering and format delivery, human and automated (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~7% (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** High — entirely file-based, with delivery specs scattered across platform documents and label emails
**Target Buyer:** Owner of a mastering studio; label production or catalogue manager
**Automation Potential:** High for loudness, format and spec compliance; medium for tonal judgement

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Mastering is the last step before release: final tonal balance and level, sequencing, and the files each outlet needs. Those are streaming masters (Spotify normalises to -14 LUFS and recommends true peak below -1 dBTP), high-resolution files, vinyl pre-masters, a DDP image for CD, Dolby Atmos files for Apple Music and others, and stems, with ISRCs and metadata embedded. AI masters cost about $10 a track from LANDR, while a human master runs ~$70–100 a song and far more at top rooms. Apple's up-to-10% royalty boost for tracks available in Spatial Audio has made Atmos deliverables, and their checks, part of the catalogue work labels commission.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Ozone 12 with its Master Assistant, LANDR, WaveLab, Sequoia and Pyramix for mastering and DDP; the Dolby Atmos Renderer for spatial files; loudness and true-peak meters. The gaps: each platform's and label's spec checked by hand, Atmos files checked by listening to binaural and stereo fold-downs, and no record of how masters translated once released.

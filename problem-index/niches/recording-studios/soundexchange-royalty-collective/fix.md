# Session Line-Ups Rebuilt From Liner Notes Years Later

**Niche:** [[niches/recording-studios/soundexchange-royalty-collective/profile|Sound-Recording & Performer Collecting Societies]]
**Industry:** [[industries/recording-studios|Recording Studios]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A recording's line-up is certain only on the day of the session, and societies reconstruct it later from liner notes, union paperwork and performers' memories.
**Tags:** #data-integration #workflow-orchestration #compliance #quick-win #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this pocket is fighting to identify every recording played, and every rights owner and performer on it, and pay them inside the distribution window before unidentified money becomes non-distributable, and whoever matches plays to recordings and line-ups most accurately and fastest keeps the labels and performers, and the international mandates, whose repertoire makes its licence worth paying for.

## The Problem
The studio knows who played on each track. The booking shows who was in the room, and the session file shows who recorded what. None of it travels to the society. By the time the recording is played on the radio, the line-up the society receives is whatever the label registered, usually the featured artist alone.

## Why It's Still Broken
There is no channel from studio to society. Distributors' upload forms collect contributors loosely, studios have no reason to send session data anywhere, and a DDEX standard for studio-captured recording information exists but is rarely used (estimate). Societies wait for claims instead.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Accept line-ups at the source. Studios and producers export per-track credits from the session record in a standard format (see [[problems/recording-studios/low-impact-2|🟡 Credits and Royalty Directions That Never Leave the Room]]). Distributors pass them with the release, and the society pre-registers the performers against the recording's ISRC before the first play is reported. Performers confirm rather than claim.

## Who Feels the Pain
Session musicians and backing vocalists paid years late or never; society researchers rebuilding line-ups by hand.

## Impact If Fixed
Line-ups known at release for a growing share of new recordings, and research effort concentrated on the back catalogue where it can't be avoided.

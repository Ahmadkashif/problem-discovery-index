# Sound-Recording & Performer Collecting Societies

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/recording-studios|Recording Studios]]
**Category:** Insight Layer
**Value-Chain Position:** Payer & intermediary layer
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this pocket is fighting to identify every recording played, and every rights owner and performer on it, and pay them inside the distribution window before unidentified money becomes non-distributable, and whoever matches plays to recordings and line-ups most accurately and fastest keeps the labels and performers, and the international mandates, whose repertoire makes its licence worth paying for.

## What They Do
Collective management organisations for sound recordings and the performances on them: SoundExchange in the US, PPL in the UK, GVL in Germany, SCPP and SPPF in France, SENA in the Netherlands, Re:Sound in Canada and their peers. They license broadcasters, venues and digital services for the use of recorded music. They then match reported plays to recordings, determine who owns each recording and who performed on it, and pay labels, featured artists and session performers after an administration deduction. SoundExchange distributed $494.7M in the first half of 2025, and IFPI puts global performance-rights revenue for recorded music at ~$2.9B in 2025. On the performer side, SCAPR's 61 member societies in 45 countries collected €955M in 2023 and exchanged €191M among themselves under bilateral agreements.

## Insight Function
**Size:** ~50–250 repertoire, line-up research, matching, distribution and data staff at a large society, excluding licensing sales (estimate)
**Output:** Distributions and statements to rights owners and performers, the matching, ownership and line-up determinations behind them, and evidence for tariff and rate cases
**Proprietary data:** Usage reports from every licensee joined to recordings, ownership and performer line-ups built over decades, plus the shared international performer and recording identifiers (SCAPR's IPD and VRDB)
**Clock:** EU societies, and UK ones under the regulations that implemented the same directive, must distribute within nine months of the end of the financial year of collection and treat money still unidentified three years after that year as non-distributable (Directive 2014/26/EU, Article 13). SoundExchange distributes monthly under rates and rules set by the Copyright Royalty Board.
**Buyer:** Chief distribution officer or head of repertoire and data

This pocket was first scored as SoundExchange alone, a US market of one (Q1 2, Q5 1, 37). It is rescoped here to the field of sound-recording and performer societies that do the same distribution matching under the same directive, and scored against its composition-side twin [[niches/music-publishers/collecting-societies-distribution/profile|🔍 Performing Rights Organisations & Collecting Societies]] (51, Q1 4, Q4 4, Q5 4).
- **Q1 = 4.** Labels and performers pay the administration deduction for the matching and for the ownership and line-up determination behind every distribution, and they judge societies on it. In the EU they can place their rights with the society of their choice. For collection abroad they choose between a society's international service and private agencies ([[niches/recording-studios/neighbouring-rights-agencies/profile|🔍 Neighbouring Rights & Session Royalty Agencies]]). It is not 5 because licensees pay for access to repertoire, not for the analysis. The deciding US difference is that SoundExchange is the single designated collective, so US rightholders cannot move their statutory royalties elsewhere. On its own that would score 3.
- **Q4 = 4.** The Article 13 windows bind the European majority of the field, and most SCAPR members are European. It is not 5 because the US collective works to Copyright Royalty Board rules rather than this directive.
- **Q5 = 4.** The field is fragmented. SCAPR alone counts 61 performer societies in 45 countries, and producer societies, some run jointly with performers, add more, roughly 80–100 organisations worldwide (estimate), each with an accountable distribution head. They exchange data through bilateral agreements and the shared IPD and VRDB, so a reference at one carries to the next. That is fewer than the ~228 composition societies, and many are small, but well beyond a handful. Scored for the US alone, the pocket would get Q1 3, Q4 3 and Q5 1, a total of 40.

The Copyright Royalty Board, which sets SoundExchange's rates, is profiled as [[niches/music-publishers/copyright-royalty-board/profile|🔍 Copyright Royalty Board]].

**Kill switches:** none live, judged one by one. *Data isn't theirs:* licensees report usage precisely so that it can be distributed, and members register recordings and line-ups for that purpose. *Entrenched incumbent:* no single vendor owns the field's distribution systems; societies run their own platforms or contract vendors, and the IPD and VRDB are shared infrastructure, not a lock-in (estimate). *Compliance gate:* statutory designation and tariff regulation govern licensing, not the purchase of distribution tooling, and no MNPI, privilege or regulated-data regime applies.
**Verdict:** Qualified — indexed

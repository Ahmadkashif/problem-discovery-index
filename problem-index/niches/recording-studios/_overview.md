# Niche Analysis — Recording Studios

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/recording-studios|Recording Studios]]

## Niche Selection

Recording studios sell two kinds of work under two NAICS codes. Music studios record, mix and master for artists and labels by the hour or the song. Audio production without a tracking room (audiobooks, voice-over, sound design, jingles and stock sound) is sold by the finished hour or the job. Both depend on an ear nobody measures, the engineer's or director's judgement of what the client means and when the work is done. Both lose margin in the work around that judgement: revisions, proofing, pickups, recalls, bookings and delivery specs. The eight niches below split the industry by what studios, freelancers and their vendors actually compete over. Market sizes are estimates of US spend on that function or by that segment, employer and solo combined. They overlap: the self-funded artist segment buys tracking, mixing and mastering, so it isn't additive with the function niches. All are estimates.

## Why These Niches

Mixing and audiobook production carry the money that can be addressed as a function. Mixing is the music side's largest per-song service, sold nationally and remotely, and the place where revision rounds decide profit. Audiobooks are the largest line of 512290 work, with US sales of $2.43B in 2025 and AI narration aimed squarely at production. The two low-digitized niches are where physical media still sets the pace. Vinyl cutting runs on decades-old lathes, a single lacquer supplier since 2020 and test pressings judged by ear. Archival transfer runs on obsolete tape machines, handwritten logs and a degradation window IASA put at 10–15 years. The two underserved audiences are the artist paying from their own pocket, who needs cost certainty that label-shaped workflows don't offer, and the voice actor running a one-person studio at home, auditioning blind and exposed to synthetic voices. The two automatable niches are the most mechanical work in the chain: preparing and checking masters for every platform's spec, and turning a night of vocal takes into a tuned, aligned comp.

## Filter Notes

Seven of the eight level-1 niches are terminal. **Audiobook Production** is not. Its contested sentence came out as "deliver finished hours a publisher accepts", which is true and decides nothing, because what wins the account depends on which titles are being produced. On a publisher's lead title the buyer is an audio executive paying for performance: a name narrator, a full cast or the author, cast and directed to be ready for the print on-sale date. The account goes to whoever's casting and direction listeners single out. On backlist, genre series and self-published titles, the buyer pays a modest per-finished-hour rate and is weighing AI narration, which Audible opened to publishers in May 2025. There the account goes to whoever needs the fewest edit, proof and pickup hours per finished hour while still passing QC. Different buyers, competitors and winners, so the niche decomposes into **Frontlist Casting & Directed Narration** and **Backlist & Independent Volume Narration**. The parent keeps the work both share: pronunciation research, production tracking and series continuity.

Four terminal calls were close. **Mastering & Delivery** could split into stereo mastering and Dolby Atmos catalogue work, which Apple's up-to-10% Spatial Audio boost created. It stays whole because Apple pays on availability, so the contest for Atmos deliverables is the same as for stereo: files that pass every check first time and translate on headphones. The Atmos remix itself is mixing work. **Self-Funded Independent Artists** had to clear the bar that "get more bookings" fails. It passes because the contest is estimating a project's real hours so a fixed price holds, and the studio that estimates best is not necessarily the one that markets best. **Home-Booth Voice Actors** has two motions, being cast and protecting the voice from synthetic replicas, but one winner: actors stay with the platform that books them, and replica protection is a feature of that platform. **Vocal Editing, Tuning & Comping** overlaps with mixing. It is kept separate because it is a different worker on a different clock (an overnight per-song job) judged on a different test: whether the artist accepts the performance as their own.

Four candidates were considered and rejected, and several boundaries settled. **Commercial tracking rooms and scoring stages** were rejected because the contest came out as "keep the big-room sessions home studios can't do", a capacity contest rather than a workflow one. Scoring stages are a handful worldwide competing on union terms and incentive geography (estimate). **Rehearsal rooms and self-service chains** came out as "fill off-peak hours", a generic utilisation contest; the chains are logged in Pass 2. **Advertising audio, jingles and sonic branding** turned on winning agency pitches, a creative relationship rather than a workflow. [[industries/postproduction-studios|Postproduction Studios]] handled advertising post differently, folding its craft into finishing and its versioning into mastering rather than making it a niche, and the recording side of ad audio needs no niche of its own here either. **Production music and stock sound libraries** are named in the 512290 index, but they license catalogues rather than produce for clients by the hour or project, so they are logged in Pass 2. Their contests are already covered under [[industries/music-publishers|Music Publishers]]: answering sync briefs in [[niches/music-publishers/brief-response-and-pitch-curation/profile|🎯 Brief Response & Pitch Curation]], and the one-stop control that lets a library clear fast in [[niches/music-publishers/split-copyright-sync-clearance/profile|🎯 Split-Copyright Sync Clearance & Quoting]]. Subscription stock audio stays with [[industries/stock-media-marketplaces|Stock Media Marketplaces]]. On boundaries: picture sound mixing, which the 512240 definition also names, stays with [[industries/postproduction-studios|Postproduction Studios]]. Podcast production stays with [[industries/podcasting-networks|Podcasting Networks]], dubbing with [[industries/localization-services|Localization Services]], and vinyl pressing with the media replication plants build, still queued. Conference and meeting recording, also in the 512290 index, is event audio-visual work, logged under [[industries/event-planning|Event Planning]].

## Why These Pockets

One pocket qualifies, and it sits downstream of the studio rather than in it. Studios themselves fail the gate: they sell time and deliverables, and even the largest employ engineers, not analysts. The qualifier is the field of sound-recording and performer collecting societies (51): SoundExchange, PPL, GVL, SCPP and SPPF, SENA, Re:Sound and the 61 performer societies in SCAPR. Each licenses the use of recorded music, matches the reported plays to recordings, determines who owns each recording and who performed on it, and pays out after a deduction members judge them on. Under Article 13 of Directive 2014/26/EU, it must do so within nine months of the collection year and loses unidentified money as non-distributable after three years. The pocket was first scored as SoundExchange alone, a US market of one (37). Rescoped to the field, which works under the same directive and buys distribution tooling one society at a time, it scores like its composition-side twin under Music Publishers, [[niches/music-publishers/collecting-societies-distribution/profile|🔍 Performing Rights Organisations & Collecting Societies]] (51). The gap this industry adds is the line-up. Labels register owners and featured artists, but the session players' line-ups are reconstructed years later from claims, although the studio knew them on the day.

The strongest near-miss is the private neighbouring-rights agencies (43), which register line-ups and claim from those same societies against the same windows. They stop short because the societies hold most of the matching data and the agencies are a few dozen firms. The AFM & SAG-AFTRA Fund (32) does line-up research too, but takes a fixed statutory share no performer can move elsewhere. Labels (34) and audiobook groups (27) hold what recordings cost and earn but monetise it through their own releases. Credits databases (28) sell credits as data, but the leader, Jaxsta, closed in November 2025. The suppliers that sit on the session or the audio itself fail the gate or score low: synthetic-voice and AI mastering vendors employ engineers building products, and DAW makers' research (22) serves their own roadmaps.

Several pockets that touch this industry are already profiled elsewhere and get no second profile. Royalty audits of labels' statements to artists and producers are done by [[niches/music-publishers/music-royalty-audit-practices/profile|🔍 Music Royalty Audit Practices]] (46, kill switch), the same ruling as [[niches/film-distributors/participation-royalty-auditors/profile|🔍 Profit Participation & Royalty Auditors]] (46). Master catalogues are valued by [[niches/music-publishers/music-catalogue-valuation-specialists/profile|🔍 Music Catalogue Valuation Specialists]] (47) and, for reporting, by [[niches/investment-banking-boutiques/financial-reporting-valuation/profile|🔍 Financial Reporting Valuation (Purchase Price Allocation & Impairment)]] (50). Consumption and chart data is [[niches/music-publishers/music-consumption-data-providers/profile|🔍 Music Consumption Data & Chart Providers]] (43). The consumer research firm that fields the Audio Publishers Association's survey is [[niches/podcasting-networks/consumer-audio-behaviour-research/profile|🔍 Consumer Audio Behaviour Research]] (41). Rights determination at scale is [[niches/independent-publishers/permissions-rights-licensing-agencies/profile|🔍 Permissions & Rights Licensing Agencies]] (53), the Copyright Office is [[niches/independent-publishers/copyright-office-registration/profile|🔍 Copyright Registration & Policy]] (42), and the body that sets SoundExchange's rates is [[niches/music-publishers/copyright-royalty-board/profile|🔍 Copyright Royalty Board]]. Conference and event audio is [[niches/event-planning/av-production-technical-teams/profile|🔍 Event Production & AV Technical Teams]] (fails gate). For a research-insight engine, the opening in this value chain is not the studios but the societies that pay for what was recorded in them.

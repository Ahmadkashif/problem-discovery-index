# Auditions Lost to the Room, Not the Read

**Niche:** [[niches/recording-studios/home-booth-voice-actors/profile|Home-Booth Voice Actors]]
**Industry:** [[industries/recording-studios|Recording Studios]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Casting clients skip auditions within seconds for room echo, hum or clipping, and the actor never learns that the booth, not the voice, lost the job.
**Tags:** #cnns #evaluation-metrics #automation #worker-facing #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to put a home-booth actor's audition in front of the casting decisions it can win, and whoever turns auditions into bookings at the highest rate keeps the actor's membership fees and the client's jobs.

## The Problem
Home booths vary from treated rooms to closets full of clothes. Reflections, computer noise, inconsistent mic distance and over-processing make an audition sound amateur before the read registers. Actors get used to their own room and stop hearing it.

## Why It's Still Broken
Marketplaces accept any upload. Actors check their audio by ear on the same headphones they recorded with. Professional feedback on the booth costs a coaching session, and conditions change with seasons and new equipment.

## What a Fix Looks Like
An automatic check on every audition before upload that measures noise floor, reverberation, clipping, sibilance and loudness against what successful auditions on the platform sound like, names the problem in plain terms, and suggests the fix (move the mic, kill the fan, turn off the noise gate). Over time it tracks the booth's condition so a new problem shows up the day it starts.

## Who Feels the Pain
Newer voice actors in untreated rooms; casting clients sorting through poor audio.

## Impact If Fixed
More auditions judged on performance, higher booking rates for actors with good reads in imperfect rooms, and less noise for clients.

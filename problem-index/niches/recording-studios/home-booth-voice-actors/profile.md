# Home-Booth Voice Actors

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/recording-studios|Recording Studios]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to put a home-booth actor's audition in front of the casting decisions it can win, and whoever turns auditions into bookings at the highest rate keeps the actor's membership fees and the client's jobs.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$200M US spend by voice actors on casting-site memberships, coaching, booth equipment and remote-session tools (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~6% (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** High — auditions, directed sessions and delivery are online; feedback on auditions is almost non-existent
**Target Buyer:** The working voice actor; the product lead at a voice casting marketplace
**Automation Potential:** High for matching, audio checks and admin; the performance is not, though synthetic voices compete for it

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Commercial, e-learning, explainer and corporate narration voice-over (the 512290 side of the industry that isn't audiobooks) is now mostly recorded by actors in booths at home, auditioning many times a day on pay-to-play sites and agents' lists and recording directed sessions over Source-Connect. Studio tools and contracts were built for studios. The actor is a one-person studio, engineer and business, with no feedback on why auditions fail, and directly exposed to synthetic voices: Tennessee's ELVIS Act (July 2024) and California's AB 2602, which voids vague digital-replica clauses, now shape what they can safely sign.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Voices.com, Voice123, Backstage and Casting Networks for auditions; agents' casting lists; Source-Connect for directed sessions; iZotope RX for cleanup; home booths. The gaps: which auditions to spend time on is guesswork, audio problems sink auditions before anyone hears the read, clients hear auditions in arrival order rather than by fit, and digital-replica clauses in booking terms are read, if at all, by the actor alone.

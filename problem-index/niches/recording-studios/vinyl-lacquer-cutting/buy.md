# Mastering Analysis Plug-ins Pointed at the Groove

**Niche:** [[niches/recording-studios/vinyl-lacquer-cutting/profile|Vinyl Lacquer Cutting]]
**Industry:** [[industries/recording-studios|Recording Studios]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Mastering engineers already meter loudness, phase and spectrum in real time; nobody translates those readings into what the groove will do.
**Tags:** #feature-engineering #evaluation-metrics #automation #quick-win #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to cut lacquers that pass the test pressing first time, and whoever wastes the fewest scarce lacquers and plant-queue weeks on recuts keeps the label's vinyl work.

## The Problem
Labels send cutting rooms the same masters they sent to streaming, loud, bright and wide, and the engineer has to rework them for vinyl. Problems are found by listening and by watching the lathe, and the conversation with the client about why a side must be quieter or shorter is based on the engineer's say-so.

## What Already Exists
Mastering analysis plug-ins (iZotope Insight and Ozone's meters, phase correlation and goniometer tools, de-essers and mid-side processors) that measure loudness, stereo phase and spectral balance.

## The Customization Gap
Vinyl needs those readings expressed in groove terms: mapping out-of-phase low frequencies to predicted vertical excursion, high-frequency energy to stylus-tracing risk near the inner grooves, side duration and level to a groove-pitch budget, and track order to inner-groove distortion. Then a pre-master report the engineer can send the client before cutting, showing which passages need work and why, so the trade-off is agreed rather than argued after the test pressing.

## Target Customer
Cutting engineers and mastering engineers preparing vinyl pre-masters; the plug-in vendors who could add a vinyl module.

## Impact If Solved
Problem passages fixed in the pre-master instead of on a recut, and client conversations about side length and level settled before the lacquer is cut.

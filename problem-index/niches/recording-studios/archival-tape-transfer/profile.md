# Archival Tape Transfer & Restoration

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/recording-studios|Recording Studios]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to transfer degrading tape and disc collections to files and metadata that meet archive standards such as IASA TC-04 and FADGI, at a cost per hour a grant budget can carry, and whoever proves preservation-grade quality at production throughput wins the institutional contracts before working playback machines run out.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$150M US spend on audio digitisation, transfer and restoration for archives, libraries, broadcasters, labels and estates (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~4% (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — the inputs are physical carriers on obsolete machines, and condition notes and process logs are often handwritten
**Target Buyer:** Head of digitisation or audiovisual archivist at a library, university or broadcaster; owner of a transfer house
**Automation Potential:** Medium for metadata, QC and triage; low for carrier handling and repair

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
512240's definition names audio restoration services. The work starts from reel-to-reel tape, cassettes, DAT, lacquer and acetate discs and wire recordings, some of them shedding binder ("sticky shed") or turning to vinegar. Engineers bake tapes, repair splices, align heads and pick the right playback equalisation, then deliver preservation files with embedded metadata. Buyers are institutions working from grants and collection budgets (libraries and archives are a separate industry, [[industries/libraries-and-archives|Libraries & Archives]]), labels transferring catalogue, and families with a box of tapes. The clock is the medium itself: IASA has put the window for magnetic tape at 10–15 years, and archive bodies cite a consensus that tape not digitised by around 2025 is at risk as machines and parts disappear.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Studer, Ampex and Otari machines kept running with salvaged parts; tape-baking ovens; restoration software such as iZotope RX and CEDAR; Plangent Processes for wow and flutter correction; BWF MetaEdit for FADGI metadata. The gaps: collection-level triage by an expert's eye, process metadata (speed, EQ curve, azimuth, baking) written by hand, and QC by listening.

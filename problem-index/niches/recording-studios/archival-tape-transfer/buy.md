# Digital Asset Management Adapted to Preservation Audio

**Niche:** [[niches/recording-studios/archival-tape-transfer/profile|Archival Tape Transfer & Restoration]]
**Industry:** [[industries/recording-studios|Recording Studios]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Asset management systems store media files well; preservation audio needs the file, its checksum and the full history of how it came off the carrier.
**Tags:** #data-integration #compliance #workflow-orchestration #automation #large-language-models
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to transfer degrading tape and disc collections to files and metadata that meet archive standards such as IASA TC-04 and FADGI, at a cost per hour a grant budget can carry, and whoever proves preservation-grade quality at production throughput wins the institutional contracts before working playback machines run out.

## The Problem
A transfer house delivers thousands of files to an archive: preservation masters, access copies, metadata sheets and reports. The archive's system has to ingest them, verify them and keep the record of how each was made. Mismatches between files and spreadsheets are common, and the process history, which future archivists need in order to trust the transfer, gets lost.

## What Already Exists
Digital asset management and digital preservation systems used by libraries and broadcasters, which store files, generate checksums and hold descriptive metadata; FADGI's BWF MetaEdit for embedding metadata in broadcast WAV files.

## The Customization Gap
Preservation audio needs structured process metadata per file (machine, head configuration, speed, playback EQ curve, azimuth adjustment, baking time and temperature, splices repaired), IASA TC-04 and FADGI-compliant embedded fields, side and track mapping back to the physical carrier, automated validation of what the transfer house delivered against the order, and links between original-carrier photos, condition reports and files.

## Target Customer
Audiovisual archivists at libraries, universities and broadcasters, and the transfer houses that want to deliver in a format those archives can ingest without rework.

## Impact If Solved
Deliveries accepted on first ingest, and transfers whose provenance a future archivist can verify, which is the point of preservation.

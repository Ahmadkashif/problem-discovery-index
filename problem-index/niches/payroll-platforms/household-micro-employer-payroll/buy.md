# Registration and Filing Infrastructure Already Built

**Niche:** [[niches/payroll-platforms/household-micro-employer-payroll/profile|Household & Micro-Employer Payroll]]
**Industry:** [[industries/payroll-platforms|Payroll Platforms]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** State registration, tax filing and remittance infrastructure exists at industrial scale inside every payroll provider, and a household employing one person cannot access any of it at a price that makes sense.
**Tags:** #workflow-orchestration #data-integration #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #compliance #automation #descriptive-statistics #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in household payroll is fighting to make a private individual a compliant employer without an accountant — and whoever gets a family from hiring to filed and paid without professional help takes the segment.

## The Problem
Everything a household employer needs — jurisdiction determination, withholding calculation, quarterly filing, remittance, year-end forms — is built, tested and operating at enormous scale inside the payroll providers. It is packaged and priced for businesses with a payroll administrator, and the onboarding assumes an entity with a registration the household does not have. The infrastructure exists and the last mile to this customer does not.

## What Already Exists
Payroll tax calculation, filing and remittance infrastructure is mature, as this industry's first niche describes. State registration processes are documented and increasingly available electronically. Workers' compensation for household employers is available through state funds and carriers in every state that requires it. Identity verification and payment infrastructure are commodity. Every operational component exists; the gap is packaging and a distribution channel.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a customer who is a private individual with one employee. It requires: (1) registration as a performed service rather than a prerequisite, since the household cannot complete state registration unaided and abandonment at that step is the dominant failure — this is the single most important adaptation; (2) obligations presented as a small, finite, completed list rather than as a compliance framework, because the household's question is "what do I have to do" and the answer must be finite; (3) household-specific rules handled natively — live-in exemptions, travel time, the distinction between a household employee and an independent contractor which families routinely get wrong in the direction that suits them, and the domestic worker protections that apply in a growing number of jurisdictions; (4) both parties served, with the worker able to see their own pay record, tax withholding and employment history, which is the thing that makes this product meaningful rather than merely compliant; and (5) a price a household will pay, which likely means subsidising with the tax credits and dependent care benefits that many of these families are entitled to and frequently do not claim.

## Target Customer
Household payroll specialists, mainstream payroll providers with unused capacity, and the nanny, senior care and domestic placement agencies who could bundle it at the point of hire.

## Impact If Solved
The infrastructure is built and the barrier is a packaging and distribution problem with a clear solution — perform the registration rather than explain it. The workers are the constituency with the most to gain, and a product that serves both parties rather than only the payer is what would make on-the-books employment the default rather than the exception in this segment.

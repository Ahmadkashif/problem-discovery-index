# The Worker With No Employment Record

**Niche:** [[niches/payroll-platforms/household-micro-employer-payroll/profile|Household & Micro-Employer Payroll]]
**Industry:** [[industries/payroll-platforms|Payroll Platforms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A domestic worker paid in cash for a decade has no employment history, no unemployment eligibility, no social security credits and nothing to show a landlord or a lender, and the decision that produced all of that was made by somebody else.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #compliance #workflow-orchestration #automation #worker-facing #confidence-intervals #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in household payroll is fighting to make a private individual a compliant employer without an accountant — and whoever gets a family from hiring to filed and paid without professional help takes the segment.

## The Problem
A carer works for a family for eight years and is paid in cash. When the arrangement ends she cannot claim unemployment, because there is no record of employment. She has eight years of missing social security credits, which will reduce her retirement income for the rest of her life. When she applies for an apartment she cannot document income. If she is injured at work there is no workers' compensation. She did not choose any of this — the employer did, frequently without understanding the consequences and sometimes at her own request, since the immediate cash is larger. The harm is deferred, substantial, and falls entirely on the party with the least power in the arrangement.

## Why It's Still Broken
The immediate incentives point the same way for both parties: the family avoids employer taxes and administration, the worker receives more cash now. The costs are deferred by years or decades and are invisible at the moment of the decision, which is the classic structure of a bad bargain that both sides accept. Nobody explains the trade-off in concrete terms, and the worker — who bears nearly all of the deferred cost — is the one least likely to have been told what they are giving up.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Make the trade-off legible and give the worker a path. Quantify it concretely at the point of the arrangement: eight years off the books costs approximately this much in eventual social security benefits, forfeits unemployment eligibility worth approximately this much, and removes workers' compensation coverage — stated in dollars rather than in principles, for both parties to see. Show the family the offsetting credits and benefits they are entitled to, since dependent care tax credits and pre-tax accounts frequently narrow the cost difference substantially and most families do not know. Give the worker a route that does not depend on the employer's initiative: a way to raise the question, a plain-language explanation of their rights in their own language, and where the arrangement has been off the books, a path to regularise going forward without accusation. And make the record the worker's own — portable across the several families many domestic workers work for, which is the structure of the job and which no employer-centred product represents.

## Who Feels the Pain
Domestic and care workers with decades of undocumented employment and the consequences arriving at retirement, unemployment or an injury; families who made a decision they did not understand; and the public programmes that carry the eventual cost.

## Impact If Fixed
Quantifying the deferred cost in dollars at the moment of the decision is the cheapest possible intervention and is currently done by nobody. A worker-held, portable employment record across multiple household employers matches how this work is actually performed and is the artefact that would let a domestic worker establish an income history for the first time.

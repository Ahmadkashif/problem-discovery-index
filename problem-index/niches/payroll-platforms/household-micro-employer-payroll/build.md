# From Hiring to Compliant Without an Accountant

**Niche:** [[niches/payroll-platforms/household-micro-employer-payroll/profile|Household & Micro-Employer Payroll]]
**Industry:** [[industries/payroll-platforms|Payroll Platforms]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A family hiring a nanny becomes an employer with a dozen obligations across two or three agencies, and the path from that decision to being compliant requires knowledge nobody in the household has.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #workflow-orchestration #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #compliance #automation #worker-facing #data-integration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in household payroll is fighting to make a private individual a compliant employer without an accountant — and whoever gets a family from hiring to filed and paid without professional help takes the segment.

## The Problem
A family hires a nanny at an agreed weekly rate. To do it correctly they need an employer identification number, state unemployment registration, workers' compensation coverage in many states, new hire reporting, a written agreement in jurisdictions that require one, correct overtime treatment for hours over forty including live-in rules that differ, quarterly filings, and year-end forms. Nobody told them any of this. They discover a subset of it through a friend or an article, attempt part of it, find the state registration process opaque, and default to paying cash — which is what a large share of household employers do, and which strips the worker of every protection the system exists to provide.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The economics are unattractive: a household employer has one employee, pays a small fee, and requires as much compliance infrastructure as a small business. The specialist services that exist have solved the operational problem and reach only the families who already know they need help, which is the minority. And the hardest part is not the payroll processing but the initial realisation — a family that does not know it is an employer will never search for a payroll service, so distribution has to happen at the moment of hiring rather than at the moment of searching.

## What to Build
A guided path that starts at hiring rather than at payroll. The product's first job is to tell a household what it has just become, in plain language, with the specific obligations for its state and its situation — live-in or live-out, hours, whether the worker has other employers — since the rules differ materially and generic guidance is what currently fails. Registration is performed rather than explained: employer identification number, state unemployment account, new hire reporting and, where required, workers' compensation, completed through whatever channel each agency offers, with the family supplying facts rather than navigating portals. The written agreement is generated where the jurisdiction requires or recommends one, in both parties' languages. Payroll then runs with correct overtime, including the live-in and travel-time rules that are the most commonly misapplied. And the whole thing is priced and packaged for a household, which means the onboarding must be nearly free of professional assistance because there is no budget for any.

## Target Customer
Households employing domestic and care workers, the agencies and platforms that place them — which are the natural distribution point and where the realisation should happen — and the specialist household payroll services.

## Impact If Built
The worker is the party who gains most: on-the-books employment produces unemployment eligibility, social security credits, workers' compensation coverage and a documented income history that affects credit, housing and retirement decades later. For the family it removes a compliance exposure they mostly do not know they have. The distribution insight is the whole thing — reaching households at hiring rather than waiting for them to search is the difference between serving the informed minority and changing the segment.

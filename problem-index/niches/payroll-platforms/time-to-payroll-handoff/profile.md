# Time-to-Payroll Handoff

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/payroll-platforms|Payroll Platforms]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in the time-to-payroll seam is fighting to surface the exceptions that will break a run days before the deadline rather than on the afternoon of it — and whoever moves detection earliest takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$2.8B US attributable to time and attendance integration, approvals and pre-run processing
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~9% of payroll revenue
**Digital Adoption:** High — the systems are connected and the process between them is human
**Target Buyer:** Payroll managers and workforce operations leaders; time and attendance vendors on the other side
**Automation Potential:** Very High — the exceptions are predictable and are discovered at the last possible moment

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Payroll runs on a fixed calendar that does not move. Between the end of a pay period and the deadline for submitting a run sits a compressed window in which hours must be complete, exceptions resolved, approvals obtained and errors corrected. What actually happens is that a payroll specialist discovers on the afternoon of the deadline that three managers have not approved, a location's clock stopped recording on Tuesday, four employees have missed punches, two new hires were never set up, and someone's termination was not processed so they are about to be paid. Every one of those was knowable days earlier. The window is the constraint, the deadline is immovable, and the entire process is organised around discovering problems at the moment there is least time to fix them — which is why payroll is the function with the most reliably terrible Thursdays in enterprise operations.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Time and attendance platforms handle capture, rules and approvals; payroll platforms provide pre-run reports and preview registers; integrations between them are standard. The gaps: exception detection is triggered by the run rather than by the deadline, so the pre-run report is the first comprehensive view and it arrives too late; manager approval, which is the most common blocker, is chased by a payroll specialist manually; missing punches and anomalous hours are detected in aggregate rather than at the moment they occur, when the employee and manager could resolve them trivially; and new hire, termination and change processing — which crosses into the HR system — is verified by the payroll specialist checking rather than by anything asserting completeness.

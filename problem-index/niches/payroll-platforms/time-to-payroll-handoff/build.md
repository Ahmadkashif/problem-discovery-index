# Exceptions Surfaced Against the Deadline, Not the Run

**Niche:** [[niches/payroll-platforms/time-to-payroll-handoff/profile|Time-to-Payroll Handoff]]
**Industry:** [[industries/payroll-platforms|Payroll Platforms]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Every exception that will break a payroll run is detectable days before the deadline, and the process surfaces all of them in a pre-run report generated when there is no time left.
**Tags:** #change-point-detection #gradient-boosting #time-series-forecasting #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #workflow-orchestration #automation #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in the time-to-payroll seam is fighting to surface the exceptions that will break a run days before the deadline rather than on the afternoon of it — and whoever moves detection earliest takes the account.

## The Problem
Thursday, two o'clock, deadline at four. The payroll specialist runs the preview and finds that a location has forty percent fewer hours than usual, eleven employees have unapproved timesheets across four managers, three have missed punches, one new hire has no tax setup, and a terminated employee is showing a full period of hours. Each has to be chased individually in under two hours, across people who are in meetings. Some get resolved, some get estimated, and some get corrected in the next period — which means somebody is paid wrongly this week. Every one of those conditions existed on Monday.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The pre-run report is the artefact the process was built around, and it is generated by the run because that is when all the data is present. Detecting earlier means predicting what will be wrong rather than observing it, which requires modelling the process rather than reporting on it — and nobody framed the payroll close as a process with a critical path. The deadline pressure also creates a perverse stability: everyone knows Thursday is terrible and has organised around it, which removes the impetus to change it.

## What to Build
A close process modelled against its deadline, with exceptions predicted and escalated on a schedule. Each period has a critical path: hours complete by this point, approvals by that point, HR changes processed by another. Progress against it is monitored continuously and projected — this location's approval rate at this point on Tuesday means it will not be complete by Thursday, escalate now. Anomaly detection runs on hours as they accumulate rather than at the end: a location whose daily hours drop sharply is flagged on the day, when the cause is findable, not on Thursday when it is a mystery. Missing punches and unapproved entries are surfaced to the employee and the manager at the moment they occur, which is when resolving them is trivial. HR change completeness — new hires with full setup, terminations processed, changes effective-dated correctly — is asserted by a check rather than by the specialist noticing. The measured outcome is the proportion of exceptions resolved before the final day, which is the number that describes whether the process has changed.

## Target Customer
Payroll providers, time and attendance vendors on the other side of the seam, and large employers whose payroll close is a recurring emergency.

## Impact If Built
The payroll close is a compressed, high-stakes, entirely predictable process run as an emergency every period, and moving exception detection from the deadline to the days preceding it changes the working life of the function substantially. It also reduces the category of error that matters most — someone paid wrongly because there was no time to fix it properly.

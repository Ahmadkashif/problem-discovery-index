## Why These Niches

There are more than eleven thousand US taxing jurisdictions with independently changing rates, wage bases, reciprocity rules, deposit schedules and filing formats, and determining them correctly for every employee every pay period is the product. That content is the industry's real moat and its real risk, and the contested capability is verification before the money moves rather than reconciliation after a notice arrives.

Wage calculation and time integration is the second large block and **failed the filter as one niche**. Computing the regular rate of pay correctly — with overtime, differentials, non-discretionary bonuses, and state-specific meal and rest premiums — is a legal correctness problem where employers configure engines wrongly and nobody checks the configuration against the law. Getting hours out of a time system and into a pay run is an operational data problem of exceptions, approvals and deadlines. Different failures, different remedies, different teams. Decomposed below.

Garnishments and household employment are the two underdigitised areas. A wage attachment arrives as a court document with jurisdiction-specific priority and exemption rules and is read by a person, and the household employer — who is an employer in law and a private individual in practice — is served almost not at all. Workers and payroll practitioners are the two underserved constituencies: the person receiving the pay statement cannot check any of it, and the specialist producing it is invisible until something fails.

The automation niches are the industry's two clearest unclaimed capabilities: a disbursement failure detected before the employee opens their banking app, and the highest-frequency wage dataset in the country, held by the providers and published as a quarterly marketing index.

## Filter Notes

**Niche 2 failed the filter as stated and was decomposed.** "Wage calculation and time integration" covers a correctness problem and a logistics problem. The regular rate of pay is a legal construct that employers get wrong through configuration, and the contest is whether a provider can detect that an employer's configuration does not match the law in the jurisdictions it operates in — the same shape as the tax content problem and solved the same way. The time-to-payroll handoff is about exceptions, approvals and a fixed deadline, and the contest is whether the exceptions that will break a run surface with enough time to fix them. Both sub-niches are terminal and are written in full.

**Niches 1, 3–8 are terminal as stated.** Each names something testable: jurisdiction determination verified pre-disbursement, an attachment order correctly applied without a person reading it, a household employer compliant without an accountant, whether a worker can reconstruct their own pay statement, whether an exception surfaces before the deadline afternoon, whether a failed payment is fixed before the worker notices, and whether the wage panel is published at all.

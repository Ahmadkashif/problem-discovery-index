# Cost Databases Extended to Park Items

**Niche:** [[niches/landscape-architecture-firms/park-capital-project-delivery/profile|Park Capital Project Delivery]]
**Industry:** [[industries/landscape-architecture-firms|Landscape Architecture Firms]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Construction cost databases price concrete and topsoil well and price a splash pad, a play structure or poured rubber surfacing badly.
**Tags:** #linear-regression #feature-engineering #descriptive-statistics #data-integration #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to produce park bid documents that come in within the construction budget at the first bid opening without missing a grant deadline, and whoever delivers parks that bid on budget takes the account.

## The Problem
Landscape estimators use general cost data for site work and fill the rest with manufacturer quotes that are list prices, exclude installation and expire in thirty days.

## What Already Exists
Gordian's RSMeans site-work and landscape cost data, indexed under [[niches/cleaning-companies/facility-cost-data-publishers/profile|Facility & Construction Cost Data Publishers]]; estimating tools such as ProEst and Sage Estimating for contractors; manufacturer quote portals.

## The Customization Gap
The park adaptation needs: a park item taxonomy with installed prices for play equipment by type and size, surfacing systems, shade structures, sports lighting, synthetic turf and splash pads; regional factors drawn from recent public bids rather than contractor surveys; escalation by item family, since play equipment and steel move differently from concrete; and quantity breaks that reflect small municipal jobs.

## Target Customer
Landscape architects estimating park construction, and agency capital project staff checking consultant estimates.

## Impact If Solved
Estimates that hold at bid opening for the volatile items that most often blow park budgets, without the firm building its own database.

# Contractor Estimating Linked to the Landscape Model

**Niche:** [[niches/landscape-architecture-firms/private-development-site-design/profile|Private Development Site Design]]
**Industry:** [[industries/landscape-architecture-firms|Landscape Architecture Firms]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Landscape contractors already estimate with purpose-built software; the designer's drawing reaches it only as a PDF to be counted again.
**Tags:** #feature-engineering #data-integration #descriptive-statistics #automation #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to design landscapes that survive the developer's value-engineering round — drawn to the installed cost the pro forma assumed while still passing landscape review — and whoever avoids unpaid VE redesign takes the developer's next project.

## The Problem
The designer produces quantities in CAD; the contractor recounts them from the PDF in its estimating system; the numbers disagree; and nobody can trace which design element drove the price.

## What Already Exists
Landscape contractor estimating and job-costing platforms such as LMN and Aspire, takeoff tools such as PlanSwift and Bluebeam, and Land F/X or Vectorworks quantity schedules on the design side.

## The Customization Gap
The bridge needs: a shared item taxonomy between the designer's plant and materials schedules and the contractor's estimating items; quantities exported from the model rather than recounted; regional installed unit costs from contractor estimates fed back to the designer anonymously; and a VE log that records each substitution's cost effect and code consequence.

## Target Customer
Landscape architects in developer work and the commercial landscape contractors who price their drawings.

## Impact If Solved
One set of quantities for designer and contractor, hours of recounting removed per bid, and cost feedback that reaches the designer before design is locked.

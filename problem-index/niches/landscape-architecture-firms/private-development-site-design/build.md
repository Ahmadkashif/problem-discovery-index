# Landscape Cost Modelled at Concept Against the Pro Forma

**Niche:** [[niches/landscape-architecture-firms/private-development-site-design/profile|Private Development Site Design]]
**Industry:** [[industries/landscape-architecture-firms|Landscape Architecture Firms]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** The developer's pro forma carries a landscape allowance per unit or per square foot, and the landscape architect designs for months before anyone compares the drawing to it.
**Tags:** #gradient-boosting #linear-regression #feature-engineering #confidence-intervals #revenue-impact #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to design landscapes that survive the developer's value-engineering round — drawn to the installed cost the pro forma assumed while still passing landscape review — and whoever avoids unpaid VE redesign takes the developer's next project.

## The Problem
A multifamily developer underwrites a project with a landscape and hardscape allowance. The landscape architect starts concept design from the site plan and the amenity programme, and the first real price arrives at design development or later, from the GC's subcontractor. By then the design is expensive to change.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Landscape firms rarely see final installed costs — the subcontract is between the GC and the landscape contractor — so they have no data to calibrate against. Estimating tools are built for contractors pricing finished drawings, not for designers at concept.

## What to Build
A parametric cost model that reads the concept plan as it is drawn — areas by paving type, planting density by bed type, tree counts and sizes, amenity elements, irrigated area, site walls and grading complexity — and predicts installed cost with a range, calibrated on the firm's and the developer's past projects where final landscape subcontract values are shared. Show the running total against the pro forma allowance at every save, and the cheapest code-compliant alternatives when it runs over.

## Target Customer
Firms of 10–100 people doing repeat work for multifamily, mixed-use and industrial developers; development managers who want landscape cost certainty early.

## Impact If Built
Value engineering shrinks from a redesign phase to a series of small choices made during design, protecting both the firm's fee and the developer's relationship.

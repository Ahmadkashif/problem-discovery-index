# Landscape Code Compliance & Water-Budget Calculations

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/landscape-architecture-firms|Landscape Architecture Firms]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to compute each jurisdiction's landscape requirements correctly from the live drawing so the set passes landscape review on the first submittal, and whoever covers the most jurisdictions accurately takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$300M a year of fee hours spent reading landscape ordinances, building compliance tables and water-budget worksheets, and answering plan-review comments (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~5% of fee hours (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — quantities come from CAD, but rules are read from code text and tables are built in Excel
**Target Buyer:** Project landscape architect on private development work; developer's entitlement manager
**Automation Potential:** High — the provisions are written rules applied to countable drawing objects

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Every jurisdiction has a landscape ordinance with its own ratios — trees per parking space, island spacing, frontage planting, buffer yards by adjacent zoning, landscaped percentage, canopy at maturity, tree removal mitigation — and California adds the Model Water Efficient Landscape Ordinance, revised in July 2015, with a hydrozone table and a water budget (MAWA against estimated total water use) in the permit set, often under a licensed professional's certification. The work is mechanical, recurring and checkable, and errors cost resubmittal cycles measured in weeks.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Municode and other code publishers for text; Land F/X and Vectorworks for quantities; Excel for tables; zoning-analysis products for height and density. Gaps: no structured rule library for landscape provisions, no recalculation when the drawing changes, and no capture of what reviewers actually enforce.

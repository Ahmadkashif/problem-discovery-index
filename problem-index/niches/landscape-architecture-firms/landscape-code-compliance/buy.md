**One-liner:** Zoning-analysis platforms already parse municipal codes for developers; they stop at the building envelope and never reach the parking-lot island.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #seq2seq #feature-engineering #compliance #data-integration #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to compute each jurisdiction's landscape requirements correctly from the live drawing so the set passes landscape review on the first submittal, and whoever covers the most jurisdictions accurately takes the account.

## The Problem
Developers use zoning tools at site acquisition to test yield, then hand the site to designers who rediscover the landscape requirements that quietly reduce the developable area — buffer yards, tree preservation zones, landscaped-area minimums.

## What Already Exists
Zoning data and analysis platforms such as Zoneomics and Gridics, and code publishers' hosted codes (Municode, American Legal Publishing, eCode360).

## The Customization Gap
The extension needs: landscape provisions parsed into the same structured form as setbacks and height; their land-area effects (buffers, preservation zones, island counts) estimated at acquisition; a hand-off of the same rules into the designer's CAD tool for detailed compliance; and water-budget rules for jurisdictions that impose them.

## Target Customer
Developers' entitlement teams and the landscape architects and civil engineers who work for them.

## Impact If Solved
Landscape requirements counted at acquisition rather than discovered in design, and one rule source shared by developer and designer.

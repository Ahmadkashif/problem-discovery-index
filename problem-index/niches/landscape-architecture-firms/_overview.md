# Niche Analysis — Landscape Architecture Firms

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/landscape-architecture-firms|Landscape Architecture Firms]]

## Niche Selection

A landscape architecture firm sells construction documents for sites — grading, drainage, planting, hardscape and irrigation — plus the planning, engagement and construction administration around them, and it is graded on whether those sites drain, pass accessibility surveys, meet the local landscape code and keep their plants alive, mostly after its fee has ended. The eight niches below split the industry by client market, by the phases where paper and memory still run the work, by two audiences that vendors ignore, and by the most mechanical production tasks. Market sizes are estimates of the fee revenue or fee hours each niche represents within an industry of ~$5.8B in employer-firm revenue (2022) and ~$9.6B including sole proprietors (2026), not of the construction value the designs control.

## Why These Niches

Public parks and private development are the two largest client markets and behave differently: park work is won by qualifications and decided in public on grant and bid clocks, while developer work is repeat-client and cost-capped, with the landscape cut first at value engineering. The two low-digitized niches are the phases where outcomes are actually observed and least recorded — construction administration, run on phone photos and evening reports, and planting design, run on remembered regional knowledge and copied plant lists. The two underserved audiences are the 17% of landscape architects who are self-employed and seal plans nobody else checks, and the agency landscape architects who buy, review and inherit the work but are not anyone's software customer. The two automatable niches are the most rule-bound tasks in the office: computing each jurisdiction's landscape provisions and water budget, and keeping a forty-sheet set coordinated through every revision.

## Filter Notes

Seven of the eight level-1 niches are terminal. Public Parks & Open-Space Design is not: writing its contested sentence produced two sentences with different winners. In planning, the contest is turning contested community input into a plan the council adopts and the capital programme funds — an evidence and persuasion contest won by firms strong in engagement, survey integration and level-of-service analysis. In capital delivery, the contest is producing bid documents that come in within a fixed budget at the first bid opening without missing a grant deadline — a cost-prediction and documentation contest won by firms with honest estimates and tight construction documents. Different clocks (adoption vote versus bid opening), different evidence and often different firms, so it decomposes into **Park System Master Planning & Engagement** and **Park Capital Project Delivery**.

Two terminal calls were close. **Planting Design & Plant Specification** could be split into availability at bid and survival after planting, but both are won by the same thing — palette knowledge grounded in what nurseries grow and what lives on comparable sites — so one contest governs. **Sole Practitioners & Micro-Firms** risked a generic sentence ("cheaper software"); it holds because the contest is specific to the solo practice's liability position: a second reviewer and production capacity on every sealed set, which no large-firm tool provides at any price.

Three candidates were considered and rejected. **Residential design-build** was rejected because its contest — visualising the design and closing the homeowner — belongs to installers and is already covered under [[industries/landscaping|Landscaping]]. **Transportation and streetscape design for DOTs** was considered; its contests split between agency review standards and capital delivery, which are captured by the public-agency and park delivery niches, and the remainder is civil scope under [[industries/engineering-consultants|Engineering Consultants]]. **Visualisation and rendering** was rejected because "better renderings" is a taste contest and fails the filter.

## Why These Pockets

No pocket qualifies, and the operators themselves fail the gate. A landscape architecture firm's invoice is design, not research; even the largest studios run research groups of a few people that support the design fee. The existing landscaping build scored [[niches/landscaping/landscape-architecture-firms/profile|landscape architecture firms]] as a 46/60 pocket from the installer's side; seen from inside the industry, that is the operator layer, and the honest result is that it has no 10-person insight function to sell to. The value chain around it is thin in the way Pass 2 cares about: the suppliers' large technical teams (stormwater devices, irrigation, furnishings, play) give their analysis away to win the specification, the regulators and licensing bodies are a few staff each, and the association research arms are member services.

The one shape that comes close is invoiced survey research with a benchmark archive. Needs-assessment survey firms (47) sell exactly what the rubric wants — the analysis is the deliverable, the work is same-shaped across hundreds of cities, and the normative database is theirs — but the clock is a plan schedule that slips rather than a filing date, and the market is a few firms rather than a fragmented field where one reference unlocks the next. Urban forest inventory consultancies (41) are large and repeatable but deliver an inventory the client city owns.

The richest dataset in the chain is the one nobody can use: design professional liability carriers hold the only systematic record of how site designs fail — water toward buildings, ice on walks, trip-and-fall at grade changes — under privilege. The same carriers are logged under [[niches/engineering-consultants/design-liability-insurers/profile|Design Professional Liability Insurers]]. Two qualifiers already indexed elsewhere touch this industry's buyers and were not re-scored here: [[niches/electrical-contractors/construction-project-lead-data/profile|Construction Project Lead & Plan Data]], which landscape firms use to find public work, and [[niches/cleaning-companies/facility-cost-data-publishers/profile|Facility & Construction Cost Data Publishers]], whose site-work data they estimate against. For this industry, the empty positions are the finding: the value of landscape architecture's outcome data — what drained, what lived — is real, but it sits unrecorded with owners and maintenance contractors rather than inside any research business.

Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## The Problem
By the time a park RFQ is posted, the firms that win have usually been tracking it for a year: they knew the bond measure passed, the state grant was awarded, the capital improvement plan moved the project into next year, and the parks director has a preference. Smaller firms rely on bid alerts and see the opportunity with three weeks to respond.

## What Already Exists
Government procurement intelligence services (Deltek GovWin, GovSpend, BidNet Direct, Periscope-style bid boards) and construction lead services such as Dodge and ConstructConnect — the latter indexed as a pocket under [[niches/electrical-contractors/construction-project-lead-data/profile|Construction Project Lead & Plan Data]].

## The Customization Gap
The park-specific signals are buried in council agendas and minutes, capital improvement plans, bond election results, state grant award lists (Land and Water Conservation Fund and state park grant rounds), and adopted master plans whose recommendations become projects. The adaptation needs: monitoring those documents per agency, extracting park projects with budget and timing, linking them to the master plan that spawned them, and scoring fit against the firm's own past work.

## Target Customer
Business development leads and principals at park-focused firms who respond to dozens of public RFQs a year.

## Impact If Solved
Seeing a park project a year before the RFQ lets a firm position honestly — attend the engagement meetings, understand the site — and shifts its win rate more than any improvement in the response itself.

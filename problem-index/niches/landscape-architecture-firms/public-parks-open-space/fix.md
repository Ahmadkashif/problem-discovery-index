## The Problem
A park-focused firm may answer several qualification requests a month. Each wants relevant projects described against its own evaluation criteria, key staff resumes with specific experience, references with current contact details, and sometimes federal SF 330 forms. The content exists in old proposals; finding the right version, updating it and reformatting it falls to a principal or a single marketing coordinator, typically at night before the deadline.

## Why It's Still Broken
Project descriptions and resumes live as text inside layout files rather than as records. Proposal-management tools built for large engineering firms are priced and configured for them, and small firms never populate them. Nothing records which descriptions scored well, because debrief scores are rarely requested or filed.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Store projects, people and references as structured records once; generate a draft response that maps each to the RFQ's criteria, in its required format, for the principal to edit; and keep debrief scores attached to the content that earned them.

## Who Feels the Pain
Principals of 5–30 person firms who write their own proposals, marketing coordinators carrying several pursuits at once, and staff whose resumes are a year out of date.

## Impact If Fixed
Cutting a response from three or four days to one lets a small firm pursue more of the right projects and spend the saved time on understanding the site and the client, which is what wins.

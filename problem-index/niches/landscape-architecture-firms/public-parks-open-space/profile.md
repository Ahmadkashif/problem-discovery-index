# Public Parks & Open-Space Design

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/landscape-architecture-firms|Landscape Architecture Firms]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is that every competitor is fighting to win the next public RFQ on the strength of the last park — but planning a park system a council will adopt and delivering a capital project that bids within its budget are different contests with different winners. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.6B a year in landscape architecture fees from park agencies, municipalities, school districts and state park systems (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~28% of fee revenue (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — drawings, GIS and online surveys are digital; selection, engagement synthesis, cost history and post-occupancy knowledge are not
**Target Buyer:** Principal or public-sector studio leader at a firm of 10–150 people
**Automation Potential:** Medium — engagement coding, estimating and grant paperwork automate well; design and public persuasion do not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Public park work is the core of the profession's identity and a large share of its fees. Firms are selected through qualifications-based selection, not price, so the last completed park is the main sales asset. The work runs on public clocks — council adoption dates, bond elections, grant application and reimbursement deadlines, and bid openings — and every decision is made in public. It splits cleanly into two businesses that share a client: system and site master planning, which ends in an adopted plan, and capital project delivery, which ends in a park that was bid and built.

## Current Tools & Gaps
GIS (ArcGIS), online engagement platforms (PublicInput, Social Pinpoint), survey research subcontractors, CAD and rendering suites, RSMeans-style cost data, and Deltek or BQE for project accounting. Nothing links a plan's recommendations to the projects built from it, their bid results, and how the park was used and maintained afterwards — the record that would make the next RFQ response evidence-based.

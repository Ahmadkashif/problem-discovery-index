# Plant Lists Copied Forward From the Last Job

**Niche:** [[niches/landscape-architecture-firms/planting-design-specification/profile|Planting Design & Plant Specification]]
**Industry:** [[industries/landscape-architecture-firms|Landscape Architecture Firms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Under deadline, designers start from the previous project's plant schedule, and its mistakes — a species that failed, a cultivar no longer grown — travel with it.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #k-nearest-neighbors #feature-engineering #quick-win #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to specify a plant palette that is available at bid and still alive at the end of the warranty, and whoever's palettes survive without substitution fights or replacement takes the account.

## The Problem
Starting from the last job's schedule is rational: it was reviewed, it carries the office's preferred species and its symbols are set up. But nothing marks which entries failed on site, which were substituted in construction, which suit a different climate zone or soil, or which nurseries stopped growing.

## Why It's Still Broken
Plant schedules are drawing objects, not a maintained office library; feedback from construction and warranty walks is never written back; and the person who knew the problems has often left.

## What a Fix Looks Like
An office plant library with every entry carrying its history — projects used on, substitutions requested, warranty replacements, notes from senior designers — and a check that flags inherited entries with problems when a schedule is copied to a new site.

## Who Feels the Pain
Junior designers who inherit lists they cannot judge; project landscape architects fighting the same substitution twice; owners replacing the same failed species on successive projects.

## Impact If Fixed
The office stops repeating its own planting mistakes, at the cost of a few minutes per schedule.

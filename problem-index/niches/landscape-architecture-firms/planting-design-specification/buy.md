# Plant Databases Wired to Live Nursery Availability

**Niche:** [[niches/landscape-architecture-firms/planting-design-specification/profile|Planting Design & Plant Specification]]
**Industry:** [[industries/landscape-architecture-firms|Landscape Architecture Firms]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Design software has plant databases and nurseries publish availability lists; nothing checks a planting schedule against what will be growing when the job bids.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #word-embeddings #k-nearest-neighbors #time-series-forecasting #data-integration #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to specify a plant palette that is available at bid and still alive at the end of the warranty, and whoever's palettes survive without substitution fights or replacement takes the account.

## The Problem
A designer specifies forty species in the sizes the design needs. A year later, at bid, a third are short or unavailable in that size, and the substitution fight begins. The designer could have checked, but availability arrives as weekly PDFs and spreadsheets from dozens of growers in a format nobody parses.

## What Already Exists
Land F/X and Vectorworks plant databases with traits and symbols; nursery availability lists and wholesale catalogues; landscape supply distributors' ordering portals; plant reference databases.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs: parsing grower and rewholesaler availability lists into species, cultivar, size and quantity; forecasting availability at the expected bid date from crop cycles and past lists, not just today's stock; checking the schedule inside the CAD tool and flagging scarce items with pre-approved substitutes; and regional sourcing radius so the check reflects what a local contractor can actually buy.

## Target Customer
Planting designers at mid-sized firms, and growers who want their stock specified rather than substituted out.

## Impact If Solved
Fewer substitution requests in construction, planting that matches design intent, and growers able to plan crops against specifications.

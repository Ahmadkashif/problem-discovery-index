# Planting Design & Plant Specification

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/landscape-architecture-firms|Landscape Architecture Firms]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to specify a plant palette that is available at bid and still alive at the end of the warranty, and whoever's palettes survive without substitution fights or replacement takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$600M a year of fee hours spent selecting species, writing planting plans, schedules and specifications (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~10% of fee hours (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — the plant schedule is in CAD, but species knowledge, availability and performance live in designers' heads, nursery emails and copied plant lists
**Target Buyer:** Planting design lead or principal; nursery sales and specification reps on the supply side
**Automation Potential:** Medium — availability matching and survival evidence automate; aesthetic composition does not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Planting is the part of a landscape architect's work that is alive. A planting plan commits to species, sizes and spacings that must be available from nurseries when the job bids, often a year later, and must survive the specific soil, exposure, irrigation and maintenance regime of the site. Experienced planting designers carry regional knowledge of what works — which species sulk in compacted urban soil, which fail in reflected heat off glass, which nurseries grow good stock — and that knowledge is rarely written down. Firms copy plant lists from project to project.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Land F/X and Vectorworks plant databases and schedules; plant reference sites and regional native plant databases; nursery availability lists by email; the designer's photos. Gaps: no link between a specification and its survival, no live availability against the schedule, and no firm-wide record of which palettes failed where.

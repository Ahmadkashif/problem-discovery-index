# Public-Agency Landscape Architects

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/landscape-architecture-firms|Landscape Architecture Firms]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor serving this niche is fighting to let an agency landscape architect review consultant submittals against the agency's own standards fast enough to keep capital projects on their grant and bid dates, and whoever shortens review without letting standards slip takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~1,900 landscape architects in government (8% of the BLS count) who review and administer an estimated ~$1B a year of consultant landscape fees and the construction behind them (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~8% of the profession's workforce; the client-side reviewer on a large share of public fees (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low-Medium — agencies run asset management and GIS, but design review is PDF markups and standards are scattered documents
**Target Buyer:** Park planning and design manager, DOT landscape unit lead, or campus landscape architect
**Automation Potential:** Medium-High for standards checking and review tracking

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Park departments, transportation agencies, school districts, universities and federal land agencies employ landscape architects who do not sell design — they buy it, review it and live with it. They write the agency's standard details and specifications, review consultant submittals at 30%, 60%, 90% and bid, manage capital projects against grant and fiscal-year deadlines, and inherit the maintenance consequences. Vendors sell to the consulting firms; the reviewer on the other side of the table works with Bluebeam and email.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Bluebeam for review, ArcGIS for inventory, asset management systems (Cartegraph, Cityworks, Brightly) run by operations, e-Builder or similar for capital programmes. Gaps: no structured link from agency standards to review comments, no record of which consultant issues recur, and landscape assets barely modelled in asset systems.

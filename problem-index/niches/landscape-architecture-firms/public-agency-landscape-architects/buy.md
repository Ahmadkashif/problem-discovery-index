# Asset Management Extended to Living Landscape Assets

**Niche:** [[niches/landscape-architecture-firms/public-agency-landscape-architects/profile|Public-Agency Landscape Architects]]
**Industry:** [[industries/landscape-architecture-firms|Landscape Architecture Firms]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Municipal asset systems track pipes, pavement and playground equipment; they do not know a bioretention cell has to infiltrate or that a street tree has a warranty and a design intent.
**Tags:** #survival-analysis #time-series-forecasting #data-integration #workflow-orchestration #compliance
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor serving this niche is fighting to let an agency landscape architect review consultant submittals against the agency's own standards fast enough to keep capital projects on their grant and bid dates, and whoever shortens review without letting standards slip takes the account.

## The Problem
After a park or green street is built, the agency's operations staff maintain it from an asset system that records what is there, not what it is supposed to do. The landscape architect who reviewed the design never sees whether the rain gardens drain or the trees establish.

## What Already Exists
Cartegraph, Cityworks, Brightly Assetic and ArcGIS-based asset management; tree inventory software such as TreeKeeper; stormwater MS4 inspection apps.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs landscape asset types with performance attributes — bioretention design ponding depth and drawdown time, tree species, planting date and warranty status, irrigation zones and water budgets — imported from the consultant's as-built drawings, and performance checks scheduled against them, so failures route back to the agency designer and into next year's standards.

## Target Customer
Agency landscape architects and green infrastructure programme managers working with operations divisions.

## Impact If Solved
Design standards updated from evidence of what failed in the agency's own parks, and green infrastructure that is inspected for function rather than presence.

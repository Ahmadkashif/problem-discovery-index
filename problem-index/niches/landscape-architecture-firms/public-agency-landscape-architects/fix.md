# Agency Standards Nobody Can Find

**Niche:** [[niches/landscape-architecture-firms/public-agency-landscape-architects/profile|Public-Agency Landscape Architects]]
**Industry:** [[industries/landscape-architecture-firms|Landscape Architecture Firms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** An agency's standard details and specifications live in a dozen PDFs of different vintages, and consultants keep using the version from three projects ago.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #compliance #workflow-orchestration #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor serving this niche is fighting to let an agency landscape architect review consultant submittals against the agency's own standards fast enough to keep capital projects on their grant and bid dates, and whoever shortens review without letting standards slip takes the account.

## The Problem
Standards accumulate: a 2014 design manual, a 2019 irrigation addendum, an approved plant list updated by email, standard details as CAD files on a shared drive. Consultants ask for "the latest", receive whatever the reviewer finds, and the review comments then cite a version the consultant never had.

## Why It's Still Broken
Nobody owns the standards as a product; updates are made in response to failures and never consolidated; and agencies have no publishing tool beyond a web page of PDFs.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A single versioned standards library — details, specification sections, approved lists — with change history, published to consultants with notifications when something changes, and each review comment linked to the clause and version it cites.

## Who Feels the Pain
Agency reviewers repeating themselves, consultants redrawing to standards they could not find, and maintenance crews living with non-standard equipment.

## Impact If Fixed
Consultants design to the current standard from the first submittal, and review comments drop accordingly.

**One-liner:** An agency landscape architect reviews a dozen consultant submittals a month against standards in their head, and writes the same comments to every consultant.

**Contested on:** Every serious competitor serving this niche is fighting to let an agency landscape architect review consultant submittals against the agency's own standards fast enough to keep capital projects on their grant and bid dates, and whoever shortens review without letting standards slip takes the account.

## The Problem
A park agency's design standards specify approved play manufacturers, irrigation controllers compatible with its central system, site furnishings its crews can maintain, tree species on the approved list, and details for edges and surfacing. Consultants ignore or miss them. The agency reviewer catches the deviations by reading, at each milestone, on every project, and writes comments that repeat across consultants and years.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Design review software is sold to building departments for code review, not to owners for their own standards. Each agency's standards are different and poorly structured.

## What to Build
A review assistant that structures the agency's standards into checkable rules, reads consultant drawing sets and specifications, flags deviations with the standard cited, drafts comments from the agency's past comment library, and tracks which issues each consultant repeats — so the reviewer spends time on design judgement rather than catalogue numbers.

## Target Customer
Park planning and design managers at mid-to-large park agencies, campus landscape architects, and DOT landscape units.

## Impact If Built
Review turnaround cut from weeks to days, fewer standards slipping into construction, and consultant performance data the agency can use at the next selection.

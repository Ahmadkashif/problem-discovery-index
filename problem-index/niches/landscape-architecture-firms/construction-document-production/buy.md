# BIM Coordination Tools Adapted to Site and Planting

**Industry:** [[industries/landscape-architecture-firms|Landscape Architecture Firms]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Building teams coordinate in model-checking software that catches clashes between ducts and beams; the landscape is usually not in the model at all.
**Tags:** #graph-theory #feature-engineering #data-integration #automation #workflow-orchestration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep the plan, plant schedule, details, specifications and estimate in sync through every revision so the bid set goes out without coordination conflicts, and whoever eliminates the manual coordination pass takes the account.

## The Problem
On campus and mixed-use projects, the architect and engineers coordinate in Revit and Navisworks, and the landscape architect delivers 2D CAD. Conflicts between tree root zones and utilities, planters and structural slabs, site lighting and irrigation mains are found in the field.

## What Already Exists
Autodesk Revit, Navisworks and BIM Collaborate, Solibri model checking, and Vectorworks Landmark's site modelling.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs: landscape objects modelled with the attributes that matter for coordination — tree root zone and mature canopy volume, soil cell and structural soil extents, planter depth and drainage, irrigation mains and sleeves; clash rules specific to site work (tree versus utility separation, root zone versus foundation, canopy versus lighting); and a lightweight path for firms working in AutoCAD to contribute without moving to Revit.

## Target Customer
Landscape architects on building-integrated projects for universities, healthcare systems and developers who require BIM execution plans.

## Impact If Solved
Site conflicts found in coordination rather than in excavation, and landscape firms able to meet owners' BIM requirements without retooling the whole office.

# Selling Evidence About What Works

**Niche:** [[niches/saas-implementation-partners/adoption-outcome-evidence/profile|Adoption Outcome Evidence]]
**Industry:** [[industries/saas-implementation-partners|SaaS Implementation Partners]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** The same experiment run hundreds of times with the result recorded zero times.
**Tags:** #causal-inference #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #data-integration #descriptive-statistics #survival-analysis #revenue-impact #hypothesis-testing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to know which of its configurations were actually adopted, when the evidence accumulates in the client's tenant the day after the partner leaves — and whoever collects it takes the account.

## The Problem
Every implementation is an experiment: configure a platform for an organisation of a certain shape and see whether it is adopted. The configurations exist as metadata in hundreds of customer tenants; the outcomes exist as usage data in the same tenants; no partner has joined them. The firm therefore competes on certifications and case studies, and cannot answer the question every buyer asks during a sales cycle — what works for an organisation like ours.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The data belongs to hundreds of different clients under separate contracts, which is assumed to end the conversation. The engagement ends at go-live and nobody is paid for what follows. No partner has framed the delivery history as a dataset. And measuring adoption might show that some configurations do not work.

## What to Build
Arrange access, measure adoption, and accumulate across clients. Negotiate post-go-live usage access as a standard engagement term rather than a favour, which is the core and is a commercial change most clients would accept if asked. Define the adoption measures at design time — which objects, workflows and fields are expected to be used and by whom — since a measure chosen afterwards is chosen to fit. Measure at defined intervals rather than only during hypercare, because ninety days is where abandonment shows. Join configuration metadata to usage so the question is which configurations were adopted rather than whether the client was happy. Accumulate across clients with the data abstracted to the pattern level, which is where confidentiality is manageable and where the value is. Report the result back to the client as a service, which is what makes them willing to participate. Identify configurations that are consistently abandoned, as those are directly actionable in the next design. Feed the evidence into the sales cycle, which is the commercial payoff. Handle the client-specific factors that affect adoption independently of the configuration. And publish pattern-level findings, since a partner that can say what works for this shape of organisation is selling something different.

## Target Customer
Implementation partners and systems integrators, firm leadership, platform vendors with partner ecosystems, and adoption analytics vendors.

## Impact If Built
Configurations sit in hundreds of tenants and their usage sits beside them, and nobody has joined the two. Adoption measured against a design-time expectation, accumulated at pattern level, changes what the firm is selling.

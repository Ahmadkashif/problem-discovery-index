# Product Telemetry From SaaS Vendors

**Niche:** [[niches/saas-implementation-partners/adoption-outcome-evidence/profile|Adoption Outcome Evidence]]
**Industry:** [[industries/saas-implementation-partners|SaaS Implementation Partners]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** SaaS vendors instrument every feature to see what customers use, and the partners configuring those platforms measure nothing.
**Tags:** #data-integration #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #survival-analysis #confidence-intervals #automation #revenue-impact #causal-inference
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to know which of its configurations were actually adopted, when the evidence accumulates in the client's tenant the day after the partner leaves — and whoever collects it takes the account.

## The Problem
Product telemetry is standard practice at every SaaS vendor. Feature usage is instrumented, adoption curves are tracked per account, abandoned features are identified and either fixed or retired, and product decisions are made on what customers actually do. The partners who configure those same platforms for customers — and who make hundreds of design decisions per engagement — have no equivalent and rely on the client's satisfaction as the only signal.

## What Already Exists
Feature-level usage instrumentation; per-account adoption curves; abandonment and depth-of-use analysis; cohort comparison across customers; and usage-driven roadmap prioritisation.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to configurations in customer-owned tenants rather than features in a vendor's product. It requires: (1) usage data owned by hundreds of separate clients rather than by the party measuring, so access is a contractual negotiation per engagement — this is the substantive difference and is the binding constraint; (2) the unit of analysis being a configuration decision rather than a product feature; (3) adoption driven heavily by the client's own change management, which must be separated from the configuration's quality; (4) measurement after the engagement has commercially ended; and (5) aggregation across clients requiring abstraction to protect confidentiality.

## Target Customer
Implementation partners, firm leadership, platform vendors, and product analytics vendors.

## Impact If Solved
SaaS vendors instrument every feature and decide on what customers do. Usage owned by hundreds of separate clients, measured after the engagement ends, is what makes access rather than analysis the binding constraint.

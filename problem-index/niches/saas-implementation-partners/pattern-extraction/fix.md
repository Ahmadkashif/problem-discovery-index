# Nobody Can List What We Have Configured

**Niche:** [[niches/saas-implementation-partners/pattern-extraction/profile|Pattern Extraction]]
**Industry:** [[industries/saas-implementation-partners|SaaS Implementation Partners]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Asked which clients have an approval workflow of this shape, the firm cannot answer.
**Tags:** #quick-win #data-integration #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #workflow-orchestration #automation #tacit-knowledge-ml #sets-and-logic
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to find out what hundreds of past implementations of the same platform actually have in common — and whoever extracts that takes the account.

## The Problem
A consultant faces a design question and wants to know how the firm has handled it before. There is no way to find out. The answer exists across dozens of past engagements and the only retrieval method is asking colleagues whether they remember something similar. Whoever is asked answers from their own handful of projects, so the firm's four hundred implementations reach the decision as three anecdotes.

## Why It's Still Broken
Nothing indexes the engagements — a delivery history stored as project folders named after clients cannot answer a question about configuration, because nothing about the configuration is in the index. Metadata was never extracted. Nobody owns the question. And asking a colleague usually produces something serviceable.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Build a rough index before attempting any extraction. Maintain a simple register of each engagement with the platform modules, integrations and notable configurations it involved, which is the fix and is a form filled in at project close. Record the client's industry, size and complexity alongside, as those are the retrieval keys. Make it searchable by the whole delivery organisation rather than by practice. Backfill the last two years from project documentation rather than the whole history, which is achievable and captures most of the value. Note the unusual solutions explicitly, since those are what people actually search for. Name a contact per engagement so a consultant can ask the right person rather than broadcasting. Record what did not work as well as what did. Review the register for recurring shapes periodically, which is the manual version of pattern extraction and produces most of the insight. Make completing it a condition of project close. And keep it to one screen per engagement, since anything longer will not be filled in.

## Who Feels the Pain
Consultants designing from three anecdotes; clients paying for a solution the firm has already built; delivery leads who cannot tell whether a proposal is novel; and the firm's experience, which never reaches the work.

## Impact If Fixed
A delivery history stored as project folders named after clients cannot answer a question about configuration, because nothing about the configuration is in the index. A one-screen register at project close makes the firm's history askable.

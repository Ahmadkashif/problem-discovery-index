# The Accelerator Nobody Has Opened in Two Years

**Niche:** [[niches/saas-implementation-partners/accelerator-productisation/profile|Accelerator Productisation]]
**Industry:** [[industries/saas-implementation-partners|SaaS Implementation Partners]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The firm's accelerator library lists twelve assets and the consultants will not deploy any of them.
**Tags:** #quick-win #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #workflow-orchestration #compliance #automation #revenue-impact #data-integration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn a known pattern into a deployable asset that survives three platform releases a year and a dozen client variations — and whoever maintains that takes the account.

## The Problem
The library exists and nobody uses it. Consultants do not know which assets still work, which platform version they were built for, or what happens when a client needs something slightly different. Deploying an unknown asset onto a client tenant is a risk they will not take under delivery pressure, so they rebuild. The firm continues to list the accelerators in proposals, which makes the gap between the claim and the practice a commercial exposure as well as a waste.

## Why It's Still Broken
Nobody knows the assets' state — an asset library with no currency information is unusable regardless of whether the assets work, because the consultant cannot tell and will not risk finding out. Nothing tests them. Nobody tracks use. And listing them in proposals costs nothing.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Establish which assets still work and say so plainly. Test every listed asset against the current platform version and publish the result, which is the fix and immediately separates the live from the dead. Retire what fails rather than leaving it listed, since a library that is half dead is trusted like one that is entirely dead. Record the platform version, last test date and last deployment against each asset. Name an owner per surviving asset, even if the ownership is nominal, so there is somewhere to send a problem. Ask consultants why they rebuild rather than deploy, which usually gives the real answer in an afternoon. Deploy a surviving asset on the next suitable engagement deliberately and record how it went. Measure deployments per asset rather than assets in the library. Stop listing untested assets in proposals, which is a commercial risk as well as a credibility one. Publish the currency status where consultants look. And keep three maintained assets rather than twelve unmaintained ones, which is the honest trade.

## Who Feels the Pain
Consultants rebuilding rather than risking an unknown asset; clients paying for work the firm claims to have industrialised; proposal teams making claims delivery cannot honour; and the investment already sunk into the library.

## Impact If Fixed
An asset library with no currency information is unusable regardless of whether the assets work, because the consultant cannot tell and will not risk finding out. Testing and publishing currency separates the live from the dead.

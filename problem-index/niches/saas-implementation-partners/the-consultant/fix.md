# Two Clients, One Afternoon

**Niche:** [[niches/saas-implementation-partners/the-consultant/profile|The Consultant on Two Engagements]]
**Industry:** [[industries/saas-implementation-partners|SaaS Implementation Partners]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The consultant switches between two client environments four times in an afternoon and loses twenty minutes each time.
**Tags:** #worker-facing #quick-win #workflow-orchestration #automation #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #data-integration #compliance
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to stop a consultant measured on billable hours from spending the unbilled remainder rebuilding what the firm has already built — and whoever fixes that takes the account.

## The Problem
Consultants staffed across concurrent engagements pay a context-switching tax all day: different tenants, different credentials, different naming conventions, different client norms, different open questions. Each switch costs real time reloading context, and the time is invisible because it is billed to whichever client they switched to. Over a week it is a substantial share of capacity and it appears nowhere.

## Why It's Still Broken
The cost is billed and therefore invisible — time spent reloading context is charged to a client like any other time, so it never appears as waste to anyone who could reduce it. Staffing is optimised for utilisation. Tooling assumes one engagement. And consultants regard it as normal.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Reduce the switches and keep the context. Block time by client rather than interleaving, which is the fix and is a scheduling decision that costs nothing. Keep per-client context — open questions, decisions, conventions, environment links — in one place the consultant returns to. Reduce credential and environment friction with managed access rather than a password manager and three browsers. Limit concurrent engagements per consultant where the work allows, since three is materially worse than two. Measure switching frequency, which nobody does and which would inform staffing. Capture the state of work when switching away, so returning is resumption rather than reconstruction. Standardise naming and conventions across the firm's deliveries, which reduces the cognitive load of every switch. Let consultants signal when the load is unmanageable, with it taken seriously. Schedule deep work blocks explicitly rather than leaving them to chance. And account for the switching cost in staffing models rather than pretending a consultant at fifty percent on two engagements equals one at full time on one.

## Who Feels the Pain
Consultants losing hours to friction they cannot bill as friction; clients paying for reloaded context; delivery schedules that assume a productivity that does not exist; and the consultants who leave.

## Impact If Fixed
Time spent reloading context is charged to a client like any other time, so it never appears as waste to anyone who could reduce it. Blocking time by client and keeping per-client context is a scheduling decision with real capacity behind it.

## The Problem
Large distributed organisations confronted the problem of people solving things colleagues had already solved, and built expertise location: profiles derived from what people have actually worked on rather than from self-description, routing of questions to the people most likely to answer, and communities of practice with real membership. The lesson is that the fastest route to an answer is usually a person, and finding that person is the hard part.

## What Already Exists
Expertise profiles derived from work history; question routing to likely answerers; communities of practice with active facilitation; answer capture for reuse; and contribution recognition.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to consultants billing time across client engagements under confidentiality. It requires: (1) expertise derived from client engagement history that is itself confidential, so profiles must describe capability without disclosing the client — this is the substantive difference; (2) people whose time is billable, so answering a colleague's question has a direct cost that must be recognised; (3) consultants distributed across client sites rather than in a shared environment; (4) questions that are platform-specific and time-critical during a delivery; and (5) a firm where the answer may exist as configuration in a tenant rather than as knowledge in a person.

## Target Customer
Implementation partners, practice leadership, consultants, and knowledge management and collaboration vendors.

## Impact If Solved
Large organisations built expertise location because the fastest route to an answer is usually a person and finding them is the hard part. Expertise derived from confidential client history, held by people billing time, is what the adaptation must handle.

# The Discovery Workshop That Starts From Nothing

**Industry:** [[industries/saas-implementation-partners|SaaS Implementation Partners]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The firm has run this workshop for twelve similar clients and opens the thirteenth with a blank whiteboard.
**Tags:** #quick-win #workflow-orchestration #data-integration #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #tacit-knowledge-ml #automation #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to stop rebuilding what the firm has already built hundreds of times, and whoever industrialises that reuse takes the account.

## The Problem
Discovery workshops are run as though the firm has never encountered this kind of client. For an organisation of a given size in a given industry, the requirements are substantially predictable — the last twelve engagements established them — and the workshop rediscovers them over several days at full rate. The client experiences it as thoroughness. The consultant experiences it as asking questions whose answers they can already guess.

## Why It's Still Broken
Nothing carries forward — requirements captured per engagement in a document nobody indexes cannot inform the next engagement, so every workshop begins where the first one did. Nobody catalogues requirements across clients. Discovery is billable. And starting from a draft feels like presumption.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Bring a straw model to the workshop instead of a blank page. Compile the recurring requirements by industry and organisation size from past engagements, which is the fix and is a document assembled from project folders. Open the workshop with a draft to react to rather than a blank whiteboard, since people confirm and correct far faster than they generate. Mark which requirements are near-universal and which genuinely vary, so the time is spent on the variation. Record the workshop's output in a comparable structure rather than as prose, which is what makes the next compilation better. Track how much of each engagement's requirements were predicted, which measures whether the practice is working. Spend the recovered time on the client's genuinely unusual needs, which is what they are actually paying for. Use the draft in the sales cycle, since demonstrating that the firm knows this shape of client is persuasive. Keep it by industry and size rather than by platform module, which is how the variation actually organises. Update it after each engagement as a standing step. And be explicit with the client that the firm is starting from experience, which is a selling point rather than a shortcut.

## Who Feels the Pain
Consultants asking questions they know the answers to; clients paying full rate for rediscovery; delivery schedules consumed by workshops; and the firm, whose experience does not reach the engagement.

## Impact If Fixed
Requirements captured per engagement in a document nobody indexes cannot inform the next engagement, so every workshop begins where the first one did. A compiled straw model by industry and size turns generation into correction.

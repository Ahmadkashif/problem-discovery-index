# Niche Analysis — SaaS Implementation Partners

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/saas-implementation-partners|SaaS Implementation Partners]]

## Niche Selection

A partner that has implemented the same platform four hundred times starts the four hundred and first from a discovery workshop. The expertise is deep and lives in individuals; the firm's delivery history is not treated as a dataset by anybody. The eight niches below follow the money through an engagement: what gets rebuilt that already existed, what happens to adoption after go-live, what the integration and release layers cost every time, and who carries the configuration nobody documented.

## Why These Niches

Configuration reuse and adoption evidence carry the money and the two things that would change what the firm sells: the same work rebuilt hundreds of times, and hundreds of natural experiments whose results are never collected. Integration architecture and release regression are the two recurring costs nobody has industrialised — the same dozen systems connected slightly differently on every engagement, and three platform releases a year against a spreadsheet of manual scripts. The two underserved audiences are the consultant billed across two engagements while rebuilding what the firm already owns, and the engineer supporting a configuration built by people who have left. The last two are mechanical: moving the data, and managing the environments.

## Filter Notes

Seven of the eight are terminal — each names one contest that every serious competitor is fighting over, and decomposing further would produce features rather than markets.

**Configuration reuse** is not. It names a practice, and inside it are two problems that fail independently and are usually confused for one another. Pattern extraction asks what the firm's hundreds of past implementations actually have in common — mining configuration metadata, requirements documents and delivery artefacts into abstracted patterns — a data and analysis problem over material the firm already holds across client engagements, sold to delivery leadership, and valuable as an internal reference even if nothing is ever productised. Accelerator productisation asks how a pattern becomes a maintained, deployable asset that survives three platform releases a year and a dozen client variations — a product engineering and maintenance problem with an owner, a roadmap and a support cost, sold as an investment rather than as an analysis. Every large partner has built accelerators and watched them rot, because they productised without extracting and had nothing to refresh them from; every partner that has catalogued patterns without funding maintenance has a document nobody uses. They are separate purchases and each fails alone.

Two adjacent candidates were rejected as belonging elsewhere: **the platform product being implemented** belongs to its vendor and sits at [[industries/crm-platforms|CRM Platforms]] and its neighbours, and **building the customer's data platform underneath it** is its own industry at [[industries/data-platform-integrators|Data Platform Integrators]].

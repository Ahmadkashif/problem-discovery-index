# Transcript Libraries

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/expert-networks|Expert Networks]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to hold a compliant, searchable transcript on the specific company or private competitor a subscriber is researching before they search for it — and whoever covers the long tail first takes the research seat.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$400M global
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~14% of industry revenue
**Digital Adoption:** High — subscription platforms with semantic search
**Target Buyer:** Directors of research and research operations at funds; content heads at library platforms
**Automation Potential:** High — transcription, redaction, tagging and coverage planning are all automatable

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Libraries convert the one-to-one call into a one-to-many product: an analyst-hosted or client-permitted call is transcribed, anonymised, compliance-reviewed and sold by subscription. AlphaSense (with Tegus), Third Bridge Forum, In Practise and the large networks' own libraries compete here. A subscriber does not care how many transcripts exist; they care whether there is one on the company they are looking at this afternoon. The contest is coverage of the long tail, decided by which calls the library chooses to commission.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Hosted calls by in-house analysts, transcription and editing pipelines, semantic search and AI summaries, entity tagging to tickers. The gaps: commissioning decisions made by editorial judgement rather than from unmet search demand; redaction that over- or under-removes; private-company tagging that misses informal references; and no measure of whether transcripts proved accurate.

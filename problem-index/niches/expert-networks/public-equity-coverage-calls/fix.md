# The Same Five Experts Every Quarter

**Niche:** [[niches/expert-networks/public-equity-coverage-calls/profile|Public-Equity Coverage Calls]]
**Industry:** [[industries/expert-networks|Expert Networks]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Analysts rebook the experts they liked, whose knowledge ages every quarter since they left the company, and nobody tracks when an expert stopped knowing.
**Tags:** #survival-analysis #logistic-regression #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #compliance #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to put an expert in front of a fund analyst who adds something the analyst's last calls and the transcript library did not already say, cleared under the fund's MNPI policy — and whoever does that most reliably becomes the fund's first call.

## The Problem
An analyst finds a former regional sales director who gives a clear, confident read on a company's pricing. They book that expert every quarter for two years. The expert left the company three years ago; their contacts there have dispersed; their read is increasingly inference. The analyst cannot tell when the information stopped being first-hand.

## Why It's Still Broken
Rebooking is the path of least resistance for the client and the highest-conversion request for the network. Expert recency is recorded as a departure date nobody looks at.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Show time-since-departure and a modelled knowledge-decay estimate for the topic on every rebooking. Track repeat-expert concentration per analyst and per ticker. Flag when an expert's statements become less specific over successive calls, which transcripts reveal. Suggest a more recent former employee for the same role.

## Who Feels the Pain
Analysts relying on stale first-hand views; fund compliance teams, since experts who remain in contact with former colleagues are a higher MNPI risk; and networks whose repeat bookings mask declining quality.

## Impact If Fixed
Repeat experts concentrate a fund's primary research in a few ageing sources. Making recency and specificity visible is cheap and changes the rebooking decision.

# The Segment Nobody Called

**Niche:** [[niches/expert-networks/deal-sprint-diligence-calls/profile|Deal-Sprint Diligence Calls]]
**Industry:** [[industries/expert-networks|Expert Networks]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Thirty calls into a sprint, the deal team discovers every customer it spoke to was enterprise and the target's growth is in mid-market.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #large-language-models #evaluation-metrics #worker-facing #quick-win #workflow-orchestration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to deliver dozens of relevant customers, former employees and competitors of an often private target inside a deal team's two-to-three-week diligence clock — and whoever fills the call plan fastest with people who actually used or sold the product wins the deal team's next sprint.

## The Problem
Experts who are easiest to find are not a random sample of the target's customers. Large-company employees are more visible and more responsive. A sprint that books whoever is available overweights them, and the bias surfaces only when the findings are written up.

## Why It's Still Broken
Calls are booked one at a time as profiles arrive, under deadline pressure, and segment balance is nobody's explicit metric.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Classify each completed call by segment from the transcript, compare the achieved mix with the target's known customer mix, and push requests for under-covered segments to the network daily.

## Who Feels the Pain
Deal associates writing the customer section; investment committees reading a sample they cannot see is skewed.

## Impact If Fixed
A skewed customer sample is the commonest silent flaw in call-based diligence. Tracking it costs almost nothing.

# The Spending-Intent Survey Nobody Backtests

**Niche:** [[niches/expert-networks/expert-survey-panels/profile|Expert Survey Panels]]
**Industry:** [[industries/expert-networks|Expert Networks]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Networks run the same IT budget-intent surveys every quarter and never compare what respondents said they would spend with what vendors later reported.
**Tags:** #linear-regression #time-series-forecasting #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to field a quantitative survey to a verified population of practitioners — physicians, IT buyers, procurement heads — fast enough for an investment decision, with respondents who are who they say they are — and whoever proves respondent quality wins the quantitative half of the research budget.

## The Problem
Recurring intent surveys are sold as leading indicators. Their predictive value is asserted, not measured.

## Why It's Still Broken
Each survey is a project; the series is nobody's product, and a weak backtest would be commercially unwelcome.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Maintain recurring surveys as time series, backtest them against subsequent reported revenue of the vendors covered, and publish predictive accuracy with the series.

## Who Feels the Pain
Fund analysts using intent data as a signal; networks unable to price their surveys on evidence.

## Impact If Fixed
A demonstrated leading indicator is a product; an undemonstrated one is a project.

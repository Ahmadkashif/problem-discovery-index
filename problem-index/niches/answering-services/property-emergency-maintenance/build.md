# One Emergency Rulebook per Portfolio, Graded by the Morning Work Order

**Niche:** [[niches/answering-services/property-emergency-maintenance/profile|Property Management Emergency Maintenance Lines]]
**Industry:** [[industries/answering-services|Telephone Answering Services]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A management company's forty properties carry forty slightly different emergency definitions, and nobody checks the night's callouts against what the technician actually found.
**Tags:** #bert #gradient-boosting #evaluation-metrics #large-language-models #workflow-orchestration #automation #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to apply each owner's own emergency definition to a resident's 2 a.m. call and get the on-call maintenance technician moving only when the call qualifies, and whoever cuts false callouts without ever missing an active leak, a gas smell or a no-heat call in a freeze takes the account.

## The Problem
Emergency rules accumulate property by property as owners and managers change, so a portfolio's rules contradict each other and the operator follows whichever account screen is open. After a callout the technician records what was found in the work order (actual leak, resident error, routine repair), and that record never reaches the answering service.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The rules are set by the manager, the calls are taken by the service, and the outcomes are recorded in the property-management system. Three parties, no shared record.

## What to Build
A portfolio rulebook: owner-level emergency definitions with property exceptions, stored as structured rules the operator screen runs. Each night's calls are joined to the morning work orders, and every callout is labelled warranted or not, every hold safe or not. A classifier learned from transcripts and outcomes flags borderline calls, and a monthly report shows each manager the false-callout and missed-emergency rates by property.

## Target Customer
Answering services with property-management portfolios; directors of maintenance at management companies with thousands of units.

## Impact If Built
False callouts fall, which is technician retention and overtime money; missed emergencies become measurable rather than discovered through insurance claims; and the manager can tune the rules with evidence.

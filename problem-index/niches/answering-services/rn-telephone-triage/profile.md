# RN Telephone Triage Lines

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/answering-services|Telephone Answering Services]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to put a nurse licensed in the caller's state on the line within minutes at 3 a.m. and reach a protocol-correct disposition that keeps safe callers out of the emergency department, and whoever covers every state a practice's patients call from with the shortest wait for a nurse takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$350M a year of after-hours nurse triage for practices and health systems, much of it billed by specialist triage providers outside NAICS 561421 (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~13% of the medical after-hours spend counted in the parent industry (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** High — triage runs on protocol software, though hand-offs from the answering service are often a warm phone transfer
**Target Buyer:** Medical director or practice administrator at pediatric, family medicine and multi-specialty groups; access-centre director at health systems
**Automation Potential:** Moderate — documentation and protocol navigation automate; the licensed disposition does not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The service sells a clinical judgement made by a registered nurse following a published protocol. Schmitt-Thompson protocols are used in over 400 call centres and, by the publisher's 2015 count, more than 20 million calls a year. The nurse must be licensed where the caller is. The Nurse Licensure Compact, enacted in 41 states and two territories, solves that for most of the map but not all of it, so coverage of non-compact states is a real differentiator. Competitors are specialist triage providers (TriageLogic, Fonemed, Conduit Health Partners and others), answering services with their own RN staff, and health systems' in-house nurse lines.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Triage software carrying Schmitt-Thompson content (TriageLogic, ClearTriage and others); EHR integration for the encounter note; the answering service's platform for the front-end call. Gaps: dispositions are never followed to outcome (did the "home care" caller go to the ER next day?); licensure coverage is managed by spreadsheet; the warm transfer from operator to nurse loses what the caller already said.

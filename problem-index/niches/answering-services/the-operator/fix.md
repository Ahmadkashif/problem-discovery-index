# Feedback Only When Something Goes Wrong

**Niche:** [[niches/answering-services/the-operator/profile|The Answering Service Operator]]
**Industry:** [[industries/answering-services|Telephone Answering Services]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** An operator hears about her calls only when a client complains, so the hard calls she got right go unacknowledged and the job feels like a minefield.
**Tags:** #evaluation-metrics #large-language-models #worker-facing #quick-win #workflow-orchestration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to make an operator two weeks out of training handle any of hundreds of client accounts correctly on the first call, and whoever gets new operators account-accurate fastest takes the account.

## The Problem
Quality review samples a few calls per operator per month, often scored on greeting and tone. Client complaints trigger a review of the bad call. Nothing tells an operator that her escalation of a borderline call was exactly right, or that her handling of a death call was praised by the funeral director.

## Why It's Still Broken
Positive outcomes are invisible to the service because clients report only failures, and QA staff time goes to compliance sampling.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A weekly operator digest: hard calls handled (escalations, distressed callers, first calls), client confirmations where available, and one or two calls flagged for coaching with the reason. Recognition and coaching come from the same record.

## Who Feels the Pain
Operators in their first year, who decide whether to stay; supervisors who lose them.

## Impact If Fixed
Operators who see that they are getting hard calls right are more likely to stay, and in this industry retention is the thing that preserves the judgement clients are paying for.

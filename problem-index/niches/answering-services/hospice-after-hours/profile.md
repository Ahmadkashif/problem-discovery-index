# Hospice & Home Health After-Hours Lines

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/answering-services|Telephone Answering Services]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to tell, at 2 a.m., which family calls need a hospice nurse in the home and which a nurse can settle by phone, inside the agency's response standard, and whoever sends a nurse to every visit that needs one and to none that don't takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$120M a year of after-hours answering and triage for hospice and home health agencies (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~5% of answering-service receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium-low — agencies run hospice EHRs, but the night line is an answering service relaying to an on-call nurse by phone or app, often without chart access
**Target Buyer:** Hospice administrator or director of clinical services; on-call manager at a multi-site agency
**Automation Potential:** Moderate — intake and routing automate; the visit decision belongs to a nurse

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Medicare's hospice Conditions of Participation (42 CFR 418.100) require nursing services, physician services and drugs to be routinely available 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, so the night line is a regulatory obligation, not a courtesy. The callers are families caring for a dying person at home, frightened by breathing changes, pain or agitation, and the right answer is almost never the emergency department. A hospital transfer can undo the plan of care and the family's wishes. General medical scripts and ED-oriented triage protocols point the wrong way. The audience is underserved because the tools were built for the opposite clinical goal. Contestants are answering services with hospice scripts, triage providers with hospice protocols, hospice EHR vendors' on-call modules, and agencies whose on-call nurses take calls directly. The agency side of this industry sits in [[industries/home-health-agencies|Home Health Agencies]].

## Current Tools & Gaps
Hospice EHRs (with on-call note functions), answering-service relays, nurse triage software built on ED-oriented protocols. Gaps: the operator cannot see the plan of care or the comfort kit in the home; dispositions are not tied to visit outcomes; families call 911 when the callback is slow.

# Triage Protocols Turned Toward Comfort Care

**Niche:** [[niches/answering-services/hospice-after-hours/profile|Hospice & Home Health After-Hours Lines]]
**Industry:** [[industries/answering-services|Telephone Answering Services]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Telephone triage protocols are built to catch emergencies and send them to the ED; for a hospice patient, that disposition is usually the failure.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #compliance #workflow-orchestration #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to tell, at 2 a.m., which family calls need a hospice nurse in the home and which a nurse can settle by phone, inside the agency's response standard, and whoever sends a nurse to every visit that needs one and to none that don't takes the account.

## The Problem
An answering service or triage nurse using standard symptom protocols will send a caller describing shortness of breath, chest pain or unresponsiveness to emergency care, which is right for the general population and wrong for an enrolled hospice patient whose plan of care anticipates exactly these symptoms.

## What Already Exists
Published telephone triage protocols (Schmitt-Thompson, used in over 400 call centres, and nursing triage texts) embedded in triage software; hospice EHR on-call notes.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs: comfort-care branches that check the patient's hospice enrolment and plan of care first; awareness of the comfort medication kit in the home and the last doses given; coaching scripts for families on expected end-of-life changes; dispositions of nurse visit, phone coaching, or chaplain or social worker call, not ED; and an explicit reminder to families not to call 911. The operator screen must show enough of the plan of care to route correctly.

## Target Customer
Triage providers and answering services serving hospices; hospice EHR vendors' on-call modules.

## Impact If Solved
Night calls follow the hospice plan instead of the ED pathway, avoiding unwanted transfers and the distress they cause families.

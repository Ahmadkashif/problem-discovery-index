# On-Call Routing & Paging

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/answering-services|Telephone Answering Services]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get each urgent patient call to the correct on-call clinician, acknowledged inside the practice's time limit, with a record that will stand up in a negligence claim, and whoever closes the most urgent pages inside the limit without waking a clinician for a routine call takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$750M a year of operator-level medical answering (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~28% of answering-service receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — paging and secure apps are digital, but schedules and swaps travel by fax, email and phone
**Target Buyer:** Practice administrator; access-centre director at hospital-owned groups
**Automation Potential:** High for schedule sync and acknowledgement tracking, moderate for the page-or-hold decision

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Here the service's operators make no clinical judgement; they apply the practice's rules and get the right physician on the phone. The contest is reliability of reach: correct schedule, correct chain, acknowledgement inside the limit, and no nuisance pages. Competitors are the operator-level medical services, from local independents to multi-site firms, plus hospital switchboards that take over a group's calls once it is acquired. The winner is decided by schedule accuracy and acknowledgement discipline, not by clinical staff.

## Current Tools & Gaps
TAS on-call modules (Startel, Amtelco, Telescan), Amtelco Secure Messages and similar apps, Spok paging, physician schedulers on the practice side. Gaps: no live link from the practice's scheduler to the service's on-call module; acknowledgement deadlines set by habit, not evidence; no report showing the practice how fast its own physicians respond.

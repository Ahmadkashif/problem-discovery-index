# The Technician Woken for a Thermostat

**Niche:** [[niches/answering-services/trades-after-hours-dispatch/profile|Home Services & Trades Answering]]
**Industry:** [[industries/answering-services|Telephone Answering Services]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Operators page the on-call technician for anything a caller calls urgent, because the contractor's emergency definition is a sentence in the notes field and nobody wants to be blamed for a frozen pipe.
**Tags:** #bert #large-language-models #evaluation-metrics #workflow-orchestration #quick-win #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn each after-hours call to a contractor into either a booked job on the contractor's dispatch board or a page for a genuine emergency, and whoever books the most billable jobs without waking a technician for a non-emergency takes the account.

## The Problem
On-call technicians at residential contractors rotate nights and weekends. Every page interrupts sleep or family time, and when half of them turn out to be a dead thermostat battery or a next-day maintenance request, the technician calls the owner, and the owner calls the answering service.

## Why It's Still Broken
The contractor's rules ("emergency if no heat and outdoor temperature below 40, or any active water, or any burning smell") are written once at setup and applied from memory. Operators are not penalised for over-paging, only for under-paging, so the incentive points one way.

## What a Fix Looks Like
The rules become a checklist the operator screen walks through, pulling the outdoor temperature for the caller's address automatically and asking the discriminating questions (is water actively flowing; is there a smell of gas). The page carries the answers so the technician can decide in seconds whether to roll. A weekly nuisance-page report goes to the contractor so the rule can be tuned.

## Who Feels the Pain
On-call technicians and their families; owners fielding complaints; operators caught between the two.

## Impact If Fixed
Fewer nuisance pages mean less on-call burnout in a trade short of technicians, and the contractor stops treating the answering service as the reason its best tech wants off the rotation.

# The Annotation Workforce

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/trust-safety-tooling-vendors|Trust & Safety Tooling Vendors]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Whether the people who look at harmful material so a classifier can learn from it are visible to anyone, or sit a layer below the moderators who are themselves barely visible.

## Profile

**Market Size:** ~$240M
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~8%
**Digital Adoption:** Low — a labelling tool and a queue
**Target Buyer:** Vendor operations, labelling suppliers, platform procurement
**Automation Potential:** Moderate — exposure reduction automates, the labelling does not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche

Before a classifier can detect harmful content, people have to look at a great deal of it and label it. That workforce is even less visible than the moderators downstream.

They are frequently employed through labelling suppliers rather than by the vendor, sometimes through further subcontracting, in markets chosen for cost. They work through queues of the worst material the internet produces, deliberately concentrated — because a training set for a harm category is by construction a concentrated collection of that harm. And their exposure is arguably more intense than a moderator's, because a moderation queue contains a mix and a training set does not.

The protections that have developed for content moderation, imperfect as they are, have barely reached this layer. Wellness provision, exposure limits, clinical support and the public attention that produced them apply to the moderators the platforms employ or contract, and the annotation workforce sits one relationship further out.

This is an underserved audience in the strictest sense: they are a supplier's supplier, invisible to the platform whose classifier they train, and their existence is rarely acknowledged in the vendor's own description of how the product is built.

## Current Tools & Gaps

Annotation platforms with task queues, quality controls and agreement measurement — built for labelling quality rather than for the labeller. Some suppliers provide wellness support of varying quality. Vendor procurement requirements on suppliers, where they exist.

The gaps are the same ones content moderation has and worse. Exposure is not measured, so there is no dose to manage in a workforce whose exposure is deliberately concentrated. Presentation controls — reduced fidelity, segment isolation, greyscale — are available in principle and rarely applied to labelling interfaces. Rotation is uncommon because a labelling project is a concentrated push. Support is thin and is the supplier's responsibility. And nobody in the chain — platform, vendor or supplier — reports anything about the conditions.

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche

Around the classifiers sits the operational layer: the queue, the routing, the reviewer interface, the decision record, the appeal, the escalation path, the enforcement action and the audit trail.

It is a real product category with genuine specialists, and it is the part of the industry where the gap between what exists and what is needed is most mechanical. Routing by specialism, deduplication across reports of the same item, linking decisions to a user's history, handling appeals as a first-class workflow, and producing the audit trail a regulator will ask for are all ordinary engineering.

The contest is whether it is built as a moderation system or as a ticketing system with moderation vocabulary. A moderation case has properties a ticket does not — it concerns a person who is affected by the outcome, it produces an enforcement action with consequences, it may be appealed, and it must be defensible to a regulator. A system that models it as a ticket to be closed misses all of that.

## Current Tools & Gaps

Purpose-built trust and safety case management from the specialists, general ticketing adapted by many platforms, and the queue interfaces inside classification products. Routing by category and language. Decision recording. Some appeals workflow. Audit logging of varying completeness.

The gaps concentrate on the affected person and on the record. Appeals are frequently a separate and weaker workflow than the original decision, handled with less context by people with less time. The decision rationale is rarely recorded in a form that makes an appeal evaluable. A user's history across decisions is not assembled, so a pattern of wrongful actions against one person is invisible. Deduplication across reports of the same item is inconsistent. And the audit trail, now increasingly a regulatory requirement, is frequently insufficient to reconstruct why a decision was made.

# Buy: Generalist Support From Adjacent Functions

**Niche:** The Sole Trust & Safety Engineer
**Industry:** [[industries/trust-safety-tooling-vendors|Trust & Safety Tooling Vendors]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Security, privacy and compliance all faced the one-person-owns-everything problem at growing companies and built maturity models, checklists and communities, and trust and safety has none of them.
**Tags:** #evaluation-metrics #compliance #confidence-intervals #worker-facing #workflow-orchestration #descriptive-statistics
**Contested on:** Whether one person can own every harm type at a growing platform using tools whose accuracy they cannot verify.

## The Problem

The sole-practitioner problem at a growing company is not new. Security, privacy and compliance each went through it and built the same kinds of support.

Security produced maturity models telling a first hire what to do in what order, foundational control checklists, a substantial community, and vendors who package a baseline for a company at this stage. Privacy produced the compliance platforms, obligation maps by jurisdiction, and a professional body with certification and a community. Compliance produced frameworks with staged implementation guidance.

In each case the insight was the same: a generalist at a growing company needs a prioritised path, a statement of what is legally required, defaults rather than configuration, and a peer community — and none of it is specialisation.

Trust and safety has a growing professional community and little else. No maturity model. No staged implementation path. No jurisdiction-by-jurisdiction obligation map for a platform to consult. No baseline package. The first hire works out what to do from vendor materials and industry conversation.

## What Already Exists

Security: maturity models, foundational control lists, the substantial practitioner community, and vendors packaging a baseline for early-stage companies.

Privacy: compliance platforms with obligation mapping, professional certification, and an established professional body.

Compliance: frameworks with staged implementation guidance and the platforms described in [[industries/grc-compliance-platforms|GRC & Compliance Platforms]].

Trust and safety: a growing professional association and community, industry conferences, and vendor materials.

## The Customization Gap

**No maturity model exists.** Security's contribution to the first hire is a prioritised path. Trust and safety's first hire has no equivalent and works out the order themselves.

**No obligation map by jurisdiction.** Privacy platforms tell a company what applies to them where. Trust and safety obligations — child safety reporting, transparency requirements, regional platform regulation — are not mapped in any product a generalist can consult.

**No baseline package.** Security vendors sell a foundational set to a company at this stage. Trust and safety vendors sell components designed for an organisation.

**The professional community is younger.** It exists and is growing and does not yet have the certification, curriculum and depth that privacy and security communities offer a new practitioner.

**The harm types do not map to one discipline.** Fraud is closer to security, child safety has its own legal regime, harassment is policy judgement. The breadth is genuinely wider than security's or privacy's, which makes the guidance harder to write and more necessary.

**The consequences of an unknown obligation are severe.** A privacy generalist missing an obligation faces a regulatory finding. A trust and safety generalist missing a child safety reporting obligation faces something considerably worse.

## Target Customer

Trust and safety professional bodies and industry associations, who are the natural authors of a maturity model and a staged path and are well placed to do it.

Vendors, for whom a baseline package for the growing-platform stage is both a product and an acquisition channel into customers who will later have teams.

Platform engineering leadership, who hire the role and would benefit from a maturity model telling them what the role should actually be doing.

## Impact If Solved

Three adjacent functions faced the same problem and built the same support, and the templates are directly available.

An obligation map by jurisdiction is the highest-consequence item, because the failure mode for a generalist is not doing something badly but not knowing an obligation exists — and in this domain that includes obligations with serious legal force.

And a maturity model giving a first hire a prioritised order would address the specific difficulty of a role where everything is urgent and nothing has an obvious sequence.

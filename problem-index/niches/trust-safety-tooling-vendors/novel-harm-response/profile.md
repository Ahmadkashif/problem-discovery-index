## What Makes This a Distinct Niche

A new abuse pattern appears. A coordinated campaign with novel framing. A scam format nobody has seen. Emergent slang that carries a meaning no classifier knows. A harm type that did not exist last month.

Classifiers trained on historical labels miss it, because the label does not exist yet. The pattern is detected when someone notices — a reviewer, a user report, a journalist, a researcher — and then labelled data must be produced, a model retrained, and the update deployed. Weeks pass.

Those weeks are when a novel harm does most of its damage. A coordinated campaign runs its course. A new scam format extracts what it will extract. An emergent harassment pattern establishes itself before anything responds.

The contest is closing that gap. The signals that something new is happening frequently exist before the labels do — unusual coordination patterns, sudden clustering of similar content, reviewer decisions that do not match any existing category, reports whose free text describes something the taxonomy has no name for. Nobody is watching for them.

## Current Tools & Gaps

Model retraining cycles, generally measured in weeks to months. Rule-based rapid response for specific patterns, written by hand. Threat intelligence feeds in some categories. Hash matching once material is known. Escalation paths from review to policy for novel cases.

The gaps are in the interval before labels exist. Nothing detects that something new is appearing — the clustering, coordination and anomaly signals that precede a label are not monitored. Reviewer escalations of uncategorisable content go into a queue rather than into a detection signal. Free text in user reports, which frequently describes a novel harm before anyone names it, is unread. Rapid rules are written by hand and are brittle. And cross-platform signals are unused, though a pattern usually appears on several platforms before any one of them names it.

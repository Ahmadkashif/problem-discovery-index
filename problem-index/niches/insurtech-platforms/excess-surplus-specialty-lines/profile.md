# Excess, Surplus & Specialty Lines

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/insurtech-platforms|Insurtech Platforms]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in E&S technology is fighting to make manuscript coverage comparable across markets so a broker can tell a client what they are actually buying — and whoever makes coverage comparison reliable takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.1B US technology spend across excess and surplus lines and specialty insurance
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~7% of insurtech revenue, in the market's fastest-growing segment
**Digital Adoption:** Low — E&S runs on email, relationships and documents
**Target Buyer:** Wholesale brokers, specialty underwriters and the MGAs operating in the segment
**Automation Potential:** High — coverage comparison and submission handling are document problems that have resisted standardisation

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Excess and surplus lines exists because the admitted market will not write certain risks at certain terms, which means it operates without the two things that make admitted insurance tractable for software: filed rates and standard forms. Every carrier writes its own manuscript wording, which is the point — the flexibility is the product. The consequence is that a broker placing a risk receives several quotes whose coverage differs in ways that are material, buried in wording, and genuinely difficult to compare. The comparison is done by a person reading policies side by side, or more often not done at all, and the client buys on price among options that were never made equivalent. That is the segment's defining problem, it is growing as more business moves into E&S, and it is a problem no admitted-market technology addresses because the admitted market solved it with standard forms.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Wholesale broker platforms handle submission distribution and some workflow; specialty MGA platforms compete on speed of product launch. Document management is universal. The gaps: no coverage comparison capability of any kind, so a broker's quote comparison is a manually built spreadsheet describing whichever differences the broker happened to notice; submission distribution to markets is a broadcast rather than a targeted placement, so brokers send to more markets than necessary and underwriters receive more submissions they will decline; no market appetite data exists in structured form, so which markets write what is relationship knowledge; and binder and policy issuance, which in E&S involves assembling manuscript wordings, remains substantially manual with the attendant risk of a policy that differs from the binder.

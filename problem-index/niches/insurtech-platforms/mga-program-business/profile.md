# MGA & Programme Business

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/insurtech-platforms|Insurtech Platforms]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor serving MGAs is fighting to give a programme a reliable loss ratio signal early enough to correct it — and whoever shortens the time from inception to a trustworthy performance picture takes the programme.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$740M US technology spend across managing general agents and programme administrators
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~5% of insurtech revenue, in a segment growing faster than the market
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — a wave of MGA platforms compete on speed to market, with analytics as an afterthought
**Target Buyer:** MGA leadership and the carriers and reinsurers providing the capacity behind them
**Automation Potential:** High — the data exists across MGA and carrier systems and is joined late and badly

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
An MGA underwrites on somebody else's paper. It holds the underwriting authority, the distribution and the customer relationship; the carrier holds the risk, the regulatory obligation and, frequently, the claims. That split creates a structural information problem that defines the segment. The MGA can see what it wrote and often cannot see, in a timely way, what those policies cost — claims data sits with the carrier or a third-party administrator and reaches the MGA in periodic bordereaux that are late, aggregated and sometimes inconsistent. The MGA's entire existence depends on the programme performing, and it is frequently the last party to know that it is not. A programme that deteriorates for three quarters before anyone can demonstrate it is a programme that gets cancelled, and the capital behind these arrangements is unforgiving about exactly this.

## Current Tools & Gaps
A wave of MGA platforms — the newer core system vendors positioning on configurability, plus specialist programme administration products — compete on how quickly a programme can launch. Bordereaux reporting is the standard mechanism for reporting to carriers and reinsurers and is largely a spreadsheet exercise. Analytics is thin. The gaps: claims data reaches the MGA late and in a form that requires manual reconciliation against its own policy records, so loss ratio is a quarterly reconstruction rather than a continuous measure; bordereaux production consumes real effort and produces a report the MGA cannot itself use; the carrier and reinsurer on the other side have the same latency problem in reverse and receive a file they must validate; and early warning — the leading indicators that a programme is deteriorating before the loss ratio shows it — is not computed by anyone.

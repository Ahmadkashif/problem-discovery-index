# The Agency That Cannot Analyse Its Own Book

**Niche:** [[niches/insurtech-platforms/small-independent-agencies/profile|Small Independent Agencies]]
**Industry:** [[industries/insurtech-platforms|Insurtech Platforms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** An agency's management system holds every policy, every carrier, every premium and every renewal outcome, and the owner's understanding of their own book is a commission statement and an impression.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #survival-analysis #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #hypothesis-testing #revenue-impact #quick-win #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor selling to small agencies is fighting to let a service representative shop a renewal to six markets without rebuilding the submission six times — and whoever makes application data reusable across carriers takes the agency.

## The Problem
An agency owner knows roughly what they write, roughly which carriers they use most, and roughly how retention is going. They cannot say what their retention rate is by carrier, by class, by producer or by account size; which carriers are taking the largest rate increases on renewal and therefore driving their own losses; which classes the agency is most profitable in once service load is considered; or which accounts are at risk of leaving next quarter. All of it is in the management system. The owner's actual instrument for running the business is a monthly commission statement, which reports what was earned and nothing about what is happening.

## Why It's Still Broken
Agency management systems were built as systems of record for policies and accounting, and their reporting reflects that — it is comprehensive on transactions and thin on analysis. Small agencies have nobody to write reports, so whatever does not come out of the box does not exist. And the industry's benchmarking, where it exists, comes from association surveys with long lags, which reinforces the sense that this kind of question is answered annually by somebody else rather than continuously by the agency itself.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Produce the standard analysis every agency should have and none does. Retention by carrier, class, producer and account size, with the trend. Rate change on renewal by carrier, which is the number that predicts where the agency will lose accounts and which the agency sees account by account without ever aggregating. Accounts at risk, from a combination of rate increase, service activity, tenure and prior remarketing. Revenue concentration, both by client and by carrier, which is a genuine business risk for a small agency and is rarely quantified. Service load per account, so the owner can see which accounts consume capacity out of proportion to commission — this is the analysis that most often changes behaviour. None of it requires modelling beyond survival analysis for retention, and all of it comes from the management system's existing data.

## Who Feels the Pain
Owners running a business on impressions and a commission statement; producers whose book composition nobody analyses; and clients on accounts that were at risk for two quarters with nobody noticing.

## Impact If Fixed
The rate-change-by-carrier analysis alone typically identifies where the agency's next round of losses will come from, months before the renewals arrive, which is enough time to remarket. Service load per account reliably surprises owners and redirects how the agency spends its scarcest resource. Both are reports over existing data, which makes the absence a product gap rather than a capability one.

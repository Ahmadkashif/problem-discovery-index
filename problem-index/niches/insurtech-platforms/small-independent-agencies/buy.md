# Comparative Rating Extended Into Commercial Lines

**Niche:** [[niches/insurtech-platforms/small-independent-agencies/profile|Small Independent Agencies]]
**Industry:** [[industries/insurtech-platforms|Insurtech Platforms]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Comparative raters solved multi-carrier quoting for personal lines two decades ago and are used by every agency daily, and small commercial — where the same agencies place a large share of their revenue — is served by a fraction of that coverage.
**Tags:** #data-integration #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #workflow-orchestration #automation #compliance #descriptive-statistics #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor selling to small agencies is fighting to let a service representative shop a renewal to six markets without rebuilding the submission six times — and whoever makes application data reusable across carriers takes the agency.

## The Problem
An agency quoting a personal auto policy enters the risk once and receives indications from eight carriers in minutes. The same agency quoting a small business owner's policy — a comparable transaction with comparable economics — enters it into three carrier portals separately, because the comparative rating infrastructure that exists for personal lines covers small commercial thinly. The difference is not conceptual; the personal lines carriers built the integrations and the commercial ones largely have not.

## What Already Exists
Comparative rating platforms — EZLynx, Applied Rater, PL Rating and others — are mature, embedded in agency workflow and trusted. The rating integration model, the data normalisation and the agency-side experience are all solved. Several vendors have extended into small commercial with partial carrier coverage. ACORD commercial forms exist. The infrastructure pattern is proven and the coverage is the gap.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to commercial lines' greater complexity and thinner carrier participation. It requires: (1) a data model that handles schedules — locations, vehicles, equipment, payroll by class — which is what makes commercial harder than personal and where a personal lines rater's assumptions break; (2) class code determination from a business description, since the classification drives the rate and is the most common source of both error and inconsistency between carriers, and it is a text classification problem with abundant training data; (3) supplemental question handling, since commercial carriers ask class-specific questions that a rating interface has to accommodate without becoming an interrogation; (4) indication rather than bindable quote where that is all a carrier will provide, presented honestly, because a comparison of indications is still far better than no comparison and pretending otherwise has held the category back; and (5) a path for the carriers who will not integrate, which means portal automation as in the build note — since waiting for full carrier participation is waiting indefinitely.

## Target Customer
Independent agencies placing small commercial business, comparative rating vendors seeking their next market, and the carriers competing for small commercial who would benefit from being in the comparison at all.

## Impact If Solved
Small commercial is a large share of independent agency revenue and is placed with a fraction of the market efficiency that personal lines enjoys. Extending a proven infrastructure pattern is a coverage and integration problem rather than an invention, and the class code determination is the one genuinely new capability — which is also the piece that would improve the accuracy of every submission the agency makes.

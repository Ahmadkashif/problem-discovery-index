# Claims Core Systems

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/insurtech-platforms|Insurtech Platforms]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in claims systems is fighting to reserve a claim correctly and route it to the right adjuster at first notice — and whoever improves reserve accuracy and cycle time most takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$2.5B US claims core systems and claims technology
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~16% of insurtech revenue
**Digital Adoption:** High — claims are administered in software and decided by adjusters
**Target Buyer:** Claims executives and operations leaders at carriers and third-party administrators
**Automation Potential:** Very High — severity prediction, complexity routing and leakage detection are all supervised problems with abundant history

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A claims organisation is measured on three things: whether reserves are accurate, how long claims take, and how much money leaks out through payments that should not have been made or should have been smaller. All three are decided in the first few days. The reserve set at first notice anchors everything that follows, and it is typically set from a table or from the adjuster's impression of a claim they have not yet investigated. Assignment determines the outcome as much as anything — a complex claim given to a junior adjuster develops badly and a simple one given to a senior adjuster wastes the carrier's scarcest resource. The carrier holds decades of claims with their first notices, their reserve histories, their handling, their payments and their final outcomes, which is as clean a supervised learning setup as exists in insurance, and claims systems use it to produce a workflow.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Guidewire ClaimCenter, Duck Creek Claims and the third-party administrator platforms handle intake, workflow, payments, litigation management and reporting comprehensively. Fraud detection has real adoption. Medical bill review and subrogation identification are established vendor categories. The gaps: initial reserve is set by rule or by adjuster judgement rather than by a severity model on the carrier's own history; assignment routes by line of business, geography and workload rather than by predicted complexity and adjuster fit; reserve development — the gap between what was reserved and what was paid, by adjuster, by claim type and by cohort — is reported to actuaries in aggregate and almost never fed back to the claims organisation at a level anyone can act on; and the decisions that produce leakage are identified in retrospective audits of small samples rather than flagged as they occur.

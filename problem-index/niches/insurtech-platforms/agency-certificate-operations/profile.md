# Agency Certificate Operations

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/insurtech-platforms|Insurtech Platforms]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in certificate operations is fighting to issue a certificate that provably matches what the policy supports without a person checking — and whoever removes the human verification step takes the agency.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$680M US spend attributable to certificate issuance and tracking within agency operations
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~4% of insurtech revenue, against a much larger share of agency labour
**Digital Adoption:** Low-Medium — issuance is automated and everything around it is manual
**Target Buyer:** Agency operations leaders; certificate requesters on the other side of the transaction
**Automation Potential:** Very High — verification against policy terms is a checkable property and is checked by reading

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A certificate of insurance is a document that summarises coverage, states explicitly that it confers no rights, and consumes an extraordinary share of agency labour. A general contractor requires certificates from every subcontractor; a landlord from every tenant; a client from every vendor. Each requester wants something slightly different — a particular additional insured endorsement, a waiver of subrogation, specific limits, wording naming the project — and somebody at the agency has to determine whether the policy actually supports what is being requested before issuing. That determination is the work: issuing the document is a button, and verifying that the endorsement exists, that the limits are right, and that the requested wording is supportable requires reading the policy. Agencies employ people substantially for this. On the other side, the requester receives thousands of certificates and files them, and whether any of them means what it says is verified by nobody.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Applied Epic, Vertafore and the agency management systems all issue ACORD certificates from policy data, and certificate tracking products serve the requester side. Issuance and distribution are automated. The gaps are the verification on both sides: nothing checks a requested wording against the policy's actual endorsements before issuance, so an agency either checks manually or issues something the policy may not support — which is a real errors and omissions exposure; renewal cascades are manual, so every policy renewal triggers a wave of reissuance; and on the requester side, certificates are collected and filed with no verification that the coverage described exists or remains in force, which means the entire compliance apparatus rests on a document nobody checks.

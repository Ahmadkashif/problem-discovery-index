# Traffic Vantage Intelligence

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/edge-cdn-providers|Edge & CDN Providers]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor that gets here is fighting to turn a view of a substantial fraction of internet traffic into decisions rather than capacity — and whoever does that holds a vantage point nobody outside the category can obtain.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$560M US, largely latent
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~6% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** None — the vantage point is sold as bandwidth and rule sets
**Target Buyer:** The providers themselves; secondarily their customers
**Automation Potential:** Very High — the traffic is observed in real time at enormous scale

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
These providers see a substantial fraction of internet traffic in real time: request patterns, response characteristics, client behaviour, attack campaigns and performance under every network condition on earth. It is a vantage point nobody outside the category can obtain, and it is monetised as delivery capacity and as security rules. What it could support is a different class of product: knowing what is genuinely cacheable across the whole web rather than for one customer, recognising an adversary's adaptation as it spreads, benchmarking a customer's performance against comparable properties, warning about a network degradation before the customer's users notice it, and telling a customer that their own configuration is unusual for their type of traffic. Almost none of it is built, because the category sells bandwidth and rulesets rather than decisions — which is precisely the observation the industry's own Analysis makes.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Aggregate internet traffic reports published as marketing, threat intelligence feeds, and network performance data used internally for capacity planning. The gaps: the published reports are annual and descriptive rather than operational; nothing benchmarks a customer against comparable properties, which is the question customers ask most; network-level degradations are visible to the provider before they are visible to the customer and are not alerted on; configuration outliers — a customer whose settings are unusual for their traffic type — are detectable and not reported; and no provider has articulated a governance position for cross-customer analysis, so the topic is avoided rather than designed.

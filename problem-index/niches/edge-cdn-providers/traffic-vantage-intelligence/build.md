# A View of the Internet, Sold as Bandwidth

**Niche:** [[niches/edge-cdn-providers/traffic-vantage-intelligence/profile|Traffic Vantage Intelligence]]
**Industry:** [[industries/edge-cdn-providers|Edge & CDN Providers]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** These providers see a substantial fraction of internet traffic in real time and monetise that position as delivery capacity and security rules.
**Tags:** #k-means-clustering #change-point-detection #dimensionality-reduction #descriptive-statistics #hypothesis-testing #confidence-intervals #compliance #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor that gets here is fighting to turn a view of a substantial fraction of internet traffic into decisions rather than capacity — and whoever does that holds a vantage point nobody outside the category can obtain.

## The Problem
A customer asks whether their cache hit rate is good for a property of their type. The provider can see the hit rates of thousands of comparable properties and answers that it depends on the workload. A customer's users in one region experience degraded performance because of a transit problem; the provider's network saw it eleven minutes before the customer's first complaint and told nobody. A customer's configuration includes a setting that is unusual for their traffic type and is costing them; the provider can see that almost nobody with this traffic profile configures it that way and does not mention it. Each of these is answerable from the vantage point and none is answered.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The category's revenue is bandwidth and its differentiators have been network footprint and security rule sets, so the product organisation has never been oriented to producing decisions. Cross-customer analysis is sensitive and no provider has articulated a governance position, so the topic is avoided rather than designed — the same pattern this vault finds in every corpus opportunity. The published traffic reports are marketing artefacts produced annually by a research team rather than operational products. And telling a customer their configuration is unusual is adjacent to telling them their engineers are wrong, which is commercially uncomfortable.

## What to Build
Turn the vantage point into operational products, governed properly. Benchmark customers against comparable properties on the metrics they ask about — cache hit rate, origin offload, response size, performance by region — with the cohort defined by traffic characteristics rather than by industry, reported as a position in a distribution rather than as a verdict. Detect configuration outliers, since a property whose settings are unusual for its traffic profile is frequently misconfigured and this is detectable structurally without examining any content. Alert on network-level degradations the provider observes before the customer does, which is the clearest unused asset here and is described in the fix note below. Publish operational rather than annual intelligence: what is happening now on the networks and in the regions a customer's users are on. Use the aggregate view to improve the caching and bot capabilities in this industry's other niches, since the same corpus underlies all of them. And build the governance in from the start — aggregate only, structural characteristics rather than content, minimum cohort sizes, customer opt-in and a public statement of what is analysed — because in this category the credibility of the governance is the licence to have the product at all.

## Target Customer
The providers themselves, primarily; and their customers, who would buy benchmarking and early warning directly.

## Impact If Built
The vantage point is genuinely unobtainable outside the category and is monetised as capacity, which leaves the most differentiated asset in the industry idle. Benchmarking and configuration outlier detection both work on structural data alone, which makes the governance story straightforward rather than fraught.

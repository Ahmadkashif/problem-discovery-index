# The Franchise Fee Disputed at Every Audit

**Niche:** [[niches/waste-haulers/franchise-tonnage-reporting/profile|Franchise Tonnage, Diversion & Compliance Reporting]]
**Industry:** [[industries/waste-haulers|Waste Haulers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Franchise fees are a percentage of gross receipts, and every audit reopens which receipts count — fuel fees, roll-off rental days, bad debt, credits.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #compliance #quick-win #data-integration #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to produce franchise and state reports straight from scale tickets and stop data, and whoever's reported tonnages reconcile to disposal-site scale records with the smallest variance takes the account.

## The Problem
Cities audit franchise fee remittances, often through consultants, and findings turn on definitions applied inconsistently across months and analysts.

## Why It's Still Broken
The fee is calculated in a spreadsheet from a billing export, and the contract's definition of gross receipts is applied by whoever runs it that quarter.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Each contract's gross-receipts definition encoded once, billing line items tagged to it, and the remittance calculated and documented automatically with an audit trail by account and month.

## Who Feels the Pain
Hauler finance and compliance staff; city auditors; the division manager facing a back-payment demand before renewal.

## Impact If Fixed
At a 10% franchise fee, a one-point disagreement over what counts on $20M of receipts is a $20K-a-year finding (estimate) that recurs until the definition is applied consistently.

# Insurance Renewal Pricing Optimisation for Container Accounts

**Industry:** [[industries/waste-haulers|Waste Haulers]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Personal-lines insurers built renewal price optimisation to balance rate against retention a policy at a time; a commercial container book renews every year in the same way.
**Tags:** #survival-analysis #gradient-boosting #causal-inference #revenue-impact #compliance
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to push the annual commercial price increase through without the customer leaving for a rival hauler or a broker, and whoever loses the fewest accounts per point of increase takes the account.

## The Problem
Haulers face the insurer's renewal problem: a book of annual contracts, a cost trend to pass on, and customers who differ in how much increase they will accept.

## What Already Exists
Insurance renewal optimisation tools estimate each policyholder's lapse probability as a function of rate change and choose increases that meet a revenue target with the fewest lapses, within regulatory constraints.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs: container size, frequency and service quality in place of policy features; local competitor and broker presence instead of market rate indices; franchise and ordinance limits where commercial rates are regulated; fee lines treated separately because they carry legal risk; and a retention-offer layer (downsize, frequency change) that has no insurance equivalent. Many commercial contracts also carry auto-renewal and notice clauses that set when an increase can take effect.

## Target Customer
Revenue management teams at consolidators and large regionals.

## Impact If Solved
Insurers report retention gains of a few points from renewal optimisation (estimate); two points of retention on a 20,000-account book keeps ~400 accounts a year.

# Fee Lines Nobody Can Justify

**Niche:** [[niches/waste-haulers/commercial-price-retention/profile|Commercial Price Increases & Account Retention]]
**Industry:** [[industries/waste-haulers|Waste Haulers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Fuel and environmental recovery fees were added to commercial invoices as cost pass-throughs and are now set without a documented link to the costs they name.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #linear-regression #compliance #revenue-impact #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to push the annual commercial price increase through without the customer leaving for a rival hauler or a broker, and whoever loses the fewest accounts per point of increase takes the account.

## The Problem
Recovery fees are a large, quiet part of commercial price. When customers or plaintiffs ask what a fee recovers, the answer is often a policy rather than a calculation; a 2025 suit against Republic alleges recovery fees rose when costs fell (search summary), and Waste Connections settled a $1.4M claim over rate increases and fuel surcharges (search summary).

## Why It's Still Broken
Fees were set by finance as percentages and adjusted to hit revenue targets; nobody owns the calculation, and contracts describe fees loosely.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A documented fee model: each fee tied to an index (diesel price, disposal and compliance costs), recalculated on a schedule, with the calculation and contract clause attached to every invoice and visible to customer service.

## Who Feels the Pain
Customer service reps fielding fee calls; legal teams; small-business customers who see fees as hidden price.

## Impact If Fixed
A defensible fee model removes a class-action exposure measured in millions and gives reps an answer in the first minute of the call (estimate: ~1–2 minutes off a fee-related call).

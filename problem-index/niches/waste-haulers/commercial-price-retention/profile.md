# Commercial Price Increases & Account Retention

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/waste-haulers|Waste Haulers]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to push the annual commercial price increase through without the customer leaving for a rival hauler or a broker, and whoever loses the fewest accounts per point of increase takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$150M in pricing analytics, CRM, retention and customer-service tooling spend on commercial books (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~0.2% of industry receipts as tooling spend; the revenue it governs is the ~$20B commercial line (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — consolidators run pricing tools; independents raise everyone by the same percentage
**Target Buyer:** VP of pricing or revenue management at a regional hauler
**Automation Potential:** High for setting and targeting increases, medium for retention offers

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Price is the industry's growth engine: Republic's average yield rose 4.1% in 2025 against a 0.6% volume decline. The competitors are other haulers, which undercut at renewal; brokers such as Quest Resource ($250.2M revenue in 2025) and RoadRunner, which sell multi-site customers a single managed contract; and, inside the hauler, the pricing tool against the sales rep's instinct. The winner is measured by accounts lost per point of increase. The input is account-level response to price, which only the hauler holds, and the clock is the hauler's own annual increase cycle, so the contest differs from billing what was serviced.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Republic's Capture tool and Priority Based Selling; general price-optimisation software from B2B pricing vendors; CRM retention workflows; brokers' benchmark rate data. Gaps: increases are set by cohort, not by modelled response; retention offers are improvised by reps; cancellation reasons are coded poorly; and fee lines are now legal exposure in their own right.

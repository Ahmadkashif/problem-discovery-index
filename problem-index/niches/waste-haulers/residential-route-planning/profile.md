# Residential Route Planning & Rebalancing

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/waste-haulers|Waste Haulers]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to build residential routes that drivers finish as planned, and whoever leaves the smallest gap between planned and actual route hours takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$200M in route optimisation software, rebalancing projects and routing staff time at private haulers (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~0.3% (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — routing modules are widely licensed and lightly used; many routes are years past their last rebalance
**Target Buyer:** Director of operations or route manager at a regional hauler
**Automation Potential:** High

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Residential routing is a repeated, well-posed optimisation: assign thousands of homes to trucks and days, balance route hours, sequence stops, respect disposal-site hours and truck capacity, and minimise left turns, reverses and dead-heading. It recurs whenever a subdivision opens, a franchise starts, a truck type changes from rear-load to automated side-load, or a city moves to separate organics collection. Route optimisation vendors (Routeware with RubiconSmartCity, AMCS, RouteSmart, Hauler Hero's Hero Route) and in-house teams at consolidators compete. A plan that looks balanced on screen but leaves one driver with ten-hour days and another finishing at noon fails in the yard, so the planned-versus-actual gap is the measure. The same gap is noted, from the delivery side, in [[niches/last-mile-delivery/route-optimization-vendors/profile|🔍 Route Optimization Vendors]].

## Current Tools & Gaps
Optimisers with street networks and capacity models; telematics with actual route times. Gaps: service time per stop is modelled from averages rather than observed; the regular drivers' own sequences are ignored; and plans are rarely re-checked after go-live.

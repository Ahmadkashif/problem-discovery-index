# The Container Size Nobody Reviews

**Niche:** [[niches/waste-haulers/commercial-container-service/profile|Commercial Front-Load Container Service]]
**Industry:** [[industries/waste-haulers|Waste Haulers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A container that overflows every week is billed an overage every week until the customer complains or leaves, because no one is assigned to notice that the service level is wrong.

Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## The Problem
Recurring overflow is a service-level signal: the restaurant needs a bigger container or another pickup. Instead it becomes a stream of overage charges and complaints, and at contract time the customer remembers the fees, not the service.

## Why It's Still Broken
Overage charges are revenue the billing team is measured on; upsizing is a sales task with its own quota and a rep who rarely sees camera data. The Nevada settlement forbids driver incentives tied to overages precisely because the incentives point the wrong way.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A weekly list of containers that overflowed on, say, three of the last four pickups, with photos, the cost of the current overages and the price of the right-sized service, routed to the account's rep to offer before the next charge.

## Who Feels the Pain
Small-business customers paying fees they see as penalties; reps defending them; customer service reps taking the calls.

## Impact If Fixed
Converting chronic overflow into an upsized service keeps the revenue as recurring rate rather than disputed fees; if a tenth of a book's ~5% chronic-overflow accounts are saved from cancelling each year, a 10,000-container hauler keeps ~50 accounts (estimate).

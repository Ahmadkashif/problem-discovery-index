# The Rate Adjustment Formula Nobody Models

**Niche:** [[niches/waste-haulers/residential-franchise-collection/profile|Residential Franchise & Municipal Contract Collection]]
**Industry:** [[industries/waste-haulers|Waste Haulers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A franchise's annual rate adjustment is tied to an index formula that may track the hauler's real costs poorly, and the gap is discovered year by year instead of priced at bid time.
**Tags:** #monte-carlo-methods #linear-regression #revenue-impact #quick-win #compliance
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to price a multi-year franchise bid against routes it has never run, and whoever's bid lands closest to the realised cost per home over the contract term takes the account.

## The Problem
Cities set adjustment formulas, often a share of CPI, sometimes with fuel and disposal components and a cap. When wages and truck prices rise faster than the index, the contract's margin shrinks for years; haulers then seek extraordinary rate relief, which strains the relationship before renewal.

## Why It's Still Broken
Bid teams price year one carefully and treat the formula as boilerplate; the formula's fit to the hauler's cost mix is never simulated.

## What a Fix Looks Like
For every RFP, a simulation of the adjustment formula against the hauler's cost components under historical and stress paths, showing the expected margin drift over the term, priced into the bid or raised as a proposal exception.

## Who Feels the Pain
Division managers running contracts whose margin erodes; city staff facing mid-term rate-relief requests.

## Impact If Fixed
A formula that lags real costs by one point a year erodes ~6% of revenue by year seven of a franchise (estimate); pricing it at bid avoids a rate-relief fight worth that margin.

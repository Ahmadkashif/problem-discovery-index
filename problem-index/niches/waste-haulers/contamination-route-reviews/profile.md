# Contamination Route Reviews & Organics Compliance

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/waste-haulers|Waste Haulers]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to find and notice contaminated carts on every route the regulation requires reviewed, and whoever brings the measured contamination rate on reviewed routes down furthest takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$250M in route-review labour, camera systems, auditing apps and generator education tied to contamination programmes (estimate; concentrated in California and in cities with organics mandates)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~0.4% (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — most reviews are auditors lifting lids on foot before the truck arrives, recorded on paper tags or a phone
**Target Buyer:** Diversion or sustainability manager at a hauler with California franchises; recycling coordinator at the jurisdiction
**Automation Potential:** Medium — truck cameras cover every lift but see the contents only as they fall

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
California's SB 1383 requires every hauler route to be reviewed for prohibited contaminants every year, notices sent to the generators, evidence kept under 14 CCR 18984.6 and counts reported in the jurisdiction's Electronic Annual Report. The obligation is the city's but is usually delegated to the franchised hauler, and the goal behind it is a 75% cut in organic waste to landfill by 2025. Contamination also costs the hauler directly, through rejected organics loads and higher processing fees. Manual auditors (Recology's diversion auditors, consultants using apps such as Zabble) compete with truck cameras (WM Smart Truck, AMCS Vision AI, Prairie Robotics) and in-container cameras (Compology at South Bayside). The Recycling Partnership says a manual audit can cost up to twice a camera system; East Lansing's camera programme cut contamination ~25%. The measure that wins the jurisdiction is contamination actually falling.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Paper "oops" tags and postcards; auditing apps; camera systems; CalRecycle's implementation record tool. Gaps: cameras see contamination as the cart is dumped, too late to reject it; manual audits see only a sample; notices rarely link to whether the same address improves.

# Address-Level Contamination History That Targets the Next Review

**Niche:** [[niches/waste-haulers/contamination-route-reviews/profile|Contamination Route Reviews & Organics Compliance]]
**Industry:** [[industries/waste-haulers|Waste Haulers]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Route reviews sample carts at random every year while camera systems record every lift, and nobody combines the two into a history that sends auditors to the addresses that keep contaminating.
**Tags:** #cnns #object-detection #logistic-regression #compliance #worker-facing #workflow-orchestration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to find and notice contaminated carts on every route the regulation requires reviewed, and whoever brings the measured contamination rate on reviewed routes down furthest takes the account.

## The Problem
The regulation sets no minimum number of containers per route, so jurisdictions sample. Auditors walk a route before the truck, lift lids and tag carts; cameras on other trucks flag contamination at the hopper. The two records sit apart, and next year's review samples at random again.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Camera vendors sell detection, auditing apps sell checklists, and the compliance obligation sits with the city while the data sits with the hauler. Nobody owns the address-level outcome.

## What to Build
A contamination record per address joining camera detections, auditor findings and notices sent. A model scores each address's likelihood of contamination so auditors review the right carts before collection, when a contaminated cart can still be left unemptied with a tag. The same record produces the 18984.6 documentation and EAR counts.

## Target Customer
Diversion managers at haulers with SB 1383 delegation; jurisdiction recycling coordinators.

## Impact If Built
Targeting reviews at the ~10–20% of addresses that cause most contamination (estimate) can match East Lansing's ~25% reduction with fewer auditor hours per route.

# Roll-Off Dispatch & Container Tracking

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/waste-haulers|Waste Haulers]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep every roll-off box earning, and whoever runs the most billable hauls per container per month takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$13B in US roll-off (large-container) revenue (estimate; WM industrial $3.11B and Republic large-container $3.10B in 2025); dispatch and container-tracking software spend ~$150M (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~19% (estimate; 18.7% of Republic's revenue)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — orders by phone and text, boxes tracked on a whiteboard at most small and mid-sized operators
**Target Buyer:** Roll-off operations manager or owner
**Automation Potential:** High for order intake, box tracking and sequencing

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Roll-off is the construction-cycle half of the industry: 10–40 yard boxes dropped at job sites, swapped when full and hauled to a landfill or transfer station, priced per haul plus tonnage and rental days. Republic runs ~4,900 large-container trucks. Many of the 1,407 "other waste collection" firms and thousands of small solid waste haulers are mostly roll-off. The business is asset-turn: a box idle at a finished job earns nothing, and a truck waiting in a landfill line earns nothing. Competitors are roll-off software packages (Cairn, now Solen's; CurbWaste; Soft-Pak; Routeware; Hauler Hero), broker marketplaces that subcontract hauls, and the dispatcher's whiteboard. Whoever turns each box most often wins.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Packages track orders and boxes when someone enters them; GPS trackers on boxes are cheap but rarely joined to billing. Gaps: orders arrive by phone and change by text; dwell time is not monitored; landfill waits are unknown at dispatch; and rental-day billing relies on memory.

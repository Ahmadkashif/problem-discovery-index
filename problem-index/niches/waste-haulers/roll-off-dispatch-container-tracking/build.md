# A Live Map of Every Box and What It Is Earning

**Niche:** [[niches/waste-haulers/roll-off-dispatch-container-tracking/profile|Roll-Off Dispatch & Container Tracking]]
**Industry:** [[industries/waste-haulers|Waste Haulers]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A roll-off operator owns hundreds of boxes spread across job sites and cannot say, on any morning, which are full, which are idle and which have been forgotten.
**Tags:** #time-series-forecasting #dynamic-programming #large-language-models #automation #revenue-impact #workflow-orchestration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep every roll-off box earning, and whoever runs the most billable hauls per container per month takes the account.

## The Problem
Boxes are the operator's inventory and its capital: a 30-yard box costs several thousand dollars (estimate). Contractors call when they want a swap, forget to call when a job ends, and move boxes around sites. The dispatcher tracks it all from memory, so boxes sit idle for weeks, the yard runs short and new orders wait.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Small operators lack sensors and data discipline; software vendors built billing and dispatch screens that depend on manual entry; GPS box trackers were sold for theft recovery, not utilisation.

## What to Build
A box-level ledger fed by cheap GPS trackers or driver scans: location, days on site, last haul, customer and job status. Forecast swaps and pulls from job type and dwell history, flag idle boxes for a pull call, and sequence trucks against landfill hours and typical waits from scale tickets. Order intake by text or voice agent writes straight into the ledger.

## Target Customer
Owners and operations managers of roll-off operations with 100–2,000 boxes.

## Impact If Built
Raising hauls per box from ~3 to ~3.5 a month (estimate) on a 500-box fleet adds ~3,000 billable hauls a year, or the same revenue with ~70 fewer boxes to buy.

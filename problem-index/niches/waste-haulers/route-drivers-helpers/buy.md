# Mining Proximity Detection for the Curb and the Riding Step

**Niche:** [[niches/waste-haulers/route-drivers-helpers/profile|Route Drivers & Helpers]]
**Industry:** [[industries/waste-haulers|Waste Haulers]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Underground mining and large construction sites use proximity detection that stops or warns a machine when a person enters its danger zone; a rear-loader working with a helper has the same hazard every few seconds.
**Tags:** #object-detection #cnns #worker-facing #compliance #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep collection crews out of the injury and collision statistics, and whoever brings the crews' OSHA recordable incident rate lowest takes the account.

## The Problem
Helpers step on and off the riding step hundreds of times a day, and pedestrians and cyclists pass close to a reversing truck; backing alarms are ignored because they always sound.

## What Already Exists
Proximity detection systems on mining machines and earthmovers use tags worn by workers, radar and camera-based person detection to warn operators or interlock motion inside defined zones.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs: zones around the hopper, arm and riding step rather than a whole machine; a distinction between the crew's own helper (tagged, expected near the truck) and the public; interlocks that respect collection workflows (arm cycles, packing); urban clutter of parked cars, carts and bins; and alerts that do not fire at every stop or drivers will tune them out.

## Target Customer
Safety leaders at haulers still running rear-load crews, and truck-body makers adding safety options.

## Impact If Solved
Struck-by and backing incidents are a large share of the 35 collection-industry deaths in 2023; a detection system that prevents even one fatality avoids a loss commonly valued in the millions of dollars (estimate).

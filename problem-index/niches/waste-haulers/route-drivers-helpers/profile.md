# Route Drivers & Helpers

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/waste-haulers|Waste Haulers]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep collection crews out of the injury and collision statistics, and whoever brings the crews' OSHA recordable incident rate lowest takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$400M in fleet video and safety technology, driver training and loss-control services bought for collection fleets (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~0.6% (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — video telematics on most large fleets; training is classroom and ride-along
**Target Buyer:** VP of safety or EHS director at a regional hauler
**Automation Potential:** Medium — detection and coaching automate; the work stays manual and dangerous

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Refuse and recyclable material collectors died at 41.4 per 100,000 full-time workers in 2023, fourth among all occupations; the solid waste collection category recorded 35 deaths that year, 24 of them transportation incidents. The people at risk are the industry's least-served users. In-cab tablets serve dispatch, cameras serve evidence and billing, and the driver gets a checklist. Republic cites an OSHA recordable rate 23% better than the industry average over ten years and runs a programme aimed at "the six most common types of serious incidents". Competitors are video-telematics vendors (Lytx, Samsara, Safe Fleet, Terex's 3rd Eye), truck makers adding blind-spot and pedestrian detection (Heil, McNeilus, Mack, Peterbilt), trainers, and insurers' loss-control teams. Haulers buy on whether recordable incidents fall.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Event recorders that flag harsh braking and distraction; 360-degree camera systems; backing alarms; ride-along evaluations. Gaps: the risks specific to collection — struck-by at the curb, helpers on the riding step, backing in alleys, arm contact with wires, lifting strains, hopper fires from batteries — are not what generic fleet-safety models were trained on, and coaching rarely reaches helpers at all.

# The Route P&L a Small Hauler Has Never Seen

**Niche:** [[niches/waste-haulers/small-independent-haulers/profile|Small Independent Haulers]]
**Industry:** [[industries/waste-haulers|Waste Haulers]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A small hauler knows its bank balance and its truck payments but not which routes, customers or towns make money, so it prices by habit and sells to a consolidator without knowing what its book is worth.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #linear-regression #data-integration #revenue-impact #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to become the one system a 2–20 truck hauler bills, routes and collects payments on, and whoever gets the owner's days sales outstanding lowest takes the account.

## The Problem
Revenue arrives by customer; costs arrive by truck, fuel card and landfill ticket. The owner cannot join them, so a distant subdivision served at the same rate as the dense town looks fine until the truck needs replacing.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Billing packages for small haulers stop at invoicing; accounting software has no route concept; and the owner has no analyst.

## What to Build
A simple route ledger joining each customer's billed revenue and payment history to route minutes (from phone GPS if the truck has no telematics), disposal tons from scale tickets and fuel by truck. It shows revenue per route-hour, slow payers, underpriced stops and the cash cycle, and produces the customer and route schedules an acquirer's diligence will ask for.

## Target Customer
Owners of 2–20 truck haulers, sold bundled with billing and payments so the data arrives without extra entry.

## Impact If Built
Repricing the bottom tenth of stops by revenue per minute could lift a small hauler's margin by ~2–4 points (estimate), and a documented route P&L supports a higher multiple at sale.

# Small Independent Haulers

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/waste-haulers|Waste Haulers]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to become the one system a 2–20 truck hauler bills, routes and collects payments on, and whoever gets the owner's days sales outstanding lowest takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$6.9B in receipts at the 5,796 solid waste collection firms with fewer than 20 employees (Census SUSB 2022), plus most of the 1,300 small "other waste collection" firms (~$1.0B); software spend ~$100M (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~10% of receipts, ~85% of firms
**Digital Adoption:** Low — QuickBooks, paper route sheets, a phone and a hauler package used for invoicing only
**Target Buyer:** Owner-operator, often with a spouse or one office manager running billing
**Automation Potential:** High for billing, payments and routing; the owner still drives

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A small hauler runs a few rear-loaders or front-loaders and some roll-off trucks, serves subscription households in unfranchised areas and local businesses, and competes with consolidators that may make it an acquisition offer at any time. The binding constraint is cash: service runs before it is billed, customers pay late, and an owner who is also a driver chases payments in the evening. The vendors fighting for this owner are Hauler Hero ($16M Series A in February 2026), Cairn (bought by Solen in December 2025, >350 customers, search summary), CurbWaste, Terex's Soft-Pak and Routeware. The consolidators' integration teams are the other buyer of these firms, through acquisition. The contest is which system becomes the operating record, and it is decided by cash collected.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Billing-first hauler packages, general field-service tools, card and ACH processors, and spreadsheets. Gaps: autopay and suspend-for-nonpayment rules are manual; routing is the owner's memory; and nothing shows the owner what each route earns, which is also what an acquirer will ask.

# Overage & Extra-Service Charge Capture

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/waste-haulers|Waste Haulers]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn what the truck camera saw into overage and extra-service charges that stick, and whoever has the lowest share of those charges credited back after dispute takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$200M in camera, review-service and billing-integration spend on commercial routes (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~0.3% as tooling spend; overage and extra-service revenue at stake is ~$1–2B (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — cameras are spreading fast; the decision to bill is still a clerk's
**Target Buyer:** Director of billing or commercial revenue recovery
**Automation Potential:** High

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Safe Fleet's WasteVision AI says overflow appears on 8–12% of commercial bins while haulers charge for ~1% of lifts (search summary, vendor claim). Charging more is easy; charging in a way that survives the customer's call, a franchise audit and a class action is the contest. WM's 2025 Nevada settlement sets the bar: one warning a year, photo notice within 24 hours, seven days before a fee, no fee after the hauler's own miss, and no driver incentives. Camera vendors (AMCS Vision AI, Safe Fleet, Prairie Robotics, Terex's 3rd Eye), billing platforms (Hauler Hero's camera feature, Routeware, Soft-Pak) and in-house systems at WM compete; the measure is the share of charges credited back.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Camera detection is good; the gaps are the rules (each franchise and ordinance defines overage differently), the matching of an image to the right container at shared enclosures, notice workflows, and dispute records a rep can show in one screen.

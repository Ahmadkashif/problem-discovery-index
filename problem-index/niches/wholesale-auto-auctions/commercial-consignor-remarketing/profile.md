# Commercial Consignor Remarketing

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/wholesale-auto-auctions|Wholesale Auto Auctions]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to sell a commercial consignor's off-lease, rental, fleet and repossessed units for the most money after fees and transport, and whoever returns the highest net proceeds as a share of MMR takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$2.2B a year in seller fees, remarketing services, inspection, transport and reconditioning sold to commercial consignors (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~30% of auction, service and floorplan revenue (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** High for listing and bidding (private-label upstream sites, simulcast), Medium for the channel and floor decisions, which still rest on account managers' judgement
**Target Buyer:** Remarketing director at a captive finance company, lessor, rental company, fleet or lender; on the auction side, the VP of commercial accounts
**Automation Potential:** High for channel, timing and floor recommendations; low for the account relationship

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Commercial sellers supplied 51.4% of in-lane sales at NAAA member auctions in 2025. Cox Automotive expects off-lease volume to rise 23% and rental 5% in 2026, while dealer-sourced units fall 3%. A commercial unit passes through a sequence the dealer never sees. The lessee's dealer gets first option ("grounding"). Then comes an upstream private-label sale to the captive's dealers, then an open digital auction, then a physical lane with simulcast. OPENLANE alone powers more than 40 private-label sites for most OEMs and captives and sold 762,000 commercial units in 2025. The auction is paid by the consignor, and the consignor measures the auction on one number: what each unit netted against the Manheim Market Report. The competitors are Manheim, OPENLANE, ACV (Copart since October 2026, which adds Copart's channel for damaged units, inferred from the merger release), Carvana's ADESA and the consignors' own dealer-direct programmes. The lender's own recovery decisions sit at [[niches/sales-finance-companies/repossession-and-remarketing/profile|Repossession, Remarketing & Deficiency]], the fleet lessor's residual work at [[niches/fleet-managers/fleet-lease-residual-analytics/profile|Fleet Lease Residual & Remarketing Analytics]], and the rental company's defleet planning in the queued Car Rental Companies build.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Private-label remarketing SaaS (OPENLANE's platform), the auctions' commercial account teams and dashboards, MMR and the Manheim index, ACV's remarketing centres, and consignor spreadsheets. Gaps: no unit-level counterfactual of what a car would have netted in the channel it skipped; floors copied from MMR without condition or timing; and EV units, ~16% of off-lease supply in the second half of 2026, priced with little history.

# The "No Arb for Oil Leaks" Template That Stopped Working on 1 June

**Niche:** [[niches/wholesale-auto-auctions/announcement-disclosure-checks/profile|Announcement & Disclosure Checks]]
**Industry:** [[industries/wholesale-auto-auctions|Wholesale Auto Auctions]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Sellers' saved announcement templates still say "No Arb for oil leaks" or "check engine light on", and since June neither protects them.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #automation #quick-win #compliance #workflow-orchestration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to make each seller announcement and condition report complete enough that buyers cannot win an arbitration on it, and whoever has the fewest arbitrations granted on announceable items per thousand units sold takes the account.

## The Problem
Dealers and commercial consignors reuse announcement templates for years. Under the guidelines effective 1 June 2026, generic "No Arb for [defect]" lines are prohibited and can themselves be grounds for arbitration, and announcing only a warning light no longer covers the condition behind it. Manheim began moving this way on 1 April. Templates saved before then are still in use.

## Why It's Still Broken
Templates live in seller accounts and upload files, not in one place the auction reviews. Sellers learn a template is broken when a claim succeeds.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A one-time sweep of every saved template and recent upload for wording the 2026 rules prohibit. The seller gets a replacement for each, specific disclosure text or a red-light, as-is recommendation, and accepts it with one click. New announcements get a blocking check at entry.

## Who Feels the Pain
Sellers losing claims they thought they had excluded; account managers explaining the rule change one seller at a time; arbitration admins handling avoidable cases.

## Impact If Fixed
A sweep of a few thousand seller templates takes days of work, once. If generic announcements sat on 1–2% of units before June (estimate), that removes a claim source affecting 250–500 cars a year at a 25,000-unit auction.

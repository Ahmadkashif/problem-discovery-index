# Marketing Compliance Review Adapted to Seller Announcements

**Niche:** [[niches/wholesale-auto-auctions/announcement-disclosure-checks/profile|Announcement & Disclosure Checks]]
**Industry:** [[industries/wholesale-auto-auctions|Wholesale Auto Auctions]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Banks and drug makers run every marketing claim through software that flags prohibited wording; auctions publish seller announcements nobody checks.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #bert #transformers #compliance #automation #workflow-orchestration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to make each seller announcement and condition report complete enough that buyers cannot win an arbitration on it, and whoever has the fewest arbitrations granted on announceable items per thousand units sold takes the account.

## The Problem
A seller announcement is a disclosure with legal effect under the auction's arbitration policy, and since June 2026 some wordings are prohibited outright. It is still written free-hand or from old templates.

## What Already Exists
Marketing and communications compliance review tools used in financial services and life sciences scan copy against a rulebook, flag prohibited or unsubstantiated claims, suggest compliant wording and keep an approval trail.

## The Customization Gap
The auction version needs:
- the NAAA guideline text and each auction's local policy as the rulebook, versioned by effective date (1 June 2026);
- defect vocabulary mapped to Appendix I categories, including ADAS;
- checks that depend on vehicle data, such as an announced warning light with no stated cause or an ADAS-equipped trim with no ADAS note;
- price-dependent thresholds;
- throughput of thousands of announcements a day with sub-second review, and seller-facing suggestions rather than a compliance officer's queue.

## Target Customer
Auction assurance teams and consignment desks; commercial consignors' remarketing operations.

## Impact If Solved
Rewriting legacy templates once, then checking every new announcement, removes the generic-announcement claims the 2026 rules created. At 0.2% of units sold (estimate), that is ~50 claims a year at a 25,000-unit auction.

# The Rerun Nobody Explains

**Niche:** [[niches/wholesale-auto-auctions/dealer-consignment-conversion/profile|Dealer Consignment & Sale-Day Conversion]]
**Industry:** [[industries/wholesale-auto-auctions|Wholesale Auto Auctions]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A dealer's car no-sells, reruns next week with the same floor and no-sells again, and the dealer is told only "no sale, high bid $X".
**Tags:** #large-language-models #descriptive-statistics #automation #quick-win #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to sell a dealer's wholesale unit on its first run, and whoever posts the highest first-run conversion rate on dealer consignments takes the account.

## The Problem
After a no-sale, the seller sees a high bid and a prompt to rerun. Whether the car no-sold because the floor sat $1,200 over MMR, because it ran in the last ten minutes of a thin lane, because the photos hid nothing but showed nothing, or because two likely buyers were at another sale is not reported. The car reruns unchanged, and the floorplan clock keeps running.

## Why It's Still Broken
The data to explain a no-sale is spread across the lane system, the bidding log, the floor and the condition report. Dealer-sales staff who could read it are managing dozens of accounts, and no screen puts the pieces together.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A one-paragraph no-sale note, generated after each run from the bid log, floor against MMR, views and watchers, slot and comparable sales: "Floor was 108% of MMR; three comparable units sold at 96–99%; 14 bidders viewed, 2 bid." It closes with one recommended change for the rerun, and the seller can accept it with one tap.

## Who Feels the Pain
Used-car managers paying floorplan interest on cars that will not sell; auction dealer-sales staff fielding "why didn't it sell?" calls; and the auction, whose lane slots fill with repeat no-sales.

## Impact If Fixed
If a third of reruns change something because the note told them what to change, and half of those then sell, second-run conversion on those units rises by ~15 points (estimate). That saves dealers a week of floorplan interest, ~$30–50 a car on a $13,800 unit.

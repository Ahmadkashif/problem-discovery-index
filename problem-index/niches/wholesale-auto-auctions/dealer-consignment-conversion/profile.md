# Dealer Consignment & Sale-Day Conversion

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/wholesale-auto-auctions|Wholesale Auto Auctions]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to sell a dealer's wholesale unit on its first run, and whoever posts the highest first-run conversion rate on dealer consignments takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.8B a year in seller and buyer fees on dealer-consigned units across physical lanes, simulcast and digital auctions (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~25% of auction, service and floorplan revenue (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** High — 20-minute digital auctions, simulcast bidding and app-based run lists are standard, and half of in-lane sales go to online bidders
**Target Buyer:** Used-car or wholesale manager at a franchised or independent dealer (the seller); the auction's general manager and dealer-sales team
**Automation Potential:** High for lane placement, run order, bidder notification and floor advice

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Dealers wholesale the trade-ins they will not retail and the aged units that did not sell. OPENLANE sold 710,000 dealer-consigned vehicles in 2025, ~48% of its volume. ACV sold 829,276 marketplace units, mostly dealer-to-dealer. Dealer supply is shrinking: Cox Automotive expects dealer-sourced wholesale units to fall 3% in 2026, so the auctions are competing harder for each one. The dealer's measure is simple. A car that sells on its first run frees the floorplan line and the lot space. A car that no-sells comes back, reruns or is retailed at a loss. NAAA members converted 58.5% of in-lane offerings in 2025, and the Manheim index reported 55.2% conversion in September 2026. The competitors are ACV (Copart), OPENLANE, Manheim's lanes and digital channels, Carvana's ADESA, independent auctions, and instant-offer engines such as CarGurus' CarOffer. The dealer's buying side, including auction-speed arbitrage, is profiled at [[niches/auto-dealers-independent/wholesale-auction-flippers/profile|Wholesale & Auction Flippers]].

## Current Tools & Gaps
Run lists published in apps (ACV's run list opens up to 24 hours before a sale), simulcast platforms, programmatic buying (ACV's Smart Acquisition Manager), buyer tools such as Carbly and autoniq, MMR and floor advice from the auction's dealer-sales staff. Gaps: no forecast of a unit's first-run sale probability given its floor, lane and slot; bidder notification by broad saved search rather than likely interest; and no explanation to the seller of why a car no-sold.

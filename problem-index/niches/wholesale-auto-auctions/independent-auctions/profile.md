# Independent Auctions

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/wholesale-auto-auctions|Wholesale Auto Auctions]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep an independent auction's dealer consignors from moving to the national chains, and whoever keeps the largest share of its dealer consignors' wholesale units year on year takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$300M a year in auction-management software, simulcast, listing networks, credentialing, marketing and outsourced services bought by ~200–250 independent auctions (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~4% of auction, service and floorplan revenue as tooling spend; independents sell an estimated 15–25% of units (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — most run simulcast and list on a shared network, but run lists, photos and condition reports are thinner than the national chains'
**Target Buyer:** Owner or general manager of a single-site or small multi-site auction
**Automation Potential:** Medium-High for listing, condition capture and bidder outreach; the auctioneer and the local dealer relationships stay human

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The average NAAA member auction sold 25,000 vehicles in 2025, and a quarter sold more than 30,000. Behind the national chains sit a few hundred family-owned and regional auctions that sell to local independent dealers on weekly sale days. They compete with Manheim, Carvana's ADESA, OPENLANE and ACV, whose digital reach draws away the same dealers' consignments. ACV's own 10-K lists "smaller chains of auctions and independent auctions" among its competitors. The tools built for the nationals do not fit: independents cannot fund their own data science, inspector networks or floorplan arms. They depend on shared infrastructure. Auction Edge's management system and EDGE Pipeline list ~206 independent auctions (a third-party count; search summary). Its Pipeline Market Report, built from sale data at 175+ of them, is licensed into OPENLANE's autoniq (search summary). AuctionACCESS credentials are accepted at 400+ marketplaces (search summary). The non-vehicle auction business is mapped under [[industries/auctioneers|Auctioneers]], whose software vendors are [[niches/auctioneers/auction-clerking-software-vendors/profile|Auction Management & Clerking Software Vendors]].

## Current Tools & Gaps
Auction Edge (management system, EDGE Pipeline, simulcast), AuctionACCESS, buyer apps such as Carbly, local transport brokers and NAAA arbitration policy. Gaps: thin condition reports that keep remote bidders away; no shared view of dealer demand across independents; run lists published late; and no way to show consignors they would net as much locally as at a national chain. More bidders, fuller listings and earlier run lists are the means; the outcome the owner is judged on is whether the same dealers are still consigning next year.

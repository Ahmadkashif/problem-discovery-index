# A Shared Buyer-Demand Exchange for Independents

**Niche:** [[niches/wholesale-auto-auctions/independent-auctions/profile|Independent Auctions]]
**Industry:** [[industries/wholesale-auto-auctions|Wholesale Auto Auctions]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Each independent auction knows its own fifty regular buyers; none can reach the dealer three counties away who bought that exact truck at a different independent last month.
**Tags:** #k-nearest-neighbors #gradient-boosting #data-integration #revenue-impact #ai-platform
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep an independent auction's dealer consignors from moving to the national chains, and whoever keeps the largest share of its dealer consignors' wholesale units year on year takes the account.

## The Problem
A national chain can push a unit to thousands of buyers whose purchase history it holds. An independent's reach is its regulars plus whoever browses the shared listing network. A car that would draw five bids nationally draws two locally, and the consignor notices.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Independents compete with each other for the same dealers and are wary of sharing buyer lists. The shared infrastructure that exists, listings and credentials, was built for access, not for matching demand.

## What to Build
A demand exchange run by a neutral party. Each participating auction contributes anonymised buyer purchase profiles: segments, price bands, regions and frequency. For each upcoming unit, the exchange returns a count and ranking of likely buyers across all members. The listing auction gets the right to notify them, but never sees their identities until they bid or register. Auctions pay per notified buyer who bids, so the exchange is paid only when it adds bidders.

## Target Customer
Owners and GMs of independent auctions, through Auction Edge-style management systems or an independent alliance.

## Impact If Built
Adding one active bidder per unit could lift an independent's conversion by 3–5 points (estimate), and consignors whose cars sell locally have less reason to send them to a national chain. If that keeps 5% more of the auction's dealer consignments year on year (estimate), an auction offering 25,000 units keeps ~1,250 units it would have lost, worth ~$250,000–375,000 in fees at ~$200–300 a unit.

# The Unwind That Costs Three Transports

**Niche:** [[niches/wholesale-auto-auctions/arbitration-post-sale-claims/profile|Arbitration & Post-Sale Claims]]
**Industry:** [[industries/wholesale-auto-auctions|Wholesale Auto Auctions]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** An unwound sale moves the car to the buyer, back to the auction and out again to the next buyer, and the cost of the middle trip is argued case by case.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #large-language-models #workflow-orchestration #automation #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to settle buyers' post-sale claims under the NAAA guidelines without taking the car back, and whoever settles the largest share of granted claims by price adjustment rather than unwinding the sale takes the account.

## The Problem
When a buyer returns a car, it travels back from their lot, often hundreds of miles for a digital purchase. It waits for inspection, is relisted and reruns, usually at a lower price. Who pays the return transport, the relisting fee and the lost week is set by local policy and argued in each case. The seller sees one figure on a settlement statement weeks later.

## Why It's Still Broken
The return is booked by the transport desk, the relisting by the sale desk and the charge-back by accounting. No one sees the full cost of an unwind at the moment the desk chooses between adjusting and unwinding.

## What a Fix Looks Like
At the decision point, the case screen shows the estimated full cost of an unwind: return transport quoted live, relisting and rerun fees, expected price drop from the index trend, and days on the floorplan. That sits next to the proposed adjustment, so the desk and the seller compare like with like before the car moves.

## Who Feels the Pain
Sellers, who pay for unwinds they did not see coming; arbitration admins, who choose without the numbers; and buyers waiting for refunds.

## Impact If Fixed
If showing the full cost moves one in ten unwinds to an adjustment, an auction unwinding 4,000 cars a year avoids 400 round trips at ~$500–1,500 each, $0.2–0.6M a year (estimates).

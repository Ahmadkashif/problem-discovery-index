# Buy: High-Volume Recruiting Tools Adapted to Tenure Rather Than Fill

**Niche:** [[niches/digital-bpo-operations/hiring-and-attrition/profile|Hiring, Onboarding & Attrition]]
**Industry:** [[industries/digital-bpo-operations|Digital BPO Operations]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** High-volume recruiting technology is optimised for speed and cost per hire; in an industry with very high attrition the only metric that matters is whether the hire is still there in ninety days.
**Tags:** #gradient-boosting #survival-analysis #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #compliance #data-integration #automation #hypothesis-testing
**Contested on:** Whether recruiting tooling optimised for fill speed can be repointed at tenure.

## The Problem

High-volume hiring technology is mature. Sourcing at scale, automated screening, chat-based application flows, scheduling automation, assessment delivery and onboarding workflow all work well and are heavily used here, because the volume is enormous and the process is repetitive.

Everything is optimised for the funnel: time to fill, cost per hire, applicant conversion, offer acceptance. In an operation replacing a third of its workforce annually, a fast cheap hire who leaves in six weeks is not a success, and no metric in the recruiting stack distinguishes them from one who stays two years.

## What Already Exists

High-volume ATS platforms, conversational application and screening products, assessment vendors with language, typing and situational judgement instruments, interview scheduling automation, onboarding workflow, and background screening. Recruitment marketing and sourcing analytics.

## The Customization Gap

**The optimisation target has to become tenure.** Every screening and sourcing decision should be evaluated against ninety-day and twelve-month survival, which means the ATS has to receive outcome data back from the operational systems — an integration essentially nobody builds, because recruiting and operations report separately.

**Assessments need local validation and mostly do not have it.** Vendors supply instruments with general validity claims. What matters is whether this instrument predicts tenure and performance in this programme, in this market, for this population, and testing it requires the outcome link above. Instruments that fail that test should be removed rather than retained on the vendor's evidence.

**Source quality should be ranked by tenure, not by volume or cost.** Sourcing analytics report applicants and cost per hire by channel. Ranking channels by the survival of the people they produce frequently reverses the ranking, and it is a straightforward analysis once the outcome link exists.

**Realistic job previews need building and testing.** A meaningful share of early attrition is expectation mismatch — people who did not know what the work involves. A preview that shows the actual job, including the difficult parts, reduces acceptance and improves survival, and whether that trade is worth it is measurable and never measured.

**Subgroup fairness is a live exposure at this volume.** Instruments applied to hundreds of thousands of applicants with unexamined pass rates by subgroup, in multiple jurisdictions, carry real legal risk. The analysis is routine and the monitoring should be continuous.

## Target Customer

BPO recruiting and people leadership, where volume is enormous, the tooling is already licensed, and the outcome link is the missing piece. Also the high-volume recruiting and assessment vendors, for whom tenure-optimised hiring is a credible differentiator in a category that competes on funnel speed.

## Impact If Solved

The sourcing, screening, scheduling and onboarding machinery gets kept, and the outcome feedback loop, local assessment validation, tenure-ranked sourcing, tested job previews and subgroup monitoring get built. Concretely: hiring decisions evaluated on whether the person stayed, in an industry organised around the assumption that they will not.

# Quality & Resolution Measurement

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/digital-bpo-operations|Digital BPO Operations]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Whether the contact resolved the customer's problem, and whether anyone is measuring that rather than how long it took.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$22.5B — 25% of US spend on outsourced support and digital back-office operations
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~25%
**Digital Adoption:** Low — a two percent sample and a survey with a poor response rate
**Target Buyer:** BPO quality leadership; client-side vendor management
**Automation Potential:** Very high on the measurement; the contract is another matter

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche

Every second of an agent's shift is recorded and whether the customer's problem was solved is estimated from a small sample and a survey almost nobody answers.

Handle time, adherence, occupancy, after-call work and schedule conformance are measured to the second across hundreds of thousands of agents. Resolution — the thing the client is actually buying — is inferred from a quality analyst scoring a couple of percent of contacts against a rubric, plus a satisfaction survey whose respondents are self-selected and few.

The niche is distinct because it is the measurement gap from which everything else in this industry follows. The metrics govern the work; the metrics measure seconds; and the consequence is a workforce optimised for speed and assessed on a lottery.

## Current Tools & Gaps

Contact centre platforms with comprehensive operational telemetry. Quality management modules with rubric scoring and calibration between analysts. Post-contact satisfaction surveys. Speech and text analytics products, deployed at varying depth, mostly for compliance phrase detection and sentiment rather than for resolution. Repeat contact rates, computed by some clients and rarely fed back.

The gaps are coverage and definition. Sampling at two percent produces an estimate whose error at the agent level is enormous, and that estimate carries consequences for pay and employment. And resolution itself is not defined operationally anywhere — repeat contact is the obvious proxy and is owned by the client's systems rather than the BPO's.

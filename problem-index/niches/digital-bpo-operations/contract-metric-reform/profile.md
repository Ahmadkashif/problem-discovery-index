# Contractual Metric Reform

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/digital-bpo-operations|Digital BPO Operations]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Whether the client pays for contacts handled or for problems resolved.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$9.0B — 40% of the quality and resolution niche
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~10% of US outsourced support and back-office spend
**Digital Adoption:** None — the commercial model has not moved
**Target Buyer:** BPO commercial leadership; client procurement and vendor management
**Automation Potential:** Very low — this is a commercial negotiation with almost no engineering in it

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche

The other half produces a resolution measurement. This half asks what to do with it commercially, and the answer is the hardest question in the industry.

Contracts here are written on cost per contact, cost per minute, handle time targets, adherence and occupancy, with service levels defined in seconds. Those terms are precise, auditable and easy to compare between vendors, which is why procurement uses them. They also reward closing contacts quickly, which a resolution measurement will demonstrate works against resolution on exactly the contacts that matter.

Three things make this a distinct market. The work is commercial and legal rather than technical. The exposure is asymmetric — a vendor pricing on resolution underwrites an outcome it only partly controls, since the client's product, policies and knowledge base determine much of it. And the competitive dynamic punishes the first mover: in a bid evaluated on price per contact, a vendor proposing an outcome-based structure is proposing something procurement's own scorecard cannot score.

## Current Tools & Gaps

Standard BPO master service agreements with detailed SLA schedules on operational metrics. Gainshare and outcome-based pilot arrangements, which exist, are a small minority of contracts and are usually bolted onto an operational core. Benchmarking services comparing vendors on cost per contact. Client procurement scorecards built on the same measures.

The gaps are a workable outcome definition both sides trust, an attribution model for the parts the vendor does not control, and a procurement evaluation framework that can score a proposal on anything other than unit price.

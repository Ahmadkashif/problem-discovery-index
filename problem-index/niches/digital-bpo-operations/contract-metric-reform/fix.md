# Fix: The Targets Were Calibrated on Work That No Longer Arrives

**Niche:** [[niches/digital-bpo-operations/contract-metric-reform/profile|Contractual Metric Reform]]
**Industry:** [[industries/digital-bpo-operations|Digital BPO Operations]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Deflection took the easy contacts away, the remaining work is harder and longer, and the handle time targets in the contract were set before any of that happened.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #change-point-detection #hypothesis-testing #confidence-intervals #evaluation-metrics #worker-facing #quick-win #compliance
**Contested on:** Whether targets will be recalibrated to the work that actually arrives now.

## The Problem

Generative and self-service deflection has worked. Password resets, order status queries, simple account changes and the rest of the straightforward volume increasingly resolve without a human.

What reaches an agent is therefore what could not be deflected: the complicated, the ambiguous, the upset, the exceptional. Longer, harder and more emotionally difficult on average than the mix the targets were calibrated on.

The targets did not move. Average handle time targets set against the old composition persist, as do adherence, occupancy and cost per contact. Agents experience this as targets becoming quietly impossible, and are told they are underperforming against a number that describes a population of contacts that no longer exists.

The composition shift is measurable. Contact mix by type, before and after, with handle time by type held constant, separates the mix effect from any change in agent performance in an afternoon.

## Why It's Still Broken

Because the targets are in the contract and in the client's own scorecard, and recalibrating them upward looks like the vendor asking to be measured more leniently — which is exactly what it would look like without the mix analysis, and nobody has done the mix analysis.

Deflection is also usually the client's initiative, reported as a cost saving, and the consequence for the residual work is not part of that report. The saving is real; the compositional effect is unbilled and lands on the agents.

And there is a perverse dynamic: as deflection removes cheap contacts, cost per contact rises mechanically, which looks like the vendor performing worse.

## What a Fix Looks Like

Measure the composition shift, then recalibrate against it.

Decompose handle time change into mix and rate. Contact types with their volumes and their handle times, period over period. If total average handle time rose 14% and the mix shift alone accounts for 12 of it, that is the whole conversation and it takes an analyst a day. Nobody has run it, which is why the conversation has not happened.

Set targets per contact type rather than in aggregate. A single average target across a shifting mix is indefensible once the mix is moving, and per-type targets are both fairer and more useful operationally. They also make future composition shifts automatically self-adjusting.

Report cost per resolved issue alongside cost per contact. Deflection raises the second and may well lower the first, and the client's saving is real in the second measure. Presenting both is what stops the vendor looking worse for a change the client made.

Adjust the emotional load too. The residual contacts are disproportionately the upset ones, which has consequences for agent wellbeing, break policy and attrition that no target set reflects. Occupancy targets calibrated on a mix that included easy contacts are not appropriate to one that does not.

Build the mix-adjustment mechanism into the contract so it happens automatically. Composition will keep moving, and a contract that requires a negotiation each time will not keep up.

## Who Feels the Pain

Agents, held to targets calibrated for work they no longer receive, told they are underperforming, and handling a residue that is harder and more upsetting than anything in the target's assumptions. Team leaders, enforcing them. Clients, who see a rising cost per contact and conclude the vendor has slipped. And the vendor, penalised commercially for a compositional change its client initiated.

## Impact If Fixed

Targets describe the work that actually arrives. The mix effect gets separated from performance, which is an afternoon's analysis and reframes the entire conversation. Cost per resolved issue appears next to cost per contact, which is where deflection's benefit actually shows. And agents stop being told they are failing at a job whose definition changed underneath them.

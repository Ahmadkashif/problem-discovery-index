# Client Knowledge Base Quality

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/digital-bpo-operations|Digital BPO Operations]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Whether the content the agents answer from is accurate and complete, and who is accountable when it is not.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$8.1B — 9% of US outsourced support and back-office spend
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~9%
**Digital Adoption:** Very low — the client's content, maintained by whoever has time
**Target Buyer:** Clients' support content owners; BPO account teams
**Automation Potential:** High — gaps and staleness are detectable from the contacts themselves

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche

The agent is answering from a knowledge base the client maintains inconsistently. That content determines the ceiling on everything the BPO can deliver, and it belongs to someone else.

The structural problem is that the party who sees the content fail — the BPO, thousands of times a day — has no authority to fix it, and the party who owns it has no visibility of the failures. Client knowledge teams are typically small, under-resourced, working from product release notes rather than from contact evidence, and unaware which of their articles is producing wrong answers.

The niche is distinct because it is the client's operational problem discovered inside the vendor's, and because assist tooling has raised the stakes considerably: a stale article that once misled the agents who read it now gets retrieved and presented confidently across every related contact.

## Current Tools & Gaps

Knowledge management platforms on the client side with authoring, versioning and publication workflow. Article analytics showing views and searches. Some review cadences. The BPO's own supplementary internal notes, which teams maintain unofficially to work around the gaps.

The gaps are evidence and accountability. Article analytics measure whether an article was viewed, not whether it was right. Coverage gaps — questions with no article at all — are invisible to any view-based analytic. And there is no defined accountability between the parties for content quality, so it is nobody's metric.

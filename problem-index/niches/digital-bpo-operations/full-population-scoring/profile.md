# Full-Population Resolution Scoring

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/digital-bpo-operations|Digital BPO Operations]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Whether every contact can be scored from its own record rather than two percent of them by a person.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$13.5B — 60% of the quality and resolution niche
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~15% of US outsourced support and back-office spend
**Digital Adoption:** Low and rising — the corpus is processed, the judgement is not made
**Target Buyer:** BPO quality leadership; interaction analytics vendors
**Automation Potential:** Very high — a complete, structured corpus with a downstream outcome signal

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche

This is the measurement half, and it is unusually well posed. The corpus is every contact the operation handled, transcribed, timestamped, tagged by type, with the agent identified and the customer's subsequent contacts observable. There are few datasets in commercial use this clean.

The distinguishing property is that everybody wins. The quality function gets coverage instead of a sample. Agents get assessment on their whole month rather than four draws, which they want — the complaint about the current system is its randomness, not its existence. Team leaders get coaching material from the contacts that actually went wrong rather than from whichever four were pulled. Clients get a measure of the thing they are buying. And the BPO gets a differentiator.

It is also self-sufficient. A resolution score is useful the day it exists, inside the current contracts, with no commercial renegotiation required.

## Current Tools & Gaps

Interaction analytics processing every contact for transcription, phrase detection, sentiment and categorisation. Quality management modules with rubrics and analyst calibration. Agent scorecards and coaching workflow. All deployed at scale.

The gaps are the judgement and the downstream link. Nothing assesses whether the issue was resolved, because until recently nothing could. And repeat contact — the strongest outcome evidence — requires the client's cross-channel history, which the BPO frequently sees only in part.

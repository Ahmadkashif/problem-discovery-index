# Buy: Deflection Platforms Adapted to the Work They Leave Behind

**Niche:** [[niches/digital-bpo-operations/deflection-and-work-composition/profile|Contact Deflection & Work Composition]]
**Industry:** [[industries/digital-bpo-operations|Digital BPO Operations]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Conversational AI and self-service platforms are measured on containment; nothing in them models what the escalated remainder does to the human operation.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #survival-analysis #data-integration #change-point-detection #automation #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Whether containment-optimised platforms can be made to account for the consequences on the other side of the escalation boundary.

## The Problem

Conversational AI and self-service platforms have improved dramatically and are deployed everywhere. Intent recognition, generative resolution, transactional self-service and escalation routing all work, and containment rates that were aspirational three years ago are now routine.

Every metric in the category stops at the boundary. Containment, deflection rate, self-service completion, escalation rate. What happens to the escalated contact, what the residual mix does to the human operation, and whether a contained session actually resolved anything are all outside the product's frame — and they are where most of the cost sits.

## What Already Exists

The conversational AI platforms, generative resolution products, self-service portals, intent classification and routing engines. Containment and escalation analytics. Increasingly capable transactional automation. Integration with contact centre platforms for handoff.

## The Customization Gap

**Containment is not resolution and the products conflate them.** A session that ended without escalation is counted as contained regardless of whether the customer's problem went away. Distinguishing resolved, abandoned and delayed requires following the customer beyond the session, which no deflection platform does.

**Escalation handoff loses the context.** The bot has the full conversation and the agent frequently receives a transcript link nobody opens, or nothing. Passing a structured summary — what was attempted, what was established, what the customer is trying to do — into the agent's workspace is a handoff design problem the platforms treat as an afterthought.

**The composition effect is invisible to the platform and definitional for the operation.** Deflection changes the mix reaching humans. No deflection product reports what it left behind, and the operation has to reconstruct it from its own data.

**Deflection candidates should be ranked by net effect, not by volume.** These platforms recommend automation targets by volume and intent frequency. The right ranking includes the abandonment risk and the difficulty of what escalates when the bot fails, which requires human-side data the platform does not see.

**Release changes are unannounced interventions downstream.** A capability improvement shipped on Tuesday changes the operation's arrival process on Wednesday. A change feed from the deflection platform into the workforce management and quality functions is a trivial integration that essentially nobody has built.

## Target Customer

Client operations teams owning both the deflection platform and the outsourced operation, who currently manage them as separate programmes with separate metrics. Also the conversational AI vendors, for whom outcome-based rather than containment-based measurement is where the category's credibility is heading.

## Impact If Solved

The intent recognition, generative resolution and self-service capability gets used as designed, and the outcome classification, context-preserving handoff, composition reporting, net-value candidate ranking and release change feed get built. Concretely: deflection measured by whether the customer was helped, and an operation that knows when its work changed.

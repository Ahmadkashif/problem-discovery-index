# The Support Agent

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/digital-bpo-operations|Digital BPO Operations]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Whether the most measured worker in the digital economy gets anything back from the measurement.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$11.7B — 13% of US outsourced support and back-office spend
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~13%
**Digital Adoption:** Low — instrumented to the second, supported barely
**Target Buyer:** Agents; BPO people and operations functions; clients with supplier standards
**Automation Potential:** High — the feedback, the load management and the record are all derivable

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche

Every second is measured, the targets were calibrated before automation took the easy contacts away, and the remaining work is harder and more upsetting than what the numbers assume.

The agent is the most comprehensively instrumented worker in the digital economy and receives almost nothing from it. They get a quality score from four sampled contacts, adherence and occupancy percentages, and a handle time average against a target. They do not get useful feedback on how they handle difficult contacts, any recognition that the work has changed, any management of emotional load, or any portable record of what they have done.

The niche is distinct because the subject is the agent's working life in a setting where the surveillance is total and the support is minimal, and because attrition in this industry — routinely very high — is the aggregate expression of it.

## Current Tools & Gaps

Adherence and occupancy dashboards visible to the agent. Quality scores from sampling. Coaching sessions with a team leader. Break schedules set by workforce management. Employee assistance programmes, generally underused. Some gamification layers.

The gaps are load, feedback and record. The emotional load of the residual contact mix is unmanaged — break scheduling is an occupancy calculation and does not account for what the last three contacts were. Feedback is a sampled score rather than an account of performance. Career progression beyond team leader is thin. And an agent with three years of experience has no portable evidence of it beyond a reference.

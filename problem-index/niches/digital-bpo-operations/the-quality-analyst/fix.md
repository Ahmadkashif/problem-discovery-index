# Fix: Coaching From a Rubric Instead of From Evidence

**Niche:** [[niches/digital-bpo-operations/the-quality-analyst/profile|The Quality Analyst & Team Leader]]
**Industry:** [[industries/digital-bpo-operations|Digital BPO Operations]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The coaching session covers the rubric items the agent scored lowest on, which is not the same as what they are actually bad at.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #hypothesis-testing #workflow-orchestration #worker-facing #quick-win #cross-validation
**Contested on:** Whether coaching will address what an agent actually struggles with rather than what the rubric happened to score.

## The Problem

Coaching in this industry is organised around the rubric. An agent scored low on "confirmed understanding" across their four sampled contacts, so the session is about confirming understanding.

Two things are wrong with that. The four contacts are too few to establish that this is a pattern rather than a draw — and often it is a draw. And the rubric's items are the things an analyst can verify consistently, which are mostly procedural, so even a correct low score points at a procedural gap rather than at the substantive problem that is costing the agent's customers their resolution.

Meanwhile the team leader frequently knows what the agent actually struggles with, from listening in and from working alongside them, and has no way to put that into the coaching record because the record is organised by rubric item.

## Why It's Still Broken

The coaching structure inherited the quality structure, which inherited the sampling constraint. Rubric items are what exists, so rubric items are what gets coached, and the process is audited on whether the low-scoring items were addressed.

Team leaders' own observations are treated as anecdote against the score's apparent objectivity, which inverts the actual reliability of the two — a team leader's impression from hundreds of overheard contacts is a better estimate than four sampled scores, and nobody treats it that way.

And coaching effectiveness is not measured, so a process that produces nothing has never been challenged.

## What a Fix Looks Like

Change what the session is about, using things that cost nothing.

Stop coaching from a single month's four scores. Aggregate over a quarter before treating any rubric item as a pattern, and say plainly that a single low score is not evidence. This is a policy change and it eliminates most of the coaching-on-noise.

Let the team leader's observation into the record as a first-class input, with the contacts that prompted it attached. A structured note — "struggles to redirect a customer who has fixed on a wrong explanation, see these two contacts" — is better material than any rubric item and is currently homeless.

Let the agent bring a contact. Asking an agent to pick one contact they found difficult and talk it through is the most useful five minutes available, costs nothing, and surfaces the things neither the rubric nor the team leader saw.

Coach on one thing. Sessions that cover five rubric items change nothing. One specific, named behaviour with examples and a review date is what actually moves.

Measure whether it worked. Even without full coverage, the next month's scored contacts on that item are a weak signal, and a team leader's structured follow-up observation is a better one. Any measurement is more than exists now.

And check the rubric itself. If the items being coached are greetings and hold procedures while the resolution rate is the client's complaint, the rubric is pointed at the wrong things and no amount of coaching against it will help.

## Who Feels the Pain

Agents, coached on noise and procedure, who correctly conclude the process is a ritual and disengage from it. Team leaders, running sessions they know are hollow and unable to raise what they actually observed. Quality analysts, whose scores are used for something they cannot support. And the BPO, running a coaching programme at significant cost with no evidence it changes anything.

## Impact If Fixed

Coaching addresses what agents actually struggle with, sourced from the people who observe them rather than from four draws. Sessions cover one thing with examples. Team leaders' knowledge enters the record. And the question of whether coaching works starts being asked, which it currently never is.

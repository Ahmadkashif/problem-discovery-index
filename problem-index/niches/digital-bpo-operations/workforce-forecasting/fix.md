# Fix: The Cost of Being Wrong Falls on the Agent

**Niche:** [[niches/digital-bpo-operations/workforce-forecasting/profile|Workforce Forecasting & Scheduling]]
**Industry:** [[industries/digital-bpo-operations|Digital BPO Operations]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** An over-forecast means agents sent home without pay and an under-forecast means an unrelieved queue, and the forecast's accuracy report treats the two as the same size of miss.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #hypothesis-testing #monte-carlo-methods #worker-facing #quick-win #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Whether the consequences of forecast error will be measured alongside its magnitude.

## The Problem

Forecast accuracy is reported as a percentage error against actuals, symmetric by construction. A forecast 8% high and one 8% low are the same miss.

They are not the same thing at all. A high forecast means too many agents scheduled, which becomes voluntary time off, cut hours, or agents sent home — real lost income for people on modest wages, allocated to whoever was on that shift. A low forecast means a queue, breached service levels, and a shift where agents take back-to-back contacts with no recovery time, which shows up later as absence and attrition.

Neither consequence appears anywhere in the forecast's performance report. The planner optimises the percentage; the cost lands elsewhere.

## Why It's Still Broken

Symmetric error is the convention in forecasting and the default in every WFM platform's reporting. Nobody chose it for this context; it arrived with the tooling.

The costs are also borne by parties with no input. Agents sent home have no representation in the forecast review. Agents working an unrelieved queue register it as a bad day rather than as a forecast failure. The client sees only the service level.

And measuring the agent-side cost means counting cut hours and their distribution, which produces an uncomfortable number — particularly the finding that cut hours concentrate on the same people, because schedule flexibility correlates with seniority.

## What a Fix Looks Like

Measure both consequences and report them next to the error.

Count the agent-side cost. Hours cut or sent home, by week, by site, by individual. The distribution matters more than the total: if the same twenty percent of agents absorb most of the cuts, that is a finding about fairness as well as forecasting.

Count the service-side cost. Intervals breached, queue depth, and the agent experience during them — back-to-back contact time without recovery, which is measurable from the telemetry and is the mechanism by which an under-forecast becomes attrition three months later.

Report error by direction. Percentage high and percentage low, separately, with the consequences attached. The moment these appear side by side, the staffing decision changes, because someone has to decide which error to prefer and can no longer avoid the question by looking at a symmetric number.

Rotate the cuts. Where hours must be reduced, distribute across agents rather than by whoever is easiest to send home, and publish the distribution. This is a scheduling policy change and it removes most of the unfairness at no cost.

Give notice. An agent told at the start of a shift that they are being sent home has lost the commute and the childcare arrangement as well as the hours. A day's notice, where the forecast supports it, is worth a great deal and is usually possible.

And when a forecast is uncertain, staff to the middle and hold a flexible tier rather than committing to a point estimate — which is what the distributional forecast in this niche's build enables.

## Who Feels the Pain

Agents, losing income to a modelling error they had no part in and often absorbing it repeatedly because the same people are easiest to cut. Agents on an unrelieved queue, taking contact after contact with no recovery. Team leaders, delivering both messages. And the BPO, whose attrition is partly generated by a scheduling practice it has never costed.

## Impact If Fixed

The two directions of forecast error get reported with their actual consequences, which immediately changes how the staffing decision is made. Cut hours get distributed rather than concentrated. And the agent-side cost of forecasting becomes a visible number rather than an invisible transfer to the people least able to absorb it.

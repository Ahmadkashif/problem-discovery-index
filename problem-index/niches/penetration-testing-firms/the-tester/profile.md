# The Tester

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/penetration-testing-firms|Penetration Testing Firms]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Whether the write-up is work the schedule makes room for, or unpaid evening labour squeezed between back-to-back engagements.

## Profile

**Market Size:** ~$480M
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~8%
**Digital Adoption:** Low — utilisation dashboards and a document template
**Target Buyer:** Firm operations and practice leadership
**Automation Potential:** High for the reporting burden, none for the testing

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche

Engagements are booked back to back because utilisation is the business model. An engagement ends on Friday and the next begins on Monday, which means the report for the first is written during the second — in the evenings, at weekends, with the technical detail fading and the next client's system occupying the front of the tester's mind.

This is not an occasional scheduling failure. It is the structural consequence of pricing a business on billable days: writing time is not billable, so it is not scheduled, so it happens outside the schedule. A quarter to a third of the effort in a typical engagement goes into producing the document, and almost none of it appears in the calendar.

The effects compound. Reports are late, which makes findings stale. They are written tired, which makes them worse. Testers burn out and leave, in a field where the supply of genuinely good operators is the binding constraint on every firm's growth. And the expertise that would take years to replace walks out over a working pattern nobody has examined, because utilisation looks excellent right up until someone resigns.

## Current Tools & Gaps

Report templates, a firm style guide, and whatever note-taking the individual tester prefers. Some firms have reporting platforms that structure findings; most have a document and a review queue. Scheduling runs on professional services resourcing tools that book billable days. Screenshot and evidence capture is manual. Peer review adds a further round trip that lands on whoever is also mid-engagement.

The gaps are specific and mostly about time rather than capability. Nothing captures evidence and context as the tester works, so the write-up reconstructs from memory and screenshots. The same finding class is written from scratch on every engagement despite the firm having described it hundreds of times. Client-specific formats mean the same content is reassembled per engagement. Scheduling does not model write-up time at all, which is why it never exists. And nobody measures the unbilled hours, so the real cost of the model is invisible in every system the firm runs.

# Leisure Destination Marketing & Campaign Attribution

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/visitors-bureaus|Convention & Visitors Bureaus]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to show that a campaign caused overnight visits that would not otherwise have happened, and whoever produces an incrementality estimate that both the city's auditor and the hotel board accept takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.5B a year of leisure media, agency fees and campaign measurement across US destination organisations, including state offices (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~35-45% of a typical bureau budget (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** High — media is bought programmatically and measured with location and card data, but the measurement rarely isolates cause
**Target Buyer:** Chief Marketing Officer / Vice President of Marketing
**Automation Potential:** Medium-High for measurement and creative versioning; low for brand strategy

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Leisure marketing is the largest single line in most bureau budgets and the hardest to prove. Bureaus sell no product, so they cannot measure conversions the way a hotel or airline can. The industry's answer has moved from survey-based advertising return studies (the most recent national study put average visitor spending at $85 per $1 of media) to "exposed visitor" attribution: matching ad exposures to devices later seen in the destination. Neither separates visitors the campaign brought from visitors who would have come anyway. Competitors for this measurement are agencies of record (MMGY Global, Miles Partnership, Madden Media), destination data platforms (Zartico, Datafy), travel ad networks (Sojern, Expedia Group Media Solutions) and research firms that run ROI studies.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Programmatic buying with location-based attribution; Visa Spend Signals and Mastercard spend data; advertising effectiveness surveys; Google and Meta conversion-lift tools that do not see offline visits. Gap: designed experiments with holdout markets, and a lift number that holds up against hotel and tax data.

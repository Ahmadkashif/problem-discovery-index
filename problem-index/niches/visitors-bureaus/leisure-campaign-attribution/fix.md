# Exposed Visitors Reported as Campaign Results

**Niche:** [[niches/visitors-bureaus/leisure-campaign-attribution/profile|Leisure Destination Marketing & Campaign Attribution]]
**Industry:** [[industries/visitors-bureaus|Convention & Visitors Bureaus]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Bureaus report every ad-exposed device later seen in town as a visitor the campaign produced.
**Tags:** #causal-inference #hypothesis-testing #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to show that a campaign caused overnight visits that would not otherwise have happened, and whoever produces an incrementality estimate that both the city's auditor and the hotel board accept takes the account.

## The Problem
Attribution reports count visitors who saw an ad and later arrived, and multiply by average spend. The figure goes to the board as campaign impact. It includes people who were already planning the trip, and people targeted because they had visited before.

## Why It's Still Broken
The vendors' reports are built this way, the numbers are large, and nobody in a small bureau is positioned to challenge them.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Require every attribution report to show a comparison group (unexposed devices with similar history and origin) and the difference, not the exposed total; and run one placebo check per year on a period with no campaign.

## Who Feels the Pain
Marketing directors whose numbers are challenged in public; board members who cannot tell what worked.

## Impact If Fixed
Smaller, credible numbers that survive scrutiny, and an end to budget decisions based on totals that include people who were coming anyway.

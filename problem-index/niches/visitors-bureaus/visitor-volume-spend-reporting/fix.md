# The Panel Changed and Nobody Noticed

**Niche:** [[niches/visitors-bureaus/visitor-volume-spend-reporting/profile|Visitor Volume & Spend Reporting]]
**Industry:** [[industries/visitors-bureaus|Convention & Visitors Bureaus]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A location vendor changes its device panel or method, the bureau's visitor count jumps, and the jump is reported as growth.
**Tags:** #change-point-detection #time-series-forecasting #descriptive-statistics #data-integration #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to produce one monthly visitor count and spend figure reconciled across device panels, card data, hotel data and lodging-tax receipts, and whoever's number survives the city budget hearing takes the account.

## The Problem
Device panels shift as apps and data suppliers come and go; the FTC's January 2025 orders against two location-data brokers are a reminder that supply can disappear. Vendors re-weight their panels, sometimes restating history, sometimes not. A bureau sees a 15% rise and reports it.

## Why It's Still Broken
Vendors announce methodology changes inconsistently, and bureaus do not compare the panel's trend with hotel and tax data monthly.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Automatic structural-break checks on every licensed series against hotel and tax benchmarks, a log of vendor methodology notices, and a rule that unexplained breaks are footnoted before publication.

## Who Feels the Pain
Research staff who must later retract figures; executives who cited them in public.

## Impact If Fixed
Fewer published numbers that later prove to be artefacts.

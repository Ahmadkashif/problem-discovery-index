# The Lodging-Tax Report Rewritten From Scratch Each Year

**Niche:** [[niches/visitors-bureaus/rural-small-community-bureaus/profile|Rural & Small-Community Bureaus]]
**Industry:** [[industries/visitors-bureaus|Convention & Visitors Bureaus]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Small bureaus produce their annual report to the county or lodging-tax committee as a fresh document every year, so nothing is comparable year to year.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #large-language-models #compliance #automation #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to give a bureau of one to four people a defensible answer to "what did the lodging tax buy?" from data it already has, and whoever delivers that board-ready answer without the bureau hiring an analyst takes the account.

## The Problem
Each year's report picks whatever metrics looked good, in a new format, often by a new director. The committee cannot compare years, and the next director inherits nothing usable.

## Why It's Still Broken
There is no standard for small bureaus' reporting, and turnover in single-person offices is high.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A fixed core of eight to ten metrics defined once (lodging-tax receipts, room nights where available, visitor centre contacts, website sessions from target markets, partner count, grant room nights), kept in one running file, with the narrative drafted from the year's changes.

## Who Feels the Pain
New directors starting from zero; committees unable to judge trends.

## Impact If Fixed
Comparable reports year after year, and continuity through staff turnover.

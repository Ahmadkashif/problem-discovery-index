# Peer Benchmarks for Towns Too Small to Measure Alone

**Niche:** [[niches/visitors-bureaus/rural-small-community-bureaus/profile|Rural & Small-Community Bureaus]]
**Industry:** [[industries/visitors-bureaus|Convention & Visitors Bureaus]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A small bureau's own numbers are too thin to read, but pooled across hundreds of similar towns they become a benchmark nobody offers.
**Tags:** #bayesian-inference #k-means-clustering #time-series-forecasting #descriptive-statistics #data-integration #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to give a bureau of one to four people a defensible answer to "what did the lodging tax buy?" from data it already has, and whoever delivers that board-ready answer without the bureau hiring an analyst takes the account.

## The Problem
A county bureau with two staff sees lodging-tax receipts up 6% and visitor counts from a panel up 11%. It cannot tell whether that is good, because it does not know what happened in comparable rural counties. Location panels are noisy for small places, and the bureau's board wants a simple answer.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Data vendors sell per-destination licences and dashboards; pooling across clients is not their product. State offices hold some of the data but report statewide.

## What to Build
A benchmark service built on public lodging-tax data where states publish it, short-term rental data, and panel data licensed in bulk: towns clustered by type (lake, mountain, highway, college, heritage), with hierarchical models that shrink noisy small-town estimates toward their peer group. Each bureau gets a one-page monthly brief: how it did against peers, with plain uncertainty statements.

## Target Customer
Executive directors of small bureaus, sold through state tourism offices or state bureau associations.

## Impact If Built
Small bureaus get a defensible comparison for the price of a shared licence, and boards get context instead of raw percentages.

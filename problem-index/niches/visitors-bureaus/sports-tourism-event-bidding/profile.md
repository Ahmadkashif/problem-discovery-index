# Sports Tourism & Event Bidding

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/visitors-bureaus|Convention & Visitors Bureaus]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to prove how many room nights a tournament actually generated, and whoever counts the teams that booked outside the stay-to-play block most completely takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$400M a year of sports commission staff, bid fees, event grants and sports housing services run by or through bureaus (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~10-15% of bureau spending, higher in mid-sized cities with tournament complexes (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — bids and host agreements are documents and relationships; room-night counts come from housing spreadsheets and team surveys
**Target Buyer:** Executive Director of the sports commission / Vice President of Sports Sales
**Automation Potential:** Medium — counting and reporting are automatable; winning rights-holders' bids is relationship work

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Sports commissions, often a division of the bureau, bid for events owned by rights-holders: national governing bodies, NCAA championships, youth tournament operators. They pay bid fees and event grants, often from hotel tax, on the promise of room nights. Youth tournaments run "stay-to-play" rules requiring teams to book through an approved housing company, and many teams book around them. The bureau's claim rests on that count. The tournament side of this market is covered under [[niches/youth-sports-orgs/travel-tournament-teams/profile|Travel Tournament Teams]].

## Current Tools & Gaps
Stay-to-play housing companies (EventConnect, Team Travel Source and others) run team housing; Tempest sells a Sports Tourism Index; the Event Impact Calculator has a sports module; tournament platforms hold team rosters. These compete to be the bureau's source of record for sports room nights. Gaps: teams booking outside the block are invisible, and grants are paid on claimed rather than counted stays.

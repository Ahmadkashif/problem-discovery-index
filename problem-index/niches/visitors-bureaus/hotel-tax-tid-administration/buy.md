# Grant Management Adapted to the Room-Night Test

**Niche:** [[niches/visitors-bureaus/hotel-tax-tid-administration/profile|Hotel Tax & Tourism Improvement District Administration]]
**Industry:** [[industries/visitors-bureaus|Convention & Visitors Bureaus]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Grant platforms already run applications and post-award reports; hotel-tax grants need a statutory eligibility screen and evidence of overnight stays.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #bert #compliance #workflow-orchestration #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to produce, for every hotel-tax and assessment dollar, a record that ties the spending to an eligible purpose and to the overnight stays it produced, and whoever's record holds up through the city audit and the district renewal vote takes the account.

## The Problem
Bureaus that re-grant hotel tax to festivals, tournaments and arts groups receive applications that rarely address whether the event will put visitors in hotels. Post-event reports arrive late, claim attendance rather than room nights, and are hard to check.

## What Already Exists
Submittable, SmartSimple and Foundant run grant cycles, scoring rubrics and post-award reporting for foundations and governments.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs: the statutory test (such as Texas Tax Code ch. 351's direct-promotion requirement and categories) as an eligibility screen with reasons; required evidence defined per grant (room-block agreements, housing reports, hotel certifications, visitor surveys) rather than free-text outcomes; links to the bureau's hotel data to sanity-check claimed room nights; and reports in the format the city or district wants.

## Target Customer
Grant and community programme managers at bureaus administering hotel-tax event funding.

## Impact If Solved
Grants go to events that put people in hotels, and the bureau can prove it.

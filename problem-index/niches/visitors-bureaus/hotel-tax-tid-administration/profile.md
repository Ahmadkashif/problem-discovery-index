# Hotel Tax & Tourism Improvement District Administration

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/visitors-bureaus|Convention & Visitors Bureaus]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to produce, for every hotel-tax and assessment dollar, a record that ties the spending to an eligible purpose and to the overnight stays it produced, and whoever's record holds up through the city audit and the district renewal vote takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$120M a year of finance and compliance staff time, audits, district formation and renewal work, and grant administration tied to hotel-tax and assessment funding (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~4-5% of bureau spending (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — accounting is digital, but eligibility, grant evidence and compliance reports are spreadsheets and Word documents
**Target Buyer:** Chief Financial Officer / Chief Operating Officer of the bureau
**Automation Potential:** High for tagging, screening and reporting; low for the funding negotiation itself

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A bureau's funding comes with rules: a city services contract that passes on hotel occupancy tax, a tourism improvement district plan (211 districts across 23 states in a 2024 count) that fixes spending categories hoteliers voted for, and in some states a statute, such as the Texas two-part test every municipal hotel-tax expenditure must pass. Bureaus that re-grant money to events must collect evidence those events drew overnight visitors. The record is tested at annual budget hearings, audits and the district's renewal vote. Tourism improvement districts are a lodging-funded variant of the business improvement districts covered under [[niches/trade-associations/local-business-improvement-districts/profile|Local Business Improvement Districts]].

## Current Tools & Gaps
Fund accounting (Sage Intacct, Blackbaud Financial Edge NXT, QuickBooks); grant platforms (Submittable, SmartSimple); Civitas and similar advisors for district formation and renewal; Granicus on the city side. These compete for the bureau's finance and compliance budget. Gaps: funder rules not encoded, grant evidence collected by email, no link from spending to stays.

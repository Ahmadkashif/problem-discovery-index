# Listings That Verify Themselves

**Niche:** [[niches/visitors-bureaus/listings-calendars-inquiries/profile|Listings, Event Calendars & Visitor Inquiries]]
**Industry:** [[industries/visitors-bureaus|Convention & Visitors Bureaus]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Bureau staff check partner listings by hand, when every fact in a listing can be compared against the partner's own website, booking system or map profile.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #word-embeddings #k-nearest-neighbors #automation #data-integration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep a destination's partner listings, event entries and venue facts accurate and machine-readable, and whoever's records AI assistants and search engines cite when visitors and planners ask about the destination takes the account.

## The Problem
A restaurant changes its hours, an attraction closes for renovation, a hotel rebrands. The bureau's listing stays wrong until a visitor complains or the annual partner update. With hundreds of listings and one content coordinator, much of the site is out of date.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Destination CMSs treat listings as content entered by staff or partners. Verification against outside sources was too costly before language models could read arbitrary websites reliably.

## What to Build
A crawler-and-checker that reads each partner's website, booking or ticketing page and map profile on a schedule, compares hours, prices, status and amenities with the bureau's record, and proposes updates with the evidence. Confirmed facts are dated and published as structured data that search engines and AI assistants can cite.

## Target Customer
Directors of digital at mid-sized and large bureaus.

## Impact If Built
Listings stay current without staff effort, and the bureau's facts become the version AI answers rely on.

# Listings, Event Calendars & Visitor Inquiries

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/visitors-bureaus|Convention & Visitors Bureaus]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep a destination's partner listings, event entries and venue facts accurate and machine-readable, and whoever's records AI assistants and search engines cite when visitors and planners ask about the destination takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$350M a year of destination websites and CMS licences, content and listings staff, visitor guides and visitor centre operations (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~10-12% of bureau spending (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium-High — listings and calendars live in a CMS, but they are kept current by staff re-keying partner submissions
**Target Buyer:** Vice President of Marketing / Director of Digital
**Automation Potential:** Very high for upkeep and answers; low for visitor centre hospitality

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A bureau's website holds hundreds to thousands of partner listings (hotels, restaurants, attractions, shops), an event calendar fed by submissions, and venue specs for planners. Staff re-key, chase and purge it constantly, and much of it is out of date at any moment. The public used to find this content through search and the visitor guide. Increasingly they ask AI assistants, which may or may not draw on the bureau's records, so the bureau's job is shifting from publishing pages to being the trusted source behind someone else's answer.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Simpleview CMS and Tempest destination websites; CrowdRiff for visitor photos; Visit Widget and Bandwango for itineraries and passes; Google Business Profiles and Tripadvisor as competing sources; vendors pitching bureaus as the source of truth for planners' AI tools. Gaps: listings verified by hand, event submissions moderated one by one, and no structured feed for AI systems.

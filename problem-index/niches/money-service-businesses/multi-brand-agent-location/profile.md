# The Multi-Brand Agent Location

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/money-service-businesses|Money Service Businesses]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep the agent's single bank account solvent across every brand's ACH sweep, so the store never has a returned debit, and whoever makes its sweeps most predictable for the agent takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$300M a year on agent settlement, sweep servicing, agent support and agent-facing reporting across US transmitters (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~3% of MSB revenue spent on serving agents rather than senders (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low to Medium — a separate terminal or web screen per brand, a daily settlement report per brand, and one store bank account debited by each transmitter
**Target Buyer:** Head of Agent Network at the transmitter; the store owner as the user who chooses which brand to push
**Automation Potential:** High for settlement forecasting and reconciliation; the customer conversation at the counter stays human

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The agent is a small business — a bodega, a travel agency, a phone shop — that sells transfers for a commission and often carries two or three competing brands (estimate). Senders' cash is the transmitter's money held in trust, and each transmitter collects it by debiting the store's bank account on its own schedule (estimate: daily to twice weekly), net of commissions, cancellations and refunds that the store cannot easily predict. The same account pays the store's suppliers and rent. A returned sweep is treated by the transmitter as a credit and compliance event and can suspend the agent. Transmitters compete for counters, and the one whose settlement the owner can predict and reconcile is the one the owner keeps at the front; commission rates and compliance prompts were considered as the contest and rejected because every brand matches commissions and compliance friction is set by regulation.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Each transmitter's terminal, settlement report and agent portal, generic small-business bookkeeping, and the store's bank app. Gaps: no view of upcoming debits across all brands; settlement reports that do not match the debit amount or date; and no warning before a sweep that the account will not cover.

# E-commerce Fraud Scores Retrained for Irreversible Payouts

**Niche:** [[niches/money-service-businesses/new-sender-fraud-interdiction/profile|New-Sender Fraud & Scam Interdiction]]
**Industry:** [[industries/money-service-businesses|Money Service Businesses]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Card-not-present fraud platforms score millions of risky first purchases a day; a remittance is a first purchase whose goods are cash in another country, gone in an hour.
**Tags:** #gradient-boosting #feature-engineering #evaluation-metrics #transfer-learning #compliance #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to approve a genuine first-time sender's card- or bank-funded transfer in minutes while stopping stolen funds and scam victims' savings before payout, and whoever separates the two most accurately takes the account.

## The Problem
Transmitters license an e-commerce fraud score and layer their own rules on top, then tune both by hand as fraud patterns move between corridors.

## What Already Exists
Sift, Forter, Sardine and similar platforms, with device, behavioural and consortium signals, covered under [[industries/payment-fraud-vendors|Payment Fraud Vendors]].

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs: loss defined at payout, not settlement, with delivery speed as a decision variable (slow the payout instead of declining); receiver and payout-location features across senders; corridor-specific base rates; thin-file new migrants treated as a protected population for conversion, not just risk; a separate scam-victim class; and outputs mapped to BSA actions (hold, request information, SAR referral).

## Target Customer
Fraud leads at app-first transmitters and fintechs adding cross-border send.

## Impact If Solved
A licensed score that understands irreversible payouts, delivery-speed levers and scam victims replaces a stack of corridor rules nobody can maintain.

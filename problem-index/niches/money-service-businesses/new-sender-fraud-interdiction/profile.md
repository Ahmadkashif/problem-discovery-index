# New-Sender Fraud & Scam Interdiction

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/money-service-businesses|Money Service Businesses]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to approve a genuine first-time sender's card- or bank-funded transfer in minutes while stopping stolen funds and scam victims' savings before payout, and whoever separates the two most accurately takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$400M a year on fraud tooling, identity verification, manual review and losses management at app-first transmitters serving US senders (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~4% of MSB revenue (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Very High — decisions are automated, with manual review queues for the uncertain middle
**Target Buyer:** Chief Risk Officer / Head of Fraud
**Automation Potential:** High; the hard part is the label, not the automation

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A remittance payout is fast and close to irreversible: cash collected in another country cannot be clawed back when a stolen card is charged back weeks later. Two different threats arrive at the same moment — a fraudster using stolen credentials, and a genuine customer sending under a scammer's direction — and a genuine new migrant sender with a thin US file looks like both. E-commerce fraud models are tuned for reversible card purchases and do not separate these cases.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Identity verification vendors ([[industries/identity-verification-vendors|Identity Verification Vendors]]), card and account fraud scoring ([[industries/payment-fraud-vendors|Payment Fraud Vendors]]), device intelligence, in-house rules. Gaps: scam victims pass every identity check because they are who they say they are; chargeback labels arrive after payout; and receiver-side network signals across senders are under-used.

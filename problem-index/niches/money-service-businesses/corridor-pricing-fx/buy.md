# Revenue Management Engines Fitted to FX Spreads

**Niche:** [[niches/money-service-businesses/corridor-pricing-fx/profile|Corridor Pricing & FX Margin]]
**Industry:** [[industries/money-service-businesses|Money Service Businesses]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Airlines and B2B sellers have priced perishable, comparable products with optimisation engines for decades; a corridor's daily FX spread is the same kind of decision without the tooling.
**Tags:** #bayesian-inference #optimization-fundamentals #time-series-forecasting #revenue-impact #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to set each corridor's fee and FX spread so the landed amount wins the sender's comparison while still paying back acquisition cost, and whoever prices each corridor-day most precisely takes the account.

## The Problem
Remittance pricing is done in spreadsheets combining treasury's FX cost, a competitor scrape and the pricing manager's sense of each corridor.

## What Already Exists
Pricing and revenue-management platforms such as PROS and Pricefx, which forecast demand, estimate price response and optimise prices under business rules.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs: price defined as landed amount (fee plus FX spread, net of the 1% cash tax where it applies); competitor landed amounts as live inputs; corridor and payout-method segmentation; FX cost and hedging updated intraday; payday and receiving-country holiday calendars; regulatory guardrails (CFPB remittance disclosures must show the rate the sender gets); and new-sender versus repeat-sender response modelled separately.

## Target Customer
Mid-sized transmitters with dozens of corridors and no in-house pricing science team.

## Impact If Solved
A pricing team moves from weekly manual matching to daily, rule-guarded optimisation without building the engine from scratch.

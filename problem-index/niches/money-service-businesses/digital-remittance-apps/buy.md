# Product Analytics Rebuilt Around Remittance Cohorts

**Niche:** [[niches/money-service-businesses/digital-remittance-apps/profile|Digital-First Remittance Apps]]
**Industry:** [[industries/money-service-businesses|Money Service Businesses]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Product analytics tools count sessions and funnels; a remittance app's success is a sender who sends on payday for years, and the tools have no concept of a corridor or a pay cycle.
**Tags:** #feature-engineering #descriptive-statistics #survival-analysis #data-integration #revenue-impact #quick-win
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is that every app is fighting to win a migrant sender's second and tenth transfer, but that is two contests with different winners: a pricing contest over the landed amount shown corridor by corridor, won by FX and pricing discipline, and a risk contest over approving a first-time sender in minutes without paying out stolen or scam-induced funds, won by data and models. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## The Problem
Product teams measure onboarding funnels and monthly active users. What predicts revenue is whether a sender establishes a rhythm — biweekly, monthly, around holidays in the receiving country — and whether a failed payout, a document request or a price change breaks it.

## What Already Exists
Amplitude, Mixpanel and warehouse-native analytics with cohort and retention charts.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs corridor and payout-method dimensions on every event; a sender-rhythm model that detects a broken cadence rather than a monthly churn flag; receiving-country calendars (holidays, harvest seasons, school fees); joins to compliance events (document requests, holds) and to price shown versus competitors; and retention reporting by acquisition promotion.

## Target Customer
Product and growth analytics leads at app-first transmitters.

## Impact If Solved
Teams see within days when a release, a policy or a price change breaks senders' habits in a specific corridor, rather than finding it in next quarter's volume.

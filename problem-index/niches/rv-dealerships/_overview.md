## Why These Niches

Towable RVs and used consignment represent the dominant revenue segments with distinct operational challenges (towable outsells motorized 4:1, consignment creates unique pricing/legal complexities). First-time buyers and full-timers are underserved audiences whose purchase journeys differ radically from experienced RV buyers. Rural dealers and service departments lag in digital adoption despite representing significant market share. Seasonal inventory management and F&I product sales are the two most rule-driven, automatable workflows unique to RV retail.

## Why These Pockets

No qualifiers. The three pockets above 50 are the automotive data stack already indexed under independent auto dealers — the same publishers running an RV book off the same methodology, the same registration and history data businesses with thinner RV coverage. Below them the industry is too small to support a distinct insight layer: $25B, with the analytical functions that exist sitting inside lenders, insurers and manufacturers.

The interesting thing is how precisely the industry's central problem maps onto that structure. Pass 1 states it plainly: trade-in valuation is the unique headache, an RV being a vehicle, a house and an appliance suite at once; water damage is the hidden killer, turning a $30,000 trade into a $5,000 wholesale unit without being visible from a walkaround; and the guides provide base values that cannot account for condition.

Every piece of evidence that would solve it exists, held by a different party for a different purpose. The extended service contract underwriters hold component-level failure and repair cost histories across hundreds of thousands of covered units — the only structured record of what actually breaks on an RV and when — and use it to price a contract. The manufacturers hold warranty claims joined to build records, floor plan, supplier and production date, which would identify exactly which units develop water intrusion, and never let it leave. The dealer groups hold appraised value against eventual realised sale price across very large trade volumes, which is the largest record of RV valuation error in existence, and report it internally. Recreational lenders hold repossession resale outcomes. Specialty insurers hold the water damage claims. And the valuation guides that everyone transacts against — published by a company scoring 56 in this index — contain none of it, because the RV book is run off an automotive methodology.

The certification body that could have closed the loop fails the gate outright: independent RV inspections produce precisely the condition assessments trade-in valuation needs, and the body certifying those inspectors neither collects nor structures a single one of them.

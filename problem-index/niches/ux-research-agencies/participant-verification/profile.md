# Participant Verification

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/ux-research-agencies|UX Research Agencies]]
**Category:** 🟠 Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to establish that the eight people in a study were who they said they were, when screening is self-report against people whose income depends on passing — and whoever verifies them takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$0.5B US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~12% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Low — self-report
**Target Buyer:** Research operations
**Automation Potential:** High — verification and fraud detection

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Screening is done by self-report against people whose income depends on passing screeners, and a study's entire validity rests on whether the eight people in it were who they said they were. A professional participant population has grown up around the panels; they learn what answers qualify, and a proportion of any study's participants are not the people the study was designed for. Nobody verifies, and the resulting findings are reported with full confidence.

## Current Tools & Gaps
A screener questionnaire, a panel's own profile data, and an incentive. The gaps: no verification of claimed attributes; no detection of professional participants; no cross-panel identity resolution; no measurement of screener failure rates; and no adjustment to findings when the sample is wrong.

# The Participant Who Has Done Nine Studies This Month

**Niche:** [[niches/ux-research-agencies/participant-verification/profile|Participant Verification]]
**Industry:** [[industries/ux-research-agencies|UX Research Agencies]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The participant is articulate, gives excellent feedback, and does this professionally several times a week.
**Tags:** #quick-win #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #compliance #data-integration #confidence-intervals #automation #logistic-regression
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to establish that the eight people in a study were who they said they were, when screening is self-report against people whose income depends on passing — and whoever verifies them takes the account.

## The Problem
A subset of research participants are effectively professionals. They are comfortable being observed, fluent in the vocabulary, quick to offer opinions, and practised at thinking aloud. They are also unrepresentative in exactly the ways that matter: they are not encountering research as a novel situation and they frequently do not match the profile they qualified under. Their sessions are pleasant and produce quotable material, which makes them the last participants anyone questions.

## Why It's Still Broken
Nothing counts participation — a person who takes part in nine studies a month appears in each one as a first-time participant, because no system spans them. Panels do not disclose frequency. Articulate participants feel like good sessions. And the screener asked them a question they knew the answer to.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Ask about frequency and check the behaviour in the room. Ask participation frequency in the screener and exclude above a threshold, which is the fix and costs one question. Require panels to report a participant's recent study count, which several can supply and none are asked. Track participation across the agency's own studies, since repeats within one agency are the easiest to catch. Include one verifiable attribute question whose answer can be checked, which sizes the problem across a study. Note fluency with the research situation in the session record, as a first-time participant behaves visibly differently. Ask early questions that a genuine member of the population would answer differently from a practised participant. Recruit outside standard panels for critical studies, budgeting for it explicitly. Exclude rather than tolerate when the mismatch is clear, even at the cost of a slot. Record how many participants were flagged, which tells the agency whether its recruitment is sound. And report the sample's composition in the findings rather than in an appendix.

## Who Feels the Pain
Clients acting on findings from a population that was not theirs; researchers who suspected and had no basis to exclude; genuine participants displaced by professionals; and the discipline's validity claim.

## Impact If Fixed
A person taking part in nine studies a month appears in each as a first-time participant, because no system spans them. One frequency question and a cross-study count catches most of it.

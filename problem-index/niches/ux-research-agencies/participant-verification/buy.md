**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to establish that the eight people in a study were who they said they were, when screening is self-report against people whose income depends on passing — and whoever verifies them takes the account.

## The Problem
Survey research confronted respondent fraud at scale and built defences: attention and trap questions, straightlining and speeding detection, digital fingerprinting, duplicate and bot detection, cross-panel deduplication, and quality scoring that removes bad respondents before analysis. The practice is mature because the alternative was unusable data. Qualitative research recruits far fewer people, depends on each one far more, and has almost none of it.

## What Already Exists
Attention and consistency checks within screeners; speeding and straightlining detection; device and digital fingerprinting; cross-panel duplicate detection; and respondent quality scoring.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to eight participants in moderated sessions rather than a thousand in a survey. It requires: (1) a sample small enough that removing a bad participant after the fact destroys the study, so detection must happen at screening rather than in cleaning — this is the substantive difference and inverts the survey approach entirely; (2) attributes that are professional role, product usage and context rather than demographics, needing different verification; (3) a moderated session where a human could notice and currently has no framework for doing so; (4) the participant being present and paid, which makes rejection socially difficult; and (5) panels whose commercial interest is in supplying participants rather than in disqualifying them.

## Target Customer
UX research agencies, research operations, recruitment panels, and respondent quality vendors.

## Impact If Solved
Survey research built quality detection because fraud made data unusable, and the practice is mature. A sample of eight means detection must happen at screening rather than in cleaning, which inverts the entire approach.

# Contribution Practice From Open Source Documentation

**Industry:** [[industries/ux-research-agencies|UX Research Agencies]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Open source projects solved getting volunteers to document things through templates, review and visible credit, and repositories rely on goodwill.
**Tags:** #workflow-orchestration #compliance #automation #evaluation-metrics #worker-facing #descriptive-statistics #tacit-knowledge-ml #data-integration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get research written up in a form worth retrieving, when doing it properly competes with the next study and benefits somebody else later — and whoever solves that takes the account.

## The Problem
Open source projects face the identical incentive problem — documentation benefits future strangers and costs the contributor now — and developed practices that work: contribution templates that make the expected shape obvious, review that catches thin contributions, documentation as a merge requirement rather than a follow-up, visible credit for contributors, and maintainers who curate and retire. The practices are well documented and demonstrably effective at scale.

## What Already Exists
Contribution templates and guidelines; review as a gate on merge; documentation required alongside the change; visible contributor credit; and maintainer curation and deprecation.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a professional services context with client confidentiality and no community. It requires: (1) contributors who are employees on billable time rather than volunteers with intrinsic motivation, so credit works differently and the cost must fall to near zero — this is the substantive difference; (2) material under client confidentiality that must be abstracted before it can be shared internally; (3) findings that decay as products change, needing active retirement rather than permanent accumulation; (4) no community norm to appeal to, so the practice must be built into the workflow; and (5) teams too small for a maintainer role, requiring automation to do the curating.

## Target Customer
UX research agencies and in-house teams, research leadership, repository vendors, and knowledge management providers.

## Impact If Solved
Open source solved the same incentive problem with templates, review gates and visible credit, and it works at scale. Contributors on billable time under client confidentiality is why the cost has to fall to near zero here.

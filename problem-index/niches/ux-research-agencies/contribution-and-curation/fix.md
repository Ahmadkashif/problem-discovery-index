# The Study That Never Got Written Up

**Niche:** [[niches/ux-research-agencies/contribution-and-curation/profile|Contribution & Curation]]
**Industry:** [[industries/ux-research-agencies|UX Research Agencies]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The readout happened, the next project started, and the findings exist only in a deck in somebody's folder.
**Tags:** #quick-win #workflow-orchestration #automation #evaluation-metrics #worker-facing #descriptive-statistics #data-integration #tacit-knowledge-ml
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get research written up in a form worth retrieving, when doing it properly competes with the next study and benefits somebody else later — and whoever solves that takes the account.

## The Problem
The commonest repository failure is that most studies never reach it. The readout deck exists, the transcripts exist somewhere, and nothing was contributed because the researcher moved straight to the next project. Six months later the study is effectively lost: nobody knows it happened, the person who ran it has forgotten the detail, and the client paid for a finding the organisation no longer holds.

## Why It's Still Broken
Contribution happens after the project ends — a task scheduled after the point at which a project has anyone's attention will not happen, however short it is. Nothing gates project close. Nobody notices a study is missing. And the cost lands on a future colleague.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Gate the close and make the contribution tiny. Require a one-page contribution before a study is marked complete, which is the fix and is a gate rather than an appeal. Keep the required form to five fields — question, method, sample, findings, date — since a longer form produces nothing. Generate the draft from the readout deck automatically, which removes most of the remaining effort. Record the study's existence even when the detail cannot be shared, as knowing a study happened is most of the value. Book the contribution time into the project plan rather than expecting it from slack. Have the project lead rather than the researcher own it, which suits both roles better. Report which studies from the last year are missing, which is the number that gets attention. Backfill the most recent year rather than the whole archive, which is achievable and captures most of the value. Tell researchers when their contribution was retrieved, which is the only feedback loop available. And measure completeness rather than volume, since a patchy corpus fails differently from a small one.

## Who Feels the Pain
Teams that lost studies they paid for; researchers rediscovering their own colleagues' work; clients funding research that evaporates; and the repository, whose emptiness confirms everyone's expectation.

## Impact If Fixed
A task scheduled after the point at which a project has anyone's attention will not happen, however short it is. A five-field gate before a study is marked complete is the difference between a corpus and a folder.

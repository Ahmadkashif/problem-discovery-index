# A Pipeline That Does Not Depend on One Person

**Industry:** [[industries/ux-research-agencies|UX Research Agencies]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)

**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to stop an entire agency's research pipeline depending on one person's spreadsheets — and whoever removes that dependency takes the account.

## The Problem
Research operations is screening, scheduling, consent, non-disclosure agreements, reminders, no-show management, incentive payments, panel relationships and records, across every concurrent study. One person holds all of it, largely in spreadsheets and email, and every researcher's fieldwork depends on it working. When they are ill, on leave or leave the firm, studies stop, and the knowledge of how any of it works goes with them.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Research operations tooling exists for large in-house teams at a price agencies will not pay. The role is small and junior and does not buy software. The work looks like administration. And the dependency is only visible when it fails.

## What to Build
Automate the pipeline and make it operable by more than one person. Automate the sequence from screener to incentive payment end to end, which is the core and is what removes both the workload and the dependency. Handle consent and non-disclosure agreements electronically with the records attached to the participant, since that is the most error-prone manual piece. Pay incentives automatically on session completion, which is a persistent friction and the commonest participant complaint. Show a pipeline view across all concurrent studies, as the person currently holds that in their head. Predict and manage no-shows with overbooking and standby lists rather than scrambling. Make the whole thing handoverable and documented, which is the actual risk being carried. Support several panels and recruitment sources in one place rather than one interface each. Track capacity so a study cannot be scheduled into a week that is already full, which is a constant and invisible pressure. Report the operations load so it is staffed rather than absorbed. And keep it priced for an agency of twenty people, which is the binding constraint on anything they will adopt.

## Target Customer
UX research agencies and in-house teams, research operations specialists, recruitment panels, and research operations tooling vendors.

## Impact If Built
The whole agency's output depends on one person's spreadsheets and the knowledge goes with them. End-to-end automation with a documented, handoverable pipeline removes both the workload and the single point of failure.

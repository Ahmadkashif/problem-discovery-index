# Everything Stops When They Are on Leave

**Niche:** [[niches/ux-research-agencies/the-research-ops-person/profile|The Research Operations Person]]
**Industry:** [[industries/ux-research-agencies|UX Research Agencies]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The operations person took a week off and three studies could not recruit.
**Tags:** #worker-facing #quick-win #workflow-orchestration #automation #compliance #data-integration #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to stop an entire agency's research pipeline depending on one person's spreadsheets — and whoever removes that dependency takes the account.

## The Problem
The operations person's knowledge is undocumented: which panel to use for which profile, what the incentive rate is by study type, who approves what, where the consent templates are, how the payment process works. None of it is written down because writing it down has never been urgent. When they are away, researchers improvise, mistakes are made with consent and payment, and studies slip. When they leave, the agency loses months.

## Why It's Still Broken
Nothing was written down — a process that lives in one person's head continues to work perfectly until the day it does not, and there is never a moment before that day when documenting it feels urgent. The role is busy. Nobody else does the work. And the risk is invisible while the person is present.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Write the process down while the person is still there. Document the operational runbook — panels, rates, approvals, templates, payment process — which is the fix and is a day of work against a real continuity risk. Have a second person run one study end to end, which surfaces everything the runbook missed. Move the records out of personal spreadsheets into a shared place, so continuity does not depend on access. Standardise incentive rates and approvals rather than deciding per study, which removes a large share of the judgement. Template the consent and agreement documents so they are not reconstructed each time. Schedule the documentation as work rather than expecting it from spare time, since there is none. Cross-train a researcher on the basics, which covers short absences. Automate the steps that are purely mechanical first, as those are the ones that block hardest. Review the runbook when anything changes rather than annually. And treat the dependency as a business risk with a named owner rather than as a feature of the role.

## Who Feels the Pain
Operations people who cannot take leave without consequences; researchers whose studies stall; clients whose timelines slip; and the agency, which discovers the dependency when the person resigns.

## Impact If Fixed
A process that lives in one person's head works perfectly until the day it does not, and there is never a moment before that day when documenting it feels urgent. A day writing the runbook removes a real continuity risk.

# Rebuilding the Screener Every Time

**Niche:** [[niches/ux-research-agencies/fieldwork-automation/profile|Fieldwork Automation]]
**Industry:** [[industries/ux-research-agencies|UX Research Agencies]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The screener for this study is being written from scratch, and a nearly identical one ran six weeks ago.
**Tags:** #quick-win #automation #workflow-orchestration #evaluation-metrics #data-integration #descriptive-statistics #compliance #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get a study from approved to in-field without a week of setup, and whoever automates that takes the account.

## The Problem
Screeners are written afresh for each study, from a blank form or by copying whichever previous one someone can find. The questions that worked, the ones that failed to discriminate, the wording that confused people — none of it is recorded, so the same mistakes recur. Meanwhile the writing itself is a day's work that was largely done before, and it delays recruitment by exactly that day.

## Why It's Still Broken
Nothing keeps the screeners — an instrument written per study and discarded after fielding leaves nothing to reuse, so every study starts from a blank form or a copy of whatever was findable. Screener performance is never measured. There is no library. And copying feels fast enough.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Keep a library and record how the questions performed. Maintain a screener question library with the profile attributes each question addresses, which is the fix and is a document that pays back immediately. Record each question's performance — pass rate, discrimination, whether it produced the right people — since that is what makes the library better than a folder. Assemble a draft screener from a profile definition rather than writing from scratch. Keep the wording that worked verbatim, as small changes in screener wording have large effects. Note the questions that professional participants have learned to answer, which have a limited useful life. Reuse across clients where the attribute is generic, which is most of them. Review the screener against the study's actual sample afterwards to see whether it selected correctly. Share the library across the agency rather than per researcher. Version it so improvements propagate. And keep it short, since a library nobody can scan is another repository nobody searches.

## Who Feels the Pain
Researchers writing instruments that already exist; studies delayed by a day of avoidable work; samples selected by questions that do not discriminate; and the operations person waiting to start recruiting.

## Impact If Fixed
An instrument written per study and discarded after fielding leaves nothing to reuse, so every study starts from a blank form. A question library with recorded performance is a document that pays back on the next study.

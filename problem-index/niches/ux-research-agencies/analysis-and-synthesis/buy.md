## The Problem
Qualitative data analysis software is mature: hierarchical coding schemes, fast retrieval of coded segments, memo writing, inter-rater reliability, matrix queries across cases and attributes, and increasingly automated first-pass coding. Academic researchers use it as standard. Commercial UX research — which produces the same kind of material under far tighter deadlines — largely uses transcripts, a spreadsheet and sticky notes on a virtual whiteboard.

## What Already Exists
Hierarchical coding and retrieval; memo and annotation systems; inter-rater reliability measurement; cross-case matrix queries; and automated first-pass coding suggestions.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a three-day turnaround and a deliverable that is a persuasive deck. It requires: (1) analysis completed in days rather than months, so the tooling must be far more automated and far less configurable than academic practice assumes — this is the substantive difference; (2) sessions that combine speech with screen recording and observed behaviour rather than interview text alone; (3) output that must persuade a product team, so findings need clips and counts rather than coded extracts; (4) coding frameworks reused across studies and clients rather than developed per project; and (5) researchers who will abandon anything that takes longer than the current method on the first study.

## Target Customer
UX research agencies, in-house research teams, repository vendors, and qualitative analysis software providers.

## Impact If Solved
Academic qualitative software solved coding, retrieval and agreement decades ago. A three-day turnaround producing a persuasive deck is what makes the commercial version need far more automation and far less configuration.

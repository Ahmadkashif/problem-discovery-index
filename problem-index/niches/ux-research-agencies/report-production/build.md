# The Deck the Analysis Already Contains

**Niche:** [[niches/ux-research-agencies/report-production/profile|Report & Deliverable Production]]
**Industry:** [[industries/ux-research-agencies|UX Research Agencies]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** The findings, the evidence and the quotes all exist in the analysis, and the deck is built from scratch anyway.
**Tags:** #automation #large-language-models #workflow-orchestration #data-integration #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #quick-win #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to produce the deliverable without a day of slide building, and whoever automates it takes the account.

## The Problem
By the time the analysis is done, everything the deliverable needs exists: the findings, the participants supporting each one, the quotes, the moments in the recordings. The researcher then spends a day moving all of it into slides by hand, cutting clips in a separate tool, and rebuilding a structure they have built dozens of times. It happens at the point in the study when time is scarcest and it adds nothing to the findings.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The analysis and the deliverable live in different tools with nothing between them. Decks are treated as craft rather than as output. Every agency's template is its own. And the time is billable or absorbed.

## What to Build
Generate the deliverable from the analysis and let the researcher edit it. Produce the deck directly from the coded findings, including counts, supporting quotes and clip references, which is the core and removes the day. Extract clips automatically from the marked moments rather than cutting them in a separate tool. Generate audience-specific versions — a full report, an executive summary, a team walkthrough — from one source, since those are currently made three times. Apply the agency's template automatically so output is consistent regardless of who produced it. Include the sample and method on the face of the deliverable rather than in an appendix, which is a quality improvement as well as an automation. Keep every claim linked to its evidence, so a reader can check and the researcher can defend. Support the researcher rewriting and reordering freely, since the argument is theirs and generated prose will not be the final word. Produce a version that survives being forwarded without the researcher present. Keep the structured findings as the artefact and the deck as a rendering, which also solves the repository contribution problem. And make regeneration cheap, so a late change does not mean rebuilding.

## Target Customer
UX research agencies and in-house teams, research operations, research platform vendors, and reporting automation providers.

## Impact If Built
Everything the deliverable needs exists in the analysis and the deck is rebuilt by hand anyway, at the point when time is scarcest. Generating it from the coded findings removes the day and makes the findings the artefact.

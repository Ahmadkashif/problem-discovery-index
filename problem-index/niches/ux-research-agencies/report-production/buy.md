## The Problem
Business intelligence eliminated manual report production. A report is defined once, generated on a schedule or on demand, rendered to several formats and audiences from one definition, and distributed automatically. Narrative generation now describes what moved and by how much. Nobody in a data team builds a recurring deck by hand. Research deliverables have the same structure — findings, evidence, a narrative — and are built manually every time.

## What Already Exists
Report definitions rendered to multiple formats; scheduled and on-demand generation; audience-specific views from one source; automated narrative description; and distribution with access control.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a deliverable whose content is qualitative evidence and whose purpose is persuasion. It requires: (1) findings that are claims about people rather than aggregates, so the narrative is an argument rather than a description — this is the substantive difference and is why generic reporting does not transfer; (2) video clips and quotes as first-class evidence, which no BI tool handles; (3) a deliverable that must persuade a sceptical stakeholder rather than inform a routine reader; (4) a one-off per study rather than a recurring report, so the definition must come from the analysis rather than be authored; and (5) a researcher who will rewrite the argument and needs to, since the reasoning is the product.

## Target Customer
UX research agencies and in-house teams, research platform vendors, and reporting automation providers.

## Impact If Solved
BI eliminated manual report production with definitions rendered to many audiences and generated narrative. Findings that are arguments rather than descriptions, evidenced by clips, is what the research version has to generate.

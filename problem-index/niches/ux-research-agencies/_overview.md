# Niche Analysis — UX Research Agencies

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/ux-research-agencies|UX Research Agencies]]

## Niche Selection

This is a discipline built to reduce uncertainty about user behaviour that has never measured its own accuracy. The instruments are good and there is no memory: an engagement produces a report and ends, the predictions in it are testable and untested, and the repository holding years of material is one nobody searches. The eight niches below follow that — what the analysis costs, what the predictions were worth, what the corpus could answer, who the participants actually were, and who keeps it all running.

## Why These Niches

Analysis and track record carry the money and the credibility problem. Twelve hours of interviews become a deck over a weekend, and the thinking gets whatever time is left; meanwhile the predictions in that deck are testable, untested, and the reason research is the first line cut in a downturn. The repository and participant verification are the two places where the discipline's own infrastructure quietly fails — years of material nobody searches, and a study's entire validity resting on whether eight people were who they said they were. The two underserved audiences are the operations person on whom everything upstream depends, and the participants themselves, whose income depends on passing screeners and about whom nobody has built anything. The last two are mechanical: running the fieldwork, and producing the deliverable.

## Filter Notes

Seven of the eight are terminal — each names one contest that every serious competitor is fighting over, and decomposing further would produce features rather than markets.

**The research repository** is not. It names an artefact, and the reason every team has one and nobody uses it is that two different problems are both unsolved and each is blamed for the other. Retrieval and synthesis asks how to get an answer out of years of transcripts, tags and reports when the question is a design question rather than a keyword — a search and synthesis problem over material the agency already holds, sold to research operations, and valuable even if nothing new is ever contributed. Contribution and curation asks how material gets in, in a form worth retrieving, when writing it up properly competes with the next study and benefits somebody else later — a practice, incentive and workflow problem sold to research leadership, and unsolvable by any amount of search technology. Teams that have bought repository tooling bought the first and got neither, because the corpus they pointed it at was never curated; teams that have mandated documentation produced material nobody could find. They are separate purchases and each fails alone.

Two adjacent candidates were rejected as belonging elsewhere: **the design work the findings feed into** belongs to [[industries/product-design-studios|Product Design Studios]], and **continuous experimentation on a live product** sits with [[industries/conversion-optimization-firms|Conversion Optimization Firms]].

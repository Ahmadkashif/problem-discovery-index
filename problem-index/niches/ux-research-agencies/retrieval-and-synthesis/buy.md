## The Problem
Retrieval over a document corpus with a synthesised, grounded answer is now a standard engineering pattern: embed the material, retrieve semantically, synthesise with citations back to the source, and evaluate retrieval quality directly. The components are commodity and the pattern is widely deployed across support knowledge bases, legal corpora and technical documentation. Research repositories, whose corpus is smaller and whose users are highly motivated, mostly run keyword search.

## What Already Exists
Semantic embedding and retrieval; grounded synthesis with citation back to source; retrieval quality evaluation; hybrid keyword and semantic ranking; and multi-turn query refinement.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a corpus of spoken sessions where the claim's weight depends on how it was gathered. It requires: (1) evidence weighting by sample size, method and recency, since a claim from five participants two years ago and one from forty last month are not interchangeable — this is the substantive difference and generic retrieval has no notion of it; (2) source material that is conversational speech with disfluency, hedging and context; (3) grounding in video clips rather than only text, because that is what persuades a product team; (4) product context that decays, making an accurate retrieval actively misleading; and (5) a user who is a researcher and will reject a synthesis that overstates.

## Target Customer
UX research agencies and in-house teams, research operations, repository vendors, and search and retrieval providers.

## Impact If Solved
Semantic retrieval with grounded synthesis is a commodity pattern deployed across many corpora. Evidence weighting by sample, method and recency is what generic retrieval lacks and what this corpus requires.

# The Incentive That Arrived Six Weeks Later

**Niche:** [[niches/ux-research-agencies/the-professional-participant/profile|The Professional Participant]]
**Industry:** [[industries/ux-research-agencies|UX Research Agencies]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The participant gave an hour of their time and was paid a month and a half afterwards, having chased twice.
**Tags:** #worker-facing #quick-win #workflow-orchestration #automation #compliance #evaluation-metrics #revenue-impact #descriptive-statistics
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to make research participation work for the people who do it repeatedly for income, because their behaviour is what determines whether any study's sample is real — and whoever serves them takes the account.

## The Problem
Incentive payment is slow, manual and frequently forgotten. It passes through an operations person, a finance process and sometimes a panel, and the participant — who may be doing this for income — waits weeks and often has to chase. It is the most common complaint participants have about research, it costs the industry the goodwill of exactly the people it depends on, and it is entirely a process failure.

## Why It's Still Broken
Payment is the last step of a finished project — a task that happens after the work everyone cares about is done will be deprioritised, and the person waiting has no leverage with anyone. The process crosses functions. Nobody measures the delay. And participants rarely complain twice; they simply stop responding.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Automate the payment and measure the delay. Trigger payment automatically on session completion rather than through a manual step, which is the fix and removes the failure mode entirely. Use instant payment methods rather than an invoice cycle, since participants are individuals and not suppliers. Tell the participant when and how they will be paid at the point of booking, which removes most of the chasing. Measure and report time-to-payment as an operational metric, as it is currently unmeasured and therefore unmanaged. Pay for a session that was cancelled by the agency, which is plainly right and frequently not done. Handle no-show and partial-session cases with a stated rule rather than case by case. Give participants a route to chase that reaches someone, rather than an unmonitored address. Reconcile unpaid sessions on a schedule rather than waiting for a complaint. Include panel-mediated payments in the measurement, since delays frequently occur there and nobody looks. And treat prompt payment as a recruitment advantage, which it demonstrably is.

## Who Feels the Pain
Participants waiting on money they earned; operations people chasing finance on their behalf; agencies whose panel response rates decline; and every study that recruits more slowly than it should.

## Impact If Fixed
A task that happens after the work everyone cares about is done will be deprioritised, and the person waiting has no leverage with anyone. Automatic payment on session completion removes the failure mode.

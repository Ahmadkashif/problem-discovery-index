# Making Honesty the Better Option

**Niche:** [[niches/ux-research-agencies/the-professional-participant/profile|The Professional Participant]]
**Industry:** [[industries/ux-research-agencies|UX Research Agencies]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** The industry's supply side is paid to qualify and nothing rewards them for not qualifying.
**Tags:** #worker-facing #k-nearest-neighbors #evaluation-metrics #workflow-orchestration #compliance #automation #descriptive-statistics #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to make research participation work for the people who do it repeatedly for income, because their behaviour is what determines whether any study's sample is real — and whoever serves them takes the account.

## The Problem
A person who takes part in research for income faces a screener that pays if they pass and nothing if they fail. They learn which answers qualify. The industry then treats the resulting mismatch as fraud and builds detection, which is an arms race against an incentive it created. Meanwhile the participant is paid late, told nothing about what their session was for, and has no standing anywhere. The validity problem and the participant experience problem are the same problem.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Participants are not the customer and nobody's budget serves them. Panels profit from qualification rather than from accuracy. The industry frames it as fraud rather than as incentive design. And the participants are individually powerless and unorganised.

## What to Build
Change what the participant is paid for. Pay for the screener attempt rather than only for qualification, which is the core — a person paid to answer honestly has no reason to shade, and the whole detection problem shrinks. Match participants to studies they genuinely fit rather than letting them chase every opportunity, since good matching is in everyone's interest and nobody does it. Build a reputation that rewards accurate self-description across studies, which is the durable version of the same incentive. Pay promptly and predictably, as delayed incentives are the commonest grievance and cost the industry goodwill it needs. Tell participants what their session contributed to where confidentiality allows, which costs nothing and changes the relationship. Show them what they are likely to be paid and how often they can expect opportunities. Let a participant decline without penalty when they know they do not fit, which currently costs them income. Cap participation frequency transparently rather than through undetected exclusion. Give participants a route to report a bad session or a non-payment. And treat the participant relationship as infrastructure the industry depends on rather than as a cost line.

## Target Customer
Recruitment panels and participant marketplaces, research agencies and in-house teams, participants themselves, and marketplace platform vendors.

## Impact If Built
A person paid only for qualifying has every reason to shade, and the industry builds detection against an incentive it created. Paying for the honest attempt and matching properly shrinks the validity problem at its source.

## The Problem
A research repository holds transcripts, clips, tagged findings and reports from years of studies. Somebody asks whether users understand a particular concept, and the fastest route to an answer is a new study. The material is there, the search returns either nothing or hundreds of loosely related fragments, and the person asking gives up. The investment in the corpus is entirely sunk and the same ground is covered again.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Repository products solved storage and tagging and assumed retrieval would follow. Tags are applied inconsistently by different people at different times. Findings are written as narrative rather than as answerable claims. And nobody has measured whether the repository is used, so nobody knows it is failing.

## What to Build
Make the corpus answer a question rather than return documents. Retrieve against the meaning of a design question rather than against keywords, which is the core and is what the current search fails at. Synthesise across studies into an answer with its supporting evidence rather than returning a list of fragments, since a list is what people abandon. Show how many studies and participants support each claim and how old they are, which is what determines whether the answer can be trusted. Surface contradictions between studies rather than hiding them, as those are the most informative results. Push relevant prior findings into the study planning process rather than waiting to be searched, which is the only reliable route to use. Normalise inconsistent tagging automatically rather than requiring the corpus to be re-tagged. Flag findings whose age or context makes them unreliable. Measure use — queries, answers found, studies avoided — which is the number that would justify the whole investment. Link every synthesised claim back to the moment it came from, since researchers will not trust a summary they cannot verify. And make it useful with the corpus as it actually is, rather than requiring a curation project first.

## Target Customer
UX research agencies and in-house research teams, research operations, repository product vendors, and knowledge management providers.

## Impact If Built
Every team has a repository and nobody searches it, which is a statement about the corpus rather than the search box. Retrieval against the meaning of a design question, synthesised with its evidence, is what makes years of paid work usable.

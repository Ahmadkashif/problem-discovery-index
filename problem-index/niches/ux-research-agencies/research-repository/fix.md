# Researching Something the Team Already Researched

**Niche:** [[niches/ux-research-agencies/research-repository/profile|Research Repository]]
**Industry:** [[industries/ux-research-agencies|UX Research Agencies]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The study now in fieldwork asks a question the team answered eighteen months ago for a different product team.
**Tags:** #quick-win #workflow-orchestration #data-integration #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #tacit-knowledge-ml #automation #word-embeddings
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to make years of accumulated research answerable to the question somebody is asking today — and whoever does takes the account.

## The Problem
Studies are commissioned to answer questions that have already been answered. A different product team, a different researcher, eighteen months earlier, ran a study covering the same ground. Nobody knew, because the only way to find out was to search a repository nobody searches, and the intake process does not include looking. The cost is a full study — weeks and a budget — to produce a finding the organisation already owned.

## Why It's Still Broken
Nobody checks at intake — a study request that is never compared against what has already been done will duplicate whatever has already been done, and no step in the process compares them. Searching is harder than commissioning. Researchers do not know what colleagues have run. And duplication is invisible because nobody looks.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Add a check at intake, before anything is scoped. Search prior research as a mandatory step when a study is requested, which is the fix and is a process change rather than a product. Maintain a one-line summary per past study that a human can scan, since that is faster than any search for this purpose. Have researchers share what they are about to run with the team, which catches duplication conversationally and costs nothing. Keep a simple index of questions previously asked rather than of findings, as the question is what people match on. Record when a prior study made a new one unnecessary, which is the number that justifies the repository. Review the intake queue for overlap on a cadence. Ask the requester what they already know and where it came from. Retire or mark findings whose product has changed, so a match is not misleading. Make the check five minutes rather than a review, or it will be skipped. And treat a duplicate study as a process failure worth noting rather than as nobody's fault.

## Who Feels the Pain
Clients paying twice for the same finding; researchers re-establishing what colleagues established; teams whose research budget buys less than it should; and the repository, whose unused state is confirmed again.

## Impact If Fixed
A study request that is never compared against what has already been done will duplicate it, and no step in the process compares them. A five-minute check at intake is a process change that saves whole studies.

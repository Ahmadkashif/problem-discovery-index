# A Record of Being Right

**Niche:** [[niches/ux-research-agencies/predictive-track-record/profile|Predictive Track Record]]
**Industry:** [[industries/ux-research-agencies|UX Research Agencies]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A discipline that exists to predict user behaviour has never scored a prediction.
**Tags:** #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #hypothesis-testing #causal-inference #descriptive-statistics #data-integration #bayesian-inference #tacit-knowledge-ml
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to show that its predictions about user behaviour were correct, in a discipline that has never recorded one — and whoever builds that record takes the account.

## The Problem
Every study makes predictions: users will struggle at this step, this concept will not be understood, this feature will go unused, this wording will be misread. Every one is testable against what happens after release. The agency is gone by then and the client rarely looks back. The field's practitioners hold real expertise and defend it entirely by method and argument, because there is no record of having been right, anywhere, for anyone.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Predictions are written as qualitative findings rather than as falsifiable statements. Verification needs client outcome data that nobody has arranged access to. Scoring implies being wrong sometimes, publicly. And the engagement ends before any of it is possible.

## What to Build
State the predictions falsifiably, then go and check them. Express each finding as a prediction with an observable consequence, which is the core — a finding that cannot be wrong cannot be evidence, and most are currently written that way. Record the predictions at delivery in a structured form, since that is the only moment they exist unambiguously. Agree with the client which are checkable and how, at kickoff rather than afterwards. Verify against post-release behaviour where access allows, and against the client's own report where it does not. Score calibration rather than a hit rate, because a researcher who is right about the severe problems and uncertain about the marginal ones is exactly what good practice looks like. Distinguish predictions that were acted on from those that were not, since an unacted prediction cannot be scored the same way. Accumulate across studies to produce an agency-level accuracy record, which is the asset. Report which kinds of prediction the method is reliable for and which it is not, which is genuinely useful to practitioners. Publish method-level findings without client specifics, which is how the discipline improves rather than one firm. And use the record commercially, since a demonstrated track record is the answer to the budget conversation the field keeps losing.

## Target Customer
UX research agencies and consultancies, in-house research functions, research professional bodies, and measurement vendors.

## Impact If Built
A finding that cannot be wrong cannot be evidence, and most are written that way. Stating findings as falsifiable predictions and scoring calibration gives a discipline its first record of being right.

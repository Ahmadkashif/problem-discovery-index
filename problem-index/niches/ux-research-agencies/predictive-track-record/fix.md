# Writing the Prediction Down

**Niche:** [[niches/ux-research-agencies/predictive-track-record/profile|Predictive Track Record]]
**Industry:** [[industries/ux-research-agencies|UX Research Agencies]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The report says users found the flow confusing, which cannot be checked against anything.
**Tags:** #quick-win #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #confidence-intervals #hypothesis-testing #workflow-orchestration #worker-facing #data-integration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to show that its predictions about user behaviour were correct, in a discipline that has never recorded one — and whoever builds that record takes the account.

## The Problem
Findings are written in a form that cannot be wrong. Users found the flow confusing; the concept was not well understood; participants expected something different. Each describes what happened in the sessions and implies something about the product, without ever stating what would be observed if the finding is correct. So even a client who wanted to check could not, and the researcher has no way of knowing whether their judgement is good.

## Why It's Still Broken
The reporting convention is descriptive — a finding phrased as a description of eight sessions makes no claim that reality could contradict, so nothing downstream can ever test it. Falsifiable phrasing feels overclaiming. Nobody asks for it. And the deck's job is to persuade rather than to commit.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Add the observable consequence to each finding. Write each finding with a stated expectation — what should be visible in the product's data if this is right — which is the fix and is one line per finding. State the direction and rough magnitude rather than only the direction, since a direction is unfalsifiable in practice. Attach the number of participants supporting each finding, which most reports omit. Separate observation from inference explicitly, as they carry different weight and are conflated. Note which findings are checkable and which are not, and say why. Record the predictions somewhere retrievable rather than only in the deck. Ask the client which they intend to act on, which is free and determines what can be scored later. Review past reports against what the client subsequently reported, even informally, which is the cheapest start available. Keep the phrasing in the discipline's voice rather than importing a statistical register that practitioners will reject. And treat a wrong prediction as information rather than as a failure, which is the cultural change the practice needs.

## Who Feels the Pain
Researchers with no way to know whether they are good at this; clients unable to evaluate what they bought; the discipline, losing budget arguments it cannot settle; and every study that could have learned from the last one.

## Impact If Fixed
A finding phrased as a description of eight sessions makes no claim that reality could contradict, so nothing downstream can ever test it. One line of observable consequence per finding is the whole change.

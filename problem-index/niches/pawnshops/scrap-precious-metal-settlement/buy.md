# Commodity Price-Lock Tools Sized for a Pawn Lot

**Niche:** [[niches/pawnshops/scrap-precious-metal-settlement/profile|Scrap Precious-Metal Lots & Refiner Settlement]]
**Industry:** [[industries/pawnshops|Pawnshops]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Bullion dealers and jewellery manufacturers lock metal prices and hedge exposure as a matter of course; a pawnshop holding a growing scrap pile is simply long gold.
**Tags:** #time-series-forecasting #monte-carlo-methods #probability-distributions #evaluation-metrics #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to settle a pawnbroker's scrap gold and silver lots for the most metal at the best moment — accurate karat sorting before shipment, an assay the shop can check, and timing against spot — and whoever closes the gap between the shop's estimate and the refiner's settlement takes the account.

## The Problem
Between buying gold over the counter and settling with the refiner, the shop carries the price risk — and in 2026 spot fell ~22% from its January record by early October.

## What Already Exists
Refiner and bullion-dealer price-lock (fixing at the time of sale for later delivery), and hedging through futures or forwards used by jewellery manufacturers and dealers.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs: lots of a few kilograms of mixed karat with uncertain fine content, not standard bars; a lock sized to expected fine weight with a tolerance for assay variance; a rule for when to lock tied to the shop's buy prices, so the margin on purchases is fixed the day they are made; and reporting in the POS rather than a trading screen.

## Target Customer
Chain finance and jewellery teams; large independent gold buyers.

## Impact If Solved
The margin on gold bought over the counter is fixed when the gold is bought, rather than left to the spot price weeks later.

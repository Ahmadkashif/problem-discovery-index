# Scrap Precious-Metal Lots & Refiner Settlement

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/pawnshops|Pawnshops]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to settle a pawnbroker's scrap gold and silver lots for the most metal at the best moment — accurate karat sorting before shipment, an assay the shop can check, and timing against spot — and whoever closes the gap between the shop's estimate and the refiner's settlement takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$150–250M a year in refining charges, payable deductions and timing losses on US pawn scrap (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~3% of revenue (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — pieces tested by hand, lots logged on paper or spreadsheets, settlements by refiner statement
**Target Buyer:** Director of Jewellery at a chain; owner-pawnbroker
**Automation Potential:** Medium — reconciliation and timing are automatable; testing is physical

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Gold that will not sell at retail, and gold bought over the counter, is sold to a refiner by weight and assay. The refiner melts the lot, assays it, deducts refining charges and pays a percentage of the fine metal at spot on the settlement date or a locked price. With gold at or near records in 2025–26, this channel grew sharply: EZCORP's US scrap sales rose 58% to $85.7M in fiscal 2025, and global gold recycling reached 1,404 tonnes in 2025, a 13-year high (Metals Focus). The shop's money depends on its own karat sorting and on when it sells.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Acid kits, electronic testers and XRF at chain hubs; legal-for-trade scales; refiner portals and statements; spot feeds. The gaps: no piece-level record joined to the lot assay, so shortfalls cannot be traced; no reconciliation of expected against settled fine weight; and sell timing set by habit or cash need rather than a rule.

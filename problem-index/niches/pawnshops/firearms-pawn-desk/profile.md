# Firearms Pawn & Sales Desk

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/pawnshops|Pawnshops]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep every firearm pawn, redemption and sale inspection-clean in the acquisition-and-disposition record and on the Form 4473, and whoever makes zero-error recordkeeping cost the counter no extra time takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$60–120M a year in firearms compliance software, electronic records and compliance services sold to pawn FFLs (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~1–2% of revenue (estimate); firearms are a larger share of collateral and sales in many rural and Southern stores
**Digital Adoption:** Low — paper Forms 4473 remain common and bound books are often paper or a separate system from the POS
**Target Buyer:** Owner holding the federal firearms licence; FFL compliance manager at a chain
**Automation Potential:** Medium — records can be generated and reconciled; the NICS check and the counter judgement cannot

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A pawnshop that takes guns must hold a federal firearms licence and keep a permanent record of every receipt and disposition. ATF treats the redemption of a pawned gun as a disposition: each one needs a Form 4473 and a NICS background check, even for a customer who has redeemed before, and a denied customer cannot have their own gun back. State waiting periods and local rules add to it, and ATF inspection findings can lead to licence revocation. No other pawn category carries a federal licence of its own.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Electronic bound-book and 4473 software (FastBound, Orchid eBound and others), POS firearms modules, ATF's NICS E-Check, and paper. The gaps: the POS, the bound book and the 4473 stack are three records reconciled by hand; pawn intake and redemption do not map cleanly to retail-sale software; and errors are found at inspection rather than at the counter.

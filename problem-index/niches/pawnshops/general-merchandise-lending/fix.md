# The Locked Phone Taken as Collateral

**Niche:** [[niches/pawnshops/general-merchandise-lending/profile|General Merchandise Lending]]
**Industry:** [[industries/pawnshops|Pawnshops]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A phone or laptop still tied to someone's account looks perfect at the counter and is worth nothing at forfeit.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #evaluation-metrics #compliance #worker-facing #quick-win #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to price a used branded item — a drill kit, a games console, a guitar, a laptop — from its exact model and condition against what that model actually resells for locally, in seconds at the counter, and whoever prices it closest to its realised resale takes the account.

## The Problem
Activation locks and carrier blacklists make a device unusable to anyone but the account holder. A rushed clerk lends on a phone that powers on and looks clean, the customer never returns, and the forfeit cannot be sold. Locked devices are also a common sign of theft.

## Why It's Still Broken
The checks exist but sit outside the ticket flow: the clerk must remember to ask the customer to sign out, or run a separate blacklist lookup, and nothing stops the ticket printing if they forget.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Make the lock and blacklist check a required step for device categories: the ticket cannot print until the clerk confirms the device was signed out in front of them and the IMEI or serial has been checked, with the result stored on the ticket.

## Who Feels the Pain
Clerks blamed for the write-off; owners holding unsaleable devices; police when a locked device turns out stolen.

## Impact If Fixed
A ten-second gate removes one of the most common avoidable losses on electronics and screens out a share of stolen devices at the door.

# Retail Analytics Rebuilt Around the Pawn Ticket

**Niche:** [[niches/pawnshops/independent-single-store-pawnbrokers/profile|Independent Single-Store Pawnbrokers]]
**Industry:** [[industries/pawnshops|Pawnshops]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Small-retailer analytics tools explain sales and stock; a pawnshop's economics run through loans, fees, renewals and forfeits that those tools cannot see.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #time-series-forecasting #data-integration #evaluation-metrics #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to give a one-store owner chain-grade loss control — which clerks, categories and loan sizes lose money at forfeit — from the POS data the owner already has, and whoever delivers it without the owner needing an analyst takes the account.

## The Problem
Owners who try a generic dashboard find it reports sales and margin but treats pawn loans as cash out and forfeits as stock appearing from nowhere.

## What Already Exists
Analytics built into retail POS platforms (Lightspeed, Square, Shopify), small-business BI tools and accounting dashboards; see [[niches/independent-retailers/retail-pos-platform-analytics/profile|Retail POS Platform Analytics]] for the data those platforms hold.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs: the loan ledger as the core object (principal, fees accrued, maturity, grace, renewals); forfeits entering stock at their loan cost with the original ticket linked; fee income separated from merchandise margin; aged inventory measured from forfeit date; police holds and statutory holds as stock that cannot be sold; and ratios a pawnbroker uses — loan balance growth, forfeit rate, loss on forfeit, fee yield.

## Target Customer
Independent owners and small multi-store groups already running a pawn POS.

## Impact If Solved
An owner gets the numbers that run a pawnshop without paying for custom reporting.

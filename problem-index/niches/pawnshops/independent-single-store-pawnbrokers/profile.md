# Independent Single-Store Pawnbrokers

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/pawnshops|Pawnshops]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to give a one-store owner chain-grade loss control — which clerks, categories and loan sizes lose money at forfeit — from the POS data the owner already has, and whoever delivers it without the owner needing an analyst takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$40–80M a year in software, analytics and services bought by independent pawnshops (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~55–65% of revenue from ~80% of shops (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — a vendor POS for tickets and police uploads, accounting software, and the owner's memory
**Target Buyer:** Owner-pawnbroker
**Automation Potential:** Medium — the reports are computable from data the owner holds; acting on them is judgement

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
FirstCash runs 1,207 US stores and EZCORP 545; most of the rest — the large majority of ~10,000 shops — are owner-operated, one or a few stores. The owner is the senior appraiser, the compliance officer, the FFL holder and the buyer of last resort, with no district manager reviewing forfeits and no central team pricing categories. Chains know which stores and categories lose money on forfeits; independents find out at year-end inventory or tax time.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Bravo Store Systems' pawn POS (and its older PawnMaster Classic), with built-in reports; QuickBooks; spreadsheets; peer conversations at National Pawnbrokers Association and state association meetings. The gaps: no loss-on-forfeit analysis by clerk, category or loan-to-value band; no benchmark against peer shops; no early warning when a category's realised prices drop; and reports that answer accounting questions rather than lending ones.

# The Fake That Passed the Acid Test

**Niche:** [[niches/pawnshops/gold-and-jewellery-lending/profile|Gold & Jewellery Lending]]
**Industry:** [[industries/pawnshops|Pawnshops]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Heavily plated and tungsten-cored fakes pass the surface tests a busy counter relies on, and the loss surfaces weeks later at the refiner with no record of which tests were run.
**Tags:** #random-forests #cnns #evaluation-metrics #worker-facing #quick-win #compliance
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to value gold and jewellery collateral against the live spot price — karat, net weight, premium over melt and the risk of a plated or tungsten-filled fake — inside the few minutes a customer will wait, and whoever values it most accurately takes the account.

## The Problem
Thick gold plating survives a nitric acid scratch test, and tungsten has nearly the density of gold, so a cored bar or bracelet passes both the acid and the weight check. Pawnbrokers in Spokane and elsewhere have warned of fake gold sold through parking-lot scams and then pawned. The store finds out when the piece forfeits and the refiner's assay comes back short, or when a sharper buyer cuts into it.

## Why It's Still Broken
Testing is done by habit and recorded not at all. The POS records "14K" whether the clerk ran one test or four, so nobody can see which tests catch which fakes, which clerks skip steps, or which pieces should have gone to the XRF at the district store.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A testing protocol recorded per piece: which tests were run, their results, a photograph of the stamp, and an escalation rule — pieces above a weight or value threshold, unstamped pieces and anything that fails a cross-check go to XRF or a cut test before money is lent. Feed refiner assay shortfalls back to the piece and the clerk.

## Who Feels the Pain
Clerks who carry the loss; owners writing off forfeits; jewellery buyers explaining assay shortfalls.

## Impact If Fixed
A few seconds of recording per piece turns fake detection from individual memory into a store-wide protocol with evidence, and the costliest single error at the jewellery counter becomes rarer.

# Gold-Loan Collateral Monitoring Fitted to a Thirty-Day Ticket

**Niche:** [[niches/pawnshops/gold-and-jewellery-lending/profile|Gold & Jewellery Lending]]
**Industry:** [[industries/pawnshops|Pawnshops]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Gold-loan lenders elsewhere already run loan-to-value monitoring on jewellery collateral at enormous scale; a US pawnshop needs the same discipline without margin calls, credit files or a regulator-set LTV.
**Tags:** #time-series-forecasting #bayesian-inference #evaluation-metrics #feature-engineering #compliance #data-integration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to value gold and jewellery collateral against the live spot price — karat, net weight, premium over melt and the risk of a plated or tungsten-filled fake — inside the few minutes a customer will wait, and whoever values it most accurately takes the account.

## The Problem
The jewellery book behaves like a collateralised lending portfolio but is managed ticket by ticket. When spot moves, the owner has no tool that shows loan-to-value across the book or that adjusts lending percentages for new tickets automatically.

## What Already Exists
India's gold-loan lenders (Muthoot and Manappuram among the largest) run jewellery loans under a regulator-set loan-to-value cap (75% by recalled rule) with systems that value collateral at intake by karat and weight and monitor LTV against the gold price across millions of loans. Bank collateral-management systems do the same for securities and commodities.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs: a 30-day maturity and state grace period rather than a term loan; no margin call, because a US pawn customer cannot be asked to top up and simply forfeits; per-piece karat uncertainty from acid or electronic testing rather than assay; a retail premium over melt for branded and diamond pieces that the Indian model largely ignores; state fee caps such as Florida's 25% per 30 days in the economics; and outputs that change the lending guide at the counter the next morning rather than a risk report.

## Target Customer
Chief lending officers and heads of jewellery at US pawn chains; finance teams at regional pawn groups with bank credit lines.

## Impact If Solved
A proven discipline from a larger gold-lending market, adapted to a ticket that ends in forfeiture rather than collection, gives US operators a daily view of jewellery exposure without building it from scratch.

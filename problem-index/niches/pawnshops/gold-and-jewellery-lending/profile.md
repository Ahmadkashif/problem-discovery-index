# Gold & Jewellery Lending

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/pawnshops|Pawnshops]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to value gold and jewellery collateral against the live spot price — karat, net weight, premium over melt and the risk of a plated or tungsten-filled fake — inside the few minutes a customer will wait, and whoever values it most accurately takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$2.5–3.5B of US pawn revenue from jewellery-backed loans, jewellery retail and scrap (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~40–50% of revenue (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low–Medium — testers and spot feeds are digital; the valuation and the premium over melt are judged by eye and hand
**Target Buyer:** Owner-pawnbroker; Director of Jewellery Operations at a chain
**Automation Potential:** Medium — the melt floor is arithmetic; authenticity and the resale premium need judgement

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Gold is the one pawn collateral with a public daily price. A loan secured by the melt value of correctly identified metal is close to riskless, so the risk sits in two places: identification (karat, net weight after stones, plated or cored fakes) and the premium over melt that branded, designer and diamond pieces may or may not fetch at retail. The spot price moves the whole book at once — it reached a record close of $5,318 an ounce in January 2026 and was ~22% lower in early October — so loans written at the peak can sit above melt after a fall, and the customer, unlike a margin borrower, simply walks away.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Acid kits, touchstones, electronic testers and, at chains, XRF analysers (Thermo Niton, Bruker, SciAps); spot feeds; the Rapaport Price List for diamonds, which sells a $1,000-a-year pawnbroker plan; the POS's metal and karat fields. The gaps: no record of the premium over melt each store actually realises by style or brand; no daily view of the jewellery book's loan-to-melt ratio against live spot; diamond values taken off an asking-price list with ad hoc discounts; and no link from the refiner's assay back to the clerk's karat call.

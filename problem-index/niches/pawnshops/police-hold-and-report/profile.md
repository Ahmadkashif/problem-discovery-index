# Police Hold-and-Report Compliance

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/pawnshops|Pawnshops]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get every transaction reported in each jurisdiction's required form by its deadline, and every hold released on its exact date, straight from the pawn ticket, and whoever makes that automatic across every location's rules takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$150–250M a year in reporting-system fees, compliance software and clerk time across US pawnshops (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~2–3% of revenue (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low–Medium — uploads are electronic in most places, but thumbprints, paper hold tags, police hold letters and ordinance tracking are manual
**Target Buyer:** Owner-pawnbroker; compliance manager at a multi-county chain
**Automation Potential:** High for filing and validation, Medium for police holds

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Reporting rules are set by state statute and city or county ordinance, not by a national regulator. Florida's Act alone specifies a thumbprint, ID details and a full description including serials and metal content, delivery by the end of each business day or electronically, and a 30-day hold on purchased and pledged goods. Other jurisdictions differ on fields, holds and photographs. Police holds on specific items come on top. The penalty for getting it wrong is a citation or a licence action, and the cost of being slow is sellable days lost.

## Current Tools & Gaps
LeadsOnline takes nightly uploads in all 50 states and lets agencies search 1.2+ billion transactions; BWI's RAPID serves other jurisdictions; pawn POS systems export the required fields. The gaps: no executable rulebook per location; required fields validated after the fact, if at all; sellable dates computed by hand; police holds tracked on paper tags and letters; and no single view of compliance for an operator spanning several jurisdictions.

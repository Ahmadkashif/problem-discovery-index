# The Aged Stock Nobody Marked Down

**Niche:** [[niches/pawnshops/forfeit-disposition/profile|Forfeit Disposition]]
**Industry:** [[industries/pawnshops|Pawnshops]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Forfeited items sit on shelves past the point where any buyer will pay the tag price, tying up cash the store needs for new loans.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #survival-analysis #evaluation-metrics #quick-win #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is "every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn a forfeited item into the most cash in the fewest days" — but for precious metal the contest is sorting and timing scrap lots against spot and a refiner's assay, and for everything else it is identifying, pricing and listing one-of-one used goods to buyers beyond the store; different inputs, clocks and winners. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## The Problem
Chains track aged general merchandise as a share of stock — EZCORP reported 2.2% for its US stores in fiscal 2025 — because it is cash that cannot be lent. Independents rarely measure it at all.

## Why It's Still Broken
Markdowns depend on someone walking the floor; the POS knows each item's forfeit date but does not prompt action.

## What a Fix Looks Like
An aged-stock list each week from the POS, sorted by days since forfeit against the category's typical sale time, with a recommended action per item: reprice, list online, wholesale or scrap.

## Who Feels the Pain
Store managers short of lending cash; owners carrying dead stock at loan cost.

## Impact If Fixed
Aged stock turns back into lending capital within weeks rather than quarters.

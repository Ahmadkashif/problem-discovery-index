# Forfeit Disposition

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/pawnshops|Pawnshops]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is "every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn a forfeited item into the most cash in the fewest days" — but for precious metal the contest is sorting and timing scrap lots against spot and a refiner's assay, and for everything else it is identifying, pricing and listing one-of-one used goods to buyers beyond the store; different inputs, clocks and winners. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$300–500M a year in refining spreads, marketplace fees, listing tools and markdown losses on US pawn forfeits (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~5% of revenue (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — online channels are digital; channel and timing decisions are habit
**Target Buyer:** VP of Merchandising at a chain; owner-pawnbroker
**Automation Potential:** High — routing, listing, repricing and lot assembly are repeatable decisions on recorded data

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
About a third of pawn loans forfeit (33.4% in Oregon's 2025 report), and purchases add to the same stock. Every forfeit must become cash: on the floor, online, in a wholesale lot or at the refiner. The chains report the result directly — EZCORP's US merchandise margin was 37% and inventory turned 2.2 times in fiscal 2025, down from 2.6, while its US scrap jewellery sales rose 58% to $85.7M on the gold price. Disposition is where valuation errors at the counter become realised losses.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Store retail, pawn POS online storefronts, eBay, GunBroker, Reverb, wholesale buyers and precious-metal refiners. The gaps: no per-item decision on channel; no markdown logic built for single units; scrap timing by feel; and no feedback from realised disposition values to the lending guide.

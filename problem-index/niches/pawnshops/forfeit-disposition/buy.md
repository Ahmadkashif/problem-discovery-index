# Markdown Optimisation Rebuilt for One-of-One Stock

**Niche:** [[niches/pawnshops/forfeit-disposition/profile|Forfeit Disposition]]
**Industry:** [[industries/pawnshops|Pawnshops]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Retail markdown optimisation estimates demand per stock-keeping unit across many identical units; a pawnshop has one unit of almost everything.
**Tags:** #bayesian-inference #time-series-forecasting #gradient-boosting #evaluation-metrics #automation #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is "every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn a forfeited item into the most cash in the fewest days" — but for precious metal the contest is sorting and timing scrap lots against spot and a refiner's assay, and for everything else it is identifying, pricing and listing one-of-one used goods to buyers beyond the store; different inputs, clocks and winners. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## The Problem
Pawn stores mark down on fixed schedules — a percentage off after so many days — regardless of whether the item is a fast seller priced too high or a slow one priced right.

## What Already Exists
Markdown and price optimisation software for chains of apparel and general retailers (Revionics and similar), which model price elasticity per product from sales of many units.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs: demand pooled across similar models, categories and stores because each item is unique; condition grade as a price driver; a cost basis equal to the forfeited loan, not a purchase cost; a melt-value floor for precious metal; channel choice inside the optimisation; and hold periods that delay the first possible sale.

## Target Customer
Chain merchandising and pricing teams.

## Impact If Solved
Prices fall when the evidence says so and not before, recovering margin on items that would have sold at a higher price and clearing ones that never would.

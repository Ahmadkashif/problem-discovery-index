# Online Resale of Forfeits

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/pawnshops|Pawnshops]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get a forfeited one-of-one item identified, photographed, priced and listed to buyers beyond the store in minutes rather than a quarter-hour, and whoever lists the most forfeits at retail rather than wholesale takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$100–200M a year in marketplace fees, listing software and staff time on US pawn online sales (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~2% of revenue (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium–High — marketplaces are digital; listing work is manual
**Target Buyer:** E-commerce manager at a chain; owner-pawnbroker
**Automation Potential:** High

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The long tail of forfeits — instruments, vintage audio, collectibles, designer goods, specialist tools — sells for more to national buyers than to the store's walk-in traffic, but each item needs listing work that costs more than a cheap item is worth. The pawnbroker's stock also carries legal state: statutory holds, possible police recalls, firearms that can ship only to a licensed dealer.

## Current Tools & Gaps
eBay with photo-to-listing drafting, GunBroker for firearms, Reverb for instruments, POS-integrated storefronts, multichannel listing tools. The gaps: listing time per item; prices from asking rather than sold data; no automatic withdrawal when an item sells in store or a police hold lands; and no rule for which items are worth listing at all.

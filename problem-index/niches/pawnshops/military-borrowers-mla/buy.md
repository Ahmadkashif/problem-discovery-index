# Bank Covered-Borrower Checks Adapted to the Pawn Counter

**Niche:** [[niches/pawnshops/military-borrowers-mla/profile|Military Service-Member Borrowers]]
**Industry:** [[industries/pawnshops|Pawnshops]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Banks and card issuers screen every credit application for covered-borrower status inside their origination systems; a pawn counter has no credit pull to hang the check on.
**Tags:** #compliance #workflow-orchestration #automation #data-integration #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to lend to a covered service member inside the Military Lending Act's 36% MAPR cap and still make a small ticket pay, and whoever verifies covered status and prices the ticket inside the cap without losing money takes the account.

## The Problem
Pawn clerks either ask customers whether they are in the military, which is unreliable, or run a separate lookup inconsistently.

## What Already Exists
Bank and card origination systems check covered status at application through the Department of Defense database or the MLA indicator that consumer reporting agencies return with a credit report (recalled), and store the result as the safe-harbour record.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs: a check run without a credit report, from ID data the police form already captures; a check at every renewal and extension, since status changes and each renewal is new credit; routing a covered customer to the compliant product rather than a decline; the MAPR computed on pawn fees, storage and any other charges; and the result stored on the ticket for examination.

## Target Customer
Chain compliance officers; POS vendors serving stores near installations.

## Impact If Solved
Covered status is checked the same way on every ticket and renewal, with a record that stands up, and the counter routes rather than refuses.

# The Service Member Turned Away at the Counter

**Niche:** [[niches/pawnshops/military-borrowers-mla/profile|Military Service-Member Borrowers]]
**Industry:** [[industries/pawnshops|Pawnshops]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Unsure of the rules, clerks near bases decline anyone who looks military, which loses lawful business and leaves service members with worse options.
**Tags:** #compliance #workflow-orchestration #worker-facing #quick-win #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to lend to a covered service member inside the Military Lending Act's 36% MAPR cap and still make a small ticket pay, and whoever verifies covered status and prices the ticket inside the cap without losing money takes the account.

## The Problem
The MLA covers active-duty members and dependants, not veterans or most reservists off orders, and outright purchases are not credit. Clerks who do not know the boundaries refuse broadly, including customers the store could lawfully serve with a standard ticket or a purchase.

## Why It's Still Broken
Store policy is written as "do not lend to military" because a precise rule needs a status check and a compliant product that most stores lack.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A counter flow: check status from the customer's ID; if not covered, proceed normally; if covered, offer the compliant ticket or an outright purchase, with the reason recorded.

## Who Feels the Pain
Service members needing cash; clerks guessing at federal law; stores near installations losing customers.

## Impact If Fixed
Lawful business is served, covered borrowers get a compliant option, and clerks stop making federal-law calls on appearance.

# A Ticket That Pays Inside the Cap

**Niche:** [[niches/pawnshops/military-borrowers-mla/profile|Military Service-Member Borrowers]]
**Industry:** [[industries/pawnshops|Pawnshops]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** At a 36% annual rate a $300 pawn loan earns about $9 a month, so the only way to serve covered borrowers profitably is a ticket designed around collateral risk and cost to serve from the start.
**Tags:** #gradient-boosting #survival-analysis #optimization-fundamentals #compliance #evaluation-metrics #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to lend to a covered service member inside the Military Lending Act's 36% MAPR cap and still make a small ticket pay, and whoever verifies covered status and prices the ticket inside the cap without losing money takes the account.

## The Problem
Pawn economics rest on fees that are high relative to principal because tickets are small and short. Cut the fee to a 36% MAPR and most tickets lose money once handling, storage and forfeit losses are counted, so operators decline covered borrowers.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The covered population is a small share of most stores' customers, the compliance risk of getting the MAPR calculation wrong is high, and until recently the largest chain did not offer a compliant product at all.

## What to Build
A product model that uses the operator's own history to find where a 36% ticket pays: collateral types with high melt or resale floors (gold above all), larger minimum loan sizes, lower loan-to-value, longer terms that spread handling cost, and customers with strong redemption histories. Price each ticket inside the cap with the MAPR computed and stored, and re-check covered status at each renewal.

## Target Customer
Compliance and product leaders at chains with stores near installations; independents in base towns.

## Impact If Built
Service members get access to a lawful small-dollar option backed by their own property, and operators near bases serve a customer group they now turn away.

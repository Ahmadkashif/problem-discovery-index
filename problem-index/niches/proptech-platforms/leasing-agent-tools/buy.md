# Speed-to-Lead Practice From Every Other Consumer Industry

**One-liner:** Speed-to-lead is one of the most thoroughly studied and most commoditised disciplines in consumer sales, with published response-time curves and off-the-shelf tooling, and rental leasing responds on a next-business-day rhythm.

**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in leasing software is fighting to answer, qualify and book a prospect before they lease somewhere else — and whoever gets time-to-first-response and tour-booking rate best takes the account.

## The Problem
Insurance, automotive, mortgage and home services all built their lead handling around a well-documented finding: response within minutes converts at a multiple of response within hours, and the curve falls steeply. They instrumented it, routed around it and staffed for it. Rental leasing, where the prospect's decision window is days and the value of a lease is a year of revenue, handles leads on an office-hours cadence with a CRM that logs them.

## What Already Exists
Lead routing and instant-response tooling is a large commercial category. Automated dialling, SMS and messaging automation are commodity. Round-robin and availability-based routing across a team is standard. Response-time analytics is built into every sales platform. The empirical literature on response-time effects is public and substantial. Rental leasing needs to import a practice, not invent one.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to the property's constraints and to fair housing. It requires: (1) routing across a portfolio rather than a property, so that an enquiry arriving while the on-site agent is touring reaches a centralised or neighbouring agent immediately — this single change addresses most of the latency and needs no automation at all; (2) after-hours coverage treated as a requirement rather than an edge case, since prospects search in the evening and properties close at five; (3) identical treatment across prospects enforced structurally, because a routing or prioritisation rule that correlates with a protected characteristic is a fair housing violation regardless of intent, and speed-to-lead tooling from other industries routinely prioritises by predicted value in ways that would be unlawful here; (4) response quality measured alongside speed, since an instant unhelpful reply converts no better than a slow one; and (5) availability accuracy as a precondition, because the fastest response in the world is worthless if it offers a unit that was leased yesterday.

## Target Customer
Property operators with staffed on-site leasing, centralised leasing teams, and the leasing CRM vendors who currently log leads rather than route them.

## Impact If Solved
Portfolio-level routing and after-hours coverage are organisational changes supported by commodity tooling and address the majority of the latency loss before any automation is involved. The fair housing constraint is the genuine adaptation and must be designed in rather than bolted on, since the imported practice carries assumptions that do not survive contact with housing law.

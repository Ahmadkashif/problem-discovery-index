# Screening Compliance Content

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/proptech-platforms|Proptech Platforms]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in tenant screening is fighting to apply the criteria that are lawful in this specific city, for this specific property, at the moment of application — and whoever keeps that rule set correct takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$340M US spend on screening compliance content and configuration within a larger screening market
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~3% of proptech revenue, with legal exposure far out of proportion to its size
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — screening is digitised and the rules governing it are configured by hand
**Target Buyer:** Compliance and content teams at screening providers and platform vendors; operators bear the consequences
**Automation Potential:** Very High — rule change detection across published ordinances is a monitoring problem with a bounded corpus

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
What a landlord may lawfully consider when evaluating an applicant now differs by state, county and city, and the divergence has grown rapidly. Jurisdictions have restricted or sequenced the use of criminal history, prohibited discrimination on source of income so that housing vouchers must be accepted, capped application fees, required individualised assessment rather than blanket denial, and mandated specific adverse action procedures. A property in one city and a property twenty minutes away may lawfully apply different criteria. The screening products ship a national default and a configuration screen, and the configuration is performed by a regional manager who is not a lawyer, once, at setup. The exposure is asymmetric and severe: a misconfigured criterion applied across hundreds of applications is a pattern, and patterns are what fair housing enforcement and private litigation are built on.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Screening is largely performed by consumer reporting agencies integrated into the platforms, which supply the data and leave the decision criteria to the operator. Platforms provide criteria configuration per property with varying granularity. Some vendors offer compliance guidance as documentation. Nothing maintains the rules as executable content per jurisdiction, nothing detects when a jurisdiction's ordinance changes, and nothing audits an operator's configured criteria against what is currently lawful where each property sits. The adverse action process — notice content, timing, the applicant's right to dispute — is templated nationally when several jurisdictions now require more. The content maintenance problem is identical in shape to court rules content and is solved the same way, and nobody is solving it.

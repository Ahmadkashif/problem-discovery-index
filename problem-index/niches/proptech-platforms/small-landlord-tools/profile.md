# Small Landlord Tools

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/proptech-platforms|Proptech Platforms]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor selling to small landlords is fighting to get a person with four units from an empty unit to a compliant lease, a working ledger and a filed tax return without hiring anyone — and whoever makes that path unassisted takes the segment.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$900M US software spend across independent landlords with 1-20 units
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~8% of proptech revenue, against a very large share of US rental housing stock
**Digital Adoption:** Low — a substantial share of this population runs on a spreadsheet, a personal bank account and a lease template found online
**Target Buyer:** The landlord, who is frequently not a professional and often has another full-time job
**Automation Potential:** Very High — this population cannot afford staff for anything, which makes every administrative task a candidate

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Independent landlords with a handful of units own a very large share of American rental housing, and the software industry has largely passed them over in favour of institutional customers with procurement budgets. The defining constraint is that there is nobody else: no property manager, no bookkeeper, no lawyer, no maintenance staff. A landlord with four units handles leasing, screening, rent collection, maintenance dispatch, accounting, tax and every compliance obligation personally, in evenings, usually without training. The consequences fall on both sides. Landlords make expensive mistakes — non-compliant leases, mishandled security deposits, screening practices that violate local ordinances they have never heard of — and residents of small landlords consequently have less consistent experiences than residents of professionally managed properties, which is a fair-housing and habitability matter as much as a software one.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Avail, TurboTenant, Innago, RentRedi and Zillow's rental tools serve this segment, largely free or cheap, monetised through screening fees, payments and insurance. They handle listing, application, screening and rent collection adequately. The gaps are in everything that requires judgement: lease documents are templates that may or may not reflect the jurisdiction's current requirements, security deposit handling rules — which differ by state on interest, separate accounts, timelines and itemisation — are not enforced by the product, maintenance is a list of contacts, and accounting is a transaction feed rather than a schedule the landlord can file from. The product does the easy parts and leaves the parts where an error is expensive.

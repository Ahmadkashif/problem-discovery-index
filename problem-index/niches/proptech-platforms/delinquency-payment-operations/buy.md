# Payment Plan Design From Consumer Credit Practice

**One-liner:** Consumer credit has decades of practice and published evidence on which hardship arrangements are actually completed, and rental payment plans are invented on the spot by a site manager who is guessing.

**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in rental payment operations is fighting to identify a household moving into arrears early enough that a payment plan or an assistance referral still works — and whoever finds them earliest, without turning the capability into a screening tool, takes the account.

## The Problem
A household is two months behind. The site manager offers a plan: catch up over three months, on top of current rent. The household agrees because agreeing is the only option offered, and defaults in week five because the plan required them to pay 150% of rent for three months on the income that could not cover 100% of it. The plan was doomed at the moment it was written, everyone involved half-knew it, and no one had any basis for writing a better one.

## What Already Exists
Hardship arrangement design is well developed in consumer credit and in utility arrears management, with substantial published evidence on completion rates by arrangement structure, affordability assessment methods, and the effect of arrangement length and instalment size on default. Regulated sectors in several countries have codified affordability-based arrangement requirements. Utility arrears programmes in the US operate at scale with documented practice. The methods and the evidence are public.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to rental arrears, which differ from credit arrears in that the household is also consuming the service that is generating the debt. It requires: (1) affordability assessed on what the household can actually pay alongside ongoing rent, rather than on arithmetic that divides arrears by a number of months; (2) arrangement structures fitted to what the operator's own history shows is completed — this is estimable from any operator with a meaningful arrears history and has never been estimated; (3) explicit integration with assistance programmes, since the right plan frequently involves a payment from a programme and structuring around it is different from structuring without it; (4) legal sequence interaction handled carefully, because a plan that pauses a notice period and then defaults leaves the household in a worse position than if it had never been offered, and the product should make that trade explicit rather than hide it; and (5) outcome measurement by arrangement type, which is what turns this from a template into a practice that improves.

## Target Customer
Property operators managing arrears at scale, assistance programme administrators structuring payments, and tenant support and legal aid organisations negotiating on the household's behalf.

## Impact If Solved
Plans designed for affordability complete at materially higher rates than plans designed by dividing arrears by three, which is better for the operator's recovery and decisive for the household. The evidence base and the methods are imported rather than invented; what has to be built is the fit to rental arrears and the honest accounting of what a defaulted plan costs the resident.

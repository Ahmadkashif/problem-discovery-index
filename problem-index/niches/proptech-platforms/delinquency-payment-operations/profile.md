# Delinquency & Payment Operations

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/proptech-platforms|Proptech Platforms]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in rental payment operations is fighting to identify a household moving into arrears early enough that a payment plan or an assistance referral still works — and whoever finds them earliest, without turning the capability into a screening tool, takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$580M US spend across rent payment processing, collections operations and delinquency management
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~5% of proptech revenue, with payment processing as a large ancillary revenue line
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — payments are digital, the delinquency process is a notice calendar
**Target Buyer:** Operations and asset management leaders at property operators; also housing assistance programmes and tenant support organisations
**Automation Potential:** High — the payment signal is continuous and the interventions that work are well documented

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Rent delinquency is managed as a calendar of notices: a late fee on the sixth, a notice on the tenth, a filing at some point after that. The process is designed around a legal sequence rather than around what actually resolves the situation, which in a large share of cases is a payment plan, a partial payment arrangement, or a referral to an emergency rental assistance programme that exists and that the household does not know about. The operator's data shows the trajectory well before the sequence starts — a household that paid on the first for two years starts paying on the eighth, then in two instalments, then late. That trajectory is a signal that intervention would work, and it is used for nothing, because nobody built the capability and because the process it would feed is a legal one rather than a supportive one.

This niche carries a real misuse risk and it should be stated plainly: the same trajectory signal, used at screening or renewal rather than for intervention, becomes a tool for excluding households likely to struggle. That use would be harmful, is in several respects legally hazardous, and is not what any of the notes below propose. The capability is worth building because early intervention keeps households housed and keeps operators out of a costly and slow eviction process — and it is worth building with explicit constraints on where the signal may be used.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Payment processing is universal and has become a significant ancillary revenue line, including fees charged to residents for certain payment methods. Delinquency modules manage the notice sequence and the legal calendar. Third-party collections and eviction filing services integrate. Emergency rental assistance programmes exist at state and local level with varying application processes and low awareness among the households that qualify. The gaps: nothing forecasts a household's arrears trajectory for intervention purposes, payment plans are offered ad hoc by site staff with no basis for what terms will succeed, assistance programme eligibility and application status are not tracked anywhere in the operator's system, and nobody measures which interventions actually resolve arrears versus merely delay a filing.

# Maintenance & Turn Operations

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/proptech-platforms|Proptech Platforms]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is that every competitor is fighting to take maintenance decisions off a busy site manager's desk — but triaging a resident's complaint and sequencing a unit turn are different problems with different data. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$2.3B US spend across maintenance management, vendor marketplaces and turn operations software
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~21% of proptech revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Medium-High — work order systems are universal; everything above them is manual
**Target Buyer:** VPs of Maintenance, operations directors and regional managers at property operators
**Automation Potential:** Very High — triage, vendor matching and turn sequencing are all structured problems currently handled by a person with a phone

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Maintenance is where a property operator's money and its resident satisfaction both go. It is also where the site manager's day goes: a work order arrives as a resident's free-text description, someone decides what it probably is, someone decides which vendor or technician should handle it, and someone follows up until it is closed. Multiply by a portfolio and the maintenance function is the operational centre of gravity of the whole business. What makes the category label misleading is that it contains two operations with almost nothing in common beyond the department they report to. Responding to an occupied unit's problem is about interpretation and matching. Turning a vacant unit is about sequencing under a clock. The products, the data and the failure modes differ completely, which is why this niche decomposes.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Every platform — Yardi, RealPage, Entrata, AppFolio, Buildium — ships work order management, and vendor marketplaces (Lessen, Opendoor-adjacent services, regional networks) have grown alongside them. The workflow layer is competent: a work order can be created, assigned, tracked and closed. Above it, nothing. Triage is a property manager's guess from a resident's text. Vendor selection is relationship-based, and no platform reports which vendors resolve which problem types on the first visit or at what cost relative to peers, despite holding every work order, every invoice and every reopen. Turn sequencing is a site manager on the phone. Preventive maintenance is a calendar rather than a condition-based programme, despite the platform knowing every unit's full repair history.

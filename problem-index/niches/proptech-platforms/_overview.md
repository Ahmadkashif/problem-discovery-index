## Why These Niches

The category's largest block is institutional multifamily, and its contested capability changed in the last few years. Revenue management modules that recommended prices to competing landlords from pooled non-public data are the subject of active antitrust litigation, which has turned a settled product category into an open question: how do you price a unit well using only what one operator legitimately knows about its own demand? That is a real and interesting problem and whoever answers it credibly wins a market that currently has a legal cloud over it.

Maintenance and turn operations is the other large block and **failed the filter as one niche**. Triaging a resident's free-text complaint into a trade, a diagnosis and the right vendor is a classification and matching problem against a corpus of prior work orders. Sequencing a unit turn across five vendors against a vacancy clock is a scheduling problem against a deadline that costs money every day. Different products, different buyers inside the same company. Decomposed below.

Small landlords hold a large share of American rental housing and buy almost nothing; affordable housing operates under a compliance regime so specific that mainstream platforms do not attempt it. Residents and leasing agents are the two underserved constituencies — the renter is the only party to a tenancy who cannot see their own record, and the leasing agent's day is dominated by returning calls to people who have already leased elsewhere.

The two automation niches are the category's permanent content and operations burdens: screening rules that now differ by state, county and city, and the delinquency process, where early identification could route a resident toward a payment plan or an assistance programme and is instead discovered at the point of filing.

## Filter Notes

**Niche 2 failed the filter as stated and was decomposed.** "Maintenance and turn operations" covers two problems that share a department and nothing else. A work order begins with a resident writing "the sink is doing something weird" and the contested capability is inferring what it actually is and which vendor fixes that class of thing well — a language and matching problem over a large corpus of prior work orders and outcomes. A unit turn begins the day a resident moves out and the contested capability is sequencing painters, floor, cleaning, punch and inspection against a vacancy cost that accrues daily — a scheduling problem with a deadline. Both sub-niches are terminal and are written in full.

**Niches 1, 3–8 are terminal as stated.** Each names a testable capability: pricing accuracy from single-operator data alone, time from a landlord's first month to a compliant lease and ledger, audit pass rate on a recertification file, whether a resident can answer a question about their own tenancy without calling, response time to a prospect enquiry, per-jurisdiction screening rule correctness, and how early an arrears trajectory is identified relative to when intervention still works.

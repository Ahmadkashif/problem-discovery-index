# Fire Alarm Inspection, Testing & Maintenance

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/security-alarm-integrators|Security Alarm Integrators]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to hold a building's NFPA 72 inspection contract by delivering an AHJ-accepted report with every deficiency priced, and whoever turns the most deficiencies into approved repairs before the fire marshal's correction deadline takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$3.5B a year of fire alarm inspection, testing, maintenance, monitoring and deficiency-repair revenue within the industry (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~12% of 561621 receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — many crews still test from clipboards or basic apps, reports travel as PDFs, and deficiencies wait in them until someone quotes the repair
**Target Buyer:** Service manager or VP of fire and life safety at an integrator; branch leaders at fire-and-security rollups
**Automation Potential:** Medium — device testing is physical and two-person; scheduling, report assembly, AHJ filing and deficiency quoting automate well

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
NFPA 72 sets device-level inspection and testing frequencies for every commercial fire alarm system, most of them annual. Fire marshals enforce them, increasingly by requiring contractors to file reports through portals such as Brycer's The Compliance Engine, which Raleigh has required since 2015 and which is funded by per-report fees paid by contractors. The integrator's position is unusual: it is both inspector and the obvious repairer, and deficiencies found in the annual test are its best-margin work. Pye-Barker's 57 acquisitions in 2025 show how attractive that recurring compliance revenue has become. The governance loop on the building-owner side, tracking every statutory inspection across a portfolio, belongs to [[niches/facilities-management-firms/statutory-inspection-compliance/profile|Statutory Inspection & Life-Safety Compliance]] under Facilities Management Firms. This niche is the integrator's own production and reporting.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Inspect Point, BuildingReports and ServiceTrade for inspection apps and reports; AHJ portals for filing; integrator billing for quotes. The gaps: device inventories that don't reconcile year to year, AHJ rules held in technicians' heads, and deficiencies re-keyed into quotes days or weeks later.

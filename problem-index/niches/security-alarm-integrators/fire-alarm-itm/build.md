# From Failed Device to Approved Repair Before the Marshal's Deadline

**Industry:** [[industries/security-alarm-integrators|Security Alarm Integrators]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** The annual fire alarm test finds the deficiencies, and then they sit in a PDF while the building stays out of compliance and the repair margin waits.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #bert #feature-engineering #compliance #workflow-orchestration #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to hold a building's NFPA 72 inspection contract by delivering an AHJ-accepted report with every deficiency priced, and whoever turns the most deficiencies into approved repairs before the fire marshal's correction deadline takes the account.

## The Problem
A crew logs failed detectors, dead batteries, a horn-strobe that didn't sound and a communicator that didn't report to the central station. The report goes to the office. Days later someone reads it, looks up parts and labour, writes a quote, e-mails the owner and waits. Owners sit on quotes, the AHJ's correction deadline passes, and the building is cited. The integrator loses margin to a competitor who quoted faster.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Inspection apps stop at the report, quoting tools start from a blank form, and AHJ portals record compliance without driving the repair. Each jurisdiction classes deficiencies and sets correction windows differently, which generic software doesn't model.

## What to Build
A pipeline from test result to booked repair. Each failed device is classified as an impairment, deficiency or recommendation under the adopted NFPA 72 edition and local rules. It is priced from the integrator's book and assembled into a quote that goes out the day of the test. Owner follow-ups run until approval, the AHJ filing is updated when the repair closes, and every open item carries its jurisdiction's deadline.

## Target Customer
Fire and life-safety service managers at integrators doing 2,000+ inspections a year, and fire-and-security rollups standardising acquired branches.

## Impact If Built
Deficiencies quoted the same day convert faster and more often, buildings return to compliance before citation, and the integrator captures repair revenue it now loses to delay.

# Inspection Software Taught Each Jurisdiction's Rules

**Niche:** [[niches/security-alarm-integrators/fire-alarm-itm/profile|Fire Alarm Inspection, Testing & Maintenance]]
**Industry:** [[industries/security-alarm-integrators|Security Alarm Integrators]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Fire inspection apps already produce NFPA-styled reports; what they lack is each fire marshal's version of what a compliant submission is.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #feature-engineering #compliance #data-integration #workflow-orchestration #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to hold a building's NFPA 72 inspection contract by delivering an AHJ-accepted report with every deficiency priced, and whoever turns the most deficiencies into approved repairs before the fire marshal's correction deadline takes the account.

## The Problem
An integrator working across thirty jurisdictions files through several portals, e-mails some reports, mails others and follows each fire marshal's forms, deficiency categories and correction deadlines. Office staff carry the rules in their heads, and rejected or late submissions lead to fines and re-work.

## What Already Exists
Inspect Point, BuildingReports and ServiceTrade capture device tests, produce reports and generate quotes. AHJ portals such as The Compliance Engine accept filings and track compliance for the fire department.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs a jurisdiction rule library: adopted NFPA 72 edition, required forms and fields, portal and fee, deficiency categories, correction windows and re-inspection rules. It needs automated submission and status tracking per portal. It must check device inventories against prior filings, so a report with fewer devices than last year is flagged before submission. And it must handle building-owner notification duties.

## Target Customer
Integrators whose service territories span many jurisdictions, and rollups merging branches with different AHJ habits.

## Impact If Solved
Submissions are right the first time across every jurisdiction, and office staff stop being the only place the rules live.

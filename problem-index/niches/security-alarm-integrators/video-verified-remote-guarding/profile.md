# Video-Verified Monitoring & Remote Guarding

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/security-alarm-integrators|Security Alarm Integrators]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to clear each camera event (real intruder or not) within seconds at a monthly cost per camera below what a guard costs, and whoever delivers the most police-priority verified dispatches per operator hour takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.5B a year of US remote video monitoring, video alarm verification and remote guarding services (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~5% of 561621 receipts (estimate), growing fastest of any segment
**Digital Adoption:** High — the work is video streams, AI filtering and operator consoles
**Target Buyer:** VP of video monitoring at a central station or remote-guarding provider; on the client side, the owner of car lots, construction sites, multifamily properties or logistics yards
**Automation Potential:** High — detection, filtering and first triage automate; the operator's verification and talk-down remain the product

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Remote guarding sells what a security officer does, watching and deterring, from a monitoring centre. AI-filtered cameras raise events, operators verify them on live video, issue audio talk-downs and call police with a verified crime in progress. Providers range from specialists (Stealth Monitoring, Pro-Vigil, Deep Sentinel) to central stations adding video (Guardian's 2025 proactive video service, Emergency24's partnerships) and platform vendors embedding remote video monitoring in station workflows (Alarm.com). Verified-response cities make video verification the route to police priority. Only about 2% of one Seattle monitoring company's customers had video verification in place when the city's policy began. The niche competes with guard hours from [[industries/security-guard-firms|Security Guard Firms]], and the contest is cost and speed per verified event.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Camera analytics (person and vehicle detection), monitoring software such as Immix and vendor portals, and talk-down audio. The gaps: analytics tuned per site by hand, nuisance events (animals, weather, lights) still consuming operator time, and no measure of which verifications led to arrests or stopped losses.

# Residential Monitored Security & Smart Home

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/security-alarm-integrators|Security Alarm Integrators]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep a monitored home paying through its contract term, its owner's move and the next communicator sunset, and whoever holds gross attrition lowest for each dollar spent acquiring the account takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$15B a year of residential monitoring, installation and service revenue within the industry (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~50% of 561621 receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** High at the device, low in retention — panels, apps and video are cloud-connected, while attrition is managed from cancellation lists
**Target Buyer:** Chief customer officer or VP of retention at a national or regional dealer; owner of a dealer with 10,000+ accounts
**Automation Potential:** High for risk detection and routine outreach; the retention conversation stays human on high-value accounts

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Residential security is a subscription business financed up front. The dealer spends roughly two years of revenue to acquire an account (ADT reported a 2.3-year revenue payback for 2025) and earns it back only if the household stays. ADT, Vivint (now owned by NRG), Brinks Home, Guardian and Vector compete with DIY-plus-monitoring brands such as SimpliSafe and Ring on price and install. All of them face the same ledger: ADT's gross revenue attrition rose through 2025 to 13.1%. Moves are the largest single cause, then non-payment, takeovers by competitors and dissatisfaction. Copper retirement and cellular sunsets add forced communicator swaps that either renew the relationship or end it.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Interactive platforms (Alarm.com, Resideo Total Connect, Qolsys) report device and app usage to dealers. Billing systems (SedonaOffice, Bold) hold contracts and cancellations, and CRM tools run retention campaigns. The gaps: no model joins signal behaviour, service history and billing into an account-level hazard, mover detection arrives after the move, and dealer-programme accounts' attrition is seen only when chargeback windows close.

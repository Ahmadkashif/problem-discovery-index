# Central-Station Trouble & Supervisory Signal Handling

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/security-alarm-integrators|Security Alarm Integrators]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to clear the supervisory and trouble signals that make up most of a station's traffic without an operator touching them, while missing no fire or life-safety trouble, and whoever handles the most signals per operator hour within UL 827 rules takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$800M a year of central-station operator labour and automation spend on non-alarm signals (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~3% of 561621 receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium-High — signals are digital and scripted in the automation platform, but each trouble is still worked by a person on a phone
**Target Buyer:** VP of monitoring operations at a proprietary or wholesale central station
**Automation Potential:** Very high — restore prediction, outage clustering and routine notifications are rules and statistics

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Behind every alarm a station handles sit many more signals that aren't alarms: AC power loss, low battery, communication failure, failure to test, supervisory troubles on fire systems, and open/close exceptions. Storms and carrier copper retirements turn them into floods. Wholesale stations sell to dealers on price per account and on service quality, and the cost of an operator touching every trouble decides both. This is distinct from alarm verification, a judgement on whether a threat is real, and from video monitoring. It is the high-volume, low-judgement work that automation should absorb. Elevator emergency phone and alarm line supervision, which some [[industries/answering-services|answering services]] offer, belongs here as line-supervision traffic. The copper lines behind those phones and fire communicators are being replaced under [[niches/telecom-resellers/pots-line-replacement/profile|Legacy POTS Line Replacement]] (Telecom Resellers), and each cut-over shows up in this queue.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Automation platforms (Manitou, MASterMind, SIMS, Dice) with action plans, delayed-processing rules and some auto-notification. The gaps: storm and carrier events handled signal by signal, no prediction of which troubles will self-restore, and routine notices still made as phone calls.

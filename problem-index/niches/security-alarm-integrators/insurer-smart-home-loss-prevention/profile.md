# Property Insurers' Smart-Home & Alarm Loss-Prevention Programmes

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/security-alarm-integrators|Security Alarm Integrators]]
**Category:** Insight Layer
**Value-Chain Position:** Payer & intermediary layer

## What They Do
Homeowners and [[industries/property-casualty-insurers|commercial property insurers]] price monitored alarms through protective-device credits, require central-station certificates on some commercial risks, and increasingly fund smart-home monitoring directly. State Farm bought about 15% of ADT for $1.2B in 2022 and committed up to $300M to a partnership it described as moving from "repair and replace" to "predict and prevent". Their loss-prevention analytics teams measure whether monitored devices (burglary, fire, water leak) reduce claims.

## Insight Function
**Size:** 10-50 data scientists and loss-prevention analysts per large carrier's smart-home programme (estimate)
**Output:** Discount and credit eligibility, device-to-claims studies, programme design and partner selection
**Proprietary data:** Policyholders' claims joined to device enrolment and alert history, the only place where an alarm signal and the loss that did or didn't follow are linked
**Clock:** Annual rate filings and programme renewal cycles
**Buyer:** Head of loss prevention or chief underwriting officer for personal lines

**Kill switches:** none — the claims and enrolment data are the carrier's own policyholder records, used to price its own risk
**Verdict:** Logged — below threshold. The insight is priced into premium, not invoiced, and only a handful of carriers run programmes at scale. Their device-to-claims evidence is the strongest available answer to whether monitoring prevents loss, and none of it reaches the alarm industry.

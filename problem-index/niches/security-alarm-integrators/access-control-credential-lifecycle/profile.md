# Access Control & Credential Lifecycle

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/security-alarm-integrators|Security Alarm Integrators]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep the access-control system's cardholders and door permissions exactly in step with the client's HR and IT identity records, and whoever makes every hire, transfer and termination reach the doors the same day takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$3B a year of enterprise access-control service, hosted subscriptions and identity integration work (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~10% of 561621 receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — door systems are networked and some are cloud-hosted, but cardholder changes still arrive by e-mail and spreadsheet in many sites
**Target Buyer:** Director of corporate security, with the CISO or identity team as co-owner
**Automation Potential:** High — credential provisioning, reconciliation and access reviews are rule-based once systems are connected

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
In an office campus, hospital, university or data centre, the access-control system is a second identity system. It holds tens of thousands of cardholders and the doors each may open, and it drifts from the HR system and the IT directory every day. Auditors (SOC 2, HIPAA, PCI, FedRAMP customers) increasingly ask for physical access reviews alongside logical ones. The integrator that wires the access platform (Lenel, Software House, Genetec, Brivo, Verkada) into the client's identity governance becomes part of the client's control environment and is very hard to displace. The contest is synchronisation, not hardware. The door hardware itself is [[niches/locksmiths/electrified-access-openings/profile|Electrified Hardware & Access-Ready Openings]] under [[industries/locksmiths|Locksmiths]].

## Current Tools & Gaps
Physical identity and access management software (for example from Genetec's and HID's portfolios, and specialist PIAM vendors), access platform HR connectors, and logical IGA tools (SailPoint, Okta, Microsoft Entra). The gaps: connectors that break on custom fields, door-group permissions nobody can explain, and quarterly access reviews done by exporting to Excel.

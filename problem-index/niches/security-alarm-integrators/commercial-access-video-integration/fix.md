# The Service Agreement That Lapsed After Warranty

**Niche:** [[niches/security-alarm-integrators/commercial-access-video-integration/profile|Commercial Access Control & Video Integration]]
**Industry:** [[industries/security-alarm-integrators|Security Alarm Integrators]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The project team installs, the warranty runs out, and the service agreement that should have followed is never offered because nobody owned the handoff.

Enterprise access control is won by whoever keeps the door system in step with the client's identity systems, and multi-site retail, bank and restaurant programmes are won by whoever proves hundreds of sites healthy at the lowest service cost per site. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## The Problem
A commercial project closes out with a one-year warranty. Sales has moved on to the next bid, and the project manager to the next job. The service department doesn't know the site exists until the client calls with a failure, at time-and-materials rates. By then the relationship is a complaint and the client is open to the next integrator who calls.

## Why It's Still Broken
Integrators organise by project, and compensation follows bookings. The service agreement is a small recurring sale that belongs to no one at close-out, and project software has no warranty-end trigger.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Every closed project generates a service proposal priced from its device list, sent 90 days before warranty end, with a simple propensity score (site size, client type, device mix) telling the service sales rep which to call first. Lapsed sites are flagged and worked as a list.

## Who Feels the Pain
Service managers who inherit angry clients; integrators whose installed base leaks to competitors; and clients whose systems go unmaintained until something fails.

## Impact If Fixed
Converting even a modest share of warranty expirations into agreements adds recurring revenue at almost no acquisition cost, from customers the integrator already won.

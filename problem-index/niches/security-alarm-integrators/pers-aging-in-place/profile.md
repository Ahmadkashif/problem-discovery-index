# Personal Emergency Response & Aging-in-Place Subscribers

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/security-alarm-integrators|Security Alarm Integrators]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get the right responder to a fallen or unwell subscriber quickly while keeping unnecessary EMS dispatches down, and whoever shows the paying Medicaid programme or health plan the fewest missed emergencies per false dispatch takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.5B a year of US personal emergency response (PERS) equipment and monitoring revenue handled by alarm dealers, medical-alert brands and their central stations (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~5% of 561621 receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — pendants and fall detectors are cellular and report to the same automation platforms as alarms, but the subscriber's routine and outcomes are rarely tracked
**Target Buyer:** VP of PERS operations at a medical-alert provider or central station; on the payer side, Medicaid waiver programme managers and Medicare Advantage supplemental-benefit leads
**Automation Potential:** Medium — fall and inactivity detection and routing automate; the call with a frightened or confused older person shouldn't

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
PERS is an alarm product whose user is an older adult living alone, often paid for by a state Medicaid home- and community-based services waiver, a Medicare Advantage plan or the family. A press of the pendant or an automatic fall detection reaches a central station operator, who talks to the subscriber, calls a family responder or sends EMS. The tools were built for burglary and fire. They treat the subscriber as an account, not a person whose normal routine is the best signal of trouble, and they don't measure what happened after the call. Medical-alert brands (Lifeline, Medical Guardian, Bay Alarm Medical) and alarm dealers' medical lines compete for the payer contracts. The boundary with [[industries/home-health-agencies|Home Health Agencies]] is clinical care: PERS providers respond to events and do not deliver care.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Cellular pendants and wearables with accelerometer fall detection, central-station automation with PERS scripts, and some caregiver apps. The gaps: fall-detection false positives that send EMS needlessly, no detection of a subscriber who has stopped wearing the device, and no outcome data for payers.

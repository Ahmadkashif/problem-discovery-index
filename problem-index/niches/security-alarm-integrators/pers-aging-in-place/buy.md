# Remote Patient Monitoring Platforms Adapted to PERS

**Niche:** [[niches/security-alarm-integrators/pers-aging-in-place/profile|Personal Emergency Response & Aging-in-Place Subscribers]]
**Industry:** [[industries/security-alarm-integrators|Security Alarm Integrators]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Remote patient monitoring platforms already track vitals and escalate to clinicians; PERS needs the same escalation discipline for falls and routines, without the clinical licence.
**Tags:** #time-series-forecasting #feature-engineering #data-integration #workflow-orchestration #compliance #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get the right responder to a fallen or unwell subscriber quickly while keeping unnecessary EMS dispatches down, and whoever shows the paying Medicaid programme or health plan the fewest missed emergencies per false dispatch takes the account.

## The Problem
PERS events are handled by alarm-station scripts designed for burglary and fire: call the premises, call the list, dispatch. Payers increasingly want care coordination, reporting and outcomes, which alarm automation platforms don't provide.

## What Already Exists
Remote patient monitoring platforms (for example those used by health systems for blood pressure and weight programmes) ingest device data, apply thresholds, route alerts to care teams and produce reports for payers.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs alarm-grade response: UL 827 station handling, two-way voice, dispatch to the PSAP and responder call lists. It needs PERS device integration (pendants, fall detectors, home sensors), and payer reporting for Medicaid waivers rather than clinical billing codes. It must also respect the line between non-clinical event response and clinical care.

## Target Customer
PERS providers and stations seeking health-plan contracts that require reporting and coordination.

## Impact If Solved
PERS providers can meet payer reporting requirements without building clinical software, and payers see what the monitoring achieved.

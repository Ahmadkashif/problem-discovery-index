# The Camera That Stopped Recording Months Before the Robbery

**Niche:** [[niches/security-alarm-integrators/multi-site-national-accounts/profile|Multi-Site National Account Service Programmes]]
**Industry:** [[industries/security-alarm-integrators|Security Alarm Integrators]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Loss prevention asks for the footage after a robbery and learns the recorder's disk failed in the spring.
**Tags:** #change-point-detection #descriptive-statistics #quick-win #automation #workflow-orchestration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep hundreds of a retailer's, bank's or restaurant chain's sites with working alarms, cameras and access points at the service level the client's loss-prevention team audits, and whoever proves the most sites healthy at the lowest service cost per site takes the account.

## The Problem
Recorders fill, disks fail, cameras get knocked out of position or painted over, and retention silently drops below the client's policy. Nobody looks at a camera until an incident, and by then the gap is a programme failure the client remembers at renewal.

## Why It's Still Broken
VMS health alerts go to a console nobody watches at scale, or to store managers who ignore them. The integrator is paid to fix what is reported, and reporting is the client's job in most contracts.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A nightly automated check of every recorder and camera: recording continuity, retention days against policy, image change against a reference frame (catches blocked or moved cameras). Failures open tickets automatically, and a weekly estate summary goes to loss prevention.

## Who Feels the Pain
Loss-prevention investigators missing evidence; integrators losing renewals over failures they could have caught; and store teams blamed after the fact.

## Impact If Fixed
Recording gaps are found in days rather than at the incident, using checks the VMS can already support, which turns the integrator's weakest moment into routine maintenance.

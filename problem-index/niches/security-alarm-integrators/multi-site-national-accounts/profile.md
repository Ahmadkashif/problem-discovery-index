# Multi-Site National Account Service Programmes

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/security-alarm-integrators|Security Alarm Integrators]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep hundreds of a retailer's, bank's or restaurant chain's sites with working alarms, cameras and access points at the service level the client's loss-prevention team audits, and whoever proves the most sites healthy at the lowest service cost per site takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$2.5B a year of multi-site intrusion, video and access service, monitoring and refresh programmes for national chains (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~8% of 561621 receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — devices report health, but programme status is assembled from tickets, subcontractor invoices and spreadsheets
**Target Buyer:** VP of loss prevention or asset protection, with facilities as co-owner
**Automation Potential:** High for health monitoring, triage and dispatch; site work itself is physical and often subcontracted

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A national retailer, bank, pharmacy or quick-service chain buys security as a programme: the same alarm, video and access standard at hundreds or thousands of sites, monitored centrally, serviced by the integrator's own technicians or a network of local subcontractors, refreshed on a cycle. The client's loss-prevention team audits it, and a camera that wasn't recording during a robbery or an alarm that didn't report during a break-in is a programme failure, not a site failure. National integrators (Securitas Technology, Everon, Convergint, Johnson Controls) and national-account arms of large dealers compete here. The contest is proving site health across the estate at a service cost per site the client accepts.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Video health-monitoring add-ons in VMS platforms, central-station supervision of intrusion panels, facilities work-order networks (ServiceChannel, Corrigo) for dispatch, and integrator ticketing. The gaps: no single estate-wide health score, subcontractor work verified by invoice rather than by device evidence, and refresh planning done from age lists rather than failure rates.

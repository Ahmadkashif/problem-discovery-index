# Entertainment Property Licensing & Approvals

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/greeting-card-publishers|Greeting Card Publishers]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to bid minimum guarantees on the film, TV, game and character properties that will still be selling when the cards reach the shelf a year later, and to get licensor approvals through inside the print window, and whoever picks and clears properties best takes the licensed pockets.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$90M a year in property royalties and minimum guarantees paid by US card, gift wrap and party publishers for card rights (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~3% of publisher revenue, concentrated in kids' birthday, Valentine's exchange and licensed humour pockets (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — licensors run approval portals; property selection runs on licensing-show meetings and judgement
**Target Buyer:** VP of Licensing at a card publisher
**Automation Potential:** Medium — approvals pre-check and tracking automate; bidding stays a judgement

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Licensed character cards (a superhero birthday card, a cartoon Valentine's classroom exchange pack) carry a royalty and usually a minimum guarantee payable whether or not the cards sell. Licensing runs in both directions (American Greetings' 2011 filing describes licensing out its own Care Bears and Strawberry Shortcake characters), but for card rights the publisher is usually the licensee, buying from studios, toy companies and game publishers. The publisher must commit roughly a year before cards ship (estimate), betting that a film or show will still be popular, and every design passes licensor approval before print. Few buyers, few properties, and a deadline set by the print slot.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Licensor portals (often built on platforms such as Flowhaven), style guides as PDFs, licensing-show meetings, and box-office and streaming chatter. The gaps: no forecast of property demand at the card's shelf date; approval tracking by email; and no pre-check of designs against style guides.

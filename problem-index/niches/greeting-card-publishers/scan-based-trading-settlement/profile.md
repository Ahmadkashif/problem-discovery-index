# Scan-Based Trading Settlement & Shrink

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/greeting-card-publishers|Greeting Card Publishers]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche (deduction-recovery and reconciliation vendors, EDI analytics providers and publishers' own settlement teams) is fighting to pin each store's missing cards under scan-based trading to a cause the retailer will accept in settlement, and whoever recovers the most disputed shrink per store takes the publisher's settlement work.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$100M a year in card shrink, unrecovered settlement differences and reconciliation labour at US publishers on scan-based trading (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~4% of publisher revenue (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — EDI feeds are digital; reconciliation is spreadsheets and email
**Target Buyer:** Director of Retail Finance or Customer Settlement at a publisher
**Automation Potential:** High — matching and anomaly detection on structured feeds

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Under scan-based trading the publisher owns the card until it scans, is paid for what sells, and carries shrink at a negotiated rate. Greeting cards are among the highest-shrink categories in a supermarket, and American Greetings had to reverse $65.5M of sales when converting accounts to the model in the mid-2000s. Every month the publisher must reconcile shipments, scans, merchandiser counts and seasonal returns by store and settle the difference with the retailer. The volumes are huge in count and tiny per item: hundreds of UPCs per store, each selling a few a week. The work is fought over by the publisher's own settlement team, deduction-recovery and reconciliation firms that work on contingency, and EDI analytics providers selling dashboards; the publisher gives it to whoever turns the most disputed shrink into credits the retailer accepts.

## Current Tools & Gaps
EDI providers (SPS Commerce, TrueCommerce) deliver the documents; deduction management and trade promotion tools serve consumer-goods suppliers; publisher finance teams reconcile in spreadsheets. The gaps: no store-and-pocket unit reconciliation; scan errors not detected; shrink not separated from receiving and stocking failures.

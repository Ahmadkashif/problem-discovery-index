# Space Planning Software That Understands Pockets

**Niche:** [[niches/greeting-card-publishers/mass-channel-card-departments/profile|Mass, Drug & Grocery Card Departments]]
**Industry:** [[industries/greeting-card-publishers|Greeting Card Publishers]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Planogram tools optimise shelves of products with depth and facings; a card fixture is a grid of single-design pockets organised by occasion and recipient, and the tools do not know the difference.
**Tags:** #optimization-fundamentals #time-series-forecasting #k-means-clustering #data-integration #automation #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to win the retailer's whole card department at the multi-year contract renewal by proving, pocket by pocket in the retailer's own scan data, more sales per linear foot net of shrink than the incumbent publisher, and whoever proves it takes the department.

## The Problem
Publishers draw the planograms for the card departments they run, in several versions per retailer for different store sizes, and redraw them at every reset. The logic they apply is card-specific: occasion and recipient blocks in a set order, sentiment and humour adjacencies, price tiers, seasonal swap zones and caption-facing rules. Much of it is enforced by hand.

## What Already Exists
Blue Yonder and NielsenIQ Spaceman are the standard space planning tools; they hold fixture libraries, generate planograms from rules and optimise facings against sales and margin. Clustering features group stores for assortment.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs: a pocket object (one design, fixed capacity, no facings) with occasion, recipient and tone attributes; block rules for occasion and recipient order that the retailer recognises; productivity metrics per pocket and position that use pooled estimates for low-volume pockets; shrink-adjusted sales under scan-based trading; seasonal overlay zones with set and pull dates; and output formats that merchandisers can use on a phone. Most of this can be configured or scripted on the existing platforms by a team that knows cards.

## Target Customer
Planogram and space-planning teams inside card publishers' category management departments.

## Impact If Solved
Planograms that encode card logic and pocket productivity cut reset preparation time and give category managers the per-pocket evidence they need at renewal without building it in spreadsheets.

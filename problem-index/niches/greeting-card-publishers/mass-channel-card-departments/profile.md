# Mass, Drug & Grocery Card Departments

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/greeting-card-publishers|Greeting Card Publishers]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to win the retailer's whole card department at the multi-year contract renewal by proving, pocket by pocket in the retailer's own scan data, more sales per linear foot net of shrink than the incumbent publisher, and whoever proves it takes the department.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.5B a year of US publisher revenue through mass merchant, drug, grocery and dollar-store card departments (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~55% of publisher revenue (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — scan data, EDI and planogram software are standard at the large publishers, but pocket productivity is reviewed as department averages in line-review decks
**Target Buyer:** VP of Category Management or Chief Customer Officer at a publisher
**Automation Potential:** Medium — analysis and planogram drafting automate; the contract is won in a negotiation

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Mass-channel card departments are sold whole. A retailer gives one publisher the department, often hundreds of linear feet in a large store, on a multi-year supply agreement, and American Greetings' filings describe the upfront incentives these agreements carry: cash, credits and allowances booked as deferred costs and earned back only as the retailer buys. Much of the channel runs on scan-based trading, so the publisher owns the stock until it scans and carries shrink. At renewal the challenger and the incumbent compete on one number, department productivity, and the incumbent holds the pocket-level history while the challenger does not.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Space planning software (Blue Yonder, NielsenIQ Spaceman) draws planograms; EDI feeds deliver point-of-sale data; publisher category teams build line-review decks in Excel and PowerPoint. The gaps: productivity is reported per department or per occasion rather than per pocket and position; shrink and out-of-stock weeks are not separated from weak designs; and no model shows what a refreshed department would earn before the reset.

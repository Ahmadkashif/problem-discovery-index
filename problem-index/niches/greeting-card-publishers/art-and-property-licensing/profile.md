# Art & Property Licensing

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/greeting-card-publishers|Greeting Card Publishers]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is that every serious competitor is fighting to get art that sells onto its fixtures with rights it can prove, but buying freelance art by the thousand and winning a handful of entertainment licences are different contests with different buyers, clocks and winners. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$150M a year paid by US card publishers in flat fees, artist advances and royalties, and property royalties and minimum guarantees (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~6% of publisher revenue (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — agreements on several template generations, amendments by email, rights answered by reading the contract, royalty statements built in spreadsheets
**Target Buyer:** Director of Licensing or VP of Creative Operations
**Automation Potential:** High for rights extraction and statements; low for choosing artists and properties

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Card publishers are licensees as much as publishers. They buy freelance designs outright (the Greeting Card Association cites flat fees of roughly $275–$500) or license them for a term, use and territory against a 4–6% royalty, and they license character and entertainment properties under minimum guarantees and approval rights. Every product decision downstream, from which format a design can go on to whether it can ship in a season, depends on what those agreements allow. The rights record is the publisher's own and is mostly unstructured.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Brand licensing platforms (Flowhaven, RoyaltyZone) built for licensors, contract repositories, and publisher ERP. The gaps: no design-level rights grid; royalty bases that ignore scan-based shrink and returns; and no link between licence expiry and the line calendar.

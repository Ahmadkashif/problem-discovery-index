**One-liner:** Brand licensing platforms already manage agreements, approvals and royalty reports; they are built for the licensor checking a few dozen licensees, not the licensee paying thousands of artists.

Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## The Problem
Card publishers need one system for agreements, approvals and royalty statements, and today use spreadsheets and contract folders.

## What Already Exists
Flowhaven, a Salesforce-based licensing platform, covers agreements, design approvals and royalty reporting and validation for licensors and agents. RoyaltyZone and similar tools compute royalties from sales feeds.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs: the licensee's view as primary, with thousands of low-value artist agreements alongside a few property licences; design-level rights, not deal-level; flat-fee ownership recorded as distinct from licences so owned art is never reported as royalty-bearing; royalty bases computed from scan-based sales net of shrink and seasonal returns; artist self-service statements; and approval tracking for property licences that ties into the publisher's print schedule.

## Target Customer
Licensing departments at mid-size and large card publishers that also hold character licences.

## Impact If Solved
One system replaces several spreadsheets and folders, with statements artists can trust and approvals tied to print dates.

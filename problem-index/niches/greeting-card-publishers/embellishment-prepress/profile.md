# Embellishment & Production Art Prepress

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/greeting-card-publishers|Greeting Card Publishers]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche (outsourced prepress houses, specialty printers' prepress departments and publishers' in-house production art teams) is fighting to turn approved card designs into foil, emboss, die-cut, glitter and spot-gloss separations that pass the printer's proof first time, and whoever delivers the most designs right first time inside the seasonal window takes the publisher's production art work.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$80M a year in production art and prepress labour at US card publishers and their printers (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~3% of publisher revenue (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** High in tools, low in automation — everything is in Illustrator and prepress software, and separations are drawn by hand
**Target Buyer:** Director of Production Art or Prepress Manager
**Automation Potential:** High — paired archives of art and finished separations make the work learnable

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Premium cards sell on finish: foil, emboss and deboss, glitter, flocking, spot gloss, die cuts, tip-ons and pop-up mechanisms. Each effect needs its own press file, built by a production artist from art painted without them in mind, at volume, in seasonal waves that hit fixed print slots. The rules are physical (how foil spreads on a given stock, how deep an emboss can go, how close a die can run to type) and the errors show up only at proof. Publishers do this work with in-house production art teams, hand it to their printers' prepress departments, or send it to outsourced prepress houses, some offshore, and move it to whichever delivers the most designs right first time before the print slot. Packaging artwork, which is copy-driven rather than effect-driven, is a different contest covered under [[niches/graphic-design-studios/packaging-artwork-and-sku-production/profile|Packaging Artwork & SKU Production]].

## Current Tools & Gaps
Adobe Illustrator and Photoshop, Esko prepress tools, printers' preflight checks, and in-house action scripts. The gaps: masks drawn path by path; printer proof rejections not fed back as rules; and variants (sizes, boxed versions, matching gift bags) rebuilt by hand.

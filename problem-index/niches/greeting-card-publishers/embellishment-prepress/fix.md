# Proof Rejections Nobody Turns Into Rules

**Niche:** [[niches/greeting-card-publishers/embellishment-prepress/profile|Embellishment & Production Art Prepress]]
**Industry:** [[industries/greeting-card-publishers|Greeting Card Publishers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The printer rejects a proof because the foil filled in a thin serif, the artist fixes that file, and the same mistake is made on the next fifty designs.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #descriptive-statistics #workflow-orchestration #quick-win #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche (outsourced prepress houses, specialty printers' prepress departments and publishers' in-house production art teams) is fighting to turn approved card designs into foil, emboss, die-cut, glitter and spot-gloss separations that pass the printer's proof first time, and whoever delivers the most designs right first time inside the seasonal window takes the publisher's production art work.

## The Problem
Proof rejections arrive as marked-up PDFs and emails: foil too fine, emboss cracking the fold, die cutting into the caption, glitter outside the adhesive. Each is fixed on its own design. The lesson stays in the head of the artist who got the rejection, if anywhere.

## Why It's Still Broken
Rejections are unstructured, sent to individuals, and never counted. Preflight rules are set once by the printer and rarely updated with the publisher's own failures.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Log every rejection with its reason, extracted from the printer's notes into a short code list; count them by effect, stock and artist; and turn the recurring ones into preflight checks run before files leave the building.

## Who Feels the Pain
Production artists redoing files in crunch weeks; production managers losing print slots; printers repeating the same notes; and outsourced prepress houses, which lose a publisher's work when their files keep failing proof.

## Impact If Fixed
Fewer proof rounds per design within one season, at almost no cost.

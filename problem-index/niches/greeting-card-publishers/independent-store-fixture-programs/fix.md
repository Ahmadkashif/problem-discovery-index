# Stock Numbers Written on a Paper Reorder Form

**Niche:** [[niches/greeting-card-publishers/independent-store-fixture-programs/profile|Independent Store Fixture Programs]]
**Industry:** [[industries/greeting-card-publishers|Greeting Card Publishers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Card reorders from independent stores still arrive as handwritten stock numbers, faxed or photographed, keyed by a clerk who cannot tell a mistyped number from a discontinued design.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #data-integration #automation #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep its own fixture in each independent store stocked with sellers, rotating slow designs out on its exchange terms with the fewest rep visits, and whoever turns pocket counts into automatic replenishment and rotation keeps the fixture when the owner reconsiders the space.

## The Problem
Store owners note empty pockets on a printed reorder form, sometimes on the back of an invoice, and send it by fax, email photo or mail. A customer service clerk keys each number. Errors ship the wrong cards; discontinued numbers are silently skipped and the pocket stays empty.

## Why It's Still Broken
The stores are small, owner-run and unwilling to adopt another portal for one vendor. The publisher's order entry system expects clean item codes. Nobody owns the gap between the form and the system.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Read the form, whatever its shape, with document extraction; validate each stock number against the store's fixture map; replace discontinued designs with the planned successor for that pocket; and send the owner a one-tap confirmation by text or email with images of what will ship.

## Who Feels the Pain
Customer service clerks keying forms; store owners who receive the wrong cards; publishers whose fixtures look tired because empty pockets stay empty.

## Impact If Fixed
Order entry falls from minutes to seconds per form, error-driven returns fall, and pockets refill with the designs the publisher intended.

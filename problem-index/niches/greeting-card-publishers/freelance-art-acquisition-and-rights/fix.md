# Flat Fee Versus Royalty Chosen by Habit

**Niche:** [[niches/greeting-card-publishers/freelance-art-acquisition-and-rights/profile|Freelance Art Acquisition & Rights]]
**Industry:** [[industries/greeting-card-publishers|Greeting Card Publishers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Publishers buy some designs outright and license others on royalty by house habit, when the choice should depend on how long and how widely each design is expected to sell.
**Tags:** #survival-analysis #gradient-boosting #monte-carlo-methods #revenue-impact #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to bring in enough fresh, on-trend freelance art for each season's pocket briefs, with rights cleared for every format it will appear in, and whoever builds the deepest pipeline of artists who submit to it first wins the line review.

## The Problem
A flat fee (the GCA cites roughly $275–$500) buys the design for good; a licence with a $150–$300 advance against a 4–6% royalty keeps upfront cost low and shares upside with the artist. A design that becomes an evergreen sympathy card for a decade is far cheaper bought outright; a design that dies in one season is cheaper licensed. Publishers apply one policy to everything, or let each art director choose.

## Why It's Still Broken
Nobody has modelled design lifetimes, and the cost of a royalty is spread across years of statements no one adds up per design.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Estimate each candidate design's expected life and volume by pocket type from history (evergreen occasions versus trend-led ones), and recommend fee or royalty terms per acquisition, with a running record of what each policy actually cost.

## Who Feels the Pain
Art directors negotiating terms; finance teams paying royalties on evergreens; artists who would sometimes prefer the certainty of a fee.

## Impact If Fixed
Lower total art cost for the same line, and a fairer, explainable offer to artists.

# Freelance Art Acquisition & Rights

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/greeting-card-publishers|Greeting Card Publishers]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to bring in enough fresh, on-trend freelance art for each season's pocket briefs, with rights cleared for every format it will appear in, and whoever builds the deepest pipeline of artists who submit to it first wins the line review.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$60M a year in freelance art fees, advances and artist royalties paid by US card publishers (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~2% of publisher revenue, governing a far larger share of designs (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low-Medium — submissions by email, portal or agent; terms negotiated per artist; rights tracked in spreadsheets
**Target Buyer:** Art Director or Creative Sourcing Manager
**Automation Potential:** Medium — matching and rights capture automate; choosing art does not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Most card publishers outside the two largest rely heavily on freelance artists, sourced through open submissions, art licensing agents and trade shows such as Surtex. A publisher competes with every other publisher for the same artists' best work, and the artist chooses where to send it based on fees, royalties, speed of decision and how well her art does. The publisher's own decisions are which art to take, on which terms (flat fee or royalty), and for which formats. The freelancer's side of the market sits with [[niches/graphic-design-studios/solo-freelance-designers/profile|Solo Freelance Designers]].

## Current Tools & Gaps
Email and submission portals, agent catalogues, shared drives and spreadsheets of terms. The gaps: no matching of open pocket briefs to artists' portfolios; no record of which artists' work sold; and terms chosen by habit rather than by a design's expected life.

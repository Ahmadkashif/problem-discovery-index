# Submission Portals With Seasonal Briefs and Rights Capture

**Niche:** [[niches/greeting-card-publishers/freelance-art-acquisition-and-rights/profile|Freelance Art Acquisition & Rights]]
**Industry:** [[industries/greeting-card-publishers|Greeting Card Publishers]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Generic submission portals collect files and forms; a card publisher needs submissions tied to open pocket briefs, with the rights on offer captured as data at the moment the art arrives.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #compliance #workflow-orchestration #data-integration #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to bring in enough fresh, on-trend freelance art for each season's pocket briefs, with rights cleared for every format it will appear in, and whoever builds the deepest pipeline of artists who submit to it first wins the line review.

## The Problem
Open submissions arrive in inboxes with unclear rights: is this art available for cards only, already licensed to a rival in another category, or the artist's to sell at all? Reviewers spend time on art they cannot use.

## What Already Exists
Submission management platforms such as Submittable, used by publishers, grant makers and competitions, handle forms, review rounds and decisions. Several card publishers run their own web forms.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs: submissions attached to specific seasonal briefs with deadlines tied to the line calendar; a rights questionnaire whose answers populate the publisher's rights ledger (formats, territories, existing licences, ownership warranty); fee or royalty offers generated from the publisher's terms; reviewer scoring that feeds the selection ledger; and artist-facing status so artists know quickly whether art is accepted.

## Target Customer
Card publishers taking open submissions and agent submissions at volume.

## Impact If Solved
Faster decisions for artists, which keeps the best ones submitting, and clean rights from the first day a design exists.

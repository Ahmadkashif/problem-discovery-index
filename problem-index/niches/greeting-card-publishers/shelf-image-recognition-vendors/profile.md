# Shelf Image Recognition & Retail Execution Vendors

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/greeting-card-publishers|Greeting Card Publishers]]
**Category:** Insight Layer
**Value-Chain Position:** Suppliers selling into the industry

## What They Do
Vendors such as Trax and ParallelDots ShelfWatch sell image recognition of store shelves, and retail execution platforms such as Repsly and GoSpotCheck sell field visit management with photo audits. Consumer-goods brands and retailers pay for planogram compliance, out-of-stock and share-of-shelf reports built from photos taken by reps, store staff or fixed cameras. Card publishers are a small, specialised customer group whose individual designs these models do not recognise.

## Insight Function
**Size:** 10-40 client insight and analytics staff per firm at the two or three largest vendors, under 10 at most others; computer-vision and software engineers are excluded (estimate). The gate passes on the largest vendors only.
**Output:** Shelf compliance and availability reports, share-of-shelf analytics, alerts to field teams
**Proprietary data:** Large libraries of labelled shelf images and the recognition models trained on them
**Clock:** Clients' reset calendars and promotional windows
**Buyer:** Chief Product Officer or VP of Insights at the vendor

**Kill switches:** *Data isn't theirs to use:* live. Images are captured in retailers' stores on behalf of a paying brand or retailer, under contracts that give the client the data; cross-client reuse is limited to model training where permitted. The field-services firms that gather similar observations with people rather than cameras are scored as [[niches/independent-retailers/merchandising-service-organizations/profile|🔍 Merchandising Service Organizations]] (45).
**Verdict:** Logged — kill switch. The reports are the product, but the observations belong to the client who paid for the visit. Q2 is 3 because report volume grows with the models rather than with the analyst team.

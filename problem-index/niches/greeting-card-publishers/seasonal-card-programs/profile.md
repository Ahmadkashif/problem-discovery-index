# Seasonal Card Programs

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/greeting-card-publishers|Greeting Card Publishers]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to put the right quantity of each seasonal design into each store before the holiday so that it sells through in the final days with the fewest cards returned or destroyed afterwards, and whoever forecasts the store-level seasonal curve best keeps the seasonal planogram.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$900M a year of US publisher revenue from seasonal programs: Christmas single and boxed cards, Valentine's Day, Easter, Mother's Day, Father's Day and graduation (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~35% of publisher revenue, overlapping the channel niches (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — quantities come from prior-year sales and planners' adjustments in spreadsheets
**Target Buyer:** VP of Seasonal Programs or Director of Seasonal Planning
**Automation Potential:** High for quantity setting; low for the creative line

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Seasonal cards live for weeks, not years, and sell most of their volume in the last few days before the holiday. Christmas alone accounts for ~1.6 billion US cards (GCA via USPS, 2019), and 41% of consumers planned to buy Valentine's cards in 2026 (NRF/Prosper). Seasonal cards are sold with a right of return, so every overestimate comes back as a credit or a destroy after the holiday, and every underestimate leaves an empty pocket on the busiest day of the year. Each holiday happens once a year, falls on a different weekday, and follows the previous season's pull by days.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Publishers forecast from last year's sell-through by store with planners' overrides in spreadsheets and ERP planning modules; retail demand-forecasting suites (RELEX, Blue Yonder) are built for repeating weekly demand. The gaps: no pooling across holidays and stores to learn the end-of-season curve; return costs not priced into quantities; and no in-season reallocation between stores.

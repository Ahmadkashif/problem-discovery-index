# The Post-Holiday Pull Counted by Hand

**Niche:** [[niches/greeting-card-publishers/seasonal-card-programs/profile|Seasonal Card Programs]]
**Industry:** [[industries/greeting-card-publishers|Greeting Card Publishers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** After every holiday, merchandisers pull and count the unsold seasonal cards store by store, and the counts that drive credits and next year's quantities arrive late, incomplete and unverifiable.
**Tags:** #cnns #object-detection #descriptive-statistics #worker-facing #automation #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to put the right quantity of each seasonal design into each store before the holiday so that it sells through in the final days with the fewest cards returned or destroyed afterwards, and whoever forecasts the store-level seasonal curve best keeps the seasonal planogram.

## The Problem
The day after a holiday, the seasonal section must come down and the next one go up. Merchandisers pull the remaining cards, count them by design, record the counts on forms or in an app, and box them for return or destroy them under the retailer's policy. The counts settle credits with the retailer and become the "unsold" half of next year's forecast. Under time pressure, counts are estimated, designs are lumped together, and some stores are never counted.

## Why It's Still Broken
The pull happens in the busiest changeover week, by people paid per assignment, and nothing checks the count against what was shipped and scanned. Publishers treat the return as an accounting event, not as forecast data.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Count from photos: the merchandiser photographs each pocket's remaining stack before pulling, and image recognition identifies the design and estimates the count. Reconcile against shipments and scans per store, flag stores where the numbers do not add up, and feed clean unsold counts straight into next season's forecast.

## Who Feels the Pain
Merchandisers doing the pull; seasonal planners forecasting from bad unsold data; finance teams settling credits with retailers.

## Impact If Fixed
Faster changeovers, credits backed by evidence, and the first trustworthy record of what did not sell, which is the missing half of every seasonal forecast.

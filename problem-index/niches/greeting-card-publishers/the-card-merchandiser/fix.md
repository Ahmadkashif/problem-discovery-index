# The Planogram Sheet Three Resets Out of Date

**Niche:** [[niches/greeting-card-publishers/the-card-merchandiser/profile|The Card Department Merchandiser]]
**Industry:** [[industries/greeting-card-publishers|Greeting Card Publishers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Merchandisers work from printed planograms and pocket tags that lag the publisher's current plan, so they carefully restore fixtures to a layout that no longer exists.
**Tags:** #data-integration #workflow-orchestration #worker-facing #quick-win #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep every pocket full, correctly filed and on season with the fewest paid merchandiser hours per store, and whoever gives the merchandiser the fastest way to see and fix what is wrong with a fixture keeps the service contract.

## The Problem
Planograms change with resets, refreshes, seasonal swaps and store-specific exceptions. The version a merchandiser holds may be a binder copy, a PDF emailed months ago, or the pocket tags themselves, which are replaced only when someone remembers. Fixtures drift from plan, and new designs sit in the back room because nobody knew which pocket they replaced.

## Why It's Still Broken
Planograms are produced in space-planning software for the retailer, not for the person maintaining the fixture. Store-level exceptions are recorded nowhere central, and pocket tag printing is a separate process.

## What a Fix Looks Like
One current planogram per store, served to the merchandiser's phone and shown section by section, with pending changes (new designs to place, pockets to swap, tags to print) as a task list at the start of each visit, and store exceptions recorded back from the field.

## Who Feels the Pain
Merchandisers; field managers explaining fixture condition; product managers whose new designs never reach the pocket.

## Impact If Fixed
New designs reach the fixture on time, and the merchandiser's effort goes into the current plan.

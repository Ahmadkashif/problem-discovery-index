# The Card Department Merchandiser

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/greeting-card-publishers|Greeting Card Publishers]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep every pocket full, correctly filed and on season with the fewest paid merchandiser hours per store, and whoever gives the merchandiser the fastest way to see and fix what is wrong with a fixture keeps the service contract.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$300M a year in in-store card merchandising labour, whether employed by publishers or bought from third-party merchandising services (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~12% of publisher revenue (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low-Medium — visit logging and photo apps exist; the planogram is still often a printed sheet or a PDF on a phone
**Target Buyer:** VP of Retail Field Operations at a publisher, or the account lead at a merchandising service
**Automation Potential:** Medium — seeing what is wrong automates; stocking stays physical

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
American Greetings reported about 17,400 part-time employees in 2011, alongside 7,400 full-time, much of that part-time force presumably in-store merchandisers (estimate; the filing does not break it out). Job ads from publishers and third-party services describe contractors paid a flat monthly rate per store to straighten racks, stock, reorder, swap seasons by planogram and process credits for out-of-date product. The merchandiser is the publisher's only regular presence in the store, and the tools built for shelf execution assume a person checking facings of branded products, not hundreds of near-identical card pockets. The analytics businesses that employ field reps for many brands are scored separately as [[niches/independent-retailers/merchandising-service-organizations/profile|Merchandising Service Organizations]].

## Current Tools & Gaps
Retail execution apps (Repsly, GoSpotCheck) for visit logs, surveys and photos; publisher reorder handhelds; printed planograms. The gaps: no recognition of individual card designs in photos; no misfile detection; seasonal changeovers not planned around the merchandiser's hours; and visit pay unrelated to fixture condition.

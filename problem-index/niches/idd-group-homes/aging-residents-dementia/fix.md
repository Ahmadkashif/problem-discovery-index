# Dementia Charted as a New Behaviour Problem

**Niche:** [[niches/idd-group-homes/aging-residents-dementia/profile|Ageing Residents with Down Syndrome and Dementia]]
**Industry:** [[industries/idd-group-homes|IDD Group Homes]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A resident with early Alzheimer's starts refusing showers and striking out, and gets a behaviour plan instead of a dementia assessment.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #worker-facing #compliance #quick-win #workflow-orchestration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep residents with dementia living in their group home as the disease progresses, and whoever keeps the most of them out of nursing facility admission takes the account.

## The Problem
New agitation, refusals or night wandering in a middle-aged resident with Down syndrome are often early dementia (estimate). They get referred to the behaviour clinician, and may draw PRN psychotropics or restrictive interventions that make confusion worse.

## Why It's Still Broken
Behaviour referral is the path staff know. Medical screening for dementia is not routine, and no rule triggers it.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A rule in the record system: for adults with Down syndrome over 40, or any resident over 50, a new behaviour referral automatically prompts a dementia screen and a nursing review before a behaviour plan is written.

## Who Feels the Pain
Residents medicated for a disease nobody named; DSPs facing behaviour they are not trained to read; families who see a move coming.

## Impact If Fixed
Dementia is recognised ~6–12 months earlier (estimate), care adjusts, and PRN psychotropics or holds used on these residents could fall by ~a third (estimate).

# Memory Care Assessment Tools Rebuilt for Lifelong Intellectual Disability

**Niche:** [[niches/idd-group-homes/aging-residents-dementia/profile|Ageing Residents with Down Syndrome and Dementia]]
**Industry:** [[industries/idd-group-homes|IDD Group Homes]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Senior care software already tracks cognition, falls and care needs for people with dementia; its scales assume a typical adult baseline.
**Tags:** #change-point-detection #descriptive-statistics #worker-facing #compliance #data-integration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep residents with dementia living in their group home as the disease progresses, and whoever keeps the most of them out of nursing facility admission takes the account.

## The Problem
Agencies looking for dementia tools find products built for [[industries/assisted-living-communities|memory care units]] and [[industries/skilled-nursing-facilities|nursing homes]], scored against norms that place every resident with intellectual disability as "impaired" from day one.

## What Already Exists
Memory care and nursing-home assessment and care-planning modules; fall-detection sensors; cognitive screens for the general population.

## The Customization Gap
Assessment must be relative to the person's own baseline, using instruments designed for adults with intellectual disability, informed by DSPs who know the person rather than self-report. Outputs have to feed the IDD world's paperwork: the person-centred plan, the health risk plan, and the state's support-needs reassessment. Staff guidance must be written for DSPs, not nurses.

## Target Customer
Providers with ageing residents; vendors of memory care tools looking for a new segment.

## Impact If Solved
Existing dementia know-how reaches group homes in a form that measures change in the person, not distance from a norm. A baseline screen takes ~1–2 staff hours (estimate) and could bring recognition forward by ~1–2 years for residents with Down syndrome (estimate).

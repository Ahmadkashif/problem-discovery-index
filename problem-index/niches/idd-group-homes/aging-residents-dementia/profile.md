# Ageing Residents with Down Syndrome and Dementia

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/idd-group-homes|IDD Group Homes]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep residents with dementia living in their group home as the disease progresses, and whoever keeps the most of them out of nursing facility admission takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$400M a year in dementia screening and care planning, added staffing, nursing consultation, environmental adaptations and reassessment work for ageing residents with IDD (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~1% of facility revenue (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — decline is tracked in narrative notes and annual plan meetings; baseline cognitive screening is often never done
**Target Buyer:** Director of nursing or health services at a provider; regional director
**Automation Potential:** Medium — tracking decline, flagging reassessment and planning staffing can be automated; care itself cannot

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
People with intellectual disabilities now live into their sixties and beyond, and adults with Down syndrome develop Alzheimer's disease far earlier and more often than the general population, most of them by their sixties (estimate, recalled). In a group home, dementia shows first as lost skills, new behaviour or falls, which staff read as behaviour problems. Without a baseline, nobody can measure decline. The house's staffing, funding tier and physical layout were set for a younger person. When needs outrun the rate, the usual outcome is a move to a nursing facility, against the person's wishes and the community-living goals of the waiver; the residents who make that move join [[niches/skilled-nursing-facilities/long-stay-dementia-residents/profile|🟣 Long-Stay Residents Living with Dementia]]. At least 27,741 people with IDD lived in nursing homes or psychiatric facilities in 2022 (RISP). Memory care tools built for assisted living ([[niches/assisted-living-communities/memory-care-residents/profile|🟣 Memory Care Residents Living with Dementia]]) assume a nurse-led building and residents without lifelong intellectual disability, so their baselines and assessments do not fit.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Dementia screening instruments designed for adults with intellectual disability (the NTG-EDSD, recalled), nursing consultation, general EHR health tracking. Gaps: no routine baseline at 40 for adults with Down syndrome; no structured tracking of skill loss; no link from documented decline to a rate-tier reassessment request.

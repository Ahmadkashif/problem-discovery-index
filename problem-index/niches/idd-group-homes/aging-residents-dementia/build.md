# Decline Tracking From Daily Notes Against a Baseline

**Niche:** [[niches/idd-group-homes/aging-residents-dementia/profile|Ageing Residents with Down Syndrome and Dementia]]
**Industry:** [[industries/idd-group-homes|IDD Group Homes]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** The skills a resident is losing are written in staff notes every day, but nobody reads them as a trend until a fall or a crisis forces a move.
**Tags:** #change-point-detection #large-language-models #gradient-boosting #survival-analysis #revenue-impact #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep residents with dementia living in their group home as the disease progresses, and whoever keeps the most of them out of nursing facility admission takes the account.

## The Problem
Staff notes record that a woman needed help with buttons she used to manage, got lost going to the bathroom at night, and refused the stairs. Each note is read alone. By the time the team agrees she has dementia, she has fallen, her funding tier no longer covers the staffing she needs, and a nursing facility looks like the only option.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Dementia tools are built for older adults without intellectual disability, and IDD record systems store notes but do not trend them. No one is paid to establish baselines.

## What to Build
A tracker that extracts daily-living skills, orientation, mobility, sleep, continence and behaviour from shift notes and observation logs. It compares them with a baseline screen taken at age 40 for adults with Down syndrome, or at entry for others, and detects sustained change. It alerts the nurse and QIDP with evidence, proposes environmental and staffing adjustments, and drafts the evidence pack for a support-needs reassessment so the rate can rise with need.

## Target Customer
Directors of health services at agencies with large ageing populations.

## Impact If Built
If earlier recognition, adjustments and a funded reassessment kept ~1 in 4 residents who would otherwise move living at home for ~12–24 more months (estimate), an agency supporting ~40 residents with dementia (estimate) would avoid ~10 nursing facility admissions, honouring those residents' choice to stay home.

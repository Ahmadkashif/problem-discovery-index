# DSP Hiring & Pre-Service Training

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/idd-group-homes|IDD Group Homes]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn a DSP applicant into a worker cleared to support residents alone, and whoever converts the highest share of applicants into DSPs working their first solo shift takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$900M a year in job advertising, applicant tracking, background and registry checks, pre-service and medication training, and recruiter and trainer time (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~2.4% of facility revenue (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — applications and online courses are digital; registry checks, fingerprinting, skills sign-offs and shadow shifts are scheduled by phone and paper
**Target Buyer:** Director of recruiting or HR at a multi-home agency
**Automation Potential:** High for screening, scheduling and course tracking; low for shadow shifts and skills checks

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Before a new DSP may work alone she usually needs a background check and abuse-registry clearance, state pre-service training, CPR and first aid, medication administration certification where she will pass meds, and person-specific training on each resident's plans, often with shadow shifts (requirements vary by state, estimate). Applicants drop out at every step, because they can start at a warehouse or fast-food job next week at similar pay. A Sevita caregiver posting in south-central Pennsylvania offered $14.50–15.50 an hour (checked 2026-10-24). The competitors are high-volume hiring platforms, screening vendors, training providers such as Relias and the College of Direct Support, and the agency's own recruiters. The scorecard is applicant-to-first-solo-shift conversion.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Job boards and text-to-apply tools; generic applicant tracking; state registry portals; learning management systems. Gaps: no single view of each applicant's remaining clearances; training scheduled in fixed weekly classes rather than on demand; and no measure of where applicants drop out.

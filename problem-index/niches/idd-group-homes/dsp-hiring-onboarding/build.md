# A Clearance Tracker That Books the Next Step the Day the Last One Clears

**Niche:** [[niches/idd-group-homes/dsp-hiring-onboarding/profile|DSP Hiring & Pre-Service Training]]
**Industry:** [[industries/idd-group-homes|IDD Group Homes]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Each new DSP passes through six to ten separate clearances, and the gaps between them, not the steps themselves, are where applicants are lost.
**Tags:** #ai-agent #large-language-models #survival-analysis #workflow-orchestration #automation #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn a DSP applicant into a worker cleared to support residents alone, and whoever converts the highest share of applicants into DSPs working their first solo shift takes the account.

## The Problem
An applicant accepts an offer, then waits for a fingerprint appointment, for registry results, for next week's training class, for a medication course, for shadow shifts at a house. Each step is booked by a different person after the previous one is noticed to be done. Days pass in between, and applicants take other jobs.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Applicant tracking systems end at the offer; learning systems start at enrolment. State clearance rules differ, so generic vendors skip them, and agencies run the gap on spreadsheets.

## What to Build
A per-applicant pipeline that encodes the state's clearance sequence and the house's person-specific training. It books each next step automatically when the previous clears: fingerprinting, on-demand online modules, a medication class seat, shadow shifts with a named mentor. It texts the applicant a clear checklist and nudges her when she stalls. A model of drop-out risk by stage and wait time tells recruiters whom to call today.

## Target Customer
Recruiting directors at agencies hiring 200+ DSPs a year.

## Impact If Built
Cutting average waiting between steps by a week (estimate) can lift applicant-to-solo-shift conversion by several points. For an agency hiring ~400 a year that means dozens more working DSPs and fewer forced doubles (estimate).

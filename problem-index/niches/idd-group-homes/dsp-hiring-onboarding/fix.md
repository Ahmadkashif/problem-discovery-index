# The Applicant Who Took the Warehouse Job While Waiting for Fingerprints

**Niche:** [[niches/idd-group-homes/dsp-hiring-onboarding/profile|DSP Hiring & Pre-Service Training]]
**Industry:** [[industries/idd-group-homes|IDD Group Homes]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Nobody tells the new hire what happens next or when, so she assumes the job fell through.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #workflow-orchestration #worker-facing #quick-win #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn a DSP applicant into a worker cleared to support residents alone, and whoever converts the highest share of applicants into DSPs working their first solo shift takes the account.

## The Problem
After the offer, the applicant hears nothing for a week while her background check runs. Then she gets a training date two weeks out. She needs a paycheck now, takes another job, and stops answering.

## Why It's Still Broken
Recruiters move on to the next applicants, and no one owns the waiting period. Agencies count hires, not starts.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A text every few days with what has cleared, what is next and the date, plus paid online pre-service modules she can start while she waits, so the wait becomes paid onboarding.

## Who Feels the Pain
Recruiters who re-fill the same opening; house managers short a worker; applicants who wanted the job.

## Impact If Fixed
Fewer offers lost before day one. Each recovered start saves ~$2,000–4,000 of repeat recruiting and vacancy overtime (estimate), for a scheduled text and ~4–8 paid training hours per applicant (estimate).

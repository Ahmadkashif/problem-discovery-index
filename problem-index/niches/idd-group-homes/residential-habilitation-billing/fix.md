# The Month-End Chase for Missing Notes

**Niche:** [[niches/idd-group-homes/residential-habilitation-billing/profile|Residential Habilitation Billing & ISP Documentation]]
**Industry:** [[industries/idd-group-homes|IDD Group Homes]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Before billing closes, house managers phone DSPs who have already left, asking them to write notes for shifts they worked weeks ago.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #compliance #workflow-orchestration #quick-win #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to make every billed residential day stand up to a state or Medicaid Fraud Control Unit audit, and whoever keeps the share of billed days recouped on audit lowest takes the account.

## The Problem
Notes go missing: a DSP forgot, a float worker had no login, an overnight shift wrote nothing. At month end the billing office sends lists, and house managers chase people, some of whom no longer work there. Notes written late are thin and look reconstructed, which auditors notice.

## Why It's Still Broken
Record systems show missing notes in a report someone has to run. Nothing prompts the DSP before she clocks out, and floats often lack access to the house's residents.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Make the note a condition of clocking out: a prompt at the end of each shift listing the residents she supported, with a spoken-report option and a draft to approve. House managers get a daily, not monthly, missing-note list. Floats get temporary access scoped to the house and shift.

## Who Feels the Pain
House managers, billing staff, and DSPs called on their day off; and the agency, whose late notes are the least defensible on audit.

## Impact If Fixed
Missing notes fall by ~90% within ~1 month (estimate), billing closes on time, and late, reconstructed notes stop showing up in audit samples.

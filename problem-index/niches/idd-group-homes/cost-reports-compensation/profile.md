# Cost Reports & Direct Care Compensation Reporting

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/idd-group-homes|IDD Group Homes]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to produce the agency's cost reports and direct care compensation figures straight from payroll, ledger and claims data, and whoever files with the fewest state audit adjustments takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$300M a year in accountant and finance-staff time, outsourced cost-report preparation and rate-study survey responses at residential providers (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~0.8% of facility revenue (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — payroll and ledgers are digital; mapping hours and costs to services, homes and Medicaid categories is done in spreadsheets each year
**Target Buyer:** CFO or controller at a provider; accounting firms serving providers
**Automation Potential:** High — allocating payroll and costs to services and computing compensation shares is rule-based once the mapping exists

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Providers report costs to the state for three reasons, on three clocks. Many states set ICF/IID rates from annual cost reports and audit them (estimate). Rate studies collect provider cost, wage and time surveys, as in New Mexico's DD waiver review by Burns & Associates, whose comment period closed 19 December 2025. And from 9 July 2028 every state must report to CMS the share of payments for habilitation, including residential habilitation, that reaches direct care worker compensation, with services in provider-operated settings reported separately (89 FR 40542). States will collect those figures from providers. Each filing asks the same underlying question (how much of each service dollar went to which worker) in a different template, and each is audited. The competitors are healthcare accounting firms, cost-report specialists, payroll and ERP vendors, and the agency's own finance team.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Payroll systems, general ledgers, spreadsheets built by the controller, outside accountants. Gaps: no persistent mapping from payroll hours to service, home and worker category; the same allocation rebuilt for each template; and audit adjustments that repeat because the method is not documented.

# The Rate-Study Survey Answered From Last Year's Spreadsheet

**Niche:** [[niches/idd-group-homes/cost-reports-compensation/profile|Cost Reports & Direct Care Compensation Reporting]]
**Industry:** [[industries/idd-group-homes|IDD Group Homes]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** When the state's rate consultant sends a cost and wage survey, the agency answers in a hurry from old figures, then lives with rates built on them for years.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #data-integration #compliance #revenue-impact #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to produce the agency's cost reports and direct care compensation figures straight from payroll, ledger and claims data, and whoever files with the fewest state audit adjustments takes the account.

## The Problem
Rate studies survey providers on wages, benefits, staff productivity and administrative costs, as Burns & Associates did for New Mexico's DD waiver review in 2025. Surveys arrive with short deadlines. Many agencies answer partially or not at all, and the state's rate models fall back on assumptions.

## Why It's Still Broken
No one at a small or mid-sized agency keeps service-level wage and productivity data ready, so answering means rebuilding it.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A standing quarterly extract of wages, benefits, hours by service and turnover, kept current from payroll, so any survey can be answered in a day.

## Who Feels the Pain
Controllers on deadline; agencies whose rates rest on assumptions instead of their own costs; DSPs whose wages follow those rates.

## Impact If Fixed
A rate-study survey answered in ~1 day instead of ~1–2 weeks (estimate), and rate models built on providers' actual costs. A study's rates typically stand for ~3–5 years (estimate), so a wage assumption ~$1 an hour too low is repeated on every DSP hour billed in that time. The report is one most payroll systems can already produce.

# Niche Analysis — IDD Group Homes

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/idd-group-homes|IDD Group Homes]]

## Niche Selection

A group home for people with intellectual and developmental disabilities is a household paid by the day. Medicaid pays a residential habilitation or ICF/IID rate for each resident, earned only when the day's notes show the person-centred plan was carried out. The house is staffed around the clock by direct support professionals, a third or more of whom will leave within the year. From July 2027 every state must investigate and close critical incidents, and review plans, against federal 90% minimums. The eight niches below split the industry by what vendors, consultants and providers' own teams actually compete over. Market sizes are estimates of the staff time, services and software spent within each niche, not of facility revenue, which is ~$38B a year (estimate) on 2025 wages of ~$24.8B across 26,070 establishments (BLS QCEW).

## Why These Niches

The two high-market-share niches are the revenue engine and the largest cost. Residential habilitation is almost all of a group home's income, and every billed day stands or falls on its documentation. DSP labour is most of the expense: wages were ~$24.8B in 2025 (BLS QCEW), turnover averaged 37% and 26% of agencies turned away referrals for lack of staff (NCI, 2024). The two low-digitized niches are where phone calls, paper sheets and Word documents still carry high-stakes work. One is critical incidents, now under a federal clock and found unreported in OIG audits. The other is behaviour data, where paper tallies decide whether a restraint-heavy plan changes. The two underserved audiences are ageing residents with Down syndrome and dementia, for whom no tool measures decline against their own baseline, and the long tail of small for-profit agencies that face the same rules as Sevita with no compliance office. The two automatable niches are the most mechanical: reconciling medication orders, fills and passes by unlicensed staff, and turning payroll into the cost and compensation figures states will collect from 2028.

## Filter Notes

Seven of the eight level-1 niches are terminal. **DSP Workforce Supply** is not. Its partial sentence, staffing every house on every shift with DSPs trained for its residents at the lowest overtime, produced two contests with different winners. Turning applicants into DSPs cleared to work alone is won by recruiting, screening and training vendors, on applicant-to-first-solo-shift conversion. Covering the schedule from the agency's own staff without forcing doubles is won by scheduling tools and supervisors, on mandated overtime shifts per house. The buyers overlap but the competitors, data and failure points do not. The niche decomposes into **DSP Hiring & Pre-Service Training** and **House Scheduling, Overtime & First-Year Retention**. The nursing-home split differs: there, agencies and per-diem marketplaces are a real second source of labour ([[niches/skilled-nursing-facilities/per-diem-shift-marketplaces/profile|🎯 Per-Diem Shift Marketplaces & Agency Labour]]). In a group home, person-specific training makes outside labour rare (estimate), so the second contest is hiring, not marketplaces.

Four terminal calls were close. **Residential Habilitation Billing** and **Cost Reports & Compensation Reporting** both concern money and the state. They stay apart because billing defends each day's documentation against auditors monthly, while cost reporting allocates payroll once a year for rate-setting, with different people (billing manager versus controller) and different audits. **Critical Incident Reporting** and **Behaviour Support** meet at restrictive interventions, a federal incident category. The incident niche is judged on closing every incident on time, the behaviour niche on making fewer interventions happen, and they are fought by different vendors (case management versus behaviour-data tools). **Medication Administration** also feeds incidents, but it is won at the medication pass, before any report exists. **Small Provider Agencies** looks as if it bundles billing, incidents and medication. The small owner, though, buys one stand-in for the missing back office and judges it on one result, findings per home at review, so the specialist contests stay in their own niches.

Five candidates were considered and rejected. **Electronic visit verification** is a major HCBS compliance burden, but CMS excludes personal care in congregate residential settings with 24-hour service from EVV (CMS EVV FAQ, May 2018). It touches only an agency's in-home and supported-living services, which belong to [[niches/home-health-agencies/medicaid-waiver-personal-care/profile|🟠 Medicaid Waiver Personal Care Programs]]. **Day habilitation and supported employment** are run by many of the same agencies but fall outside NAICS 623210, and their attendance-billing contest mirrors residential billing. **Per-diem DSP staffing marketplaces** compete on fill speed, a generic contest, and are already logged as [[niches/skilled-nursing-facilities/staffing-marketplace-data-teams/profile|🔍 Per-Diem Staffing Marketplace Data Teams]]. **Family and guardian communication** apps compete on ease of use, a generic contest that fails the filter. **Waiting-list management** is real (about 649,900 people on HCBS lists in 2026, 72% with IDD, KFF), but it is run by states and their case-management agencies before a person reaches a provider. It belongs to [[industries/nonprofits-social-services|Social Services Nonprofits]] and the regulators logged below.

## Why These Pockets

**No pocket qualifies.** The operators themselves fail the gate: house managers, QIDPs, nurses and billing clerks are production, and even the largest providers' analytics groups invoice nothing. Above them, the IDD system's analysis is commissioned by states, on data states own. That is the single fact behind the highest score. The rate-study consultancies (42) sell the analysis itself, in a repeatable rate-model format, to a state that publishes it for comment. But each study runs on provider cost, wage and time-study data collected for that state under contract, so "data isn't theirs" is live. They score next to [[niches/health-insurance-carriers/medicaid-rate-setting-actuaries/profile|🔍 Medicaid Capitation Rate-Setting Actuaries]] (46, kill switch). That 46 is pre-ruling: it rests on a recalled CMS certification clock tied to the state's contract rating period, a client date the current rule caps at Q4 4 (~44, logged for Stage E). The gap is then one Q4 point, because a waiver renewal or rate review is the client's calendar with no certification filing behind it. The quality review contractors (37) and the record-system vendors' data teams (37) repeat the pattern with protected health information: the reviewers read identified records for the state, and the vendors hold agencies' notes, incidents and MARs under business associate agreements. The rollups' analytics teams (34) are internal, as under skilled nursing.

National Core Indicators (36) is the most interesting data asset. Its report is what states pay for, and it is the only cross-state outcome and workforce dataset in IDD. But each state's person-level data is collected for that state under its annual agreement, so the switch is live on the half that is paid for; only the pooled national dataset is undetermined. About a dozen people run it on its own annual calendar for state buyers reached through one association, so it would sit far below 50 even without the switch. No clean pocket reaches scoring. Everything else fails the gate: fiscal intermediaries and behaviour clinicians are production, training vendors' course writers make the product rather than analyse a corpus, and associations, university projects, settlement monitors, M&A advisers and remote-support vendors each have fewer than ten analysts. Regulators and Protection and Advocacy agencies are investigators with no commercial buyer.

Several neighbouring pockets are already in the vault and score the same way, so they are linked rather than profiled again. The managed care plans that authorise HCBS in states that use them are [[niches/nonprofits-social-services/medicaid-hcbs-managed-care/profile|🔍 Medicaid Home & Community-Based Services Managed Care]] (50, kill switch). The state EVV aggregators are [[niches/home-health-agencies/evv-aggregation-vendors/profile|🔍 Electronic Visit Verification Aggregators]] (48, kill switch), and they do not reach group homes, which EVV excludes. Several quality review contractors also review Medicaid managed care plans, logged as [[niches/health-insurance-carriers/medicaid-eqros/profile|🔍 Medicaid External Quality Review Organizations]] (46, kill switch). Long-term care pharmacies serving group homes are [[niches/skilled-nursing-facilities/ltc-pharmacy-consultant-services/profile|🔍 Long-Term Care Pharmacy Clinical Consulting]] (45, kill switch). Single audits of the 35% of agencies that are nonprofits run through [[niches/nonprofits-social-services/nonprofit-audit-firms/profile|🔍 Nonprofit Audit & Single Audit Firms]] (45, below threshold). ICF/IID survey-readiness consultants exist as small practices; their larger analogues are [[niches/skilled-nursing-facilities/snf-survey-consultancies/profile|🔍 Nursing Home Survey Readiness & Regulatory Consultancies]] (48, kill switch) and [[niches/home-health-agencies/home-health-compliance-consulting/profile|🔍 Home Health Regulatory & Survey Consulting]] (48, below threshold). Adaptive-behaviour and intelligence instruments used in IDD eligibility come from [[niches/behavioral-health-clinics/psychological-assessment-publishers/profile|🔍 Psychological Assessment Publishers]] (50, indexed under behavioural health). That qualifier sells to clinicians and is not an IDD pocket. DSP wage surveys sit beside [[niches/hr-consultants/compensation-survey-publishers/profile|🔍 Compensation Survey Publishers]] (54). Federal payment policy research is [[niches/health-insurance-carriers/medpac-macpac/profile|🔍 MedPAC & MACPAC]] (30). Three of those scores predate this run's clock rulings: the psychological assessment publishers take Q4 3 on their own restandardisation cycles (Q4 2 under the ruling, ~48, which would leave the index), and the HCBS managed care plans and EVV aggregators take Q4 5 on state contract reporting dates, which are client clocks (Q4 4 at most, ~48 and ~46). They are logged for Stage E, and none of the changes makes an IDD pocket qualify. For a research-insight engine that means no buyer here. For a builder it means the openings are in documentation, incident and staffing tools sold to providers and states, and in the vendors hiring engineers to build them: Kibu and iCareManager this month (see the source log).

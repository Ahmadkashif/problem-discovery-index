# Turnover Counted Once a Year for the Board

**Niche:** [[niches/idd-group-homes/dsp-workforce/profile|DSP Workforce Supply]]
**Industry:** [[industries/idd-group-homes|IDD Group Homes]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Agencies report a single annual turnover number, which hides that most leavers go in their first months.
**Tags:** #survival-analysis #descriptive-statistics #data-integration #worker-facing #quick-win
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is that every competitor here is fighting to staff every house on every shift with DSPs trained for its residents at the lowest overtime. That is two contests with different winners. One is turning applicants into DSPs cleared to work alone, which recruiting, background-screening and training vendors win. The other is covering shifts from the agency's own staff without forcing doubles, which scheduling tools and the agency's own supervisors win. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## The Problem
Turnover is computed once a year, often for the NCI State of the Workforce survey or the board, as separations over headcount. The 37% national average (NCI, 2024) hides the shape. Nearly two in three leavers had stayed under a year, so a cohort hired in March may be half gone by September while long-tenured staff stay for years.

## Why It's Still Broken
HR systems report counts, not cohorts, and nobody owns the first ninety days between HR, training and the house manager.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A monthly cohort report: of DSPs hired each month, how many reached first solo shift, 30, 90 and 180 days, by house, supervisor and recruiting source. Flag houses where new hires leave fastest.

## Who Feels the Pain
House managers who train people who leave; DSPs who pick up the overtime; executives who cannot see where retention money should go.

## Impact If Fixed
Retention efforts move to the houses and weeks where people actually leave. If first-year exits concentrate in a few houses, targeting them could cut first-year separations by ~10% (estimate), for the cost of a monthly report.

# The Expired CPR Card Found by the Licensor

**Niche:** [[niches/idd-group-homes/small-provider-agencies/profile|Small Provider Agencies]]
**Industry:** [[industries/idd-group-homes|IDD Group Homes]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A licensing reviewer pulls five staff files and finds two expired certifications the owner never knew had lapsed.
**Tags:** #workflow-orchestration #compliance #worker-facing #quick-win #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to stand in for the compliance and billing office a small agency cannot afford, and whoever leaves the fewest findings per home across licensing reviews and Medicaid audits takes the account.

## The Problem
Staff certifications (CPR, first aid, medication administration, abuse-registry rechecks) expire on individual dates. In a small agency nobody watches them, and a DSP with a lapsed medication certification who keeps passing meds is a citation and a possible billing problem.

## Why It's Still Broken
Certificates are paper copies in a filing cabinet, and expiry dates are not entered anywhere that sends reminders.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Photograph each certificate, have the dates extracted automatically, text the DSP and the owner sixty and thirty days before expiry, and block scheduling of med passes for anyone whose certification has lapsed.

## Who Feels the Pain
Owners facing citations; DSPs taken off shifts at short notice; residents whose medication passer is not certified.

## Impact If Fixed
Expired-credential findings drop by ~90% (estimate) for ~5 minutes of setup per employee (estimate).

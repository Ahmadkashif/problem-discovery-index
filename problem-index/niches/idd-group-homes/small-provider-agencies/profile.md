# Small Provider Agencies

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/idd-group-homes|IDD Group Homes]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to stand in for the compliance and billing office a small agency cannot afford, and whoever leaves the fewest findings per home across licensing reviews and Medicaid audits takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$500M a year in software, outsourced billing, compliance consulting and owner time at agencies running one to ten homes (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~1.3% of facility revenue, at agencies that together run a large share of homes (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — the owner or a family member does billing, policies and licensing preparation on spreadsheets and the state portal
**Target Buyer:** Owner-operator or executive director
**Automation Potential:** Medium — billing, document control and deadline tracking automate; the owner's relationships with case managers and licensors do not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The industry has a long tail. Private for-profit agencies were 62% of the 3,936 agencies in NCI's 2024 workforce survey, nonprofits 35% and government-run entities 5% (NCI), and private sites averaged about 17 employees each in 2025 (BLS QCEW: 420,745 employees across 24,971 private establishments, derived). A small agency is often founded by a former DSP or nurse who runs two to six homes. It faces the same licensing reviews, waiver audits, incident rules and, from 2027, Access Rule incident and planning requirements as Sevita, with no compliance department. The Access Rule lets states set a lower compensation-share minimum for small providers, which shows the gap is recognised (89 FR 40542). The owner buys one stand-in for the missing back office and judges it on one result: whether reviews and audits find anything.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Low-cost IDD record systems (Statewise starts at $1,000 a month, vendor pricing page), billing services, freelance policy writers and licensing consultants. Gaps: no single calendar of every licensing, training, plan-review and incident deadline across homes; policies bought as templates that do not match practice; and no mock review before the real one.

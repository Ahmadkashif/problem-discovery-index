# Behaviour Support Plans & Restrictive Intervention Data

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/idd-group-homes|IDD Group Homes]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn DSPs' observations of behaviour into data a clinician uses to revise the plan, and whoever cuts restrictive interventions per resident furthest takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$900M a year in behaviour analyst and psychologist time on group-home residents, behaviour-data software, human rights committee review, and DSP data-collection time (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~2.4% of facility revenue (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — behaviour data is collected on paper ABC sheets and tally charts, typed up monthly, and graphed by hand for plan reviews
**Target Buyer:** Director of clinical or behaviour services at a provider; contracted behaviour analysts
**Automation Potential:** Medium — capture, graphing and restrictive-intervention reporting automate; writing and revising the plan does not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Residents whose behaviour can hurt themselves or others have a positive behaviour support plan, written by a behaviour analyst or psychologist and carried out by DSPs on every shift. Restrictive interventions (physical holds, PRN psychotropics for behaviour, restricted access to items) must be approved in the plan, reviewed by a human rights committee and documented each time. Their misuse is a federal critical incident category (89 FR 40542). The clinician revises the plan from the data DSPs collect. That data is usually incomplete paper tallies, so plans run for months unchanged while restraints continue. ABA practice software built for autism clinics (CentralReach and others) serves the clinician's caseload in a clinic, not the DSP's shift in a house.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Paper ABC sheets, IDD record-system behaviour modules, clinic ABA platforms adapted by some agencies, and spreadsheets for human rights committee reports. Gaps: capture during the shift is hard, so data is reconstructed later; there is no link between PRN or restraint use and the plan's de-escalation steps; and graphs arrive too late to change the plan.

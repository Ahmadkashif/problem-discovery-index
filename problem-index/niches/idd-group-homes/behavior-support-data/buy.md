# Clinic ABA Software Adapted to Twenty-Four-Hour Houses

**Niche:** [[niches/idd-group-homes/behavior-support-data/profile|Behaviour Support Plans & Restrictive Intervention Data]]
**Industry:** [[industries/idd-group-homes|IDD Group Homes]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** ABA platforms already do data collection, graphing and plan management for autism clinics; a group home's data is collected by rotating DSPs across three shifts.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #time-series-forecasting #worker-facing #compliance #data-integration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn DSPs' observations of behaviour into data a clinician uses to revise the plan, and whoever cuts restrictive interventions per resident furthest takes the account.

## The Problem
Agencies borrow clinic ABA software for their behaviour clinicians, then find DSPs will not use it on shift.

## What Already Exists
Clinic ABA practice platforms (CentralReach, which positions itself as autism and IDD care software (search summary), among others) with session data collection, graphing and treatment plans.

## The Customization Gap
The house version needs continuous, not session-based, data collection across three shifts by staff with minimal training. It needs restrictive-intervention records with the de-escalation steps tried, human rights committee approvals and expiry dates, PRN psychotropic links to the MAR, critical incident triggers when a hold is misused, and summary reports for the person's annual planning meeting. It must work in the agency's IDD record system rather than a separate clinic login.

## Target Customer
Agencies whose behaviour clinicians already use a clinic ABA platform.

## Impact If Solved
Clinicians and DSPs work from the same data, and restrictive interventions become visible within ~1–7 days rather than up to ~90 days later at the quarterly committee meeting (estimate).

# Critical Incident Reporting & Investigations

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/idd-group-homes|IDD Group Homes]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get each critical incident reported, investigated and closed with corrective action inside the state's timeframes, and whoever keeps the share of incidents closed late lowest takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$700M a year in incident-system licences, provider investigator and compliance staff time, house-manager and DSP reporting time, and state-side incident systems (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~2% of facility revenue (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — first reports go by phone to an on-call supervisor; narratives are retyped into state portals, investigations run on Word documents and email
**Target Buyer:** Director of quality and compliance at a provider; incident management director at a state DD agency
**Automation Potential:** Medium-High — classification, deadline tracking, packet assembly and corrective-action follow-up are rule-bound; the investigation judgement is not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
From 9 July 2027 the Access Rule requires every state to define critical incidents (abuse, neglect, exploitation, misuse of restrictive interventions or seclusion, medication errors reaching a poison centre or hospital, unexplained deaths). Providers must report within state timeframes, and states must start, finish and correct at least 90% of investigations on time. From 9 July 2029 states need an electronic system that tracks and trends incidents, and they must use claims and other data to find unreported ones (89 FR 40542). HHS OIG audits found up to 99% of group-home critical incidents unreported to the required agencies (OIG). The IDD version differs from assisted living's state incident reporting ([[niches/assisted-living-communities/state-licensing-incident-reporting/profile|🟠 State Licensing Inspections & Incident Reporting]]). Investigations are mandatory and often run by the provider itself, restrictive interventions are a federal category, and the federal 90% minimum now sits over every state.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Therap general event reports (a state system in some states), state-built portals, provider record-system incident modules, and spreadsheets for investigation tracking. Gaps: the same event retyped into several systems; investigation clocks tracked by hand; corrective actions rarely verified as closed; no linkage of ER visits to reports.

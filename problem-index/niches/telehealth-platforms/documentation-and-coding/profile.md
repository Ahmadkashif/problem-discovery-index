# Documentation & Coding

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/telehealth-platforms|Telehealth Platforms]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Whether a clinician spends as long writing the note as conducting the visit, on a record that mostly goes nowhere.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.8B — 6% of US virtual care delivery
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~6%
**Digital Adoption:** Moderate — ambient documentation tooling is arriving quickly
**Target Buyer:** Platform revenue cycle and clinical operations; clinicians
**Automation Potential:** Very high — this is the clearest generative application in healthcare

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche

The clinician spends as long documenting the visit as conducting it, on a record that will not reach the patient's actual doctor.

Documentation in virtual care carries the same burden as everywhere else in medicine — it is the legal record, the billing substrate and the clinical communication — with two aggravating features. The encounters are short, so documentation is a larger proportion of the total; and the record frequently serves no downstream clinical purpose, because it goes nowhere, which makes the effort feel wasted in a way that institutional documentation does not.

The niche is distinct because it is the most automatable thing in the industry and because the automation arrived recently and unevenly. Ambient documentation is genuinely working in institutional settings and is not uniformly deployed here, in the setting where the structured intake and clean two-party audio make it easiest.

## Current Tools & Gaps

Templates and structured note formats. Dot phrases and macros. Coding assistance from the EHR. Some platforms have deployed ambient documentation tools; adoption is uneven. Asynchronous encounters generate documentation from questionnaire responses with clinician additions.

The gaps are deployment and reuse. Virtual encounters have the cleanest audio and the most structured intake in medicine, which makes them the easiest ambient documentation case, and many platforms have not implemented it. And the note, once written, is used for billing and stored — it is rarely reused for the patient thread, the primary care write-back, the progress summary, or the structured decision record that outcome measurement needs, though it contains all of them.

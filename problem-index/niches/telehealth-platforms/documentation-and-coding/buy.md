# Buy: Ambient Documentation Adapted to Short and Asynchronous Encounters

**Niche:** [[niches/telehealth-platforms/documentation-and-coding/profile|Documentation & Coding]]
**Industry:** [[industries/telehealth-platforms|Telehealth Platforms]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Ambient documentation products are built for an in-person consultation with room audio and an EHR behind them; virtual encounters are shorter, cleaner, sometimes silent, and the EHR may be minimal.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #compliance #data-integration #automation #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Whether ambient scribing tuned for a twenty-minute in-person visit works on a nine-minute video call and an asynchronous questionnaire.

## The Problem

Ambient clinical documentation is the fastest-moving category in healthcare software. Products capture the consultation, generate a structured note, and are demonstrably reducing documentation time in institutional settings, with several mature vendors and real evidence behind them.

Virtual care is in some ways the easiest deployment — two speakers, clean digital audio, no room noise, structured intake already collected — and in other ways a poor fit. The encounters are short, so a product tuned to a twenty-minute narrative consultation produces over-long notes. A meaningful share of virtual care is asynchronous, with no conversation to capture at all. And the platform's clinical system may be lightweight rather than a major EHR the vendor integrates with.

## What Already Exists

Abridge, Nuance DAX, Ambience and the ambient documentation category, plus the generative documentation features now appearing inside EHRs. Medical speech recognition. Coding assistance products. Template and macro systems. The capability is real and improving quickly.

## The Customization Gap

**The encounter is short and the note should be shorter.** Products tuned for in-person consultations generate comprehensive notes from a rich conversation. A nine-minute focused encounter needs a concise note, and over-generation creates review burden that eliminates the saving. Tuning for brevity is a real adaptation and not a setting.

**Asynchronous encounters have no audio.** A substantial share of virtual care is questionnaire-based with clinician review and no conversation. Generating documentation from structured responses plus the clinician's decision is a different input modality that no ambient product handles, and it needs building.

**The structured intake is an asset the products do not consume.** Virtual platforms collect a detailed structured history before the encounter. An ambient product that ignores it and derives everything from the conversation is discarding the best data available and producing notes that contradict the intake.

**The EHR integration target is different.** These vendors integrate deeply with the major institutional EHRs. A telehealth platform frequently has its own lightweight clinical system, so the integration is an API project rather than a supported connector — cheaper in some ways, unsupported in others.

**Consent and recording posture differ.** In-person ambient capture operates under institutional policy. Here the session may already be recorded for safeguarding or quality, the patient is remote, and consent for capture and generation has to be obtained and evidenced in the platform's own flow.

## Target Customer

Platform clinical operations evaluating ambient documentation, who need to know which parts of the vendor's value transfers to a nine-minute encounter. Also the ambient vendors, for whom virtual care is a large, technically favourable deployment context their in-person tuning does not yet fit.

## Impact If Solved

The speech recognition, note generation and coding capability gets bought at the point where it is best in class, and the brevity tuning, asynchronous path, intake consumption, lightweight integration and consent flow get built. Concretely: documentation time falls in the setting where it is the largest proportion of the clinician's work.

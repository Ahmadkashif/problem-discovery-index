# Prescribing Pattern Accountability

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/telehealth-platforms|Telehealth Platforms]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Whether the platform measures its own prescribing against clinical norms, and what it does when the answer is unflattering.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$3.24B — 40% of the prescribing and decision-making niche
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~11% of US virtual care delivery
**Digital Adoption:** Very low — measured where regulators have required it, rarely otherwise
**Target Buyer:** Regulators, payers and state boards; platform legal and compliance; medical directors
**Automation Potential:** High — the computation is descriptive statistics on the platform's own data

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche

This is the half pointed at the business. What share of visits for this presentation end in a prescription, and how does that compare to guideline-concordant rates and to in-person practice for the same presentation? Which clinicians sit far outside the distribution after case mix adjustment? Does the prescribing rate move with the subscription structure, the time of day, the throughput target, or the clinician's compensation model?

Three things make it a distinct market. The measurement is aimed at the platform's own model rather than at improving any individual encounter. The finding is consequential and discoverable — a written record showing that a platform's prescribing for a given condition runs far above clinical norms is a document that exists afterwards. And it names individual clinicians, whose licences are at stake and who are contractors with no institutional protection.

It is also the half with the strongest external demand. Direct-to-consumer prescribing has already drawn federal scrutiny and enforcement, particularly around controlled substances, and the regulatory position on remote prescribing of controlled substances has been repeatedly extended rather than settled — which means the question is being asked whether or not the platform has prepared an answer.

## Current Tools & Gaps

PDMP checks where mandated. Payer-side prescribing analytics, which exist and are aimed at network providers rather than at telehealth platforms specifically. State medical board oversight, which is complaint-driven. Some platforms run internal clinical quality review, generally case-based rather than distributional.

The gaps are that almost nobody computes their own prescribing distribution against external benchmarks, that case mix adjustment is absent where any measurement exists, and that the governance — what happens when a clinician is an outlier — is undefined, which is itself a reason the measurement does not get made.

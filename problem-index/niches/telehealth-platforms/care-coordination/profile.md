# Care Coordination & Referral Closure

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/telehealth-platforms|Telehealth Platforms]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Whether anyone finds out if the referral was taken up, the authorisation approved and the medication actually dispensed.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$2.7B — 9% of US virtual care delivery
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~9%
**Digital Adoption:** Very low — telephone, fax and four systems that do not connect
**Target Buyer:** Platform operations leadership; payers
**Automation Potential:** High — most of it is status tracking and structured exchange

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche

The referral was made, the specialist needs records, the pharmacy needs a prior authorisation, and a coordinator is on the phone reconstructing a patient's care from four systems that do not connect.

A virtual encounter frequently ends with an instruction rather than a resolution: see a specialist, get a test, collect this medication, come back if it worsens. Whether any of that happened is the difference between care and advice, and almost nothing in this industry tracks it.

The niche is distinct because it is about what happens after the encounter and outside the platform — a workflow spanning specialists, labs, pharmacies and payers, executed by a coordinator with a telephone. It is separable from continuity, which is about the record, and from outcome measurement, which is about whether the patient got better; this is about whether the plan was even carried out.

## Current Tools & Gaps

E-prescribing to pharmacies, which works. Lab ordering where the platform has a network. Referral generation as a document or a message, which is mostly where it ends. Prior authorisation handled by phone, fax and payer portals. Coordinators working across a payer portal, the EHR, a spreadsheet and a phone.

The gaps are closure and status. A referral is issued and its fate is unknown. A prior authorisation is submitted and tracked in someone's head. A prescription is sent and whether it was collected is not checked, though the network reports it. There is no single view of a patient's open items, so nothing is systematically chased and the things that fall through are discovered when the patient returns worse.

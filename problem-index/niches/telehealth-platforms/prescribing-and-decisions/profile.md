# Prescribing & Clinical Decision-Making

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/telehealth-platforms|Telehealth Platforms]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Whether the decision to prescribe, refer or reassure is being made with the information and the time it requires.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$8.1B — 27% of US virtual care delivery
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~27%
**Digital Adoption:** Moderate — e-prescribing is solved, the decision behind it is unsupported
**Target Buyer:** Platform clinical leadership and medical directors
**Automation Potential:** High for support; the decision itself stays with the clinician

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche

A clinician deciding whether to prescribe, refer or reassure is doing so from a questionnaire and a short interaction, without the longitudinal record and without the physical examination, and the platform's metrics reward the visit closing rather than the problem resolving.

That is the central clinical tension of virtual care. The decision is the product — everything else the platform does is logistics around it — and it is made under three simultaneous constraints: less information than in-person care, less time than the clinician would choose, and a commercial structure whose revenue depends on visits completing and, in the direct-to-consumer segment, frequently on a prescription being written.

The niche is distinct because it is where the industry's regulatory exposure sits. Direct-to-consumer platforms combining a subscription, a short assessment and a prescription have faced federal scrutiny and enforcement, particularly around controlled substances, and the regulatory position on remote prescribing of controlled substances has been repeatedly extended rather than settled.

## Current Tools & Gaps

E-prescribing with pharmacy routing, formulary checking, and interaction alerts from the EHR vendor. Structured intake questionnaires. Clinical protocols and templates for common presentations, which vary widely in quality. Prescription drug monitoring program checks where required.

The gaps are on both sides of the decision. The clinician lacks the patient's actual history, which usually exists elsewhere and is not retrieved; lacks time to pursue an uncertain presentation; and lacks any feedback on whether previous similar decisions worked out. And the platform lacks any measurement of its own prescribing patterns against clinical norms, so it cannot tell whether its model is producing appropriate care or whether a particular clinician is an outlier.

# The Contract Clinician

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/telehealth-platforms|Telehealth Platforms]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Whether the clinician carrying the professional and licensure risk of every decision gets any feedback, support or say in the conditions they work under.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$3.6B — 12% of US virtual care delivery
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~12%
**Digital Adoption:** Low — a queue, a target and a payout screen
**Target Buyer:** Clinicians directly; platforms competing for clinical supply
**Automation Potential:** High — the feedback and the accounting are both derivable

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche

Physicians, nurse practitioners, physician assistants and therapists work these platforms as contractors, many across several at once. They carry the licence, the malpractice exposure and the professional obligation, and they work to throughput expectations set by someone else against a per-visit rate they did not negotiate.

What they do not have is any of the things a clinician in an institution has. No feedback on whether their decisions worked. No colleagues to ask about a difficult case. No peer review that is not disciplinary. No visibility into how their practice compares. No portable record of the volume and quality of work they have done. And, working across multiple platforms, no consolidated view of their own income, their licensure obligations or their CME.

The niche is distinct because the subject is the clinician's own professional life, and because much of it is buildable independently of any platform.

## Current Tools & Gaps

Platform dashboards showing visits completed, hours worked, satisfaction scores and earnings. Credentialing and licensure managed by the platform for its own purposes. CME obtained independently. Malpractice coverage arranged by the platform or the clinician depending on the model.

The gaps are professional rather than administrative. No decision feedback, which is the central one. No case consultation route — a clinician facing a difficult presentation at 11pm has nobody to ask. No cross-platform view of income, hours, licensure and scheduling. And no portable evidence of their practice, which means a clinician's record of thousands of encounters is the platform's data.

# Build: A Professional Instrument for the Virtual Clinician

**Niche:** [[niches/telehealth-platforms/the-contract-clinician/profile|The Contract Clinician]]
**Industry:** [[industries/telehealth-platforms|Telehealth Platforms]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Give the clinician their own decision feedback, their own practice comparison, their own cross-platform accounting and a portable record of the work they have done.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #bayesian-inference #confidence-intervals #evaluation-metrics #survival-analysis #data-integration #worker-facing #compliance
**Contested on:** Whether a clinician can assemble a professional record of their own practice from platforms that consider it their data.

## The Problem

A clinician completes four thousand virtual encounters a year across two platforms. They cannot tell you their prescribing rate for any presentation, their return-visit rate, how either compares to peers, what share of their prescriptions were collected, or what happened to any patient after the video call ended.

An institutional clinician has some of this — peer review, morbidity and mortality conference, quality reporting, colleagues. A contractor on a virtual platform has none. Their entire professional feedback consists of satisfaction scores and the absence of complaints.

The administrative picture is equally fragmented. Income across platforms, hours, state licences with their renewal dates and CME requirements, malpractice coverage, and the tax position of contractor income in multiple states — all held in pieces across systems that do not connect.

## Why Nobody Has Built This

Platforms have no incentive. A clinician with portable evidence of their practice quality can move; one with cross-platform income visibility can compare; one with decision feedback might work more slowly.

Third parties face a data access problem that is real but not prohibitive. A clinician can export their own encounter data in most cases, and much of what matters — hours, visits, income, licences, CME — is in their own hands already. The decision feedback is harder because outcomes live with the platform, though return visits within a platform and dispense data are both things a clinician can reasonably ask for.

And the market has been thought of as small, which underestimates it: this is a large, growing, well-paid and increasingly multi-platform workforce with a genuine professional deficit.

## What to Build

A clinician-side professional record and practice instrument.

**Consolidate the administrative picture first**, because it drives adoption. Income by platform, hours, effective hourly rate including documentation time, tax position across states, licence inventory with renewal dates and CME requirements per state, and malpractice coverage. Multi-state licensure is a real administrative burden with real deadlines, and nothing currently manages it.

**Compute realised hourly rate including documentation.** A clinician paid per visit who spends as long documenting as consulting has an effective rate far below the headline, and almost none of them has calculated it. It is the number that governs which platforms are worth working.

**Build the practice record.** Encounters by presentation, decisions, prescribing rates, return rates where obtainable, patient feedback, and volume by state and modality. Owned by the clinician, exportable, presentable — for credentialing, for a new platform, for a board, for their own understanding.

**Add peer comparison where a cohort exists.** Once enough clinicians contribute, a clinician can see their own prescribing and return rates against a peer distribution, anonymously and voluntarily. This is the professional feedback the institutional world provides and this workforce lacks entirely, and it can be built outside any platform.

**Track licensure properly.** State licences, compact participation, renewal deadlines, per-state CME requirements and credentialing documents in one place with reminders. This is pure administration and it is the reason a clinician installs the thing.

**Keep it the clinician's.** The proposition only works if the data belongs to them and the tool is not reporting to a platform. That is both the ethical position and the product's entire differentiation.

## Target Customer

Clinicians directly, particularly the substantial number working across multiple platforms as a primary income. Also professional associations, and platforms competing for clinical supply, for whom supporting a clinician's portable record is a recruitment advantage against incumbents whose clinicians are locked in.

## Impact If Built

A clinician gets the professional feedback the rest of medicine takes for granted and this workforce has never had. Their realised rate becomes visible, which governs where they work. And their practice record becomes portable, which is what makes a contractor workforce mobile — and mobility is what gives them any influence over the conditions they work under.

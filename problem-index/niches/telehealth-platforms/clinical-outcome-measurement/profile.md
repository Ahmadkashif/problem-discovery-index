# Clinical Outcome Measurement

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/telehealth-platforms|Telehealth Platforms]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Whether the platform knows if the patient got better.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$5.4B — 18% of US virtual care delivery
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~18%
**Digital Adoption:** Very low — throughput and satisfaction stand in for resolution
**Target Buyer:** Platform clinical leadership; payers and employers contracting virtual care
**Automation Potential:** High — much of the signal is in the platform's own data

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche

The metrics are visit volume, wait time and satisfaction, and the question a clinician actually needs answered — did my decision work — is asked by nobody.

Whether the symptom resolved, whether the patient ended up in an emergency department a week later, whether the prescription was refilled or abandoned: all of that is either in the platform's own data or obtainable through claims and exchange networks, and is not systematically examined.

The niche is distinct from prescribing because it is about what followed rather than what was decided, and because its buyer is increasingly external. Payers and employers contracting virtual care are moving from access metrics toward outcome and total-cost measures, and a platform that can demonstrate resolution has a commercial argument that one reporting satisfaction does not.

## Current Tools & Gaps

Post-visit satisfaction surveys, sent routinely and answered by a self-selected minority. Visit and wait time dashboards. Some platforms send a follow-up message a few days after a visit; few analyse the responses systematically. Payer-contracted platforms sometimes report utilisation and cost measures derived from claims.

The gaps are the return-visit signal, which sits unanalysed in the platform's own database; the fill signal, which is available through the e-prescribing network; and the downstream escalation signal, which requires external linkage and is the one most worth having. Also absent is any condition-specific outcome instrument — the validated symptom scales that exist for many conditions and take a patient ninety seconds.

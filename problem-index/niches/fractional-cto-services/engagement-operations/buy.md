# Buy: Professional Services Automation Sized Down

**Niche:** Engagement Operations
**Industry:** [[industries/fractional-cto-services|Fractional CTO Services]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Professional services automation solved engagement lifecycle management for firms of fifty and has nothing for the three-person practice that needs the same thing at a hundredth of the complexity.
**Tags:** #workflow-orchestration #automation #data-integration #evaluation-metrics #compliance #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Whether the administrative shell around an engagement is set up once and reused or rebuilt for every client.

## The Problem

Managing an engagement lifecycle — scope, contract, resourcing, milestones, time, billing, close — is a mature software category. Professional services automation platforms handle it well for consultancies, agencies and accounting firms, with real depth in resource planning, margin tracking and multi-project portfolios.

None of it reaches the fractional practitioner, for a simple reason of shape. PSA is built around a firm with a bench, a pipeline and a resourcing problem: who is available, what is the utilisation, what is the margin on this project. A fractional practice has one to fifteen practitioners, no bench, and no resourcing problem worth modelling — its problems are the parts PSA treats as peripheral, which are access provisioning, scheduling across client organisations, document collection and engagement close-down.

So the practitioner runs on a scheduling link, an e-signature account, an invoicing tool and a spreadsheet, and does the joining by hand.

## What Already Exists

PSA and project delivery: Kantata (formerly Mavenlink), Scoro, Projectworks, Accelo, Productive, and the lighter end — Harvest, Toggl, FreshBooks — which covers time and invoicing only.

Legal and accounting practice management: Clio, Karbon, Canopy, Practice Ignition. Karbon and Practice Ignition are the more interesting references because they handle repeatable client engagements with checklists, client-side requests and automated chasing, which is much closer to the fractional shape than generic PSA is.

Adjacent components all exist individually: e-signature, scheduling (Calendly and its class), client portals, secure file exchange, document request and chasing tools from the accounting world.

## The Customization Gap

**Engagement templates, not projects.** PSA models a project with tasks and resources. A fractional engagement is a repeatable type — assessment, diligence, ongoing advisory — that should instantiate as a complete shell. Karbon's work templates for accounting are the right pattern and are specific to accounting workflows.

**Access provisioning is unmodelled anywhere.** No platform in any adjacent category has a concept of "the technical access this engagement needs, requested, justified, tracked, and revoked at close." It is the single largest administrative cost in this profession and there is no software anywhere that addresses it.

**Cross-organisation scheduling.** Scheduling tools handle one-to-one booking. Arranging eight interviews across a client's organisation in a two-week window with ordering dependencies is a different problem that everyone solves by email.

**Client-side visibility.** The accounting platforms have this and PSA largely does not: a portal where the client sees outstanding requests and can act. It is the thing that most reduces elapsed time, because delay is nearly always a visibility problem rather than a refusal.

**Close-down obligations.** Nothing anywhere tracks access revocation and data deletion against the terms of an engagement letter. Given how much confidential material advisory practitioners hold, this is a surprisingly large unmanaged exposure.

**Sizing and price.** PSA implementation takes weeks and pricing assumes a firm with an operations team. This market needs self-serve setup in an hour and a price an individual pays on a card.

**Multi-client separation.** The practitioner works inside four client organisations' systems under four NDAs, and no PSA models the confidentiality boundary — which connects directly to the tenancy problem in [[niches/fractional-cto-services/the-fractional-cto/profile|🟣 The Fractional CTO]].

## Target Customer

Karbon or Practice Ignition are the most credible adapters — their engagement-template and client-request patterns transfer almost directly, and fractional executive services are an adjacent vertical to the accounting practices they serve.

The lighter PSA vendors — Productive, Accelo — are the alternative, reaching down-market rather than across.

Buyers are fractional practitioners of every function, plus the boutique practices that employ them. The market is small per function and substantial across all of them, since fractional arrangements now span technology, finance, marketing, people and legal.

## Impact If Solved

Days of senior time recovered per engagement, concentrated at the start where they are worth most.

Access provisioning gets treated as a managed process for the first time, with a standard package and an auditable revocation — which benefits the client's security posture as much as the practitioner's schedule.

And a fast-growing category of work gets operational tooling proportionate to its actual shape, rather than being told to buy software built for a fifty-person consultancy or to keep using a spreadsheet.

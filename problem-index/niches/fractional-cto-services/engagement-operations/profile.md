# Engagement Operations

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/fractional-cto-services|Fractional CTO Services]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Whether the administrative shell around an engagement — scoping, access, contracting, scheduling, invoicing — is set up once and reused, or rebuilt by a senior practitioner for every client.

## Profile

**Market Size:** ~$90M
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~3%
**Digital Adoption:** Low — spreadsheets, email and a template contract
**Target Buyer:** Practice operations, independent practitioners
**Automation Potential:** Very high — it is entirely repeatable process

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche

Around every engagement is a shell of administration: scoping the work and pricing it, writing the statement of work, getting the NDA and the engagement letter signed, arranging access to repositories and systems, booking the interviews with people across the client's organisation, tracking which documents have been requested and which have arrived, scheduling the sessions, invoicing, and closing everything down at the end.

For a full-time employee none of this exists. For a fractional practitioner it happens four times a year per client, and the practitioner does most of it personally because there is nobody else. It is the single largest unbilled time sink in the profession after context reload, and unlike context reload it has no judgement in it whatsoever.

The access piece deserves particular mention. Getting a guest advisor into a client's repository, issue tracker, cloud console and Slack routinely takes a week of an eight-week engagement, involves a security review that nobody scheduled, and is rebuilt from nothing at every client because there is no standard shape for what a technical advisor needs.

This is the smallest niche in the industry and the most straightforwardly fixable, which is why it sits at the automatable end alongside [[niches/fractional-cto-services/deliverable-production/profile|⚡ Assessment Deliverable Production]].

## Current Tools & Gaps

Practitioners use general business tooling: an e-signature service, a scheduling link, an invoicing tool or an accountant, a spreadsheet for the document request list. Professional services automation platforms — Kantata, Scoro, Projectworks, Harvest — handle time, billing and resourcing for larger firms and are oversized for a three-person practice. Proposal tools cover the statement of work. Nothing is joined up.

The gaps are mundane and expensive. No standard access package for what a technical advisor needs, so every client's security review starts from first principles. No engagement checklist that runs itself, so things are forgotten and discovered late. Interview scheduling across eight people in a client organisation, by email, in a two-week window, is a genuine multi-day task done by hand every time. The document request list lives in a spreadsheet and chasing it is manual. And engagement close-down — revoking access, archiving material, deleting client data under the terms of the engagement letter — is done from memory, which is a compliance exposure nobody has priced.

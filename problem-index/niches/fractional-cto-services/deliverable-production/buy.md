# Buy: Document Automation From Legal and Proposals

**Niche:** Assessment Deliverable Production
**Industry:** [[industries/fractional-cto-services|Fractional CTO Services]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Document assembly is a mature category in legal and in proposals, and technical advisory produces the same shape of document with none of the tooling.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #bert #word-embeddings #evaluation-metrics #data-integration #workflow-orchestration #automation
**Contested on:** Whether the report that carries the engagement's judgement is assembled from the evidence that produced it or retyped from scratch.

## The Problem

Assembling a long, structured, high-stakes document from reusable components and client-specific content is a solved problem with an industry behind it. Legal document assembly handles clause libraries, conditional inclusion, version control and consistency checking across documents far longer and more consequential than an assessment report. Proposal automation handles content libraries, audience variants, brand enforcement and collaborative review at high volume.

Technical advisory produces documents of exactly that shape — structured sections, reusable framing for recurring findings, client-specific evidence, multiple audience versions, a house style that matters — and uses a word processor and a copy of last month's report.

The adaptation is not conceptually hard. It is that nobody has done it, because the advisory market is small and fragmented compared with the legal and sales markets these products were built for.

## What Already Exists

Proposal and sales document automation: PandaDoc, Proposify, Qwilr, Loopio and RFPIO, with content libraries, dynamic assembly, brand enforcement and analytics. Loopio and RFPIO in particular have solved the reusable-answer-library problem at scale, which is very close to what a findings language library needs.

Legal document assembly: HotDocs, Contract Express, Documate and the assembly features inside the CLM platforms — mature conditional logic, clause libraries and versioning.

Report generation in adjacent professional services: audit and financial reporting tools that bind figures to a source so the document updates when the underlying data changes, which is the property that matters most here and is entirely standard in accounting.

Business intelligence platforms handle chart generation and embedding.

## The Customization Gap

**Evidence binding is the missing link.** Financial reporting tools bind a figure in a document to its source. Proposal tools do not, because proposals do not cite computed evidence. An advisory report must, and the connection to the analysis layer is the whole adaptation.

**Findings are not clauses.** Legal assembly selects from a library of alternative texts by condition. An advisory finding is partly reusable framing and partly a unique claim about this client, and the split between them has no analogue in either source category.

**Audience variants are a first-class requirement.** The same finding set must render at three depths for three audiences with guaranteed consistency. Proposal tools do variants by conditional sections, which drift; the advisory case needs one source of truth rendered several ways.

**The narrative must stay the practitioner's.** Proposal automation assumes the content library supplies the text. Advisory practitioners will not accept generated argument, and a product that appears to write their opinion will be rejected regardless of quality. Automate assembly, evidence and consistency; leave the claim alone.

**Consistency checking across the document set.** Severity stated once, numbers agreeing everywhere, no residue from the previous client. Legal tools have versions of this for defined terms and cross-references; nobody has it for findings and severities.

**Volume economics.** Proposal tools are priced for organisations producing hundreds of documents a month. A boutique produces a few dozen a year at far higher individual value, which is a different pricing shape entirely.

## Target Customer

Loopio or RFPIO are the most plausible adapters — the reusable-content-library problem is the one they have solved best, and professional services deliverables are an adjacent expansion from the proposals they already serve.

The financial reporting vendors are the more interesting candidate on capability, because evidence binding is native to them and it is the hardest part.

Buyers are boutique advisory and diligence practices, and the larger consultancies' technology practices, which produce these documents in real volume and have operations functions that buy tooling.

## Impact If Solved

A mature category reaches a profession that has been doing the work by hand, with an immediate two-to-three-day saving per engagement and no change in what the practitioner is asked to think about.

Evidence binding brings accounting's discipline to technical advisory: numbers in a report trace to the analysis that produced them, which is a standard the category has never held itself to and which clients would value immediately if they knew it was available.

And a firm's reports become consistent across practitioners, which in a market where quality is otherwise unobservable is a real commercial asset.

# Assessment Deliverable Production

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/fractional-cto-services|Fractional CTO Services]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Whether the report that carries the engagement's judgement is assembled from the evidence that produced it or retyped from scratch in the last three days.

## Profile

**Market Size:** ~$180M
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~6%
**Digital Adoption:** Low — a word processor and a previous engagement's file
**Target Buyer:** Practice operations, boutique firm owners, independent practitioners
**Automation Potential:** Very high — it is assembly, formatting and consistency work

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche

Every engagement ends in a document. An assessment report, a diligence report, a strategy memo, a board deck. It is the only thing the client physically receives, it is what the fee is nominally for, and producing it consumes a startling share of the engagement — commonly two to four days of a ten-day assessment, and in diligence a similar fraction under harder deadlines.

Most of that time is not judgement. It is opening the last engagement's report, deleting the client-specific content, reformatting, retyping findings from notes, rebuilding charts, chasing the house style, checking the executive summary matches the body, and producing the version for the board that says the same things with less detail. The practitioner is a senior, expensive person doing document assembly.

It is a distinct niche because the work is genuinely mechanical, the buyer is practice operations rather than the practitioner, and the value is measured in recovered senior days rather than in better judgement. It sits alongside [[niches/fractional-cto-services/engagement-operations/profile|⚡ Engagement Operations]] as the pure-efficiency pair in an industry that otherwise sells judgement.

## Current Tools & Gaps

Word processors and presentation software, plus a folder of previous engagements used as the real template system. Some larger practices maintain proper template libraries and a style guide. Proposal and document automation tools exist in adjacent professional services — PandaDoc, Proposify, and the heavier document assembly platforms used in legal — and have almost no penetration here. Assistants are increasingly used for drafting, informally and without structure.

The gaps: nothing connects the evidence gathered during the engagement to the report that cites it, so every figure is retyped and every chart rebuilt by hand. Nothing generates the audience variants — board summary, technical appendix, investment committee memo — from one source, so the three documents drift apart and the practitioner reconciles them manually. Nothing enforces internal consistency, which is where reports most often embarrass their authors: a severity in the summary that does not match the body, a number quoted two ways. And nothing carries the firm's accumulated language for recurring findings, so every practitioner writes the technical-debt paragraph again from nothing.

# Niche Analysis — Fractional CTO Services

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/fractional-cto-services|Fractional CTO Services]]

## Niche Selection

This is a profession that sells rapid assessment of complex systems and has no instrument for it. The evidence needed to form a technical opinion is substantially derivable from repository history, issue trackers and delivery data, and practitioners form it by reading and asking. The corpus that would make any of it calibrated is their own history across engagements, and nobody retains it. The eight niches below follow that, and then the consequential decisions the assessment feeds and the people on either side of the engagement.

## Why These Niches

Technical assessment and due diligence are the same instrument pointed at different decisions, and both are performed on interviews and a few days of reading against consequences measured in millions. Technology selection and handover are the two places where the advice outruns its evidence — a platform recommendation built on vendor material and the advisor's last three projects, and a direction set in motion whose reasoning leaves with its author. The two underserved audiences are the practitioner holding four unrelated complex situations at once, and the senior engineer who inherits a half-executed plan. The last two are mechanical: producing the deliverable, and running the engagement.

## Filter Notes

Seven of the eight are terminal — each names one contest that every serious competitor is fighting over, and decomposing further would produce features rather than markets.

**Technical assessment** is not. It names the core work, and the two gaps in it are separate and addressable separately. Evidence extraction asks what can be derived about a codebase, a team and a delivery process from the artefacts they already produce — where change concentrates, which components consume effort, how work flows and where it stalls, what capacity actually looks like — a measurement problem over repository history and issue trackers, deliverable as an instrument for a single engagement, and useful from the first day it exists. Assessment calibration asks whether this practitioner's judgements have historically been right — a corpus problem over their own past engagements and what happened afterwards, which requires retaining assessments and following up, produces nothing for a year, and is what would turn accumulated pattern recognition into something the firm owns rather than something that leaves with the person. The instrument improves one assessment; the corpus grades the practitioner. A firm can build the first without the second and almost all would, which is precisely why the second is where the durable advantage sits.

Two adjacent candidates were rejected as belonging elsewhere: **implementing the platform decisions the assessment recommends** belongs to [[industries/saas-implementation-partners|SaaS Implementation Partners]], and **the infrastructure engineering work itself** sits with [[industries/cloud-infrastructure-consultants|Cloud Infrastructure Consultants]].

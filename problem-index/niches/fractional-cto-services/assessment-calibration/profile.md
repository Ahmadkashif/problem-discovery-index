# Assessment Calibration

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/fractional-cto-services|Fractional CTO Services]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Whether an advisory practice retains its own assessments and their eventual outcomes well enough to know which of its judgements have historically been right.

## Profile

**Market Size:** ~$320M
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~11%
**Digital Adoption:** Very low — nothing is retained past delivery
**Target Buyer:** Practice owners, private equity operating partners, insurers of advisory liability
**Automation Potential:** Moderate — extraction and scoring automate, outcome capture does not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche

This is the corpus half of technical assessment, and it is a different business from its sibling. [[niches/fractional-cto-services/evidence-extraction/profile|🎯 Evidence Extraction]] makes one assessment better on the day it runs. Calibration asks whether this practitioner's assessments have historically been right, which requires retaining what was concluded, following up on what happened, and scoring the pair — and produces nothing at all for the first year.

The two are separable in every way that matters. Different data (the client's artefacts versus the practice's own history), different time horizon (hours versus years), different buyer within the same firm (the practitioner versus the owner), different failure mode. A firm can build the instrument and never build the corpus, and almost all of them would, because the instrument pays this quarter.

The contest is over whether a firm's judgement can be made into a measured thing. Every practice claims calibrated judgement. None can produce a distribution of its past estimates against actuals, a hit rate on its risk flags, or a record of which recommendations were executed and what followed. The firm that can has an argument no competitor can answer, and an asset that survives the departure of the person who built it.

## Current Tools & Gaps

Nothing in this space is purpose-built. Forecasting-calibration practice exists in intelligence analysis and in superforecasting research, with well-developed scoring rules and a strong literature on how calibration improves with feedback — and has essentially no presence in technical advisory. Knowledge management platforms store documents without extracting comparable records. Professional services automation tracks time and margin, not conclusions. CRM records the engagement, not what was said inside it.

The gaps are structural. Assessments are bespoke prose written to persuade a particular board, so the comparable layer has to be extracted rather than captured. Outcomes sit on the other side of a boundary that closes at engagement end and is never reopened. Confidentiality obligations are treated as prohibiting reuse when what most engagement letters actually prohibit is identification, a distinction almost no practice has bothered to establish. And the people who would supply the material are the ones whose personal leverage the corpus would reduce.

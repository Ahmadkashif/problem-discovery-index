## Why These Niches

Injectables and laser services together represent 60%+ of med spa revenue, making them the dominant market-share segments with distinct operational challenges (injectable inventory management vs. laser device utilization optimization). Body contouring and regenerative aesthetics are fast-growing but digitally neglected — practitioners track multi-session treatment protocols on paper or in spreadsheets. Male aesthetics is the fastest-growing demographic segment but med spa marketing, intake forms, and treatment protocols are all designed for female clients. Rural med spas face unique challenges around medical director oversight and limited referral networks. The two automation niches target the highest-ROI operational workflows: compliance documentation (critical given state-by-state medical supervision requirements) and client retention (where the difference between 2 and 4 annual visits per client doubles revenue).

## Why These Pockets

**No pocket qualified**, and the reason is the industry's age rather than its structure. Med spas are a 12-15% annual growth segment with roughly 8,000 operators, and the insight layer that would normally form above a maturing industry has not formed yet. Every position exists at a scale one order of magnitude below where it would qualify.

The compliance advisory pocket illustrates it precisely. What a med spa may legally do — who may inject, what supervision is required, whether a non-physician may own the practice — differs substantially by state across medical practice, nursing scope, and corporate practice of medicine rules, and changes constantly. That is exactly the permanent externally-driven research burden that made accounting, employment law, and immigration rich in this sweep. Here it is served by fifteen-to-sixty-person practices and an association's compliance team, because the operator base is 8,000 businesses rather than 500,000.

Where the real analytical capability sits, it belongs to pharmaceutical companies. The injectable manufacturers hold the industry's entire clinical evidence base — registration trials, post-marketing surveillance across enormous real-world use, and the injection technique training every practitioner receives — and generate all of it to sell vials.

The most interesting near-miss is the smallest. Pass 1 records that before-and-after photo systems exist and lack standardized analysis, and that treatment recommendation is entirely provider-dependent with no decision support. The practice platforms hold paired outcome imagery joined to treatment records across thousands of practices — a genuine outcome dataset for a field that has none — behind a fifteen-person product team, treated as storage.

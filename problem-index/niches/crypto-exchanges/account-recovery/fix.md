# Three Weeks and No Timeline

**Niche:** [[niches/crypto-exchanges/account-recovery/profile|Account Recovery]]
**Industry:** [[industries/crypto-exchanges|Crypto Exchanges]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The customer submitted everything asked for, cannot see where the request stands, and has been told to wait for an unspecified period.
**Tags:** #worker-facing #quick-win #automation #workflow-orchestration #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #compliance #change-point-detection
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get a locked-out customer back to their funds without opening the door to the attacker running the same play — and whoever resolves the honest cases automatically without raising the takeover rate clears the queue that dominates support.

## The Problem
Locked out, the customer submits a document, waits, receives a request for a second document, submits it, waits again. There is no status, no estimate, no queue position, and no way to tell whether the case is being worked or is simply sitting. Their assets are visible in an app they cannot use. Support replies are generic because the agent replying cannot see the review either. This is the most common serious complaint the category produces.

## Why It's Still Broken
Recovery is a security process and security processes default to disclosing nothing, so opacity was inherited rather than chosen for this case — and the harm falls on the customer. Documents are requested sequentially because the requirements are not stated up front. The support agent and the reviewer work in different systems. And nobody reports queue duration, so the delay is not visible to anyone who could fix it.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Say what is needed, show where it stands, and measure the queue. State all requirements up front rather than one document at a time, which is the fix and removes most of the round trips immediately. Show the request's status and stage to the customer, since opacity here protects nothing an attacker does not already know. Give a realistic timeframe and update it, because an honest estimate is what turns an unbearable wait into a manageable one. Give support agents visibility into the review, as they are currently answering questions they cannot see the answer to. Report queue duration and its tail, which is a single query and will show a distribution nobody has looked at. Prioritise by balance and by case age, since the tail is where the real harm sits. Auto-resolve the cases that are unambiguous on the behavioural evidence, which clears volume and shortens everyone's wait. Explain a denial with a route to escalate, because a refusal with no reason and no appeal is the complaint that becomes public. Track how many cases are abandoned by the customer, as that number is the measure of how many people simply gave up on their own assets. And review the longest-running cases weekly, since nothing else will surface them.

## Who Feels the Pain
Customers separated from their assets for weeks with no information; support agents with no visibility; reviewers working an unprioritised queue; and exchanges whose worst public complaints all come from here.

## Impact If Fixed
Opacity was inherited from security process defaults rather than chosen for this case, and the cost falls entirely on the customer. Stating requirements up front and showing status removes the round trips and the largest source of complaint at once.

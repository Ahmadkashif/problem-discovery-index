# Model Risk Management Without Labels

**Niche:** [[niches/crypto-exchanges/screening-decision-quality/profile|Screening Decision Quality]]
**Industry:** [[industries/crypto-exchanges|Crypto Exchanges]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Banking model risk management specifies validation, documented threshold rationale and ongoing performance monitoring, all of which assume outcomes that return.
**Tags:** #compliance #confidence-intervals #hypothesis-testing #evaluation-metrics #cross-validation #bayesian-inference #descriptive-statistics #monte-carlo-methods
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to measure and defend a freeze decision whose ground truth returns on a fraction of a percent of cases — and whoever can state a precision with a defensible interval sets the threshold everyone else guesses at.

## The Problem
Model risk management is a codified discipline: independent validation, documented conceptual soundness, outcomes analysis, benchmarking, ongoing monitoring, and a governance structure that supervisors examine. Exchanges are increasingly expected to apply it to screening. Every element that depends on outcomes analysis is inapplicable as written, because the outcomes do not arrive.

## What Already Exists
Model risk frameworks with validation standards; outcomes analysis and back-testing methodologies; benchmarking against alternative models; conceptual soundness documentation; and ongoing performance monitoring with escalation.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is validation of a model whose outcomes are structurally unobservable. It requires: (1) outcomes analysis replaced by partial identification and weak-label aggregation, which is the substantive adaptation and would apply to several other controls in financial services; (2) a primary input purchased from a vendor who will not disclose its method, which conceptual soundness documentation cannot accommodate as written; (3) benchmarking against other vendors' scores as the only available comparison, which is weaker than benchmarking against outcomes but is not nothing; (4) conservatism that is not free, since over-freezing has a real customer cost that model risk frameworks built for credit do not price; and (5) an adversary adapting to the model, which makes stable historical performance an unsafe assumption.

## Target Customer
Exchange model risk and compliance functions, examiners applying bank standards to crypto controls, and model risk vendors for whom the unobservable-outcome case is unaddressed.

## Impact If Solved
Every outcome-dependent element of model risk management is inapplicable here as written. Substituting partial identification and weak-label methods is a genuine extension of the discipline, not a compliance exercise.

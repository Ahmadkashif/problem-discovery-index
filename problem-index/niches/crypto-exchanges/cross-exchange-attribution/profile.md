# Cross-Exchange Attribution

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/crypto-exchanges|Crypto Exchanges]]
**Category:** ⚡ Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn the identity-to-address junction that exchanges generate into an asset they own rather than one they rent back from a vendor — and whoever organises that first controls the input the whole industry's compliance runs on.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.4B US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~9% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Very Low — asset unexploited
**Target Buyer:** Exchange data and compliance leadership
**Automation Potential:** Very High — a labelling and pooling problem

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Every deposit and withdrawal an exchange processes joins a verified real-world identity to an on-chain address. That junction is where blockchain attribution ultimately comes from, and the exchanges produce it as a by-product of operating. The analytics vendors accumulate the cross-exchange picture and sell it back as a scored feed, with each exchange retaining only its own slice. The contest is whether the label producers can organise their own asset, and what governance would make that legitimate.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Vendor feeds, bilateral information sharing in specific cases, and industry associations. The gaps: own junction data not used for attribution; no pooling structure between exchanges; sanctions and fraud signals not shared systematically; travel rule infrastructure not exploited as an attribution channel; and no assessment of the asset's value or of the governance required.

**Industry:** [[industries/crypto-exchanges|Crypto Exchanges]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Credit bureaus and fraud consortia solved competitor data pooling with governance, contribution rules and regulatory framing, and crypto pays a vendor instead.
**Tags:** #compliance #graph-theory #data-integration #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #k-means-clustering #automation #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn the identity-to-address junction that exchanges generate into an asset they own rather than one they rent back from a vendor — and whoever organises that first controls the input the whole industry's compliance runs on.

## The Problem
Competing institutions pooling data for a shared risk purpose is solved practice. Credit bureaus, fraud consortia, card network shared intelligence and sanctions screening utilities all handle the same obstacles: antitrust, consumer consent, contribution quality, free riding, dispute rights and regulatory framing. Crypto exchanges face exactly this and have resolved it by all buying from the same vendors.

## What Already Exists
Bureau furnishing and dispute frameworks; fraud consortium contribution and access rules; shared utilities for sanctions and identity; antitrust-safe information sharing structures; and the consumer protection regimes that govern all of them.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to an address graph rather than to a person's record. It requires: (1) the shared object being an attribution about an address rather than a fact about a named consumer, which changes the consent and dispute architecture fundamentally — this is the substantive adaptation; (2) an adversary who adapts to the pool, since the subject can simply generate new addresses, which credit bureaus never face; (3) an incumbent vendor layer with the strongest existing position, unlike the bureau case where the utility was built by the contributors; (4) public underlying data, which means the pool adds the identity join rather than the transaction record; and (5) global participants under divergent regimes, where bureau structures are national.

## Target Customer
Exchange compliance and legal leadership, industry associations, analytics vendors, and consortium operators for whom this sector is unentered.

## Impact If Solved
Every obstacle to pooling has an existing answer in bureau and consortium practice. The one genuinely new question is what consent and dispute mean for an attribution about an address rather than a record about a person.

# Securities Admission Standards

**Niche:** [[niches/crypto-exchanges/token-listing-diligence/profile|Token Listing Diligence]]
**Industry:** [[industries/crypto-exchanges|Crypto Exchanges]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Stock exchanges have published, enforced listing standards with continued listing requirements and delisting procedures, and crypto venues have committee discretion.
**Tags:** #compliance #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #workflow-orchestration #confidence-intervals #graph-theory #automation #gradient-boosting
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to assess an asset's code, distribution, team, legal characterisation and market integrity faster and more accurately than the venue that listed it first — and whoever does it without redoing the same analysis every other exchange already did takes the listing advantage.

## The Problem
Securities exchanges run listing as a published standard: quantitative admission criteria, governance requirements, continued listing standards that are monitored, deficiency notices with cure periods, and delisting procedures with appeal. Issuers know what is required, investors know what a listing means, and the exchange can defend every decision. Crypto listing is a committee, a memorandum and an announcement.

## What Already Exists
Published admission and continued listing standards; governance and disclosure requirements; ongoing compliance monitoring against quantitative thresholds; deficiency and cure processes; and delisting with appeal.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to an asset with no issuer relationship and a public technical substrate. It requires: (1) criteria over code, distribution and protocol behaviour rather than over financials and governance, which is the substantive translation and has no template; (2) frequently no issuer to serve a deficiency notice on, removing the cure mechanism entirely; (3) continued listing monitoring that reads a ledger rather than a filing, which is more automatable than the original and is not being done; (4) legal characterisation that is contested and jurisdiction-dependent rather than settled; and (5) assets listed simultaneously on venues with no coordination, where securities exchanges have a primary listing convention.

## Target Customer
Listings and legal leadership, regulators assessing venue standards, issuers seeking predictability, and market infrastructure vendors serving both worlds.

## Impact If Solved
Published standards with monitored continued listing is the mature shape and the crypto version is discretion. Translating criteria onto code, distribution and on-chain behaviour makes the monitoring more automatable than its securities original.

# Exchange Operations From Traditional Markets

**Industry:** [[industries/crypto-exchanges|Crypto Exchanges]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Securities and futures exchanges run market operations as a discipline with halts, circuit breakers and defined incident procedures, all designed around a session that ends.
**Tags:** #workflow-orchestration #change-point-detection #evaluation-metrics #worker-facing #compliance #time-series-forecasting #automation #descriptive-statistics
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to page a human only when the market's behaviour is actually a system fault — and whoever separates a violent but ordinary market from a broken exchange makes a permanently staffed function survivable.

## The Problem
Traditional exchanges have a mature market operations discipline: surveillance, halts and circuit breakers with defined triggers, erroneous trade procedures, incident communication protocols, and an operational rhythm built around opening, closing and a maintenance window. Crypto venues inherited the matching engine and none of the operational scaffolding, and the one structural assumption underneath all of it — that the session ends — does not hold.

## What Already Exists
Market surveillance and integrity monitoring; halt and circuit breaker frameworks; erroneous trade cancellation procedures; incident communication standards; and operations rhythms built around defined sessions.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a market with no close. It requires: (1) maintenance and deployment with no window, so every change is made into a live market — this is the substantive operational difference and reshapes everything downstream; (2) halts with no reopening auction convention and with the asset trading elsewhere regardless, which changes what a halt even accomplishes; (3) a global venue set with no primary market, removing the reference the procedures assume; (4) settlement that is immediate and irreversible, so erroneous trade remedies work entirely differently; and (5) a rota with no natural low-activity period, which is a human-factors problem the traditional discipline never had to solve.

## Target Customer
Exchange operations and engineering leadership, regulators specifying market integrity requirements, and trading infrastructure vendors from traditional markets.

## Impact If Solved
Every piece of the discipline assumes a session that ends. Rebuilding halts, maintenance and rota design for a continuous market is the adaptation, and the human-factors half has no precedent at all.

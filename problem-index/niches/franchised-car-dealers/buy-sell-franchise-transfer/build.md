**Industry:** [[industries/franchised-car-dealers|Franchised Car Dealers]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Buyers price a dealership on adjusted earnings and a brand multiple, then discover the OEM obligations that change both in the data room's last week.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #gradient-boosting #data-integration #compliance #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get a dealership sale through OEM approval at the price the parties signed, and whoever closes the highest share of signed buy-sells without an OEM rejection, right-of-first-refusal exercise or price retrade takes the account.

## The Problem
A store's value depends on things buried in documents: a facility upgrade the dealer agreed to under an image programme, a sales-effectiveness rating below the OEM's standard that invites a performance notice, open warranty or incentive audits, a relocation restriction, a pending add-point nearby. Diligence teams read them by hand across dozens of rooftops in a group deal.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Each deal is bespoke, the documents are confidential, and advisors treat their diligence checklists as their edge.

## What to Build
A diligence extractor for dealer agreements, facility and image agreements, OEM performance reports, audit letters and DMS financial statements, producing per rooftop a structured list of OEM obligations and risks with the clause cited, plus an approval-risk score from the buyer's standing with that OEM.

## Target Customer
Corporate development teams at public and large private dealer groups; sell-side advisors preparing a clean book.

## Impact If Built
Obligations surface before price is agreed, which is what prevents a retrade; on a deal with a $10M+ blue sky (estimate), one avoided retrade pays for years of the tool.

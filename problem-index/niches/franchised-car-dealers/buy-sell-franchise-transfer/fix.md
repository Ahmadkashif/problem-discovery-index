# The Facility Commitment Found After Signing

**Niche:** [[niches/franchised-car-dealers/buy-sell-franchise-transfer/profile|Dealership Buy-Sell & Franchise Transfer]]
**Industry:** [[industries/franchised-car-dealers|Franchised Car Dealers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The OEM's approval letter makes the sale conditional on a facility renovation the buyer never priced.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #compliance #workflow-orchestration #quick-win #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get a dealership sale through OEM approval at the price the parties signed, and whoever closes the highest share of signed buy-sells without an OEM rejection, right-of-first-refusal exercise or price retrade takes the account.

## The Problem
OEMs often attach facility or image requirements to a transfer approval (estimate). If the buyer learns of them only from the approval letter, the price gets reopened or the deal dies.

## Why It's Still Broken
The OEM's expectations sit in facility guides, regional manager emails and the seller's past correspondence, none of which is in a standard diligence request list.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A pre-signing request to the seller for every facility, image and performance communication with the OEM in the last three years, read and summarised against the OEM's current facility guide, and the cost estimate built into the price.

## Who Feels the Pain
Buyers' corporate development teams, sellers whose deal reopens, advisors whose fee depends on closing.

## Impact If Fixed
Fewer retrades and failed closings, for the cost of one diligence step done early; with image renovations commonly running into the millions per store (~$2–10M, estimate), the surprise is often larger than a year of the store's earnings.

# Virtual Data Rooms Fitted to OEM Approval Packets

**Niche:** [[niches/franchised-car-dealers/buy-sell-franchise-transfer/profile|Dealership Buy-Sell & Franchise Transfer]]
**Industry:** [[industries/franchised-car-dealers|Franchised Car Dealers]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** M&A data rooms already organise diligence; none knows what each OEM requires before it approves a new dealer operator.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #data-integration #workflow-orchestration #compliance #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get a dealership sale through OEM approval at the price the parties signed, and whoever closes the highest share of signed buy-sells without an OEM rejection, right-of-first-refusal exercise or price retrade takes the account.

## The Problem
After signing, the buyer files an application with each OEM: operator qualifications, capitalisation, facility plan, ownership chart, performance at existing stores. Missing items restart the clock.

## What Already Exists
Virtual data rooms and M&A deal-management platforms organise documents, Q&A and checklists for any acquisition.

## The Customization Gap
The dealer version needs OEM-specific approval checklists, capitalisation guides and facility standards, a calendar of state franchise-law notice and response windows (~60 days in many states, estimate), right-of-first-refusal deadlines, and the buyer's performance record at its other rooftops of the same brand.

## Target Customer
Dealer franchise attorneys and acquiring groups' integration leads.

## Impact If Solved
Complete packets on first submission and visible OEM deadlines reduce the chance of a rejection on a technicality. They also shorten time to close: cutting ~30 days from a ~120-day approval cycle (estimate) moves a sale's proceeds a month earlier on a market that closed 462 transactions in the twelve months to mid-2026 (Kerrigan Advisors).

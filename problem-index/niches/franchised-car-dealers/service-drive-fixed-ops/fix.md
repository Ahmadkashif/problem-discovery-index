# The Service Lead That Exists in No System

**Industry:** [[industries/franchised-car-dealers|Franchised Car Dealers]]

**One-liner:** A customer who calls, doesn't book and hangs up is a lost repair order nobody records, so nobody calls them back.

**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is "every serious competitor is fighting to raise customer-pay gross per service customer", but it splits into two contests with different winners: capturing inbound demand into booked appointments, won on the share of inbound service calls and messages that become kept appointments, and converting the vehicle once it is in the drive, won on approved labour hours per repair order from inspection findings. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## The Problem
Sales leads go into the CRM and get worked. Service enquiries that don't book — a voicemail, a hang-up on hold, a price question — usually go nowhere. Car Wars counted about 19 million of roughly 53 million inbound service calls across its network as missed opportunities in 2025, more than half ending in voicemail.

## Why It's Still Broken
Service departments have no lead-management habit, and the CRM belongs to the sales side. Voicemails sit in an advisor's inbox.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Every unbooked service contact becomes a task with the caller's number, VIN if known and concern, transcribed and pushed to a callback queue or an automated text offering times, closed only when booked or declined.

## Who Feels the Pain
Fixed ops directors watching car count; advisors returning voicemails at closing time; customers who go to an independent shop instead.

## Impact If Fixed
A store missing ~500 service contacts a month that books back one in ten at ~$350 a repair order (both estimates) adds ~$17,500 a month in service sales with no marketing spend.

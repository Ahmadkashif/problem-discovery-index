# Service Drive & Fixed Ops Revenue

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/franchised-car-dealers|Franchised Car Dealers]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is "every serious competitor is fighting to raise customer-pay gross per service customer", but it splits into two contests with different winners: capturing inbound demand into booked appointments, won on the share of inbound service calls and messages that become kept appointments, and converting the vehicle once it is in the drive, won on approved labour hours per repair order from inspection findings. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$3B a year in fixed-ops software, BDC and scheduling services, inspection tools and service marketing sold to franchised stores (estimate), serving a $164B service and parts business (NADA Data 2025)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~45–50% of dealership gross profit comes from fixed operations (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — online scheduling and video inspections are common at large stores; phones, paper inspection sheets and advisor memory still run much of the drive
**Target Buyer:** Fixed Operations Director
**Automation Potential:** High for booking and status, medium for inspection selling

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Franchised service departments do warranty and recall work for the OEM as well as customer-pay repairs, which independent shops largely cannot (compare [[industries/auto-repair-shops|Auto Repair Shops]]). The drive wrote more than 276 million repair orders in 2025 (NADA Data 2025). Revenue depends on two separate moments: getting the customer to book, and getting the inspection findings approved once the car is on the lift.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Xtime (Cox), myKaarma and Dealer-FX scheduling and inspection tools (named from recall); DMS service modules (CDK, Reynolds & Reynolds, Tekion); call tracking from Car Wars and CallRevu; BDC outsourcers; AI voice and text agents (BizzyCar, Numa, Impel, Toma). Gaps: the phone still loses customers before they book, and the inspection-to-approval step depends on how well each advisor sells.

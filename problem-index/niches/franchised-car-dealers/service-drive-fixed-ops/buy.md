# Retail Contact-Centre Analytics for the Service Drive

**Niche:** [[niches/franchised-car-dealers/service-drive-fixed-ops/profile|Service Drive & Fixed Ops Revenue]]
**Industry:** [[industries/franchised-car-dealers|Franchised Car Dealers]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Contact-centre analytics already score every call; they don't know what a repair order, an op code or a declined brake job is.
**Tags:** #transformers #large-language-models #evaluation-metrics #data-integration #workflow-orchestration
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is "every serious competitor is fighting to raise customer-pay gross per service customer", but it splits into two contests with different winners: capturing inbound demand into booked appointments, won on the share of inbound service calls and messages that become kept appointments, and converting the vehicle once it is in the drive, won on approved labour hours per repair order from inspection findings. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## The Problem
Dealers already pay for call tracking and grading; the outcome they need sits in the DMS, two systems away.

## What Already Exists
Contact-centre and conversation-intelligence platforms used by retailers and banks, and the general call-tracking vendors profiled under [[niches/answering-services/call-tracking-conversation-intelligence/profile|Call-Tracking & Conversation Intelligence Vendors]], score calls for intent, sentiment and booking.

## The Customization Gap
Service-drive analytics need the DMS join (did the booked customer arrive, what did the repair order contain, which inspection items were declined), dealership intents (recall, warranty, status, approval), op-code and labour-time awareness, and per-advisor and per-technician views, plus consent handling that varies by state.

## Target Customer
Fixed ops directors and BDC managers at stores that already record calls.

## Impact If Solved
Existing recordings become a measure of revenue rather than of politeness, at the cost of an integration rather than a new platform. A store that finds and fixes the stage where ~5% of booked customers fall away, on ~1,500 appointments a month at ~$350 a repair order (all estimates), recovers ~$26,000 a month in service sales.

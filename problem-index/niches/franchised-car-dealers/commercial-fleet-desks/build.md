# One Tracker From Chassis Order to Upfitted Delivery

**Niche:** [[niches/franchised-car-dealers/commercial-fleet-desks/profile|Commercial & Fleet Desks]]
**Industry:** [[industries/franchised-car-dealers|Franchised Car Dealers]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** An upfitted truck passes through the OEM plant, a ship-through upfitter and the dealer, and nobody can see the whole path or predict the delivery date.
**Tags:** #survival-analysis #gradient-boosting #time-series-forecasting #data-integration #workflow-orchestration #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to deliver an upfitted work vehicle on the date the business customer was promised, and whoever shortens order-to-delivery days on upfitted units takes the account.

## The Problem
The commercial manager promises a date based on experience. The chassis build date moves, the upfitter's queue moves, a body component is back-ordered, and the customer learns of it when the crew has no truck.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
OEM order status, upfitter shop schedules and dealer records sit in three companies' systems; each upfitter is a small firm with its own process.

## What to Build
A shared tracker fed by OEM order status, upfitter milestones (received, in build, complete, shipped) and dealer delivery prep, with a predicted delivery date from historical stage durations by upfitter and body type, and proactive alerts to the customer.

## Target Customer
Commercial sales managers at truck-heavy stores and the upfitters that serve them.

## Impact If Built
Accurate dates and early warnings keep business customers; cutting order-to-delivery by even a week (estimate) gets a contractor's crew working sooner, which is what wins the next order.

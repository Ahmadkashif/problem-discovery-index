# Retail Assortment Planning for a Car Lot

**Niche:** [[niches/franchised-car-dealers/new-vehicle-allocation-turn/profile|New-Vehicle Allocation & Inventory Turn]]
**Industry:** [[industries/franchised-car-dealers|Franchised Car Dealers]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Apparel and grocery chains plan assortment by store and size; car stores decide trim and colour mix from a manager's memory.
**Tags:** #gradient-boosting #time-series-forecasting #survival-analysis #data-integration #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to put the configurations this market buys on the lot without losing allocation, and whoever cuts average days-to-turn on new units takes the account.

## The Problem
Configuration mix is an assortment problem — which variants, in what depth, at which store — and franchised stores solve it without assortment tools.

## What Already Exists
Retail assortment and allocation planning software used by apparel, footwear and grocery chains forecasts demand by store and variant and recommends buys.

## The Customization Gap
The car version needs OEM allocation constraints (you can only order what the formula grants, in the order windows set), turn-and-earn feedback, VIN-level configuration decoding, incentive and stair-step effects, dealer trades as a rebalancing move, local registration share as demand signal, and floorplan cost per day as the holding cost.

## Target Customer
Dealer groups with central inventory managers across several stores of one brand.

## Impact If Solved
Group-level rebalancing and buys based on forecasts rather than habit. Five days off average turn on a ten-store group's ~9,500 new units a year, at ~$8–10 a unit-day (estimate), saves ~$380,000–475,000 a year in floorplan.

# The Dealer Trade Found by Phone

**Niche:** [[niches/franchised-car-dealers/new-vehicle-allocation-turn/profile|New-Vehicle Allocation & Inventory Turn]]
**Industry:** [[industries/franchised-car-dealers|Franchised Car Dealers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** When a customer wants a configuration the store doesn't have, a manager calls around other dealers to find one and trade for it, by phone and favour.
**Tags:** #k-nearest-neighbors #data-integration #workflow-orchestration #automation #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to put the configurations this market buys on the lot without losing allocation, and whoever cuts average days-to-turn on new units takes the account.

## The Problem
Dealer trades rebalance inventory between stores of the same brand. Finding the unit means searching the OEM's locator, then phoning the other store's manager, negotiating which unit comes back and arranging a driver. Sales are lost while that happens.

## Why It's Still Broken
OEM locators show stock, not willingness to trade or which of the other store's aged units it wants to move; trade terms live in managers' relationships.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A trade board that matches the wanted configuration to nearby stores' units with the longest days in stock, proposes the return unit from the requesting store's slow inventory, and books the transfer, for stores within a group first.

## Who Feels the Pain
Sales managers holding a customer; the managers fielding trade calls all day; aged units nobody offers.

## Impact If Fixed
Faster trades save sales, and pairing each trade with an aged unit moves inventory both stores want gone; a store doing ~20 trades a month (estimate) that cuts each from a day of calls to an hour saves a sales manager several days of phone time a month.

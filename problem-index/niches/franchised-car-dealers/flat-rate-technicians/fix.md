# The Diagnostic Hour Nobody Sold

**Niche:** [[niches/franchised-car-dealers/flat-rate-technicians/profile|Flat-Rate Technicians]]
**Industry:** [[industries/franchised-car-dealers|Franchised Car Dealers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** An intermittent electrical fault takes two hours to find, the repair order authorised half an hour of diagnosis, and the technician eats the difference.
**Tags:** #gradient-boosting #survival-analysis #worker-facing #quick-win #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to cut the unpaid minutes between jobs — waiting for parts, approvals and repair information — and whoever raises technicians' flagged hours per clocked hour takes the account.

## The Problem
Advisors sell a standard diagnostic charge at write-up. Hard faults (intermittent electrical, drivability, network communication codes) routinely overrun it. Unless the technician stops, finds the advisor and the advisor reaches the customer for more authorisation, the overrun is unpaid flat-rate time, so technicians learn to avoid diagnostic work and the hard jobs go to whoever can't refuse.

## Why It's Still Broken
Nobody estimates diagnostic time from the concern and the vehicle, and nothing alerts the advisor when clocked diagnosis passes what was sold. The independent-shop version is [[problems/auto-repair-shops/worker-life-1|Flat-Rate Pay Pressure on Technicians]]. At a franchised store it is worse on warranty jobs, where the OEM's guide caps the diagnostic time paid and extra time needs the OEM's technical assistance line to approve it.

## What a Fix Looks Like
At write-up, a diagnostic-time estimate from the store's own history of similar concerns on similar vehicles (time actually clocked to find the cause), so the advisor sells a realistic first block; during the job, an alert to the advisor when clocked time nears the sold time, with a ready text asking the customer to approve the next block.

## Who Feels the Pain
Flat-rate technicians, especially the strongest diagnosticians; advisors who hate the second call; customers whose car sits while the authorisation is chased.

## Impact If Fixed
Paid diagnostic hours rise and the best technicians stop avoiding hard jobs; recovering ~30 unpaid diagnostic minutes a day per diagnostic technician (estimate) is ~10 flagged hours a month each.

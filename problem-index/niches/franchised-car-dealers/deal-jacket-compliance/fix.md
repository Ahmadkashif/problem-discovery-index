# The First Quote Nobody Kept

**Niche:** [[niches/franchised-car-dealers/deal-jacket-compliance/profile|Deal-Jacket Compliance Review]]
**Industry:** [[industries/franchised-car-dealers|Franchised Car Dealers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** California now requires the total price in the first written communication, and most stores can't produce what their salesperson texted the customer first.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #data-integration #compliance #quick-win #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to catch a defective deal jacket — a missing add-on consent, a price that doesn't match the total first quoted, an unsigned disclosure — before the contract is funded, and whoever catches the highest share of defective deal jackets before funding takes the account.

## The Problem
The first written communication may be a CRM email, a text from a salesperson's phone, a chat transcript or a credit-application screen. The CARS Act requires it to carry the total price and requires the store to keep it for two years.

## Why It's Still Broken
Customer messages are spread across the CRM, texting tools and personal phones; nobody links the first message to the deal it became.

## What a Fix Looks Like
All customer messaging routed through logged channels, the first written communication per customer identified and stored with the deal, and a check that it carried a total price consistent with the vehicle advertised.

## Who Feels the Pain
Compliance officers, sales managers and business managers asked to prove a disclosure made by someone else.

## Impact If Fixed
A gap that would otherwise appear on every California deal is closed with message logging most stores already half have; for a store retailing ~100 vehicles a month (estimate), that is ~1,200 deals a year whose first quote can be produced on request instead of reconstructed.

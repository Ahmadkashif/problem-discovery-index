# Mortgage Loan Quality Control for the Deal Jacket

**Niche:** [[niches/franchised-car-dealers/deal-jacket-compliance/profile|Deal-Jacket Compliance Review]]
**Industry:** [[industries/franchised-car-dealers|Franchised Car Dealers]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Mortgage lenders already run document quality control on every closed loan; car dealers sample a few jackets a month.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #compliance #evaluation-metrics #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to catch a defective deal jacket — a missing add-on consent, a price that doesn't match the total first quoted, an unsigned disclosure — before the contract is funded, and whoever catches the highest share of defective deal jackets before funding takes the account.

## The Problem
The deal jacket is a closing package: disclosures, contracts, consents and identity checks that must agree with each other.

## What Already Exists
Mortgage loan quality-control and document-classification tools check closing packages for completeness, data consistency and regulatory disclosures (see [[industries/mortgage-lenders|Mortgage Lenders]]).

## The Customization Gap
The dealer version needs retail installment contract and buyer's order forms by state, add-on product contracts and eligibility, state retail rules such as California's CARS Act, the dealer's first written quote as a control document, and outputs routed to the business manager rather than a post-closing QC team.

## Target Customer
Dealer group compliance officers and compliance service firms.

## Impact If Solved
Checks move from a post-sale sample to every deal before funding. Today a monthly audit might sample ~10 of ~100 deals at a store (estimate). Checking all 100 finds the ~9 in 10 defects the sample never sees, each exposed to penalties of up to $2,500 per violation under California's Unfair Competition Law.

# The Address That Says Serviceable and Isn't

**Niche:** [[niches/wired-broadband-providers/order-to-activation/profile|Order-to-Activation & Zero-Touch Provisioning]]
**Industry:** [[industries/wired-broadband-providers|Wired Broadband Providers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The website accepts an order, the install is booked, and the technician arrives to find no drop path, a full splitter or plant that was never finished.
**Tags:** #gradient-boosting #k-nearest-neighbors #data-integration #quick-win #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn an online order into working service without a human touch — serviceability answer, port assignment, ONT or modem activation and install slot — and whoever closes the most orders without a call or a second visit takes the account.

## The Problem
Serviceability comes from passings in the network record, which assume a drop can be built and a port is free. Failed installs waste a technician slot and lose a customer who had already decided to buy.

## Why It's Still Broken
Nobody feeds failed-install reasons back into the serviceability data, and the website and the network record are owned by different teams.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Record a structured reason for every failed or rescheduled install, push it back to the location record, and score serviceability confidence per address from records quality, nearby install outcomes and port capacity, routing low-confidence orders to a pre-install check.

## Who Feels the Pain
Customers left waiting; installers sent to impossible jobs; sales teams whose orders evaporate.

## Impact If Fixed
Fewer failed installs, more accurate serviceability for sales and for BDC filings, and orders that turn into customers.

# Utility Payment-Arrangement Programmes Adapted to Broadband

**Niche:** [[niches/wired-broadband-providers/low-income-households/profile|Low-Income & Post-ACP Households]]
**Industry:** [[industries/wired-broadband-providers|Wired Broadband Providers]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Electric and water utilities have run regulated payment arrangements, budget billing and arrearage management for years; broadband collections still look like consumer credit.
**Tags:** #gradient-boosting #logistic-regression #compliance #workflow-orchestration #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche (billing and collections platforms, affordability-programme administrators and operators' own credit teams) is fighting to match each payment-constrained household to a plan and payment arrangement it can sustain without ending in non-pay disconnection, and whoever predicts that fit best takes the account.

## The Problem
Broadband operators suspend service on a fixed schedule after a missed payment; reconnecting costs fees and a call, and many households simply leave.

## What Already Exists
Utility customer-information systems with payment-arrangement, budget-billing, arrearage-management and assistance-programme modules, built under state commission rules on disconnection and deferred payment.

## The Customization Gap
Broadband needs: arrangements sized to much smaller monthly bills; speed-reduced service as an alternative to disconnection; Lifeline and low-income plan eligibility checks; payment dates aligned to benefit deposits; and treatment of equipment returns on disconnection. Utility modules assume a regulated, non-substitutable service; broadband customers can leave.

## Target Customer
Collections and billing leaders at operators, especially co-op and municipal entrants already using utility systems.

## Impact If Solved
Proven arrangement mechanics keep more payment-constrained households connected at lower collection cost.

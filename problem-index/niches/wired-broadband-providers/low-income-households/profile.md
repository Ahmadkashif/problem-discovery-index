# Low-Income & Post-ACP Households

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/wired-broadband-providers|Wired Broadband Providers]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche (billing and collections platforms, affordability-programme administrators and operators' own credit teams) is fighting to match each payment-constrained household to a plan and payment arrangement it can sustain without ending in non-pay disconnection, and whoever predicts that fit best takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$2B a year in affordability-plan discounts, collections cost and bad debt attributable to payment-constrained households (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~0.6% of industry receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low-Medium — eligibility checks and payment arrangements are manual or rule-based
**Target Buyer:** VP of Customer Lifecycle or Credit & Collections at an operator
**Automation Potential:** Medium — plan fit and payment risk are predictable; eligibility verification and outreach need care

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The Affordable Connectivity Program ran out of funding in 2024, and operators reported the disconnects that followed (Charter cited ACP-related losses in its prior-year comparison). Households that relied on it are still customers, or would be on the right plan. Operators offer low-income plans and Lifeline, but the economics depend on matching each household to a plan it can pay every month; get it wrong and the account cycles through late fees, suspension and disconnection, costing the operator a subscriber and the household its connection.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Operator low-income plans with eligibility via SNAP, Medicaid, school lunch or Lifeline verification; collections systems with standard dunning; credit checks at sign-up. Gaps: dunning treats a household that misses one payment after a job loss like a habitual non-payer; eligibility re-verification drops eligible households; and no tool recommends the plan or payment arrangement a household can sustain.

# Re-Verification That Drops Eligible Households

**Niche:** [[niches/wired-broadband-providers/low-income-households/profile|Low-Income & Post-ACP Households]]
**Industry:** [[industries/wired-broadband-providers|Wired Broadband Providers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Annual eligibility re-checks for low-income plans move households to full price because a letter went unanswered, not because they stopped qualifying.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #compliance #automation #quick-win #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche (billing and collections platforms, affordability-programme administrators and operators' own credit teams) is fighting to match each payment-constrained household to a plan and payment arrangement it can sustain without ending in non-pay disconnection, and whoever predicts that fit best takes the account.

## The Problem
The household qualified through SNAP last year. The re-verification notice went to an old address or got lost in email, the deadline passed, and the bill doubled. Many such households then fall behind and are disconnected.

## Why It's Still Broken
Re-verification is a compliance step owned by a back-office team, measured on completing checks, not on keeping eligible customers.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Check eligibility databases automatically where permitted, reach the household by text in plain language with a one-step upload when proof is needed, and route non-responders to a call before any price change.

## Who Feels the Pain
Low-income households; care agents fielding confused calls after the price change; operators losing customers they meant to keep.

## Impact If Fixed
Eligible households stay on the plan they qualify for, with less agent time and fewer avoidable disconnections.

# Cable Save Offers Against Fixed Wireless & Fibre

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/wired-broadband-providers|Wired Broadband Providers]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche (BSS churn modules, retention analytics vendors and operators' own data science teams) is fighting to price each save offer to the alternative the household can actually get at its address, and whoever prices save offers to each address's real alternative best takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$4B a year in cable retention discounts and save-desk labour (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~1.2% of industry receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — risk scores and offer menus exist; uplift measurement and address-level alternatives do not
**Target Buyer:** VP of Retention or Customer Lifecycle at a cable operator
**Automation Potential:** High for targeting, medium for the save conversation

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The cable version of retention is a pricing decision made monthly for every cohort whose promotion expires, under pressure from fixed wireless (which took roughly all net additions in 2023) and fibre overbuilders. The winner is not whoever offers the deepest discount, which is the generic contest; it is whoever knows which households have a real alternative at the address and how much each offer changes their decision. That knowledge comes from address-level competitive data and randomised measurement, both of which most operators lack. Whether fixed wireless is a live alternative at an address is decided by the carrier's sector capacity, the other side of [[niches/wireless-carriers/ran-capacity-planning/profile|RAN Capacity Planning & Spectrum Deployment]] under wireless carriers, so the save model needs to track where carriers cap fixed wireless sales.

## Current Tools & Gaps
BSS churn modules, CRM offer engines, save-desk scripts with tiered discount authority. Gaps: no per-address view of fixed wireless and fibre availability; offers targeted by churn risk rather than by uplift; no standing holdout; and outage history ignored, though a household that has had repeated outages responds to service fixes and credits, not price.

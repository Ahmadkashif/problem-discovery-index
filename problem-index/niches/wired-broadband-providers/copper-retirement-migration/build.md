# Wire-Center Migration Sequencer

**Industry:** [[industries/wired-broadband-providers|Wired Broadband Providers]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Telcos retire copper wire center by wire center with spreadsheets, when the order of outreach, installs and cutover decides how many customers they keep.
**Tags:** #gradient-boosting #survival-analysis #optimization-fundamentals #dynamic-programming #workflow-orchestration #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche (migration programme integrators, field-service contractors and telcos' own transformation offices) is fighting to move each remaining copper line, attached device included, onto fibre or a working substitute before the wire center's retirement date, and whoever completes those migrations with the fewest lost lines and failed devices takes the account.

## The Problem
A wire center scheduled for retirement holds thousands of lines: DSL households, voice-only seniors, small businesses with alarm and fax lines. Fibre covers part of the area; elsewhere the substitute is fixed wireless or a voice product. Crews, notice periods and customer readiness all constrain the plan, and the order is set by geography and habit.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Copper retirement at scale is recent; the rules eased only in 2025–2026. Order systems treat each migration as an individual order, not a campaign with a deadline.

## What to Build
A planner that, for each wire center, predicts per line the likelihood of migrating versus leaving under each outreach path, identifies lines with attached devices needing special handling, and schedules outreach, installs and cutovers against crew capacity and the retirement date. It re-plans weekly as migrations complete.

## Target Customer
Copper retirement programme offices at large telcos and at mid-sized and rural carriers modernising their networks.

## Impact If Built
More lines retained through migration, fewer emergency extensions, and earlier retirement of copper maintenance costs.

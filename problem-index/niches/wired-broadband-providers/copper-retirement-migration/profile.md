# Copper Retirement & Customer Migration

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/wired-broadband-providers|Wired Broadband Providers]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche (migration programme integrators, field-service contractors and telcos' own transformation offices) is fighting to move each remaining copper line, attached device included, onto fibre or a working substitute before the wire center's retirement date, and whoever completes those migrations with the fewest lost lines and failed devices takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$2B a year in migration labour, customer equipment, outreach and incentives at US telcos (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~0.6% of industry receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — migration runs on wire-center spreadsheets, outbound call lists and door hangers
**Target Buyer:** Copper retirement programme lead or VP of Network Transformation at a telco
**Automation Potential:** Medium-high for sequencing and device discovery, low for the in-home work

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Telcos carry copper networks that cost far more to maintain than fibre and are losing customers. The FCC streamlined copper retirement with waivers in March 2025 and rules adopted March 26, 2026, and AT&T aims to leave copper outside California by 2030. Retiring a wire center means every remaining voice and DSL line — including alarm panels, elevator phones, fax lines and medical alert devices — must move to fibre, fixed wireless or a voice substitute before the date. The contest is operational: sequencing and completing migrations with the least loss and the fewest broken devices, not pricing. The boundary with [[niches/telecom-resellers/pots-line-replacement/profile|Legacy POTS Line Replacement]] under telecom resellers is who holds the line records: here the telco runs a wire-center campaign over its own lines; there a reseller inventories and certifies replacement lines across one enterprise's many sites, whoever the underlying carrier is.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Network inventory and order systems, wire-center retirement plans in spreadsheets, outbound call centres and field crews. Gaps: no per-line view of what device is attached and what substitute it needs; no sequencing that balances fibre availability, crew capacity and notice periods; no prediction of which customers will leave rather than migrate.

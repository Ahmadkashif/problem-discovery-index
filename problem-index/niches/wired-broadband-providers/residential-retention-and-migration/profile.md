# Residential Retention & Migration

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/wired-broadband-providers|Wired Broadband Providers]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is that every competitor is fighting to keep a household's broadband revenue when its current connection stops being its best option, but for cable operators that is a pricing contest at promo roll-off against fixed wireless and fibre, and for telcos it is an operational contest to move households off copper before the wire center is retired. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$6B a year in retention discounts, save-desk labour and copper-to-fibre migration programmes (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~2% of industry receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — churn scores exist at large operators; address-level competitive context and migration sequencing are mostly manual
**Target Buyer:** VP of Retention / Customer Lifecycle at cable operators; copper retirement programme leads at telcos
**Automation Potential:** High for targeting and sequencing, medium for the conversation itself

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Residential broadband is where the industry's revenue sits and where it is now under the most pressure. Fixed wireless took roughly all US net additions in 2023 (Leichtman Research Group) while top cable operators lost subscribers, and Charter lost 119,000 Internet customers in Q4 2025. At the same time telcos are retiring copper under rules the FCC eased in March 2026, with AT&T aiming to leave copper outside California by 2030. Both are about keeping a household whose current product is losing.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Churn modules in Amdocs and CSG, CRM and marketing automation, save-desk scripts, and copper migration programmes run from spreadsheets and outbound call lists. The gaps differ by side, which is why the niche splits: cable lacks address-level knowledge of each household's real alternative and a measured view of which offers change outcomes; telcos lack a sequenced, device-aware migration plan per wire center.

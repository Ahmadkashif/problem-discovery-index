# The Closure Opened to Find Out What's Inside

**Niche:** [[niches/wired-broadband-providers/fiber-network-records/profile|Fibre Network Records & As-Built Inventory]]
**Industry:** [[industries/wired-broadband-providers|Wired Broadband Providers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** During a fibre cut, crews open closure after closure to learn what is spliced where because the record can't be trusted.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #object-detection #quick-win #worker-facing #data-integration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche (fibre records and GIS vendors, records-conversion services and operators' own records teams) is fighting to keep a strand-, splice- and port-level record that matches what is actually in the ground and on the poles after every repair and install, and whoever keeps that record truest takes the account.

## The Problem
A feeder cable is cut at 2 a.m. The splice crew needs to know which strands serve which customers and where to restore first. The record says one thing; the closure says another. Each closure opened costs time and adds risk to working fibre.

## Why It's Still Broken
Record updates after field work depend on someone remembering to send redlines, and someone else keying them.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Require a photo of every opened splice tray and the closure label at job close, read it automatically, and compare it to the record. Flag mismatches immediately while the technician is still on site to confirm.

## Who Feels the Pain
Splicers and maintenance crews on night calls; customers waiting for restoration; operators paying overtime for discovery work.

## Impact If Fixed
Shorter restorations and a record that improves every time a closure is opened instead of degrading.

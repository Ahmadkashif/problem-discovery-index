# Utility Network GIS Adapted to Splice Records

**Niche:** [[niches/wired-broadband-providers/fiber-network-records/profile|Fibre Network Records & As-Built Inventory]]
**Industry:** [[industries/wired-broadband-providers|Wired Broadband Providers]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Electric and gas utilities model their networks with connectivity, versioning and field editing in utility GIS; small fibre operators run on map pins without connectivity.
**Tags:** #graph-theory #data-integration #workflow-orchestration #automation #descriptive-statistics
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche (fibre records and GIS vendors, records-conversion services and operators' own records teams) is fighting to keep a strand-, splice- and port-level record that matches what is actually in the ground and on the poles after every repair and install, and whoever keeps that record truest takes the account.

## The Problem
Many electric co-ops and municipal utilities entering broadband already run a utility GIS for their electric network, while their fibre subsidiary keeps records in a separate, simpler mapping tool or in spreadsheets.

## What Already Exists
Utility network GIS (Esri ArcGIS Utility Network and similar), which models connectivity, supports versioned editing by field crews, traces networks and integrates with outage and work management systems.

## The Customization Gap
Fibre needs: strand- and colour-level connectivity inside cables and buffer tubes; splice-tray and closure models; splitter port assignment tied to subscriber accounts; optical loss budgets per path; integration with BDC passings and billing serviceability; and simple field capture a splicer will actually use. Off-the-shelf telecom data models exist but need configuration most small operators can't do alone.

## Target Customer
Electric co-ops and municipal utilities with fibre subsidiaries, and their GIS staff.

## Impact If Solved
One records platform across electric and fibre, using staff skills the utility already has.

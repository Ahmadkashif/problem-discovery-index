# Fibre Network Records & As-Built Inventory

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/wired-broadband-providers|Wired Broadband Providers]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche (fibre records and GIS vendors, records-conversion services and operators' own records teams) is fighting to keep a strand-, splice- and port-level record that matches what is actually in the ground and on the poles after every repair and install, and whoever keeps that record truest takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$800M a year in network records software, records staff and field verification at US wireline operators (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~0.25% of industry receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — many small operators still hold fibre records in Google Earth files, spreadsheets, PDFs of as-builts and technicians' memories
**Target Buyer:** OSP Engineering Manager or Director of Network Records
**Automation Potential:** Medium-high for reconciliation and data capture, low for physical verification

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Once a fibre network is turned up, the operator lives on its records: which strand in which cable feeds which splitter port, what is spliced in each closure, where slack loops are coiled. Every install assigns a port, every repair re-splices, and every record that drifts from reality turns the next fibre cut into hours of opening closures to find out what's inside. This is the operator's living record after construction; design and make-ready during construction belong to the contractor's [[niches/utility-contractors/telecom-fiber/profile|Telecom & Fiber]] niche under utility contractors and are not claimed here.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Vetro FiberMap (cloud FTTx mapping for small and mid-sized ISPs, integrated with gaiia and Sonar), 3-GIS, OSPInsight, IQGeo and Esri-based records at larger operators. The gaps: as-builts from contractors arrive as PDFs and redlines that are keyed in by hand, months late; repairs and re-splices in the field rarely make it back into the record; and nothing checks the record against OTDR traces or port activity.

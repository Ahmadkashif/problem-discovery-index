# Subsidy & Buildout Compliance

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/wired-broadband-providers|Wired Broadband Providers]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche (regulatory consultants, compliance and grants software vendors and carriers' own regulatory staff) is fighting to prove, location by location, that each subsidised address was served on time at the required speed with evidence USAC verification and state BEAD audits accept, and whoever produces that evidence best takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$400M a year in compliance staff time, consultants and reporting tools across subsidised carriers (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~0.1% of industry receipts, but gating multi-billion-dollar support streams (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — obligation lists, milestone tracking and evidence live in spreadsheets, shared drives and consultants' files
**Target Buyer:** Regulatory and compliance manager or CFO at a subsidised carrier
**Automation Potential:** High for evidence assembly and tracking, low for the engineering certification

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Rural carriers fund their networks with layered subsidies, each with its own location list, speed obligation and clock: RDOF (40% of committed locations by end-2024, 60% by end-2025, with defaults such as Lumen's 41,000+ locations in 2025), Enhanced A-CAM, USDA ReConnect, state grants and now BEAD, whose subgrantees must serve every funded location within four years of the subgrant. A carrier can have one location funded under one programme and the next street under another, and missing a milestone means withheld support or clawback. The work is proving deployment, not doing it.

## Current Tools & Gaps
USAC's High Cost Universal Broadband (HUBB) portal for location uploads; state broadband office grant portals; general grants-management software; regulatory consultants; spreadsheets. Gaps: no single record per location across programmes; evidence (install date, speed test, photos) scattered across billing, field service and engineering systems; milestone forecasting done by hand; and nothing flags a slipping milestone early enough to fix it.

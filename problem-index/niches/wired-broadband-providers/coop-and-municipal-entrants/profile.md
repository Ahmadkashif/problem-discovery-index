# Electric Co-op & Municipal Fibre Entrants

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/wired-broadband-providers|Wired Broadband Providers]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor serving this segment is fighting to run a first-time operator's network, billing and care up to the take rate its loan or bond model assumed, and whoever gets a new entrant to that take rate inside its first three years becomes its long-term operating partner.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.5B a year in managed operations, billing and OSS, care outsourcing and advisory bought by co-op and municipal broadband entrants (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~0.5% of industry receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — strong utility systems for electric or water, little telecom tooling or experience
**Target Buyer:** General manager of the co-op or utility, or the broadband subsidiary's first general manager
**Automation Potential:** Medium — sales, provisioning and care can be largely automated; community trust can't

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Electric co-ops and municipal utilities building fibre are a distinct buyer: utility-grade at construction and member relations, inexperienced at selling and running a broadband service, and financed on take-rate assumptions (USDA loans, municipal bonds, BEAD and state grants) that must be met for the debt to work. Telecom vendors are built for operators who already know telecom; utility vendors don't know broadband. The entrant needs a partner who can run the service end to end, and judges that partner on whether subscribers sign up and stay.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Co-op billing platforms such as NISC extended to broadband; small-ISP OSS such as Sonar and gaiia; managed operations and network partners such as Conexon, NRTC and Hometown Connections; consulting engineers for design and feasibility. Gaps: take-rate forecasts in feasibility studies that are rarely revisited against actual sign-ups; pre-registration campaigns that don't learn which members convert; and operations metrics invisible to a board used to electric reliability indices.

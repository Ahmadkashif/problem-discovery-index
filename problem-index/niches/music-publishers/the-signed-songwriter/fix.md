# The Statement No Writer Can Read

**Niche:** [[niches/music-publishers/the-signed-songwriter/profile|The Signed Songwriter]]
**Industry:** [[industries/music-publishers|Music Publishers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The writer statement is a long list of codes, sources and deductions, so every question goes to a creative manager who cannot answer it either.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #worker-facing #quick-win #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to show a signed writer what their songs are earning and when the advance will recoup, sooner and more believably than rival publishers, and whoever does that keeps the writer when the option comes up.

## The Problem
A writer opens a statement with hundreds of lines of society codes, sub-publisher deductions and recoupment entries. They ask their manager why a hit paid so little in a territory, the manager asks the royalty department, and the answer takes weeks.

## Why It's Still Broken
Statements are produced by accounting systems for accountants. Royalty teams are sized for processing, not for explaining.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A plain-language summary on every statement (top songs, sources, changes since last period, recoupment balance) and an assistant that answers questions from the writer's own lines and deal terms. Anything it cannot answer is routed to the royalty team with the context attached.

## Who Feels the Pain
Writers, their managers and lawyers, and creative managers caught in the middle.

## Impact If Fixed
Fewer escalations, fewer disputes that turn into audits, and writers who trust their publisher's numbers.

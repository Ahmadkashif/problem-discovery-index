# Split-Copyright Sync Clearance & Quoting

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/music-publishers|Music Publishers]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to quote and clear a song whose composition is split among several publishers, and whose master sits with a label, before the edit locks, and whoever can give a cleared answer fastest wins the placement over equally good songs.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$650M of US sync fees on songs with two or more publishers (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~9% of US publishing revenue (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — approvals are collected by email and phone
**Target Buyer:** Head of sync business affairs or licensing director
**Automation Potential:** High for status, quoting and approval routing; low for the negotiation itself

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Modern hits commonly have several writers signed to different publishers, and every co-publisher must approve a sync and its fee. The master owner, usually a label, has to agree as well, and most-favoured-nation terms tie the composition fee to the master fee. A supervisor with two equally good songs licenses the one that clears first and stays in budget, and a one-stop catalogue that controls everything wins on certainty. This is a contest of control and speed, fought by different people (business affairs, not creative), and it is the reason equally good songs lose.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Synchtank's quoting and licence generation, email chains to co-publishers and labels, spreadsheets of restrictions ("no political ads", "no alcohol") and one-stop catalogues that sidestep the problem. The gaps: no shared view of who controls which share and how fast each party usually replies, quotes built by hand from memory of comparable fees, and silence treated as delay when it is often a refusal.

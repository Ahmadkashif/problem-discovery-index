# A Pre-Cleared Answer for Every Multi-Publisher Song

**Niche:** [[niches/music-publishers/split-copyright-sync-clearance/profile|Split-Copyright Sync Clearance & Quoting]]
**Industry:** [[industries/music-publishers|Music Publishers]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A rep cannot tell a supervisor in minutes whether a multi-publisher song is clearable for a use and at what fee, so it loses to a song that can be.
**Tags:** #gradient-boosting #logistic-regression #large-language-models #evaluation-metrics #workflow-orchestration #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to quote and clear a song whose composition is split among several publishers, and whose master sits with a label, before the edit locks, and whoever can give a cleared answer fastest wins the placement over equally good songs.

## The Problem
A supervisor asks, "Can I have this for a beer ad, US, one year, all media, for $40,000 all-in?" The rep knows their own share's answer. The other three co-publishers and the label have to be asked, and each takes days. By then the edit has moved on.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Every co-publisher's approval history and restriction list is private, the master side is another company, and the industry has treated clearance as relationship work rather than data.

## What to Build
A clearance model built on each publisher's own records. For every song it holds the control map (who owns which share, the master owner, standing restrictions) and each party's past responses by use type and fee level. From that it predicts the probability and time to full approval for a requested use and proposes a quote consistent with past comparable fees and most-favoured-nation terms. It also lets co-publishers that opt in exchange standing pre-approvals, such as "approve any non-political ad above $X".

## Target Customer
Sync business affairs heads at publishers with large shares of co-written repertoire.

## Impact If Built
Quotes and likely-clearance answers in minutes, which means more placements won on certainty and less deal time lost on songs that were never going to clear.

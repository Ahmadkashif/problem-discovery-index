# An Earnings Expectation for Every Song in Every Territory

**Niche:** [[niches/music-publishers/sub-publishing-and-unclaimed-recovery/profile|Sub-Publishing & Unclaimed Royalty Recovery]]
**Industry:** [[industries/music-publishers|Music Publishers]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A publisher learns a song went unpaid in a territory only if someone happens to look, and often after the society has shared the money out.
**Tags:** #gradient-boosting #linear-regression #hypothesis-testing #feature-engineering #data-integration #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to claim a song's earnings in every territory before the collecting society's identification period runs out and the money is shared out by market share, and whoever claims most completely and earliest takes the administration and sub-publishing mandates.

## The Problem
A song streams heavily in Brazil and Germany. Did the local societies and sub-publishers pay what that usage implies? Nobody computes the expectation, so a missing registration or a wrong claim stays invisible until a writer asks or the retention period ends.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Converting usage into expected income needs each territory's rates and distribution rules, and the usage data (streams by country, airplay detections) sits with vendors outside the publisher's royalty system.

## What to Build
A model of expected income per work, territory, source and period, estimated from usage data and the publisher's own historical conversion rates, compared against what arrived. Gaps are ranked by dollars and by deadline, such as the MLC snapshot or a society's identification period, and each comes with a likely cause and a prepared claim or registration.

## Target Customer
International administration heads at independent publishers and administrators; catalogue owners auditing their sub-publishers.

## Impact If Built
Recovered income measured in dollars per analyst hour, and claims filed before the money is gone. Administrators can prove their "we collect more" pitch with data.

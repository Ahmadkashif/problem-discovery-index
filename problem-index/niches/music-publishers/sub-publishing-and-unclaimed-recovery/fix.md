# Sub-Publisher Statements That Arrive as PDFs, Late

**Niche:** [[niches/music-publishers/sub-publishing-and-unclaimed-recovery/profile|Sub-Publishing & Unclaimed Royalty Recovery]]
**Industry:** [[industries/music-publishers|Music Publishers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Foreign income arrives a year or two after use, in a sub-publisher's own layout, so gaps are found when they can no longer be claimed.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #feature-engineering #data-integration #quick-win #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to claim a song's earnings in every territory before the collecting society's identification period runs out and the money is shared out by market share, and whoever claims most completely and earliest takes the administration and sub-publishing mandates.

## The Problem
Sub-publishers report semiannually or annually, often as PDF or spreadsheet statements with local titles and their own codes. By the time the US publisher sees that a song earned nothing in a territory, the society's identification period may be close to ending.

## Why It's Still Broken
Sub-publishing agreements rarely specify a data format, and the US publisher has little leverage to demand one. Parsing each layout is manual.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Parse statements automatically on arrival, match local titles to works, and compare each territory's income with the previous period and the usage evidence. Flag absent songs immediately, and put a standard data schedule into new sub-publishing agreements.

## Who Feels the Pain
International administration teams, writers missing foreign income and owners auditing sub-publishers.

## Impact If Fixed
Gaps found in weeks rather than years, while there is still time to claim.

# Sync Licensing

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/music-publishers|Music Publishers]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is that competitors fight to turn a brief into a licensed song, but it splits into two contests with different winners: putting the right song in front of the supervisor within hours, which is won by catalogue knowledge and a record of what each buyer licenses, and clearing and quoting a song whose copyright is split among several publishers, which is won by control of the shares and speed of approval. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.75B US sync revenue in 2025 (estimate, from NMPA's 24% share of $7.29B)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~24% of US publishing revenue (NMPA mix)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — pitching tools are digital, while briefs, approvals and quotes run on email
**Target Buyer:** Head of sync or SVP of creative licensing
**Automation Potential:** Medium — retrieval, clearance status and quoting automate, but the final pick is taste

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Sync is the only one of the three income streams a publisher negotiates directly, deal by deal, rather than collecting through a society at a statutory or blanket rate. Briefs come from ad agencies, trailer houses, music supervisors for film, TV and games, and brands, usually with 24–48 hours to respond. The fee is set by negotiation against the use, media, term and territory, and every co-owner of the song has to approve. That makes it the most commercial, fastest-moving part of a publisher, and the one whose staff are hardest to replace.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Synchtank's SyncUp for pitches, quotes and licences; DISCO for catalogue sharing and AI tagging; audio-similarity search from vendors such as Cyanite and AIMS; Tunefind and trade press for tracking placements; and email for almost everything else. The gaps: no forecast of where briefs will come from, sync revenue reported without the use and brief attached, and none of the history joined up.

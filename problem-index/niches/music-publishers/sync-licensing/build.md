# Anticipating Briefs Before They Arrive

**Niche:** [[niches/music-publishers/sync-licensing/profile|Sync Licensing]]
**Industry:** [[industries/music-publishers|Music Publishers]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Sync teams react to briefs, although most of the demand is visible weeks ahead in production slates, campaign calendars and release schedules.

## The Problem
Briefs feel random to the team answering them, but much of the demand behind them is foreseeable. Series go into post-production on announced schedules, studios cut trailers ahead of dated releases, car and retail brands launch campaigns in the same seasons each year, and video games ship on public dates. A team that only reacts competes on the same 24-hour window as every other publisher.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Pitching tools manage the brief once it exists. Production and campaign signals are scattered across trade press, production listings and announcements, and nobody has joined them to a publisher's own record of which kinds of project licensed its songs.

## What to Build
A demand map that reads production slates, release calendars, campaign announcements and trade press. It predicts which projects and buyers are likely to send briefs, of what kind and when, using the publisher's own licence history to estimate which catalogue songs fit. The output is a weekly proactive-pitch list sent to supervisors before the brief exists.

## Target Customer
Heads of sync at major and independent publishers with deep catalogues.

## Impact If Built
More placements that go out without competition and a steadier sync pipeline. Proactive pitches avoid the deadline that forces every publisher into the same few obvious songs.

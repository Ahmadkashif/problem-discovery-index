# Sync Income Booked as One Line

**Niche:** [[niches/music-publishers/sync-licensing/profile|Sync Licensing]]
**Industry:** [[industries/music-publishers|Music Publishers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Sync fees are booked by song and amount, so the team cannot see which uses, buyers and fee levels its catalogue actually wins.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #feature-engineering #data-integration #quick-win #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is that competitors fight to turn a brief into a licensed song, but it splits into two contests with different winners: putting the right song in front of the supervisor within hours, which is won by catalogue knowledge and a record of what each buyer licenses, and clearing and quoting a song whose copyright is split among several publishers, which is won by control of the shares and speed of approval. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## The Problem
Finance records a sync licence as a fee against a song. The use (ad, trailer, series, game), the buyer, the media and term, and the brief it answered sit in the licence PDF or the rep's email. Leadership asks which kinds of use are growing and which reps or buyers bring the most revenue, and the answer has to be assembled by hand.

## Why It's Still Broken
The royalty system was built to pay writers their share of a fee, not to analyse the sync market. Reps have no incentive to re-key licence details.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Extract the use, media, term, territory, buyer and fee from every licence automatically at booking. Attach them to the song record and report sync revenue by use type, buyer, rep and song age every quarter.

## Who Feels the Pain
Heads of sync setting strategy, finance teams forecasting and catalogue valuers trying to separate repeatable sync income from one-off fees.

## Impact If Fixed
A small extraction step gives the team its first real picture of its own market. It also yields the fee history any quoting or pitch model will need.

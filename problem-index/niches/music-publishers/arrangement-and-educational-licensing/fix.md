# Eight Weeks for a Yes, and Silence Means No

**Niche:** [[niches/music-publishers/arrangement-and-educational-licensing/profile|Custom Arrangement & Educational Print Licensing]]
**Industry:** [[industries/music-publishers|Music Publishers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Bands are told to ask eight weeks before they arrange, and an unanswered request is legally a refusal.
**Tags:** #workflow-orchestration #descriptive-statistics #quick-win #automation #compliance
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to grant a band director or arranger a custom-arrangement or print licence in days, at a price set by rule rather than by negotiation, and whoever does that becomes the default route before directors give up and perform unlicensed.

## The Problem
An arranger designing a fall marching show in spring sends requests to several publishers. Some answer, some take months and some never reply. Under publisher guidance, silence is not permission, so the show is redesigned or performed at risk.

## Why It's Still Broken
Requests are low value, so they queue behind everything else. Nobody tracks turnaround, and requesters cannot see their status.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A tracked queue with published service levels, automatic reminders, and status visible to the requester. Requests that match a standard profile are pre-approved, and unreachable owners are escalated to permissions services.

## Who Feels the Pain
Band directors and arrangers, publishers losing licence fees and writers whose songs are performed without permission.

## Impact If Fixed
Most standard requests answered in days, and the case for performing unlicensed largely disappears.

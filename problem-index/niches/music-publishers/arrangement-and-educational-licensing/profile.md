# Custom Arrangement & Educational Print Licensing

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/music-publishers|Music Publishers]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to grant a band director or arranger a custom-arrangement or print licence in days, at a price set by rule rather than by negotiation, and whoever does that becomes the default route before directors give up and perform unlicensed.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$150M a year of US print, custom-arrangement and educational licensing (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~2% of US publishing revenue (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — request forms, email and weeks of waiting
**Target Buyer:** Head of print licensing at a print or educational publisher
**Automation Potential:** High for rights lookup, pricing and licence issue

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
512230 includes sheet music and educational publishers, and their audience is tens of thousands of school band directors, show choir and drum corps designers, church musicians and freelance arrangers. Arranging a copyrighted song for a marching show needs written permission. Music for All tells bands to request it at least eight weeks before arranging, the print-rights holder may differ from the copyright owner, and silence is not consent. Licences often run one to three years. The buyer pays tens to hundreds of dollars (estimate) and is not served by tools built for six-figure syncs. Hal Leonard, now owned by Muse Group, and Alfred lead print, with Tresona handling permissions for hard-to-find owners.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Publisher request forms and email, permissions services such as Tresona, and print storefronts. The gaps: owner and print-rights lookup done by hand, every request priced individually, co-publisher approvals collected serially, and no instant answer for standard cases.

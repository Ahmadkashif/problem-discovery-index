# Performing Rights Organisations & Collecting Societies

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/music-publishers|Music Publishers]]
**Category:** Insight Layer
**Value-Chain Position:** Payer & intermediary layer
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this pocket is fighting to identify every use of every work and pay the right share to the right member inside the distribution window, before unidentified money becomes non-distributable, and whoever does that most accurately and fastest keeps the writers and publishers whose repertoire makes its licence worth buying.

## What They Do
ASCAP, BMI, SESAC and GMR in the US, and about 228 CISAC member societies in 111 countries, sell blanket licences for their members' repertoire to broadcasters, streaming services, venues and businesses. They then identify which works were used and distribute the money to writers and publishers after a commission. ASCAP took in $1.945B in 2025 and made $1.759B available for distribution, and CISAC societies collected €12.59B for music in 2024. Their analytical core is distribution: matching usage to works and shares, weighting survey and census data, and defending the method to members. It is also what members compare when they choose a society. In the US, writers choose among four societies. In the EU, since the 2014 directive, rightholders can move their online rights to any society.

## Insight Function
**Size:** 50-300 distribution, data, survey and economics staff at a large society, excluding licensing sales and member services (estimate)
**Output:** Distributions to members and the methodology behind them (usage identification, share determination, survey and census weighting, credit values), plus economic evidence for rate settings
**Proprietary data:** The works and membership database and decades of matched usage, including performance logs, broadcast detections and digital service reports joined to works and shares
**Clock:** EU societies must distribute within nine months of the end of the financial year of collection and treat money still unidentified three years after that year as non-distributable (Directive 2014/26/EU, Article 13). US societies set their own distribution calendars, and their rate court cases and settlements are episodic.
**Buyer:** Chief distribution officer or chief data officer

The scores follow the indexed [[niches/independent-publishers/permissions-rights-licensing-agencies/profile|Permissions & Rights Licensing Agencies]] (53, Q1 4, Q4 4, Q5 4) and record where this pocket differs.
- **Q1 = 4.** An earlier draft scored 3 on the ground that a blanket licence needs no rights determination for each use. That ground does not hold, because the permissions agency's annual licence is blanket too. Members pay the commission for collection and for the work-and-share determination behind every distribution, and they choose and leave societies on how well it is done. It is not 5 because licensees pay for access to repertoire, not for the analysis.
- **Q4 = 4.** The pocket is scored as the collecting-society market as a whole, because the buyer field in Q5 is global. On that basis most of the pocket works under a statutory distribution deadline and a statutory point at which unidentified money is lost. The vault's US focus is kept by not scoring 5: the four US societies set their own calendars, so the clock binds the pocket's foreign majority rather than the American members. Scored for the US alone, the pocket would get Q4 3 and Q5 2, a total of 45.
- **Q5 = 4.** There are about 228 societies, each with an accountable distribution head. They exchange data through reciprocal agreements and CISAC standards, so a reference at one carries to the next.

**Kill switches:** none live, judged one by one. *Data isn't theirs:* licensees supply usage reports for distribution, which is exactly the purpose of the analysis, and the works database is built from members' registrations. *Entrenched incumbent:* PRS, STIM and GEMA process through ICE, a joint venture they own. That narrows the buyer at three large societies without locking the data away from them, and US and most other societies run their own systems or contract vendors. *Compliance gate:* ASCAP's and BMI's consent decrees regulate licensing, not the purchase of distribution tooling, and no MNPI or privilege regime applies.
**Verdict:** Qualified — indexed

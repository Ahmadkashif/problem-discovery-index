# Proxy Distributions Nobody Measures

**Niche:** [[niches/music-publishers/collecting-societies-distribution/profile|Performing Rights Organisations & Collecting Societies]]
**Industry:** [[industries/music-publishers|Music Publishers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Licence fees from bars, gyms, shops and other venues that report no playlists are commonly distributed using radio, TV or streaming data as a proxy, and nobody measures how far the proxy is from what was actually played.
**Tags:** #hypothesis-testing #confidence-intervals #monte-carlo-methods #descriptive-statistics #compliance
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this pocket is fighting to identify every use of every work and pay the right share to the right member inside the distribution window, before unidentified money becomes non-distributable, and whoever does that most accurately and fastest keeps the writers and publishers whose repertoire makes its licence worth buying.

## The Problem
General and background licensing is a large and growing source of society revenue (estimate). Most venues report nothing, so the money is distributed by reference to other usage data. A writer whose songs fill gym playlists but rarely reach radio can be paid less than their actual use, and the society cannot show members how accurate the method is.

## Why It's Still Broken
Measuring real venue usage costs money, and a published error estimate invites complaints. The proxy has been accepted practice for decades, and members cannot audit it.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Monitor a stratified sample of venues with fingerprinting, and compare the sample's work shares with the proxy's for each distribution. Publish the error and its confidence interval, and re-weight sources where the proxy is biased. Repeat the check every distribution so drift shows up.

## Who Feels the Pain
Writers and publishers whose music is used heavily in venues, distribution teams answering complaints, and boards asked to defend the method.

## Impact If Fixed
A measured, defensible distribution for one of the largest unidentified revenue pools. That is the evidence a society needs to keep members who could take their rights elsewhere.

# Royalty Processing & Writer Accounting

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/music-publishers|Music Publishers]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn every incoming statement line into the right writer's account by the contractual statement date, with each figure traceable to its source so that a writer's audit finds nothing, and whoever does that at volume becomes the royalty department's system of record.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$350M a year of royalty department labour and software across US publishers (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~5% of US publishing revenue (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium-High — systems of record exist, but imports and suspense are worked by hand
**Target Buyer:** VP of royalties or CFO at a publisher or administrator
**Automation Potential:** Very High — format mapping, matching and split application are rule and pattern work

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A publisher's royalty department receives statements from every society, sub-publisher, DSP licence and sync licensee, and must pay writers by contractual dates, often semiannually. Every line has to be matched to a work, split by shares and deal terms (at-source or receipts, administration and sub-publishing fees, advances and reserves) and posted. Writers can audit, typically within a contractual objection period (estimate). The work is high-volume and repetitive, and its errors are found by auditors years later. That makes it the most automatable function in the industry, and the one where a system change is a multi-year decision.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Vistex's Counterpoint Suite and Music Maestro, Curve Royalty Systems, statement-import tools for 150 or more sources, and in-house data teams at the majors. The gaps: new formats need developers, unmatched lines are worked in spreadsheets, matching does not learn from manual decisions, and nothing flags a line whose amount is implausible.

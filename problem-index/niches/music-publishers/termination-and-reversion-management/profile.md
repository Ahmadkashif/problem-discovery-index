# Termination & Reversion Management

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/music-publishers|Music Publishers]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to know, for every grant in a catalogue, when it can be terminated or will revert, and to act inside the statutory window, and whoever finds the window first either keeps the song or gets it back.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$300M a year of US publishing earnings on grants inside or approaching a termination or reversion window (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~4% of US publishing revenue (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Very Low — typed agreements, lawyers' calendars and memory
**Target Buyer:** Head of business and legal affairs at a publisher; counsel to writers' estates
**Automation Potential:** High for extraction and window calculation, low for the decision to serve or contest

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Section 203 lets an author terminate a grant made on or after 1 January 1978 during a five-year window opening 35 years after publication or 40 years after the grant, whichever is earlier, with notice served two to ten years ahead and recorded with the Copyright Office. Grants from the late 1980s and 1990s, the CD era, are now inside or near their windows. Contractual reversions add triggers of their own. The work is reading old paper against a statute, and both sides fight over the same knowledge. Publishers want to see notices coming and renegotiate. Writers and estates want to serve in time. Buyers need both in diligence.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Contract management systems that store agreements, rights systems that record terms, and music lawyers' spreadsheets of windows for their clients. The gaps: grant and publication dates not captured per work, author execution and work-for-hire status unchecked, and windows never joined to earnings, so the costly ones are found late.

# Brief Response & Pitch Curation

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/music-publishers|Music Publishers]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to put the song the supervisor will actually license among the first few they hear, inside the brief's 24–48 hour window, and whoever does that most often becomes the supervisor's first call.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.1B of US sync fees won through briefs and pitches (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~15% of US publishing revenue (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — catalogues are searchable, but the match is made from memory
**Target Buyer:** Head of sync or creative director at a publisher
**Automation Potential:** Medium — retrieval and re-ranking automate; the final selection stays with the rep

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
This contest is matching under a deadline. Given a brief's loose language, its reference tracks and the habits of the person who sent it, find the handful of songs that will be licensed. It is won by catalogue knowledge and by knowing each buyer's past choices. Supervisors receive pitches from every publisher and label on the same brief, keep their own stashes, and rarely explain a rejection. The rep who repeatedly sends the song that gets used is the one whose emails are opened first. The tacit-knowledge problem for the whole industry sits here (see the industry's [[problems/music-publishers/high-impact|high-impact problem]]).

## Current Tools & Gaps
DISCO and Synchtank playlists, mood and genre tags, audio-similarity search and AI tagging from Cyanite, AIMS and DISCO, and the rep's memory. The gaps: briefs not linked to pitches and outcomes, no per-buyer history, search that returns hundreds of plausible songs instead of five likely ones, and a deep catalogue that nobody on the current team remembers.

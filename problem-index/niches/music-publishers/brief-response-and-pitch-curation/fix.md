# The Deep Catalogue Nobody Remembers

**Niche:** [[niches/music-publishers/brief-response-and-pitch-curation/profile|Brief Response & Pitch Curation]]
**Industry:** [[industries/music-publishers|Music Publishers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Most songs in a large catalogue are never pitched, because nobody on the current team knows they exist.
**Tags:** #word-embeddings #k-nearest-neighbors #descriptive-statistics #quick-win #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to put the song the supervisor will actually license among the first few they hear, inside the brief's 24–48 hour window, and whoever does that most often becomes the supervisor's first call.

## The Problem
Reps pitch from the songs they know: recent signings, past placements and their own favourites. Acquired catalogues and older songs sit unpitched, even when they fit briefs well, and a publisher that paid 15 times earnings for a catalogue leaves its sync upside unused.

## Why It's Still Broken
Pitching is memory-driven and deadline-bound, and nobody measures what share of the catalogue is ever surfaced. Acquired songs often arrive with thin metadata.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A monthly coverage report on which songs were pitched, licensed or never touched, by catalogue and era. Untouched songs are auto-tagged and suggested whenever a brief's profile matches them, so deep cuts enter the shortlist alongside the familiar ones.

## Who Feels the Pain
Catalogue owners who priced in sync income, writers of older songs, and sync heads asked to grow revenue without more staff.

## Impact If Fixed
Placements for songs that earned nothing from sync before, which is the cheapest new revenue a publisher has.

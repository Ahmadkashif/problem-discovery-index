# Audio Similarity Search Re-Ranked by Outcomes

**Niche:** [[niches/music-publishers/brief-response-and-pitch-curation/profile|Brief Response & Pitch Curation]]
**Industry:** [[industries/music-publishers|Music Publishers]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Audio-similarity and auto-tagging vendors already make a catalogue searchable by sound; they rank by resemblance, not by what gets licensed.
**Tags:** #contrastive-learning #transformers #k-nearest-neighbors #evaluation-metrics #worker-facing #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to put the song the supervisor will actually license among the first few they hear, inside the brief's 24–48 hour window, and whoever does that most often becomes the supervisor's first call.

## The Problem
Reps can already paste a reference track and get two hundred sound-alikes. That list is longer than their own memory and no more accurate.

## What Already Exists
Audio-similarity search and AI tagging from vendors such as Cyanite and AIMS, and tagging inside DISCO, which DISCO says hosts over 110 million tracks.

## The Customization Gap
The publisher's version would re-rank similarity results on the team's own outcome history, conditioned on who sent the brief. It would parse the brief's text as well as its reference tracks, filter out anything that cannot be cleared for the stated use, attach a fee band from comparable licences, and explain each pick in the rep's terms. Similarity becomes the first stage of retrieval and stops being the answer.

## Target Customer
Sync teams already licensing a tagging or similarity tool.

## Impact If Solved
The shortlist shrinks from two hundred to the five the rep would have chosen, built on tools the team already pays for.

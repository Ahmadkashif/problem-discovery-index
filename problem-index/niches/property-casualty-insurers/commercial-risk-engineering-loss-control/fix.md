# The Forty-Page Report the Underwriter Skims

**Niche:** [[niches/property-casualty-insurers/commercial-risk-engineering-loss-control/profile|Commercial Risk Engineering & Loss Control]]
**Industry:** [[industries/property-casualty-insurers|Property & Casualty Insurers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** An engineer spends a day on site and another writing, and the underwriter reads the first page and the photos.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #worker-facing #quick-win #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn a field engineer's survey into recommendation-level risk data an underwriter will price on, and whoever can show that completed recommendations lower loss takes the account.

## The Problem
Survey reports are long because they must document everything. Underwriters working renewals have minutes per account. The findings that should change price or terms sit on page twenty-three. Engineers know this and resent writing reports nobody reads.

## Why It's Still Broken
Report formats were designed for completeness and file defence, not for underwriting decisions, and nobody owns the hand-off between the two departments.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Draft the report from the engineer's notes, photos and checklist. Generate a one-screen underwriting brief that lists the three findings that matter for this occupancy, what changed since the last survey and the open recommendations, and link each point to its evidence.

## Who Feels the Pain
Loss control engineers writing evenings, underwriters at renewal, and insureds who receive recommendations that never come up again.

## Impact If Fixed
Engineers get writing time back, and the risk the engineer actually saw starts reaching the price.

# Homeowners Seeking Mitigation Credit in Wildfire & Hurricane Zones

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/property-casualty-insurers|Property & Casualty Insurers]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to verify a homeowner's mitigation — a fortified roof, defensible space, ember-resistant vents — at underwriting cost, and whoever gets verified mitigation credited in a filed rate and kept at renewal takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$600M a year of mitigation inspection, verification, property-data and discount administration spend by carriers and state programmes (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~0.2% of US P&C carriers' underwriting and loss-adjustment expense (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — mitigation is evidenced by paper certificates, inspection forms and homeowner photos that rarely reach the rating engine
**Target Buyer:** Head of Homeowners Product / Underwriting, with state insurance departments as programme sponsors
**Automation Potential:** High for verification from imagery and documents; field confirmation still needed for some features

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
In high-hazard states the homeowner is the most ignored party in the pricing debate. A policyholder who replaces a roof to a fortified standard, clears defensible space or installs ember-resistant vents reduces expected loss, and several states now require carriers to recognise some of this: California's wildfire mitigation discount rules and Alabama's fortified-roof programme are examples (recalled). Yet the homeowner's evidence is a certificate, a receipt or a phone photo, the carrier cannot verify it cheaply, and the credit is often small, missing or lost at renewal. Meanwhile carriers non-renew whole ZIP codes on hazard scores the homeowner cannot see or contest.

## Current Tools & Gaps
IBHS FORTIFIED and Wildfire Prepared Home designations (recalled), state mitigation inspection forms, property-condition and wildfire scores from imagery vendors, and carrier discount tables. The gaps: credits not tied to modelled loss reduction; verification by costly inspection; designations not carried into rating at renewal; and no channel for a homeowner to show what they have done.

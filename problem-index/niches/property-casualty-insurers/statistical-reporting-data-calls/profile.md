# Statistical Reporting & Regulatory Data Calls

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/property-casualty-insurers|Property & Casualty Insurers]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to produce statistical plan submissions and regulator data calls that reconcile to the annual statement on the first pass, and whoever clears the statistical agent's edit checks and the department's deadline without a manual rework cycle takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$800M a year of statistical reporting staff, statistical agent fees, data-call effort and reconciliation work (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~0.25% of US P&C carriers' underwriting and loss-adjustment expense (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — the data is digital, but mapping, edit fixes and reconciliations are manual and repeated every cycle
**Target Buyer:** Director of Statistical Reporting / Chief Actuary / Chief Compliance Officer
**Automation Potential:** Very High — rule-bound, recurring, with objective edit checks and reconciliation targets

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The industry's shared data infrastructure depends on carriers reporting correctly. Statistical plan data feeds the advisory loss costs most carriers build rates on (logged in the vault as [[niches/independent-insurance-agents/advisory-loss-cost-forms-bureaus/profile|🔍 Advisory Loss Cost & Policy Form Bureaus]] and, for workers' compensation, [[niches/insurance-tpa/workers-comp-rating-bureaus/profile|🔍 Workers' Compensation Rating Bureaus]]). Regulator data calls are growing in size and granularity: the NAIC's 2024 property market intelligence call asked more than 400 insurers for ZIP-code-level data on more than 70 data points within 90 days. The work is mechanical and deadline-driven, and every rework cycle costs the same people who prepare the annual statement.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Data warehouses and BI tools, statistical agents' submission portals and edit reports, core-system reporting modules, and consultancies that build plan adapters. The gaps: code mappings that break with every product change; edit failures fixed one by one; reconciliations rebuilt from scratch; each data call treated as a new project.

# Personal Lines Pricing & Rate Filings

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/property-casualty-insurers|Property & Casualty Insurers]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is that every serious competitor is fighting to keep filed personal-lines rates ahead of loss trend, but writing it produces two contests with different winners: in auto, ranking drivers more finely than the carrier quoting next to them and getting that segmentation through rate review; in homeowners, getting a modelled catastrophe load and the cost of reinsurance accepted in high-hazard states without being forced to shrink. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$7B a year of carrier spend on pricing actuaries and data scientists, third-party rating data (credit-based insurance scores, motor vehicle records, prior-claims reports, telematics, catastrophe models and property scores), rating engines and filing staff for personal auto and homeowners (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~2% of US P&C carriers' underwriting and loss-adjustment expense (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** High — rating plans are built in GLM and gradient-boosting workflows and run in rating engines; filings and objection responses are still Word documents and PDFs
**Target Buyer:** Chief Pricing Actuary / Head of Personal Lines Product
**Automation Potential:** High for indications, monitoring and filing assembly; low for the regulatory negotiation

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Personal lines are roughly half of the industry's ~$1.1T of direct premium: $371.0B of private passenger auto and $188.7B of homeowners in 2025 (NAIC). Pricing is where the carrier's model meets the regulator. Every rate change in a prior-approval state is a filing with an actuarial memorandum, a rate-level indication and support for each rating variable, followed by objection letters with response deadlines. The pricing team's work is judged twice: by the loss ratio of the business it keeps, and by whether the regulator lets it charge the rate at all.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Rating engines inside Guidewire, Duck Creek and Earnix, predictive modelling in Python, R and SAS, competitive rate data from Quadrant and S&P, and filings through SERFF. The gaps: no standing link between a filed rate change and the business it actually retained or lost; indications rebuilt by hand each cycle; and objection responses drafted from scratch.

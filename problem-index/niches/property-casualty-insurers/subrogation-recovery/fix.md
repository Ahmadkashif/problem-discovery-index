# The Water Heater That Went to the Landfill

**Niche:** [[niches/property-casualty-insurers/subrogation-recovery/profile|Subrogation & Recovery]]
**Industry:** [[industries/property-casualty-insurers|Property & Casualty Insurers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A product-failure recovery dies because nobody told the insured or the restoration contractor to keep the failed part.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #bert #workflow-orchestration #quick-win #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to flag the recoverable claim at first notice rather than after payment, and whoever files the most recoveries inside each state's limitation period and each arbitration deadline takes the account.

## The Problem
Product and contractor recoveries require the evidence: the failed supply line, the water heater, the space heater, the wiring. Restoration crews tear out and discard damaged material within days. By the time anyone thinks of recovery, the evidence is gone and the manufacturer's insurer declines.

## Why It's Still Broken
Evidence preservation is nobody's first-week task. The adjuster is focused on coverage and mitigation, and the restoration contractor is paid to clear the site.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Flag likely product or contractor causes from the first-notice description. Send automatic evidence-retention instructions to the insured and the assigned contractor the same day, schedule pickup to a storage vendor, and diary a joint-inspection notice to potential defendants.

## Who Feels the Pain
Subrogation specialists, adjusters blamed for lost recoveries, and loss ratios on water and fire claims.

## Impact If Fixed
A cheap first-week step that keeps product recoveries alive.

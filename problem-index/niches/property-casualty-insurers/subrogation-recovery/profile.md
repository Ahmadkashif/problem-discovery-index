# Subrogation & Recovery

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/property-casualty-insurers|Property & Casualty Insurers]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to flag the recoverable claim at first notice rather than after payment, and whoever files the most recoveries inside each state's limitation period and each arbitration deadline takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.5B a year of carrier recovery-unit staff, contingency recovery vendors, inter-company arbitration and recovery counsel (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~0.4% of US P&C carriers' underwriting and loss-adjustment expense (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — identification depends on adjuster memory, demand packages are assembled by hand, and deadlines are diaried manually
**Target Buyer:** VP Claims Recovery / Director of Subrogation
**Automation Potential:** High for identification, packaging and deadline tracking; medium for negotiation and litigation

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Recovery is the only part of claims that brings money back in. It has its own clocks: state limitation periods, which Florida cut from four years to two for negligence in 2023, arbitration filing windows, and evidence that disappears once the damaged water heater or vehicle is discarded. Its own rules govern it too: comparative fault regimes, the made-whole doctrine, waivers of subrogation in commercial contracts. Leakage of ~10–15% of recoverable dollars at a typical carrier (estimate) goes straight to the loss ratio.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Subrogation flags in core claims systems, propensity scores from analytics vendors, contingency recovery firms that work closed files, and inter-company arbitration. The gaps: identification after payment; evidence not preserved; deadlines tracked in diaries; product and contractor recoveries missed because nobody read the cause-of-loss facts closely.

# A Recovery Screen at First Notice With the State's Rules Attached

**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to flag the recoverable claim at first notice rather than after payment, and whoever files the most recoveries inside each state's limitation period and each arbitration deadline takes the account.

## The Problem
Most recoverable claims announce themselves early: "the tenant upstairs left the bath running", "the furnace installer was there the day before", "the other driver ran the red". Whether a recovery is worth pursuing depends on the state's fault rule, the limitation period, the insured's contracts and the identity of the third party's insurer. Today these checks happen, if at all, when a recovery vendor reviews closed files.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Core-system rules catch only the obvious auto cases. Vendors paid on contingency are rewarded for working closed files, not for changing intake. The legal rules vary by state and claim type and need maintaining.

## What to Build
A first-notice screen that reads the narrative, police or fire report and photos for a third-party cause. It classifies the recovery type, checks the governing state's fault and limitation rules and any waiver in the policy and the insured's contracts, sends evidence-preservation letters on day one, and opens a deadline-tracked recovery file with an expected net value.

## Target Customer
Claims recovery leaders at personal and commercial property and auto carriers.

## Impact If Built
More recoveries identified while evidence exists, fewer lost to limitation periods, and recovery yield that no longer depends on which adjuster took the call.

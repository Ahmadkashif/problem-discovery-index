# Personal Auto Segmentation & Telematics Rating

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/property-casualty-insurers|Property & Casualty Insurers]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to rank drivers by expected loss more finely than the carrier quoting next to them and get that segmentation through state rate review, and whoever keeps its best risks after a rate change without a regulator disapproval takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$4.5B a year of auto pricing staff, rating data (credit-based insurance scores, motor vehicle records, prior-claims reports, telematics) and rating-engine spend (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~1.3% of US P&C carriers' underwriting and loss-adjustment expense (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Very High — rating is fully algorithmic and quotes are returned in seconds through comparative raters and direct channels
**Target Buyer:** Chief Pricing Actuary, Personal Auto
**Automation Potential:** High for modelling and monitoring; constrained by regulatory approval and consumer-data rules

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Private passenger auto is the largest line in the industry ($371.0B of direct premium in 2025, NAIC) and the most price-shopped. A carrier that segments less finely than its competitors keeps the drivers its competitors underprice it on and loses the ones it overprices. That is adverse selection, and it shows up within a renewal cycle. Telematics added the strongest new signal in decades. The FTC's January 2026 order barring GM from sharing driver data with consumer reporting agencies for five years shows how far consent rules now govern which data can be used. The win condition is statistical and regulatory at once: the finest segmentation that rate reviewers will approve and consumers will consent to.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Telematics programmes and scores from Cambridge Mobile Telematics, Arity and LexisNexis; credit-based insurance scores; competitive rate data from Quadrant; pricing platforms from Earnix, Akur8 and WTW. The gaps: no routine measure of who left after a rate change; vendor scores fitted to someone else's losses; and variables that cannot be explained in rate review.

# Reserve Changes Nobody Explains to the Actuary

**Niche:** [[niches/property-casualty-insurers/casualty-claims-litigation-reserves/profile|Casualty Claims Litigation & Reserve Adequacy]]
**Industry:** [[industries/property-casualty-insurers|Property & Casualty Insurers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A case reserve jumps on a two-year-old claim, and the reserving actuary learns why only by emailing the adjuster, if the adjuster still works there.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #change-point-detection #data-integration #quick-win #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to spot, within days of first notice, the liability claims that will draw an attorney and run toward a verdict, and whoever moves those claims to early resolution or an adequate reserve before the demand letter arrives takes the account.

## The Problem
Case reserves change for many reasons: new medical information, a demand, a coverage decision, a supervisor's review, a new reserving guideline. The claims system records the new figure, not the reason. When reserves strengthen across a line, the actuary cannot tell a change in losses from a change in practice, which is exactly the distinction a reserve opinion depends on.

## Why It's Still Broken
Reason codes exist in some systems but are optional, coarse and rarely accurate, and adjusters see no benefit from completing them.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Draft the reason for every material reserve change from the notes and documents added since the last change. Have the adjuster confirm it in one click, and roll the reasons up so actuaries see, by line and accident year, how much movement came from new facts versus practice changes.

## Who Feels the Pain
Reserving actuaries before each quarter close and the 1 March opinion, claims leaders explaining development to the board, and auditors.

## Impact If Fixed
Reserve movements arrive explained, and practice changes stop masquerading as loss trend.

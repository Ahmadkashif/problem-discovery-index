# Homeowners Catastrophe Loads & Rate Adequacy

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/property-casualty-insurers|Property & Casualty Insurers]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get a modelled catastrophe load and the cost of reinsurance accepted in a rate filing for a high-hazard state, and whoever wins approval for an adequate rate without committing to write more than it can carry takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$2.5B a year of catastrophe model licences, property risk data, cat modelling and pricing staff, and filing work for homeowners (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~0.7% of US P&C carriers' underwriting and loss-adjustment expense (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** High for modelling — event sets, exceedance curves and property scores; low for the regulatory exchange, which runs on exhibits and hearings
**Target Buyer:** Chief Actuary / Head of Homeowners and Catastrophe Management
**Automation Potential:** Medium — model runs and exhibits automate; the negotiation over what may enter the rate does not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Homeowners premium was $188.7B in 2025 (NAIC), and in wildfire and hurricane states the line's profitability is set less by the attritional loss ratio than by two contested inputs. One is the expected catastrophe loss, which modern models estimate and many regulators long refused to accept in rates. The other is the cost of the reinsurance the carrier must buy to survive a bad year. California completed its first review of a wildfire model for ratemaking in July 2025 (Verisk's), and carriers using one must commit to writing more policies in distressed areas. The contest is a regulatory one fought with modelling evidence, and its losers non-renew.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Catastrophe models from Verisk, Moody's RMS and KCC; property-level hazard and condition scores from Cotality, ZestyAI and CAPE; reinsurance broker analytics; filing consultancies. The gaps: rate indications rebuilt by hand whenever a model version changes; little evidence linking a mitigation credit to modelled loss; and distressed-area commitments tracked in spreadsheets.

# Safe, Vault & Security Container Service

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/locksmiths|Locksmiths]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get a locked-out safe, vault or security container open and back in service with the least damage, and whoever opens the most units without destroying the lock or the container's rating takes the account.

**Automation Potential:** Low for the work itself; medium for service records, scheduling and method selection support

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Opening a locked-out safe is the craft end of the trade. A technician manipulates the combination by feel, scopes through a drilled hole, or drills a precise point to defeat the lock without triggering relockers that make the job harder. Burglary-rated safes (UL 687 TL-15 and TL-30) and high-security combination locks are designed to resist exactly that. After drilling, the repair must restore the rating or the client's insurer may not accept it. Banks contract vault door, time lock and ATM safe service across branches. Federal work runs on GSA-approved containers with FF-L-2740 locks, where the DoD Lock Program is the technical authority and trains technicians on the locks, and an opened container has to be repaired to federal standards before it can hold classified material again. SAVTA represents the safe and vault specialists.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Manufacturer service manuals, guarded drilling references, borescopes, autodialers and specialist drill rigs; bank facilities work-order systems; paper container records. The gaps: no per-container history across lockouts and repairs, method choice based on memory, and certification paperwork kept separately from the service record.

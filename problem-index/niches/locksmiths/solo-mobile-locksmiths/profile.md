# Solo & Two-Truck Mobile Locksmiths

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/locksmiths|Locksmiths]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to put the owner's own verified, licensed business in front of the emergency caller ahead of the lead-generation networks, and whoever lowers the owner's cost per completed job takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** $840M in 2022 receipts at the 2,867 locksmith firms with fewer than five employees (Census SUSB 2022), plus an estimated ~10,000–20,000 nonemployer sole proprietors (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~32% of employer-firm receipts (SUSB 2022)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium-Low — the business runs from a phone: Google Business Profile, a call-forwarding number, texts and a payment app, with paper or a basic field-service app for records
**Target Buyer:** Owner-operator
**Automation Potential:** Medium — call answering, quoting, lead accounting and paperwork automate; the owner is still the technician

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Three in four locksmith firms are this size. The owner answers the phone, drives the van, does the job, collects payment and does the books at night. They compete for the same emergency searches as call-centre networks that pose as local businesses. Google and UC San Diego found that 11% of search results for locksmiths were fraudulent in 2017, and Google now requires Advanced Verification for every locksmith in its Local Services ads. The owner pays per lead to Google, Angi or pay-per-call networks, or loses the call. Tools are built for larger field-service firms or for consumers, not for a one-person regulated trade.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Google Business Profile and Local Services ads, Angi and Thumbtack, answering services such as those analysed under [[industries/answering-services|Telephone Answering Services]], general field-service apps (Workiz, Jobber, Housecall Pro), payment apps and QuickBooks. The gaps: missed calls at night and on jobs, no view of which lead channel actually pays after disputes and no-shows, review attacks from fake competitors, and compliance paperwork kept separately.

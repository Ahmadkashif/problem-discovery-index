# Lead Accounting That Shows Which Channel Pays

**Niche:** [[niches/locksmiths/solo-mobile-locksmiths/profile|Solo & Two-Truck Mobile Locksmiths]]
**Industry:** [[industries/locksmiths|Locksmiths]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A solo locksmith pays Google, Angi and a pay-per-call network every month and cannot say which one produces paid jobs once wrong numbers, price shoppers and disputed leads are taken out.
**Tags:** #logistic-regression #causal-inference #large-language-models #descriptive-statistics #data-integration #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to put the owner's own verified, licensed business in front of the emergency caller ahead of the lead-generation networks, and whoever lowers the owner's cost per completed job takes the account.

## The Problem
Lead platforms report leads, and the owner's bank account reports deposits. In between are calls that were spam, locked-out drivers who called five numbers and took the first to answer, jobs quoted and lost on price, and leads charged though they fell outside the service area. The owner tops up whichever channel felt busiest last week.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Each platform reports only its own leads and has no incentive to show its true cost per job. Field-service apps record jobs but not the lead source reliably. A one-person business has no analyst and no time to reconcile.

## What to Build
A small tool that joins call-tracking records, platform lead logs and the job and payment records. A language model classifies each call from its transcript (genuine job, price shopper, spam, out of area, wrong service) and drafts dispute requests where a platform's rules allow a credit. The owner sees cost per completed job and margin by channel, with the effect of answering speed and time of day, and gets a monthly recommendation on where to spend.

## Target Customer
Owner-operators and two- to five-truck locksmith firms spending on two or more lead channels.

## Impact If Built
Marketing spend moves to the channels that pay, disputed leads are credited instead of absorbed, and the owner stops funding the networks that undercut them. For a solo operator spending a few thousand dollars a month on leads, recovering even a tenth matters (estimate).

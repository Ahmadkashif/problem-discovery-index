# Key Cabinets Taught What a Key Opens

**Niche:** [[niches/locksmiths/master-key-systems-key-control/profile|Master-Key Systems & Key Control]]
**Industry:** [[industries/locksmiths|Locksmiths]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Electronic key cabinets already log who took which key and when; they treat each key as an object with a tag, not as a bitting that opens a set of doors.
**Tags:** #graph-theory #combinatorics-and-counting #data-integration #compliance #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to hold the authoritative record of who can open which door, and whoever can answer a lost master or a termination with a costed rekey plan the same day takes the account.

## The Problem
Hospitals, campuses, car dealers and property managers use electronic key cabinets and key-tracking software to control who can take keys. When a key is not returned, the system knows who had it last but cannot say what that person can now open.

## What Already Exists
Key management systems such as KeyTrak and Traka offer access-controlled cabinets, audit trails, user permissions and alerts for overdue keys. Some integrate with access-control systems for user lists.

## The Customization Gap
The extension links each tracked key to its position in the master-key system (bitting, level, keyway) and each cylinder to its door. A missing key then resolves to a door list and a risk level. Overdue alerts escalate by exposure (a grand master outranks a change key), and leaver workflows from HR trigger a key-return check and, when keys are missing, a rekey plan from the locksmith. Restricted-keyway rules (who may authorise duplicates) live in the same record.

## Target Customer
Institutions and property owners who already run key cabinets, and the locksmiths who maintain their systems.

## Impact If Solved
The cabinet's audit trail becomes a security answer, not just a custody log, inside a system the client already owns.

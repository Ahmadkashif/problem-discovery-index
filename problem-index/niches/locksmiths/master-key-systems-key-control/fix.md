# The Bitting Chart That Leaves With the Locksmith

**Niche:** [[niches/locksmiths/master-key-systems-key-control/profile|Master-Key Systems & Key Control]]
**Industry:** [[industries/locksmiths|Locksmiths]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** When a client changes locksmiths, or the one who designed the system retires, the bitting chart often goes with them, and the next locksmith has to decode the system from the doors.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #descriptive-statistics #compliance #data-integration #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to hold the authoritative record of who can open which door, and whoever can answer a lost master or a termination with a costed rekey plan the same day takes the account.

## The Problem
Many clients never take custody of their own key system records. The designing locksmith holds the chart as a matter of security and, quietly, of retention. When the relationship ends, the client discovers it cannot extend or audit its own system without paying to reconstruct it, by disassembling cylinders and decoding keys.

## Why It's Still Broken
Key records are sensitive, so locksmiths avoid sharing them. Clients don't know to ask, and there is no standard format for handing a system over.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A standard, encrypted system export that the client holds and the locksmith maintains. It contains the hierarchy, bittings and pinning by door, and the key-holder ledger, versioned with every change and released to a new locksmith only on the client's signed authority. Contracts can require it, and the locksmith's software produces it automatically.

## Who Feels the Pain
Facility managers locked into one vendor; incoming locksmiths reconstructing systems; security officers who cannot audit what they own.

## Impact If Fixed
Clients keep control of their own security design, handovers take days instead of weeks, and honest locksmiths compete on service rather than on holding the chart.

# Commercial Door Hardware Service & Code Compliance

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/locksmiths|Locksmiths]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is that every serious competitor is fighting to be the vendor a facility calls for its openings. But a fire-rated or egress door is won by inspecting it and closing deficiencies before the fire marshal or accreditation surveyor arrives, while an access-controlled door is won by delivering electrified hardware that works with the client's access system and still releases for egress on commissioning day. The buyers, data and clocks differ. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.1B a year of commercial door hardware service, retrofit, inspection and electrified-hardware work by locksmith firms (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~40% of 561622 receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low-Medium — tickets arrive through clients' work-order portals, but opening records, hardware schedules and inspection results live in PDFs, photos and technicians' memory
**Target Buyer:** Facilities director or maintenance manager at a school district, hospital, university, retailer or property manager; service manager at the locksmith firm
**Automation Potential:** Medium — documentation, parts prediction and quoting automate; the repair stays hands-on

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
This is the business-to-business core of the larger locksmith firms, the 101 with twenty or more employees and many shops just below them. The unit of work is the opening: a door, frame, hinges, lock or exit device, closer, protection plates and often electrified hardware. The client is a facility with hundreds or thousands of openings, under service agreements or per-call rates. The work is closers, exit devices, mortise and cylindrical locks, door and frame repair, ADA hardware retrofits, fire-door inspection and electrified locking. Residential lockouts and car keys are a different trade with different buyers. Hanging doors and installing their hardware in new construction belongs to the queued Finish Carpentry Contractors industry.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Clients run work orders in facility platforms such as ServiceChannel or Corrigo, or a CMMS. Locksmiths run field-service software and manufacturers' catalogues, with DHI and manufacturer training behind the technicians. The gaps: no record keyed to the individual opening, callbacks not linked to the first visit, hardware unidentified until someone climbs a ladder, and compliance status reported as a PDF. Two contests sit inside this niche and are split out as sub-niches. Fire and egress doors are run by an inspection clock. Electrified doors are run by commissioning with the [[industries/security-alarm-integrators|access-control integrator]].

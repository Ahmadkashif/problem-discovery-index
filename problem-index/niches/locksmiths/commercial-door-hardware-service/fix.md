# The Callback Logged as a New Job

**Niche:** [[niches/locksmiths/commercial-door-hardware-service/profile|Commercial Door Hardware Service & Code Compliance]]
**Industry:** [[industries/locksmiths|Locksmiths]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** When a repaired door fails again, the return visit arrives as a fresh ticket and is billed or absorbed as new work, so nobody knows which fixes hold.

**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is that every serious competitor is fighting to be the vendor a facility calls for its openings. But a fire-rated or egress door is won by inspecting it and closing deficiencies before the fire marshal or accreditation surveyor arrives, while an access-controlled door is won by delivering electrified hardware that works with the client's access system and still releases for egress on commissioning day. The buyers, data and clocks differ. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## The Problem
A technician files a strike to make a door latch, closes the ticket and leaves. Three weeks later the door is dragging again because the real cause was a worn top hinge. The second visit is a new ticket from the client. Depending on the contract it is billed again, which annoys the client, or absorbed, which the firm never measures. First-visit fix rate, the number a facility director judges a vendor by, goes uncounted on both sides.

## Why It's Still Broken
Jobs are keyed to the address and the client's ticket number, not to the door. Clients' platforms do not share a stable opening identifier with vendors. Technicians have no incentive to flag their own callbacks, and service managers have no report that would show them.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Give every serviced opening a stable identifier (a tag on the frame or the client's room-and-door number) and record it on every job. Any new ticket on the same opening within 30 or 90 days is flagged as a callback and linked to the earlier visit, with both technicians' notes and photos side by side. A monthly report shows callback rates by technician, hardware type and fault, so the shop can see which fixes are band-aids and who needs coaching.

## Who Feels the Pain
Facility managers paying twice for one fault; service managers who cannot see which technicians' work holds; and technicians blamed for repeat failures they did not cause.

## Impact If Fixed
A low-cost change in recording makes first-visit fix rate measurable. It turns callbacks into a coaching list and gives the firm a number to put in front of clients at renewal.

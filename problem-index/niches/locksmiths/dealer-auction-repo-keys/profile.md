# Dealer, Auction & Repossession Key Work

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/locksmiths|Locksmiths]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to put a working key in every keyless unit on a dealer lot, auction lane or repossession yard before it sells, and whoever turns around the most VINs per visit at a fixed per-unit price takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$150M a year of batch key cutting and programming for used-car dealers, wholesale auctions, repossession agencies and lenders' remarketing (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~6% of 561622 receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — VIN lists arrive by email or spreadsheet and units are tracked in dealer and auction systems, but key status and feasibility are worked out unit by unit
**Target Buyer:** Used-car manager at an independent dealer; reconditioning manager at an auction; repossession agency owner or lender remarketing manager
**Automation Potential:** Medium-High — batch feasibility, parts ordering, scheduling and per-unit pricing are rule-based

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
These buyers have many vehicles and no keys, or one key where two are expected. Repossessed vehicles often arrive without keys, and NASTF's terms allow security data to be drawn against a repossession title. Trade-ins and auction units often come with a single key, which buyers treat as a condition item. Franchised dealers' service departments are slow and expensive for this, and retail locksmith pricing does not fit volume. The work is batch automotive key programming at a negotiated per-unit price. Its rhythm is set by reconditioning and sale dates, not emergencies. The buyers sit in [[industries/auto-dealers-independent|Independent Auto Dealers]] and [[industries/repossession-services|Repossession Agencies]]. Lender-side repossession work also requires the locksmith to handle ownership authority carefully under the NASTF rules.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Dealer management systems, auction condition reports, repossession assignment platforms, spreadsheets and the same programmers and NASTF portal used for retail work. The gaps: no batch feasibility check across a VIN list, no per-unit price grounded in each vehicle's difficulty, and no key status field that travels with the unit.

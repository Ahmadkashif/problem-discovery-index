# The Former Tenant's Key Still Works

**Niche:** [[niches/locksmiths/rental-turnover-rekeying/profile|Rental-Turnover Rekeying & Smart-Lock Retrofit]]
**Industry:** [[industries/locksmiths|Locksmiths]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A rushed turn skips the rekey, or the locksmith rekeys the front door but not the side door, and the previous resident's key still opens the home.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #compliance #workflow-orchestration #quick-win #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to reset access at every unit turn before the next resident moves in, and whoever can prove every door was reset before move-in, by rekey or smart-lock code change, at the lowest cost per turn takes the account.

## The Problem
Turns are compressed between move-out and move-in, and the lock is often the last task. Rekeys get skipped, done on some doors only, or done with the same pinning as before. Nobody checks, because nothing records which doors a unit has or what was done to each.

## Why It's Still Broken
Work orders say "rekey" without listing the doors, and the locksmith invoices a lump sum. The manager has no way to verify completion short of trying old keys.

## What a Fix Looks Like
List every exterior door per unit once, at the next visit. Require a per-door completion entry (new core or pinning reference, photo) on each rekey order. Flag any turn released for move-in without it.

## Who Feels the Pain
Residents whose homes are less secure than they believe; managers exposed to liability; maintenance coordinators chasing incomplete turns.

## Impact If Fixed
A per-door checklist closes the most common gap in rental security for almost no added cost per turn.

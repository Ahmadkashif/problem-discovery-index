# Rental-Turnover Rekeying & Smart-Lock Retrofit

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/locksmiths|Locksmiths]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to reset access at every unit turn before the next resident moves in, and whoever can prove every door was reset before move-in, by rekey or smart-lock code change, at the lowest cost per turn takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$250M a year of turnover rekeys, lock changes, master-keyed apartment systems and smart-lock retrofits for rental owners and property managers, by locksmith firms (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~10% of 561622 receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — rekey requests arrive as work orders from property-management software, while key and code records sit in spreadsheets and smart-lock portals
**Target Buyer:** Maintenance director or turn coordinator at a multifamily or single-family rental manager
**Automation Potential:** High — turn rekeys repeat at every move-out, and smart locks replace much of the physical work with a code reset

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Every unit turn needs access reset before the next resident moves in. In apartments that means re-pinning or swapping a core within a building master-key system; in scattered single-family rentals it means a truck roll to each house. Smart-lock platforms (SmartRent, Latch and residential locks from Schlage, Yale and Kwikset) turn the rekey into a credential change, and Aliro 1.0, released in February 2026, will put those credentials in phone wallets. The locksmith's position shifts from rekeying every turn to retrofitting once and maintaining the hardware. The buyer runs on [[industries/property-management|Property Management]] software and judges vendors on cost per turn and readiness by the move-in date.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Work orders from Yardi, AppFolio or RealPage; interchangeable-core cylinders; smart-lock platforms; spreadsheets of keys and codes. The gaps: no unit-level access history across mechanical and digital, no economics for rekey versus retrofit by property, and turn scheduling that leaves the lock until last.

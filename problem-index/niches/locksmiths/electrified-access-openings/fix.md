# The Fail-Secure Strike on an Egress Door

**Niche:** [[niches/locksmiths/electrified-access-openings/profile|Electrified Hardware & Access-Ready Openings]]
**Industry:** [[industries/locksmiths|Locksmiths]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A stairwell door that should unlock on fire alarm stays locked because the strike ordered was fail-secure, and the mistake is found by the fire marshal at final inspection.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #compliance #workflow-orchestration #quick-win #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to deliver electrified locks, strikes and exit devices that work with the client's access-control system and still release for egress and fire alarm the day the door is commissioned, and whoever passes commissioning without a return visit takes the account.

## The Problem
Fail-safe hardware unlocks when power is cut; fail-secure stays locked. Which one a door needs depends on its use, fire rating, egress path and local code, including stair re-entry rules in many jurisdictions. The choice is made in a schedule line that few people read closely, and the error is found at the worst moment.

## Why It's Still Broken
The decision sits between three parties: the hardware consultant, the integrator and the fire-alarm contractor. No single document shows a door's fail mode next to its egress and fire requirements, and installers fit what was ordered.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Add a one-line fail-mode and release check per electrified door to the submittal review. It states whether the door is on an egress path, fire-rated or in a stairwell, and what releases it, and it flags any combination that contradicts the hardware ordered. The locksmith confirms it at installation with a photo of the device label.

## Who Feels the Pain
Installers sent back to swap hardware; integrators failing commissioning; building owners whose occupancy is held up.

## Impact If Fixed
A few minutes per door at submittal avoids reorders and failed inspections on exactly the doors that matter most for life safety.

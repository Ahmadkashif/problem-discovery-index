## Why These Niches

Commercial real estate fragments along asset type (office vs. industrial vs. retail), geography (metro vs. rural), buyer demographic (institutional vs. immigrant investor vs. minority entrepreneur), and operational function (deal-making vs. back-office administration). These 8 niches cover the two largest revenue-generating asset specializations (office leasing and industrial), the two most digitally neglected segments (rural brokers operating without CoStar and owner-occupied deal brokers ignored by institutional tech), two underserved buyer populations (minority-owned firms lacking network access and immigrant investors navigating CRE without culturally-aligned services), and two high-volume back-office workflows ripe for automation (lease administration and commission tracking). Excluded: institutional REIT asset management (distinct industry), residential-to-commercial crossover agents (too diffuse), and national platform brokerages (already well-tooled).

## Why These Pockets

The densest industry in the sweep after accounting, and for the same structural reason: commercial property has no public price disclosure in most of the country, so every consequential number — what a building is worth, what it rents for, what it should be assessed at — is established by somebody's research and then argued about.

Three qualifiers, and they are the three positions where that argument is settled in writing. Property data platforms employ up to two thousand researchers whose job is to phone-verify facts no public record contains, and sell the result. Appraisal practices produce the written opinion a regulated lender is required to obtain before closing. Property tax consultancies build the valuation case that an assessment is too high and prosecute it against a jurisdiction that wants the revenue, under statutory deadlines where a miss costs a client a full year with no remedy.

All three share the pattern this sweep keeps finding, in an unusually pure form. Each holds thousands of prior instances of its own core judgment — verified facts and their provenance, comparable selections and adjustments, appeal arguments and what the board did with them — stored as delivered artefacts indexed by client rather than as retrievable reasoning. And in all three the outcome that would grade the judgment is generated and discarded: verification calls confirm or contradict prior records, review appraisers find the same weaknesses repeatedly, and appeal boards accept or reject specific arguments in front of the same assessors year after year.

Ten pockets logged without qualifying. Two carry information-barrier kill switches, which is what MNPI looks like when it appears — rating analysts and public securities researchers both work on unannounced material. The largest research organizations in the industry sit in brokerage, where several hundred economists produce the market reports the whole sector cites, funded as a lead generator. And the earliest demand signal in commercial real estate — leasing pipeline activity, visible months before a lease is recorded — is published free by software vendors as marketing.

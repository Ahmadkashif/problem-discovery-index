# The Privacy Officer

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/synthetic-data-providers|Synthetic Data Providers]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to give the person who signs the release something they can defend afterwards — and whoever does that takes the account, because that signature is the last gate every deal passes through.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$120M US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~8% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** None — the approval artefact does not exist
**Target Buyer:** Privacy, data protection and compliance functions
**Automation Potential:** Medium — the evidence assembly is automatable, the judgement is not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Every synthetic data purchase terminates at a privacy officer being asked to approve a release. They are handed a parameter they cannot interpret, a set of attack results whose methodology they cannot assess, run by the party selling the product, and a request to take personal and organisational accountability for a guarantee the research field has not settled. Their tools are a spreadsheet and a meeting. They are the decision-maker in the transaction, the least served constituency in the category, and the reason a large share of evaluations stall indefinitely — not because the answer is no, but because nobody can construct the basis for yes.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Privacy impact assessment templates, data protection impact assessment processes, legal opinion, and the vendor's own report. The gaps: no standard artefact for approving a synthetic release; no way to compare one vendor's privacy claim against another's because the accounting assumptions differ; no residual risk statement in terms the officer's own framework uses; no record of what was approved on what basis when a regulator asks two years later; and no independent party to defer to.

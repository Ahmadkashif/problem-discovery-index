# The Function That Is Represented by Somebody Else

**Niche:** [[niches/work-collaboration-tools/cross-functional-work-management/profile|Cross-Functional Work Management]]
**Industry:** [[industries/work-collaboration-tools|Work Collaboration Tools]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Functions that did not adopt the programme tool are represented in it by a task that a project manager maintains on their behalf, which is the single largest source of fiction in every cross-functional programme view.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #hypothesis-testing #confidence-intervals #data-integration #workflow-orchestration #quick-win #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in cross-functional work management is fighting to assemble the real state of a programme spanning functions that each work in a different tool — and whoever produces that view without asking anyone takes the account.

## The Problem
The programme board has a task called "Legal review — contract terms", assigned to a legal counsel who has never logged into the tool, with a status the project manager updates after a conversation every week or two. Legal is working in its own matter management system where the review has a queue position, a reviewer and a genuine state. The board's task is a rumour maintained by a third party. There are four such tasks on this programme, covering the functions most likely to delay it, and the programme view is most fictional exactly where it most matters.

## Why It's Still Broken
Licences cost money and functions resist adopting a tool that does not fit their work, both of which are reasonable, so representing them by proxy became the pragmatic answer and then became invisible as a problem. The proxy task looks identical to a real one on the board, which is the specific design failure — nothing distinguishes a status somebody set from a status somebody guessed on another team's behalf. And the project manager maintaining it has no incentive to mark their own entries as unreliable.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Distinguish observed from represented, visibly, and then reduce the represented share. Every item on a programme carries its provenance: maintained by the owning function in this tool, read from that function's own system, or represented by a third party — and the third category is rendered differently and carries the date and person of its last confirmation, which is the honest correction and costs nothing. Report the proportion of a programme that is represented rather than observed, which is a number every programme leader would want and none has. Then reduce it: read state from the owning function's system where an integration is possible, which is the federation path; give the owning function a lightweight way to update their own item without a full licence or a new tool, since the resistance is usually to adoption rather than to reporting; and where neither is available, at least schedule and record the confirmation rather than leaving it to a corridor conversation. The proportion observed becomes a quality measure for the programme view itself.

## Who Feels the Pain
Programme leaders making decisions on a board whose least reliable entries cover the functions most likely to delay them; the functions represented by somebody else's approximation of their work; and project managers maintaining fiction they know to be fiction.

## Impact If Fixed
Marking provenance is a display change and immediately tells a reviewer which parts of a programme view to believe. The proportion-observed metric turns an invisible structural weakness into something a programme office can improve, and reducing it through lightweight self-reporting is usually far easier than the licence-and-adoption conversation that currently blocks it.

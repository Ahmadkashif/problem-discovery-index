# Regulated & Records-Managed Collaboration

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/work-collaboration-tools|Work Collaboration Tools]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in regulated collaboration is fighting to let people work in modern tools while every communication that constitutes a record is retained, retrievable and appropriately restricted — and whoever makes that true without crippling the tools takes the sector.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$2.1B US collaboration technology spend in regulated and records-obligated organisations
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~11% of collaboration software revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Low relative to the rest of the category — adoption is deliberately restricted by compliance
**Target Buyer:** Compliance, legal and records management functions, who hold a veto over the technology choice
**Automation Potential:** High — classification, retention and restriction are all rule-governed and largely manual

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
In a regulated organisation, a message may be a business record. A chat thread between two people about a client's account, a document shared in a channel, a decision taken in a meeting — each may be subject to retention obligations, discoverable in litigation, required to be supervised, or restricted to those with a legitimate need. That reality inverts the category's usual dynamics. Where most organisations adopt collaboration tools bottom-up and worry later, these organisations cannot: compliance evaluates the tool before anyone uses it, and the evaluation turns on retention, supervision, export, restriction and legal hold rather than on whether the product is good. The result is a sector running older, more restricted, less capable tools than the rest of the economy, with people working around them — which is the worst outcome for compliance as well as for productivity, since work that has moved to a personal channel is neither retained nor supervised.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Microsoft and Google provide substantial compliance capability in their enterprise tiers; specialist archiving and supervision vendors serve financial services; information governance products handle retention and legal hold. The gaps: retention is applied at the container level — a whole channel or site — rather than to the record, so organisations over-retain everything and then cannot find anything; classification of what is actually a record is manual or absent; restriction is by membership rather than by content, so a channel containing one sensitive matter is restricted entirely; legal hold across modern collaboration surfaces is incomplete, particularly for ephemeral and third-party content; and the workarounds — personal messaging, personal email, unmanaged file sharing — are neither measured nor addressed, which is the largest actual exposure and the least discussed.

## The Problem
An organisation has a records retention schedule covering document types with defined periods, a classification scheme, a disposition process and a legal hold procedure. All of it was designed for documents and email. Substantive business decisions now happen in chat threads, in collaborative documents with no final version, in meeting recordings, in comments on a design, and in a channel shared with an external party — none of which the schedule contemplates and most of which is handled by a container-level rule applied as an approximation.

## What Already Exists
Records management practice is mature and codified, with professional standards, established retention scheduling methodology and defensible disposition practice. Information governance and archiving products are a developed commercial category. Legal hold and eDiscovery tooling is sophisticated. Classification technology is commodity. Everything required exists, developed for surfaces that are no longer where the work is.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to collaborative, mutable, multi-participant content. It requires: (1) records defined for content that has no final version — a collaborative document edited by six people over a year is not a document in the records sense and the schedule has to say what it is; (2) conversation threads treated as the unit rather than individual messages, since a record's meaning lives in the exchange and retaining messages individually produces a corpus that is complete and unreadable; (3) ephemeral and external content handled explicitly, including reactions, edits, deletions and content shared by parties outside the organisation, which is where container-level hold most commonly fails; (4) disposition that actually executes, since the schedules exist and the deletion frequently does not, and an organisation that has a policy it does not apply is in a worse position than one with no policy; and (5) the practice extended to meeting recordings and transcripts, which are now generated automatically in enormous volume and which almost no retention schedule addresses at all.

## Target Customer
Records management and compliance functions in regulated organisations, collaboration platform vendors, and the information governance vendors whose coverage stops at email and documents.

## Impact If Solved
The practice is mature and the surfaces have moved, which makes this an extension rather than an invention. Meeting recordings and transcripts are the most urgent gap — they are being generated at scale right now, contain substantive business content, and sit outside almost every organisation's retention schedule.

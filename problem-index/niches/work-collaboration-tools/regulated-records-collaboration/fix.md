# The Workaround Nobody Measures

**Niche:** [[niches/work-collaboration-tools/regulated-records-collaboration/profile|Regulated & Records-Managed Collaboration]]
**Industry:** [[industries/work-collaboration-tools|Work Collaboration Tools]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** When the sanctioned tools are too restricted to work in, people move the conversation to personal messaging — where it is neither retained nor supervised — and the organisation's compliance posture is worse than if the tool had been permissive.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #hypothesis-testing #confidence-intervals #compliance #worker-facing #quick-win #workflow-orchestration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in regulated collaboration is fighting to let people work in modern tools while every communication that constitutes a record is retained, retrievable and appropriately restricted — and whoever makes that true without crippling the tools takes the sector.

## The Problem
A firm restricts its collaboration tools heavily: no external channels, no file sharing with clients, no mobile access to certain content, message retention that makes threads disappear. People still have to do their jobs, so an increasing share of substantive conversation moves to personal messaging applications — which the firm does not control, cannot supervise and cannot retain. Enforcement actions in several sectors have made the consequences of exactly this pattern expensive and public. The restriction that was intended to ensure compliance has produced a large, invisible, unretained communication channel, which is a materially worse position than a well-supervised permissive one.

## Why It's Still Broken
Each restriction was individually defensible and was decided by a function accountable for compliance rather than for whether people can work. Nobody measures the workaround, so the restriction's cost is invisible while its benefit is asserted. And the workaround is uncomfortable to measure, since establishing that a substantial share of business communication happens on unmanaged channels creates an obligation to do something about it — which is why organisations typically discover it through an enforcement action rather than through their own monitoring.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Measure displacement and treat it as the primary compliance metric. Surveys are the honest starting point and are more informative than they sound, since people will say what they use when asked without blame. Beyond that: sanctioned-channel volume against the volume of work being done, external collaboration requests denied, and — where the organisation can legitimately observe it — the share of client interactions with no corresponding record in a supervised channel. Then treat every restriction as a trade with a measured cost: this control prevents this risk and displaces this much conversation, which is the calculation nobody currently performs and which would change several decisions. Provide a sanctioned path for the thing people are working around, since the displacement is almost always concentrated in one or two specific needs — external collaboration and mobile access, usually — and meeting those needs inside the supervised estate is the only intervention that works. Policy reminders do not; that has been established repeatedly and at considerable expense across the sector.

## Who Feels the Pain
Compliance functions whose posture is worse than their controls suggest; employees choosing between doing their job and following a policy; and the firms that discover the extent of it in an enforcement action.

## Impact If Fixed
Displacement is the metric that determines whether a restriction improved compliance or damaged it, and essentially nobody measures it. Meeting the specific needs that drive it — usually external collaboration and mobile access — inside the supervised estate is the intervention that works, and it is a product decision rather than a policy one.

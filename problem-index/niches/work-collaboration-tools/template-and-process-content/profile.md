# Template & Process Content

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/work-collaboration-tools|Work Collaboration Tools]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in onboarding content is fighting to give a team a starting structure that matches how that team actually works — and whoever produces a template teams keep past the first month takes the adoption problem.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$890M US spend attributable to templates, onboarding content and implementation services in the category
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~4% of collaboration software revenue, gating adoption of far more
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — template galleries are universal and abandonment is universal
**Target Buyer:** Onboarding, customer success and content teams at the platforms
**Automation Potential:** Very High — a team's actual process is inferable from what they do, and templates are written from imagination

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Every platform in the category ships hundreds of templates as an onboarding accelerant, on the reasonable theory that a blank workspace is intimidating and a starting structure helps. Teams adopt one, use it for three weeks and abandon it, because a generic marketing campaign template describes nobody's actual marketing campaign — it describes an idealised one written by a content team who inferred it from a blog post. The abandonment is near-universal and is treated as an adoption problem to be solved with better onboarding, when it is a content problem: the template was generic because writing a specific one required knowing how this team works, which nobody did. Meanwhile the platform holds detailed records of how thousands of teams doing similar work actually structure it, which is the empirical answer to the question the templates guess at.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Every platform has a template gallery, most have a marketplace with community contributions, and implementation partners supply bespoke structures for large customers. The gaps: templates are authored rather than derived, so they reflect a content writer's model of a process rather than any observed one; nothing measures template survival, so the abandonment is invisible as a metric despite being the dominant outcome; customisation from a template is not fed back, so the modifications thousands of teams make to the same template — which collectively describe what the template should have been — are discarded; and the largest customers get bespoke structures from consultants while everyone else gets the gallery.

# Templates Derived From How Teams Actually Work

**Niche:** [[niches/work-collaboration-tools/template-and-process-content/profile|Template & Process Content]]
**Industry:** [[industries/work-collaboration-tools|Work Collaboration Tools]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Templates are written by a content team imagining how a process works, and the platform holds the observed structure of thousands of teams doing that exact process successfully.
**Tags:** #k-means-clustering #graph-theory #bert #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #hypothesis-testing #descriptive-statistics #transfer-learning
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in onboarding content is fighting to give a team a starting structure that matches how that team actually works — and whoever produces a template teams keep past the first month takes the adoption problem.

## The Problem
A marketing team adopts a campaign template with eleven stages, six custom fields and four automation rules. Within a fortnight they have deleted three stages that do not apply, added two the template omitted, ignored four fields, and stopped using the automations because the first one fired at the wrong moment. By week five they are working in a structure they built themselves and the template is a residue. The same sequence happens to thousands of teams with the same template, each making broadly similar modifications, and the platform observes all of it and learns nothing from any of it.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Template content is produced by a content function whose craft is writing rather than analysis, and the templates have always been authored because that is what a content function does. Deriving them requires clustering thousands of workspaces by structure and outcome, which is a cross-customer analysis and raises the same corpus governance question that recurs throughout this vault — structural patterns disclose nothing about any customer's content, and the question has nonetheless not been taken to anyone. And abandonment is invisible, so the current templates appear to be working.

## What to Build
Templates derived from observed structure, specialised to the adopting team. Workspaces are clustered by their structural signature — stages, field types, relationships, cadence, team size, workflow shape — within a process category, which reveals the several genuinely distinct ways teams do the same nominal process rather than the one idealised way a content writer assumed. Outcome is attached where it can be inferred: which structures are still in active use after six months, which teams complete work predictably, which structures accumulate abandoned items. The recommendation is then conditional on the adopting team rather than generic: teams of your size, in your function, working at your cadence, converge on this structure, and here is what they add and remove from it. The modifications teams make after adoption feed straight back, which is the compounding loop and is currently discarded entirely. And the starting structure is deliberately minimal, since the observed pattern is that teams strip templates down — a template that starts with what most teams keep survives, and one that starts with everything a process might need does not.

## Target Customer
Work management platform vendors, their onboarding and customer success functions, and the implementation partners who currently supply bespoke structures to large customers only.

## Impact If Built
Template abandonment is the dominant outcome of the category's principal onboarding mechanism and is a content problem rather than an engagement one. Derived templates specialised to the adopting team's characteristics are the first version that could plausibly survive, and the modification feedback loop means each cohort's templates are better than the last — which nothing in the current arrangement achieves.

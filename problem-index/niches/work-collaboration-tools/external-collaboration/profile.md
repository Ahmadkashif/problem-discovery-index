# External Collaboration

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/work-collaboration-tools|Work Collaboration Tools]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in external collaboration is fighting to let someone outside the organisation participate properly without a licence, an account provisioning process or a loss of control — and whoever makes the outside party a first-class participant takes the work that currently happens over email.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.6B US software spend attributable to external and cross-organisational collaboration
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~8% of collaboration software revenue, against a very large share of actual work
**Digital Adoption:** Low — most cross-organisational work still happens over email and attachments
**Target Buyer:** Agencies, professional firms, and any organisation whose work crosses its own boundary
**Automation Potential:** High — access, identity and document exchange are all solved problems applied poorly here

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
An enormous proportion of professional work crosses organisational boundaries: an agency and its clients, a law firm and its counterparties, a manufacturer and its suppliers, a consultancy and its engagement teams, a company and its auditors. Every collaboration product is built for people inside one organisation with accounts in one directory, and the external participant is handled as an exception — a guest account, a shared link, a licence somebody has to buy, an approval somebody has to grant. The friction is sufficient that the work falls back to email with attachments, which is why version confusion, missing context and lost decisions are still the normal condition of cross-organisational work in a decade when internal collaboration has been transformed. The problem compounds for the smaller party: an agency working with eleven clients maintains eleven client-side accounts in eleven different tools, which is the same many-portals burden that afflicts suppliers in procurement.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Slack Connect, Microsoft Teams external access, and guest access in most work management products provide some capability; file sharing services handle documents; email remains the default. The gaps: identity and access for external participants is provisioned per relationship rather than federated, so the burden scales with the number of relationships; permission models are built around internal membership rather than around a shared workspace with two organisations' rules applying simultaneously; the smaller party bears the cost of the larger party's choices, maintaining a login per client; there is no clean way to end a relationship and retain the record, which is a real need in professional services; and document exchange with external parties remains the largest source of version confusion in professional work.

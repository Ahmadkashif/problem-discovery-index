# Invisible Contribution Work

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/work-collaboration-tools|Work Collaboration Tools]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor that takes this seriously is fighting to make the work that produces no ticket — reviewing, unblocking, mentoring, responding, coordinating — visible in whatever the organisation uses to judge contribution, and whoever does it changes who gets promoted.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$780M US addressable across contribution analytics, performance evidence and team effectiveness tooling
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~4% of collaboration software revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Low — the work is recorded in the tools and counted in none of them
**Target Buyer:** Engineering and operations leadership; the people affected are those whose work is invisible
**Automation Potential:** High — the traces exist in the same systems that produce the visible measures

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Every organisation has people whose contribution does not produce an artefact that any measurement system counts. The engineer who reviews everyone's changes carefully and catches the problems. The person who answers the questions that would otherwise block four colleagues for a day. The one who responds to incidents, who mentors new joiners, who maintains the documentation, who does the coordination that makes a cross-functional programme possible. None of that produces a ticket, a commit or a deliverable with their name on it, and all of it is recorded — in review histories, in message threads, in incident timelines, in document edits. The consequence is that contribution measurement systematically undercounts exactly the work that makes teams function, and it does so with a well-documented demographic skew, since this kind of work is disproportionately performed by people from groups already disadvantaged in promotion processes. That makes this a fairness problem with a measurement cause, which is the kind that is actually fixable.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Performance management systems collect ratings and goals; engineering analytics products count output artefacts; collaboration tools record everything and report activity feeds. The gaps: no product surfaces review, response, mentoring or coordination work as contribution; the artefact-producing work is easy to count and is therefore what gets counted, which is a measurement convenience that has become an organisational value; individuals have no way to assemble evidence of their own non-artefact contribution when they need it, at promotion time, so they reconstruct it from memory; and nobody measures the distribution of this work across a team, which is where the inequity becomes visible and is a single query away.

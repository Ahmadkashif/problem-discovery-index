# Work Status Inference

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/work-collaboration-tools|Work Collaboration Tools]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in work management is fighting to say what is actually happening from the activity the tools already captured rather than from what somebody typed — and whoever makes status observed rather than reported takes the category's founding promise.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$3.8B US attributable to status, reporting and portfolio visibility within work management
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~19% of collaboration software revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Low in substance — reporting modules are universal and are built on self-reported fields
**Target Buyer:** Programme, portfolio and operations leaders; product leads at every platform in the category
**Automation Potential:** Very High — the activity record is complete and is used for an audit trail

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The category exists to make work visible. Its mechanism is a status field that a person sets, which is a self-report about work the system watched them do. The field is stale by construction — nobody updates a task the moment its situation changes — so every report built on it describes a state that was true at various unknown moments in the past. The consequence is the most characteristic ritual in modern knowledge work: a project manager spends the week asking people what is happening and then typing a summary of it into the tool whose purpose was to make that unnecessary. Meanwhile the tools hold a detailed record of what actually occurred — commits, document edits, message threads, review cycles, meeting attendance, file activity — which is behavioural, hard to game, and continuous. The gap between that record and the status field is the category's central unexploited opportunity and the reason its own promise reads as unfulfilled.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Asana, Monday.com, Atlassian, Smartsheet and Notion all ship reporting, dashboards and portfolio views; every one of them aggregates self-reported fields. Automation rules can update status on triggers, which helps at the margins and is configured rarely. AI features have arrived mainly as summarisation of documents and threads rather than as inference about state. The gaps: no product estimates a task's true state from activity; slippage is reported when a date passes rather than predicted from the pattern that precedes it; blocked work is identified only when somebody marks it blocked, which is exactly when they are least likely to; and nobody measures status accuracy — how often the field matched reality — despite it being the property everything else depends on.

# Attention & Notification

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/work-collaboration-tools|Work Collaboration Tools]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor that takes this seriously is fighting to make the total interruption load a measured quantity with an owner — and whoever gives an organisation control over the aggregate takes the attention nobody is currently accountable for.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.2B US addressable across focus, notification management and digital workplace experience
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~6% of collaboration software revenue, against a cost borne by every knowledge worker
**Digital Adoption:** Low — every tool has notification settings and no tool is accountable for the total
**Target Buyer:** Nominally IT and digital workplace functions; the beneficiaries are everyone and the buyer is nobody
**Automation Potential:** High — relevance, batching and routing are all tractable and the aggregate is unmeasured

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A knowledge worker receives interruptions from a dozen systems, each of which defaults to notifying because each vendor's engagement metrics improve when it does. No tool is accountable for the total and no function in the organisation owns it, so the load has grown without limit — and the effect on the ability to do sustained work is well documented and widely experienced. This is the clearest case in the vault of a business model working directly against the customer's interest: the category's own economics reward more notifications, more updates and more time in the tool, while the customer's actual objective is less of all three. Individual defences — do-not-disturb, focus modes, muting — place the entire burden on the individual, require them to guess what they will miss, and are undermined by every colleague's reasonable expectation of a reply. The aggregate is a collective action problem inside an organisation that nobody has been assigned.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Every platform provides granular notification settings, quiet hours and priority controls; operating systems provide focus modes; a small category of digital workplace and focus products exists. The gaps: nobody measures the total, so an organisation has no idea how many interruptions its people receive or what the distribution looks like; relevance is determined by subscription rules — you are notified because you are a member — rather than by whether the message concerns you; batching and deferral are per-tool where they need to be cross-tool; urgency is asserted by the sender with no cost, so everything is urgent; and the organisation has no policy instrument at all, because the settings live with individuals and vendors.

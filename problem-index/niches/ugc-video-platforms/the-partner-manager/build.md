# Seeing What Happened

**Niche:** [[niches/ugc-video-platforms/the-partner-manager/profile|The Partner Manager]]
**Industry:** [[industries/ugc-video-platforms|UGC Video Platforms]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** The only person a creator can reach has exactly the same view of what happened as the creator does.
**Tags:** #worker-facing #data-integration #workflow-orchestration #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #automation #descriptive-statistics #large-language-models
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to let the one person a creator can reach actually see what happened to them — and whoever gives that role visibility turns a sympathetic dead end into a resolution.

## The Problem
A creator's distribution collapses or a video is demonetised. They contact their partner manager, who opens the same dashboard the creator has, sees the same policy category, and can offer sympathy and an escalation. The escalation goes into a queue owned by another team, often returns the original decision, and the manager relays it. The platform's most senior human relationship with its supply base is with someone who cannot find out what the platform did.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Enforcement systems were built for trust and safety, so access is scoped to that organisation and creator support was never a consumer of them — a permission model drawn around a function excludes everyone outside it regardless of need. Giving support staff enforcement visibility raises leak and pressure concerns. The role is measured on relationship rather than resolution. And nobody counted what proportion of contacts the manager can actually resolve.

## What to Build
Give the role the information and some authority. Give partner managers read access to the enforcement and classification record for their creators, which is the core and is the difference between sympathy and an answer. Show distribution changes and their likely cause, since the quiet suppression is the most common complaint and is currently invisible to both parties. Assemble the case before the call, as most contacts are predictable from a recent event. Give the manager a limited authority to resolve or to escalate with priority, because an escalation with no weight is a queue entry. Catalogue recurring issues, since a handful dominate and each is a product problem the manager is currently absorbing individually. Route the creator's question to the team that can answer rather than through the manager, where that is faster. Handle the leak and pressure concern with logging and scoping rather than with exclusion. Measure resolution rate, which nobody tracks and which is the honest description of the role. Feed the contact patterns into product, as this is the richest source of information about what the enforcement systems get wrong. And tell the creator honestly what the manager can and cannot do, since the current ambiguity produces more resentment than a clear limit would.

## Target Customer
Creator support leadership, partner managers, creators, and customer support platform vendors.

## Impact If Built
A permission model drawn around a function excludes everyone outside it regardless of need, so the support role sees what the creator sees. Read access to the enforcement record is the difference between a sympathetic dead end and a resolution.

# Revenue Share Practice From Platform Economies

**Niche:** [[niches/ugc-video-platforms/creator-monetisation/profile|Creator Monetisation]]
**Industry:** [[industries/ugc-video-platforms|UGC Video Platforms]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** App stores and marketplaces learned that unpredictable terms drive suppliers away and built notice periods and published rules, and creator monetisation changes quietly.
**Tags:** #compliance #revenue-impact #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #workflow-orchestration #confidence-intervals #worker-facing #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to make the rules that determine a creator's income legible and predictable — and whoever does it retains the supply that every platform in the category is competing for.

## The Problem
Platform economies with dependent suppliers — app stores, marketplaces, delivery platforms — went through a cycle of unilateral terms, supplier anger, regulatory attention and then structured commitments: published rules, notice periods for changes, explanations for enforcement, and dispute routes. The commitments were not voluntary. Creator monetisation is at the earlier stage, with rules that move without notice and effects that are not explained.

## What Already Exists
Published terms with change notice periods; supplier-facing explanations of ranking and eligibility; dispute and appeal routes; supplier representative engagement; and transparency reporting.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to income determined by advertiser suitability rather than by a fee schedule. It requires: (1) rules driven by a third party's requirements, since advertisers set what is monetisable and the platform is an intermediary — this is the substantive difference and is why the rules feel arbitrary; (2) a per-video classification rather than a per-supplier fee, so explanation must be at item level; (3) rate variation that is a genuine market outcome and cannot be guaranteed, which limits what stability can be offered; (4) creators who are individuals rather than businesses, with no capacity to absorb volatility; and (5) competing platforms actively courting the same supply, which is the commercial reason to move first.

## Target Customer
Monetisation and policy leadership, creators, advertisers, and regulators examining platform terms.

## Impact If Solved
Platform economies were pushed into published rules and notice periods by their suppliers and their regulators. Here the rules are set by advertisers through the platform, which is why they feel arbitrary and why explanation matters more than notice.

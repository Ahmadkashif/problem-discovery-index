# A Throughput Target on the Worst Queue

**Niche:** [[niches/ugc-video-platforms/the-content-moderator/profile|The Content Moderator]]
**Industry:** [[industries/ugc-video-platforms|UGC Video Platforms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The reviewer is measured on decisions per hour on the queue containing the worst material on the internet.
**Tags:** #worker-facing #quick-win #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #compliance #workflow-orchestration #confidence-intervals #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to reduce what a human has to see while keeping the judgement the work requires — and whoever does it addresses an occupational harm documented in litigation, in reporting and in the platforms' own policies.

## The Problem
Throughput is the operating metric. A reviewer handling the hardest categories is measured on how many cases they clear per hour, which sets the pace at which they are exposed to the material and leaves no time between items. The target exists because the queue must be cleared, it is the direct mechanism of the occupational harm, and it also degrades the decision quality the human review exists to provide.

## Why It's Still Broken
Throughput is the contractible metric between platform and vendor, so it is what the contract specifies and therefore what the operation optimises — a commercial relationship defined by volume produces a workplace defined by pace. Quality is harder to measure. Reducing pace costs money directly. And the workers are not party to the contract.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Change what the queue is measured on. Set category-specific limits on the hardest material per reviewer per shift, which is the fix and is the single most effective protective measure available. Measure decision quality rather than volume on those queues, since the reason a human is there is judgement and speed destroys it. Build pace and breaks into the queue rather than leaving them to the individual, because a target makes a break a personal cost. Rotate reviewers away from the hardest categories on a schedule rather than on request. Present the minimum necessary — stills, blurring, summaries — as the default rather than as an option. Measure exposure per reviewer and report it, since it is the basic occupational metric and is often not collected. Put the limits into vendor contracts, as that is the only mechanism that reaches the actual workplace. Give reviewers a route to flag an unsustainable queue without it being a performance matter. Track attrition and sickness by queue, which will show the effect immediately. And publish the standards, because reporting and litigation have driven every improvement in this area so far and a published standard can be held to.

## Who Feels the Pain
Reviewers paced through the worst material on the internet; vendor operators caught between a contract and a workforce; the decision quality the review exists to provide; and platforms carrying a documented and litigated occupational harm.

## Impact If Fixed
A commercial relationship defined by volume produces a workplace defined by pace. Category exposure limits and quality-based measurement on the hardest queues are the protective changes, and they only reach the workplace through the vendor contract.

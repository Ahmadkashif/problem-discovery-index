# Reducing What a Person Has to See

**Niche:** [[niches/ugc-video-platforms/the-content-moderator/profile|The Content Moderator]]
**Industry:** [[industries/ugc-video-platforms|UGC Video Platforms]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** How much distressing material a reviewer sees is a design choice, and the platforms have the best available tool for reducing it.
**Tags:** #cnns #transformers #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #worker-facing #compliance #automation #semantic-segmentation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to reduce what a human has to see while keeping the judgement the work requires — and whoever does it addresses an occupational harm documented in litigation, in reporting and in the platforms' own policies.

## The Problem
Human review exists because classifiers cannot decide everything. But the volume and the nature of what reaches a human is determined by where the thresholds are set, how much is decided automatically, how material is presented, and how the queue is composed — all of which are choices. The reviewers are largely employed through vendors, work to throughput targets, and carry documented occupational harm. The platforms' own content understanding models are the most capable instrument in existence for reducing their exposure, and are pointed elsewhere.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The workforce is at arm's length through outsourcing vendors, so their exposure is a vendor's operating condition rather than the platform's — a harm mediated by a contract is managed as a contract term. Automating more decisions raises accuracy and liability questions. Throughput targets are set to clear a queue. And the affected workers have limited voice in a market where the work is concentrated.

## What to Build
Spare the person and keep the judgement. Raise automated decision coverage where the classifier is confident, since every case decided automatically is a case a person does not see, and this is the largest available reduction. Blur, mute, still-frame and summarise material by default, presenting the minimum required to make the decision, which is standard practice in some settings and inconsistent across the category. Pre-classify by harm type so a reviewer is not moved between categories unpredictably. Compose the queue for wellbeing — limits on the hardest categories, rotation, spacing — rather than purely for throughput. Summarise rather than show where a summary supports the decision, which the platforms' models can do well. Measure exposure per reviewer by category, which is the basic occupational metric and is frequently not collected. Replace throughput targets with quality-weighted measures on the hardest queues, because a speed target on this work is the mechanism of the harm. Extend the platform's own standards to vendor-employed reviewers, since the arm's-length arrangement is where the gap lives. Report exposure and support provision, as the reporting is what has driven every improvement so far. And treat the reduction of human exposure as a product objective with a named owner, because at present it is nobody's.

## Target Customer
Trust and safety operations leadership, moderators and vendor operators, regulators and litigators examining the work, and moderation technology vendors.

## Impact If Built
A harm mediated by a contract is managed as a contract term, which is why exposure is a vendor's operating condition. Raising automated coverage and presenting the minimum necessary are the two largest reductions and both use capability the platform already has.

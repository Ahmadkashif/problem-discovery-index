**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to run an appeal process that can actually overturn decisions at a volume nobody has resourced for — and whoever builds one that reverses the wrong calls converts an unaccountable system into a governed one.

## The Problem
High-volume dispute resolution is a developed field: tribunals, ombudsman schemes and internal review bodies handle large numbers of contested determinations with triage against merit, tiered review depth, published outcome statistics, feedback into the original decision-making, and independence from the original decision-maker. The volumes are smaller than a platform's and the structural problem is identical.

## What Already Exists
Merit-based triage and summary disposal; tiered review with escalating depth; published outcome and reversal statistics; feedback loops into first-instance decision-making; and independence arrangements.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to millions of appeals against automated decisions. It requires: (1) triage performed by a model rather than by a case officer, since merit assessment at this volume cannot be human — this is the substantive adaptation and is what makes the rest affordable; (2) first-instance decisions made by classifiers, so feedback into first instance is model retraining rather than officer guidance, which is a considerable advantage; (3) no statutory independence, so the review's credibility must be built through published outcomes instead; (4) appellants who are commercially dependent on the decision-maker, which sharpens the fairness problem; and (5) speed requirements measured in days, where tribunals take months.

## Target Customer
Trust and safety operations and policy leadership, creators, regulators, and dispute resolution technology vendors.

## Impact If Solved
Tribunals handle contested determinations with triage, tiers and published outcomes, at volumes far below a platform's. Model-based merit triage is what makes the same structure affordable here, and reversals feeding back into the classifier is an advantage tribunals do not have.

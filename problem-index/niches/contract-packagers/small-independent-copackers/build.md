# A Job Ticket That Becomes the Whole Record

**Niche:** [[niches/contract-packagers/small-independent-copackers/profile|Small Independent Contract Packagers]]
**Industry:** [[industries/contract-packagers|Contract Packagers]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A small co-packer already runs every job from a paper ticket; digitising that ticket on a phone could give it inventory, labour, traceability and costing without an ERP.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #cnns #descriptive-statistics #workflow-orchestration #quick-win #automation #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to become the first system a ten-person co-packer runs its jobs on, and whoever gets client-owned inventory, job labour and lot records off the whiteboard in a week, without an ERP project, takes the account.

## The Problem
At a ten-person packager, a job is a paper ticket: client, product, components, quantity, instructions, due date. Receiving counts, hours worked and finished counts are written on it or not at all. When a brand asks for a reconciliation, a lot trace or a cost, the owner rebuilds it from memory and paper.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Software vendors aim at larger plants with budgets; small packagers have no administrator and will not sit through an implementation.

## What to Build
A phone-first job ticket. The owner photographs the client's purchase order or spec and the system drafts the ticket. Crew members clock onto the job by scanning it, receiving and finished counts are scanned or typed, and lot codes are photographed. From those, the system keeps each client's inventory, labour by job, a lot trace and a quote-versus-actual cost without anyone doing data entry beyond the floor's normal motions.

## Target Customer
Owner-operators of co-packers with 3–20 employees.

## Impact If Built
Reconciliations, traces and job costs produced in minutes, and the owner learns which jobs actually make money.

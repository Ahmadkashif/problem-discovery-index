# Small Independent Contract Packagers

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/contract-packagers|Contract Packagers]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to become the first system a ten-person co-packer runs its jobs on, and whoever gets client-owned inventory, job labour and lot records off the whiteboard in a week, without an ERP project, takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.75B in receipts at the 1,231 firms in the code with fewer than 20 employees (Census SUSB 2022)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~16% of receipts and ~76% of firms in 561910
**Digital Adoption:** Low — whiteboards, paper job tickets, spreadsheets and email; accounting software is often the only system
**Target Buyer:** Owner-operator
**Automation Potential:** Medium — record-keeping, counts and quoting automate; the owner is still the estimator, scheduler and salesperson

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Most contract packagers are small. Census counts 791 firms with fewer than five employees and 1,231 with fewer than twenty, out of 1,629. They hand-assemble kits, sticker and relabel, bag and band, and build small displays for regional brands and importers, with crews that swell with temps in peak season. Their brand clients increasingly ask for what the large packagers provide — lot traceability, component reconciliation, certificates, electronic ship notices — and the tools built for the large packagers assume an implementation project, a dedicated administrator and a price the owner cannot justify.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Accounting software, spreadsheets, label printers and their software, and, at the upper end, adapted food or manufacturing ERPs (MRPeasy, Business Central add-ons). Nulogy targets larger multi-line sites. The gaps: no separate record of each client's inventory, labour not tracked by job, and lot records reconstructed when a brand asks.

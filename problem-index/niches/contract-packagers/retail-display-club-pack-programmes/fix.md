# The Pallet Rejected for Its Height

**Niche:** [[niches/contract-packagers/retail-display-club-pack-programmes/profile|Retail Display, Club-Pack & Promotional Programmes]]
**Industry:** [[industries/contract-packagers|Contract Packagers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A display pallet built perfectly to the brand's drawing is refused at the retailer's distribution centre because the drawing broke the retailer's rule.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #object-detection #compliance #quick-win #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to build a seasonal display or club-pack programme to the retailer's specification inside its ship window, and whoever lands every pallet in the window, at the quoted unit price, without a rejected load or an on-time-in-full fine takes the account.

## The Problem
The brand's display drawing, the converter's corrugate and the retailer's receiving rules come from three parties. When they conflict on pallet height, overhang, label position or case count, the co-packer usually finds out when a load is refused or charged back, after the programme has shipped.

## Why It's Still Broken
No one compares the three documents before the build. The co-packer is paid to build to the drawing and assumes someone else checked it.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A pre-build check that reads the drawing, the pallet pattern and the retailer's requirements, lists every conflict, and requires sign-off from the brand before the first pallet is built. At pack-out, a photo check confirms height and label position.

## Who Feels the Pain
Co-packer project managers, brand trade-marketing teams, and the line crews who rebuild refused pallets.

## Impact If Fixed
Refused loads and chargebacks avoided for the cost of a check that takes an hour.

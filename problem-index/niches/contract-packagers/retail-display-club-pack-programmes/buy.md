# Vendor Compliance Libraries Applied to Display Builds

**Niche:** [[niches/contract-packagers/retail-display-club-pack-programmes/profile|Retail Display, Club-Pack & Promotional Programmes]]
**Industry:** [[industries/contract-packagers|Contract Packagers]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Supply chain vendors already maintain retailers' routing and labelling rules for suppliers; the co-packer building the pallet needs them turned into build checks.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #compliance #automation #data-integration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to build a seasonal display or club-pack programme to the retailer's specification inside its ship window, and whoever lands every pallet in the window, at the quoted unit price, without a rejected load or an on-time-in-full fine takes the account.

## The Problem
Retailers publish supplier requirements for case labels, barcodes, pallet patterns and heights, display construction and shipping. Brands pass them to their co-packer, often as a forwarded PDF, and the co-packer's project manager rereads them for every programme.

## What Already Exists
EDI and retail compliance networks that maintain retailer requirements for suppliers' orders, ship notices and labels, and product content services that map item data to each retailer's specification (see the [[niches/ecommerce-sellers/product-content-syndication/profile|product content pocket]] under E-Commerce Sellers).

## The Customization Gap
A co-packer needs the physical rules — pallet height and overhang, display footprint, label placement, case pack, barcode level — extracted per retailer and programme. It needs them checked against the build drawing and the pallet pattern before the quote, and turned into checks on the line at pack-out.

## Target Customer
Project managers and quality leads at display and club-pack co-packers.

## Impact If Solved
Non-compliant builds caught at the drawing stage, not at the retailer's dock.

# Retail Display, Club-Pack & Promotional Programmes

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/contract-packagers|Contract Packagers]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to build a seasonal display or club-pack programme to the retailer's specification inside its ship window, and whoever lands every pallet in the window, at the quoted unit price, without a rejected load or an on-time-in-full fine takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$3B US receipts for display builds, club and variety packs, banded promotions and gift sets (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~25% of contract packaging receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low-Medium — programmes arrive through EDI and retailer portals, but builds run from paper work instructions and whiteboards
**Target Buyer:** Owner or VP of Operations at a consumer co-packer; trade marketing or packaging lead at the brand
**Automation Potential:** Medium — the assembly is hand work; quoting, readiness and compliance checks automate

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
This is the industry's seasonal core: pallet and PDQ displays, club packs for Costco, Sam's Club and BJ's, variety packs and gift sets, built from the brand's product and display corrugate on a retailer's timetable. Programmes are planned four to six months ahead (Valentine's Day in October, Easter in December, per one confectionery co-packer), priced per unit (that firm publishes $0.75–1.25 per club pack and $30–50 per display build), and shipped into a narrow window. Walmart charges ~3% of cost of goods on cases that miss its on-time, in-full rules, and Walmart, Costco and Target require food safety certification before approving a co-packer for food programmes. Competitors are independent co-packers (Assemblies Unlimited, Cubit Packaging, ActionPak and hundreds of regional shops), display manufacturers that also pack out, and 3PLs offering value-added services.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Retailer portals and EDI for orders and routing; Nulogy or spreadsheets on the floor; display structural design from the corrugate supplier. The gaps: each retailer's display and pallet rules re-read for every programme, component readiness chased by phone, and no record joining a programme's quote to its actual build rate.

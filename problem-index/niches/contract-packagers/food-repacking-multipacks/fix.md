# The Changeover Signed Off Without a Label Check

**Niche:** [[niches/contract-packagers/food-repacking-multipacks/profile|Food Repacking & Multipack Assembly]]
**Industry:** [[industries/contract-packagers|Contract Packagers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The line is cleaned and signed off, but last job's roll of film or box of labels is still on a shelf beside it.
**Tags:** #cnns #object-detection #compliance #quick-win #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to run many brands' foods through one set of lines with allergen and label control that a GFSI auditor and a retailer will accept, and whoever passes audits and changeovers with no undeclared-allergen hold takes the account.

## The Problem
Changeover checklists focus on cleaning. The packaging materials from the previous job — printed film, labels, cartons — are sometimes left within reach, and a crew member reloads the wrong one. The result is the right product in the wrong pack, which is an undeclared allergen if the two products differ.

## Why It's Still Broken
The check is a signature on paper, made by a lead under time pressure, with no requirement to verify the first packs against the approved label.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A changeover step that requires a photo of the cleared line and material staging area, and a scan of the first packs' label and barcode against the job's approved version before the line runs.

## Who Feels the Pain
Line leads signing changeovers; food safety managers facing audits; brands facing recalls.

## Impact If Fixed
One avoidable route to a mislabel recall is closed with a check that takes about two minutes (estimate).

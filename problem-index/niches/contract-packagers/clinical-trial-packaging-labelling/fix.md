# The Expiry Extension Relabel Planned on a Spreadsheet

**Niche:** [[niches/contract-packagers/clinical-trial-packaging-labelling/profile|Clinical Trial Packaging & Labelling]]
**Industry:** [[industries/contract-packagers|Contract Packagers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** New stability data extends a product's expiry, and someone has to find every kit in every depot and site and plan its relabelling by hand.
**Tags:** #optimization-fundamentals #descriptive-statistics #compliance #workflow-orchestration #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn a protocol and its amendments into blinded, correctly labelled patient kits at sites before they are needed, and whoever goes from approved label text to released kits in the fewest days takes the account.

## The Problem
Clinical supplies are often packaged with a short initial expiry that is extended as stability data accrues. Each extension means relabelling stock at depots and sites, or letting it expire. Planning which kits to relabel where and when is done from IRT exports and spreadsheets.

## Why It's Still Broken
Inventory sits in the IRT system, labels in the packager's systems and stability decisions with the sponsor, and nobody owns the joined view.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A relabelling planner that reads inventory by location and lot, the new expiry and the enrolment forecast. It proposes which stock to relabel, ship or let expire, and generates the relabel orders and labels.

## Who Feels the Pain
Clinical supply managers at sponsors and packagers; depot staff doing the relabelling; sites waiting for usable stock.

## Impact If Fixed
Less expired drug written off, fewer emergency shipments, and relabelling planned in hours rather than days.

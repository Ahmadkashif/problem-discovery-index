# Line-Side Label Verification, Coding & Inspection

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/contract-packagers|Contract Packagers]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to catch every wrong label, missing lot code and unreadable barcode at full line speed, and whoever reaches zero escapes with the fewest false rejects stopping the line takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$400M annual spend by contract packagers on code verification, vision inspection, label verification and the QA labour they replace (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~4% of contract packaging receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** High on pharmaceutical and fast machine lines, Low on hand-pack and display lines, where checks are by eye and by sampling
**Target Buyer:** Quality manager or plant manager at a co-packer
**Automation Potential:** Very High — the checks are well defined and repeated millions of times

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Every unit a co-packer ships carries someone else's label and someone else's liability. The checks are mechanical: is this the approved label version, is the lot code and expiry present and correct, does the barcode scan at the right packaging level, is the right variant in the right orientation. Pharmaceutical lines must document them under cGMP. Food lines need them to keep allergen labels straight. Retail programmes need them to avoid rejected loads. The vision and verification vendors are mature (see [[niches/food-manufacturing/machine-vision-inspection-vendors/profile|Food Machine Vision Inspection Vendors]]), but their products are built for fixed, high-speed machine lines, not for a hand-pack line that changes job three times a day.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Smart cameras and code verifiers (Cognex, Keyence), checkweighers, artwork comparison (GlobalVision, Esko), and label printing software for in-house labels. The gaps: per-job setup needs an integrator or an engineer, hand-pack lines have no fixed cameras, and results are not tied to the job record or the client's approved artwork.

# Emerging Brands & Short-Run Clients

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/contract-packagers|Contract Packagers]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to make a run of a few thousand units pay for its own setup and changeover, and whoever can quote and schedule short runs at a margin, without a minimum order the brand cannot meet, takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$800M US receipts from start-up and small brands buying packaging, kitting and relabelling in short runs (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~7% of contract packaging receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium on the brand side (online stores, marketplaces), Low on the co-packer side for short-run quoting and scheduling
**Target Buyer:** Founder or operations lead at a small food, beverage, supplement, beauty or household brand; owner of a co-packer that serves them
**Automation Potential:** Medium — quoting, onboarding and scheduling automate; the runs are hand work

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
PMMI's 2024 study found 48% of brand owners outsource for customised products or short or limited runs, and 11% use a contract provider as their entire production operation. Many of those are young brands, often selling through their own sites and marketplaces (see [[industries/d2c-brand-operators|D2C Brand Operators]]), that need a few thousand units, not a programme. Most co-packers set minimums high enough to cover changeover and setup, or quote short runs at prices the brand cannot afford. Some display shops advertise minimums from around 300 units, which is the exception. Brands that also need the product made go to small-run contract manufacturers instead, mapped as [[niches/contract-manufacturing/small-run-consumer-goods/profile|Small-Run Consumer Goods CMs]]. This niche covers brands with finished goods that only need packing. Their real need is a packager that will take small, irregular runs, explain its price, and help with the retailer or marketplace requirements the brand has never met before.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Co-manufacturer and co-packer directories and matching marketplaces; the Contract Packaging Association directory; word of mouth. The gaps: no way for a brand to know which packager's lines suit a short run, no standard quote format, and no way for a packager to price a short run's changeover honestly.

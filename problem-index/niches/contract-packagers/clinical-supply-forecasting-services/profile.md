# Clinical Trial Supply Forecasting & Label Design Services

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/contract-packagers|Contract Packagers]]
**Category:** Insight Layer
**Value-Chain Position:** Specialist advisory & valuation

## What They Do
The clinical supply planning teams inside the large clinical packaging providers — Almac, Thermo Fisher's clinical services, PCI, Sharp, Catalent — and specialist supply-forecasting firms. They model how much drug, in which kit configurations, a trial needs by country and site over time, simulate enrolment and randomisation, design the packaging and labelling campaigns, and plan resupply and expiry relabelling. Sponsors pay as part of a clinical supply contract or as a standalone forecasting service.

## Insight Function
**Size:** ~20–80 clinical supply planners, forecasters and label designers at a large provider (estimate)
**Output:** Supply forecasts and simulations, packaging and labelling campaign plans, resupply strategies, expiry relabelling plans
**Proprietary data:** Enrolment, dispensing and wastage histories across many sponsors' trials — held under each sponsor's contract and largely the sponsors' data
**Clock:** Product expiry dates and stability-based extensions, country label approvals, and sponsors' enrolment milestones; the last are client schedules
**Buyer:** Head of clinical supply services at the provider

**Kill switches:** **data isn't theirs** — trial enrolment, randomisation and dispensing data belong to the sponsors and are used under confidentiality and quality agreements; and **FDA computer-system validation**, since supply and labelling systems used for GMP decisions must be validated, which stalls procurement of new tools.
**Verdict:** Logged — kill switch. Forecasting is a real analytical service with repeatable shape, but it is mostly sold to win the packaging and distribution contract rather than as a product in its own right, its clock is largely the sponsor's, and the cross-trial history that would make it distinctive is the sponsors' data.

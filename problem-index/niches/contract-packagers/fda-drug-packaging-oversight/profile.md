# FDA Drug Packaging & Labelling Oversight

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/contract-packagers|Contract Packagers]]
**Category:** Insight Layer
**Value-Chain Position:** Regulatory & enforcement bodies

## What They Do
The parts of FDA that oversee drug packaging and labelling: the drug centre's quality offices, which run surveillance of registered sites (including contract packagers and relabelers) and choose sites for inspection by risk, and the investigators who inspect them against cGMP rules such as 21 CFR 211.130's line clearance and labelling controls. FDA also runs DSCSA implementation and enforcement. It is funded by appropriations and user fees, not by the packagers.

## Insight Function
**Size:** ~10–40 analysts on site surveillance and risk modelling relevant to packaging sites (estimate); investigators are production and not counted
**Output:** Inspection selection, inspection findings, warning letters, recall classifications, guidance
**Proprietary data:** Establishment registrations, inspection histories, recall and complaint records, and serialisation compliance information
**Clock:** Statutory and programme deadlines set by Congress and FDA itself
**Buyer:** Office director in the drug centre's quality organisation

**Kill switches:** **statutory confidentiality** — establishment and inspection files contain trade secrets and confidential commercial information that federal law bars FDA from disclosing.
**Verdict:** Logged — kill switch. FDA holds the definitive record of which packaging sites fail on labelling and line clearance, but its analysis is enforcement rather than a product, it is a single buyer, and the files are statutorily confidential.

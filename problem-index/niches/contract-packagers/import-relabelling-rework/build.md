# From Customs Notice to Rework Order in an Hour

**Niche:** [[niches/contract-packagers/import-relabelling-rework/profile|Import Relabelling, Re-marking & Rework]]
**Industry:** [[industries/contract-packagers|Contract Packagers]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A notice to mark or a labelling hold takes a day of reading, phone calls and hand-written instructions before anyone can start fixing the goods.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #bert #compliance #workflow-orchestration #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn a held or non-compliant import into marked, correctly labelled, sellable goods before Customs or the retailer's deadline runs out, and whoever clears the most held units back into compliance before liquidation or the ship date takes the account.

## The Problem
The importer forwards a CBP notice, an FDA hold notice or a retailer's compliance rejection to its packager. Someone has to work out what each item needs (origin mark wording and placement, English labelling, corrected barcode, sticker over a claim), design labels, write instructions for the floor, quote, schedule, and arrange supervision or certification where required.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Customs brokers see the notice but not the floor; packagers see the floor but not the rules; the work is episodic and each case looks different.

## What to Build
An intake system that reads the notice and the entry data and applies the marking and labelling rules for the product and destination. It produces a rework specification per SKU, label files, a floor instruction with photos and a quote. It then tracks the deadline per entry and assembles the evidence pack, with before-and-after photos, counts and supervision records, for CBP or the retailer.

## Target Customer
Port-area packagers and 3PL rework departments; customs brokers that refer rework to them.

## Impact If Built
Rework starts the same day, more goods are re-marked before liquidation, and the 10% marking duty and retailer chargebacks are avoided more often.

# The Label Reconciliation Done on a Clipboard

**Industry:** [[industries/contract-packagers|Contract Packagers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Labels are counted out, counted back and written on a form, and a count that does not reconcile stops the lot.

## The Problem
Labelling for each run is issued in counted quantities. At the end, labels applied, labels destroyed and labels returned must add up to labels issued within narrow limits, and any discrepancy is investigated before release (estimate, recalled from 21 CFR 211.125). Counts are made by hand, sometimes by weighing, written on a form and checked by a second person. A miscount means an investigation even when nothing went wrong.

## Why It's Still Broken
Label counters and roll-weight methods exist, but the numbers still travel on paper and are typed into the batch record. Nothing connects the issued count to the line's own applied-label count in real time.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Scan-based issue and return, with applied counts taken from the labeller or vision system. A running reconciliation shows the gap during the run, not after it, so a miscount is caught while the evidence is still on the line.

## Who Feels the Pain
Line operators and QA checkers recounting labels at shift end; QA reviewers chasing unexplained differences; sponsors waiting on release.

## Impact If Fixed
Fewer false investigations, shorter release times, and a reconciliation that is evidence rather than arithmetic done twice by hand.

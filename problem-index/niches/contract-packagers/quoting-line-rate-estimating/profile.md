# Quoting, Line-Rate Estimating & Job Costing

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/contract-packagers|Contract Packagers]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to predict what a new job will actually cost to run before the quote goes out, and whoever quotes closest to the run's actual labour per unit, changeover time and component scrap takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$150M annual spend on estimating labour, quoting and costing tools across contract packagers (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~1.5% of receipts spent; the margin it governs is the whole of the per-unit price (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — spreadsheets and templates, with the estimator's judgement as the main input
**Target Buyer:** Owner, general manager or VP of Operations
**Automation Potential:** High for spec extraction, similar-job retrieval and costing; the final rate call stays with a person

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Contract packaging is priced per unit before the job has ever run, and the industry's demand is tilted towards first runs: PMMI found brand owners outsource mainly for formats they lack, tests and short runs. Quote structures vary — a single all-in unit price, a setup charge plus a run rate, or hourly pricing with a throughput band and a not-to-exceed cap — but all rest on the same estimate of units per labour-hour, crew size, changeover time and scrap. Large contract manufacturers keep cost-engineering departments for this (see [[niches/contract-manufacturing/cm-quoting-cost-engineering/profile|Contract Manufacturer Quoting & Cost Engineering]]); co-packers mostly rely on one or two experienced people.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Spreadsheets and estimating templates; co-pack and ERP software that records actuals, if anyone joins them to quotes; generic configure-price-quote tools built for catalogue products. The gaps: no system holds the quote's assumptions and grades them against the run, and nothing helps a new estimator learn from the firm's history.

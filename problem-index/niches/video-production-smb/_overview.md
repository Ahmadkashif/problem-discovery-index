## Why These Niches

SMB video production spans wildly different markets united only by the equipment used. Wedding videography and corporate training represent the largest revenue segments with the most clients and the most repetitive operational challenges. Real estate media and social media content studios operate in digitally underserved spaces — high-volume, fast-turnaround work that has outgrown artisanal workflows but hasn't attracted purpose-built tools. Legal video and nonprofit documentary serve specialized audiences with unique compliance, ethical, and funding constraints ignored by general production tools. Drone aerial production and multicamera livestreaming are the most equipment-intensive and workflow-heavy segments, where automation can eliminate the most manual operational overhead. Excluded: film/TV production (different scale and union structure) and in-house corporate video teams (different buyer dynamics).

## Why These Pockets

No new qualifiers. The two pockets above 50 are indexed elsewhere — rights licensing under independent publishers, ad verification under marketing agencies — and both are businesses that sit above video production without being of it.

The near miss is the most technically interesting thing here. Production payroll and residuals administration at 48 has encoded hundreds of pages of collective bargaining agreements into calculation logic that determines what talent is paid when material is reused in a different medium, market or territory. Nobody else has mechanised those rules, they change every negotiation cycle, and the record of how genuinely ambiguous reuse questions were settled with the guilds sits in case files rather than in a precedent base. What holds it below threshold is that every rule it encodes is set by bargaining it does not participate in.

What this industry illustrates most sharply is a producer's total blindness to the fate of their own work. The platforms hold second-by-second retention on every video ever published — the direct measurement of whether an edit held an audience — and return a graph to the creator, with no access at all for the production company that made it. Creative performance measurement firms hold thousands of scored executions with response data attached, which is the empirical answer to what makes video work, and publish best practice lists rather than models. And the stock media platforms hold a labelled visual archive joined to what buyers actually searched for and licensed, one of the better creative-intent datasets anywhere, in a business that generative media is displacing underneath them.

Two further pockets hold the only record of something and use it for pricing. Production insurers know which shooting conditions actually produce accidents and overruns. Crew marketplaces hold day rates and repeat-hire behaviour for a freelance workforce that has no other pricing reference at all.

# Financial Close Software Extended to the Actuarial Close

**Niche:** [[niches/life-insurance-carriers/actuarial-valuation-reserving/profile|Actuarial Valuation, Reserving & Quarter Close]]
**Industry:** [[industries/life-insurance-carriers|Life Insurance Carriers]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Finance already runs the close on task, reconciliation and disclosure software; the actuarial close that feeds it runs on email and spreadsheets.
**Tags:** #workflow-orchestration #data-integration #descriptive-statistics #compliance #automation #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to close each quarter's statutory, principle-based and LDTI reserves on the filing calendar with every movement attributed to a driver the auditor accepts, and whoever delivers an attributed close with the fewest overnight reruns takes the account.

## The Problem
The controller's close is tracked task by task with sign-offs and reconciliations. The actuarial steps feeding it, including extracts, assumption loads, model runs, review sign-offs and journal entries, are tracked in spreadsheets, so delays surface only when a number is late.

## What Already Exists
Financial close and reconciliation platforms (BlackLine, FloQast, Workiva for disclosure) with task checklists, reconciliation templates, audit trails and SOX evidence.

## The Customization Gap
The actuarial adaptation needs: tasks tied to model runs and their status; reconciliations between seriatim in-force counts, admin files and valuation results; assumption-table versions recorded as controlled evidence; LDTI disclosure roll-forwards (liability for future policy benefits, market risk benefits) generated from model outputs; and peer-review sign-offs that satisfy actuarial standards of practice and the appointed actuary's opinion.

## Target Customer
Valuation leaders and controllers at mid-size and large life carriers already licensing a close platform.

## Impact If Solved
One visible close calendar across finance and actuarial, with SOX evidence produced as a by-product rather than assembled afterwards.

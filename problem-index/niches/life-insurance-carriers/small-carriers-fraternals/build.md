# A Statutory Calendar Engine for the Five-Person Actuarial Team

**Niche:** [[niches/life-insurance-carriers/small-carriers-fraternals/profile|Small Carriers & Fraternal Benefit Societies]]
**Industry:** [[industries/life-insurance-carriers|Life Insurance Carriers]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A small carrier faces the same reserve, opinion and filing calendar as a giant, and meets it with a consultant per deliverable.
**Tags:** #monte-carlo-methods #large-language-models #workflow-orchestration #compliance #automation #data-integration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep a carrier with a handful of actuaries on the statutory calendar (PBR or its exemption, asset adequacy, filings and opinions), and whoever lets a five-person actuarial team meet every deadline without a consultant on retainer for each one takes the account.

## The Problem
The annual cycle for a small carrier runs through the annual statement, the actuarial opinion and memorandum, the asset adequacy analysis, the VM-20 exemption tests or reserves, the VM-22 transition for annuity writers, illustration actuary certifications, product filings and the state exam when it comes. Each piece is a separate spreadsheet or consultant engagement, re-assembled every year.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The market is many small buyers with small budgets, and vendors have optimised for large carriers. Consultants are paid by the deliverable.

## What to Build
A hosted service that holds the carrier's in-force, assets and assumptions once. It runs the prescribed scenario sets (including the NAIC's GOES files), the exemption tests and the asset adequacy projections on standard product templates, and drafts the required memoranda in regulators' expected structure for the appointed actuary to review and sign. A calendar tracks every filing by state.

## Target Customer
Chief actuaries and CFOs at small stock and mutual carriers and fraternal benefit societies.

## Impact If Built
Statutory compliance at a fraction of today's consulting spend (estimate: a third or less), with documentation that survives staff turnover.

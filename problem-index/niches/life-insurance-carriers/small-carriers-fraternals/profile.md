# Small Carriers & Fraternal Benefit Societies

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/life-insurance-carriers|Life Insurance Carriers]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep a carrier with a handful of actuaries on the statutory calendar (PBR or its exemption, asset adequacy, filings and opinions), and whoever lets a five-person actuarial team meet every deadline without a consultant on retainer for each one takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$600M a year of actuarial software, consulting and outsourced valuation spend by small and mid-size life carriers and fraternals (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~0.6% of US life carriers' operating expense, but most of the industry's legal entities (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low-Medium — one projection model, a few key spreadsheets and the consulting actuary's files
**Target Buyer:** Chief Actuary / CFO at a carrier with under ~$1B of premium (estimate)
**Automation Potential:** High for calculation and documentation; the appointed actuary's opinion stays personal

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Of the 711 US life insurers at end-2024, 65 were fraternal benefit societies and most of the rest are small: regional mutuals, home-service and final-expense writers, and single-state carriers. They face the same statutory machinery as the largest writers: asset adequacy testing and the appointed actuary's opinion, VM-20 or a documented small-company exemption (available below ~$300M of ordinary life premium, estimate), VM-22 for any annuities from 2026, LDTI if they report on GAAP, and product filings in every state. The tools are priced and staffed for carriers with fifty-person actuarial departments.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Projection engines licensed at enterprise prices, consulting actuaries for opinions and PBR, and fraternal associations' shared resources. Gaps: no packaged statutory calendar; exemption tests recomputed by hand each year; key-person dependence on one actuary or one consultant.

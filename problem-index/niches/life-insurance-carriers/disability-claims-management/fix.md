# The ERISA Letter That Gets Rewritten on Appeal

**Niche:** [[niches/life-insurance-carriers/disability-claims-management/profile|Disability Income Claims Management]]
**Industry:** [[industries/life-insurance-carriers|Life Insurance Carriers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Denial and termination letters drafted from templates miss the specific reasons and evidence ERISA requires, so they are reversed or remanded on appeal and in court.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #compliance #workflow-orchestration #evaluation-metrics #worker-facing #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to decide each disability claim on a complete, organised medical file before its ERISA deadline and to return more claimants to work, and whoever shortens average claim duration without raising the appeal-overturn and litigation rate takes the account.

## The Problem
An adverse benefit determination must state the specific reasons, the plan provisions relied on, the information needed to perfect the claim and the appeal rights. Examiners under caseload pressure adapt templates. Gaps in the letter become the claimant attorney's argument, and the file goes back for another review.

## Why It's Still Broken
Letters are written in a word processor from a template library, reviewed (when there is time) by a peer, and never checked systematically against the claim file and the regulation's required content.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Draft each letter from the file: cite the policy provisions, quote the medical and vocational evidence with dates, list what would change the decision. Then check the draft against the regulation's required elements before it goes out, and track which letter defects precede overturns.

## Who Feels the Pain
Examiners rewriting letters; appeals units; litigation counsel; claimants who cannot tell why they were denied.

## Impact If Fixed
Fewer remands and overturns, shorter appeals, and clearer decisions for claimants.

# Mainframe Modernisation Tools Taught Life Product Rules

**Niche:** [[niches/life-insurance-carriers/legacy-inforce-administration/profile|Legacy In-Force Administration & Block Conversions]]
**Industry:** [[industries/life-insurance-carriers|Life Insurance Carriers]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Code-modernisation tools can explain and translate COBOL; they cannot tell whether the translated program credits interest the way the 1987 contract promised.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #seq2seq #data-integration #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to move decades-old policies off legacy administration systems with every cash value, loan and dividend reconciled to the penny, and whoever converts a block with the fewest unreconciled policies at cut-over takes the account.

## The Problem
Carriers trying to retire mainframe admin systems face millions of lines of code that encode product rules, often patched over decades. The developers who wrote it have retired.

## What Already Exists
Mainframe modernisation and code-assistant tools from the cloud providers and IBM, and migration services that analyse, document and convert COBOL to modern languages.

## The Customization Gap
Life administration needs: product-rule extraction organised by plan code, rider and state variation rather than by program; links from each rule to the contract language and the rate file it implements; test cases built from real policy histories, including reinstatements, loans and nonforfeiture options; and actuarial verification that values match guarantees.

## Target Customer
CIOs and in-force operations heads at carriers with mainframe-resident closed blocks.

## Impact If Solved
Modernisation programmes that preserve contractual promises, with a reviewable map from code to contract instead of a black-box translation.

# Skip-Tracing Tools Adapted to Beneficiary Law

**Niche:** [[niches/life-insurance-carriers/beneficiaries-unclaimed-benefits/profile|Beneficiaries & Unclaimed Death Benefits]]
**Industry:** [[industries/life-insurance-carriers|Life Insurance Carriers]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Collections and legal teams already locate people with mature skip-tracing databases; finding a beneficiary also means working out who the beneficiary legally is.
**Tags:** #k-nearest-neighbors #large-language-models #data-integration #compliance #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to find and pay the beneficiary of a policy whose insured has died, before the proceeds escheat to the state, and whoever pays the highest share of confirmed death matches to the right person within the statutory search window takes the account.

## The Problem
Claims analysts use the same people-search tools as collectors, but the tool assumes you know whom you are looking for. A beneficiary search often starts from a relationship, not a name.

## What Already Exists
Skip-tracing and identity databases (LexisNexis Accurint, TransUnion TLOxp and similar) with address histories, relatives and associates, used under permissible-purpose rules.

## The Customization Gap
Beneficiary search needs: designation parsing (named, class, per stirpes, contingent, estate); state rules such as revocation-on-divorce statutes that change who is entitled; minor and incapacitated beneficiaries; deceased-beneficiary chains; outreach logging against each state's unclaimed benefits due-diligence rules; and permissible-purpose controls documented for audits.

## Target Customer
Claims operations at carriers and TPAs administering old blocks.

## Impact If Solved
Higher locate rates per analyst hour, on a tool the team already knows.

# Beneficiaries & Unclaimed Death Benefits

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/life-insurance-carriers|Life Insurance Carriers]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to find and pay the beneficiary of a policy whose insured has died, before the proceeds escheat to the state, and whoever pays the highest share of confirmed death matches to the right person within the statutory search window takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$400M a year of death-matching data, beneficiary search, unclaimed-property compliance and related claims operations spend (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~0.4% of US life carriers' operating expense (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — matching is automated; finding and contacting the person is phone, mail and skip-tracing
**Target Buyer:** VP of Claims / Unclaimed Property Compliance Officer
**Automation Potential:** Medium — matching and search automate; identity confirmation and family situations need people

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The beneficiary is the one party to a life policy who usually never dealt with the carrier, may not know the policy exists, and cannot be served by any channel built for policyholders. After multistate regulatory audits in the 2010s, state unclaimed life insurance benefits laws require carriers to compare in-force policies, annuities and retained asset accounts against death records periodically (commonly ~semi-annually, estimate), then confirm the death and search for beneficiaries before the proceeds escheat. Payments to beneficiaries were $88.5B in 2024 (ACLI). The death records come from vendors logged as [[niches/funeral-homes/death-verification-data-providers/profile|🔍 Death Verification Data Providers]] (54, qualified); this niche is the carrier's work after the match.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Death-match services, skip-tracing databases, the NAIC's Life Insurance Policy Locator for consumer searches, and claims systems. Gaps: free-text beneficiary designations that cannot be resolved; no structured tracking of search effort by state rule; beneficiaries reached by letters they mistake for scams.

# Full Medical Underwriting & APS Review

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/life-insurance-carriers|Life Insurance Carriers]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn an attending physician statement into a defensible risk class in days rather than weeks, and whoever cuts APS-to-decision time without moving class agreement on blind re-underwriting takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$3B a year of APS retrieval, medical underwriting staff, medical director and summarisation spend (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~3% of US life carriers' operating expense (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — records arrive as faxes and scans; reading is manual
**Target Buyer:** Chief Underwriter / Chief Medical Director
**Automation Potential:** High for retrieval and summarisation; low for the final class on complex impairments

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
These are the cases accelerated programmes refer out: older applicants, larger face amounts and disclosed impairments. Here the work is reading. Retrieval from physicians' offices and EHR networks takes weeks. The underwriter then reads a long, unordered file and applies the manual and their own experience. The winners are whoever shortens retrieval and reading without making the class less consistent. That is a different contest from the accelerated path's statistical trade-off, with different vendors (APS retrieval firms, EHR networks, summarisation tools) and a different buyer inside underwriting.

## Current Tools & Gaps
APS retrieval vendors and EHR data networks (Milliman IntelliScript's EHR product, Human API and similar), document indexing, early LLM summarisation pilots inside workbenches, and reinsurers' facultative underwriting for hard cases. Gaps: summaries not mapped to the carrier's manual; no capture of what the senior underwriter adjusted; retrieval status tracked by phone and email.

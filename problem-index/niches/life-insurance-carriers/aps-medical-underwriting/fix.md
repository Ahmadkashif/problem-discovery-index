# The APS That Takes a Month to Arrive

**Niche:** [[niches/life-insurance-carriers/aps-medical-underwriting/profile|Full Medical Underwriting & APS Review]]
**Industry:** [[industries/life-insurance-carriers|Life Insurance Carriers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Case managers chase physicians' offices by fax and phone for records, and the wait, often weeks, is where placed cases are lost.
**Tags:** #data-integration #workflow-orchestration #logistic-regression #quick-win #worker-facing #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn an attending physician statement into a defensible risk class in days rather than weeks, and whoever cuts APS-to-decision time without moving class agreement on blind re-underwriting takes the account.

## The Problem
An APS request goes to a doctor's office or a release-of-information vendor with the applicant's HIPAA authorisation. The office queues it, charges a fee, and sends pages when it gets round to it. Case managers follow up by phone. Meanwhile the applicant cools and the producer places the case elsewhere. Retrieval of two to six weeks is common (estimate).

## Why It's Still Broken
Retrieval is split between carriers, retrieval vendors and thousands of practices with no shared status. EHR networks can return records in minutes for many patients but not all, and carriers have not rebuilt the workflow around trying electronic retrieval first. One carrier's testimonial for medical-claims data reported APS orders cut nearly in half.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Try electronic sources first (medical claims, EHR networks), order a paper APS only where they leave a gap the case needs, and track every outstanding request with a predicted arrival date and an escalation rule.

## Who Feels the Pain
Case managers making chase calls all day; underwriters with half-complete files; producers and applicants waiting.

## Impact If Fixed
Weeks off fully underwritten cycle time and higher placement, at lower retrieval cost.

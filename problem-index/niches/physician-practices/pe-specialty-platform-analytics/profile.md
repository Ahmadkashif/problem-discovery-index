# Private-Equity Specialty Platforms & Physician MSO Analytics

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/physician-practices|Physician Practices]]
**Category:** Insight Layer
**Value-Chain Position:** Aggregator / rollup platforms

## What They Do
Private-equity-backed platforms consolidate single-specialty practices (dermatology, gastroenterology, ophthalmology, orthopaedics, urology, oncology) behind a management services organisation that runs billing, contracting and operations. US Dermatology Partners, Gastro Health and OneOncology are representative (named from recall). 6.5% of physicians described their practice as private-equity owned in 2024 (AMA survey, via search summary), and 22.3% were employed by corporate entities by January 2026 (PAI–Avalere). The corporate development and analytics teams screen acquisitions, model productivity and payer mix, and track integration. The same function at outpatient behavioural and therapy rollups is logged as [[niches/behavioral-health-clinics/behavioral-mso-analytics/profile|🔍 Behavioral Health MSO Analytics]] (38) and [[niches/physical-therapy/pt-rollup-corporate-analytics/profile|🔍 Therapy Practice Rollup Corporate Analytics]] (38). Hospital-employed groups belong to [[niches/general-hospitals/health-system-corporate-analytics/profile|🔍 Health System Corporate Strategy & Analytics]] (39).

## Insight Function
**Size:** ~20-150 analysts, data scientists and corporate development staff per large platform (estimate)
**Output:** Acquisition models, physician productivity and payer-mix analytics, integration tracking, lender and board reporting; none of it sold
**Proprietary data:** Encounter, claims and financial records across hundreds of acquired practices
**Clock:** Deal timelines and lender covenant reporting; state transaction notice laws on some deals
**Buyer:** Chief strategy officer or head of analytics at the platform

**Kill switches:** **HIPAA**. The productivity, payer-mix and referral analytics that make up most of the work run on identified encounter and claims data. The deal-screening half runs on practice financials but would fail Q1 anyway, since it is never invoiced.
**Verdict:** Logged — kill switch. An internal function that spends its insight on the platform's own deals and margins.

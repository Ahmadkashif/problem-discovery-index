# Referral Intake in Specialty Practices

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/physician-practices|Physician Practices]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn each inbound faxed referral into a scheduled, insurance-cleared first visit within a day, and whoever converts the most referrals into kept first visits takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1B a year of referral coordinators, intake staff and intake software at specialty practices (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~0.2% of office revenue (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — most referrals still arrive as fax or e-fax PDFs of 5–40 pages, read and keyed by hand
**Target Buyer:** Practice administrator or director of patient access at a specialty group
**Automation Potential:** High — extraction, insurance checks and scheduling are mechanical once the packet is read; urgency triage needs a clinician's eye

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A cardiology, gastroenterology, neurology or orthopaedic group lives on referrals from primary care. The referral arrives as a fax: a cover sheet, a few visit notes, maybe labs or imaging, sometimes missing the insurance card or the reason. A coordinator reads it, keys the patient into the PM system, checks whether the plan needs a referral or authorization, calls the patient, and calls the sending office for what is missing. A referral that sits for a week goes to a competitor or lapses, and lapsed urgent referrals are a safety risk. Each one is worth a new patient and the procedures that follow.

## Current Tools & Gaps
E-fax services, EHR document queues, referral management modules in athenahealth and ModMed, and newer intake automation (Tennr). Gaps: urgency is judged by whoever opens the fax; missing items are chased by phone; no one measures referral-to-kept-visit time by source.

# Peer-to-Peer Calls Booked Into the Middle of Clinic

**Niche:** [[niches/physician-practices/imaging-procedure-authorization/profile|Imaging & Procedure Authorization]]
**Industry:** [[industries/physician-practices|Physician Practices]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** When a request is pended, the payer offers a peer-to-peer window that collides with the physician's clinic, and the physician goes in without the facts the reviewer will ask for.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #workflow-orchestration #worker-facing #revenue-impact #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get each ordered scan or procedure authorized before its scheduled date without a peer-to-peer, and whoever clears the most orders on first submission, or proves none was needed, takes the account.

## The Problem
A peer-to-peer is a phone call between the ordering physician and the payer's reviewing physician. Windows are short, set by the payer, and often during clinic. The physician steps out between patients, waits on hold, and argues from memory. A missed window becomes a denial and an appeal.

## Why It's Still Broken
The pend arrives by fax or portal message to a coordinator, who must find a slot on the physician's schedule and brief them. Briefs are rarely written. Payers schedule to their own reviewers' availability.

## What a Fix Looks Like
On a pend, a one-page brief appears: the criteria the reviewer will test, the chart evidence for each with citations, and the gap that caused the pend. A scheduler books the call into a protected slot the practice reserves each day. Outcomes are logged by payer, reviewer and service to show which requests need a different first submission.

## Who Feels the Pain
Physicians who lose clinic minutes and patience; coordinators chasing schedules; patients waiting for the scan.

## Impact If Fixed
Better briefs and protected slots could win ~20% more peer-to-peers (estimate). They would also save ~15–30 minutes of physician time per call (estimate), on a task the AMA survey ties to delays in care.

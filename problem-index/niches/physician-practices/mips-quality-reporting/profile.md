# MIPS & Quality Measure Reporting

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/physician-practices|Physician Practices]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to land each clinician's MIPS score above the 75-point threshold from data the practice already holds, and whoever clears the threshold for the most clinicians without adding manual chart abstraction takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$0.8B a year of registry and QCDR fees, quality reporting software and practice quality staff for MIPS (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~0.15% of office revenue, protecting Medicare Part B payments far larger (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium-High — measures are computed from EHR data and submitted electronically, but much of the numerator is still found by hand
**Target Buyer:** Practice quality manager or administrator; MIPS lead at a specialty group
**Automation Potential:** High — measure logic is published, and the gap is extracting what the chart already says

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Clinicians outside qualifying APMs report to MIPS each year. Their score sets a Medicare payment adjustment two years later, up to -9% for scores below a quarter of the threshold (CMS-1832-F). The performance threshold stays at 75 points through the 2028 performance period, and CMS finalised 27 MIPS Value Pathways for 2026 as it moves to retire traditional MIPS by 2029 (AASM summary; the 2029 date is from a search summary). Specialists carry most of the burden: their measures are specific (an ophthalmology outcome, a dermatology biopsy turnaround), and the EHR's certified measures rarely cover them. The work runs on a hard federal calendar: the performance year ends 31 December and data is submitted in the first quarter.

## Current Tools & Gaps
EHR certified measure modules, QCDRs and registries run by specialty societies (IRIS, AQUA, DataDerm, GIQuIC) and vendors (MDinteractive, Healthmonix), and consultants. Gaps: no in-year score forecast by clinician; measure selection made once and never revisited; numerators found by abstractors reading charts in January.

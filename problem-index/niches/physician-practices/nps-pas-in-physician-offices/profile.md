# Nurse Practitioners & Physician Assistants in Physician Offices

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/physician-practices|Physician Practices]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to bill each NP and PA visit under the right rule (incident-to under the physician or the clinician's own NPI), with the state's supervision or collaboration terms met, and whoever captures the most compliant revenue per advanced practice clinician takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$0.6B a year of credentialing, compliance and scheduling-rule tooling and staff time spent on advanced practice clinicians (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~0.1% of office revenue, on ~$25B–$35B of NP and PA visit revenue (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — every visit is in the EHR, but which rule applies is decided at the front desk or by the coder after the fact
**Target Buyer:** Medical group COO or practice administrator
**Automation Potential:** High — the rules are written down; applying them at scheduling is mechanical

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
BLS counts 137,030 nurse practitioners and 80,610 physician assistants in physicians' offices (OEWS, May 2023), against 381,060 physicians. Their visits are paid two ways. Under their own NPI, Medicare pays 85% of the fee schedule. "Incident-to" a physician's established plan of care, with the physician on site, pays 100% (CMS incident-to page). State law adds its own layer: collaboration or supervision agreements, chart co-signature ratios and prescribing limits that differ for NPs and PAs, and for each state. EHRs, schedulers and productivity reports are built around the physician. The NP or PA sees the patients and the rules land on them.

## Current Tools & Gaps
EHR scheduling, credentialing tools (CAQH, Medallion), compensation plans keyed to physician RVUs, coder review. Gaps: no check at scheduling of whether a visit can bill incident-to; collaboration-agreement terms tracked in PDFs; APC productivity credited to the supervising physician's NPI.

# Independent Pediatric Practices

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/physician-practices|Physician Practices]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep a pediatric practice's vaccine stock reconciled across VFC and private inventory, with every dose billed under the right administration codes, and whoever leaves the fewest doses wasted, borrowed or unbilled takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$0.4B a year of vaccine inventory systems, immunisation billing services and staff time at independent pediatric practices, on several billion dollars of vaccine purchases (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~0.1% of office revenue (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low-Medium — doses are recorded in the EHR and the state registry, but stock is counted by hand in the fridge and reconciled on paper logs
**Target Buyer:** Practice owner or administrator of a 2–20 clinician pediatric group
**Automation Potential:** High — every dose has a lot number, an eligibility status and a billing code that can be matched

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Pediatric practices buy vaccines worth a large share of their supply spend (~20%–30%, recalled) and give them under two inventories. Vaccines for Children stock, supplied free by CDC through 61 state and local programmes to 37,000+ enrolled providers (CDC), may be given only to eligible children: Medicaid-eligible, uninsured, underinsured, or American Indian or Alaska Native. Privately bought stock is for everyone else. Each dose must come from the right fridge, be logged against the child's eligibility on that day, be reported to the state registry, and be billed with the right administration codes (90460/90461) and product code. Most practice software is built for adult primary care, and independent pediatricians run this with a part-time vaccine coordinator.

## Current Tools & Gaps
EHR immunisation modules, state immunisation information systems, VFC ordering portals, fridge temperature loggers, and inventory services such as VaxCare. Gaps: no dose-level match across EHR, registry, fridge count and claim; borrowing between inventories logged on paper; payer vaccine reimbursement below acquisition cost goes unnoticed.

# The Placed Executive's First 18 Months

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/executive-search-firms|Executive Search Firms]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep a newly placed executive in role through the first eighteen months, and whoever spots a failing integration early enough for the client to intervene before the departure is announced takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$500M global spend on executive onboarding, integration coaching and post-placement support (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~3% of the ~$20B global industry (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — a check-in call at 30 or 90 days, a coaching contract if someone buys one
**Target Buyer:** Chief people officer of the hiring company; the search firm's practice leader, who carries the guarantee
**Automation Potential:** Medium for monitoring and structured check-ins, low for the coaching itself

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The search firm's relationship with a placed executive peaks at acceptance and fades after the start date. The executive arrives knowing the brief and the interview panel, not the informal power structure, the unwritten history or the CEO's real priorities. The Center for Creative Leadership puts outright failure in the first 18 months at between 38% and more than half of new executives. The placed executive is the person the search process cares most about until day one and least about afterwards: neither the search firm (paid, guarantee usually six to twelve months) nor the hiring company's onboarding (built for staff hires) is designed around them.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Executive coaching (individual coaches, CCL, coaching platforms such as BetterUp and CoachHub), integration programmes sold by the large firms' leadership advisory arms, and the hiring company's onboarding. Gaps: the search firm's assessment — derailers, development needs, the reasons for doubts — rarely reaches whoever supports the executive; no structured early-warning signal; departures seen first in an 8-K or a press release.

# Functional C-Suite Search (CFO, CHRO, CIO, CMO)

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/executive-search-firms|Executive Search Firms]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to put a calibrated slate of sitting functional leaders in front of the CEO within weeks of the brief, and whoever has the function already mapped, with current compensation and move-readiness, takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$4.5B global client fee spend on functional C-suite and top-team searches (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~23% of the ~$20B global industry (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — practice research teams keep function maps, mostly in spreadsheets and CRM lists
**Target Buyer:** Global functional practice leader (CFO, CHRO, technology officer); the client's CEO and chief people officer
**Automation Potential:** High for mapping and benchmarking, low for assessment and closing

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A CFO or CHRO search is bought by the CEO and the chief people officer, not the board, and starts when a vacancy appears rather than on a succession timetable. The pool is finite and well defined — sitting functional leaders and their deputies in comparable companies — so the firm that already knows who they are, what they earn, how long they have been in post and whether they would take a call can put a slate in front of the client in weeks. The large firms organise functional practices for exactly this reason. The contest is preparation and speed on a known pool, where CEO succession is assessment and board trust.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Practice research teams, search CRMs, proxy pay data for named officers (Equilar, ISS-Corporate), executive data from BoardEx and Equilar ExecAtlas, and the firm's past interview notes. Gaps: function maps rebuilt per search; no firm-wide record of who was approached by whom and when; move-readiness judged by feel; compensation for non-proxy officers estimated from memory.

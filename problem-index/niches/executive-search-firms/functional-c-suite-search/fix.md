# The CFO Called Again and Again by One Firm

**Niche:** [[niches/executive-search-firms/functional-c-suite-search/profile|Functional C-Suite Search (CFO, CHRO, CIO, CMO)]]
**Industry:** [[industries/executive-search-firms|Executive Search Firms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A strong sitting CFO is approached by several consultants from the same firm about different searches within weeks, and stops taking the firm's calls.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #data-integration #workflow-orchestration #quick-win #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to put a calibrated slate of sitting functional leaders in front of the CEO within weeks of the brief, and whoever has the function already mapped, with current compensation and move-readiness, takes the account.

## The Problem
The best candidates appear on many longlists at once. In a large firm, different offices and sector teams run CFO searches in parallel, each approaching the same people, and a candidate who has already said "not this year, but call me about a listed company role" hears the same pitch again from someone who did not know.

## Why It's Still Broken
Approach records live in each search's CRM activity, often logged late or not at all, and there is no firm-wide view of a candidate's recent contacts, stated preferences or "do not call before" date.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A candidate-level contact record visible across the firm: every approach, by whom, about what, and what the candidate said, captured from email and call logs automatically; a cooling-off rule the researcher sees before calling; and the candidate's stated preferences routed to the partner who holds the relationship.

## Who Feels the Pain
Senior candidates pestered by one firm; partners whose relationships are spent by colleagues; researchers who walk into a call blind.

## Impact If Fixed
Fewer burned relationships with the scarcest candidates, and approaches that start from what the firm already knows about the person.

# Off-Limits, Conflicts & Candidate-Data Compliance

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/executive-search-firms|Executive Search Firms]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to clear every name on a longlist against every client's off-limits terms and every candidate's privacy obligations before the first approach, and whoever keeps the most names callable without a single client breach takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$200M global spend on conflict-checking, privacy compliance tooling and the staff time spent clearing names in search firms (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~1% of the ~$20B global industry (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium-Low — CRM flags, spreadsheets of client terms, privacy notices sent by template
**Target Buyer:** General counsel or risk and compliance lead at large firms; chief operating officer at mid-sized ones
**Automation Potential:** High — the checks are rules applied to resolved entities and dates

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Two rule sets govern who a search firm may approach and how. On the client side, off-limits agreements: Heidrick's 10-K describes refraining from recruiting a client's employees typically for up to a year from the start of a search, and Korn Ferry's lists off-limits terms as a reason it loses mandates. On the candidate side, data-protection law: under GDPR Article 14, a firm that compiles a European candidate's profile from public sources must give a privacy notice within a reasonable period and at the latest within one month, or at first contact. Both are mechanical once the facts are resolved — which employer, which corporate family, which date — and both are breached by accident rather than intent.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Search CRMs with client flags and privacy triggers (Cluen Encore advertises GDPR compliance triggers), law-firm conflict and relationship tools such as Intapp, general privacy platforms (OneTrust, TrustArc), corporate hierarchy data. Gaps: off-limits terms not stored as rules; employer-to-client-family resolution done by memory; notice deadlines tracked by template rather than per record.

# The Article 14 Notice Sent Late or Never

**Niche:** [[niches/executive-search-firms/off-limits-and-candidate-data-compliance/profile|Off-Limits, Conflicts & Candidate-Data Compliance]]
**Industry:** [[industries/executive-search-firms|Executive Search Firms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Every European executive a researcher adds to a longlist from public sources starts a one-month GDPR notice clock, and firms track it by template and memory.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #compliance #automation #workflow-orchestration #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to clear every name on a longlist against every client's off-limits terms and every candidate's privacy obligations before the first approach, and whoever keeps the most names callable without a single client breach takes the account.

## The Problem
GDPR Article 14 requires a firm that collects personal data not obtained from the person to inform them within a reasonable period and at the latest within one month, or at first contact if earlier. A longlist of ~100 or more European names (estimate), most never approached, creates as many notice obligations, plus retention and erasure duties for each record.

## Why It's Still Broken
Records are created in bulk during research and notices are a separate, manual step. Researchers fear that a notice to an executive who is never called looks like spam and tips off the client's competitors, so notices are delayed or skipped.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Notice at record creation for every in-scope candidate, generated from the firm's privacy policy with the legitimate-interest basis, sent within the window and logged; retention dates set per record; automatic deletion or re-notice prompts at expiry; and a dashboard showing records past their deadline.

## Who Feels the Pain
Data-protection officers at search firms; researchers creating records; executives who discover their profile in a firm's database.

## Impact If Fixed
A compliance exposure that scales with every longlist reduced to a background process.

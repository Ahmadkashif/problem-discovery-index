# A Sponsor-Level Placement Survival Record

**Niche:** [[niches/executive-search-firms/private-equity-portfolio-search/profile|Private Equity Portfolio Leadership Search]]
**Industry:** [[industries/executive-search-firms|Executive Search Firms]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Sponsors choose search panels on relationships and anecdotes, when the firm could show how its placements into that sponsor's portfolio, and portfolios like it, survived to exit.
**Tags:** #survival-analysis #causal-inference #gradient-boosting #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to place portfolio-company CEOs and CFOs who are still in the seat at exit, and whoever can show a sponsor the survival record of its placements across that sponsor's own past portfolios takes the account.

## The Problem
A sponsor's talent partner keeps a mental scorecard of which search firm's CEOs worked. It is shaped by recent memories and by the one spectacular failure. The search firm holds the complete record of its placements into PE-backed companies — who, when, which sponsor, what the plan was — but does not follow them to exit or grade them.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Placement outcomes in private companies are less visible than in listed ones, partners prefer to pitch on relationships, and a firm whose record is average gains nothing by computing it.

## What to Build
A survival and performance record for every PE placement: time in role, departure reason where inferable, whether the executive stayed through exit, and, where the sponsor shares it, plan delivery. Stratified by sponsor, deal type (carve-out, founder transition, buy-and-build), sector and whether the hire was first-time CEO. Survival curves with honest uncertainty for the sponsor's own portfolio and comparable ones, plus the assessment signals that preceded early exits.

## Target Customer
Private-capital practice leaders at global and specialist firms, selling to talent partners at sponsors with many portfolio companies.

## Impact If Built
Panel decisions made on evidence, and a firm that can show which kinds of placements it gets right — the most persuasive argument for a buyer who will place the same roles again next year.

# The CFO Search Started After Close

**Niche:** [[niches/executive-search-firms/private-equity-portfolio-search/profile|Private Equity Portfolio Leadership Search]]
**Industry:** [[industries/executive-search-firms|Executive Search Firms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The sponsor knew in diligence that the target's CFO would not scale to PE reporting, and still starts the search months after close.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #workflow-orchestration #data-integration #quick-win #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to place portfolio-company CEOs and CFOs who are still in the seat at exit, and whoever can show a sponsor the survival record of its placements across that sponsor's own past portfolios takes the account.

## The Problem
A sponsor's lender reporting, monthly operating reviews and add-on acquisitions demand a finance leader the founder-era CFO often is not. Diligence flags it; the search begins only after close and a few missed reporting cycles, and a new CFO arrives well into the hold, when the plan is already behind.

## Why It's Still Broken
Nobody owns the gap between the diligence view and the post-close plan: the deal team moves on, the operating partner inherits a list of issues, and the search firm is engaged only when the vacancy is declared. Pre-close outreach is sensitive while the deal is confidential.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A standing bench for the roles a sponsor replaces most often — portfolio CFOs by sector and size — kept warm by the search firm, with a flag from the diligence assessment that starts confidential mapping at signing and an approach plan ready for the day after close.

## Who Feels the Pain
Operating partners carrying an underpowered finance function; portfolio CEOs doing the CFO's job; lenders waiting for reporting.

## Impact If Fixed
A replacement in place months earlier in the hold, when the value-creation plan most depends on finance capacity.

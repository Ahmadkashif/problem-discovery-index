# The Research Record That Leaves With the Researcher

**Niche:** [[niches/executive-search-firms/boutique-retained-firms/profile|Boutique Retained Search Firms]]
**Industry:** [[industries/executive-search-firms|Executive Search Firms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A boutique's one researcher keeps the market maps in personal spreadsheets, and when they leave, years of mapping go with them.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #data-integration #workflow-orchestration #quick-win #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to give a firm of fewer than twenty people the research bench it cannot afford to hire, and whoever turns the boutique's own past searches into a longlist its partner trusts takes the account.

## The Problem
In a small firm the researcher's spreadsheets, browser bookmarks and notes are the research function. CRM entry is a chore done partially at the end of a search. When the researcher moves to a larger firm — the usual career path — the maps, the record of who responded to which approach, and the levelling judgements walk out of the door.

## Why It's Still Broken
CRM entry is manual and duplicative, and partners do not enforce it because the researcher is busy on live searches. Nothing captures the work as it happens.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Capture by default: research done in the browser and email is logged to the CRM automatically — companies mapped, people found, approaches made, responses received — with a weekly prompt for the researcher to confirm rather than enter. Maps are saved as firm assets attached to sectors, not mandates.

## Who Feels the Pain
Boutique founders rebuilding maps after every departure; incoming researchers starting cold; clients whose next search takes longer than the last.

## Impact If Fixed
The firm keeps what it paid to learn, at the cost of a browser extension and a weekly confirmation.

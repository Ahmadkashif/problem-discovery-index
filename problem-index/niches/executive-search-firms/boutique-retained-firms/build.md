**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to give a firm of fewer than twenty people the research bench it cannot afford to hire, and whoever turns the boutique's own past searches into a longlist its partner trusts takes the account.

## The Problem
A boutique specialising in, say, medical-device commercial leaders has interviewed most of the sector's sales and marketing heads over many years. That history is spread across a CRM, old spreadsheets, Word candidate reports and the founder's memory. When a new search comes in, the founder sketches a target list from memory and starts calling.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Vendors sell boutiques a CRM and a sourcing database, both of which start from the outside world; nobody treats the boutique's own archive as the asset. The archive is messy and boutiques have no data staff.

## What to Build
An engine that ingests a boutique's archive — CRM records, past longlists, candidate reports, notes — resolves people across them, re-levels them to current roles using public signals, and, given a new brief, retrieves the closest past searches, the candidates who fit and are likely movable now, and the firm's prior notes on each. Off-limits checks run against the firm's own client list.

## Target Customer
Founding and managing partners of sector-specialist boutiques with a long search history and one or no researchers.

## Impact If Built
A longlist in hours that reflects years of the firm's own judgement, and a boutique that can match a large firm's preparation on its home ground.

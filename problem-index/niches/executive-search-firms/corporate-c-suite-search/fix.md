## The Problem
Compensation is discussed early in broad terms — base, target bonus, an equity range — and in detail only when an offer is drafted. That is when the candidate's unvested performance shares, options and in-year bonus are added up, the make-whole number lands on the chief people officer's desk, and the search either stalls while the compensation committee reconvenes or collapses.

## Why It's Still Broken
The detailed numbers need the candidate's grant statements, which candidates share late and reluctantly, and the valuation of performance shares is genuinely uncertain. Associates build the vesting table by hand, so it is done once, at the end.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A forfeiture estimate at the shortlist stage: proxy and Form 4 data for named officers, an approximate schedule from the candidate's own description for everyone else, valued with simple payout ranges and flagged against the client's budget before the client meets the finalist. The detailed version follows at offer from the actual statements.

## Who Feels the Pain
Consultants whose searches fall over at the last step; chief people officers explaining an over-budget offer to the committee; candidates whose move dies after they have mentally resigned.

## Impact If Fixed
Fewer late collapses, shorter offer stages, and clients who know before the final interview whether they can afford the person in front of them.

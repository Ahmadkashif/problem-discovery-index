# A Graded Placement Record by Role Type

**Niche:** [[niches/executive-search-firms/corporate-c-suite-search/profile|Corporate C-Suite & CEO Search]]
**Industry:** [[industries/executive-search-firms|Executive Search Firms]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** The large firms pitch C-suite mandates on reputation and named past placements, and none can show a client how its CFO or CHRO placements fared at 18 and 36 months.
**Tags:** #survival-analysis #large-language-models #causal-inference #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #revenue-impact #tacit-knowledge-ml
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is that every serious competitor is fighting to be the firm a company calls for its top roles, but a CEO succession is won on the board's trust in an assessment of insiders against the outside market, while a functional C-suite search is won on having the function already mapped when the brief arrives. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## The Problem
A pitch for a C-suite mandate lists the firm's recent placements in the sector and the partner's track record. What the client cannot see, and the firm does not compute, is how those placements fared: how many of the firm's CFO placements in industrials were still in role at two years, whether its placements into PE-owned companies failed faster than into listed ones, or whether the partner's "strong recommendation" predicted anything. The Center for Creative Leadership puts outright failure in the first 18 months at between 38% and more than half of new executives; a firm that could show a materially better record would have the strongest argument in the market.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The fee is earned at acceptance and the guarantee ends inside a year, so nothing in the business model asks for longer follow-up. Partners resist a scorecard of their own judgement, and a firm whose numbers turn out to be ordinary has no reason to publish them. Outcome data is scattered across filings, announcements and biographies.

## What to Build
A survival record built from the firm's own placements: each placement tagged by function, sector, ownership, internal-versus-external slate and consultant, followed through Form 8-K Item 5.02 filings and announcements, with departures caused by takeovers or restructurings censored. Survival curves with honest confidence intervals for each practice, and a comparison against the public base rate for the same roles.

## Target Customer
Global practice leaders and the chief knowledge officer at the large firms; managing partners at mid-sized firms competing against them.

## Impact If Built
A defensible track record in every C-suite pitch, and an internal view of which practices, partners and assessment habits produce placements that last.

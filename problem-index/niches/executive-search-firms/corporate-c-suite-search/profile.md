# Corporate C-Suite & CEO Search

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/executive-search-firms|Executive Search Firms]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is that every serious competitor is fighting to be the firm a company calls for its top roles, but a CEO succession is won on the board's trust in an assessment of insiders against the outside market, while a functional C-suite search is won on having the function already mapped when the brief arrives. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$6B global client fee spend on CEO and C-suite mandates at corporate clients (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~30% of the ~$20B global industry (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — mandates run in search CRMs, assessment and references in notes
**Target Buyer:** Global practice leader for CEO & Board or for a functional practice (CFO, CHRO, CIO)
**Automation Potential:** Medium for research and reporting, low for assessment and closing

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
This is the core of the industry's revenue: chief executives, chief financial officers, chief people officers, chief technology and information officers and the other top-team roles at listed and large private companies, paid at about one-third of first-year compensation. The large global firms — Korn Ferry ($846.2M of search fee revenue in fiscal 2025), Heidrick & Struggles, Spencer Stuart, Russell Reynolds and Egon Zehnder — earn most of it, and organise by function and sector practice. The work differs from mid-level search in confidentiality, in the weight of assessment, and in who buys: the board for the CEO, the CEO and the chief people officer for the rest.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Search CRMs, sector research teams, proprietary assessment frameworks and psychometric instruments (Korn Ferry reports over 108 million assessments conducted across its businesses), and board and executive data from BoardEx and Equilar. The gaps: no graded record of past C-suite placements; compensation and make-whole questions surfacing at the offer stage; interview evidence captured inconsistently across partners.

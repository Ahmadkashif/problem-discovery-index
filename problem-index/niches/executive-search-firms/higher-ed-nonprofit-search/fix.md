# The Failed Search That Restarts From Zero

**Niche:** [[niches/executive-search-firms/higher-ed-nonprofit-search/profile|Higher Education & Nonprofit Leadership Search]]
**Industry:** [[industries/executive-search-firms|Executive Search Firms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** When a presidential search ends without an appointment, the relaunch discards the assessments of every candidate the committee already met.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #data-integration #workflow-orchestration #compliance #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to bring a committee-run search to a finalist the governing board confirms in a single cycle, and whoever's searches least often fail and reopen takes the account.

## The Problem
Searches fail when finalists withdraw, the board rejects the committee's choice, or a leak drives candidates away. The relaunched search usually has a reconstituted committee, a revised profile and sometimes a new firm. The earlier candidates' interview notes, references and committee scores sit in the previous firm's files or in members' inboxes, and the new committee starts again.

## Why It's Still Broken
The record of the first search is fragmented across the firm, the committee and the institution, and confidentiality promises to candidates make reuse awkward. A firm that ran a failed search is often replaced.

## What a Fix Looks Like
An institution-owned search record — profile, sourcing universe, candidate assessments with consent for reconsideration, committee scores and the reasons for failure — kept under confidentiality and handed to the relaunch, so the new committee can recontact strong earlier candidates and learn why the first attempt broke.

## Who Feels the Pain
Boards facing a long stretch under an interim leader; committee members repeating months of work; candidates asked the same questions twice.

## Impact If Fixed
Relaunched searches that start from what was learned, finishing faster and with fewer repeat failures.

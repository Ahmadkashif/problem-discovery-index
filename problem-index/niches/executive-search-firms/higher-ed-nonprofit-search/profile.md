# Higher Education & Nonprofit Leadership Search

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/executive-search-firms|Executive Search Firms]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to bring a committee-run search to a finalist the governing board confirms in a single cycle, and whoever's searches least often fail and reopen takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$900M global client fee spend on presidential, provost, dean, hospital and nonprofit chief-executive searches (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~5% of the ~$20B global industry (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — committee portals, emailed dossiers, paper scoring sheets and in-person airport interviews
**Target Buyer:** Search committee chair and board chair; the firm's education or nonprofit practice leader
**Automation Potential:** Medium for committee logistics and dossier preparation, low for consensus-building

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A university presidency or a large nonprofit's chief-executive role is filled by a committee of trustees, faculty, staff, students and sometimes alumni, under a governing board that votes at the end and, for public institutions, under state open-records law. Florida made presidential applicants confidential until the finalist stage only in 2022, with finalists public at least 21 days before interviews or an offer, and its Board of Governors now requires committee members to sign NDAs. Specialist practices (and the education practices of the large firms) run the process as much as the search: managing the committee, the listening sessions and the confidentiality. A failed search that reopens is the visible failure.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Practice CRMs, document-sharing portals for committees, faculty-hiring applicant systems such as Interfolio repurposed for leadership searches, and the consultant's facilitation. Gaps: committee scoring on paper or spreadsheets; disagreements surfacing only at the final vote; prior candidate pools discarded when a search reopens.

# The Candidate the Committee Already Knew

**Niche:** [[niches/executive-search-firms/board-director-search/profile|Board Director Search]]
**Industry:** [[industries/executive-search-firms|Executive Search Firms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A board pays a search firm to widen its field and receives a slate drawn from the same circle of directors its members already sit with.
**Tags:** #graph-theory #k-nearest-neighbors #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to fill the gap on a board's skills matrix with a director the nominating committee can put in the next proxy, and whoever brings candidates the committee did not already know, clear of interlocks and independence problems, takes the account.

## The Problem
Director slates gravitate to sitting directors and recently retired CEOs because they are known, vetted and easy to defend. The committee often recognises most names. Spencer Stuart found first-time public-company directors fell to 24% of new S&P 500 directors in 2026, from 31% a year earlier.

## Why It's Still Broken
Partners source from their own networks, and nothing measures how far a slate reaches beyond the board's existing connections, so a familiar slate looks like a safe one.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Score every slate's network distance from the current board using director-connection data: how many candidates are connected through existing directors, how many are first-time directors, how many come from outside the usual sectors. Show the committee the reach alongside the skills fit, and let the partner add candidates the graph says nobody on the board knows.

## Who Feels the Pain
Nominating committees seeking genuinely new perspectives; first-time candidates who never surface; board practices competing on the same names.

## Impact If Fixed
A visible, measurable difference between a slate the board could have assembled itself and one it paid for.

# The Emergency Succession Plan Nobody Has Opened

**Niche:** [[niches/executive-search-firms/ceo-succession-benchmarking/profile|CEO Succession & Insider-Outsider Benchmarking]]
**Industry:** [[industries/executive-search-firms|Executive Search Firms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The emergency succession plan in the board minutes names an interim leader who has since left the company.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #compliance #workflow-orchestration #quick-win #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to give a board a defensible comparison of its internal CEO candidates against the external market before the incumbent's departure is announced, and whoever has already assessed the internal bench when the CEO resigns takes the account.

## The Problem
Boards approve an emergency succession plan — who steps in if the CEO is suddenly unable to serve — and revisit it rarely. A Conference Board figure cited in trade press puts the share of organisations with a formal CEO succession plan at only 21%. When a sudden departure comes, the named interim may have moved roles or left, and the board improvises in public.

## Why It's Still Broken
The plan is a document approved once, owned by nobody between reviews, and its staleness is invisible until it is needed.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Treat the plan as a monitored record: each named interim and successor linked to their current role, with alerts when they change jobs internally, leave, or are named in an 8-K; a scheduled review prompt to the governance committee chair; and a one-page current version ready for the day it is used. The adviser who maintains it is the one the board calls.

## Who Feels the Pain
Board chairs facing a sudden vacancy; general counsel managing disclosure; the search firm hired in a crisis with no current plan to start from.

## Impact If Fixed
A plan that is current on the day it is needed, at almost no cost, and a standing reason for the board to talk to its adviser between successions.

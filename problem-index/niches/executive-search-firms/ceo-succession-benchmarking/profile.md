# CEO Succession & Insider-Outsider Benchmarking

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/executive-search-firms|Executive Search Firms]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to give a board a defensible comparison of its internal CEO candidates against the external market before the incumbent's departure is announced, and whoever has already assessed the internal bench when the CEO resigns takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.5B global spend on CEO searches, succession assessments and benchmarking for boards (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~7% of the ~$20B global industry (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — board-confidential work held in partners' files
**Target Buyer:** Board chair and nominating/governance committee chair; global head of a firm's CEO & Board practice
**Automation Potential:** Low for the assessment, medium for benchmarking and monitoring

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Most CEO successions are now insiders: Spencer Stuart's 2026 Board Index found 69% of new S&P 500 CEOs were promoted internally, and Russell Reynolds counted 234 CEO exits across its tracked indices in 2025, a record. The search firm's job in a CEO succession is therefore less to find an outsider than to tell the board, credibly, how its insiders compare with the market — and the firm already engaged to assess the internal bench years earlier is usually the one hired when the CEO goes. The buyer is the board, the clock is the incumbent's tenure, and the evidence is assessment, which is a different contest from a functional search.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Succession assessment programmes from the large firms (Korn Ferry's Assessment & Succession and Board & CEO Services, Heidrick Consulting, Russell Reynolds' and Spencer Stuart's leadership advisory), psychometric instruments, and external benchmark slates assembled by research teams. Gaps: benchmarks refreshed only when a succession starts; emergency plans that go stale; no record of how the firm's past insider-versus-outsider recommendations performed.

# The Adjustments That Live in One Head

**Niche:** [[niches/app-marketing-firms/the-ua-manager/profile|The UA Manager]]
**Industry:** [[industries/app-marketing-firms|App Marketing Firms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The manager discounts one network by a factor they worked out two years ago, treats another's numbers as reliable, and none of it is written down anywhere.
**Tags:** #tacit-knowledge-ml #worker-facing #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #quick-win #workflow-orchestration #confidence-intervals #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to give the manager a defensible allocation instead of a weekly judgement across four disagreeing sources — and whoever does that changes how a large share of mobile advertising spend is decided.

## The Problem
Over three years the manager has learned that one network's reported returns run high by a consistent factor, that another's numbers hold up, that a third's installs churn faster than their value model suggests, that a particular creative format works in one geography and not another, and that the aggregated signal for the most recent week should be discounted in a specific way. All of this is correct, all of it was expensively learned, and none of it exists outside their head. When they leave, their replacement rediscovers it over three years, making the same expensive mistakes, and the business notices only that performance dipped for a while.

## Why It's Still Broken
The adjustments were learned informally and applied mentally, so there was never a moment where writing them down was the natural next step — knowledge acquired by experience has no artefact unless someone builds one. Documenting them makes an individual more replaceable, which is a real personal disincentive. There is no field for a discount factor in any tool. And nobody asks until the person has resigned.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Turn the adjustments into parameters. Capture each adjustment as an explicit named factor with its rationale and its evidence, which is the fix, takes an afternoon, and converts personal knowledge into an organisational asset. Apply them in the tooling rather than mentally, so the reconciled view already reflects them and the reasoning is visible to others. Validate each against realised outcomes, since some will be right, some will be stale and some will be superstition, and only the comparison distinguishes them. Update them automatically where the data supports it, which is better than a static factor learned two years ago. Attribute the source of each factor, so a successor knows what it rests on. Share across the team and across accounts in an agency, where the same adjustments apply and are currently rediscovered per person. Make the knowledge useful to the person capturing it, because a system that only helps their successor will not be filled in. Include the negative knowledge — the approaches that did not work — which is as valuable and is lost even more completely. Run a structured handover when someone leaves, drawing on the captured factors rather than on a fortnight of meetings. And measure how long a new manager takes to reach competence, since that interval is what this fix shortens and it is currently measured in years.

## Who Feels the Pain
Businesses losing years of expensive learning when one person leaves; new managers repeating costly mistakes; and managers whose real expertise is invisible and unrewarded.

## Impact If Fixed
Knowledge acquired by experience has no artefact unless someone builds one, and nobody asks until the resignation. Capturing each adjustment as a named factor with its evidence takes an afternoon and lets the factors be validated, updated and inherited.

# Claims Adjudication Practice

**Niche:** [[niches/app-marketing-firms/attribution-reconciliation/profile|Attribution Reconciliation]]
**Industry:** [[industries/app-marketing-firms|App Marketing Firms]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Insurance built subrogation and contribution rules for when several parties claim the same loss, and app attribution lets every network claim the same install.
**Tags:** #compliance #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #workflow-orchestration #confidence-intervals #hypothesis-testing #graph-theory #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to produce one allocation from four sources that each report a different number — and whoever does that credibly replaces a weekly judgement call on a seven-figure budget.

## The Problem
Several parties claiming the same event is an old commercial problem with settled machinery. Insurance developed contribution and subrogation rules, defined priority between policies, and built adjudication processes so that a loss covered by two policies is apportioned rather than paid twice. Financial clearing did the equivalent for trades. App attribution has multiple networks claiming the same install by design, with each network's own rules governing its claim and no adjudication layer at all.

## What Already Exists
Contribution and apportionment rules between overlapping claims; priority hierarchies between claimants; adjudication processes with defined evidence; clearing and netting between counterparties; and dispute escalation frameworks.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to claimants who control their own evidence and have no obligation to cooperate. It requires: (1) claims made under each claimant's own rules with no common standard, since insurance apportionment assumes policies written to a market convention and here every network defines its own window and view-through policy — establishing a common basis is the whole problem; (2) no adjudicating institution, so the buyer must construct the adjudication and the networks may simply disagree with it; (3) evidence that is partly unavailable on one platform by design, where aggregation removes the identifiers adjudication would use; (4) volumes of millions of events, ruling out per-claim handling; and (5) a commercial relationship the buyer wants to preserve, which limits how adversarially the adjudication can be applied.

## Target Customer
User acquisition teams and agencies, measurement vendors, and the advertiser industry bodies who could establish a convention.

## Impact If Solved
Insurance apportions when two policies cover one loss, and attribution lets every network claim the same install under its own rules. The absence of a common standard and of any adjudicating institution is what the buyer must construct for themselves.

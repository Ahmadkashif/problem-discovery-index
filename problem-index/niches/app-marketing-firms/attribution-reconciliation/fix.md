# The Install Three Networks Claimed

**Niche:** [[niches/app-marketing-firms/attribution-reconciliation/profile|Attribution Reconciliation]]
**Industry:** [[industries/app-marketing-firms|App Marketing Firms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The network dashboards sum to forty percent more installs than the app store recorded, everyone knows why, and the budget is still allocated from those dashboards.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #compliance #quick-win #hypothesis-testing #revenue-impact #data-integration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to produce one allocation from four sources that each report a different number — and whoever does that credibly replaces a weekly judgement call on a seven-figure budget.

## The Problem
Add up what every network says it delivered and the total exceeds what the app store says was installed, frequently by a wide margin. Each network is reporting honestly by its own rules — it showed an advertisement within its claim window and therefore claims the install. Several of them did. The team knows the totals cannot be added and does it anyway when building the weekly view, then applies a discount from experience. Budget moves between networks on the basis of numbers that are individually defensible and collectively impossible, and the discount is a feeling.

## Why It's Still Broken
The impossible sum is visible on the first day of any account and is normalised immediately, so it never becomes a question — the absurdity is so familiar it has stopped registering. Deduplication requires the measurement partner's record and a stated rule, which is work nobody has been assigned. Networks benefit from the ambiguity and will not volunteer a fix. And the mental discount works well enough to prevent the fix.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Put the store's number next to the sum. Report the network total against the app store's install count in every weekly view, which is the fix and makes the impossibility unignorable rather than familiar — most teams have never seen the two figures presented together deliberately. Deduplicate against the measurement partner's attribution with a stated rule, so the adjustment is a method rather than an instinct. Quantify each network's over-claim rate, which is directly computable once deduplication exists and is the most useful negotiating fact a buyer can have. Normalise claim windows before any comparison, since a network with a longer window claims more by construction and a raw comparison rewards the most permissive rules. Separate view-through from click-through claims, because they are different assertions and merging them is how the inflation compounds. Present the reconciled number as the basis for allocation and the network numbers as diagnostics. Raise over-claim rates with the networks, which is a commercial conversation that only becomes possible once the number exists. Track the gap over time, since a widening gap indicates a rule change somewhere. Adopt a consistent method across all accounts so comparisons hold. And report the share of spend allocated on unreconciled numbers, because that figure names the actual exposure.

## Who Feels the Pain
Managers allocating on figures that cannot all be true; networks with conservative claiming rules losing to permissive ones; and finance receiving performance numbers that do not reconcile to anything.

## Impact If Fixed
The impossible sum is so familiar it has stopped registering, and the discount applied to it is a feeling. Putting the store's own install count beside the network total makes it unignorable, and deduplication turns the instinct into a per-network over-claim rate you can negotiate with.

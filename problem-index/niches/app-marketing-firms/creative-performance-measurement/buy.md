## The Problem
Learning about many factors from a limited number of expensive runs is exactly what design of experiments addresses. Factorial and fractional factorial designs, screening designs to identify the factors that matter, blocking to control nuisance variation and response surface methods to optimise are all established, and industrial and agricultural research have used them for a century precisely because each run is costly. Creative testing has many factors, expensive runs and no design — variants are produced and shipped, and whatever comes back is read.

## What Already Exists
Factorial and fractional factorial designs; screening designs for many factors; blocking and randomisation to control nuisance variation; response surface methodology; and power analysis for designed experiments.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to factors that are creative choices and a response measured through an aggregation layer. It requires: (1) factors that are qualitative creative decisions — hook, mechanic, framing, pacing — which must be defined as a factor space before any design is possible, and defining that space is the substantive work; (2) a response measured at cohort level through a delayed and coarsened signal, so the design must account for the measurement's own limitations; (3) allocation controlled by the network's optimiser rather than by the experimenter, which breaks randomisation unless the buy is structured to enforce it — this is the practical obstacle and it is a media buying problem rather than a statistical one; (4) factor levels that are produced by humans, so a full factorial is a production commitment as well as a media one; and (5) interactions that matter, since creative factors combine non-additively and a screening design that assumes additivity will mislead.

## Target Customer
User acquisition and creative teams, agencies running creative at volume, and experimental design practitioners for whom creative testing is unserved.

## Impact If Solved
A century of design of experiments exists because runs are expensive, and creative testing ships forty variants with no design. Defining the creative factor space is the substantive work, and enforcing randomisation against the network's own optimiser is a media buying problem rather than a statistical one.

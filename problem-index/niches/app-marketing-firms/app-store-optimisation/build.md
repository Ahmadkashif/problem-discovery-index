# The Page Every Channel Passes Through

**Niche:** [[niches/app-marketing-firms/app-store-optimisation/profile|App Store Optimisation]]
**Industry:** [[industries/app-marketing-firms|App Marketing Firms]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** The store listing converts every paid click and every organic browse, is optimised with keyword tools built on estimated volumes, and is tested with store experiments most teams run once a quarter.
**Tags:** #hypothesis-testing #confidence-intervals #evaluation-metrics #large-language-models #diffusion-models #revenue-impact #descriptive-statistics #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to improve the one page that converts every paid click and every organic browse — and whoever tests it properly gets a multiplier on all acquisition spend rather than an improvement to one channel.

## The Problem
Every user the team acquires passes through the store listing. A ten percent improvement in its conversion rate is worth the same as a ten percent reduction in cost per click across every channel simultaneously, and it compounds with organic discovery on top. The team runs a store experiment occasionally, using screenshots produced for something else, informed by keyword tools reporting estimated volumes with no stated error. Meanwhile they spend most of their week on campaign-level optimisation with far smaller leverage and far worse measurement.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Store optimisation sits between marketing and product and is owned by neither, so it receives the attention of a shared responsibility — the organisational gap is the main cause rather than any technical difficulty. The store's experimentation tools are limited and slow, which makes a testing programme feel impractical. Keyword tools sell estimates that look like data. And campaign optimisation produces visible daily activity while listing work does not.

## What to Build
Run it as a continuous testing programme. Test systematically rather than occasionally, with a queue of hypotheses, proper power calculation and a defined cadence, which is the core — the store's own experiments give clean causal answers, which is rare in this discipline and is being wasted on quarterly one-offs. Produce assets designed for the listing rather than reusing campaign creative, since the two surfaces do different jobs and the reuse is a false economy. Treat keyword volume estimates as estimates with error, connecting to the same problem in search tooling, rather than as the basis for a content decision. Measure the interaction between traffic source and listing conversion, because a listing optimised for organic browsers may convert paid traffic worse and this is measurable and almost never examined. Localise properly rather than translating, since store conversion varies enormously by market and a translated listing is leaving the largest available gains untouched. Generate variants at volume, which makes the testing queue sustainable and is now cheap. Monitor competitors' listing changes, since the comparison set on a search results page determines relative conversion. Connect listing conversion to downstream value, because a listing that attracts poorly-matched users raises conversion and lowers cohort quality — a trap nobody checks. Quantify the multiplier effect explicitly in business terms, which is the argument that gets the resource allocated. And report listing conversion as a headline acquisition metric alongside cost per install, since it multiplies everything and is currently reported nowhere near it.

## Target Customer
Growth and product leadership, app marketing agencies, and the store optimisation vendors selling keyword tools rather than testing programmes.

## Impact If Built
The listing multiplies every channel simultaneously and is owned by neither marketing nor product, so it gets shared-responsibility attention. The store's own experiments give clean causal answers that are rare in this discipline and are being spent on quarterly one-offs.

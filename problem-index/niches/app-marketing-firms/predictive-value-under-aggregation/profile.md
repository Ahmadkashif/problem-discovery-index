**Target Buyer:** User acquisition and data leadership
**Automation Potential:** Very High — both halves are quantitative

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Acquisition economics require predicting six-month user value from the first few days, and on one platform those days arrive as a few bits, with a random delay, nulled out entirely when the campaign is small — which is precisely where a new campaign starts. Every bidding decision, budget allocation and channel judgement in this discipline rests on that prediction, which makes it the largest concentration of contested value in the category.

It is also **not terminal**. Two different problems live inside it. The conversion value schema is an information design decision: a handful of bits must encode whatever the team most needs to know about an early user, and that choice determines the ceiling on what any downstream model can learn. Predicting value from the resulting signal is a modelling problem under delay, coarsening and suppression. One is decided once and constrains everything after it; the other is refit continuously within the constraint. Different skills, different owners, different winners. The two contests are stated in the sub-niches.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Conversion value schemas chosen at launch, predictive lifetime value models fitted to early signals, network-side optimisation, and aggregated reporting. The gaps: schemas never revisited; no measurement of the error a schema imposes; suppression concentrated exactly where new campaigns operate; delay unmodelled; and predictions optimised against with no validation of their accuracy.

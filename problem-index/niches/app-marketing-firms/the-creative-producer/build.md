# Told Which One Spent, Never Which One Worked

**Niche:** [[niches/app-marketing-firms/the-creative-producer/profile|The Creative Producer]]
**Industry:** [[industries/app-marketing-firms|App Marketing Firms]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A motion designer produces dozens of ad variants a week, sends them into a system that tells them which one spent, and never learns which one worked or why.
**Tags:** #worker-facing #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #transformers #descriptive-statistics #revenue-impact #hypothesis-testing #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to tell the person making the work which of it succeeded and why — and whoever does that turns a production treadmill into a craft that improves.

## The Problem
The designer makes forty variants a week. They go into the networks. A report comes back showing which ones received spend, which reflects the network's internal allocation rather than the advertiser's outcome, and under aggregated attribution the two are not the same thing. The designer sees spend numbers, infers preferences from them, and makes more of what spent. They have no idea whether those users were valuable, whether the winning variant won on merit or on an allocation artefact, or what about it worked. After two years of this they are faster and no better, because nothing that came back could have taught them anything.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The feedback path ends at the media team, who consume performance data and do not route it back — the designer is outside the measurement loop by organisational construction rather than by any technical barrier. Under aggregated attribution the creative-level outcome is genuinely hard to establish, which gives a real reason not to try. Assets are identified by file name rather than by concept. And creative is treated as production rather than as a variable to be learned about.

## What to Build
Close the loop to the person making the work. Route outcome data back to the designer at the concept level, which is the fix and is mostly an organisational and plumbing change rather than a measurement breakthrough. Distinguish spend from performance explicitly in everything the designer sees, since conflating them is what has taught them the wrong lessons and is the fix note's subject. Describe what worked in terms of creative attributes — the hook, the pacing, the mechanic, the framing — rather than by asset identifier, because a designer cannot act on a file name. Report value rather than installs, so a variant that acquires cheap low-value users is not read as a success. Provide the comparison against their own baseline, since a designer needs to know whether this is better than what they usually make. Show cross-account patterns where an agency has them, which is knowledge no individual designer can accumulate. Deliver it fast enough to inform the next batch, because feedback arriving a month later is history rather than learning. Involve the designer in the hypothesis, since they have views about what will work and those views are currently untested and unrecorded. Track their improvement over time, which is the point and which nothing measures. And measure the value of the feedback loop, since the argument for building it is that the creative gets better and that is checkable.

## Target Customer
Creative producers and motion designers, creative and growth leadership, and the agencies whose creative output is their main deliverable.

## Impact If Built
The designer is outside the measurement loop by organisational construction, so two years of production teaches them nothing. Routing concept-level outcomes back, with spend and performance clearly separated, is mostly plumbing and turns a treadmill into a craft that compounds.

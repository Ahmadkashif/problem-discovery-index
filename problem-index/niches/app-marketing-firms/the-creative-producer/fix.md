# Spend Reported as Performance

**Niche:** [[niches/app-marketing-firms/the-creative-producer/profile|The Creative Producer]]
**Industry:** [[industries/app-marketing-firms|App Marketing Firms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The creative report ranks variants by spend, which is the network's allocation decision rather than the advertiser's outcome, and everyone reads it as a performance ranking.
**Tags:** #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #confidence-intervals #hypothesis-testing #quick-win #revenue-impact #worker-facing #causal-inference
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to tell the person making the work which of it succeeded and why — and whoever does that turns a production treadmill into a craft that improves.

## The Problem
The weekly creative report lists variants by spend, descending. The top one received most of the budget, so it is treated as the winner, briefed against, and used as the template for next week's batch. Spend is the network's allocation decision, made by the network's own model, optimising the network's own objective, on signals that may not correspond to the advertiser's outcome — particularly under aggregated attribution where the advertiser's outcome is not fully observable to anyone. A ranking by spend is a ranking of what an algorithm chose to buy, and it is being used as a ranking of what worked.

## Why It's Still Broken
Spend is the one creative-level number that is available, complete and immediate, so it became the metric — availability again standing in for validity, which is the pattern across this whole cluster. Under aggregated attribution the true creative-level outcome is hard to obtain, which makes the substitution feel necessary. The network's allocation correlates with performance often enough to seem reasonable. And nobody has compared the ranking to anything.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Label it and replace it. Stop calling the spend ranking a performance ranking, which is the fix and is a labelling change that immediately stops the wrong lesson being learned. Report what outcome data exists at the creative or concept level, with its uncertainty, even where it is coarse — a noisy signal about the right thing beats a precise signal about the wrong one. Aggregate to the concept level to gain statistical power, since individual variants rarely have enough volume and concepts do. Compare the spend ranking against the outcome ranking where both exist, which is the exercise that shows how often they diverge and is the argument that changes the practice. Run deliberate creative tests with controlled budget rather than relying on the network's allocation, which is the only way to get a clean read on what worked. Report cohort value rather than installs, since a creative that attracts poorly-matched users spends well and performs badly. Explain to the creative team what the spend number means, because they are drawing conclusions from it daily and nobody has told them what it is. Track the divergence over time and by network, since it differs. Feed the outcome ranking rather than the spend ranking into briefing. And measure whether creative performance improves once the feedback is corrected, because that is the entire case for the change.

## Who Feels the Pain
Designers learning the wrong lessons from an algorithm's preferences; advertisers whose creative strategy is shaped by a network's allocation; and teams whose creative improves at the network's objective rather than their own.

## Impact If Fixed
Spend is complete and immediate, so availability stands in for validity once again, and a ranking of what an algorithm chose to buy is read as a ranking of what worked. Relabelling it and reporting a coarse outcome signal instead stops the wrong lesson at the point it is currently taught.

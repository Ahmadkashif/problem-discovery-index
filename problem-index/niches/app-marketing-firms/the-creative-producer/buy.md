**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to tell the person making the work which of it succeeded and why — and whoever does that turns a production treadmill into a craft that improves.

## The Problem
The conditions under which people improve at a skill are well understood: repeated attempts, immediate and specific feedback, and a clear standard. Fields that produce reliable expertise — surgery, aviation, competitive sport, forecasting — engineer those conditions deliberately, and fields that do not produce experts despite long experience are usually missing the feedback. Creative production for performance advertising has enormous repetition, a clear standard in principle, and essentially no feedback, which is precisely the configuration under which experience does not become expertise.

## What Already Exists
Deliberate practice frameworks; feedback design in skill acquisition; forecasting calibration training with scored predictions; structured review in professional practice; and performance analysis in sport.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to feedback that is noisy, delayed and confounded. It requires: (1) an outcome signal that is genuinely uncertain rather than a clear score, so the feedback must convey confidence and avoid teaching lessons the data does not support — teaching a designer a false pattern is worse than teaching them nothing, which is the risk the current spend-based feedback already realises; (2) attribution of an outcome to a creative choice among many simultaneous factors, which requires the concept-level measurement rather than per-asset numbers; (3) delay between production and result, where practice frameworks assume immediacy and the design must compress what it can; (4) a practitioner who is a creative professional and will reject feedback that reads as formulaic, so the delivery matters as much as the content; and (5) collective rather than individual learning, since patterns emerge across designers and accounts rather than within one person's output.

## Target Customer
Creative leadership at agencies and studios, creative producers, and learning design practitioners for whom performance creative is an unserved application.

## Impact If Solved
Enormous repetition with no feedback is exactly the configuration under which experience does not become expertise. Conveying confidence matters more than usual here, because teaching a designer a false pattern is worse than teaching them nothing and the current spend-based feedback already does it.

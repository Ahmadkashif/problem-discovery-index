# Redistribution Response

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/digital-goods-marketplaces|Digital Goods Marketplaces]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to make responding to redistribution a platform function rather than a creator's second job — and whoever does that defends the economics the whole category is built on.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$2.2B US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~24% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Low — forms and manual notices
**Target Buyer:** Platform trust and creator success leadership
**Automation Potential:** High — search, notice and follow-up are all mechanisable

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A digital good copies perfectly at zero cost, so redistribution is not leakage but substitution for a sale, and enforcement is delegated to individual creators filling in takedown forms. The unresolved question is not the detection technology but who bears the cost of acting, and the answer has been the person least equipped to bear it: a solo creator who must find the infringing site themselves, identify the host, file a statutory notice correctly, and repeat it forever. This is a distinct contest because it is an operations and economics problem rather than a detection one, which is a separate fight from identifying copies at scale.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Statutory notice-and-takedown forms, creator-submitted reports, occasional platform-initiated action on the largest sites, and third-party anti-piracy services priced for studios. The gaps: the creator does the finding and the filing; no repeat-infringer tracking across creators; no economics attached to any of it; no pre-emption; and no measurement of what enforcement actually recovers.

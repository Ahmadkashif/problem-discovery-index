# Enforcement Delegated to the Person With the Least Capacity

**Niche:** [[niches/digital-goods-marketplaces/redistribution-response/profile|Redistribution Response]]
**Industry:** [[industries/digital-goods-marketplaces|Digital Goods Marketplaces]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A digital good copies perfectly at zero cost, so redistribution is not leakage but substitution for a sale, and enforcement is delegated to individual creators filling in takedown forms.
**Tags:** #workflow-orchestration #automation #compliance #evaluation-metrics #revenue-impact #descriptive-statistics #worker-facing #graph-theory
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to make responding to redistribution a platform function rather than a creator's second job — and whoever does that defends the economics the whole category is built on.

## The Problem
A creator sells a template pack. Within a week it is on six aggregation sites, two forums and a messaging channel, free. Their sales halve. The platform's answer is a help article explaining how to file a takedown notice: find the infringing page, identify the host, determine the correct contact, draft a legally sufficient notice, send it, follow up, and do the same again next week when it reappears. The creator is one person who makes design assets. They do a few, exhaust themselves, and stop — and the sites know that, which is why the model works.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The statutory framework places the burden on the rights holder, and platforms have adopted that allocation as a product decision rather than examining it — this is the origin of the whole situation. Acting on creators' behalf raises questions about the platform's own liability posture that legal teams prefer to avoid. Enforcement is a cost with no revenue line. And the loss is the creator's, not the platform's, except through a commission nobody attributes.

## What to Build
Make enforcement a service the platform runs. Find the copies centrally rather than asking each creator to search, since one search covers thousands of assets and the marginal cost per creator collapses — this is the entire economic argument and it is why the current allocation is simply wrong. File notices automatically with correct host identification and a valid legal form, which is mechanical work that defeats creators purely through unfamiliarity. Track repeat infringers across the whole catalogue, because the same handful of sites take everything and treating each notice as an isolated event is what makes the process endless. Escalate beyond notices where the pattern justifies it — host, registrar, payment provider, advertising network — which is the step individual creators cannot reach and where the actual leverage is. Quantify the loss per asset so enforcement effort goes where the money is rather than where the creator is most upset. Detect the leak's origin where possible, since a specific purchase account leaking repeatedly is both identifiable and actionable. Offer creators an enforcement tier as a product, which funds the function and makes it sustainable rather than charitable. Report outcomes honestly — notices sent, content removed, reappearance rate — because creators currently have no idea whether anything works. Address the search engines and aggregators that route the traffic, which matters more than the hosts. And measure recovered sales rather than notices filed, since activity metrics are how enforcement programmes become theatre.

## Target Customer
Digital goods marketplaces, the creators whose income is substituted, and the anti-piracy service providers priced out of this segment.

## Impact If Built
The statutory allocation of burden was adopted as a product decision and never examined, leaving enforcement to a solo creator who makes design assets. One central search covers thousands of assets, which collapses the marginal cost and makes the current allocation economically indefensible.

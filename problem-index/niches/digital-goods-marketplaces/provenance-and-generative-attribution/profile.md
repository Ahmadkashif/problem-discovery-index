# Provenance & Generative Attribution

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/digital-goods-marketplaces|Digital Goods Marketplaces]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to establish what an asset was made from before it is listed for sale — and whoever can answer that decides what a marketplace is allowed to sell.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$800M US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~9% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Very Low — declarations and guesswork
**Target Buyer:** Platform policy and legal leadership
**Automation Potential:** Medium — detection partial, records feasible

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Generative tools have added a new and unresolved question about what may be sold and under what provenance. A marketplace now receives assets that may be generated, partly generated, generated from a model trained on the marketplace's own catalogue, or traced from another creator's work, and it has a checkbox asking the uploader to declare. Buyers increasingly need to state provenance to their own clients, some jurisdictions and industries require it, and creators whose work was used to train the model that produced a competing asset have no visibility at all. This is distinct from copyright review, which adjudicates a claim between two parties; here the question is what the asset is, before anyone claims anything.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Uploader self-declaration checkboxes, policy statements, occasional detection tools, and reactive removal. The gaps: declarations that are unverifiable and often unknowing; no record of the tools and inputs used; no detection for partly generated work; no path for a creator whose work trained a model; and no way for a buyer to obtain a provenance statement they can rely on.

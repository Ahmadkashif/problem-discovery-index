# Content Authenticity Practice

**Niche:** [[niches/digital-goods-marketplaces/provenance-and-generative-attribution/profile|Provenance & Generative Attribution]]
**Industry:** [[industries/digital-goods-marketplaces|Digital Goods Marketplaces]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** News and photography built content provenance standards with cryptographic signing and edit histories, and asset marketplaces have a checkbox.
**Tags:** #compliance #transformers #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #automation #workflow-orchestration #data-integration #transfer-learning
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to establish what an asset was made from before it is listed for sale — and whoever can answer that decides what a marketplace is allowed to sell.

## The Problem
Content provenance has a real standards effort behind it, driven by news organisations, camera manufacturers and photo agencies: cryptographically signed capture, recorded edit histories, tamper-evident manifests travelling with the file, and verification at publication. It is deployed in journalism and stock photography because the cost of a fabricated image there is severe. Digital goods marketplaces, where provenance now determines what may legally be sold and resold, have adopted almost none of it.

## What Already Exists
Content provenance and authenticity standards with signed manifests; edit history recording in creative tools; verification and display of credentials at publication; synthetic media detection services; and industry governance around the standards.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is from a photograph with a single origin to a composite asset built from many sources over months. It requires: (1) provenance for composite works where a template draws on dozens of components each with its own history, which the capture-oriented standards handle poorly and is the structural gap; (2) coverage of non-image asset types — fonts, code plugins, presets, three-dimensional models — where the standards effort has concentrated on photography and video; (3) resale and derivation chains, since a buyer using an asset in a derived work needs the provenance to compose forward, which is a graph rather than a manifest; (4) creator adoption without enterprise tooling, because a solo creator will not run a signing infrastructure and the credential must come free with the tools they already use; and (5) an explicit treatment of training-data provenance, which the current standards do not address and which is the specific question this category faces.

## Target Customer
Digital goods marketplaces, creative tool vendors, enterprise buyers with provenance requirements, and the provenance standards bodies for whom composite creative assets are unaddressed.

## Impact If Solved
The standards were built around a photograph with one origin, and these assets are composites assembled over months. Derivation chains need a graph rather than a manifest, and training-data provenance — the question this category actually faces — is not addressed at all.

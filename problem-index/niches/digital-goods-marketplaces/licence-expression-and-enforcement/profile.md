# Licence Expression & Enforcement

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/digital-goods-marketplaces|Digital Goods Marketplaces]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to make a licence something the delivery path can read and check rather than prose in an agreement — and whoever does that lets compliance happen by default instead of by goodwill.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.3B US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~14% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Very Low — prose and tier names
**Target Buyer:** Platform legal and product leadership
**Automation Potential:** High — the terms are enumerable and checkable

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Every purchase carries licence terms in prose, no part of the delivery path knows what they say, and neither the buyer who wants to comply nor the creator whose terms were exceeded can establish what was permitted. Most buyers intend to comply and cannot, because compliance requires reading an agreement per asset, remembering it across a project that lasts two years, and knowing which of nine assets in a deliverable had which terms. This is a distinct contest from redistribution because the counterparty is a paying customer acting in good faith, which makes it a representation problem rather than an enforcement one.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Prose purchase agreements, tier names such as personal and commercial, occasional extended licence upsells, and creator-initiated disputes. The gaps: no machine-readable terms; no record of which licence attaches to which file in a project; no check at the point of use; no upgrade path when a project's scope changes; and no way for either party to establish what was permitted after the fact.

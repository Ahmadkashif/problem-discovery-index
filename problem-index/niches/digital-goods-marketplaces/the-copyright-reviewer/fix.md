# The Claim Made in Bad Faith

**Niche:** [[niches/digital-goods-marketplaces/the-copyright-reviewer/profile|The Copyright Reviewer]]
**Industry:** [[industries/digital-goods-marketplaces|Digital Goods Marketplaces]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A competitor files infringement claims against a rival's listings, the listings come down pending review, and the takedown itself was the objective.
**Tags:** #graph-theory #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #compliance #hypothesis-testing #quick-win #confidence-intervals #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to give the copyright reviewer evidence about which file came first and what was derived from what — and whoever supplies that turns an unanswerable adjudication into a determinable one.

## The Problem
A seller files claims against the top-ranking listings in their category. The platform takes them down pending review, as its process requires. Two weeks later the claims are found baseless and the listings are restored — into a ranking position that has collapsed, in a seasonal window that has passed, with sales history broken. The claimant lost nothing. They file again next month, against different listings, sometimes through a different account. The process's presumption in favour of the claimant, which exists to protect rights holders, has become the most efficient competitive weapon on the platform.

## Why It's Still Broken
The statutory framework encourages prompt removal on notice and platforms implement it literally without a counterweight. Claims are assessed individually, so a claimant's pattern never surfaces. Restoration is treated as a complete remedy, which ignores that ranking, momentum and seasonality do not restore. And there is no penalty for a baseless claim, which makes filing one free.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Make the claim carry a cost and the pattern visible. Track claim outcomes per claimant and display them to reviewers, which is the fix, is a single field, and immediately distinguishes a rights holder from a serial filer. Require more evidence from claimants with poor histories before removal, which is proportionate and legally defensible and stops the cheapest form of abuse. Delay removal on high-quality listings from established sellers where the claim is weak, since the presumption should scale with the evidence rather than being uniform. Restore ranking position and sales history on reversal, because restoration without that is not a remedy and is the specific asymmetry that makes the attack profitable — closing it removes the incentive entirely. Penalise repeated baseless claims with rate limits, deposits or suspension, so filing is no longer free. Link accounts by payment, device and behavioural signals, as serial filers rotate identities and single-account tracking misses them. Prioritise review by the respondent's harm, since a top listing removed in a seasonal peak needs hours rather than weeks. Notify respondents immediately with the full claim and a route to respond, which is often enough to resolve it in a day. Publish the platform's handling of bad-faith claims, because deterrence requires that the policy be known. And report time-to-resolution and reversal rate, since a two-week reversal is a successful attack regardless of the eventual outcome.

## Who Feels the Pain
Creators whose income is removed by a competitor's assertion; reviewers processing claims designed to abuse the process; and platforms whose protective mechanism is the most effective attack available.

## Impact If Fixed
Restoration without ranking and history is not a remedy, and that asymmetry is exactly what makes the attack profitable. Tracking claim outcomes per claimant is a single field that separates rights holders from serial filers immediately.

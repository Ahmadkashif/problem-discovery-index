# Category Management Practice

**Industry:** [[industries/digital-goods-marketplaces|Digital Goods Marketplaces]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Retail category management has a mature method for finding assortment gaps and telling suppliers what to make, and creator marketplaces publish a bestseller list.

**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to tell a creator what to make next from the demand the platform already observes — and whoever does that controls the supply side of the marketplace.

## The Problem
Retailers run category management as a discipline: assortment gap analysis, share-of-shelf and duplication review, price architecture, and structured supplier development where the retailer tells a manufacturer what the category needs. Grocers and general merchandisers do this continuously, with worse demand data than a marketplace has. Digital goods marketplaces have a complete record of demand and supply and do not run any equivalent, leaving assortment to whatever creators happen to make.

## What Already Exists
Assortment gap and duplication analysis; share-of-category and space allocation methods; price architecture and good-better-best structuring; supplier development programmes; and category performance review cycles.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is from a buyer directing a few suppliers to a platform informing thousands of independent creators. It requires: (1) demand measured from search and rejection rather than from sales of what is stocked, which is a far better signal than retail has ever had — the retailer can only see what they chose to carry, and the marketplace sees what buyers asked for and did not get; (2) no ability to direct supply, only to inform it, so the output is an invitation rather than a purchase order and must be persuasive rather than contractual; (3) the recommendation changing the market, since telling many creators the same thing fills the gap immediately — a reflexivity retail category management never faces because supplier counts are small and lead times are long; (4) creator capability as a constraint, as an unfillable gap is not an opportunity for a given creator; and (5) delivery to an individual working alone rather than to a planning process.

## Target Customer
Digital goods and creative marketplaces, creator communities, and category management vendors for whom marketplace supply development is unserved.

## Impact If Solved
A retailer sees only what they chose to stock, and the marketplace sees what buyers asked for and did not get — a strictly better demand signal. The reflexivity of informing thousands of independent suppliers at once is the problem retail never had to solve.

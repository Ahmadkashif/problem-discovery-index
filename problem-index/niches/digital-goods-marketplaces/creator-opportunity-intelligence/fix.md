# The Search With No Results

**Niche:** [[niches/digital-goods-marketplaces/creator-opportunity-intelligence/profile|Creator Opportunity Intelligence]]
**Industry:** [[industries/digital-goods-marketplaces|Digital Goods Marketplaces]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Four hundred buyers a week search for the same thing, find nothing, and leave — and the query is logged, counted by nobody, and shown to no one.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #word-embeddings #evaluation-metrics #quick-win #revenue-impact #automation #bert #workflow-orchestration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to tell a creator what to make next from the demand the platform already observes — and whoever does that controls the supply side of the marketplace.

## The Problem
The zero-result search is the clearest statement of demand a marketplace will ever receive: someone arrived, knew what they wanted, asked for it in their own words, and the catalogue did not have it. It happens thousands of times a day. The query is written to a log and nothing happens. No creator is told, no category manager reviews it, no report counts it, and the buyer leaves and is not replaced. Every marketplace in the category has this data and treats it as exhaust.

## Why It's Still Broken
Zero-result searches are classified as a search quality defect rather than as a demand signal, which puts them with the search team as something to eliminate rather than with the supply team as something to act on — that misclassification is the whole problem. Near-misses, where results were returned but all rejected, are not even counted as failures. Query text is messy and clustering it takes deliberate work. And no one owns the supply side.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Treat the failed search as the demand report it is. Cluster zero-result and all-rejected queries by meaning rather than by string, which is the fix, is a short piece of work on data already held, and turns noise into a ranked list of missing things. Count near-misses alongside true zero-results, since a search that returned forty irrelevant items is the same failure with a different symptom and is far more common. Attach volume, trend and the searcher's segment, so the size of each gap is visible rather than just its existence. Publish the ranked gap list to creators, which is the single highest-return action available and costs a report. Distinguish a catalogue gap from a findability gap, because the asset often exists and is untagged — a five-minute fix — while a genuine gap needs weeks of creation, and confusing the two wastes both. Route findability gaps to the creators who already own the matching asset, which is the fastest revenue available anywhere in this analysis. Notify the buyer when the gap is later filled, since they stated an intent and are the highest-converting audience for the new asset. Track how long gaps persist, which is a direct measure of whether the marketplace's supply side is working. Feed the gaps into creator recommendations and onboarding, so new creators start with demand rather than with a guess. And report satisfied-search rate as a headline metric, because a marketplace measured only on conversion cannot see the buyers who left.

## Who Feels the Pain
Buyers who leave without what they came for; creators who would have made it and were never told; and platforms losing demand they measured precisely and never read.

## Impact If Fixed
The zero-result search is the clearest demand statement a marketplace receives and is classified as a search defect rather than a supply signal. Clustering failed queries by meaning produces a ranked list of missing things, and separating findability gaps from catalogue gaps surfaces revenue available in five minutes.

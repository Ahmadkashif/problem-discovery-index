# Forty Minutes of Scrolling

**Niche:** [[niches/digital-goods-marketplaces/aesthetic-and-style-search/profile|Aesthetic & Style Search]]
**Industry:** [[industries/digital-goods-marketplaces|Digital Goods Marketplaces]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The buyer scrolls past nine hundred results, rejects every one instantly, buys something adequate, and the platform records a successful conversion.
**Tags:** #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #hypothesis-testing #confidence-intervals #revenue-impact #quick-win #dimensionality-reduction #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to match a buyer to a feeling they cannot put into words — and whoever builds the interaction that extracts an aesthetic intent wins the searches that keyword and similarity search both fail.

## The Problem
The session lasted forty-one minutes and touched nine hundred results across eleven queries. The buyer bought a template they described to a colleague as fine. The platform's metrics show a converted session with high engagement. Both the frustration and the compromise are invisible, and because the metric is satisfied the search team has no signal that anything is wrong. Meanwhile the buyer's nine hundred rejections — the most informative aesthetic data the platform will ever collect — are logged as impressions and used for nothing.

## Why It's Still Broken
Conversion is the metric and a compromise purchase converts, which makes the whole failure mode invisible by construction. Session length reads as engagement rather than as difficulty. Rejections are treated as non-events. And nobody asks buyers whether they found what they wanted, because the answer is uncomfortable and unmeasured.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Measure the difficulty, then use the rejections. Report effort-to-purchase — results viewed, queries reformulated, time elapsed — as a headline search metric alongside conversion, which immediately reveals that a large share of successful sessions are failures; this reframing is the fix and needs no new data. Treat query reformulation as an explicit failure signal, since a buyer rewriting a query four times is telling the system something precise. Capture rejections as usable feedback rather than impressions, which is the largest untapped signal in the category and costs nothing to start recording properly. Ask a single question at the end of long sessions about whether this was what they wanted, which most platforms never do and which calibrates everything else. Detect the compromise purchase — long session, late purchase, low return rate to the category — and follow up, since these buyers are the most likely to churn. Offer to converge rather than to keep listing once a session exceeds a threshold, which is the intervention the build note describes and can be triggered by the metric. Report per-query difficulty so the search team can see which intents the system handles badly, which is currently averaged away. Show creators when buyers viewed and rejected their asset repeatedly, which is actionable positioning information. Segment metrics by intent type, since a buyer looking for a specific known thing and one looking for a feeling have entirely different profiles and averaging them hides both. And set a target on effort rather than on conversion, because whichever number is reported is the one that improves.

## Who Feels the Pain
Buyers spending forty minutes to reach adequate; creators whose better-fitting work was never surfaced; and platforms whose search metrics report success during systematic failure.

## Impact If Fixed
Conversion is satisfied by a compromise purchase, which makes the failure invisible by construction. Reporting effort-to-purchase reframes a large share of successful sessions as failures, and nine hundred rejections per session are the richest aesthetic signal available and are currently logged as impressions.

# Causal Attribution of Visibility Change

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/seo-tooling-vendors|SEO Tooling Vendors]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to answer whether what a site did worked or whether the algorithm moved — and whoever does it owns the only dataset capable of settling the customer's recurring question.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$500M US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~13% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Low — a decade of natural experiments, one line chart
**Target Buyer:** Vendor data leadership and enterprise customers
**Automation Potential:** Very High — the corpus is the method

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
These vendors hold a longitudinal record of what changed on millions of sites, when, and what happened to their visibility afterwards, across every algorithm update of the last decade. That is a natural-experiment corpus of considerable power, and it is currently used to draw a line chart. The customer's recurring question is causal — did what I did work, or did the algorithm move — and the vendor holds the only dataset outside the search engines capable of answering it while selling a tool that cannot. This is the category's own corpus problem in its clearest form.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Visibility line charts, algorithm update markers overlaid on them, correlation studies of ranking factors, and case studies. The gaps: no control group construction; no separation of update effects from site changes; correlation studies conflating association with cause; no per-site counterfactual; and no accumulation of what actually worked.

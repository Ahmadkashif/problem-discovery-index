# A Discipline You Have to Learn First

**Niche:** [[niches/seo-tooling-vendors/the-small-site-owner/profile|The Small Site Owner]]
**Industry:** [[industries/seo-tooling-vendors|SEO Tooling Vendors]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Every tool in the category assumes the user's job is SEO, and the largest population of sites is owned by people whose job is running a business.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #automation #evaluation-metrics #workflow-orchestration #revenue-impact #gradient-boosting #quick-win #descriptive-statistics
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to tell a small business the two things to do this month without teaching them a discipline — and whoever does that reaches the enormous audience the category's tooling structurally excludes.

## The Problem
A local business owner signs up for an SEO tool because their web traffic is down. They are presented with domain authority, keyword difficulty, crawl errors, backlink profiles, SERP features and a content score. Every one of these is a concept requiring explanation, and the tool explains none of them because it assumes they are known. The owner spends twenty minutes, understands nothing actionable, and either cancels or leaves it unused and pays an agency instead. The tooling was not badly built; it was built for somebody else, and the somebody else is a small fraction of the people who need it.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The category grew up serving practitioners and agencies, who could pay and who understood the vocabulary — the product's assumptions were correct for the market it started in and were never revisited. Simplified tiers are the professional product with features removed, which does not address the comprehension barrier at all. Generating genuinely specific advice without a human was not practical until recently. And the segment pays little individually, so it has never been anyone's priority.

## What to Build
Produce decisions, not data. Ask what the business does and who its customers are, then generate specific advice for that business, which is the whole product — the owner needs to know what to do, not what their domain authority is. Give two or three actions a month rather than a dashboard, since capacity is the binding constraint and a list of forty items is equivalent to no list. Write in the language of the business, not of the discipline, because every term of art is a point where the user leaves. Do the work where possible — generate the page, write the description, fix the listing, structure the data — rather than instructing someone who cannot. Prioritise what actually moves a small local or niche business, which is usually a handful of things and is not what a general audit surfaces. Report in customers and enquiries rather than in sessions and positions, since that is what the owner cares about and the translation is the vendor's job. Connect to what they already use — their website builder, their business listing, their booking or commerce tool — since integration is the only way the work gets done. Tell them plainly when search is not their opportunity, which is honest, rare, and builds the trust the segment lacks. Give them a way to evaluate an agency, which is the fix note's subject. And price for the segment, because a professional price point is the final exclusion after all the others.

## Target Customer
Small and local business owners, website builder and commerce platforms serving them, and SEO vendors seeking a market beyond practitioners.

## Impact If Built
The product's assumptions were correct for the practitioner market it started in and were never revisited, so the largest population of site owners is excluded by vocabulary before price. Generating two specific actions a month, in business language, is a different product rather than a simplified one.

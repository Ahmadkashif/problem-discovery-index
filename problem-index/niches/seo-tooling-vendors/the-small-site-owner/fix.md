# The Agency Nobody Can Evaluate

**Niche:** [[niches/seo-tooling-vendors/the-small-site-owner/profile|The Small Site Owner]]
**Industry:** [[industries/seo-tooling-vendors|SEO Tooling Vendors]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The business pays a monthly retainer, receives a report full of terms they do not understand, and has no way to tell whether anything was done.
**Tags:** #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #compliance #quick-win #workflow-orchestration #revenue-impact #confidence-intervals #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to tell a small business the two things to do this month without teaching them a discipline — and whoever does that reaches the enormous audience the category's tooling structurally excludes.

## The Problem
A business pays an agency monthly. Each month a report arrives: rankings for terms nobody at the business chose, a count of backlinks acquired, domain authority moving by a decimal, and a summary of optimisations performed. The owner cannot evaluate any of it. They do not know whether the terms matter, whether the links are real, whether the optimisations happened, or whether their enquiries went up. The information asymmetry is complete, the relationship runs on trust with nothing to verify it, and the honest agencies in this market are indistinguishable from the ones doing almost nothing.

## Why It's Still Broken
The client cannot evaluate the work, which means good and bad agencies present identically and the market has no quality signal — this is a straightforward adverse selection problem and nobody has supplied the verification. Reports are written in the discipline's vocabulary because that is what demonstrates expertise. The outcome the client cares about is not connected to anything the agency reports. And the client has no independent source of truth.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Give the client an independent check. Monitor the site's actual changes independently, so the owner can see what was modified and when regardless of what the report says — this is the fix and it converts a trust relationship into a verifiable one. Report in enquiries, calls and sales rather than in rankings, which requires connecting to the business's own systems and is what the owner actually wants to know. Verify the claimed work: pages changed, listings updated, links acquired and whether those links are real, which is checkable and is where the largest discrepancies appear. Benchmark against comparable local businesses, so an owner knows whether their result is good rather than merely positive. Translate the report into plain language automatically, which removes the vocabulary barrier that sustains the asymmetry. Flag the recognisable patterns of low-value work, since the common ones are identifiable and an owner has no way to spot them. Show what changed in visibility and attribute it honestly between the agency's work and everything else, which protects good agencies as much as clients. Give the agency a way to demonstrate value credibly, because the honest ones are currently penalised by the market's inability to tell. Support the conversation rather than replacing the relationship, since most small businesses need an agency and simply need to be able to evaluate one. And publish outcome benchmarks for the segment, because a market with no visible standard is the root of the problem.

## Who Feels the Pain
Small businesses paying for work they cannot verify; good agencies competing against ones that do nothing for the same fee; and a segment whose experience of the discipline is poor enough to deter them permanently.

## Impact If Fixed
Good and bad agencies present identically because the client cannot evaluate the work, which is adverse selection with no verification supplied. Independent monitoring of the site's actual changes, reported in enquiries rather than rankings, makes the relationship verifiable.

# The Position Improved and Nobody Knows What Happened

**Niche:** [[niches/seo-tooling-vendors/outcome-connection/profile|Outcome Connection]]
**Industry:** [[industries/seo-tooling-vendors|SEO Tooling Vendors]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** The vendor sees rankings and crawl data, conversions and revenue sit in the customer's systems, and the tooling can report improvement without knowing whether anything happened.
**Tags:** #data-integration #causal-inference #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #revenue-impact #descriptive-statistics #survival-analysis #gradient-boosting
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to connect a position change to a revenue change inside the customer's own systems — and whoever does that solves the hardest recurring task in the customer's job.

## The Problem
A customer's SEO reports that visibility improved fourteen percent this quarter. Finance asks what that was worth. The answer requires joining the vendor's ranking data to the customer's sessions, then to conversions, then to revenue, across a consideration cycle that may be months, with brand and non-brand demand mixed together and assisted conversions invisible. Nobody has built that join, so the SEO builds it by hand in a spreadsheet every quarter, with assumptions they know are shaky, and presents a number they cannot fully defend. This is the recurring hardest task in the job and it recurs because the wall between the two datasets has never been crossed.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The vendor's data and the customer's data are on opposite sides of a boundary neither party has been motivated to bridge — the vendor sells a tool rather than an integration and the customer's analytics team has other priorities. Keyword-level attribution is genuinely difficult since search data is aggregated and partially withheld. Long cycles make the join statistical rather than exact. And the SEO's spreadsheet makes the problem survivable.

## What to Build
Cross the wall. Join ranking and visibility data to the customer's own analytics, commerce and customer systems as a first-class integration, which is the foundation and is what turns a reporting tool into a business case generator. Attribute at the level the data supports — landing page, topic cluster, intent group — rather than pretending to keyword-level precision that the available data cannot give. Separate brand from non-brand demand, since brand search is largely a consequence of other marketing and including it inflates the channel's apparent value in a way that will not survive scrutiny. Handle long consideration cycles with a time-to-conversion model rather than a fixed window, connecting to the same delayed-outcome problem that runs through this cluster. Include assisted and cross-device paths as far as the data allows, and state what is missing rather than silently omitting it. Estimate incremental rather than attributed value where possible, since a business case built on attributed revenue will be challenged by any finance function that understands the difference. Produce the business case as an output, with the assumptions visible and adjustable, which is the deliverable and is what the SEO currently builds by hand. Forecast the value of proposed work, since budget conversations are about next year rather than last. Update continuously rather than at budget time, so the case is standing rather than assembled under pressure. And be honest about uncertainty, because an overstated case that collapses under questioning is worse for the channel than a modest one that holds.

## Target Customer
In-house SEO and marketing leadership, SEO tooling vendors whose products stop at visibility, and the agencies who must justify retainers.

## Impact If Built
The two datasets sit on opposite sides of a boundary neither party was motivated to cross, so the hardest recurring task in the job is a quarterly spreadsheet. A first-class join that separates brand demand and models the consideration cycle turns a reporting tool into a standing business case.

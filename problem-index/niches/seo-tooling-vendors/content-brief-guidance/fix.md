# The Content Score That Rewards Conformance

**Niche:** [[niches/seo-tooling-vendors/content-brief-guidance/profile|Content Brief & Production Guidance]]
**Industry:** [[industries/seo-tooling-vendors|SEO Tooling Vendors]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The content score goes up as the page gets closer to the average of everything already ranking, and writers are edited until they hit it.
**Tags:** #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #hypothesis-testing #word-embeddings #quick-win #revenue-impact #confidence-intervals #large-language-models
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to guide a writer toward something worth reading rather than toward a statistical average of whoever currently ranks — and whoever does that stops the tooling from manufacturing the page everyone complains about.

## The Problem
A writer produces a piece with a genuine angle and first-hand detail. The content score says seventy-one. The editor asks them to raise it to ninety, which means adding the twelve subheadings the competitors use, repeating the target term more often, and padding to the expected length. The piece now scores ninety and is worse — longer, blander and less distinctive. Everyone involved knows this happened. The score is chased anyway, because it is the only number in the process and numbers get chased. The tool is optimising for similarity to the existing average and calling it quality.

## Why It's Still Broken
The score is the product's visible output and its whole appeal, so removing it is removing what customers bought — which is why no vendor has. Similarity to top results is easy to compute and correlates weakly enough with ranking to seem justified. Writers and editors are measured on hitting it. And the harm is a gradual flattening that nobody can attribute to a specific decision.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Score contribution, not similarity. Measure what the page adds that the existing results do not — new information, evidence, specificity, a question answered nowhere else — which is the fix and is now computable in a way it was not when similarity scoring was designed. Report similarity separately and frame it as a warning rather than a target, since being highly similar to everything ranking is a risk and the current scale has the sign inverted. Remove word count targets, which are the single most damaging instruction in the category and have no basis. Show the specific gaps a piece could fill instead of a number to raise, because a writer given a gap writes something better and a writer given a number pads. Track outcomes by score so the correlation can be examined openly, which no vendor publishes and which would settle the argument. Reward first-hand material — original data, testing, direct experience — which is what actually differentiates and which no current score can see. Give editors a view of whether a piece is distinctive rather than whether it is complete. Warn when a draft has converged on the competitive average, which is the inverse of the current alert and is the more useful one. Deprecate the score in reporting so teams are not managed against it. And validate any replacement against real outcomes, because the previous score was never validated and that is how the category arrived here.

## Who Feels the Pain
Writers edited into blandness against a number; readers given ten identical pages; and businesses whose content investment produces something no one would choose.

## Impact If Fixed
The score measures similarity to the existing average and calls it quality, so the sign is inverted on the thing that matters. Measuring contribution is now computable, and replacing a number to raise with a specific gap to fill changes what the writer does.

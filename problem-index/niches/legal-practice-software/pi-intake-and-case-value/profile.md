# Personal Injury — Intake Selection & Case Value

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/legal-practice-software|Legal Practice Software]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in personal injury firm software is fighting to tell a firm which of this week's intakes to sign and what each is worth against this venue and this carrier — and whoever predicts that best takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$600M US software spend across plaintiff personal injury firms
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~17% of legal practice software revenue
**Digital Adoption:** High — PI firms run marketing, intake, medical record retrieval and demand generation on software and measure it closely
**Target Buyer:** Managing partners and intake directors at 5-100 lawyer PI firms
**Automation Potential:** Very High — intake scoring, record retrieval and demand assembly are the three largest labour blocks and all are structured

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A personal injury firm spends heavily to make the phone ring and then makes a decision about each call that determines its year. Sign a case with thin liability, a treatment gap and a minimum policy limit and the firm spends eighteen months and $8K to net nothing; decline a case that was worth $400K and never know. The decision is made in minutes by an intake specialist working from a script, and it is the highest-leverage judgment in the business. What makes the niche terminal under the filter is that the judgment is a prediction with a ground truth: every signed case eventually resolves, and the platforms hold hundreds of thousands of those resolutions with the intake facts that preceded them. The declined cases are the missing arm and their absence is the central methodological problem, not a footnote.

## Current Tools & Gaps
CASEpeer, SmartAdvocate, Filevine and Litify all provide intake pipelines with configurable qualification questions and lead-source attribution, and the marketing side is instrumented to the keyword. Case value estimation is absent across the category; where it appears it is a rules table of average settlements by injury type, which ignores venue, carrier, adjuster, treatment pattern and liability posture — the factors practitioners actually weight. Verdict and settlement databases (Jury Verdict Research, Trellis, VerdictSearch) exist and are used as reference points rather than as model inputs. No platform tracks its own intake decisions against outcomes, so a firm's qualification criteria have never been tested against anything.

# Immigration Practice Platforms

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/legal-practice-software|Legal Practice Software]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in immigration case management is fighting to keep an open case's forms, evidence set and procedural posture correct when the policy underneath it changes mid-case — and whoever propagates a policy change across an active caseload fastest takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$280M US software spend across immigration law firms, nonprofit legal service providers and corporate immigration functions
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~8% of legal practice software revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Low-Medium — the large corporate immigration practices are well tooled; the family and removal defense practice that serves most people is not
**Target Buyer:** Founders of immigration case management vendors; practice owners and nonprofit programme directors
**Automation Potential:** High — the work is form-driven and evidence-driven, and the forms and evidence requirements are published

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Immigration practice is form-heavy in a way that looks automatable and resists automation for one specific reason: the ground moves. A case filed under one set of eligibility criteria, evidentiary expectations and processing procedures may be adjudicated under another, because the governing policy is a mixture of statute, regulation, agency policy manual updates, federal court injunctions and unannounced adjudicative practice, all of which change on their own schedules. A firm with 400 open cases learns of a change and then must work out which of the 400 are affected, in what way, and what to do about each — by hand, in a spreadsheet, usually in a week where the answer matters urgently. No other practice area has this combination of high caseload, high form volume and an unstable substrate.

## Current Tools & Gaps
INSZoom, Docketwise, Cerenade and LollyLaw serve the practice with form libraries, case management and client questionnaires; the corporate immigration side is better served than the family and removal side. Every vendor's form library is updated when USCIS revises a form, which is the easy and visible part. The hard part is unaddressed: nothing models a case's procedural posture as a live state against current policy, so nothing can tell a firm which open cases a change affects. Evidence requirements are handled as checklists that are static per case type rather than derived from the specific case's facts and the current adjudicative expectations. And requests for evidence — the most consequential and most avoidable event in the practice — are treated as an incoming task rather than as feedback about what the filing should have contained.

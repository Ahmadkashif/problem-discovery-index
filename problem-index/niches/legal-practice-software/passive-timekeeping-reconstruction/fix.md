# Nobody Measures the Leak They Are Selling Against

**Niche:** [[niches/legal-practice-software/passive-timekeeping-reconstruction/profile|Passive Timekeeping & Billable Reconstruction]]
**Industry:** [[industries/legal-practice-software|Legal Practice Software]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The entire category is sold on recovering unbilled time, and no firm knows its own unbilled fraction, because measuring it requires comparing recorded time against time actually worked and nobody has ever put those two numbers side by side.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #hypothesis-testing #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #revenue-impact #worker-facing #quick-win #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in legal timekeeping is fighting to produce a reconstructed timesheet a lawyer accepts with minimal editing — and whoever gets the accepted-unedited share highest takes the account.

## The Problem
A vendor tells a managing partner he is losing 25% of his billable hours. He believes it, because it matches his instinct, and he buys the software. Two years later he cannot say whether it helped, because he never knew his own number and does not know it now. The industry statistic is a survey estimate applied to a firm that has never been measured. Every purchase decision, every adoption push and every evaluation in this category rests on a figure nobody has computed for the firm making the decision.

## Why It's Still Broken
Measuring it means comparing recorded time to an independent estimate of time worked, which requires the passive capture that the firm has not adopted — a circularity that has kept the number unknown. It is also a delicate number internally: a per-lawyer unbilled fraction looks like a surveillance metric and will be received as one if it is introduced carelessly, so cautious firms avoid it. And vendors are content with the survey figure, which is large, unfalsifiable and favourable.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Measure it at the firm level, deliberately and with consent, before deploying anything. A two-week baseline with passive capture running and no change to timekeeping practice yields the comparison: recorded hours against observed working hours, by lawyer and by matter type, with the gaps located — which days, which activities, which matters leak. Report it in aggregate to the firm and individually only to the lawyer it concerns, which is the design decision that determines whether the programme survives its first month. Then re-measure after any intervention, so the firm can say what changed rather than believing a vendor's claim. The same baseline immediately shows where the leak is concentrated, and it is usually concentrated: short interruptions, phone calls, and work done away from the desk, each of which needs a different remedy.

## Who Feels the Pain
Managing partners buying on a survey statistic; lawyers told they are losing time with no evidence about their own practice; and the vendors themselves, who cannot demonstrate value because the baseline was never taken.

## Impact If Fixed
A firm that knows its own unbilled fraction and where it originates can target the remedy instead of buying a timer, and can evaluate whether anything worked. Establishing the baseline is two weeks of unobtrusive measurement and is the necessary first step for every other improvement in this niche — including telling the firm honestly when the leak is smaller than the industry claims.

# The Referral That Goes Nowhere

**Niche:** [[niches/legal-practice-software/legal-aid-intake-triage/profile|Legal Aid & Access-to-Justice Intake]]
**Industry:** [[industries/legal-practice-software|Legal Practice Software]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** When a legal aid organisation cannot take a case it refers the person elsewhere, to an organisation whose current capacity it does not know, and nobody ever finds out whether the referral led to help.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #hypothesis-testing #confidence-intervals #workflow-orchestration #automation #compliance #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor serving legal aid is fighting to triage far more requests than the organisation can ever serve, defensibly and consistently, and to send the rest somewhere real — and whoever makes that triage defensible takes the contract.

## The Problem
An applicant is told the organisation cannot represent them and is given two phone numbers. One organisation stopped taking that case type six months ago; the other has a four-week intake backlog and a hearing is in nine days. The referring worker does not know either fact, because referral lists are maintained as documents and updated occasionally. The applicant calls, gets nowhere, and stops calling. The referring organisation records a referral, which in its statistics looks like a person who was helped.

## Why It's Still Broken
Nobody owns the network. Each organisation maintains its own list, funded separately, with no shared infrastructure and no obligation to publish capacity — and publishing capacity is uncomfortable, because "we are full" is a statement a grant-funded organisation is reluctant to make in writing. Outcome tracking across organisations raises real confidentiality questions that have never been worked through, so the easy answer has been not to track. And the sector's reporting counts services delivered, so a referral costs nothing to record and nothing to get wrong.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Make capacity a live fact and the referral a tracked handoff. Each participating organisation publishes what it is currently accepting — case types, counties, and whether intake is open — updated as a matter of routine rather than annually, which is a small amount of work that pays off across the whole network. Referrals are sent as records with the applicant's consent rather than as phone numbers read aloud, and the receiving organisation acknowledges receipt, which alone distinguishes a handoff from a dismissal. Outcome is closed at a coarse, privacy-preserving level — was contact made, was assistance provided — because even that is far more than exists now and avoids sharing case detail across organisations. The sector then gets its first picture of where referrals actually land and which needs have no destination at all.

## Who Feels the Pain
Applicants making calls that lead nowhere while a deadline runs; intake workers handing out numbers they suspect are stale; and executive directors reporting referral counts they know overstate what happened.

## Impact If Fixed
Live capacity data removes the most common failure immediately — referring to a closed door. Coarse outcome tracking converts referral counts from a comfort into a measurement, and the first honest picture of where referrals land is likely to be uncomfortable and is exactly the evidence the sector needs to argue for capacity. This is a coordination fix rather than a technology one, which is why it has not happened and why it is cheap.

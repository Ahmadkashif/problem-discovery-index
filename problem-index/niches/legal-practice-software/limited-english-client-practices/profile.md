# Limited-English-Client Practices

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/legal-practice-software|Legal Practice Software]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor selling to firms whose clients do not speak English is fighting to make the entire client-facing path — intake, documents, status, signature, court communication — work in the client's language without a bilingual staff member in the middle, and whoever covers that path end to end takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$200M US software spend across firms whose client base is substantially limited-English-proficient — immigration, personal injury, family, workers' compensation and consumer practice
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~6% of legal practice software revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Low — these firms buy the same software as everyone else and operate it around a language barrier the software ignores
**Target Buyer:** Practice owners at firms serving immigrant communities, and the bilingual staff who hold the practice together
**Automation Potential:** High — translation and multilingual generation are mature; the gap is that no legal platform treats language as a property of the client rather than of the interface

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A large number of American law firms serve clients who are more comfortable in Spanish, Mandarin, Vietnamese, Haitian Creole, Arabic or Portuguese than in English, and those firms are structured around bilingual staff who translate continuously and informally. Every client-facing artefact — the retainer, the intake questionnaire, the status update, the document request, the settlement explanation — passes through a person. This makes the bilingual paralegal the most load-bearing and least replaceable role in the firm, creates a bottleneck on every matter, and produces a specific risk: informal translation of legal documents by staff who are fluent but not trained translators, where a mistranslated term in a retainer or a release has consequences. The practice area is invisible to the software category because language is treated as an interface setting rather than as a property of the relationship.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Practice management platforms offer interface localisation for the lawyer, which is the wrong side of the transaction — the user who needs the language is the client. Client portals are English-only in most products. Machine translation is excellent and cheap and is used by staff informally, copy-pasted, with no record of what the client was actually shown. Court interpretation is a separate system covering hearings only. E-signature platforms offer localised interfaces and English documents. The consistent gap is that no product maintains a client-language version of the matter's communications as a first-class artefact, which means the firm has no record of what it told the client in the client's language — a documentation problem before it is a translation problem.

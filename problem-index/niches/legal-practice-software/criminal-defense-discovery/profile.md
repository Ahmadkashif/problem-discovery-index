# Criminal Defense & Digital Discovery

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/legal-practice-software|Legal Practice Software]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in criminal defense case management is fighting to make a terabyte of body-worn camera and digital discovery reviewable by one lawyer before the next hearing — and whoever gets time-to-reviewable lowest takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$320M US software spend across private criminal defense firms and public defender offices
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~9% of legal practice software revenue, with the public side funded separately and erratically
**Digital Adoption:** Low — a large share of the practice area runs on email, shared folders and external hard drives
**Target Buyer:** Private defense practice owners; IT and operations directors at public defender offices and county indigent defense programmes
**Automation Potential:** Very High — the bottleneck is media review, which is exactly what transcription and video indexing are for

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Criminal defense had a discovery problem invented for it in the last decade. A routine case that once meant a police report, some photographs and a witness list now means body-worn camera footage from six officers, dash camera, station booking video, cell phone extractions, surveillance footage and CAD logs — commonly hundreds of hours of media per case, delivered on a drive or a download link, with a hearing in three weeks. The constitutional obligation is to review it, and the practical reality is that a public defender with 120 open cases and a private practitioner on a flat fee both cannot. No other practice area has had its core workload multiplied by an order of magnitude by a change in what the other side collects, and none is worse equipped to absorb it.

## Current Tools & Gaps
The practice-area platforms — Clio and MyCase configured for criminal work, JusticeWorks, Karpel and defenderData on the public side — provide matter management, calendaring and court date tracking, and treat media as attachments. Evidence platforms are built for the prosecution side: Axon's ecosystem manages body-worn camera capture and sharing for the agencies that own it, and the sharing interface a defense lawyer sees is a download portal. eDiscovery tooling with genuine video and audio capability exists in civil litigation at civil litigation prices. The gap is stark and unusually clean: the tooling needed — transcription, speaker identification, entity and timeline extraction, cross-source synchronisation — is mature and cheap in every other industry, and this practice area, which needs it more than any other, has the least money and the least access.

# Plaintiff & Contingency Firm Platforms

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/legal-practice-software|Legal Practice Software]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is that every competitor is fighting to make a contingency firm's capital go further — but "further" means case selection at a personal injury firm and claimant throughput at a mass tort firm, which are different products. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$900M US software spend across plaintiff-side personal injury, mass tort and employment firms
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~26% of legal practice software revenue and the highest per-seat pricing in the category
**Digital Adoption:** High — contingency firms are the most software-intensive segment of small-firm legal practice, because their economics reward operational leverage directly
**Target Buyer:** Managing partners and directors of operations at contingency firms; product leads at Filevine, Litify, CASEpeer, SmartAdvocate and their competitors
**Automation Potential:** High — intake scoring, record retrieval, demand assembly and lien resolution are all structured work currently performed by paralegals

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Contingency practice inverts the economics of legal software. A firm billing hourly buys software to capture time it is giving away; a firm working on contingency has no billable hour to capture and instead buys software to deploy a fixed amount of working capital across a portfolio of cases whose outcomes are uncertain. Every decision is an investment decision: sign or decline, invest in experts or not, settle now or try. This makes contingency firms the segment most willing to pay for software and the segment whose central question — what is this case worth, and when — no vendor answers. The platforms hold the data that would answer it: matter type, jurisdiction, injury, treatment, adjuster, carrier, duration, demand, offer, and final resolution, across thousands of firms and hundreds of thousands of resolved cases.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Filevine and Litify anchor the upper end, with CASEpeer, SmartAdvocate and Assembly serving the mid-market; all offer matter management, intake pipelines, medical record tracking and demand assembly. The workflow layer is genuinely good. The analytical layer is a dashboard: case counts by stage, aging, and a settlement total. Nothing predicts. Firms therefore make their most consequential decisions — which intakes to sign, what to demand, when to accept — on the managing partner's experience, which is real but is a sample of one firm's history and is not transferable when that partner retires. Where the segment splits is what the leverage looks like, and the two sub-niches below make that split explicit rather than averaging over it.

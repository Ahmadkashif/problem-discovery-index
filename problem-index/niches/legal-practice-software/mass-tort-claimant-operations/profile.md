# Mass Tort — Claimant Operations at Scale

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/legal-practice-software|Legal Practice Software]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in mass tort software is fighting to move forty thousand claimants through record retrieval, proof of exposure and lien resolution without an operation that collapses under its own headcount — and whoever holds throughput per claimant lowest takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$300M US software and services spend across mass tort and MDL plaintiff practice
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~9% of legal practice software revenue, highly cyclical with active litigations
**Digital Adoption:** Medium-High — the largest firms have built internal systems; the mid-market runs on spreadsheets and contract labour
**Target Buyer:** Directors of operations at mass tort firms and the claims administration vendors serving MDLs
**Automation Potential:** Very High — the work is document-heavy, rule-governed and performed at a scale where a per-claimant minute is a real budget line

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Mass tort is a manufacturing problem wearing legal clothing. A firm signs tens of thousands of claimants against a product, and its economics are set not by the merits of any one case but by cost per claimant across the population: acquiring them, proving each one used the product and was injured by it, retrieving medical records from thousands of uncoordinated providers, surviving the defendant's challenges to unsupported claims, and — at the end — resolving Medicare, Medicaid and private liens before a dollar reaches anybody. Every one of those is a throughput problem. The per-case judgment that defines personal injury practice is unavailable here, which is exactly why the two cannot share a product, and why the contested capability is operational rather than predictive.

## Current Tools & Gaps
The large firms have built bespoke claimant databases; the rest use case management systems stretched past their design point, plus spreadsheets, plus offshore contract teams doing record review. Claims administrators (Archer, BrownGreer, and the settlement administration firms) run the back end of resolved MDLs competently and engage only after settlement. Lien resolution is a specialist services industry charging per claimant. Record retrieval is outsourced to vendors who fax. The two gaps that define the niche are that nothing measures cost per claimant per stage — so a firm cannot tell which part of its operation is expensive — and that proof of use and proof of injury, the two determinations everything depends on, are made by contract reviewers reading documents with no consistency measurement of any kind.

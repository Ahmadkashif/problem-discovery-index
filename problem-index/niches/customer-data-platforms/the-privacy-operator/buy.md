# Records Disposition Practice

**Niche:** [[niches/customer-data-platforms/the-privacy-operator/profile|The Privacy Operator]]
**Industry:** [[industries/customer-data-platforms|Customer Data Platforms]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Records management built certificates of destruction and verified disposition because someone has to prove it happened, and privacy fulfilment ends at an acknowledgement.
**Tags:** #compliance #workflow-orchestration #automation #evaluation-metrics #data-integration #descriptive-statistics #graph-theory #confidence-intervals
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to let one person fulfil a deletion across every downstream system within a statutory window, using an identity graph that is a set of guesses — and whoever does that turns an unverifiable obligation into a provable one.

## The Problem
Records management has a mature discipline for disposing of records provably: retention schedules, disposition authorities, certificates of destruction, chain-of-custody documentation and audit trails that survive scrutiny. Regulated industries run it because they must demonstrate that something was destroyed, not merely that they asked for it to be. Privacy request fulfilment across a customer data stack ends when a downstream system returns an acknowledgement, which is a receipt for a request rather than evidence of an outcome.

## What Already Exists
Retention schedules with disposition authorities; certificates of destruction; chain-of-custody documentation; disposition audit trails; and legal hold management.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to distributed systems the organisation partly does not control. It requires: (1) verification across systems that expose no way to confirm a deletion, which is the practical obstacle — records management assumes custody and here the data is in forty vendors' systems with varying capability; (2) an identity scope that is probabilistic, so what constitutes the record set is itself uncertain, which has no records management analogue where a file either is or is not the file; (3) derived and aggregated data that contains the person's contribution without containing their record, which the discipline handles for documents and not for a model's parameters; (4) statutory deadlines measured in days rather than schedules measured in years, requiring automation rather than process; and (5) volumes that make per-request manual handling impossible at scale.

## Target Customer
Privacy and compliance operations, customer data platform vendors, and records management vendors for whom distributed customer data is an unserved domain.

## Impact If Solved
Records management proves destruction because someone must, and privacy fulfilment collects receipts for requests. A probabilistic identity scope means the record set itself is uncertain, which the discipline has no analogue for.

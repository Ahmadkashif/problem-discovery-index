# The Privacy Operator

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/customer-data-platforms|Customer Data Platforms]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to let one person fulfil a deletion across every downstream system within a statutory window, using an identity graph that is a set of guesses — and whoever does that turns an unverifiable obligation into a provable one.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$350M US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~7% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Low — a checklist and a deadline
**Target Buyer:** Privacy and compliance leadership
**Automation Potential:** High — propagation and verification are mechanisable

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A privacy operator has thirty days to delete everything about a person from every downstream system, using an identity graph that is a set of probabilistic guesses about who that person is. Both halves of that are hard and they compound: the operator cannot be sure which records belong to the person, and cannot verify that the systems they propagated the request to actually did anything. They sign off regardless, because the deadline is statutory. This is distinct from identity accuracy because the consequence here is legal rather than commercial, and the failure mode — deleting too much or too little — has no good side.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Request intake workflows, deletion propagation to connected systems, a checklist of downstream tools, and manual follow-up. The gaps: identity scope determined by a probabilistic graph; propagation to systems that acknowledge without acting; no verification that deletion occurred; backups and derived data unaddressed; and no evidence package for a regulator.

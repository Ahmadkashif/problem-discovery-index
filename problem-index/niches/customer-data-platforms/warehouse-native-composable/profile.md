# Warehouse-Native Composable

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/customer-data-platforms|Customer Data Platforms]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to activate from a warehouse the vendor never touches, at the reliability and latency a customer-facing use case needs — and whoever does that takes the organisations that already own their data.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$500M US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~10% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** High — growing and incomplete
**Target Buyer:** Data platform and engineering leadership
**Automation Potential:** Very High — sync and modelling are engineering

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The composable model's premise is that the organisation's warehouse already contains the customer data, modelled and governed, and the vendor's job is to activate from it without holding a copy. The contest is over sync reliability, latency, modelling support, governance integration and the operational burden the model places on the customer's own team. It sells to a data organisation rather than to a marketing one, and the vendor is explicitly not the system of record — a structurally different business from the packaged model.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Reverse activation tools syncing warehouse tables to destinations, transformation frameworks for modelling, and governance inherited from the warehouse. The gaps: identity resolution largely absent; real-time activation limited by warehouse latency; sync failures visible only downstream; modelling burden on the customer's team; and no unified profile concept beyond whatever the customer built.

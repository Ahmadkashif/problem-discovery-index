# The Platform Architecture

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/customer-data-platforms|Customer Data Platforms]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** This niche is not terminal — the packaged platform and the warehouse-native stack compete on different things for different buyers, and they are stated separately in the sub-niches below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.0B US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~20% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** High — two models, one category name
**Target Buyer:** Data platform leadership
**Automation Potential:** High — both models are engineering contests

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The category's architecture has been overturned. The warehouse became the place customer data lives, and reverse activation tools work from it directly, which removes the packaged platform's central justification. The incumbents have repositioned toward identity, governance and activation breadth. Where the customer profile is assembled and from what is now the defining architectural question, and it determines who a vendor sells to, what they are accountable for, and whether they hold the data at all.

It is also **not terminal**. The label names a product shape rather than a contest. The packaged model competes on breadth of collection, reliability of the pipeline, number of activation destinations, and time to value for an organisation with no data team — a coverage and operability contest. The warehouse-native model competes on activating data the organisation already governs, where the fight is over modelling, governance, sync reliability and explicitly not owning the customer's data — a different proposition, a different buyer and different vendors. The two contests are stated in the sub-niches.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Packaged platforms with collection, identity, storage and activation; reverse activation tools working from the warehouse; hybrid repositioning by incumbents. The gaps: a category name covering two different products; buyers comparing incompatible offerings; identity treated as a feature in one model and a gap in the other; governance split across the boundary; and no clear guidance on which model suits which organisation.

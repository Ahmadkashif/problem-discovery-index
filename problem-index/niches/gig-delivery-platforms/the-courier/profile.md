# The Courier

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/gig-delivery-platforms|Gig Delivery Platforms]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Whether the person whose entire working life is governed by these systems has any instrument for understanding or planning around them.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$10.8B — 12% of US gross order value
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~12%
**Digital Adoption:** Low — an app that issues instructions and reports totals
**Target Buyer:** Couriers directly, worker organisations, and third-party tooling vendors
**Automation Potential:** High — nearly everything needed is a derivation from data the courier or the platform already has

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche

Several million people deliver on these platforms in a given year, many of them full-time, and the app they work through is built to dispatch them rather than to help them. It shows offers, a map, and a running earnings total. It does not show net earnings after vehicle cost, which zones and hours are actually worth working, what their realised rate has been, when their vehicle costs will bite, or what their tax position is.

The niche is distinct because the subject is the courier's own operation rather than the platform's mechanics, and because a large part of it is buildable without platform cooperation. Couriers work across two or three apps, have their own trip data, and a substantial third-party tooling ecosystem already exists — mileage trackers, earnings aggregators, offer screeners — built by people who deliver.

## Current Tools & Gaps

Platform apps show gross earnings by day and week, sometimes with a per-delivery breakdown. Third-party tools fill gaps: Everlance, Stride and similar for mileage and tax, Gridwise and Solo for multi-app earnings aggregation and some zone guidance, plus a large informal layer of spreadsheets, forums and local knowledge about which merchants and zones to avoid.

The gaps are net accounting, forecasting and comparison. No tool tells a courier their realised net rate per engaged hour by zone and hour with any precision, because the cost side requires trip-level mileage that most tools capture crudely. None forecasts which hours next week are worth working. And none compares across platforms on the basis that matters — net per engaged hour rather than gross per delivery — which is the single most valuable question for someone deciding where to log in.

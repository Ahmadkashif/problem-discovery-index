# Offer Construction & the Accept Decision

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/gig-delivery-platforms|Gig Delivery Platforms]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Whether the number a courier is shown, and the seconds they have to judge it, bear any reliable relationship to what the work will actually be worth.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$27B — 30% of US gross order value across food, grocery and local delivery
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~30%
**Digital Adoption:** Very high and deliberately opaque — every component is algorithmic and none is disclosed
**Target Buyer:** Platform marketplace and pay-model leadership
**Automation Potential:** Fully automated already; the contest is over what is computed and what is shown

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche

The offer is the entire interface between a multi-billion-dollar allocation system and the person doing the work. It states a guaranteed amount and an estimated time, arrives with a countdown, and the courier accepts or declines dozens of times a shift.

Everything that determines whether that was a good decision is absent from it. The amount's composition is undisclosed. The time estimate omits merchant wait, which is frequently the largest single component. Nothing states the vehicle cost, so the number is gross where the courier's decision needs net. And the acceptance rate itself is often an input to future offer quality, which means declining a bad offer has a cost the courier cannot quantify either.

This is a distinct niche from dispatch because dispatch decides who to offer to and offer construction decides what they are told. The two are built by different teams with different objectives, and the gap between what dispatch knows and what the offer says is the whole subject.

## Current Tools & Gaps

Every platform constructs offers algorithmically from base pay, distance, expected effort, market conditions and promotional overlays, refined continuously against acceptance rates. Time estimates come from routing models that are accurate on the driving portion. Several platforms now display an expected hourly rate for batched offers, and pay model disclosure has been forced into some jurisdictions by statute.

The gaps are consistent across the category: the estimate excludes waiting, the amount is gross, the composition is hidden, and no offer states the probability that it will turn out materially worse than described. Every one of these quantities is computed or computable inside the platform at the moment the offer is generated.

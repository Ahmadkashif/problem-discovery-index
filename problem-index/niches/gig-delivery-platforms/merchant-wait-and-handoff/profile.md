# Merchant Wait & Handoff

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/gig-delivery-platforms|Gig Delivery Platforms]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Whether the minutes a courier spends standing in a lobby are measured, paid for, and attributed to the party who can reduce them.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$10.8B — 12% of US gross order value
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~12%
**Digital Adoption:** Low — a courier, a counter and a guess about when the order will appear
**Target Buyer:** Platform merchant operations; merchants; couriers
**Automation Potential:** High — the measurement is automatic today and goes nowhere

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche

Twenty minutes in a restaurant lobby for an order that was promised ten minutes ago, unpaid, with the clock running on an hourly rate that already assumed the order would be ready. It is the single largest source of unpaid working time in the industry and the one most fully measured by the platform — geofenced arrival to pickup scan, on every delivery, for years.

The niche is distinct because the party causing the cost, the party bearing it and the party measuring it are three different people. The merchant is late. The courier waits unpaid. The platform records it to the second and uses it for dispatch timing. No mechanism connects the three, so the merchant has no feedback and no incentive, the courier has no recourse, and the platform treats the wait as a free input to its delivery promise.

It separates cleanly from dispatch because dispatch decides when to send someone and this is about what happens when they arrive, and from offer construction because the offer merely fails to mention it.

## Current Tools & Gaps

Platforms send merchants order tickets through tablets or POS integrations, predict readiness to time dispatch, and operate merchant-facing dashboards with some operational metrics. Several have wait-time compensation programmes, generally threshold-based, sometimes requiring the courier to claim, and often capped. Merchant-side prep-time tooling exists at the larger chains.

The gaps are the three connections. Wait time is measured and not paid automatically. It is measured and not reported to the merchant against a peer benchmark. And it is measured and not fed back into the offer estimate, so the courier accepts on a number that excludes it. Each is a routing problem over an existing measurement rather than a new capability.

# Pay Composition Disclosure

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/gig-delivery-platforms|Gig Delivery Platforms]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Whether a courier is told what the number they are being offered is made of.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$10.8B — 40% of the offer construction niche
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~12% of US gross order value
**Digital Adoption:** Very low by choice — the components are known exactly and shown selectively
**Target Buyer:** Platform legal and compliance leadership; legislators and regulators
**Automation Potential:** Trivial — no computation is required; the values already exist when the offer is generated

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche

The other half of the offer is a prediction problem. This half is not a computation at all.

When a platform generates an offer it knows, exactly and by construction, what the amount is made of: base pay, a distance or effort component, any promotional or peak-period overlay, any guarantee, and — in configurations that produced public controversy and regulatory action — an estimated customer tip that offsets rather than adds to the platform's contribution. These are variables in the pay model at the moment of generation. Displaying them requires a serialisation, not a model.

That is what makes this a distinct market rather than a feature of the other half. There is no research agenda, no dataset problem and no accuracy question. Whether the courier sees the breakdown is purely a decision about whether to show them, and the entire history of this industry's movement on it has come from legislation and litigation rather than from product roadmaps.

## Current Tools & Gaps

Disclosure exists where it has been mandated. Several jurisdictions now require pay composition breakdowns, separate display of customer tips, and in some cases minimum earnings standards with itemised reporting — and the compliance systems built to satisfy them demonstrate that the underlying data was always available. Outside those jurisdictions the offer typically shows a single guaranteed number, sometimes with a partial breakdown after completion.

The gap is that disclosure is jurisdictional patchwork rather than product, and that the post-hoc breakdown — shown after delivery, when the decision has been made — is a substantially weaker thing than disclosure at the moment of the offer. The two are frequently conflated in platform communications.

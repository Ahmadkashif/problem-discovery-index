# SKU Classification Databases Pushed to the Store Floor

**Niche:** [[niches/hazardous-waste-firms/retail-pharmacy-hazwaste/profile|Retail, Pharmacy & Healthcare Hazardous Waste]]
**Industry:** [[industries/hazardous-waste-firms|Hazardous Waste Firms]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Retailers pay for a regulatory classification of every SKU they sell, and the classification stops at headquarters.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #data-integration #compliance #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn each damaged, returned or expired item in a store or pharmacy into the right disposal decision, and whoever leaves a chain with the fewest findings per regulator audit takes the account.

## The Problem
The data to decide every disposal already exists: chemical data vendors classify each SKU from its safety data sheet for transport and waste. The store associate cannot see it.

## What Already Exists
3E and UL's WERCS maintain SKU-level classifications for retailers, used for shipping, storage and waste-programme design.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs a lookup by barcode at the point of disposal; branches for condition (intact return, leaking, empty container, partly used); state overlays where state rules are stricter; Subpart P logic for pharmacy items, separating creditable returns for the reverse distributor from hazardous waste pharmaceuticals; and container names that match the contractor's bins in that store.

## Target Customer
Chains already licensing SKU classification data, and the data vendors themselves extending to store-level delivery.

## Impact If Solved
Putting existing classifications in associates' hands turns a sunk data cost into fewer misdirected items; a 50% cut in audit findings (estimate) is the measure the chain's regulators see.

# Creditable or Waste — the Pharmacy Return Nobody Sorted

**Niche:** [[niches/hazardous-waste-firms/retail-pharmacy-hazwaste/profile|Retail, Pharmacy & Healthcare Hazardous Waste]]
**Industry:** [[industries/hazardous-waste-firms|Hazardous Waste Firms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Pharmacy staff put expired and returned drugs in one tote, and Subpart P expects them to separate what goes to a reverse distributor for credit from what is hazardous waste.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #k-nearest-neighbors #compliance #worker-facing #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn each damaged, returned or expired item in a store or pharmacy into the right disposal decision, and whoever leaves a chain with the fewest findings per regulator audit takes the account.

## The Problem
Under Subpart P, potentially creditable pharmaceuticals can go to a reverse distributor, while non-creditable hazardous waste pharmaceuticals must be managed as hazardous waste and may never be sewered. Technicians sort under time pressure, and mixed totes either lose credit or misroute waste.

## Why It's Still Broken
Reverse distributors and hazardous waste contractors run separate programmes, and the pharmacy system that knows each drug's NDC does not know its waste status or return eligibility.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Scan the NDC at the counter; return the item's credit eligibility and hazardous waste status; name the tote; log the event for both the reverse distributor and the waste contractor.

## Who Feels the Pain
Pharmacy technicians, pharmacy managers facing inspections, and chains losing manufacturer credits.

## Impact If Fixed
Recovering credit on ~5–10% of items currently misrouted to waste (estimate) and cutting misrouted waste in totes is worth ~$1,000–3,000 a year per pharmacy (estimate), ~$1–3M across a 1,000-pharmacy chain.

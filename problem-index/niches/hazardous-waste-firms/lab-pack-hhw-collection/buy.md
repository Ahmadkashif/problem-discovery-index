# University Chemical Inventory Systems as the Lab-Pack Pick List

**Niche:** [[niches/hazardous-waste-firms/lab-pack-hhw-collection/profile|Lab Pack & Household Hazardous Waste Collection]]
**Industry:** [[industries/hazardous-waste-firms|Hazardous Waste Firms]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Many universities already barcode every chemical container they buy; when it is time for a clean-out, that inventory is printed out and re-read by the lab packer.
**Tags:** #data-integration #large-language-models #k-nearest-neighbors #compliance #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to clear a laboratory's or a collection event's chemical inventory into compliant shipments, and whoever ships each inventory in the fewest drums takes the account.

## The Problem
Research institutions track containers for fire code and security reporting. Expired, orphaned and unwanted containers are known in the system, yet the clean-out starts with a walk-through and a new handwritten inventory.

## What Already Exists
Barcode-based chemical inventory platforms used by universities and hospitals track container, location, owner and quantity, and link to safety data sheets.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs an export of containers marked for disposal as a pre-classified pick list, with proposed DOT class, waste codes and drum group from each safety data sheet; reconciliation of what the lab packer actually found against the record; and the drum inventory written back so the institution's own generator-status and accumulation records update.

## Target Customer
University and hospital EHS directors with a chemical inventory system, and the lab-pack firms that serve them.

## Impact If Solved
Starting from a pick list removes ~1 day of on-site inventory per large clean-out (estimate), about a fifth of the job's labour, and makes the quote match the invoice.

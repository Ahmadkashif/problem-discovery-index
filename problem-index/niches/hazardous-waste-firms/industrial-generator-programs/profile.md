# Industrial Generator Waste Programs

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/hazardous-waste-firms|Hazardous Waste Firms]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to take a large generator's long-term disposal liability off its books, and whoever diverts the largest share of the generator's tons away from land disposal takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$4B in US technical services, transport and disposal management for large quantity generators (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~30% (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — vendors run customer portals and generators run EHS platforms, but each stream's outlet and liability history is held in spreadsheets across several vendors
**Target Buyer:** Corporate EHS director or sustainability lead at a multi-site manufacturer, chemical, pharmaceutical or aerospace company
**Automation Potential:** Medium — reporting and outlet comparison automate; vendor approval and stream decisions stay with people

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A large generator buys a programme, not a pickup: profiling, transport, outlets, on-site staff (Clean Harbors' InSite service), reporting and emergency response, usually under a national contract with two or three vendors chosen by RFP. Clean Harbors (Environmental Services revenue $5.19B in 2025), Veolia (Clean Earth added in 2026), Republic's environmental solutions unit (~11% of Republic's revenue) and WM compete with Triumvirate and regional firms. The generator's lasting exposure is CERCLA: if an outlet later becomes a Superfund site, every generator that sent waste there can share the cleanup. Generators therefore audit TSDFs before approving them and increasingly set landfill-avoidance goals, preferring destruction or recycling to land disposal. The vendor that moves more of the generator's tons to those outlets, without breaking budget, holds the account.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Vendor portals show one vendor's shipments; EHS platforms (Enablon, Cority, Sphera) store records; procurement suites hold contracts. Gaps: no single list of a generator's streams across vendors with outlet, method and liability; no comparison of the cost of moving a stream from landfill to destruction or recycling; and vendor-audit results kept in documents.

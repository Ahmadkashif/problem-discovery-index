# Burn Slots Promised by Phone

**One-liner:** Sales reps commit incinerator capacity to customers in calls and emails, operations learns about it when the trucks arrive, and deferrals land on the customers with the weakest relationships.

**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is that every serious competitor is fighting to destroy generators' high-hazard and high-heat-value wastes inside a combustion permit, but incinerator operators win by guaranteeing scarce burn capacity to the streams only an incinerator can take, while fuel blenders win by turning liquids into fuel a cement kiln will accept. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## The Problem
When capacity is tight, each sales region promises volumes to its customers without a shared view of what is already committed. The plant receives more than it can burn, storage fills, and loads are deferred, often to customers who did not shout loudest. Large generators then split their volume across competitors to protect themselves.

## Why It's Still Broken
Commitments sit in CRM notes, emails and contracts in different systems, and the plant's capacity figure is a planning assumption that ignores outages. No one owns the reconciliation.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A single commitments ledger: every promised ton by customer, stream, month and outlet, set against practical capacity net of planned outages and recent unplanned downtime, visible to sales before a promise is made. Deferrals are allocated by stated contract priority and logged.

## Who Feels the Pain
Generators whose loads are deferred; sales reps who lose accounts; plant schedulers absorbing over-commitment.

## Impact If Fixed
Keeping one national account with ~2,000 tons a year of incineration volume (estimate) protects ~$1.5–2M of annual revenue (estimate), and the ledger costs little more than a shared spreadsheet with discipline.

# Airline Revenue Management for Burn Capacity

**Niche:** [[niches/hazardous-waste-firms/commercial-incineration/profile|Commercial Hazardous Waste Incineration]]
**Industry:** [[industries/hazardous-waste-firms|Hazardous Waste Firms]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Airlines and hotels decide every day which customers get scarce capacity at what price; incinerators make the same decision without the tools.
**Tags:** #optimization-fundamentals #dynamic-programming #time-series-forecasting #bayesian-inference #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to guarantee generators a burn slot for the streams only a hazardous waste incinerator can take, and whoever honours the most committed tons through a capacity crunch takes the account.

## The Problem
Scarce burn capacity is sold through long-term contracts, project bids and spot loads at very different margins. Without a protection rule, low-margin spot work fills slots that contract customers will need next month.

## What Already Exists
Revenue management systems from airlines and hotels (PROS, IDeaS) forecast demand by fare class, set protection levels and accept or reject bookings against remaining capacity.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs: capacity in tons constrained by permit feed limits and waste compatibility rather than seats; storage-time limits that force burn within a window; contract tiers that carry a guaranteed-acceptance promise; PFAS and other test-first streams that need a trial burn slot; and deferral cost measured as the risk of losing a generator's whole account, not one booking.

## Target Customer
Pricing and capacity teams at incinerator operators and the brokers that buy capacity from them.

## Impact If Solved
Protecting contract capacity through a crunch keeps the accounts that make up most burn revenue; a 2% margin gain on ~$2.5B of incineration revenue (estimate) is ~$50M a year across the sector.

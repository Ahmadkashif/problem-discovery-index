# The Outage the Customer Hears About Last

**Niche:** [[niches/hazardous-waste-firms/commercial-incineration/profile|Commercial Hazardous Waste Incineration]]
**Industry:** [[industries/hazardous-waste-firms|Hazardous Waste Firms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** When a kiln goes down, the plant knows in minutes and the generator whose drums are waiting finds out when its truck does not come.
**Tags:** #time-series-forecasting #large-language-models #workflow-orchestration #data-integration #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to guarantee generators a burn slot for the streams only a hazardous waste incinerator can take, and whoever honours the most committed tons through a capacity crunch takes the account.

## The Problem
Unplanned outages and repeated automatic waste feed cut-offs cut capacity for days. Customer service learns second-hand, and generators with storage-time limits of their own scramble to find another outlet or apply for an extension.

## Why It's Still Broken
Plant events live in the control system and maintenance logs; customer commitments live in the ERP. Nobody connects an outage to the list of generators it will affect.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Link outage and feed cut-off events to forecast capacity and to the commitments scheduled against it, then draft notices to affected generators with a revised date or an alternative outlet the same day.

## Who Feels the Pain
Generators' EHS managers facing their own accumulation limits; customer service reps; the plant manager fielding escalations.

## Impact If Fixed
A same-day notice gives each affected generator days, not hours, to re-route. Saving one large account a year (~$1–2M of revenue (estimate)) pays for the link many times.

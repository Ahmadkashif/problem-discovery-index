# A Site-Level Status Meter Fed by the Route Truck

**Niche:** [[niches/hazardous-waste-firms/small-quantity-generators/profile|Small & Very Small Quantity Generators]]
**Industry:** [[industries/hazardous-waste-firms|Hazardous Waste Firms]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** The collector's driver weighs and records every container he picks up from a small shop, yet nobody turns those records into the shop's generator status and accumulation clock.
**Tags:** #change-point-detection #time-series-forecasting #descriptive-statistics #compliance #worker-facing #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep a small generator compliant on a scheduled pickup it never has to think about, and whoever keeps the most customer sites free of accumulation-limit citations takes the account.

## The Problem
A body shop or dental office does not know its monthly generation, so it does not know whether it is a very small or small quantity generator this month, or how close each container is to its accumulation limit. An inspector does the arithmetic from its manifests.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Branch route businesses sell pickups by the visit; compliance tracking for thousands of small accounts was never priced, and the data sits in route and billing systems built for invoicing.

## What to Build
A customer-facing meter per site: monthly quantities from route pickups and e-Manifest records, inferred status, the accumulation clock on each container type, upcoming duties (training, re-notification in 2029) and a forecast of when the next pickup is needed. It drives route frequency and gives the owner a one-page compliance record for an inspector.

## Target Customer
Branch and route-service managers at Safety-Kleen, Heritage-Crystal Clean and regional collectors, who sell it as a premium tier to their small accounts.

## Impact If Built
At ~$10–20 a month per site (estimate) across 50,000 small accounts (estimate), the tier earns ~$6–12M a year for a large route business, and fewer citations reduce churn.

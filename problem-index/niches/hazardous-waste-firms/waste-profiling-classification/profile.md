# Waste Profiling & Waste-Code Classification

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/hazardous-waste-firms|Hazardous Waste Firms]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn a generator's safety data sheets, process notes and lab results into an approvable waste profile, and whoever gets the highest share of profiles approved on first submission takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$250M in profile preparation and review time at generators, consultants and TSDF technical services, plus profile software (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~2% (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — profiles are submitted through web forms, but their content is assembled by hand from PDFs, old profiles and phone calls
**Target Buyer:** Technical services director at a TSDF; EHS manager at a generator; environmental consultants preparing profiles for clients
**Automation Potential:** High for drafting and completeness checks; certification stays with the generator and approval with the chemist

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The profile is the document every later step relies on: it decides acceptance, routing, the manifest description, land-disposal treatment and the Biennial Report entry. It is produced by the people least equipped to write it, generators' EHS coordinators, and reviewed by approval chemists. Many TSDFs require annual recertification (estimate), so the same streams are re-described every year. Profile software (WASTELINQ, Wastebits, Terralink's TDXConnect (search summary)) supplies forms and routing. Consultants (see [[industries/environmental-consultants|Environmental Consultants]]) and the generator's EHS platform compete for the drafting. The contest is first-pass approval, because a returned profile stalls a waste shipment while the generator's own accumulation clock runs.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Web profile forms; safety data sheet libraries; the generator's last profile as a template. Gaps: no drafting from the safety data sheet and process description; completeness checks only for empty fields, not implausible ones; recertification by copy and paste.

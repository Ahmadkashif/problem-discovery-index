# The Unmatched Pool

**Niche:** [[niches/music-distribution-platforms/rights-matching-and-metadata/profile|Rights Matching & Metadata]]
**Industry:** [[industries/music-distribution-platforms|Music Distribution Platforms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Money sits unmatched for years and is then distributed by market share to people who did not write the songs.
**Tags:** #compliance #quick-win #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #data-integration #confidence-intervals #automation #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to make the money from a stream reach the people who own the recording and wrote the song — and the contest splits cleanly enough that it is not terminal.

## The Problem
Royalties whose composition cannot be matched to a writer accumulate. After a holding period they are distributed according to market share, which sends them to the parties with the largest catalogues rather than to the writers who earned them. The independent artist whose registration was incomplete loses their money twice: once by not receiving it and again by watching it go to someone else. The mechanics are public, the amounts are substantial, and the individual artist has almost no route to claim.

## Why It's Still Broken
The holding-and-distributing mechanism was designed to prevent money sitting forever, so the default outcome favours the largest catalogues — a rule written to avoid one problem created an incentive against solving the other. Claiming requires an artist to know the money exists and to navigate a process built for administrators. Distributors are not compensated for claims on their artists' behalf. And nobody reports how much of a given distributor's catalogue is unmatched.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Find the money and claim it. Report each artist's unmatched exposure by identifying their works with incomplete registration, which is the fix and uses data the distributor holds. Cross-reference against the published unmatched data where it is available, since matching a distributor's catalogue against a public pool is a mechanical join nobody runs. Claim on behalf of artists as a service, because the individual artist cannot and the distributor can. Prompt the specific registration fix rather than telling the artist their data is incomplete, as a precise instruction is actionable and a warning is not. Report the distributor's own unmatched rate, which is competitive information and a genuine quality signal. Prioritise by amount, since the exposure is concentrated. Fix the registration at source once identified so the leak stops rather than recurring. Tell artists plainly how the pool works, as most do not know and cannot act on what they do not understand. Track claims recovered, which is the number that justifies the service and is also a marketing asset. And push for shorter holding periods and better reporting collectively, since the rule is the structural cause.

## Who Feels the Pain
Writers whose money goes to strangers; independent artists with no route to claim; distributors whose artists blame them; and an industry whose default rule redistributes upward.

## Impact If Fixed
A rule written to avoid money sitting forever created an incentive against solving the matching problem. Joining a distributor's catalogue against the published unmatched data is mechanical, and claiming on the artist's behalf is a service nobody offers.

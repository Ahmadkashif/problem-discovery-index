# Rights Matching & Metadata

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/music-distribution-platforms|Music Distribution Platforms]]
**Category:** 🔵 High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to make the money from a stream reach the people who own the recording and wrote the song — and the contest splits cleanly enough that it is not terminal.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$420M US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~28% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Low — a form filled in by an artist
**Target Buyer:** Data and rights leadership
**Automation Potential:** Very High — matching and capture, both unexploited

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A stream generates money that must reach a recording owner and, separately, the writers and publishers of the underlying composition. Those rights are administered by different systems with different identifiers and no shared key, and the join between them depends on metadata supplied by whoever uploaded the track. When it is wrong, the money accumulates unmatched and is eventually distributed to people who did not write the song. This is the category's largest contest and it is two: matching across existing corpora, and capturing correctly at the source.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Upload forms, validation rules, identifier registries and manual claims. The gaps: no systematic matching across corpora; capture relies on artists understanding rights concepts; unmatched money not traced back to its cause; and the distributor's own corpus used only to validate its own uploads.

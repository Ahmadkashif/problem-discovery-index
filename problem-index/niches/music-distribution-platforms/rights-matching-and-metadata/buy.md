# Identifier Systems From Other Rights Industries

**Niche:** [[niches/music-distribution-platforms/rights-matching-and-metadata/profile|Rights Matching & Metadata]]
**Industry:** [[industries/music-distribution-platforms|Music Distribution Platforms]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Publishing, film and securities all built identifier registries that actually join, and music has several that do not join to each other.
**Tags:** #data-integration #graph-theory #compliance #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #automation #word-embeddings #descriptive-statistics
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to make the money from a stream reach the people who own the recording and wrote the song — and the contest splits cleanly enough that it is not terminal.

## The Problem
Other rights industries solved identity. Book publishing has an identifier that reliably resolves a work and its editions. Film has registries linking titles, versions and rights holders. Securities have identifiers that join across every system that touches a trade. Each required a central authority, a registration discipline and an obligation to use them. Music has multiple identifier systems for recordings, works, parties and releases, and the joins between them are incomplete by design and by history.

## What Already Exists
Work and edition identifier registries; party and contributor identifiers; registration authorities with governance; mandatory identifier use in transaction systems; and cross-registry resolution services.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to an industry with many registries and no authority. It requires: (1) resolution across registries that were built separately and have no shared key, so the join must be inferred rather than looked up — this is the substantive difference; (2) registration performed by artists and distributors rather than by professional registrars, so data quality at entry is the binding constraint; (3) a composition that may have many recordings and a recording that may embody several compositions, which is a many-to-many structure simpler industries avoid; (4) splits that are percentages among parties rather than a single owner, adding a layer no book identifier needs; and (5) no party with the authority to mandate anything, so adoption must be earned commercially.

## Target Customer
Rights and data leadership, registries and collecting organisations, publishers and labels, and music data vendors.

## Impact If Solved
Other rights industries built registries that join because an authority required it. Music must infer the join instead, which makes the distributors' matched corpus the closest thing to an authority the industry has.

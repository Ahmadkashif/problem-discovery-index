# Registered in One Territory

**Niche:** [[niches/music-distribution-platforms/publishing-administration/profile|Publishing Administration]]
**Industry:** [[industries/music-distribution-platforms|Music Distribution Platforms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The song earns in forty countries and is registered in one, and the rest of the money goes nowhere.
**Tags:** #quick-win #workflow-orchestration #automation #compliance #evaluation-metrics #revenue-impact #data-integration #descriptive-statistics
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to register a composition correctly in every territory and collect what it earns from societies that each work differently — and whoever makes that work for an artist with four songs opens a revenue stream most of them never see.

## The Problem
An artist registers their work with their home society, which is the one they know about. Their music streams globally. The composition earnings generated in other territories are collected by those territories' societies, which have no registration linking the work to this writer, so the money either sits unmatched or flows through reciprocal arrangements that require the home registration to be complete and correct — which it frequently is not. The artist sees a fraction of what they earned and assumes that is what streaming pays.

## Why It's Still Broken
Registration is a one-time action the artist performs where they live, so the territorial dimension never comes up — a process framed as joining a society does not prompt anyone to think globally. The reciprocal arrangements are assumed to work and partly do. Nobody tells the artist their earnings are territorial. And the distributor, which knows exactly where the streams occurred, does not connect that to the registration.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Use the streaming data to drive the registration. Report to the artist where their streams actually occurred, which is the fix and is data the distributor already has — most artists have never seen it and it immediately makes the territorial issue concrete. Flag the territories with material streaming and no corresponding registration coverage. Explain reciprocal collection plainly, since artists assume it is automatic and complete and it is neither. Check the home registration is complete and correct, as it is the precondition for everything reciprocal. Prompt the specific registration action per territory rather than a general warning, because a precise instruction is actionable. Estimate the money at stake per territory, which turns an administrative chore into a decision. Track whether earnings appear after a registration is made, so the fix is verified rather than assumed. Prioritise by streaming volume, since the exposure is concentrated in a few territories for most artists. Offer to handle it rather than instructing the artist, as the administrative burden is exactly why it does not happen. And report registration coverage as a metric, which nobody currently produces.

## Who Feels the Pain
Writers collecting a fraction of what they earned; artists who conclude streaming pays nothing; societies holding money with no claimant; and distributors whose artists blame the platform for the missing half.

## Impact If Fixed
A process framed as joining a society does not prompt anyone to think globally, so registration stops at the home territory. Showing an artist where their streams actually occurred makes the gap concrete, and the distributor already has the data.

# Collecting the Other Half

**Niche:** [[niches/music-distribution-platforms/publishing-administration/profile|Publishing Administration]]
**Industry:** [[industries/music-distribution-platforms|Music Distribution Platforms]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** The artist wrote the song and is owed money on both sides of it, and only one side has a system that finds them.
**Tags:** #workflow-orchestration #data-integration #automation #compliance #evaluation-metrics #revenue-impact #confidence-intervals #descriptive-statistics
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to register a composition correctly in every territory and collect what it earns from societies that each work differently — and whoever makes that work for an artist with four songs opens a revenue stream most of them never see.

## The Problem
Distribution solved the recording side for independent artists: upload, deliver, get paid. The composition side was never solved for them. It requires registering the work with the right bodies in the right territories, maintaining those registrations, chasing statements, reconciling what arrives against what should have arrived, and claiming what is missing. For an artist with a handful of songs, the administrative burden exceeds the money in prospect unless somebody automates it, and mostly nobody does.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Publishing administration was built for catalogues, so the economics assume enough works to justify a person — an operation priced per catalogue cannot serve an artist with four songs and nobody rebuilt it for that scale. The processes are per-society and manual. The money per artist is small even where the aggregate is large. And distributors treat it as an adjacent business rather than as the other half of the one they are in.

## What to Build
Automate registration and collection at independent-artist scale. Register the composition automatically from the release data already captured, which is the core and removes the step most artists never take. Cover the territories that matter rather than only the home one, since streaming revenue is global and registration is not. Track what should have been earned against what arrived, because the gap is the claim and nobody computes it. Reconcile society statements automatically, as they arrive in formats designed for administrators and are ignored by artists. Claim the shortfall on the artist's behalf, which is the service and is what justifies a fee. Maintain registrations as splits and affiliations change, since a stale registration silently stops paying. Handle the artist who is unaffiliated by guiding them through joining, as that is the precondition and is a barrier most never cross. Explain the two royalty streams plainly, because a large share of artists do not know the composition side exists. Report what has been collected and what remains outstanding, which is the transparency this side has never had. And price it so that it works at small catalogue scale, since that is the entire point.

## Target Customer
Rights administration leadership, independent songwriters, collecting societies receiving better registrations, and publishing administration vendors serving catalogues.

## Impact If Built
An operation priced per catalogue cannot serve an artist with four songs, and nobody rebuilt it for that scale. Registering automatically from data the distributor already holds removes the step most independent writers never take.

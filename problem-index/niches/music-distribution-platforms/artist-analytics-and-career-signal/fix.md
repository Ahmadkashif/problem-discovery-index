# A Dashboard of Counts

**Niche:** [[niches/music-distribution-platforms/artist-analytics-and-career-signal/profile|Artist Analytics & Career Signal]]
**Industry:** [[industries/music-distribution-platforms|Music Distribution Platforms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The artist opens their analytics, sees a number that went up, and has no idea what to do about it.
**Tags:** #quick-win #descriptive-statistics #worker-facing #evaluation-metrics #automation #confidence-intervals #change-point-detection #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn the earliest visible evidence of an artist's trajectory into something the artist can act on — and whoever surfaces it first holds the signal the whole industry pays to discover late.

## The Problem
The analytics page shows streams by service, by month, by track and by country. Every figure is accurate and none of it tells the artist anything they can act on. They cannot tell whether their growth is good for an artist at their stage, whether the spike last month was a playlist that has now ended, whether the city at the top of the list is worth playing, or whether anything they did contributed. So they look at it, feel briefly encouraged or discouraged, and close it.

## Why It's Still Broken
The dashboard reports the dimensions the upstream data contains, so its structure is the data's rather than the artist's question — a view organised around available fields answers none of the questions a person actually has. Context requires comparison across artists, which nobody assembles. The audience is assumed to be able to interpret. And nobody asked artists what they wanted to know.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Answer the questions instead of showing the fields. Tell the artist whether their growth is typical, strong or weak for an artist at their stage, which is the fix and requires only a cohort comparison the distributor can compute. Annotate the chart with what happened — a playlist added, a release, a promotion — since an unexplained spike teaches nothing and an explained one teaches a lot. Say when a spike has ended rather than leaving it in the trend, because artists routinely plan on a peak that is already over. Name the cities worth paying attention to, rather than listing forty countries by volume. Identify the best-performing track relative to its promotion, as that is directly actionable. Report retention of new listeners rather than only their arrival, which is the difference between an audience and a moment. Say plainly when the numbers are too small to conclude anything, since false confidence is the more common harm at this scale. Lead with a short written summary instead of a grid, as that is what the audience will read. Ask artists what decisions they are trying to make, because the current product was designed without doing so. And keep the raw data available for the minority who want it.

## Who Feels the Pain
Artists making career decisions with no usable information; managers reconstructing context from screenshots; distributors whose most-visited product teaches nothing; and an industry where the earliest signal reaches everyone except the person it is about.

## Impact If Fixed
A view organised around available fields answers none of the questions a person actually has. A cohort comparison and event annotation are computable today and turn an accurate chart into something an artist can act on.

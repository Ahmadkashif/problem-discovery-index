# Guided Data Entry From Tax and Benefits

**Niche:** [[niches/music-distribution-platforms/metadata-capture-at-upload/profile|Metadata Capture at Upload]]
**Industry:** [[industries/music-distribution-platforms|Music Distribution Platforms]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Tax and benefits software extracts legally consequential information from people with no expertise by asking plain questions, and music asks for a publisher.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #workflow-orchestration #evaluation-metrics #automation #worker-facing #compliance #confidence-intervals #data-integration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get correct rights data out of an artist who does not know what a publisher is — and whoever designs that capture well prevents the matching problem instead of repairing it downstream.

## The Problem
Consumer tax software solved this exact class of problem: legally consequential information, a form full of terms nobody understands, a user with no expertise and every incentive to guess. The answer was to replace the form with plain-language questions, derive the technical fields from the answers, validate against external records, and explain consequences at the point of decision. It works, it is widely used, and the underlying domain is at least as arcane as music rights.

## What Already Exists
Plain-language interview flows deriving technical fields; validation against external records; contextual explanation at the point of entry; error detection with targeted prompts; and prefill from prior filings.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to information about other people. It requires: (1) data about collaborators who are not present, so the artist is answering on behalf of others and the accuracy depends on someone not in the flow — this is the substantive difference and points toward collaborator confirmation; (2) splits as a negotiated agreement rather than a fact, which means the form is capturing a decision that may not have been made; (3) consequences that arrive years later and to a different person, removing the immediate feedback tax software has at filing; (4) no authority checking the submission, unlike a tax return; and (5) a moment of excitement rather than obligation, which changes tolerance for a long flow in both directions.

## Target Customer
Product leadership, artists and collaborators, collecting organisations, and guided-entry software vendors.

## Impact If Solved
Tax software extracts legally consequential data from non-experts by asking plain questions and deriving the rest. The new problem is that the artist is answering for collaborators who are not there, which points at confirmation rather than a better form.

## The Problem
Every industry that exchanges structured documents between many partners hit this: a shared standard with partner-specific implementation guides, versions that drift, and transactions rejected for reasons that are obvious to the receiver and opaque to the sender. Supply chain data interchange built the answer — partner profiles, validation before transmission, certification testing, acknowledgement standards and change notification conventions.

## What Already Exists
Partner-specific implementation profiles; pre-transmission validation against profiles; certification and conformance testing; functional acknowledgement standards; and version and change management practice.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to partners who do not certify and do not announce changes. It requires: (1) rules inferred from behaviour rather than published in an implementation guide, which is the substantive difference and makes rejection history the primary documentation; (2) a release date that cannot move, so a rejection has a hard commercial consequence rather than a delay; (3) media assets as well as structured data, since artwork and audio have their own conformance rules; (4) an end user who is an artist rather than a trading partner, so the validation must produce instructions they can act on; and (5) no certification process, meaning conformance is discovered in production.

## Target Customer
Integration leadership, streaming services, standards bodies, and data interchange platform vendors.

## Impact If Solved
Interchange solved this with partner profiles and pre-transmission validation because partners published guides. Here the rules must be inferred from rejections, which makes rejection history the specification and a shared observation set genuinely valuable.

# Penalised and Not Told Why

**Niche:** [[niches/music-distribution-platforms/stream-manipulation-detection/profile|Stream Manipulation Detection]]
**Industry:** [[industries/music-distribution-platforms|Music Distribution Platforms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The artist's release was removed for artificial streaming and nobody will tell them which streams or when.
**Tags:** #compliance #quick-win #worker-facing #evaluation-metrics #automation #descriptive-statistics #workflow-orchestration #confidence-intervals
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to identify artificial streams before the penalty lands and to tell a wrongly accused artist why — and whoever detects accurately and explains defensibly stops the enforcement falling on the innocent.

## The Problem
An enforcement notice arrives. The artist is told their release was removed or their royalties withheld because of artificial streaming activity. They are not told which streams were flagged, from what period, on what basis, or what they should do differently. Many of them paid a promotion service in good faith. They cannot contest what they cannot see, the distributor passes on a message it did not author, and the artist concludes the industry is arbitrary.

## Why It's Still Broken
Detection detail is withheld to avoid helping evasion, so the notice contains a policy citation and nothing else — a disclosure rule designed against the fraudster is applied identically to the artist who was defrauded. The distributor is a relay with no detail of its own. Appeals have no evidentiary basis and therefore rarely succeed. And nobody measures how many enforcements hit artists who bought a bad service.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Say what can be said and build a usable appeal. Provide the period and the scale of the flagged activity even where the method is withheld, which is the fix and gives the artist something to work with without teaching evasion. Tell the artist what commonly causes this, since the purchased-promotion case is the majority and naming it lets honest artists identify their own situation. Ask the artist what promotion they bought, as that is both diagnostic and builds the intelligence about which services are fraudulent. Build an appeal that accepts evidence and can actually succeed, because an appeal that never succeeds is a formality. Distinguish first occurrence from repeat, since treating them identically is what makes the system feel arbitrary. Publish guidance on promotion services in advance, which prevents far more than any enforcement corrects. Warn on anomalous growth before the penalty where the distributor can see it. Report enforcement volumes and appeal outcomes, which nobody publishes and which would discipline the whole process. Give the artist a named route to a person, as the current dead end is the core grievance. And press the services for better notice, since the distributor is relaying a message it cannot defend.

## Who Feels the Pain
Artists penalised for buying a service they were told was legitimate; support agents relaying notices they cannot explain; distributors held responsible for detections they did not make; and honest artists whose pool is drained by the fraud that does succeed.

## Impact If Fixed
A disclosure rule designed against the fraudster is applied identically to the artist who was defrauded. Giving the period and scale, and naming the purchased-promotion case, lets honest artists act without teaching anyone to evade.

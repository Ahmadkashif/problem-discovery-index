# The Researcher

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/bug-bounty-platforms|Bug Bounty Platforms]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Whether a week of skilled work has a knowable expected value, or a payoff decided by who submitted first and how someone rated it afterwards.

## Profile

**Market Size:** ~$180M
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~12%
**Digital Adoption:** Low — a reputation score and a payout history
**Target Buyer:** Independent researchers; platforms competing for supply
**Automation Potential:** High for targeting information, none for the research

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche

A researcher spends a week on a genuine vulnerability. Someone submitted the same thing that morning. The payment is zero.

That is the defining economic fact of this side of the market, and everything else follows from it. Duplicates are the most common source of grievance in the community. Payment depends on being first, on a severity assessment made afterwards by the party who pays, and on scope interpretations decided after the work. A researcher cannot compute the expected value of a week before spending it, on any programme, ever.

The people absorbing this are the product. A bounty platform's marketplace is worth exactly what its researcher population can find, and the strongest researchers are the ones with the most alternatives — full-time security roles, consulting, private engagements, or simply better-run programmes. Every friction in the payoff falls hardest on the participants the market can least afford to lose.

This is an underserved audience in the strict structural sense: they generate the value, are not the paying customer, are independent participants across many jurisdictions with no collective structure, and are bound by terms that limit what they can say publicly about how they were treated.

## Current Tools & Gaps

A reputation score and a submission history per platform. Leaderboards. Private programme invitations, which are the main economic reward for reputation. Payment infrastructure across jurisdictions, which the platforms handle genuinely well. Community forums and independent write-up culture where disclosure terms permit.

The gaps define the niche. There is no expected-value information before choosing a target — no visibility of how contested a programme is, how recently it was tested, or what a class typically pays there. Duplicate risk is unknowable and unverifiable when claimed. Reputation does not transfer between platforms, so a researcher is locked to wherever they built it. Tax and business support for people earning irregular international income is absent. There is no collective structure, no standard terms, and no mechanism by which the supply side can express a position. And nothing distinguishes a researcher who is strong in one technology area from one with a high overall count, which means matching is done by reputation rank rather than by capability.

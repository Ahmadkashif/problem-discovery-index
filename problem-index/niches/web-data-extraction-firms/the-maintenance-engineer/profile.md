# The Maintenance Engineer

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/web-data-extraction-firms|Web Data Extraction Firms]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn an unending repair queue into prioritised, mostly-automated work — and whoever does that takes the account, because this queue is where the industry's engineering capacity goes.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$220M US in loaded engineering cost
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~11% of category revenue equivalent
**Digital Adoption:** None — a queue that never empties
**Target Buyer:** Extraction operations teams and their leadership
**Automation Potential:** Very High — prioritisation and repair are both mechanical

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A maintenance engineer starts each day with a queue of broken extractions and ends it with a queue of broken extractions. They fix selectors against sites that will change again, in an order determined by whoever complained, with no signal about which of the working extractions are quietly wrong. The work is repetitive, unending, invisible when it goes well, and the only thing anyone notices when it goes badly. It is the largest consumer of engineering capacity in these firms and the role with the highest attrition, and nothing in the tooling acknowledges that the job exists as a job rather than as a series of tickets.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Monitoring for failed extractions, ticket queues, selector editors, and the engineer's accumulated familiarity with particular sites. The gaps: no prioritisation by customer impact, so a trivial target and a critical one are queued the same; no grouping of related breakages, so one site redesign produces forty tickets; no automated repair, though most fixes are mechanical; no visibility into silent breakage, which is the work they most need to do; and no accumulation of site knowledge, so it leaves with the person.

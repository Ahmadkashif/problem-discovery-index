# Structured Extraction Services

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/web-data-extraction-firms|Web Data Extraction Firms]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this sub-niche is fighting to return the right fields from a page nobody wrote a parser for, at a cost per page that beats the customer doing it themselves — and whoever does that takes the account, because the alternative is now genuinely available to the buyer.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$220M US and the fastest-growing line in the category
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~11% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Moderate — model-based extraction is displacing selectors
**Target Buyer:** Data teams who want structure rather than pages
**Automation Potential:** Very High — the whole capability is a model application

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Model-based extraction reads a rendered page and returns the requested fields without anybody writing a selector. It is more resilient to redesigns and materially more expensive per page than a selector, which makes this a correctness-against-cost contest rather than an access one. It is also the half of the business where the customer's alternative has become genuinely credible: a data team can point a model at a page themselves. That changes what the service has to be — not access to a capability, but accuracy the customer cannot easily match, at a per-page cost they cannot easily beat, with validation they would not build.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Model-based field extraction, schema-guided output, template learning from examples, and hybrid selector-plus-model approaches. The gaps: accuracy is claimed and not measured per field per source; cost per page is not optimised, so expensive models run on pages a cheap path would handle; no reuse of learned templates across customers extracting from the same site; and the quality difference against a customer's own implementation is never demonstrated, which is the comparison the buyer is silently making.

# The Same Question Answered Differently Each Time

**Niche:** [[niches/web-data-extraction-firms/the-compliance-reviewer/profile|The Compliance Reviewer]]
**Industry:** [[industries/web-data-extraction-firms|Web Data Extraction Firms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Two near-identical collection requests are refused in March and approved in September by different reviewers, and neither knew the other decision existed.
**Tags:** #compliance #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #worker-facing #hypothesis-testing #data-integration #quick-win #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to give the reviewer a precedent library and a framework instead of an unsettled legal landscape and a deadline — and whoever does that takes the account, because this decision gates every collection the firm runs.

## The Problem
In March a reviewer refuses a request to collect user-generated reviews from a site, citing personal data and terms of use. In September a different reviewer approves a materially identical request from another customer. Neither knew about the other. The firm now has an inconsistent position it cannot explain, one customer who was refused business a competitor received, and no record that would let anyone notice the contradiction — which will surface, if it surfaces at all, in the worst possible forum.

## Why It's Still Broken
Decisions live in email and in individual memory, which makes consistency a function of who happens to review. There is no structured record, so even a diligent reviewer cannot search for precedent. Reviewers rotate and the institutional memory rotates with them. And nobody audits for consistency, because there is nothing to audit.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Make prior decisions findable at the moment of decision. Record every decision in structured form — target, fields, rate, purpose, customer type, jurisdictions, outcome, conditions, reasoning — which is a form at the point of decision and is the entire precondition for consistency. Surface similar prior decisions automatically when a new request arrives, matched on the factual features rather than on text, so the reviewer sees precedent without having to remember it exists. Require a reviewer departing from precedent to say why, which is how a position evolves deliberately rather than drifting. Audit for inconsistency periodically, since contradictions are findable once the record exists and are otherwise discovered externally. Publish the firm's standing positions internally, so sales knows what will be refused before promising a customer. Version the positions as the law moves, and re-examine affected decisions when one changes. Record who applied pressure and what the commercial consequence was, which protects the reviewer and makes the pattern visible. And treat the precedent library as the asset it is, since it is the firm's accumulated understanding of an unsettled area and currently has no existence outside people's heads.

## Who Feels the Pain
Reviewers making decisions they cannot check against their own firm's position; customers refused business a competitor was granted; and the firm, whose inconsistency is a liability it cannot see.

## Impact If Fixed
Consistency is currently a function of which reviewer is on duty. A structured record plus feature-matched retrieval of prior decisions makes precedent available at the moment of decision, and departure-with-reason is how a position evolves deliberately rather than drifting.

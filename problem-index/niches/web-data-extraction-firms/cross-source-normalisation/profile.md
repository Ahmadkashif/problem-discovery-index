# Cross-Source Normalisation

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/web-data-extraction-firms|Web Data Extraction Firms]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn a thousand sites' worth of the same entity into one coherent dataset — and whoever does that takes the account, because that reconciliation is the work customers assume they are buying.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$290M US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~15% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Low — a thousand sites, a thousand shapes
**Target Buyer:** Data teams consuming multi-source extraction
**Automation Potential:** High — the mappings are learnable and reusable

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Extraction from a thousand sites produces a thousand shapes of the same entity. Prices appear with and without tax, in different currencies, with different notions of what is included. Availability is expressed as a boolean, a count, a date, or a phrase. Categories come from a thousand incompatible taxonomies. Addresses are formatted a thousand ways. The customer expected a dataset and received a thousand feeds with a shared name. Reconciliation is the work they assume is included, it is where their integration cost actually goes, and it is the capability a firm collecting from all those sites is uniquely positioned to build and has not.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Per-site field mapping, unit and currency conversion where somebody wrote it, and delivery of whatever the site provided. The gaps: no canonical schema per domain, so every customer builds one; no taxonomy crosswalk, though the firm sees every site's taxonomy; semantic differences between nominally identical fields are undocumented; entity identity across sites is left entirely to the customer; and nothing is reused between customers extracting from the same sites.

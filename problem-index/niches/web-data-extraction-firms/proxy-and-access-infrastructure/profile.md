# Proxy & Access Infrastructure

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/web-data-extraction-firms|Web Data Extraction Firms]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this sub-niche is fighting to deliver successful requests against evolving detection at the lowest cost per success — and whoever does that takes the account, because the buyer meters exactly that and switches on it.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$400M US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~20% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** High
**Target Buyer:** Extraction engineers and the firms building on top of the network
**Automation Potential:** High — routing and rotation are optimisable

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Reaching a page that does not want to be reached is an adversarial engineering and economics problem. The competitive variables are success rate against current detection, the size and quality of the address pool, geographic coverage, session persistence, and cost per successful request. The buyer is technical, meters the outcome precisely, and moves between networks on the number. Alongside that sits a question the market has largely declined to ask: where the addresses come from. Residential pools are frequently assembled through consent mechanisms buried in software installations, and the individuals whose connections carry the traffic are the least informed party in the arrangement.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Residential, mobile and datacentre address pools, rotation and session management, geographic targeting, and retry logic. The gaps: success rates are quoted in aggregate rather than per target, which is the only figure that matters; address pool provenance is not disclosed; no reporting on what share of a pool is already blocked at common targets; and no shared signal about which targets are hostile, despite every network observing it continuously.

# Where the Residential Addresses Come From

**One-liner:** Residential address pools route customer traffic through ordinary people's home connections, the consent for that is frequently buried in a free software installation, and nobody in the transaction discloses it.

## The Problem
A residential pool works because its addresses belong to real households. Those households joined by installing a free application, a browser extension or a mobile game whose terms included a clause about sharing bandwidth. Most did not read it and none understood that their connection would carry commercial data collection traffic to sites they have never visited. The buyer purchasing access does not know where the addresses came from. The target site sees a residential address and treats it as a person. The one party bearing the risk — whose address appears in the target's logs — is the household, and they are the only participant with no information at all.

## Why It's Still Broken
Pool provenance is a competitive secret and disclosing it invites scrutiny of a supply chain that will not always survive it. The consent is legally defensible in most cases and ethically thin, and the gap between those two is where the practice sits. Buyers do not ask because the answer would complicate a purchase they need to make. And the households have no visibility, no channel and no idea the arrangement exists.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Disclose the supply chain and improve the consent. Publish how addresses are sourced, including the applications through which participants joined, which is the minimum disclosure and lets a buyer make an informed decision about a supply chain they are currently funding blind. Require the consent to be genuinely informed — plainly worded, separate from other terms, explaining that commercial traffic will traverse the connection — which is a higher standard than the law strictly demands in many places and is the substance of the fix. Give participants visibility and control: how much traffic traversed their connection, the ability to leave immediately, and exclusion of categories they object to. Compensate participants directly and transparently, rather than as a free application whose value they cannot compare to what they are giving. Exclude connections where the account holder cannot have consented, such as shared or institutional networks, which is checkable and currently is not. Report pool composition to buyers, since some buyers have their own obligations and are currently unable to discharge them. Support certification of ethically-sourced pools as a product tier, which is where buyer pressure would actually come from. And be honest with target sites' operators about what a residential address means, since the whole mechanism depends on them not knowing.

## Who Feels the Pain
Households carrying commercial traffic they did not meaningfully agree to; buyers funding a supply chain they cannot see; and target sites treating automated traffic as human because the address says so.

## Impact If Fixed
The household bearing the risk is the only participant with no information. Genuinely informed, separately-obtained consent with visibility and direct compensation is the fix, and a certified ethically-sourced tier is where buyer pressure could make it commercial.

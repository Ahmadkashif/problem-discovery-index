# Response Matchback & Campaign Reporting

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/direct-mail-agencies|Direct Mail Agencies]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to credit each response to the right list, segment and version within the client's planning cycle, in numbers the client's finance team and the list owners both accept — and whoever delivers a reproducible matchback before the next circulation plan takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$500M a year in matchback, response analysis and campaign reporting fees and staff time (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~1% of combined direct mail and door-to-door spend (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — keycodes and order data are digital; matching and roll-up run on per-client SQL and spreadsheets
**Target Buyer:** Director of Analytics at the agency; circulation or direct marketing director at the mailer
**Automation Potential:** Very high

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Mail's response is mostly not traceable at the moment it happens: buyers type a URL, walk into a store or call without a code. Matchback reconstructs it afterwards by matching buyers to the mail file over a window, and every downstream decision — which lists to re-order, which versions to roll out, how deep to mail — reads from it. The work recurs for every campaign of every client with the same shape, which makes it the most mechanical analytical job in the industry and a natural candidate for a shared engine. Generic multi-touch attribution, analysed under [[industries/marketing-attribution-vendors|Marketing Attribution Vendors]], works on digital touchpoints and does not perform name-and-address matching to a mail file.

## Current Tools & Gaps
In-house SQL and analyst templates; customer data platforms' identity resolution; basic response dashboards in print-and-mail APIs (Lob, Postalytics); co-ops and list brokers' own post-campaign analyses. Gaps: results arrive after the next plan is built; credit rules differ from merge/purge priorities; no holdout calibration of matchback credit.

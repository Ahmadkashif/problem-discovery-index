# Matchback Delivered After the Next Plan

**Niche:** [[niches/direct-mail-agencies/response-matchback-reporting/profile|Response Matchback & Campaign Reporting]]
**Industry:** [[industries/direct-mail-agencies|Direct Mail Agencies]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The final matchback for a campaign lands after the following campaign's lists were ordered, so every plan is built on the campaign before last.
**Tags:** #survival-analysis #time-series-forecasting #evaluation-metrics #automation #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to credit each response to the right list, segment and version within the client's planning cycle, in numbers the client's finance team and the list owners both accept — and whoever delivers a reproducible matchback before the next circulation plan takes the account.

## The Problem
Matchback is run once, at the end of a 60–90 day window. Mailers on monthly or six-weekly cycles order lists for the next campaign well before that, so the strategist reorders lists based on the previous-but-one campaign and a few early reads.

## Why It's Still Broken
Running matchback is manual enough that analysts do it once per campaign, and early partial results are distrusted because response curves differ by source.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Automated daily matchback from the in-home date, with response-curve models per source type projecting each cell's final result from its first two or three weeks, and confidence bands that narrow as data arrives — available on the day lists are ordered.

## Who Feels the Pain
Circulation strategists ordering blind; list owners whose lists are dropped or renewed on stale results; clients paying for another round of an underperforming source.

## Impact If Fixed
Plans built on projected results from the latest campaign rather than the one before it cut a full cycle from the time it takes to stop mailing a bad source.

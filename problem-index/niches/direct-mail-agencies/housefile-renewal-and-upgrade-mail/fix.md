# Ask Strings Set by Rule of Thumb

**Niche:** [[niches/direct-mail-agencies/housefile-renewal-and-upgrade-mail/profile|Housefile Renewal & Upgrade Mail]]
**Industry:** [[industries/direct-mail-agencies|Direct Mail Agencies]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** "Last gift, 1.25 times, 1.5 times, other" is printed on millions of reply devices because it worked once, years ago, for a different file.
**Tags:** #hypothesis-testing #confidence-intervals #evaluation-metrics #quick-win #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to set each existing donor's ask amount, cadence and package so renewal and upgrade rates rise without mailing donors into lapse — and whoever gets the ask right donor by donor takes the account.

## The Problem
Ask-string rules are inherited between strategists and agencies and applied across the whole file. Donors who habitually give round numbers, donors whose last gift was an outlier, and donors who give the same amount every year all receive the same multipliers.

## Why It's Still Broken
Changing the rule risks the next appeal's revenue, and the test needed to justify a change takes several appeals to read. Agencies rotate strategists and the reasoning behind the current rule is lost.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A standing ask-string test in every appeal on a small randomised share of the file, comparing the current rule with two challengers per segment (anchor on most frequent gift versus highest gift; rounded versus exact amounts), with results accumulated and the rule updated when a challenger wins.

## Who Feels the Pain
Housefile strategists defending a rule they did not write; nonprofits leaving revenue on the reply device; donors asked for amounts that make no sense to them.

## Impact If Fixed
A continuous, low-risk test replaces folklore with evidence, and lifts average gift on every appeal once a better rule is found.

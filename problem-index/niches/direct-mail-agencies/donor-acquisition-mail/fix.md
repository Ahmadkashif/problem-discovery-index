# List Exchanges Tracked in Spreadsheets

**Niche:** [[niches/direct-mail-agencies/donor-acquisition-mail/profile|Donor Acquisition Mail]]
**Industry:** [[industries/direct-mail-agencies|Direct Mail Agencies]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Nonprofits swap donor names one for one, and the balances, usage dates and results of those swaps live in a list manager's spreadsheet.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #data-integration #workflow-orchestration #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to find first-time donors who go on to give a second gift, at an acquisition cost the client can recover from that later giving — and whoever predicts second-gift donors rather than first-gift responders takes the account.

## The Problem
Exchanges are the cheapest acquisition source: two charities trade names for one-time use rather than paying rental fees. Each trade must be balanced, scheduled to avoid both organisations mailing the same names in the same week, and later judged on results. List managers track balances and clearances in spreadsheets and email, and the nonprofit often cannot see what it owes, what it is owed, or which partners' names actually produced donors.

## Why It's Still Broken
Exchanges are arranged person to person between list managers, the volumes per trade are small, and no shared system exists between competing charities and their brokers.

## What a Fix Looks Like
An exchange ledger per nonprofit: balances by partner, usage dates, merge/purge net counts, and second-gift results by partner, with alerts when a balance is overdrawn or a partner's names stop performing.

## Who Feels the Pain
List managers reconciling trades; acquisition managers choosing partners blind; nonprofits that end up owing names they never meant to give.

## Impact If Fixed
A visible ledger turns exchanges from a favour economy into a measurable acquisition source, and retires the partners whose names never stay.

# Donor Acquisition Mail

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/direct-mail-agencies|Direct Mail Agencies]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to find first-time donors who go on to give a second gift, at an acquisition cost the client can recover from that later giving — and whoever predicts second-gift donors rather than first-gift responders takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$2B a year in nonprofit acquisition mail spend, including postage, data, production and agency fees (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~5% of combined direct mail and door-to-door spend (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium-high — co-op models and list exchanges are data-driven; the outcome they optimise is the wrong one
**Target Buyer:** Director of Donor Acquisition at the nonprofit; acquisition strategist at the fundraising agency
**Automation Potential:** High in selection and list planning

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Acquisition mail is an investment that loses money in year one and pays back only through retention, and most new donors do not give again (new-donor retention is commonly quoted near ~19%, an estimate). Names come from data cooperatives that model first-gift response from pooled giving histories, from rental lists of other charities' donors, and from one-for-one list exchanges between nonprofits, all merged and paid on net names. The contest is not who produces the highest response rate but whose names stay: a source with a lower response but a higher second-gift rate is worth more, and few models are trained on that target.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Co-op modelled selections (Wiland, Epsilon Abacus), list brokers and exchange managers, agency circulation spreadsheets. Gaps: models trained on first gift; exchange balances tracked by hand; second-gift outcomes rarely fed back to the co-op or broker.

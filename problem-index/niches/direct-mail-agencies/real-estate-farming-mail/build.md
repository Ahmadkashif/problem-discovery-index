# A Farm Share Tracker Built From MLS Data

**Niche:** [[niches/direct-mail-agencies/real-estate-farming-mail/profile|Real Estate Agent Farming Mail]]
**Industry:** [[industries/direct-mail-agencies|Direct Mail Agencies]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** An agent mails the same farm for three years without ever seeing, month by month, how many listings came up there and how many they won.
**Tags:** #survival-analysis #logistic-regression #causal-inference #evaluation-metrics #data-integration #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to show an agent that a farm area's monthly mail produced listing appointments — and whoever matches MLS listings and the agent's CRM back to the farm's mailing history keeps the agent subscribed.

## The Problem
Farming costs an agent a steady monthly sum and pays off, if at all, in a handful of listings a year. Whether the farm is working is a feeling: the agent remembers the two listings that came from it and forgets the eight that went to competitors.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Mailing services sell postcards and see no outcomes; MLS data is licensed to brokerages and agents, not to print vendors; and the agent's CRM records lead source inconsistently.

## What to Build
A tracker that takes the farm's address list and mailing history, pulls listings and sales in the farm from MLS feeds the agent is entitled to, and reports the agent's listing share over time against comparable unmailed areas. It adds a likely-to-list score per home from ownership tenure, estimated equity and life events visible in public records, so the agent can weight mailings and door-knocks toward homes nearing a sale.

## Target Customer
Individual agents and small teams spending on monthly farming; brokerages that subsidise farming for their agents.

## Impact If Built
Agents can see whether a farm is building share before the third year's fees, move budget to farms that work, and focus on the homes most likely to list.

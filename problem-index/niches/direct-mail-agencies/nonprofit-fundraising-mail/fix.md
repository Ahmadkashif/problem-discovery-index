# Year-End Volume With No Read on What It Caused

**Niche:** [[niches/direct-mail-agencies/nonprofit-fundraising-mail/profile|Nonprofit Fundraising Mail]]
**Industry:** [[industries/direct-mail-agencies|Direct Mail Agencies]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The year-end appeal is the biggest mailing of the year and the one whose effect is hardest to read, because December giving arrives through every channel at once.

## The Problem
Nonprofits mail their deepest file and most packages between late November and late December, ahead of the 31 December tax-year deadline. Donors respond by mail, online, by phone and through donor-advised funds; many would have given in December anyway. Agencies credit the mailing with whatever arrives in the window, and every year's plan grows on that number.

## Why It's Still Broken
No one wants to hold out donors in the most valuable month, and the agency's reporting has no way to separate mail's effect from seasonal giving.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Small, randomised holdouts in a few segments each year (low-value or lapsed donors first, where the risk is smallest), read against matched mailed donors across all channels, accumulated year over year into an estimate of the year-end mailing's incremental effect by segment.

## Who Feels the Pain
Development directors deciding how deep to mail; agencies whose largest invoice rests on an unmeasured number; donors who receive five appeals in a month.

## Impact If Fixed
A few years of holdouts tell the nonprofit which year-end segments pay for their postage and which are crediting mail with gifts that would have come anyway.

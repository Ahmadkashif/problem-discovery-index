# Delivery Route Planners Adapted to Walk Routes

**Niche:** [[niches/direct-mail-agencies/door-to-door-distribution/profile|Door-to-Door & Door-Hanger Distribution]]
**Industry:** [[industries/direct-mail-agencies|Direct Mail Agencies]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Route optimisation for delivery vans is a mature product; door-to-door walking needs routes built from doors, sidewalks and crew drop-off points, not stops on a road network.
**Tags:** #dynamic-programming #optimization-fundamentals #graph-theory #automation #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to prove to the client that every paid door got the piece — coverage evidenced street by street, with dumped bundles caught before the client finds them — and whoever sells verified doors rather than walked routes takes the account.

## The Problem
Crew leaders cut target areas into walker routes by eye on a printed map or a phone, drop walkers from a van, and pick them up hours later. Routes are uneven, walkers double back, and some streets are never assigned.

## What Already Exists
Vehicle route-optimisation tools for last-mile delivery and field service, which sequence stops, balance workloads and send turn-by-turn directions to drivers.

## The Customization Gap
Walk routing needs: every door as a stop, sequenced to work both sides of quiet streets but one side at a time on busy ones; sidewalk and footpath networks rather than roads; route sizes balanced by doors per hour for the walker's pace; van drop-off and pick-up points as a second routing layer; exclusion areas removed before planning; and output as a simple phone map the walker follows, linked to the verification record.

## Target Customer
Operations managers at distribution firms running daily crews of five to fifty walkers.

## Impact If Solved
Balanced, complete routes raise doors per walker-hour, cut missed streets at the planning stage and give the verification layer a plan to measure against.

# Door-to-Door & Door-Hanger Distribution

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/direct-mail-agencies|Direct Mail Agencies]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to prove to the client that every paid door got the piece — coverage evidenced street by street, with dumped bundles caught before the client finds them — and whoever sells verified doors rather than walked routes takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$3B a year in hand-delivered door hangers, flyers, product samples and polybagged packs, within the ~$5.0B 2022 revenue of advertising material distribution firms (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~7% of combined direct mail and door-to-door spend (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — routes drawn on printed maps or a crew leader's phone, walkers paid in cash or through gig apps, proof sent as GPS dots and photos
**Target Buyer:** Owner or operations manager of a distribution firm; local marketing manager at franchise systems, home-service companies, restaurants and campaigns that buy it
**Automation Potential:** Medium — verification and planning, not the walking

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
This is the 541870 half of the industry. The campaign looks like saturation mail — pick neighbourhoods, print a piece, time the drop — but federal law (18 U.S.C. § 1725) bars unstamped material from mailboxes, so the last mile is a walker hanging a piece on a doorknob. The cost structure flips from postage to labour, the failure mode from undeliverable addresses to dumped bundles, and the regulatory surface from USPS rules to local handbill and solicitation ordinances and HOA restrictions. Clients buy it because it is cheaper per door than mail in dense areas and can be on doors within days.

## Current Tools & Gaps
National networks such as FlyerNation USA and GPS Flyers subcontract to local crews and advertise GPS tracking and photo verification; walker apps such as FlyerBuddy log routes; generic route planners serve vehicles. Gaps: no door-level coverage measure, no maintained exclusion map, and no comparison against EDDM on the same routes.

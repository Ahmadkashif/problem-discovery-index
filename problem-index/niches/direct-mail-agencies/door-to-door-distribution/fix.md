# Ordinance and HOA Exclusions Learned by Complaint

**Niche:** [[niches/direct-mail-agencies/door-to-door-distribution/profile|Door-to-Door & Door-Hanger Distribution]]
**Industry:** [[industries/direct-mail-agencies|Direct Mail Agencies]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Distributors find out that a town bans unsolicited handbills, or that an HOA forbids door hangers, when a resident complains to the client or code enforcement calls.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #evaluation-metrics #compliance #data-integration #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to prove to the client that every paid door got the piece — coverage evidenced street by street, with dumped bundles caught before the client finds them — and whoever sells verified doors rather than walked routes takes the account.

## The Problem
Local rules on handbill distribution, posted no-solicitation signs, apartment and gated-community access, and HOA covenants vary street by street. They decide which doors may lawfully receive a piece, yet most firms keep them in a crew leader's head and a list of "don't go there" notes.

## Why It's Still Broken
The rules are scattered across municipal codes, HOA documents and property managers' policies; firms are small; and the cost of a violation lands on the client's brand, not on the distributor's books.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A maintained exclusion layer per service area: municipal handbill and solicitation provisions extracted from code text, known gated and access-controlled properties, HOA restrictions reported by crews and residents, and complaint history — applied at route planning and reported to the client as "excluded doors" rather than silently skipped.

## Who Feels the Pain
Crew leaders fielding complaints; clients whose brand gets a code-enforcement letter; walkers sent onto properties where they are not welcome.

## Impact If Fixed
Known exclusions stop complaints before they happen and make honest coverage reporting possible, because "not delivered" can be separated into "not allowed" and "missed".

# Litigation Brief Drafting Adapted to Payer Appeals

**Niche:** [[niches/general-hospitals/clinical-denials-status-appeals/profile|Clinical Denials & Inpatient Status Appeals]]
**Industry:** [[industries/general-hospitals|General Hospitals]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Legal AI already drafts arguments that cite the record and the governing rule; a clinical appeal is the same kind of document with a chart for a record and a payer policy for a statute.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #evaluation-metrics #workflow-orchestration #automation #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to overturn clinical denials of the hospital stay itself (inpatient status, medical necessity and DRG validation) before the payer's appeal window closes, and whoever wins back the most denied dollars per reviewer hour takes the account.

## The Problem
A good appeal quotes the payer's own policy, applies it to dated chart evidence and cites the governing rule, such as the two-midnight benchmark that CMS-4201-F applies to Medicare Advantage (recalled). Nurses write these one at a time from templates.

## What Already Exists
Legal drafting platforms used by law firms draft briefs that cite exhibits and authority, with citation checking. Generic LLMs draft fluent appeal letters from pasted summaries.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs:

- chart retrieval with dated evidence spans (labs, vitals, physician statements of expected length of stay);
- a library of payer medical policies and contract clauses, versioned by date of service;
- the clinical criteria sets (MCG, InterQual, Sepsis-3 versus SEP-1);
- appeal-level formats and deadlines for each payer and for Medicare's reconsideration and external review routes;
- a citation checker that refuses any statement not traceable to the chart.

## Target Customer
Denial-management and physician advisor teams at hospitals and the outsourced revenue-cycle firms that serve them.

## Impact If Solved
Drafting time per clinical appeal falls from ~45–60 minutes to ~10–15 (estimate), so smaller denials stop being written off as not worth the labour.

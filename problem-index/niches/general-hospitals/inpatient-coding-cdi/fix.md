# The Query Nobody Answers Before Discharge

**Niche:** [[niches/general-hospitals/inpatient-coding-cdi/profile|Inpatient Coding & Clinical Documentation Integrity]]
**Industry:** [[industries/general-hospitals|General Hospitals]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** CDI queries sit in a physician's inbox until after discharge, so the claim waits or drops without the documentation the query was meant to settle.
**Tags:** #workflow-orchestration #logistic-regression #worker-facing #quick-win #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to assign the DRG the documentation truly supports before the claim drops, and whoever captures the most legitimate severity with DRG shifts that survive payer audit takes the account.

## The Problem
Hospitalists receive queries as inbox messages among dozens of others, phrased in compliance language, often about patients they handed off. Many are answered days late, after the coder has had to hold the chart (recalled).

## Why It's Still Broken
Queries are designed to be non-leading and compliant, which makes them long and abstract. Physicians are not measured on query response, and the query arrives where they are least likely to act.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Queries delivered in the note-writing workflow while the physician is documenting on that patient, with the clinical indicators summarised in one line and a compliant multiple-choice response. They are timed before the expected discharge, and escalation goes to the physician advisor at 24 hours.

## Who Feels the Pain
CDI specialists chasing answers; coders holding charts; hospitalists annoyed by retrospective queries about patients they barely remember.

## Impact If Fixed
Faster concurrent responses cut retrospective queries by ~30–50% (estimate) and shorten coding holds by ~1–2 days (estimate), and documentation improves while the clinical picture is fresh.

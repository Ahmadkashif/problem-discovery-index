# Nurse Staffing Office & Contingent Labour

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/general-hospitals|General Hospitals]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to fill tomorrow's open shifts from the hospital's own nurses before agency and travel staff are called, and whoever covers the most shifts at core rates without breaching ratios takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$4B a year of workforce scheduling, internal float-pool and shift-marketplace software and staffing-office labour (estimate), against hospital contract-labour spend in the tens of billions (~$51B in 2023 per an AHA figure seen in search, not checked)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~0.3% of revenue in tools and staff; contract labour is several percent of expenses (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — schedules live in UKG, API Healthcare or symplr, but tomorrow's gaps are filled by charge nurses and staffing coordinators texting, calling and posting on group chats
**Target Buyer:** Chief Nursing Officer / Director of Staffing and Scheduling
**Automation Potential:** Medium-High — forecasting demand and matching willing, qualified nurses is mechanical; the safety judgement on each unit's mix stays with the house supervisor

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Every shift on every unit needs a staffing level that matches census and acuity, and in California a statutory ratio (recalled). The staffing office works a 24–72-hour horizon. It sees census forecasts, call-outs and open shifts, then calls float nurses, offers incentive pay, mandates overtime, or calls the agency or the vendor-management system for travellers. Travel rates average $91 an hour and range to $160, and 70.7% of hospitals say they want to reduce reliance on travel and agency staff (NSI 2026). The contest is filling shifts internally before premium labour is called.

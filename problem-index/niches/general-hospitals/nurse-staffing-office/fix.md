# The 5 a.m. Text Blast to Fifty Nurses

**Niche:** [[niches/general-hospitals/nurse-staffing-office/profile|Nurse Staffing Office & Contingent Labour]]
**Industry:** [[industries/general-hospitals|General Hospitals]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** When two nurses call out for the day shift, the staffing coordinator texts everyone on the list at once, and the first reply wins regardless of fit, fatigue or cost.
**Tags:** #workflow-orchestration #logistic-regression #worker-facing #quick-win #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to fill tomorrow's open shifts from the hospital's own nurses before agency and travel staff are called, and whoever covers the most shifts at core rates without breaching ratios takes the account.

## The Problem
Call-outs arrive between 04:00 and 06:00. The coordinator sends a group message to dozens of nurses, including some who worked last night and some who are not oriented to the unit, and then sorts out the replies by phone.

## Why It's Still Broken
The scheduling system can show who is eligible, but the call-out workflow lives on personal phones and group chats, outside any system that knows fatigue rules or competencies.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A call-out button that shows only eligible, rested, competent nurses, ranked by likelihood of accepting. It sends offers in waves with a short response window and confirms the fill in the schedule automatically.

## Who Feels the Pain
Staffing coordinators on early shifts; nurses woken by messages for shifts they cannot work; charge nurses who start the day short.

## Impact If Fixed
Call-out fills happen ~30–60 minutes faster (estimate), with fewer mismatched floats and fewer days starting below safe staffing.

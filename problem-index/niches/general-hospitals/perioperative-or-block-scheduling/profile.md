# Perioperative Services & OR Block Scheduling

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/general-hospitals|General Hospitals]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn released and unused OR block minutes into staffed, completed cases, and whoever fills the most released minutes without adding overtime takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$2B a year of perioperative scheduling, analytics and optimisation software and services (estimate), sitting on surgical services that produce a large share (~40–60%, recalled) of hospital revenue
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~0.15% of revenue in tools; the revenue at stake is far larger (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — cases are booked in Epic OpTime or Oracle, but block release, back-fill and add-on matching run on phone calls, emails and the OR desk's memory
**Target Buyer:** VP Perioperative Services / Chief Operating Officer
**Automation Potential:** High — duration prediction and release forecasting are well-posed; block policy changes are political

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Operating rooms are the hospital's highest-margin and highest-fixed-cost resource. OR time costs roughly $36–$37 a minute in one widely cited study (recalled), and a staffed room sitting idle at 1 p.m. is lost margin that cannot be stored. Time is allocated in blocks to surgeons and groups under negotiated policies. The binding problem is the gap between allocated and used minutes, and how late unused minutes are released. This is distinct from inpatient capacity, which is a downstream consequence. Its buyer, perioperative leadership, and its vendors are their own market.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Epic OpTime and Oracle surgical modules; LeanTaaS iQueue for Operating Rooms; Qventus Perioperative Solution; Opmed.ai (AI block allocation and case-duration prediction, $15M Series A); anaesthesia scheduling tools. All three specialists had engineering or forward-deployed roles open on 24 October 2026. Gaps: durations booked at surgeon estimates; release deadlines enforced by spreadsheet; back-fill offered first-come-first-served without checking equipment, implant reps or anaesthesia.

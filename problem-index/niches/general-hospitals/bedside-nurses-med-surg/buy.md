# Physician Ambient Scribes Adapted to Nursing Workflow

**Niche:** [[niches/general-hospitals/bedside-nurses-med-surg/profile|Bedside Nurses on Med-Surg Units]]
**Industry:** [[industries/general-hospitals|General Hospitals]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Hospitals already license ambient scribes for physicians; extending them to nurses means a different capture pattern, a different output and a different success metric.
**Tags:** #transformers #large-language-models #evaluation-metrics #worker-facing #workflow-orchestration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to cut the bedside nurse's documentation time without degrading the flowsheet data that quality measures, audits and billing depend on, and whoever gives back the most charting minutes per shift with audit-clean flowsheets takes the account.

## The Problem
Physician scribes assume a bounded encounter: one visit, one note. Nursing is continuous, with dozens of short interactions per patient per shift across five or six patients.

## What Already Exists
Abridge, Ambience, Microsoft Dragon Copilot and Epic AI Charting for physicians (Epic's broadly available from February 2026, with Microsoft supplying ambient transcription). Abridge and Ambience were hiring engineers, including a forward-deployed product role at Abridge, on 24 October 2026.

## The Customization Gap
Nursing adaptation needs:

- always-available capture on a badge or phone, with patient identification by room or barcode;
- output to flowsheet rows, the MAR (medication administration record) and care-plan items, not narrative;
- reassessment timers that trigger on what was charted;
- handling of noisy multi-patient environments and privacy in semi-private rooms;
- success measured as overtime and late charting, not note-writing time.

## Target Customer
CNOs at systems that have already rolled out physician ambient tools and have contracts to extend.

## Impact If Solved
Extending an existing licence to nurses reaches the largest clinical workforce (1.75M hospital RNs against 189,800 physicians, BLS OEWS) without a new procurement. At ~20–30 minutes saved per nurse per shift (estimate), a 1,000-nurse hospital gets back ~120,000–180,000 hours a year.

# Revenue Cycle: Denials & Payer Contract Yield

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/general-hospitals|General Hospitals]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is: every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to collect what the hospital is owed on each claim. Clinical denials are won by whoever builds the strongest chart-and-policy argument before the appeal deadline, and contract yield by whoever models every payer's contract terms against every remittance. Decomposed into contested sub-niches.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$30B a year of hospital spend on revenue-cycle staff, outsourcing and software that touches denials, appeals and payer yield (estimate), against ~$25.7B a year of provider administrative cost spent fighting denials (Premier survey, 2025)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~2.5% of general hospital revenue (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — claims and remittances are electronic end to end, but appeals, contract terms and payer policies are PDFs read by people
**Target Buyer:** CFO / VP Revenue Cycle
**Automation Potential:** High for prioritisation, drafting and variance detection; human for peer-to-peer and contract negotiation

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
This is where hospital money is decided after care is delivered. About 15% of claims to private payers are denied first time, 15% for Medicare Advantage and 28.5% for Medicaid managed care, and ~70% of denials are eventually overturned (Premier). Separately, payers underpay claims they do approve, through contract terms that are misloaded, carve-outs that are missed and lesser-of clauses that are misapplied. Both leak money at a 2.5% median margin (Kaufman Hall, H1 2026). But the work, evidence and vendors differ. Denials are a clinical argument against a deadline. Underpayments are a reconciliation against a contract.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Epic Resolute and Oracle revenue cycle; Waystar, Experian and Availity for claims; outsourced revenue cycles from R1, Ensemble and Optum; AI-native vendors (AKASA, Commure, Smarter Technologies); contract-management modules and underpayment recovery firms. The gap is that no system joins the denial, the appeal argument, the contract clause and the outcome, so the hospital cannot say which payer behaviours cost it most or which arguments win. That join is split across the two sub-niches.

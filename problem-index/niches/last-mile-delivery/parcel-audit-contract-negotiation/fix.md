# Negotiators Know What a Carrier Will Concede and It Lives in Them

**Niche:** [[niches/last-mile-delivery/parcel-audit-contract-negotiation/profile|Parcel Audit & Contract Negotiation Firms]]
**Industry:** [[industries/last-mile-delivery|Last-Mile Delivery]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The firm's real product in a negotiation is knowing which asks a carrier gives up and which it never does, and that knowledge is a handful of careers.
**Tags:** #tacit-knowledge-ml #evaluation-metrics #graph-ml #worker-facing #data-integration

## The Problem
A parcel negotiation is a sequence of asks: deeper tier discounts, a dimensional divisor change, waived residential surcharges, a capped peak surcharge, a minimum commitment reduction. Which of these a carrier will move on depends on the moment, the account team, the shipper's profile, and where the carrier is against its own targets — and a negotiator who has done fifty of these knows the pattern.

That knowledge is what clients pay for. It exists as individual experience. The engagement record contains the outcome — the contract that was signed — and not the asks that were made, the order they were made in, what was conceded, what was refused, and what the carrier offered instead.

So each negotiator carries their own model, the firm cannot say whether one negotiator is better than another or why, and the strongest asset in the business walks between firms.

## Why It's Still Broken
Negotiation is relationship work with an outcome, and the outcome is a contract. Nothing in the workflow asks for the process, and recording it feels like bureaucracy around a craft.

There is also a competitive-secrecy instinct inside the firm: a senior negotiator's knowledge of carrier behaviour is their standing, and nothing rewards making it portable.

And the analytical side of the firm — the audit operation with all the data — sits apart from the negotiation side. The people who could measure what concessions are typical are not the people in the room.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Record the negotiation as structured events and connect it to the pricing data.

**Ask-level records.** Each concession requested, the justification used, the carrier's response, and the final position. Typed and dated, attached to the carrier, the account team where known, and the client profile. This is a short form at the end of a negotiation, not a diary.

**Carrier behaviour profiles.** What each carrier concedes, at what shipper profile, at what point in the year and quarter. This is the map every negotiation currently starts by reconstructing from memory.

**Link asks to achieved rates.** The audit data shows what the shipper subsequently paid. Joining the asks to the realized outcome is what turns anecdote into evidence about which asks are worth making.

**Surface at preparation.** A negotiator opening an engagement should see what the firm has achieved for comparable shippers with this carrier, which asks landed, and which never do. That is the payoff and it is what makes the capture worth doing.

**Measure negotiator effect properly.** Controlling for shipper profile and carrier, which negotiators achieve more? That is a coaching input and an assignment input, and it is unanswerable today.

## Who Feels the Pain
Negotiators, rebuilding a picture colleagues already have. Newer consultants, who need years of engagements to be credible in a room. Firm leadership, whose differentiating capability is a small number of tenures in a market where people move. And clients, whose outcome depends on which negotiator was assigned.

## Impact If Fixed
Contract negotiation is where the value in this business is migrating as audit commoditizes, and it currently runs on individual memory over a dataset the same firm owns. Structuring the asks and joining them to realized rates makes the firm's negotiation capability measurable, teachable, and — unlike a contingency audit percentage — genuinely hard for a competitor to match.

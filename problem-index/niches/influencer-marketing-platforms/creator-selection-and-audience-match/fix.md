# The Engagement Rate That Means Nothing Across Tiers

**Niche:** [[niches/influencer-marketing-platforms/creator-selection-and-audience-match/profile|Creator Selection & Audience Match]]
**Industry:** [[industries/influencer-marketing-platforms|Influencer Marketing Platforms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Engagement rate falls systematically as an account grows, so comparing a creator with ten thousand followers to one with a million on that metric is comparing account sizes.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #hypothesis-testing #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #quick-win #revenue-impact #dimensionality-reduction #gradient-boosting
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to predict whether a creator's audience contains buyers this brand does not already have — and whoever answers that replaces follower count as the currency of the category.

## The Problem
Engagement rate is the category's quality metric and it is mechanically related to account size: small accounts have higher rates because their audiences are closer to them and the platforms' distribution works differently at scale. A brand comparing a creator at eight percent engagement to one at one point two percent is largely comparing follower counts a second time. Selections are made on this, rate cards are justified by it, and campaign reports are graded against it. It is used as though it were a measure of audience quality, and it is a measure of tier with noise on top.

## Why It's Still Broken
The metric is simple, universally available and looks like a quality measure, which is enough for it to have become the standard — a case of an available number becoming the definition. The size relationship is known to practitioners and never adjusted for, because there is no established adjustment. Creators at every tier have an interest in the metric that flatters them. And no platform wants to be the one whose numbers look different from everyone else's.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Normalise it, or stop using it. Publish engagement rate relative to the distribution for accounts of comparable size and category, which is the fix, is a percentile calculation on data every platform already holds, and makes the metric comparable for the first time. Report the raw number alongside so nothing is hidden and the adjustment is inspectable. Separate the components — reach, view-through, saves, shares, comments, clicks — since they mean different things and an aggregated rate conceals which signal is present. Weight by the actions that correlate with purchase in the corpus rather than by what is easy to count, which is where the fix connects to real outcomes. Detect inauthentic engagement explicitly and report it as a separate figure rather than folding it into an opaque authenticity score. Show the trend, since a rate rising or falling against the creator's own baseline is far more informative than its level. Provide a category-adjusted benchmark, because engagement norms differ enormously between beauty and finance and a cross-category comparison is meaningless. Deprecate the raw rate in rate-card and reporting contexts, which is where the misuse causes real money to move. Publish the size relationship openly so buyers understand what they have been comparing. And validate any replacement against sales outcomes in the corpus, because a better-looking metric that predicts nothing is the same mistake again.

## Who Feels the Pain
Brands selecting on a number that encodes account size; large creators penalised and small creators flattered by an artefact; and the category, whose quality currency does not measure quality.

## Impact If Fixed
The standard quality metric is mechanically determined by tier, so comparing across tiers compares follower counts a second time. A size-and-category percentile is a calculation on data every platform holds and makes the number comparable immediately.

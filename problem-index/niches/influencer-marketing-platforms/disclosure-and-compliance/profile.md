# Disclosure & Regulatory Compliance

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/influencer-marketing-platforms|Influencer Marketing Platforms]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to prove that hundreds of pieces of paid content were actually disclosed and made no prohibited claim — and whoever can evidence that turns a regulatory exposure into a checkable record.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$800M US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~8% of category spend
**Digital Adoption:** Very Low — contract clauses and hope
**Target Buyer:** Brand legal and compliance leadership
**Automation Potential:** Very High — verification is mechanical

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The brand is responsible for the disclosure and the claims in content it paid for, across hundreds of posts by people it does not employ, on platforms whose disclosure mechanisms differ, in formats where a disclosure can be technically present and effectively invisible. The current control is a clause in a contract. Nobody systematically verifies that the disclosure appeared, stayed, was prominent, or that the content avoided the claims legal prohibited. This is distinct from creator risk, which asks who to work with; this asks whether what was published was compliant.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Contract clauses requiring disclosure, brief instructions, spot checks, and platform-native disclosure tools of varying quality. The gaps: no verification that disclosure actually appeared; no check that it persisted after publication; no assessment of prominence; no claim checking against the brand's prohibited list; and no evidence record if a regulator asks.

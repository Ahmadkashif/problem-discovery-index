# A Clause in a Contract as the Only Control

**Niche:** [[niches/influencer-marketing-platforms/disclosure-and-compliance/profile|Disclosure & Regulatory Compliance]]
**Industry:** [[industries/influencer-marketing-platforms|Influencer Marketing Platforms]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** The brand is responsible for disclosure and claims across hundreds of posts by people it does not employ, and the control is a sentence in a contract.
**Tags:** #compliance #transformers #large-language-models #automation #evaluation-metrics #bert #confidence-intervals #workflow-orchestration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to prove that hundreds of pieces of paid content were actually disclosed and made no prohibited claim — and whoever can evidence that turns a regulatory exposure into a checkable record.

## The Problem
A brand runs three hundred partnerships a year. Each contract says the creator must disclose the commercial relationship clearly and must not make claims the brand has not substantiated. Whether any of that happened is unknown. Some posts disclosed properly, some used a tag buried in a wall of hashtags, some used a platform tool that displays differently across surfaces, some disclosed in a video after the product claim, and some did not disclose at all. Several made claims about efficacy that legal explicitly prohibited. Nobody checked, and if a regulator asks what the brand did to ensure compliance, the answer is a contract clause.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Responsibility is contractual and enforcement has historically been rare enough that a clause felt sufficient — the control matched the perceived risk rather than the actual obligation. Verification means processing video and audio at scale, which was impractical until recently. Platforms' disclosure mechanisms differ and change. And nobody in the brand owns post-publication checking, since the campaign is considered complete at go-live.

## What to Build
Verify rather than require. Check every published post for disclosure presence, form and prominence, which is the core and is mechanical — the platform, the format and the rules are all knowable and the check is a comparison. Handle video and audio, since a spoken disclosure at the end of a two-minute video and a caption tag are different compliance positions and most content is video. Assess prominence rather than presence, because a tag inside thirty hashtags is the standard evasion and a presence check passes it. Check claims against the brand's own prohibited and substantiated lists, which is where the expensive exposure sits and which nobody does systematically. Re-check after publication, since content is edited and disclosures are removed, which is the fix note's subject. Alert quickly, because a non-compliant post corrected within hours is a different matter from one left standing for months. Keep an evidence record with captures and timestamps, which is what a brand actually needs if questioned and which no campaign currently produces. Cover every platform and format the brand uses, as partial coverage gives false assurance. Report a compliance rate per campaign and per creator, which makes the problem visible and manageable for the first time. And feed repeat failures into creator selection, since compliance behaviour is a legitimate and currently unused selection signal.

## Target Customer
Brand legal and compliance functions, influencer platforms selling to regulated categories, and the agencies carrying the same obligation.

## Impact If Built
The control matched the perceived risk rather than the actual obligation, so a contract clause stands in for verification of three hundred posts. Checking presence, prominence and claims across video and captions is mechanical, and the evidence record is what the brand needs if asked.

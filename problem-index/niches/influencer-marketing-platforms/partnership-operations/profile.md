# Partnership Operations

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/influencer-marketing-platforms|Influencer Marketing Platforms]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** This niche is not terminal — making hundreds of bespoke agreements machine-readable and making a hundred parallel creative conversations tractable are different contests with different winners, and they are stated separately in the sub-niches below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$2.0B US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~20% of category spend
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — workflow exists, fit does not
**Target Buyer:** Platform product leadership
**Automation Potential:** High — both halves are structurable

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Contracting, briefing, approvals, usage rights and payment for two hundred bespoke partnerships is handled by workflow software that assumes every brand runs the same process, and none of them do. The clerical weight of the category is unusually high because every partnership is individually negotiated, and that weight is the practical cap on how many partnerships a brand can run — which makes it the second-largest concentration of contested value here.

It is also **not terminal**. The label names a functional area rather than a contest, and writing the contested statement produces two sentences describing different winners. One fight is over the partnership as a legal object: the deliverables, the usage rights and their expiry, the exclusivity, the whitelisting permissions and the payment terms, all currently expressed as prose in hundreds of separate agreements that nothing can read. The other is over the creative collaboration: briefing, drafts, review rounds, revisions and go-live across a hundred simultaneous conversations with people who do not work for the brand. A competitor can lead decisively on either while being ordinary at the other. The two contests are stated in the sub-niches.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Campaign workflow platforms with contracting, briefing, approval and payment modules, template libraries, and status dashboards. The gaps: fixed process assumptions that fit no brand; rights stored as documents rather than as terms; approval workflows that do not match how creative review actually happens; no visibility for the creator; and a status question that has to be asked by message.

# Two Hundred Bespoke Deals in One Workflow

**Niche:** [[niches/influencer-marketing-platforms/partnership-operations/profile|Partnership Operations]]
**Industry:** [[industries/influencer-marketing-platforms|Influencer Marketing Platforms]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Contracting, briefing, approvals, usage rights and payment for two hundred bespoke partnerships is handled by workflow software that assumes every brand runs the same process, and none of them do.
**Tags:** #workflow-orchestration #automation #data-integration #compliance #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #revenue-impact #quick-win
**Contested on:** This niche is not terminal — making hundreds of bespoke agreements machine-readable and making a hundred parallel creative conversations tractable are different contests with different winners, and they are stated separately in the sub-niches.

## The Problem
Every brand runs this differently. One requires legal review on every contract, another has a pre-approved template with negotiable fields. One reviews concepts then drafts then final, another only final. One handles rights centrally, another per campaign. The platform implements a process, the brand's process does not match it, and the difference is absorbed in a spreadsheet running alongside the platform — which becomes the real system of record while the platform holds a partial and increasingly stale copy. Every brand ends up with a workflow tool and a shadow process.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Workflow products are built around a canonical process because that is what makes them buildable and demonstrable, and configurability is deferred until it is architecturally expensive. Partnerships are genuinely bespoke, so the variation is real rather than incidental. The spreadsheet workaround makes the mismatch survivable, which removes the pressure. And the platform is bought by a manager who cannot change the brand's legal or finance processes.

## What to Build
Fit the brand rather than requiring the brand to fit. Make the process itself configurable — stages, roles, approvals, conditions — rather than offering settings within a fixed one, which is the structural change and the difference between a tool and a shadow process. Model the partnership as a set of obligations with states, so the system can tell you what is outstanding regardless of the brand's particular sequence. Make rights and terms machine-readable rather than documentary, which is the first sub-niche's contest and is where the expensive failures live. Make the creative collaboration genuinely multi-party, which is the second sub-niche and is what the current approval modules do badly. Integrate with what the brand already uses for legal, finance and asset management, because the spreadsheet exists partly because the platform is an island. Give the creator real visibility, since a large share of the status traffic is a creator asking a question the system could answer. Handle exceptions as first-class, as every campaign has partnerships that deviate and a system that cannot represent them exports them to the spreadsheet. Report status without anyone assembling it, which is the manager's whole week. Support the volume pattern of the category — a hundred partnerships starting at once — rather than a project model. And measure how much of the process happens outside the platform, because that figure is the honest measure of whether the product fits.

## Target Customer
Brand partnership teams running partnerships at volume, the agencies operating them, and platform vendors whose customers keep a parallel spreadsheet.

## Impact If Built
Every brand runs a workflow tool and a shadow spreadsheet, and the spreadsheet is the real system of record. Making the process itself configurable rather than offering settings inside a fixed one is the structural change that closes the gap.

# Quoting Blind Into a Market With Data

**Niche:** [[niches/influencer-marketing-platforms/the-creator/profile|The Creator]]
**Industry:** [[industries/influencer-marketing-platforms|Influencer Marketing Platforms]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A creator quoting for a brand deal has no market data, no idea what the same brand paid someone comparable last month, and will wait three months to be paid whatever they agreed.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #gradient-boosting #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #revenue-impact #worker-facing #workflow-orchestration #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to give a creator the market data and the settlement terms a supplier in any other industry would take for granted — and whoever does that changes the balance of every negotiation in the category.

## The Problem
A brand asks a creator for a quote. The creator has no rate card that means anything, no data on what this brand paid comparable creators, no sense of whether the usage rights being requested are worth more than the post itself, and a suspicion that asking too much loses the deal. They pick a number based on what a peer mentioned once. The brand, which runs three hundred of these a year and has a benchmark for every tier and category, knows exactly whether the number is high or low. The information asymmetry is total and it is the normal operating condition of the category's supply side.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The data that would produce benchmarks sits with platforms and agencies whose customers are the brands, and publishing it would weaken their customers' position — the asymmetry is an asset to whoever holds the data. Creators are individually small and have never had a collective mechanism. Deal terms are usually confidential by contract. And nobody has built for the creator as a buyer in this category.

## What to Build
Give the supply side information and terms. Build rate benchmarks by tier, platform, category, deliverable and usage scope, from contributed and observed deal data, which is the core and needs a contribution mechanism rather than a technical breakthrough — creators will share what they were paid if they get benchmarks back. Price usage rights separately from the content, since rights are frequently the larger value and are routinely bundled at no extra cost because the creator does not know to charge for them. Turn the creator's own performance history into negotiating evidence, which is their only real leverage and which they cannot currently assemble across brands. Model the value of the specific ask — exclusivity, whitelisting, extended term, multi-platform — so a creator can price an amendment rather than accepting it. Provide contract review in plain language, since creators sign agreements they do not understand containing terms that cost them later. Handle invoicing, chasing and payment terms, which is the fix note's subject and is a large part of the grievance. Give creators a view of which brands pay on time, which is information they trade informally and would use to decide who to work with. Support the whole relationship rather than one deal, since repeat partnerships are where creator income actually is and nothing helps them manage renewals. Keep the creator as the customer, because a product funded by brands will serve brands. And measure realised rates over time, since the point is whether the supply side's position actually improves.

## Target Customer
Creators earning from brand partnerships, creator business and finance vendors, and the talent representatives who negotiate on their behalf.

## Impact If Built
The information asymmetry is total and it is an asset to whoever holds the data, which is why it persists. Benchmarks built from contributed deal data plus separate pricing for usage rights address the two places creators lose the most.

# The Revision That Made It Worse

**Niche:** [[niches/influencer-marketing-platforms/content-performance-prediction/profile|Content Performance Prediction]]
**Industry:** [[industries/influencer-marketing-platforms|Influencer Marketing Platforms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The brand asked for the logo earlier, the script tightened and the tone lifted, and turned a creator's video into an advertisement the audience scrolled past.
**Tags:** #hypothesis-testing #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #confidence-intervals #causal-inference #quick-win #revenue-impact #transformers
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to say what a specific piece of creator content will do before it is published — and whoever can do that turns briefing and approval from taste into evidence.

## The Problem
The creator's first draft is in their own voice, opens the way their content always opens, and mentions the product forty seconds in. The brand asks for the product in the first five seconds, the brand name said twice, the tagline included and the messy bit at the start removed. The creator complies. The video underperforms everything else on their channel. The brand concludes the creator was overrated. The creator concludes the brand ruined it. Neither position is tested, and the same revision requests are made on the next campaign, because there is no record connecting requested changes to what happened afterwards.

## Why It's Still Broken
Revision requests are made in comment threads and never recorded as structured changes, so the connection to outcomes cannot be drawn — the data to settle this is destroyed at the moment it is created. Brand taste carries organisational authority that evidence would have to overcome. Nobody compares a first draft to a final version systematically. And the creator has no standing to argue.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Record the changes and connect them to outcomes. Capture every revision request in a structured form — what was changed and why — which is the fix, takes a tick box at request time, and creates the dataset that settles the argument within two campaigns. Compare final content against the creator's own baseline performance, since that is the right comparison and is available for every creator. Report which categories of revision correlate with underperformance, which is a straightforward analysis once the requests are structured and reliably shows the same two or three requests doing the damage. Test the change before requesting it where prediction is available, connecting to the build note. Give creators a documented basis to push back, since they are usually right about their own audience and currently have only an opinion against a client's authority. Distinguish compliance-driven changes, which are non-negotiable, from preference-driven ones, which should be evidence-based — conflating them is why every request is treated as mandatory. Publish the findings to brand stakeholders, because the people making the requests are not the people reading performance reports. Track revision rounds against outcomes, as heavily revised content tends to underperform and that pattern alone changes behaviour. Build the guidance into briefs so the requests stop being needed. And measure first-draft acceptance, since a brand that accepts more first drafts is usually getting better results and shorter cycles at once.

## Who Feels the Pain
Creators watching their work made worse and then being blamed; brands whose own revisions cost them the performance they paid for; and managers relaying requests they suspect are wrong.

## Impact If Fixed
Revision requests live in comment threads, so the data that would settle the argument is destroyed as it is created. A tick box at request time builds a dataset that within two campaigns shows which specific requests are costing the performance.

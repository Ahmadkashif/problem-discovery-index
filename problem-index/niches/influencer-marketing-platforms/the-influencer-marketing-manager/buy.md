## The Problem
Tracking many parallel workstreams with dependencies, owners, due dates and blockers is exactly what project management software does, and it does it well. Portfolio views, automated status rollups, dependency tracking, blocked-item surfacing and stakeholder reporting are all standard. Influencer campaigns have a hundred parallel workstreams with identical structure, and they are tracked by a person remembering and a spreadsheet.

## What Already Exists
Project and portfolio management platforms; automated status rollup and reporting; dependency and blocker tracking; workload and capacity views; and integrations pulling status from connected systems.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to workstreams whose owner is an external individual who will not use the tool. It requires: (1) status inferred from communication and published outcomes rather than reported by the owner, since the person doing the work will never update a task — this single fact disqualifies the entire project management model and is the central adaptation; (2) a hundred near-identical short workstreams rather than a structured plan, which changes the interface from a timeline to an exception queue; (3) the creator's own channels as the interaction surface, which project tools have no concept of; (4) the coordinator having no authority over the workers, so the mechanic is persuasion and reminder rather than assignment; and (5) obligations continuing after delivery, since rights and usage persist past go-live and a project model closes at completion.

## Target Customer
Brand partnership teams, agencies, influencer platform vendors, and project management vendors for whom external-owner workstreams are unserved.

## Impact If Solved
Project tooling assumes the owner updates the task, and here the owner is an external creator who never will. Inferring status from communication and published outcomes is the adaptation that makes a mature category applicable at all.

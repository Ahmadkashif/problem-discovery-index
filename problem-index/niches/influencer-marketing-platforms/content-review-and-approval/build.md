# A Hundred Drafts and a Direct Message Inbox

**Niche:** [[niches/influencer-marketing-platforms/content-review-and-approval/profile|Content Review & Approval]]
**Industry:** [[industries/influencer-marketing-platforms|Influencer Marketing Platforms]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A hundred creators are drafting at once, the review conversations happen in direct messages because that is where creators are, and the platform records decisions that were made elsewhere.
**Tags:** #workflow-orchestration #automation #large-language-models #worker-facing #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #quick-win #data-integration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to make a hundred simultaneous creative conversations with people outside the company tractable — and whoever does that decides how many partnerships one team can actually run.

## The Problem
The manager has a hundred creators at various stages. Eleven have not acknowledged the brief. Twenty-three have submitted drafts. Of those, nine are waiting on legal, six need revisions the manager must write up from three stakeholders' scattered comments, and two are disputing feedback. Four are due to post tomorrow and have not confirmed. All of this is tracked by scrolling through direct messages across three apps, because creators live on those apps and will not log into a brand's tool to check a comment. The platform has an approval module; it is used to mark things approved after the fact.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Review tooling assumes participants who log in, and creators will not — that single behavioural fact defeats most of the category's approval modules and is rarely designed around. Creators are not the buyer, so the interface that would reach them is nobody's priority. Brand stakeholders review in whatever tool they prefer. And the manager's message-chasing is invisible work that never appears as a product requirement.

## What to Build
Meet the creator where they are and consolidate on the brand side. Operate through the creator's own channels — messaging, email, the platforms they already use — rather than requiring a login, which is the design decision the whole product turns on and the one most competitors get wrong. Consolidate stakeholder feedback into one actionable instruction, since a creator receiving three contradictory comments produces a fourth draft nobody wanted and this consolidation is the manager's most time-consuming task. Track state without anyone updating it, inferring from messages, submissions and posts, so the status board is real rather than maintained. Chase automatically at the right intervals, which is a large share of the manager's week and is entirely mechanical. Show every draft's position in one view, since the question a manager is asked all day is where is everything. Version content properly, including drafts shared as screenshots and links, which is how they actually arrive. Route review by rule — legal only when a claim is present, brand only for a first-time creator — because reviewing everything with everyone is why cycles take a week. Detect the go-live risk early, as a creator who has not responded in four days and posts tomorrow is the specific failure the system should surface. Give the creator clarity on what is expected and when, which removes a large share of the inbound questions. And measure cycle time and revision rounds, since those two numbers determine how many partnerships a team can run.

## Target Customer
Brand partnership and creative teams, the agencies running campaigns at volume, and the creators receiving contradictory feedback through three channels.

## Impact If Built
Creators will not log into a brand's tool, which defeats every approval module built on that assumption. Operating through their own channels while consolidating stakeholder feedback into one instruction removes the manager's two largest tasks.

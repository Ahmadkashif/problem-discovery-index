# The Asset Still Running After the Licence Expired

**Niche:** [[niches/influencer-marketing-platforms/contracting-and-rights/profile|Contracting & Rights]]
**Industry:** [[industries/influencer-marketing-platforms|Influencer Marketing Platforms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The six-month usage term ended in March and the asset has been running in paid social ever since, because nothing connected the date to the campaign.
**Tags:** #compliance #automation #workflow-orchestration #evaluation-metrics #quick-win #data-integration #revenue-impact #descriptive-statistics
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn hundreds of bespoke partnership agreements into terms a system can read and enforce — and whoever does that stops brands from running assets they no longer have the right to use.

## The Problem
A creator licensed a piece of content for six months of paid usage. The asset performed well, so the media team kept it in rotation. Six months passed. Nothing happened — no alert, no block, no change — because the expiry date exists in a document and the asset exists in an ad account, and the two have never been introduced. Four months later the creator notices their face in an advertisement, contacts the brand, and the conversation is now about back payment and possibly damages. The media team acted entirely in good faith on an asset that was in their library.

## Why It's Still Broken
Expiry is a date in a contract rather than a property of the asset, which is the mechanical cause and means the information is in the wrong place entirely. Asset libraries have no rights fields, and ad platforms have no concept of a licence. Calendar reminders depend on someone having set them and still being in the role. And the breach is invisible until the creator notices.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Put the expiry on the asset. Record usage terms as metadata on every asset file at the point it enters the library, which is the fix, is a handful of fields, and puts the date where the decision is made rather than in a document. Block or flag at the point of use in the ad platform and asset manager, since a warning that arrives after launch has already failed. Alert owners before expiry with enough lead time to renew, which converts a breach into a renewal conversation and is frequently what the creator wanted anyway. Scan currently running campaigns against held rights, which most brands have never done and which typically finds live breaches on the first run. Distinguish channel and territory scope rather than treating rights as a single flag, because the common breach is running an organic-only asset in paid rather than running an entirely expired one. Archive expired assets automatically so they cannot be picked up by someone unaware. Keep an audit record of what ran where and when, which is what resolves the dispute when it happens. Offer the creator an extension as a routine transaction rather than an apology, since a simple renewal path monetises the situation instead of litigating it. Report expiring rights on a rolling horizon so renewals are planned. And measure live breaches as a standing compliance number, because a brand that cannot say whether it is currently in breach is not managing this at all.

## Who Feels the Pain
Creators whose likeness runs unpaid; brands facing back payments and reputational damage; and media teams blamed for using an asset that was sitting in their library.

## Impact If Fixed
The expiry is in a document and the asset is in an ad account, and the two have never been introduced. Putting terms on the asset as metadata and checking at the point of use turns a breach discovered by the creator into a renewal conversation.

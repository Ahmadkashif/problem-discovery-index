**One-liner:** Corporate due diligence firms produce reasoned reputational assessments of individuals, and influencer platforms produce a number between one and a hundred.

**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to tell a brand whether this specific creator is a risk for this specific brand — and whoever answers that replaces a generic authenticity score with a judgement somebody can act on.

## The Problem
Assessing whether an individual poses a reputational risk to an organisation is an established professional service. Due diligence firms research public records, media, litigation and associations, produce a reasoned report with evidence and severity, and tailor it to the client's specific exposure. Employers, investors and boards commission it routinely. Influencer marketing, where brands attach their name to individuals at volume, buys a generic score computed from a keyword scan.

## What Already Exists
Reputational and integrity due diligence services; adverse media screening; public record and litigation research; association and network mapping; and tiered assessment depth by risk level.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is from a bespoke report on one individual to an automated assessment of hundreds. It requires: (1) a cost per assessment measured in cents rather than thousands, since a brand assesses hundreds of creators a year and the professional model is uneconomic at that volume — this is the constraint that forces automation and is the whole opportunity; (2) the subject's own published content as the primary source rather than third-party records, which inverts the research approach and is far more tractable to automate; (3) video and audio as the dominant medium, which the text-oriented due diligence practice does not handle; (4) brand-specific risk criteria expressed once and applied repeatedly, rather than a bespoke scoping conversation per subject; and (5) continuous monitoring rather than a point-in-time report, since the relationship persists and the risk changes.

## Target Customer
Brand safety and legal teams, influencer platforms, agencies, and due diligence firms for whom high-volume creator assessment is an unserved market.

## Impact If Solved
The professional model costs thousands per subject and brands assess hundreds a year, which forces automation and is the opportunity. The creator's own published video is the primary source, which inverts the research approach and is far more automatable than record searching.

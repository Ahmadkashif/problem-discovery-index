# The Post From Four Years Ago

**Niche:** [[niches/influencer-marketing-platforms/creator-risk-and-suitability/profile|Creator Risk & Brand Suitability]]
**Industry:** [[industries/influencer-marketing-platforms|Influencer Marketing Platforms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The screening scanned the last ninety days, the incident came from a video posted four years ago, and it took someone on the internet an afternoon to find it.
**Tags:** #transformers #large-language-models #compliance #evaluation-metrics #quick-win #automation #bert #confidence-intervals
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to tell a brand whether this specific creator is a risk for this specific brand — and whoever answers that replaces a generic authenticity score with a judgement somebody can act on.

## The Problem
A brand announces a partnership. Within a day, someone has found a four-year-old video in which the creator said something the brand cannot be associated with. The brand's screening covered recent posts and text captions, because that is what the tooling scans. The material that caused the incident was public, indexed, and findable by anyone motivated to look — which describes the entire internet the moment a partnership is announced. The screening was not wrong; it was scoped to a window that bears no relationship to where the risk lives.

## Why It's Still Broken
Recency is the default scan window because processing a full back catalogue of video was expensive, and the default outlived the constraint — the cost that justified it has fallen by orders of magnitude and nobody revisited the setting. Video and audio were not scannable at reasonable cost until recently. Deleted-but-archived content is outside most tooling entirely. And an incident is attributed to the creator rather than to the screening.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Scan where the risk actually is. Cover the full available history rather than a recent window, which is the fix and is now affordable in a way it was not when the window was set. Process video and audio, since that is where the material is for most creators and caption scanning misses nearly all of it. Search archives and mirrors for deleted content, because deletion does not remove it from the internet and the people who look will find it. Weight by discoverability, as the risk is not that something exists but that it is findable, and the most viewed and most indexed material is where to concentrate. Assess comments and replies as well as posts, which is a common source and is never scanned. Check associations and collaborations, since appearing alongside someone is a frequent route to an incident. Re-screen before announcement rather than only at selection, because the gap between the two is often months. Give the creator a chance to disclose and contextualise, which is fairer, frequently produces the item the scan would have missed, and is standard practice in every other kind of vetting. Record the screening scope explicitly, so that when something surfaces the brand knows whether it was a miss or out of scope. And measure incidents against screening coverage, because that is the only way to know whether the window is right.

## Who Feels the Pain
Brands announcing partnerships that unravel in a day; creators whose old content is discovered publicly rather than discussed privately; and managers who ran the screening the tooling offered.

## Impact If Fixed
The recency window was set when processing a video back catalogue was expensive and outlived that constraint entirely. Scanning the full history including video, audio and archived deletions covers where the risk actually lives, which is anywhere the internet can reach in an afternoon.

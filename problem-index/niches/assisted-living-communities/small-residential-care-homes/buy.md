# Chain-Sized EHRs Cut Down to a Six-Bed Home

**Niche:** [[niches/assisted-living-communities/small-residential-care-homes/profile|Small Residential Care Homes]]
**Industry:** [[industries/assisted-living-communities|Assisted Living Communities]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Senior living EHRs assume a business office, a wellness nurse and an IT contact; a small home has an owner with a phone.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #workflow-orchestration #compliance #automation #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to take the owner-operator off the night and weekend schedule of a six-to-sixteen-bed home, and whoever covers the home's round-the-clock staffing requirement with the fewest owner hours takes the account.

## The Problem
Owners keep medication records, service plans, staff training files and resident billing in separate paper and spreadsheet systems, and do the paperwork at night after a care shift.

## What Already Exists
Senior living EHR and eMAR suites (Yardi, PointClickCare, August Health) built for chain communities, and a handful of small-home apps covering medications and charting.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs: (1) phone-first eMAR and charting usable by two caregivers per shift; (2) the home's state forms (physician report, service plan, incident report) generated from the record; (3) staff training and clearance tracking per state rules; (4) simple private-pay and waiver billing; (5) pricing per bed that makes sense for six beds.

## Target Customer
Owner-operators of small homes in states that license them in large numbers.

## Impact If Solved
Cutting the owner's paperwork from ~8–10 hours a week to ~3–4 (estimate) gives back ~250–300 hours a year, much of which today comes out of her nights.

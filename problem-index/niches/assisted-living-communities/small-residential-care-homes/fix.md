# The Owner Who Sleeps at the Home When a Caregiver Quits

**Niche:** [[niches/assisted-living-communities/small-residential-care-homes/profile|Small Residential Care Homes]]
**Industry:** [[industries/assisted-living-communities|Assisted Living Communities]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** When one of three caregivers leaves, the owner of a small home becomes the night shift until she finds a replacement.
**Tags:** #time-series-forecasting #logistic-regression #workflow-orchestration #worker-facing #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to take the owner-operator off the night and weekend schedule of a six-to-sixteen-bed home, and whoever covers the home's round-the-clock staffing requirement with the fewest owner hours takes the account.

## The Problem
A small home runs on two or three caregivers working long shifts or live-in rotations. Losing one means weeks of the owner covering nights while she recruits, trains and waits for a background check to clear.

## Why It's Still Broken
Owners recruit by word of mouth and job boards, can't match chain wages, and have no early warning that a caregiver is about to leave. Industry turnover for aides ran near 40% in Ziegler's 2026 survey, and in a three-person team one departure is a third of the staff.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A simple retention and hiring kit: early-warning signals from schedules and call-offs (more swaps, late arrivals), stay interviews prompted at the right moments, a standing list of pre-cleared candidates shared among nearby homes, and onboarding packets that track the state's training deadlines.

## Who Feels the Pain
Owner-operators, remaining caregivers who absorb the extra shifts, and residents who notice when tired staff change.

## Impact If Fixed
Cutting the gap between a departure and a trained replacement from ~6 weeks to ~2 (estimate) saves the owner ~150–200 hours of covered shifts per departure.

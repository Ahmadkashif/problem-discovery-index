# Small Residential Care Homes

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/assisted-living-communities|Assisted Living Communities]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to take the owner-operator off the night and weekend schedule of a six-to-sixteen-bed home, and whoever covers the home's round-the-clock staffing requirement with the fewest owner hours takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$400M a year in software, relief staffing, training and consulting bought by homes licensed for about 16 or fewer residents (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~0.5% of NAICS 6233 revenue (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — schedules on a whiteboard or group text, medication records on paper or a basic eMAR, billing in a spreadsheet, the owner's phone as the on-call system
**Target Buyer:** Owner-operator, often a nurse or former caregiver who owns one to three homes
**Automation Potential:** Medium — scheduling, records and billing automate; a small home's staffing is still a few people who must be awake and present

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Assisted living is not only 100-unit buildings. The average licensed community has 33 beds and 42.7% are independently owned (NCAL), and many states license converted houses for six to sixteen residents under assisted living or residential care rules (recalled; California's RCFE licence is the best-known example). In a six-bed home, one empty bed is a sixth of revenue, and two or three caregivers must cover every hour of the week. When one quits or calls off, the owner covers the shift herself, often overnight. Software is priced and designed for chains: multi-building dashboards, CRM pipelines, enterprise implementation. Group homes for people with intellectual and developmental disabilities, which look similar, belong to [[industries/idd-group-homes|IDD Group Homes]] and are excluded here.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Small-home eMAR and charting apps, generic scheduling apps, local placement agents for move-ins, and state association training. Gaps: no relief pool shared across small homes, no affordable records tool that covers medications, service plans and state forms together, and no cover when the owner is sick.

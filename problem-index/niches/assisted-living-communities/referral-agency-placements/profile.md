# Referral Agency Placements & Fee Disputes

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/assisted-living-communities|Assisted Living Communities]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to decide which move-ins a referral agency is owed a fee for, and whoever holds the timestamped first-contact record that both the community and the agency accept takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1B a year in referral fees paid by communities (estimate: ~550,000 assisted living move-ins a year, ~30% through paid agencies, ~$6,000 a placement)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~1.3% of NAICS 6233 revenue (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — agencies send leads through portals and email, but disputes over who introduced a family are argued by phone and spreadsheet
**Target Buyer:** VP of Sales or revenue cycle director at a chain; executive director at an independent
**Automation Potential:** High for matching, timestamping and invoice reconciliation; relationship management with agencies stays human

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A Place for Mom lists ~14,000 communities, and by its own description the community pays "first month's rent and care" for each placement (Senate Aging Committee letter, 2024). Caring.com and hundreds of local placement agents work on similar terms (named from recall). The same family often calls the community directly, fills in a web form, and gets the community's name from an agency, all in one week. Who is owed the fee depends on the agency's registration rules and on whose record shows first contact. The money is large relative to margin, and the relationship matters: agencies send families to the communities that pay reliably. The contest here is attribution, not conversion, which is why it is split from [[niches/assisted-living-communities/owned-lead-tour-conversion/profile|🎯 Owned-Lead Response & Tour Conversion]].

## Current Tools & Gaps
Agency partner portals, CRM lead-source fields, call tracking, and business-office spreadsheets of invoices. Gaps: no matching across households, phone numbers and spellings; timestamps that differ between systems; and no evidence pack when a fee is disputed.

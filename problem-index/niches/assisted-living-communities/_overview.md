# Niche Analysis — Assisted Living Communities

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/assisted-living-communities|Assisted Living Communities]]

## Niche Selection

An assisted living community earns its money twice: once when a family chooses it, usually within weeks of a crisis and often through a referral agency, and every month after, through base rent set against the market and a care fee set by a nurse's assessment. It answers to a state licensing agency, not a federal one, and life plan communities add an entrance-fee contract with refund promises. The eight niches below split the industry by what vendors, consultants and operators' own teams actually compete over. Market sizes are estimates of the staff time, services and software spent within each niche, not of community revenue, which was $78.4B across NAICS 6233 in 2022 (Census Service Annual Survey).

## Why These Niches

The two high-market-share niches are the two ways revenue arrives. Move-in sales fills units in a mostly private-pay business where occupancy is the whole game, and level-of-care billing decides whether the care aides deliver is paid for. The two low-digitized niches are where binders, faxes and consultants' spreadsheets still run high-stakes work: fifty different state rulebooks with unannounced inspections, and life plan communities' refund promises projected only when the actuary visits. The two underserved audiences are memory care residents, who cannot use a call light or explain a fall and are served by tools built for staffed nurses' stations, and the small residential care homes whose owner-operators are ignored by software priced for chains. The two automatable niches are the most mechanical: pricing vacant units and annual increases from demand data, and keeping Medicaid waiver authorisations and claims current against fixed rules and dates.

## Filter Notes

Seven of the eight level-1 niches are terminal. **Move-In Sales & Lead Conversion** is not. Its partial sentence, turning a family's search into a signed move-in, produced two contests with different winners. Deciding which channel is owed a fee for a family is won by whoever holds a first-contact record both community and agency accept; referral agencies, CRM vendors and call-tracking tools fight over it, and the scorecard is fees paid against fees owed. Converting the inquiries a community already owns is won by whoever turns the highest share of them into tours; sales teams, CRM agents and voice-AI vendors fight over it, and the scorecard is inquiry-to-tour conversion. The niche decomposes into **Referral Agency Placements & Fee Disputes** and **Owned-Lead Response & Tour Conversion**.

Three terminal calls were close. **Resident Acuity Assessments & Level-of-Care Billing** and **Street-Rate & Renewal Pricing** both set what a resident pays. They are kept apart because the care fee is set by a nurse from the resident's health and the rent by a revenue manager from the market, with different data, buyers and failure modes. **Medicaid Waiver Billing** also turns on assessments, but the assessor is the state or a managed care plan, the rate is fixed, and the clock is an authorisation expiry, so its contest is about deadlines, not pricing. **Small Residential Care Homes** looks as if it bundles licensing, records and staffing. The owner-operator, though, judges every vendor on one result, whether it takes her off the night and weekend schedule, so the contest is narrowed to round-the-clock staffing cover. Licensing and records sit in their own niches and serve the small home only as features.

Four candidates were considered and rejected. **Caregiver scheduling and agency labour** is the industry's largest cost, but its contests are the ones already split under skilled nursing, keeping the community's own staff on the schedule and filling same-day holes. They are linked rather than profiled again: [[niches/skilled-nursing-facilities/internal-scheduling-retention/profile|🎯 Internal Scheduling, Float Pools & Retention]] and [[niches/skilled-nursing-facilities/per-diem-shift-marketplaces/profile|🎯 Per-Diem Shift Marketplaces & Agency Labour]]. **Dining services** is a large spend whose contest is food cost per resident day, a generic price contest that fails the filter; contract foodservice is covered in [[niches/restaurant-tech-platforms/non-commercial-foodservice/profile|🟣 Non-Commercial Foodservice]]. **Family communication apps** compete on ease of use, a generic contest. **Independent living and active adult** units are apartment leasing without care, outside this industry's licensed-care boundary. Home care agencies, including the AxisCare customer base, belong to [[industries/home-health-agencies|Home Health Agencies]], and group homes for people with intellectual and developmental disabilities to [[industries/idd-group-homes|IDD Group Homes]].

## Why These Pockets

**No pocket qualifies.** Assisted living fails for a different reason than skilled nursing. Nursing homes are surrounded by large analytical teams fenced by HIPAA and federal programmes. Assisted living has no federal payment system and no federal survey, so the large audit, case-mix and quality-measure industries that grew around nursing homes never formed here. What remains is small, internal or built on clients' records. The highest score, the valuation and feasibility consultancies (45), sells the analysis itself in a repeatable format. But its clocks are clients' financing dates and its comparables corpus is built from borrowers' operating statements under engagement, so it carries a "data isn't theirs" switch. The software vendors' data teams (34) sit on the care and billing records of thousands of communities, held under service agreements and business associate agreements, which carry a live switch; their CRM benchmark half is undetermined because the terms are unread, and the verdict follows the care-record half. The long-term care insurers (34) hold decades of claims as protected health information.

The clean pockets are internal or thin. The REITs that hold large operating portfolios under RIDEA (30) and the large operators (34) analyse their own data deeply and invoice nothing. The fall-detection vendors (37) hold the best labelled corpus in dementia care, captured by their own sensors, but bundle the analysis into a subscription with no external deadline. The referral platforms (25) sell placements, not research. Everything else fails the gate. Rating agencies, actuaries, associations, survey vendors and state regulators each employ fewer than ten analysts on this industry, or only production staff.

For a builder it means the opening is in public state inspection records and operators' own pricing and care data, sold to chains, REITs and insurers, and in the vendors already hiring engineers.

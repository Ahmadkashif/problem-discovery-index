# The Reassessment That Waits for the Calendar

**Niche:** [[niches/assisted-living-communities/acuity-level-of-care-billing/profile|Resident Acuity Assessments & Level-of-Care Billing]]
**Industry:** [[industries/assisted-living-communities|Assisted Living Communities]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A resident comes back from hospital needing twice the help, and the paperwork to change her care level waits until the nurse has a free afternoon.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #workflow-orchestration #automation #quick-win #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to bill each resident for the care staff actually deliver, and whoever turns caregivers' daily documentation into an evidenced level-of-care change before the next invoice takes the account.

## The Problem
Change-of-condition reassessments are required in most states after a hospital stay or significant decline (recalled), but the wellness nurse is also doing move-in assessments, medication oversight and family calls. The reassessment slips, and with it the service plan and the fee.

## Why It's Still Broken
Nothing tells the nurse which residents have changed. Hospital returns are logged in one place, falls in another, and caregivers' notes in a third. Family notice periods add a further delay once the change is decided.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A daily list for the wellness nurse: residents back from hospital, with new falls, new medications or a jump in documented assistance, each with a pre-filled reassessment showing the evidence. Once she signs, the service plan, the caregiver task list and the family notice update together.

## Who Feels the Pain
Wellness nurses behind on paperwork; caregivers doing unplanned work; business offices explaining sudden fee jumps to families.

## Impact If Fixed
Closing reassessments within a week of a hospital return instead of a month (estimate) recovers ~3 weeks of the new care fee per event, ~$300–400 each; at ~2–4 events a month in an 80-unit building that is ~$7,000–19,000 a year (estimate).

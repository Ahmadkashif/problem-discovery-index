# Points-Based Assessment Tools Calibrated to Measured Care Time

**Niche:** [[niches/assisted-living-communities/acuity-level-of-care-billing/profile|Resident Acuity Assessments & Level-of-Care Billing]]
**Industry:** [[industries/assisted-living-communities|Assisted Living Communities]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Every chain's care-points tool assigns minutes to tasks; few have checked those minutes against what aides actually spend.
**Tags:** #linear-regression #gradient-boosting #feature-engineering #evaluation-metrics #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to bill each resident for the care staff actually deliver, and whoever turns caregivers' daily documentation into an evidenced level-of-care change before the next invoice takes the account.

## The Problem
Care tiers were set years ago by a clinical team estimating minutes per task. Residents with dementia behaviours or night-time needs consume far more time than the points imply, and the price list never learns.

## What Already Exists
EHR assessment modules and chains' own points tools, plus older industry calculators such as NCAL's AL Cost Calculator, which estimated monthly direct-care cost across 25 health and service categories (search summary).

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs: (1) time-and-motion sampling or sensor-measured care time on a sample of residents; (2) a regression of measured minutes on assessment items, cognition and behaviours to re-weight points; (3) state rules on what a care-level change requires (notice periods, physician input); (4) a price list derived from re-weighted minutes and local wage rates; (5) annual recalibration as the resident mix changes.

## Target Customer
VP of clinical services and revenue leaders at regional chains.

## Impact If Solved
Re-weighting a tool so memory care and behaviour items carry their true time typically moves a few hundred dollars a month for the heaviest-care residents (estimate), ~$30,000–60,000 a year per 80-unit community.

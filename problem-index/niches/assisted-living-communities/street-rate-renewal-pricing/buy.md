# Multifamily Revenue Management with a Care-Fee Layer

**Niche:** [[niches/assisted-living-communities/street-rate-renewal-pricing/profile|Street-Rate & Renewal Pricing]]
**Industry:** [[industries/assisted-living-communities|Assisted Living Communities]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Apartment pricing engines are mature; they assume a 12-month lease and rent as the whole price.
**Tags:** #time-series-forecasting #gradient-boosting #survival-analysis #feature-engineering #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to set each vacant unit's street rate and each resident's annual increase from live demand, and whoever raises revenue per occupied unit without a rise in move-outs in the 90 days after the increase takes the account.

## The Problem
Operators that tried apartment revenue management found its recommendations ignored care revenue, community fees and referral costs, and that its expiration curves did not exist in a business without leases.

## What Already Exists
Multifamily revenue management engines that forecast demand by unit type and recommend daily asking rents and renewal offers.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs: (1) revenue per unit including expected care fees by acuity; (2) length-of-stay survival curves replacing lease expirations; (3) inquiry and tour data from the senior living CRM as demand signals; (4) referral-fee cost by source; (5) annual-increase logic for in-place residents; (6) each operator's own data plus public market data, given antitrust scrutiny of pooled rent algorithms (recalled).

## Target Customer
Revenue management leads at chains of 20+ communities.

## Impact If Solved
A 1–2% gain in revenue per occupied unit (estimate) is ~$53,000–106,000 a year for an 80-unit community billing ~$5.3M.

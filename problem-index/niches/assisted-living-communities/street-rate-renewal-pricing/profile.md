# Street-Rate & Renewal Pricing

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/assisted-living-communities|Assisted Living Communities]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to set each vacant unit's street rate and each resident's annual increase from live demand, and whoever raises revenue per occupied unit without a rise in move-outs in the 90 days after the increase takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$300M a year in revenue management software, pricing analysts, market data subscriptions and pricing consultants (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~0.4% of NAICS 6233 revenue (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — large owners run pricing teams and data platforms; most operators set rates in spreadsheets from call-arounds and NIC MAP reports
**Target Buyer:** Revenue management lead or VP of Sales at a chain; REIT asset managers
**Automation Potential:** High — demand forecasting, rate recommendations and increase modelling are data work; owners and executive directors approve

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Pricing is now where the money moves. NIC MAP put assisted living occupancy at 89.1% in Q3 2026, with inventory growing only 0.4% a year. Welltower's senior housing RevPOR rose 5.2% in Q2 2026 and Brookdale's 5.2%. Ventas's CEO credits "VentasOI" with calibrating rate and occupancy. An assisted living price has two parts: street rent for a vacancy, and an annual increase applied to residents who already live there, often all at once. Raise either too far and families move residents out, complain, or go to the press; leave them too low and a full building underearns for two years. The care fee is set separately, by assessment, in [[niches/assisted-living-communities/acuity-level-of-care-billing/profile|🔵 Resident Acuity Assessments & Level-of-Care Billing]].

## Current Tools & Gaps
Multifamily revenue management engines (named from recall), REITs' in-house platforms, NIC MAP market data, and consultants. Gaps: no length-of-stay or acuity in demand models, no measured link between increase size and move-outs, and rate sheets built on competitor call-arounds.

# The January Increase Letter Sent to Everyone at Once

**Niche:** [[niches/assisted-living-communities/street-rate-renewal-pricing/profile|Street-Rate & Renewal Pricing]]
**Industry:** [[industries/assisted-living-communities|Assisted Living Communities]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Many communities send every resident the same increase letter on the same day, then spend February handling the fallout.
**Tags:** #causal-inference #gradient-boosting #workflow-orchestration #revenue-impact #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to set each vacant unit's street rate and each resident's annual increase from live demand, and whoever raises revenue per occupied unit without a rise in move-outs in the 90 days after the increase takes the account.

## The Problem
A uniform increase lands on a resident paying well below today's street rate and on one who moved in last month at full price. Families compare notes, complaints pile up at the executive director's door, and some residents leave.

## Why It's Still Broken
Uniform increases are easy to administer and defend. Operators lack the data to set individual increases fairly, and fear accusations of targeting residents who cannot move.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Increases set by rules the operator can explain: the gap between a resident's rate and the current street rate, time since move-in, and care-level changes already absorbed. Increases are staggered on move-in anniversaries rather than in one month, and letters are drafted with the reason and the family's options.

## Who Feels the Pain
Executive directors fielding complaints; families on fixed incomes; residents who move out for a few hundred dollars.

## Impact If Fixed
If staggered, explained increases avoid two move-outs a year in an 80-unit community (estimate), they save ~$12,000–25,000 in vacancy and referral fees, plus the director's February.

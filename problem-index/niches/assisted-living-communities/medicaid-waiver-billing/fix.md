# The Claim Denied Because the Service Plan Expired Last Week

**Niche:** [[niches/assisted-living-communities/medicaid-waiver-billing/profile|Medicaid Waiver Billing & Level-of-Care Reassessments]]
**Industry:** [[industries/assisted-living-communities|Assisted Living Communities]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The care was given every day; the payment is lost because a signature date passed.
**Tags:** #workflow-orchestration #automation #compliance #quick-win #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep each waiver resident's authorisation current and the month's claim paid on first submission, and whoever files every reassessment and reauthorisation before it lapses takes the account.

## The Problem
Waiver payment requires a current service plan signed by the right people and an authorisation covering the dates of service. A missed renewal by a few days can void a month's claim, and retroactive approval is slow or impossible.

## Why It's Still Broken
Renewal dates live in the care manager's system, the community's EHR and a business-office spreadsheet, which disagree. The wellness nurse who updates the plan is also doing move-in assessments.

## What a Fix Looks Like
One renewal calendar per resident drawn from all three sources, with tasks assigned to the nurse, the care manager contact and the resident's representative, escalating as dates approach, and a hold on claim submission until the plan and authorisation are confirmed current.

## Who Feels the Pain
Business office managers, wellness nurses, and communities whose Medicaid margin is thin to begin with.

## Impact If Fixed
Avoiding one lapsed month per year in a 20-resident waiver census (estimate) saves ~$2,000–3,000 of service revenue and the hours spent appealing it.

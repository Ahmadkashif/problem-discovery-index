# Memory Care Residents Living with Dementia

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/assisted-living-communities|Assisted Living Communities]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get a caregiver to a resident living with dementia before the unwitnessed fall or the open door, and whoever cuts the minutes from event to caregiver arrival takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.2B a year in fall-detection video and sensors, secured doors and wander management, dementia-care programmes and staff training sold into assisted living and memory care (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~1.5% of NAICS 6233 revenue (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — most rooms have no sensors, door alarms are binary and ignored, and falls are reconstructed from an aide's account at the end of the shift
**Target Buyer:** Chief operating or clinical officer and memory care director at a chain; executive director at an independent
**Automation Potential:** Medium — detection, alert ranking and documentation automate; redirecting a frightened resident at 3 a.m. does not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Memory care is the highest-priced, highest-risk part of assisted living: secured neighbourhoods for residents with dementia, staffed more heavily by day (about one caregiver per 5–8 residents, Ziegler 2026) and thinly at night. 44% of residential care residents had a dementia in 2022 (NCHS). The resident cannot use a call light reliably or explain what happened. The Washington Post counted more than 2,000 senior living residents who wandered away since 2018, 98 of whom died. Families pay a premium and judge the community on falls and safety, and referral data show memory care families most often exceed their budget (55% in APFM's own figures, Senate Aging Committee letter). This is assisted living's memory care unit, not the certified nursing home's long-stay residents measured on the MDS, which are [[niches/skilled-nursing-facilities/long-stay-dementia-residents/profile|🟣 Long-Stay Residents Living with Dementia]].

## Current Tools & Gaps
SafelyYou (AI video fall detection with post-fall clinical review), Inspiren (AUGi room sensors and e-call), Teton.ai, wander-management door systems, and dementia-care training programmes. Gaps: alerts designed for a staffed nurses' station rather than one roaming caregiver, no link between night-time patterns and the resident's care level, and door alarms with no context.

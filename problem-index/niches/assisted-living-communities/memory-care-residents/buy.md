# Fall-Detection Video Adapted to One Caregiver per Hall

**Niche:** [[niches/assisted-living-communities/memory-care-residents/profile|Memory Care Residents Living with Dementia]]
**Industry:** [[industries/assisted-living-communities|Assisted Living Communities]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Fall detection works; the alert still has to reach the one person on the floor, ranked against everything else she is doing.
**Tags:** #cnns #object-detection #evaluation-metrics #worker-facing #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get a caregiver to a resident living with dementia before the unwitnessed fall or the open door, and whoever cuts the minutes from event to caregiver arrival takes the account.

## The Problem
At night one or two caregivers cover the neighbourhood (estimate). Alerts from cameras, bed sensors, door contacts and call lights arrive on different devices with no priority.

## What Already Exists
SafelyYou's AI video with post-fall review (a vendor study reported 75% fewer EMT visits after falls in 11 memory care communities), Inspiren's sensors and e-call, Teton.ai's computer vision, and nurse-call systems.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs: (1) one ranked alert stream across devices on the caregiver's phone; (2) urgency by event type and resident risk (floor beats bed exit beats bathroom); (3) awareness of where the caregiver is and what she is doing; (4) auto-documented response times for the shift record; (5) tuning to roommates, low light and residents who sit on the floor by habit.

## Target Customer
Memory care directors at chains already piloting fall-detection hardware.

## Impact If Solved
Cutting average time from fall to caregiver arrival from ~15 to ~5 minutes (estimate) shortens time on the floor, a driver of injury and ED transfer, for the ~20–40 falls a year in a typical neighbourhood (estimate).

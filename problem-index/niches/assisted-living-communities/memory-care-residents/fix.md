# The Door Alarm Everyone Has Learned to Ignore

**Niche:** [[niches/assisted-living-communities/memory-care-residents/profile|Memory Care Residents Living with Dementia]]
**Industry:** [[industries/assisted-living-communities|Assisted Living Communities]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Exit doors alarm many times a day for staff, visitors and deliveries, so the one alarm that matters gets the same shrug.
**Tags:** #cnns #object-detection #change-point-detection #automation #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get a caregiver to a resident living with dementia before the unwitnessed fall or the open door, and whoever cuts the minutes from event to caregiver arrival takes the account.

## The Problem
Common causes in the elopements the Washington Post documented were doors left improperly unlocked and alarms that did not properly alert staff (search summary). In daily life, alarms sound for vendors, family members who know the code and staff taking out rubbish, and staff silence them by reflex.

## Why It's Still Broken
Door systems know that a door opened, not who opened it or whether a resident was nearby. Keypad codes get shared. Nobody reviews alarm logs until after an incident.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Door events joined to camera or sensor context and resident location: an alarm escalates only when a resident at risk is near or through the door, with a photo and direction of travel sent to the nearest caregiver. Weekly reports show which doors and codes generate false alarms so they can be fixed.

## Who Feels the Pain
Night and weekend caregivers, memory care directors, and families who trusted a "secured" unit.

## Impact If Fixed
Cutting nuisance alarms by ~80% (estimate) restores trust in the real ones; each elopement prevented avoids the risk of the death or injury, the state investigation and the lawsuit that follow.

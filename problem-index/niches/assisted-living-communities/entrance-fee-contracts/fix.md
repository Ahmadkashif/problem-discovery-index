# The Refund Owed on a Unit That Hasn't Resold

**Niche:** [[niches/assisted-living-communities/entrance-fee-contracts/profile|Entrance-Fee Contracts & Refund Liabilities]]
**Industry:** [[industries/assisted-living-communities|Assisted Living Communities]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A family waits months for a refund promised "on resale", and the community cannot tell them when that will be.
**Tags:** #survival-analysis #time-series-forecasting #workflow-orchestration #automation #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to price entrance fees and refund terms the community's reserves can honour, and whoever keeps the refund liability fully covered by reserves at every actuarial study takes the account.

## The Problem
Many refundable contracts pay out only when the unit is reoccupied by a new entrance-fee resident. Estates wait, call and sometimes complain to legislators; states are now debating maximum waits (a 2026 Maryland bill would require payment within two years of termination, search summary).

## Why It's Still Broken
Refund queues sit in spreadsheets, units are marketed without regard to which ones carry the oldest refunds, and nobody gives the family a credible estimate.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A refund queue linked to the sales pipeline: each owed refund shows its unit, the projected resale date from current demand, and the expected payout date. Marketing sees which units to prioritise, and the business office sends families a dated estimate.

## Who Feels the Pain
Residents' families and executors; business office staff fielding calls; boards facing reputational and legislative risk.

## Impact If Fixed
Prioritising units with the oldest refunds could cut average refund waits by ~2–3 months (estimate), reducing complaints and the risk of state-imposed deadlines.

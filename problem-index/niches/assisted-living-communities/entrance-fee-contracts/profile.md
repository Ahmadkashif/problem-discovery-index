# Entrance-Fee Contracts & Refund Liabilities

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/assisted-living-communities|Assisted Living Communities]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to price entrance fees and refund terms the community's reserves can honour, and whoever keeps the refund liability fully covered by reserves at every actuarial study takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$250M a year in actuarial studies, entrance-fee accounting, contract and refund administration, and pricing advice for life plan communities (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~0.3% of NAICS 6233 revenue (estimate); ~0.6% of the ~$41.1B CCRC share (derived)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — contracts, refund queues and resale contingencies are tracked in spreadsheets and the accounting system; the actuarial projection is a consultant's model refreshed every few years
**Target Buyer:** CFO of a not-for-profit life plan community or multi-site system
**Automation Potential:** Medium — projection, refund tracking and disclosure drafting automate; pricing and contract design are board decisions

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A life plan community (CCRC) sells a contract, not a lease. A resident pays an entrance fee, often several hundred thousand dollars (estimate), plus a monthly fee, in exchange for housing and access to assisted living, memory care and nursing care as needs grow. Contracts range from all-inclusive (Type A) to fee-for-service (Type C), and many promise a partial refund, often payable only when the unit resells. The entrance fee works like a single-premium life annuity, and ASOP No. 3 governs actuarial reports on CCRCs; some states require one every three or five years for care-inclusive contracts (American Academy of Actuaries). Fitch tracks 169 rated not-for-profit life plan communities with a median rating of BBB, so bondholders watch the same numbers (Senior Housing News, 2026). Ziegler's database counts ~1,955 life plan communities (search summary).

## Current Tools & Gaps
Consulting actuaries, CPA firms' senior living practices, general ledger systems with deferred-revenue schedules, and senior living suites' contract modules. Gaps: refund liabilities projected rarely, no unit-level view of resale delays, and pricing set by comparison with competitors rather than by projected cost of care.

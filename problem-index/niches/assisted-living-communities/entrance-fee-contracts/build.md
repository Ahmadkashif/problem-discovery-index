# A Unit-by-Unit Refund Liability Model

**Niche:** [[niches/assisted-living-communities/entrance-fee-contracts/profile|Entrance-Fee Contracts & Refund Liabilities]]
**Industry:** [[industries/assisted-living-communities|Assisted Living Communities]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A life plan community knows its refund promises to the dollar and its likely timing only when the actuary visits every few years.
**Tags:** #survival-analysis #monte-carlo-methods #probability-distributions #time-series-forecasting #compliance #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to price entrance fees and refund terms the community's reserves can honour, and whoever keeps the refund liability fully covered by reserves at every actuarial study takes the account.

## The Problem
Each resident's contract carries a refund percentage, a resale contingency and a care entitlement. The CFO's cash plan depends on when residents die, move to the care centre or leave, and how fast units resell, but the projection is refreshed only with each actuarial study.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The actuarial work is done by small specialist practices in bespoke spreadsheet models. Community-level data is small, so mortality, morbidity and transfer rates must be borrowed from industry tables. Software vendors treat contracts as billing records, not as liabilities.

## What to Build
A model that holds every contract and resident and simulates move-outs, care transfers and deaths with survival models calibrated to the community's own history and industry tables. It projects resale times by unit type from the sales pipeline and estimates refund cash flows, care-centre use and reserve adequacy monthly. It re-runs every time a unit turns over or a price changes, and drafts the inputs the consulting actuary and bond disclosures need.

## Target Customer
CFOs of not-for-profit life plan communities and multi-site systems; the actuaries and CPA firms that serve them.

## Impact If Built
For a community holding ~$50–150M of refundable entrance fees and paying out ~$5–10M a year (estimate), seeing refund timing a year earlier avoids a credit-line draw that would cost ~$25,000–50,000 a month of interest at ~6% (estimate) or a covenant scare, and prices each new contract on projected cost instead of the competitor's brochure.

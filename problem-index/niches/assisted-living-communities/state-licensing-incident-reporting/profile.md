# State Licensing Inspections & Incident Reporting

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/assisted-living-communities|Assisted Living Communities]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep a community out of a state citation, and whoever gets every reportable incident filed inside the state's deadline takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$600M a year in compliance staff time, licensing consultants, mock inspections, incident and policy software, and training (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~0.8% of NAICS 6233 revenue (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — policies live in binders, incident reports go to the state by fax, email or a state portal, and readiness for an unannounced visit depends on the executive director's memory
**Target Buyer:** Executive director; VP of compliance or clinical services at a multi-state chain
**Automation Potential:** Medium-High — rules lookup, reportability checks, deadlines and form filling automate; the walk-through and the conversation with the inspector stay human

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Assisted living has no federal certification, survey or star rating. Each state licenses communities, inspects them on its own cycle and after complaints, and sets its own rules for staffing, training, medication handling, dementia care and incident reporting (recalled). The rules move: 18 states changed theirs in 2025, most often on direct-care staff training (NCAL). Inspection reports are public in many states, which makes citations a reputational as well as regulatory risk, but 19 states publish no downloadable reports (Washington Post, 2023). Nursing homes are surveyed against one federal rulebook, which is why their survey-readiness tools, described in [[niches/skilled-nursing-facilities/survey-readiness-2567/profile|🟠 State Survey Readiness & Plans of Correction]], do not transfer.

## Current Tools & Gaps
EHR incident modules, policy-manual subscriptions, state association training, licensing consultants and former state inspectors. Gaps: no maintained 50-state rules engine, no check of whether an incident note describes a reportable event, and no readiness view built from a state's own inspection history.

# Benefit Estimates & Final Calculations

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/pension-funds|Pension Funds]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to produce an estimate that matches the final benefit and a final benefit that never needs recalculating, and whoever issues final calculations within the plan's service standard with the fewest post-retirement recalculations takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$700M a year of calculation staff, member counseling time and calculation tooling across US public, multiemployer and corporate DB administration (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~5% of pension funds' non-investment-management operating spend (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — calculation engines exist inside PAS platforms, but final calculations still involve manual verification of service, salary and purchased credit
**Target Buyer:** Director of Member Services / Benefits Manager
**Automation Potential:** Very high for standard cases; complex cases (reciprocity, disability, domestic relations orders) need an analyst

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Every member who retires receives at least one estimate and one final calculation, and many receive several estimates over the years. The formula is codified (service × multiplier × final average salary, adjusted for age, tier and payment option), but the inputs are not always clean. Purchased service, leaves of absence, part-time service, final-average-salary windows, reciprocity with other systems and domestic relations orders all need checking. Final calculations normally take four to five weeks in Alaska and stretched to 12–14 weeks during a staffing shortage. In North Carolina, estimate and calculation questions make up much of a call volume that rose 17% in a year. The clock is the member's retirement date and the first payment, which members plan their lives around.

## Current Tools & Gaps
PAS calculation engines, member self-service estimate portals, and DB administration outsourcers for corporate plans. The gaps: estimates are produced on unverified records, so they differ from the final calculation; final calculations wait for employers' last payroll reports and for staff to verify history; and no one measures the gap between estimate and final across the membership.

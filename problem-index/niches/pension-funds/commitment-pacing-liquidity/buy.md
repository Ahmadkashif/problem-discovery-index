# Corporate Treasury Cash Forecasting Adapted to Unfunded Commitments

**Niche:** [[niches/pension-funds/commitment-pacing-liquidity/profile|Commitment Pacing & Liquidity Planning]]
**Industry:** [[industries/pension-funds|Pension Funds]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Corporate treasuries forecast cash daily across accounts and entities; a pension's investment office forecasts its biggest liquidity draw once a year in a spreadsheet.
**Tags:** #time-series-forecasting #exponential-smoothing #data-integration #workflow-orchestration #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to forecast the programme's capital calls, distributions and NAV under stress well enough to set next year's commitment budget, and whoever keeps the plan inside its allocation ranges through a drawdown without forced sales of liquid assets takes the account.

## The Problem
The plan must hold enough liquidity for benefit payments, capital calls, collateral on derivatives and rebalancing. The inputs sit in different places: the custodian, the monitoring platform, the actuary's benefit projection and the finance office's contribution schedule.

## What Already Exists
Treasury management systems such as Kyriba and SAP treasury modules aggregate bank positions, forecast cash flows by category, run scenarios and set liquidity buffers for corporations.

## The Customization Gap
The pension version needs: unfunded commitments as a liability with uncertain timing; call and distribution forecasts by fund feeding the cash forecast; benefit payments and employer contributions from the actuarial projection; the policy allocation ranges as constraints; and stress scenarios that move public markets, private NAVs and distributions together. The output is a board-level liquidity report rather than a treasurer's daily position.

## Target Customer
Investment operations and risk teams at public plans with large private programmes and derivatives overlays.

## Impact If Solved
One liquidity view across all sources, updated monthly, instead of an annual study and a scramble when markets move.

# Multiemployer Pension Fund Offices

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/pension-funds|Pension Funds]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to post every contributing employer's monthly remittance to the right participant's hours and pension credit, and whoever credits every reported hour correctly with the fewest delinquencies and credited-service disputes takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1B a year of administrative spend at US multiemployer defined benefit plans: fund office staff, third-party administrators, payroll compliance audits and systems (estimate, against ~$729B of assets in 1,341 plans in 2023)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~7% of pension funds' non-investment-management operating spend (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — remittance reports still arrive as spreadsheets, PDFs and paper from small contractors
**Target Buyer:** Fund Administrator, reporting to the joint board of trustees
**Automation Potential:** High for remittance capture and hours posting; delinquency collection and disputes stay human

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A multiemployer (Taft-Hartley) pension covers workers in a trade (construction, trucking, entertainment, food) who move between many employers. The plan does not get salary from one payroll. It gets monthly remittance reports from hundreds or thousands of contributing employers, many of them small contractors. Each report lists hours worked per member, and pension credit, vesting and breaks in service are computed from those hours under the plan's rules. A board of union and employer trustees governs the plan and hires an administrator, a fund office or a third-party administrator. The plans covered 10.7M participants in 2023 (DOL Form 5500). Many are still working through PBGC's Special Financial Assistance, about $70B to 108 plans by January 2025. The clock is the monthly remittance due date and the actuary's zone certification by the 90th day of the plan year. Health and welfare funds run by the same offices belong to [[industries/self-insured-benefit-pools|Self-Insured Benefit & Risk Pools]], where the parallel hours-to-coverage contest is [[niches/self-insured-benefit-pools/hour-bank-eligibility/profile|🟠 Hour-Bank Eligibility & Self-Pay at Health and Welfare Funds]] and the outsourced fund offices are logged as [[niches/self-insured-benefit-pools/taft-hartley-fund-administrators/profile|🔍 Taft-Hartley Fund Administrators]].

## Current Tools & Gaps
Fund offices run benefit administration systems built for multiemployer plans or in-house databases. Employer online remittance portals cover the larger contributors. Payroll compliance auditors visit employers on a rotating schedule, and fund counsel pursues delinquencies. The gaps: small employers still send unstructured reports; hours posted to the wrong member surface only at retirement; and audit selection does not use the remittance data.

# Niche Analysis — Pension Funds

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/pension-funds|Pension Funds]]

## Niche Selection

A pension fund runs two offices on different clocks. The investment office commits capital, increasingly to private funds it cannot exit for a decade. The benefits office keeps a 40-year record of service and pay for every member and turns it into a monthly payment for life. Both make judgements that are graded long after the people who made them have left. The eight niches below split the industry by what vendors, advisers and internal teams actually compete over. Market sizes are estimates of the tooling, data, administration and advisory spend within each niche, not of pension assets or benefit payments. The parent denominator for "share" is pension funds' non-investment-management operating spend, estimated at ~$15B a year.

## Why These Niches

Private markets programmes lead on market share because they are where public pension money and attention have moved: alternatives rose to 35% of public pension assets by 2022. Every decision there is irreversible and documented by hundreds of GPs in different formats. Pension administration system modernisation is second because it is the largest technology spend a retirement system makes ($60 million over ten years in Iowa's case) and the riskiest moment in benefits administration. The two low-digitized niches are where member records are still made by hand: employer payroll reports whose pensionability nobody checks until retirement, and multiemployer remittances keyed from small contractors' spreadsheets. The two underserved audiences are the ~4,600 small local public plans run by part-time boards, and the mid-size corporate sponsor preparing a frozen plan for exit with no pension staff. In 2025, more than 80% of buy-out contracts written through the third quarter were under $50M. The two automatable niches are the most rule-bound, high-volume work in administration: calculating benefits from a record, and stopping payments to the dead or the impersonated before each payroll.

## Filter Notes

Seven of the eight level-1 niches are terminal. Private Markets Programmes at Public Plans is not: its contested sentence produced two contests with different winners. One is reconciliation, turning every GP statement, notice and partnership agreement into NAV, cash flow, fee and carry records that tie to the custodian and the LPA before the board's quarterly review and the annual public fee report. The other is forecasting, setting next year's commitment budget so the programme stays inside its allocation ranges through a drawdown. Inputs differ (documents against cash-flow histories and scenarios), clocks differ (quarter-end and the statutory fee report against the annual pacing plan) and winners differ (monitoring platforms and operations teams against consultants and quant teams). It decomposes into **Private Markets Monitoring & Fee Verification** and **Commitment Pacing & Liquidity Planning**. The re-up judgement itself, the high-impact problem, stays with the parent because both contests feed it.

Three terminal calls were close. **Employer Contribution Reporting** and **Benefit Estimates & Final Calculations** touch the same error, a misreported pay item caught either in the pay period or at the final calculation. They stay apart because input, clock and buyer differ: the employer's payroll file each pay period, owned by employer services, against the member's whole record at retirement, owned by member services. **Mid-Size Corporate Sponsors With Frozen Plans** could split into lump-sum windows and buy-outs, but one contest governs both: data clean enough to price. **Multiemployer Pension Fund Offices** could have split off withdrawal liability, but that is an actuarial and legal contest belonging to the plan's actuary and counsel (logged as a Pass 2 pocket below), so the fund office's contest, hours to credit, is terminal.

Four candidates were considered and rejected. **Board governance and trustee education at large public plans** fails the filter: the contest is generic (better board portals and training), and the small-plan version is already captured in niche 5. **Liability-driven investing for corporate plans** belongs to [[industries/asset-managers|Asset Managers]] and the consultants who design the glide paths, not to the plan's own staff. **Retiree health (OPEB) trusts** belong to the risk-pool and municipal territory, [[industries/self-insured-benefit-pools|Self-Insured Benefit & Risk Pools]] (see [[niches/self-insured-benefit-pools/retiree-medical-trusts/profile|🟣 Retiree Medical VEBAs & OPEB Trusts]]) and [[industries/municipal-services|Municipal Services]]. **Proxy voting and stewardship at public funds** is already analysed under [[niches/asset-managers/stewardship-and-proxy-voting/profile|🟠 Stewardship & Proxy Voting]] and [[niches/asset-managers/proxy-advisory-firms/profile|🔍 Proxy Advisory Firms]].

## Why These Pockets

No pocket qualifies, and the reason is structural. The pension fund is an asset owner: its research is spent on returns and benefits, never invoiced, and its aggregators (state investment boards and consolidated local pools, pocket 20) are investment offices too. The firms that see across many plans mostly work on data that belongs to the plans: the multiemployer actuaries (49), the investment consultants (44), the outsourced private markets monitoring services (39; the monitoring software vendors are already logged as [[niches/private-equity-firms/portfolio-monitoring-software-vendors/profile|🔍 Portfolio Monitoring & Fund Software Vendors]], 38), the performance universes (35) and the PRT insurers pricing other sponsors' censuses (36). The plaintiff firms that monitor pension portfolios work behind privilege. The neighbours already logged in the vault show the same constraint and are linked rather than re-profiled: [[niches/municipal-services/public-pension-actuarial/profile|🔍 Public Pension Actuarial Services]] (plan data), [[niches/asset-managers/institutional-consultant-manager-research/profile|🔍 Institutional Investment Consultant Manager Research]] (manager confidentiality) and [[niches/private-equity-firms/lp-private-markets-consultants/profile|🔍 LP Private Markets Consultants & GP Due Diligence]] (GP confidentiality). The manager-submitted data and response work on the other side of the consultant relationship sit in [[niches/asset-managers/consultant-database-platforms/profile|🔍 Consultant Database & Manager Search Platforms]] and [[niches/asset-managers/rfp-and-consultant-databases/profile|🟠 RFP & Consultant Database Responses]].

The clean pockets own their data but fall short elsewhere. CEM-style cost benchmarking (44) and pooled longevity data (42) hold corpora no competitor can rebuild, but each is essentially one specialist with a soft clock and a periodic product rather than labour-linear work. Consultants' capital market assumptions (37) are a forecast archive nobody grades, but clients pay for the advice, not the forecast. Two qualifiers already in the vault do work a pension buyer would recognise: [[niches/financial-data-vendors/private-markets-research-teams/profile|🔍 Private Markets Research & Collection Teams]] (52) files public-records requests to pension funds for private fund performance, and [[niches/funeral-homes/death-verification-data-providers/profile|🔍 Death Verification Data Providers]] (54) sells the death matching pension payrolls depend on.

The best clean near-misses tie at 44: cost benchmarking, above, and asset owner board and search intelligence. Dakota, With Intelligence and the trade press read every public board packet and sell what they find to asset managers before RFP deadlines. The research is the invoice and the work scales with coverage, but the sources are public and several firms read the same minutes, so the archive is a head start rather than a moat. Its unused asset, the joined history of which searches went to which managers through which consultants, is the nearest thing here to the vault's favourite shape: a decision record whose outcome the producer holds and does not grade. The highest raw score, the multiemployer actuaries (49), has the only hard statutory clock on the producer itself, the 90th-day zone certification, and is blocked by plan-owned data and counsel-commissioned withdrawal liability work. Custody search sits under [[industries/trust-companies|Trust Companies]], where [[niches/trust-companies/custody-search-consultants/profile|🔍 Custody Search & Fee Benchmarking Consultants]] already fails the gate.

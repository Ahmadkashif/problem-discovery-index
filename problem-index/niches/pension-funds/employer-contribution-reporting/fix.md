# The Employer Audit That Arrives Five Years Late

**Niche:** [[niches/pension-funds/employer-contribution-reporting/profile|Employer Contribution Reporting & Pensionable Pay Review]]
**Industry:** [[industries/pension-funds|Pension Funds]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Employer compliance audits rotate through participating employers every few years, so a misreported pay code runs for years before anyone looks.
**Tags:** #gradient-boosting #evaluation-metrics #compliance #workflow-orchestration #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to validate each employer's payroll report against the plan's pensionability rules in the pay period it arrives, and whoever catches the most non-pensionable pay and tier errors before they reach a benefit calculation takes the account.

## The Problem
A system with a thousand employers and a small audit team reaches each employer every few years (estimate). An error found in an audit has usually affected every pay period since it began, across every member it touched, and correcting it means recalculating contributions and service for all of them.

## Why It's Still Broken
Audit plans are built on rotation and size because there is no risk signal from the reporting data itself. The reporting team and the audit team often sit apart and do not share error histories.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Rank employers by reporting risk every quarter from their own files. Use rejected-record rates, new pay codes, member-level pay spikes, late files and prior findings. Audit the top of the list first, and send the rest a targeted desk review of the specific codes that changed.

## Who Feels the Pain
Employer audit staff; employers billed for years of back corrections; members whose service or benefit changes after an audit.

## Impact If Fixed
Errors are caught in months rather than years, and the audit team's limited hours go where the reporting data says the problems are.

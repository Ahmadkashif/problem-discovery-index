# Employer Contribution Reporting & Pensionable Pay Review

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/pension-funds|Pension Funds]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to validate each employer's payroll report against the plan's pensionability rules in the pay period it arrives, and whoever catches the most non-pensionable pay and tier errors before they reach a benefit calculation takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$400M a year of employer reporting staff, employer compliance audit and reporting-portal tooling at US public retirement systems (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~3% of pension funds' non-investment-management operating spend (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low to medium — files arrive through portals, but pensionability is checked by people, mostly after the fact
**Target Buyer:** Director of Employer Services / Employer Audit Manager
**Automation Potential:** High for rule checks and anomaly flags; disputes and determinations stay with staff

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A public retirement system is a multiple-employer arrangement. School districts, cities, counties and agencies (hundreds to a few thousand per statewide system, estimate) report each member's pay, pay codes, service and contributions each pay period. Whether a pay item counts toward the pension is a statutory question that differs by tier and sometimes by bargaining unit. Errors do not hurt anyone until retirement, when they become benefit cuts, repayment demands or appeals. California's 2022 disallowed-compensation law (Government Code 20164.5) moved most of that cost onto employers, which made early detection an employer concern as well as the system's. The clock is the pay period. The consequence arrives at the final benefit calculation.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Employer portals inside the pension administration system validate formats and required fields. Employer payroll systems produce the files. The retirement system's own audit staff review a sample of employers every few years. Compensation reviews happen at retirement. The gaps: pay codes are mapped to pensionability once, at onboarding, and drift as employers add codes; no member-level history check runs on each file; and audit selection is by rotation rather than risk.

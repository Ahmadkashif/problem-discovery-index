# Board Portal Software Adapted to Pension Trustees

**Niche:** [[niches/pension-funds/small-local-public-plans/profile|Small Local Public Plans]]
**Industry:** [[industries/pension-funds|Pension Funds]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** School boards and city councils run on agenda and minutes software; the town's pension board next door runs on emailed PDFs.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #workflow-orchestration #compliance #quick-win #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to run a small board's whole compliance calendar, from census data for the actuary to GASB 67/68 schedules, state filings and investment monitoring, without the plan hiring staff, and whoever keeps the most small plans off the state regulator's late-filing and non-compliance lists takes the account.

## The Problem
Trustees receive packets from the consultant, actuary and attorney by email, approve items in meetings governed by open-meeting laws, and keep minutes that must be published. Decisions such as a manager hire, an assumption change or a disability approval are hard to find later.

## What Already Exists
Board management and public-meeting platforms used by school boards and municipalities handle agendas, packets, voting records, minutes and public posting.

## The Customization Gap
The pension version needs: standing agenda items driven by the plan's obligations (quarterly investment review, annual valuation adoption, disability hearings); fiduciary records (who voted, on what advice, with what documents) searchable by topic; trustee education tracking against state requirements; confidential handling of member disability and benefit matters inside a public-meeting workflow; and an archive of consultant and actuary reports linked to the decisions they supported.

## Target Customer
Local pension boards, often bought by the municipality's clerk or finance office alongside its existing board software.

## Impact If Solved
A small board gets a fiduciary record it can defend, at the cost of a module on software its town already uses.

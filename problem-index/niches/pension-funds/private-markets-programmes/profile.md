# Private Markets Programmes at Public Plans

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/pension-funds|Pension Funds]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is that every serious competitor is fighting to own the plan's private markets record, but it splits into two contests with different winners: reconciling what GPs report each quarter to custodian records and partnership terms before the board's review, and forecasting calls and distributions well enough to set next year's commitments without breaching allocation ranges in a drawdown. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.5B a year of monitoring software, private markets consulting, data and internal analyst cost spent by US pension plans on their private programmes (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~10% of pension funds' non-investment-management operating spend (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — cash flows and statements are digitised by monitoring platforms; the decision record and the re-up read are not
**Target Buyer:** Deputy CIO / Head of Private Markets
**Automation Potential:** High for document capture and reconciliation, low for the commitment decision itself

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Alternatives were 35% of public pension assets in 2022, up from 27% in 2019 (Pew). Most of that sits in closed-end funds whose capital is called and returned over a decade. The work is distinct from public-markets manager oversight in three ways. Commitments cannot be undone. The data arrives as documents from hundreds of GPs rather than as custodian feeds. And the plan must manage an unfunded commitment book that moves with markets. The investment office, the private markets consultant (see [[niches/private-equity-firms/lp-private-markets-consultants/profile|🔍 LP Private Markets Consultants & GP Due Diligence]]) and the board share the decision, and a public audience reads the fee report.

## Current Tools & Gaps
MSCI's Burgiss, FactSet's Cobalt, Chronograph, Nasdaq's Solovis and Colmore capture GP documents and report performance. Custodians offer private markets accounting. Consultants model pacing and write re-up recommendations. Peer benchmarks come from the firms in [[niches/private-equity-firms/private-capital-benchmark-providers/profile|🔍 Private Capital Performance Benchmark Providers]]. The gaps: no record of what staff believed at each commitment, fees reported rather than recomputed, and pacing models that ignore each GP's own call behaviour.

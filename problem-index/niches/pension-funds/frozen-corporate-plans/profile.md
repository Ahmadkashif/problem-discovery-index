# Mid-Size Corporate Sponsors With Frozen Plans

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/pension-funds|Pension Funds]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get a frozen plan's participant data, benefit definitions and assets clean enough for a lump-sum window or an annuity buy-out, and whoever brings a plan to insurer bids with the narrowest data-uncertainty loading in the premium takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$800M a year of actuarial, de-risking advisory, data clean-up and administration spend by mid-size corporate sponsors preparing frozen DB plans for exit (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~5% of pension funds' non-investment-management operating spend (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — benefits are administered on platforms, but records for long-separated participants are thin
**Target Buyer:** CFO or Treasurer of the sponsoring company
**Automation Potential:** High for data validation and missing-participant searches; the exit decision stays with the sponsor

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Most corporate DB plans are frozen: no new accruals, a shrinking active population, and a sponsor whose goal is to transfer the liability rather than run it. With large corporate plans around 110% funded in mid-2026 (Milliman 100), exits are affordable. In 2025 insurers wrote $31.3B of buy-outs across 683 contracts, and more than 80% of contracts written through the third quarter were under $50M (LIMRA). Those small and mid-size deals are this niche. The sponsor has no pension staff, and its benefits manager has other jobs. Insurers price what they cannot verify: missing addresses, unconfirmed beneficiaries, benefit definitions that were never documented. The sponsor pays that uncertainty in premium.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Actuarial consultants run the projects. PRT insurers bid through placement consultants. DB administrators hold the records, and missing-participant search services find lost members. The gaps: data clean-up starts only when a transaction is planned; benefit definitions for old plan mergers sit in scanned documents; and the sponsor cannot see how much premium its data quality is costing. The insurer side of this market belongs to [[industries/life-insurance-carriers|Life Insurance Carriers]], and the insurers' pricing teams are logged here as a Pass 2 pocket.

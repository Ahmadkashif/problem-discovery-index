# M&A Data Rooms Adapted to Annuity Bidding

**Niche:** [[niches/pension-funds/frozen-corporate-plans/profile|Mid-Size Corporate Sponsors With Frozen Plans]]
**Industry:** [[industries/pension-funds|Pension Funds]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Selling a pension liability to an insurer is a competitive auction with diligence questions, yet it runs on emailed spreadsheets rather than the data-room machinery every M&A sale uses.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #data-integration #workflow-orchestration #automation #compliance
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get a frozen plan's participant data, benefit definitions and assets clean enough for a lump-sum window or an annuity buy-out, and whoever brings a plan to insurer bids with the narrowest data-uncertainty loading in the premium takes the account.

## The Problem
Several insurers receive census files, plan documents and benefit specifications, ask overlapping questions, and submit preliminary and final bids on a timetable. Answers given to one bidder must be given to all, and the record must support the fiduciary's selection under DOL guidance.

## What Already Exists
Virtual data rooms used in M&A (Datasite, Intralinks and others) manage documents, permissions, Q&A workflows and audit trails across bidders.

## The Customization Gap
PRT needs: participant-level census data as structured, versioned files rather than documents; Q&A tagged to data fields so corrections propagate to the census; benefit specifications linked to plan document provisions; a bid comparison that normalises insurer assumptions; and a fiduciary file assembled for the independent fiduciary's annuity-provider selection review.

## Target Customer
Actuarial and placement consultants running PRT transactions for mid-size sponsors; sponsors' treasury teams.

## Impact If Solved
Bidders work from one corrected census, questions stop repeating, and the fiduciary record is built as the transaction runs rather than reconstructed afterwards.

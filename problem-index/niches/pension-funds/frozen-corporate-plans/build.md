# A Buy-Out Readiness Score Insurers Would Price On

**Niche:** [[niches/pension-funds/frozen-corporate-plans/profile|Mid-Size Corporate Sponsors With Frozen Plans]]
**Industry:** [[industries/pension-funds|Pension Funds]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A sponsor learns how clean its plan data is from the insurer's bid, after it has already paid for the uncertainty.
**Tags:** #gradient-boosting #logistic-regression #feature-engineering #evaluation-metrics #data-integration #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get a frozen plan's participant data, benefit definitions and assets clean enough for a lump-sum window or an annuity buy-out, and whoever brings a plan to insurer bids with the narrowest data-uncertainty loading in the premium takes the account.

## The Problem
Insurers bidding on a buy-out review the census and benefit specifications and load the premium for what they doubt. That includes deferred members with no confirmed address, missing spouse dates of birth for joint-and-survivor annuities, benefit forms from merged plans, and data fields that changed meaning. The sponsor sees the loading only as a higher price or a narrower bidder list.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Consultants clean data project by project, and insurers keep their pricing adjustments private. No one has turned the pattern of insurer data questions into a standard measure a sponsor can act on years before the transaction.

## What to Build
A readiness score at participant and plan level, built from the data checks insurers apply. It covers address and death confirmation, beneficiary completeness, benefit-form documentation, and consistency across valuations. It is trained on past transaction data questions and outcomes where consultants can pool them. It shows the sponsor which fixes most reduce expected premium loading, and tracks progress to a target date.

## Target Customer
CFOs and treasurers of companies with frozen plans of $10M–$500M in liabilities, and the actuarial consultants who prepare them for transactions.

## Impact If Built
Data work starts two years before the transaction instead of during it. More insurers bid, the premium loading shrinks, and the sponsor can see what each clean-up dollar buys.

# Pension Administration System Modernisation

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/pension-funds|Pension Funds]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to move decades of member service, salary and contribution history into a new system that calculates every benefit the old one did, and whoever goes live with the fewest payment errors in the first retiree payrolls takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.2B a year of pension administration system licences, implementation, data conversion and oversight consulting across US public, multiemployer and corporate DB administration (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~8% of pension funds' non-investment-management operating spend (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — every system is digital; many are decades old, with rules embedded in code
**Target Buyer:** Executive Director and Chief Information Officer of the retirement system
**Automation Potential:** High for conversion testing and rule extraction; the cutover decision stays human

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The pension administration system (PAS) is the benefits office's book of record. It holds member service, salary, contributions, employer reports, benefit calculations, retiree payroll and correspondence. Replacing one is the largest technology decision a retirement system makes. Iowa's IPERS received a $60 million, ten-year appropriation for a cloud PAS. Oregon PERS decided in 2025 to buy a commercial system in an eight-to-ten-year programme. Washington DRS targets a mid-2027 go-live on Sagitec's Neospin, and North Dakota went live on it in March 2025. The risk is concentrated at cutover. A system that cannot reproduce the old benefit for every edge case pays retirees the wrong amount, in public.

## Current Tools & Gaps
PAS vendors (Sagitec, Vitech under Majesco since 2026, LRS PensionGold and others) sell configurable platforms. Systems integrators and independent oversight consultants manage requirements, data conversion and testing. The gaps: the plan's rules are documented in statute but implemented through decades of code changes and staff workarounds; data conversion is tested by sampling; and parallel calculation of the old and new systems across the whole membership is rarely complete before go-live.

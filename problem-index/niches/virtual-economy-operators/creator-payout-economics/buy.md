**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to make a creator economy legible to the people earning in it, when the platform issues the currency, sets the rate, sizes the pool and controls discovery — and whoever makes it legible takes the account.

## The Problem
Music went through this and came out the other side. Royalties were opaque, statements were unreadable, the pool allocation method was undisclosed, and artists could not audit anything. Decades of pressure, litigation and eventually regulation produced itemised statements, published allocation methodologies, audit rights, and collecting societies with governance. The result is imperfect and vastly better than what preceded it. Platform creator economies are at the earlier stage with the same structure.

## What Already Exists
Itemised royalty statements; published allocation methodologies; contractual audit rights; collective representation and governance; and standardised reporting formats.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a platform that is also the venue, the currency issuer and the discovery mechanism. It requires: (1) a counterparty that controls demand as well as payment, since discovery determines earnings before any rate applies — no music rights framework addresses a party that also decides who hears you, and this is the substantive difference; (2) a currency the platform issues, adding an exchange rate nobody else sets; (3) creators who are individuals with no collective body, unlike a music industry with societies and unions; (4) earnings driven by an algorithm rather than by plays that can be counted independently; and (5) no regulatory forcing function, so the change must be commercial or reputational.

## Target Customer
Platform creator economies, creator relations leadership, creator organisations, and rights and royalty technology vendors.

## Impact If Solved
Music moved from opaque statements to published methodologies and audit rights under sustained pressure. A counterparty that also controls discovery is the part no royalty framework addresses, and it is the part that decides earnings first.

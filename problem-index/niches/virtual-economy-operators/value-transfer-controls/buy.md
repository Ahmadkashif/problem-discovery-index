# Financial Crime Controls From Regulated Payments

**Niche:** [[niches/virtual-economy-operators/value-transfer-controls/profile|Value Transfer Controls]]
**Industry:** [[industries/virtual-economy-operators|Virtual Economy Operators]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Regulated payment institutions run identity, monitoring, thresholds and reporting as a licence condition, and item economies run terms of service.
**Tags:** #compliance #graph-theory #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #data-integration #workflow-orchestration #change-point-detection #logistic-regression
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to establish whether its item economy is being used to move money between people, and to hold a defensible position about it — and whoever builds that regime takes the account.

## The Problem
Regulated payments and money transmission built a complete apparatus for exactly this risk: customer identification proportionate to risk, transaction monitoring against typologies, threshold-based enhanced diligence, sanctions and prohibited-party screening, suspicious activity reporting, and a documented risk assessment the regulator can inspect. The vendor ecosystem is large and mature. Item economies that move comparable value operate none of it and rest on a contractual prohibition.

## What Already Exists
Risk-based customer identification; transaction monitoring against typology libraries; threshold-driven enhanced diligence; sanctions and watchlist screening; and suspicious activity reporting workflows.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a product that is not a payment product and a user who is a player. It requires: (1) transactions denominated in items rather than money, so the monitoring typologies must be rewritten for a medium where value is implicit in a floating price — this is the substantive difference and no existing typology library covers it; (2) a consumer product where identity friction is commercially damaging and most users are entirely ordinary; (3) a cash-out step that occurs on third-party platforms the operator does not control; (4) a large minor population, which changes both the risk profile and the appropriate controls; and (5) no licence and therefore no defined obligation, so the regime must be designed against an uncertain standard rather than a stated one.

## Target Customer
Virtual economy operators, compliance leadership, third-party marketplaces, and financial crime technology vendors.

## Impact If Solved
Regulated payments built identity, monitoring and reporting because a licence required it, and the vendor ecosystem is mature. Value denominated in items at floating prices means the typologies have to be written from scratch.

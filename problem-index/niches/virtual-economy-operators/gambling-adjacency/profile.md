# Gambling Adjacency

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/virtual-economy-operators|Virtual Economy Operators]]
**Category:** 🟠 Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to hold a defensible position on third-party sites that accept its items as stakes, when the items, the trading infrastructure and the audience are all the operator's — and whoever builds that position takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.2B US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~10% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Very low — disclaimed
**Target Buyer:** Legal and policy leadership
**Automation Potential:** Medium — detection and controls

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Third-party sites that accept game items as stakes have been the subject of litigation and regulatory action in several jurisdictions, and the participants have repeatedly included minors. The operators' position — that these are unauthorised third parties — is true and does not resolve the fact that the items, the trading infrastructure and the audience are theirs. It is the sharpest ethical and legal exposure in the category, it is addressable with data the operator already holds, and addressing it means accepting a responsibility the current position avoids.

## Current Tools & Gaps
A terms-of-service prohibition, occasional legal action against specific sites, and an API that continues to serve the trades. The gaps: no detection of accounts trading with known gambling operators; no controls on the trading paths involved; no minor protection specific to this risk; no measurement of the volume; and no stated position beyond disclaimer.

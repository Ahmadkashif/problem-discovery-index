**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to police a market with real monetary value using rules written for stolen credit cards — and whoever brings real surveillance to it takes the account.

## The Problem
Regulated exchanges were required to solve this and did. Surveillance systems monitor every order and trade for wash trading, layering, spoofing, marking the close, ramping and cornering; alerts are triaged by a market operations function; cases are investigated with retained evidence; rules are published; and enforcement is reported. The vendor category is mature. Item markets with comparable turnover and no regulator have none of it.

## What Already Exists
Continuous order and trade surveillance; manipulation pattern libraries; alert triage and case management; published market rules; and enforcement reporting.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a market whose participants are pseudonymous and whose assets are game items. It requires: (1) participants with no verified identity and multiple accounts, so account-level surveillance must be paired with linkage analysis — this is the substantive difference and makes collusion both easier to commit and harder to attribute; (2) assets whose supply the operator itself controls, creating a conflict no exchange has; (3) settlement partly on third-party platforms outside the operator's systems; (4) no regulatory obligation, so the case for the spend must be commercial and reputational; and (5) participants who are frequently minors, which changes what enforcement is appropriate.

## Target Customer
Virtual economy operators, trust and compliance leadership, third-party marketplaces, and market surveillance vendors.

## Impact If Solved
Exchanges built surveillance because a licence depended on it and the vendor category is mature. Pseudonymous participants with multiple accounts, and an operator that also controls supply, are what make this a different surveillance problem.

# Market Data Publication From Exchanges

**Niche:** [[niches/virtual-economy-operators/price-discovery-and-market-data/profile|Price Discovery & Market Data]]
**Industry:** [[industries/virtual-economy-operators|Virtual Economy Operators]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Exchanges publish reference prices, volume and depth to a defined standard, and item markets publish a last-sale number.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #time-series-forecasting #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #data-integration #automation #compliance #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to publish a reference price people can rely on for goods they buy with real money — and whoever publishes it credibly takes the account.

## The Problem
Exchanges and market data providers built the whole apparatus of price publication: volume-weighted average prices, official closing and settlement prices computed to a published methodology, depth and liquidity measures, corrections policies, and distribution to anyone who needs them. It exists because participants cannot trade fairly without a common reference. Item markets with real turnover publish a last-sale price or nothing, and the gap is filled by third parties with no accountability.

## What Already Exists
Volume-weighted and official price calculations; published index methodologies; depth and liquidity reporting; correction and revision policies; and standardised market data distribution.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to hundreds of thousands of thin item markets rather than a few liquid instruments. It requires: (1) an enormous number of items, most of which trade rarely, so the methodology must handle thin and stale markets honestly rather than assuming liquidity — this is the substantive difference and is where naive last-sale publication fails; (2) items with condition, rarity and cosmetic variation that are not fungible, complicating what is even being priced; (3) an operator who also controls supply, creating a conflict no exchange has; (4) settlement partly on third-party venues, fragmenting the data; and (5) a consumer audience rather than professional traders, so presentation matters as much as computation.

## Target Customer
Virtual economy operators, third-party marketplaces, trading communities, and market data vendors.

## Impact If Solved
Exchanges publish prices to a defined standard because participants cannot trade fairly without a common reference. Hundreds of thousands of thin, non-fungible item markets are what the methodology has to be rebuilt for.

## The Problem
Financial crime investigation is a developed discipline with developed tooling: network and link analysis, entity resolution across accounts, case management with evidence chains, typology libraries, quality assurance sampling, and documented decision standards. Investigators making comparable decisions in banks work in purpose-built environments. Analysts making financially equivalent decisions in item economies work in a moderation queue with a fraud rules engine.

## What Already Exists
Link and network analysis interfaces; entity resolution across accounts; case management with evidence chains; typology and red-flag libraries; and quality assurance and calibration programmes.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to accounts rather than customers, and items rather than money. It requires: (1) entity resolution over pseudonymous accounts with no identity data, where financial investigation starts from verified customers — this is the substantive difference and it makes linkage the hardest step rather than a given; (2) value measured in items at floating market prices, so the magnitude of a decision must be computed rather than read; (3) outcomes that are account actions rather than reports to an authority; (4) a typology library that does not exist and must be written for this medium; and (5) analysts trained in content moderation rather than in financial investigation.

## Target Customer
Virtual economy operators, trust and safety leadership, marketplaces, and financial crime investigation vendors.

## Impact If Solved
Financial crime investigation has purpose-built environments because the decisions are consequential. Pseudonymous accounts make linkage the hardest step, and value at floating prices means the magnitude has to be computed before any decision is proportionate.

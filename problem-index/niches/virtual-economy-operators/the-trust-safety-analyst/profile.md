# The Trust & Safety Analyst

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/virtual-economy-operators|Virtual Economy Operators]]
**Category:** 🟣 Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to equip the person making financial-crime-grade decisions about accounts with tools built for game abuse — and whoever re-equips them takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$700M US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~6% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Low — wrong tools
**Target Buyer:** Trust and safety leadership
**Automation Potential:** High — case tooling and decision support

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The analyst reviewing these cases is deciding whether to seize inventories worth real money, ban accounts people have spent years and thousands on, and judge whether a trade pattern constitutes laundering. Their tooling is a moderation queue, their rules were written for stolen credit cards, and their training was for game abuse. The consequences are financial and sometimes legal; the instruments are not. It is a role carrying responsibility far beyond what it was designed for.

## Current Tools & Gaps
A moderation queue, an account view, a ban button and a fraud rules engine. The gaps: no trade graph in the case view; no valuation of what a decision confiscates; no decision framework for financial-grade calls; no evidence retention for disputes; and no calibration across analysts.

# Niche Analysis — Virtual Economy Operators

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/virtual-economy-operators|Virtual Economy Operators]]

## Niche Selection

An operator issuing items that trade for real money is conducting monetary policy, running a market and operating a payment rail, and describes all three as game features. It holds a more complete transaction record than any central bank has of any real economy and uses it for fraud response and revenue reporting. The eight niches below follow the consequences: supply decisions with unmodelled price effects, a market with no surveillance, a creator economy with one counterparty, an adjacency the operator disclaims while supplying its infrastructure, and the people on both sides of the resulting failures.

## Why These Niches

Monetary policy and market integrity carry the money and the two things the operator is doing without admitting it: setting a money supply, and running a market with no surveillance. Creator payout economics and the gambling adjacency are the two places where the operator's position is weakest and least instrumented — a creator paid in a currency the platform issues at a rate it sets, and a third-party ecosystem the operator disclaims while supplying the items, the trading infrastructure and the audience. The two underserved audiences are the player whose inventory was stolen and told nothing can be returned, and the analyst policing a financial market with fraud rules designed for stolen credit cards. The last two are mechanical: protecting inventories properly, and publishing the prices.

## Filter Notes

Seven of the eight are terminal — each names one contest that every serious competitor is fighting over, and decomposing further would produce features rather than markets.

**Market integrity** is not. It names a domain, and inside it are two regimes with different evidence, different buyers and different law. Manipulation detection asks whether trades within the economy are genuine — wash trading, price ramping, coordinated cornering of a limited release — a surveillance problem over the operator's own complete order and trade record, sold to a market operations function, and answerable entirely from data already held. Value transfer controls ask whether the economy is being used to move money between people for reasons unconnected to the game — a financial crime problem requiring identity, thresholds, reporting obligations and a defensible position with regulators, sold to compliance, and impossible to run on trade data alone. One protects the market's prices; the other protects the operator from being a payment rail it never registered as. An operator can build the first and still have no answer to the second, which is where most of them currently are.

Two adjacent candidates were rejected as belonging elsewhere: **operating the live game, its events and its content calendar** belongs to [[industries/game-liveops-services|Game LiveOps Services]], and **the payment rails and settlement underneath the marketplace** belong to [[industries/payment-processors|Payment Processors]].

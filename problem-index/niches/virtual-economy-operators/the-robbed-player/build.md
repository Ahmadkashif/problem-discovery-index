# Recovery for Something Fully Recorded

**Niche:** [[niches/virtual-economy-operators/the-robbed-player/profile|The Robbed Player]]
**Industry:** [[industries/virtual-economy-operators|Virtual Economy Operators]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** The theft is recorded end to end, the items still exist, and the policy is that nothing can be done.
**Tags:** #worker-facing #graph-theory #compliance #automation #evaluation-metrics #workflow-orchestration #confidence-intervals #data-integration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to give a player whose inventory was stolen something other than a policy saying nothing can be returned — and whoever builds that recovery takes the account.

## The Problem
An account is compromised and its inventory is transferred away within minutes. The operator has a complete record: the login anomaly, the session, every trade, and where each item went. The items usually still exist and are frequently still traceable. The player is told that trades are final and nothing can be returned. For goods of genuine monetary value this is an extraordinary position, and it is defended on the grounds that reversal is complex — which it is, and which is not the same as impossible.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Reversal creates disputes, ambiguity and a support cost the operator currently avoids entirely. Finality is operationally simple. Distinguishing theft from a regretted trade or a staged claim requires judgement and evidence. And the loss falls on the player, so there is no line item creating pressure.

## What to Build
Trace the items and build a graduated response short of universal reversal. Trace stolen items through subsequent trades automatically, which is the core — a graph traversal over records already held answers where everything went within seconds. Freeze identified items at their current holders pending review rather than reversing immediately, since the intermediate holders may be innocent and freezing preserves the option. Distinguish compromise from regret using the evidence available — login anomaly, device change, session characteristics, trade pattern — because a policy that cannot tell them apart must refuse both. Define a graduated response: full reversal where the chain is short and clear, partial restitution where it is not, and a stated basis for refusing. Compensate from an operator-funded pool where reversal is impossible, which is what any custodian of value does and none of these operators do. Act within minutes rather than days, as the recovery window is short and the current response time guarantees failure. Apply the same treatment to the receiving accounts consistently, which is where deterrence actually comes from. Publish the policy so players know what protection exists, since the current uncertainty is itself a harm. Measure recovery rates and publish them. And separate the account security failure from the restitution question, because conflating them is how blame gets placed on the victim.

## Target Customer
Virtual economy operators, support and trust leadership, third-party marketplaces, and consumer protection bodies.

## Impact If Built
A graph traversal over records already held answers where every stolen item went within seconds, and the policy is that nothing can be done. Tracing and freezing, with a graduated response, is what a custodian of real value owes.

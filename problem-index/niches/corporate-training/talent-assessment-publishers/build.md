# The Validation Archive as a Standing Evidence Base

**Niche:** [[niches/corporate-training/talent-assessment-publishers/profile|Talent Assessment Publishers]]
**Industry:** [[industries/corporate-training|Corporate Training]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Decades of validation studies establishing that these assessments predict job performance sit as individual client reports, so each new validation starts from scratch and the accumulated evidence is never pooled.
**Tags:** #bayesian-inference #hypothesis-testing #confidence-intervals #evaluation-metrics #cross-validation #dimensionality-reduction #feature-engineering #descriptive-statistics #data-integration #compliance

## The Problem
The legal and commercial basis of employment assessment is validity evidence — a demonstrated relationship between the assessment and job performance for the role in question. Establishing it is expensive: a criterion validation study requires collecting performance data on enough incumbents to support an inference, which takes months and a cooperative client. Firms have run these studies for decades, across thousands of roles and organizations, and each one exists as a report delivered to the client who commissioned it. The accumulated evidence is enormous and is never pooled, so every new engagement either commissions another study or relies on generalization arguments the firm cannot quantify from its own record. The single most valuable asset in the business is stored as a filing cabinet.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Validation studies are performed under client agreements that govern the performance data, and the terms usually contemplate the study rather than a pooled research use — so the safe reading has been that nothing may be combined, and nobody has done the work of mapping which agreements actually permit de-identified meta-analytic use. Combining studies is also methodologically demanding: performance criteria differ across organizations, range restriction varies, and naive pooling produces misleading estimates. Because the naive version is wrong and the rigorous version is real work, the archive has stayed a filing cabinet.

## What to Build
A validation evidence base holding every study at the level a meta-analysis needs — assessment used, role and job family, criterion measure and how it was collected, sample characteristics, observed relationships, and the consent and reuse terms attached. The terms layer is what makes the whole thing usable rather than a liability, because every query can state which portion of the archive it is entitled to draw on. On that base, the firm gains what it currently cannot produce: cumulative validity estimates by job family with proper correction for the artifacts that distort individual studies, quantified generalization evidence for roles where no local study exists, and identification of the specific role families where evidence is genuinely thin and a study is genuinely needed. Fairness evidence accumulates in the same structure, which matters increasingly as employment testing draws regulatory attention — a firm able to report subgroup differences across thousands of studies is in a different position from one that reports them study by study.

## Target Customer
Chief science officers and heads of assessment research at publishers running 100-400 psychometricians, and the talent leaders at client organizations who currently fund local validation studies to establish what the publisher's archive could already support.

## Impact If Built
Converts decades of sunk study cost into a standing asset, and replaces per-engagement validation with cumulative evidence in the many cases where that is defensible. It also positions the firm for the question that is arriving from regulators and litigants alike — on what evidence does this assessment predict performance, and how does it perform across groups — with an answer drawn from thousands of studies rather than from the one nearest to hand.

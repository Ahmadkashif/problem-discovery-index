# Item Development Adapted to Cross-Instrument Constructs

**Niche:** [[niches/corporate-training/talent-assessment-publishers/profile|Talent Assessment Publishers]]
**Industry:** [[industries/corporate-training|Corporate Training]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Item banking platforms serve one testing programme with one blueprint; an assessment publisher's catalogue is dozens of instruments measuring overlapping constructs with no shared bank and no way to find an equivalent item.
**Tags:** #evaluation-metrics #hypothesis-testing #confidence-intervals #dimensionality-reduction #word-embeddings #bert #transformers #feature-engineering #automation #workflow-orchestration

## The Problem
Building a new assessment starts by writing items, piloting them, discarding most, and calibrating the survivors — an expensive cycle repeated for every instrument. A substantial share of those items measure constructs the publisher has already measured in another instrument, with items whose psychometric properties are known from prior samples. Nobody can find them, because items are owned by the instrument that published them rather than catalogued by construct. So the firm repeatedly pays to discover properties it already knows, and construct overlap between its own instruments — a real issue when a client administers a battery that measures conscientiousness three times in different words — goes unmeasured.

## What Already Exists
Item banking is a mature category. FastTest, Concerto, TAO, and the enterprise assessment platforms handle authoring with metadata, versioning, IRT calibration, form assembly, exposure control, and adaptive delivery, and large certification programmes run on them successfully.

## The Customization Gap
Each assumes a testing programme: one construct domain, one blueprint, one calibrated scale, items reused across forms of the same instrument. A talent assessment catalogue is structurally different — dozens of instruments measuring overlapping constructs under different theoretical frameworks, calibrated on unlinked samples, with no controlled construct vocabulary that would let anyone find semantically equivalent items across them. Reuse also carries constraints these tools cannot express: an item's calibration is sample-specific, its behaviour may differ by language and market, and its use in a new instrument raises validity questions that should be surfaced rather than assumed away. The adaptation is a catalogue-wide bank where items carry construct tags against a controlled framework, semantic search finds near-equivalents across instruments regardless of wording, every calibration is retained with the sample and instrument context that produced it, and reuse proposals arrive with explicit caveats about what transfers. Localization needs the same treatment, since a translated item is a new item until evidence says otherwise.

## Target Customer
Heads of assessment development and psychometric leads at publishers, and the item writers who currently start from a blank page because the catalogue offers them no view of what exists.

## Impact If Solved
Cuts the slowest and most expensive phase of instrument development by starting from characterized material. It also makes catalogue-level construct overlap visible, which is both a validity issue and the commercial argument for the shorter, better-linked battery the publisher cannot currently design.

# Review Findings Are Cleared Per Client and Never Aggregated

**Niche:** [[niches/food-manufacturing/food-regulatory-affairs-consultancies/profile|Food Regulatory Affairs & Label Compliance]]
**Industry:** [[industries/food-manufacturing|Food Manufacturing]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The firm finds the same label defects across hundreds of clients every year, resolves each one individually, and has no view of which mistakes the industry actually makes.
**Tags:** #k-means-clustering #bert #word-embeddings #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #dimensionality-reduction #tacit-knowledge-ml #compliance #data-integration #worker-facing

## The Problem
Every review produces findings — a missing disclosure, a claim without substantiation, an allergen statement in the wrong form, a serving size derived incorrectly. Each is communicated to the client and cleared. Nothing aggregates. So the firm cannot say which defects are most common, which client types or product categories generate them, or whether its own reviewers find them consistently. Reviewers develop personal checklists; two specialists reviewing comparable labels catch different things, and by how much is unmeasured in a service whose whole value is that nothing was missed.

## Why It's Still Broken
Findings live in review deliverables written for a client audience, so the structure that would make them countable was never captured. Reviewers are measured on turnaround, which rewards clearing the queue. And the aggregate view has no obvious owner — it benefits the practice rather than any engagement, which is exactly the kind of asset a per-matter operating model never builds.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Findings recorded against a controlled taxonomy at the point of review, keyed to the specific requirement and the product context, as a step in the work rather than as documentation after it. Aggregated, that answers what nobody can answer today: the defect frequency distribution across the industry, which reviewers find which classes of issue, and where the firm's own consistency is weakest. Reviewer variance becomes measurable and therefore trainable. Common defect patterns become pre-review checks, so the routine findings are caught automatically and specialist attention goes to the judgment calls. And the aggregate itself is a publishable asset — the most common labelling errors by category, evidenced across hundreds of clients, is content no competitor can produce and exactly the material that generates inbound work from manufacturers who suspect they have the same problem.

## Who Feels the Pain
Reviewers rebuilding personal checklists because the firm has no shared one; practice leaders accountable for consistency across a review book with no instrument to measure it; clients whose label passes one reviewer and would have failed another; and the practice, whose accumulated view of how the industry gets labelling wrong exists only as individual recollection.

## Impact If Fixed
Makes review quality measurable and transferable, which lifts the capacity ceiling set by experienced reviewer headcount. It also produces the firm's most saleable content asset from work it is already paid to perform, and it is the prerequisite for the enforcement layer — findings must be countable before their outcomes can be joined to them.

# Wildlife Compliance & Curtailment

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/renewable-power-producers|Renewable Power Producers]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep eagle and bat take inside permit limits while stopping turbines as little as possible, and whoever loses the fewest megawatt-hours to wildlife curtailment per turbine-year takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$300M a year of post-construction fatality monitoring, detection systems, biological consultancy and curtailment losses (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~1% of wind and solar energy revenue (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — carcass searches are walked on foot and logged on field forms, curtailment rules are blanket schedules set in permits
**Target Buyer:** Director of Environmental Compliance or permitting lead at a wind owner
**Automation Potential:** Medium — detection and curtailment decisions automate; field searches and agency negotiation stay manual

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Wind farms kill birds and bats, and two federal regimes turn that into operating rules. The US Fish and Wildlife Service's 2024 eagle rule (89 FR 9920) created general permits for qualifying wind projects, with specific permits still required where eagles are common. Endangered bat species drive "low wind speed" curtailment at many Midwest and Eastern sites, typically stopping turbines below a cut-in speed on warm nights in late summer and autumn (recalled). The compliance evidence is post-construction monitoring: biologists walk search plots under turbines, correct for scavenging and searcher efficiency, and estimate fatalities. Competitors are biological consultancies (WEST, Stantec, Tetra Tech), eagle detection camera systems (IdentiFlight, DTBird), acoustic bat detection and "smart curtailment" vendors, and owners' own environmental teams (named from recall).

## Current Tools & Gaps
Paper or tablet field forms, fatality estimators such as USGS's GenEst (recalled), camera and radar detection at some sites, and blanket curtailment schedules programmed into the turbine controller. Gaps: curtailment rarely responds to actual bat activity, detection data and fatality searches are not joined, and the lost energy is seldom reported against the take it prevented.

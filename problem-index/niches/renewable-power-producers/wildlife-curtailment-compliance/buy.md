# Airport Bird-Strike Radar for Wind Farms

**Niche:** [[niches/renewable-power-producers/wildlife-curtailment-compliance/profile|Wildlife Compliance & Curtailment]]
**Industry:** [[industries/renewable-power-producers|Renewable Power Producers]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Airports already track birds with avian radar and act in real time to keep them away from aircraft; wind farms need the same detection tied to turbine shutdowns and permit records.
**Tags:** #object-detection #cnns #k-nearest-neighbors #compliance #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep eagle and bat take inside permit limits while stopping turbines as little as possible, and whoever loses the fewest megawatt-hours to wildlife curtailment per turbine-year takes the account.

## The Problem
Eagle take limits push owners toward broad shutdowns when eagles are present in an area, because they cannot tell which turbines a bird is approaching.

## What Already Exists
Avian radar at airports tracks bird targets in three dimensions, classifies them by size and behaviour, and alerts wildlife control staff, with logs that feed the airport's strike-prevention programme.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs: species-level classification (eagles versus vultures and hawks) from radar and cameras; per-turbine trajectories and collision risk; automatic stop commands to only the turbines at risk, sent through the owner's control system; restart logic; and logs formatted for permit reporting and the fatality monitoring that verifies the system.

## Target Customer
Wind owners in high-eagle-use areas operating under specific eagle permits.

## Impact If Solved
Stopping only the 2–3 turbines in a bird's path instead of a whole string of 20 cuts eagle-related curtailment by up to ~85% (estimate) where shutdowns are triggered by presence alone.

# Carcass Searches Logged on Paper Forms

**Niche:** [[niches/renewable-power-producers/wildlife-curtailment-compliance/profile|Wildlife Compliance & Curtailment]]
**Industry:** [[industries/renewable-power-producers|Renewable Power Producers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Biologists walk transects under turbines, record each carcass on a field form, and the season's data reach the fatality estimator months later, too late to change that season's curtailment.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #bayesian-inference #cnns #compliance #quick-win #data-integration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep eagle and bat take inside permit limits while stopping turbines as little as possible, and whoever loses the fewest megawatt-hours to wildlife curtailment per turbine-year takes the account.

## The Problem
Post-construction monitoring produces carcass records, searcher-efficiency trials and scavenger-removal trials. They are transcribed, checked and run through a fatality estimator at season end, and the report goes to the agency long after the nights it describes.

## Why It's Still Broken
Monitoring is contracted to consultants per season, field crews change, species identification of bat carcasses needs specialists, and nobody joins the searches to turbine operating data in near real time.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A field app that records each find with location, photo and condition, suggests a species from the photo for specialist confirmation, and updates a running fatality estimate per turbine each week, joined to that turbine's curtailment and operating history.

## Who Feels the Pain
Environmental compliance managers, consulting biologists, and operators who curtail all season without knowing whether it is working.

## Impact If Fixed
Weekly estimates instead of an end-of-season report let an owner adjust curtailment mid-season, and cut ~4–6 weeks of transcription and quality control per site-season (estimate).

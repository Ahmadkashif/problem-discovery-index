# Solar Plant Availability & Underperformance Recovery

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/renewable-power-producers|Renewable Power Producers]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to find and fix the inverters, trackers and strings that drag a plant below its expected output, and whoever has the smallest energy-based availability loss across a fleet takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$2.5B a year of utility-scale solar O&M, monitoring and inspection spend (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~7% of wind and solar energy revenue (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — inverter and tracker data stream to monitoring platforms, but field crews find many faults on foot or by drone flight once a year
**Target Buyer:** Director of Solar Operations or performance engineering lead at an IPP
**Automation Potential:** High for detection and prioritisation; repairs stay physical

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A utility-scale solar plant fails in small pieces: one inverter of fifty tripped, a tracker row stuck flat in the morning, a combiner fuse blown, soiling on one block, a string of modules damaged by hail. Each loss is small, together they set the plant's energy-based availability, and finding them is a search problem across hundreds of thousands of modules per site. Utility-scale solar produced 296,000 GWh in 2025, up 34% (EIA), so the fleets are growing faster than O&M teams. Competitors are asset performance platforms, O&M contractors, drone thermography vendors, inverter makers' service arms and tracker makers' controls teams.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Monitoring and asset performance platforms (Power Factors, Bazefield; the vendor field is profiled at [[niches/solar-installers/solar-asset-performance-monitoring/profile|🔍 Solar Asset Management & Performance Monitoring]]), inverter and tracker vendor portals, annual drone thermography and IV-curve tracing, and computerised maintenance systems. The gaps: tracker positions are rarely compared with what they should be; string-level losses hide in inverter totals; and drone findings sit in PDF reports that never reach the work-order queue ranked by lost megawatt-hours.

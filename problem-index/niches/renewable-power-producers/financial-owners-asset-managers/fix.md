# A Budget P50 Nobody Retests

**Niche:** [[niches/renewable-power-producers/financial-owners-asset-managers/profile|Financial Owners & Third-Party Asset Managers]]
**Industry:** [[industries/renewable-power-producers|Renewable Power Producers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Every year's budget repeats the energy estimate the asset was bought on, even after five years of actual output say the plant runs below it.
**Tags:** #bayesian-inference #confidence-intervals #linear-regression #descriptive-statistics #quick-win #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to explain each month's production and revenue variance against the budget, and whoever leaves the smallest unexplained share of that variance takes the account.

## The Problem
The acquisition model's P50 becomes the annual budget. When the plant underperforms for several years, the gap is explained as weather each year, and valuation, debt sizing and distributions stay anchored to a number the data no longer support.

## Why It's Still Broken
Re-basing the P50 means admitting the purchase price assumption was high, the operational data needed to separate resource from performance are held by the operator, and nobody is paid to do it.

## What a Fix Looks Like
An annual re-estimate that combines the original energy assessment with weather-normalised actual output in a Bayesian update, reports the revised P50 and P90 with intervals, and shows how much of the gap is resource, availability or performance.

## Who Feels the Pain
Fund asset managers, valuation committees, lenders relying on stale coverage ratios, and investors receiving distributions that assume the old figure.

## Impact If Fixed
For a 300 MW wind farm running ~5% below its acquisition P50 (estimate), re-basing removes a recurring ~$2M budget miss and corrects valuation before the next refinancing.

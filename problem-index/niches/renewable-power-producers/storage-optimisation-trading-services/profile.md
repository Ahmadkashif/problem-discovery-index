# Battery Storage Optimisation & Merchant Trading Services

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/renewable-power-producers|Renewable Power Producers]]
**Category:** Insight Layer
**Value-Chain Position:** Suppliers selling into the industry

## What They Do
Firms that bid batteries into energy and ancillary markets in one of three ways: as software the owner runs, as an agency or revenue-share service in which the optimiser bids the owner's asset as its agent, or under a toll in which the optimiser pays the owner a fixed fee and trades the battery for its own account. Tesla's Autobidder, Fluence's Mosaic, Ascend Analytics' SmartBidder, Habitat Energy, GridBeyond and Gridmatic are representative (named from recall).

## Insight Function
**Size:** ~10–50 quantitative analysts, traders and data scientists per optimiser (estimate)
**Output:** Bids and dispatch schedules every market interval, monthly revenue and capture-rate reports
**Proprietary data:** Bid, dispatch and revenue histories across many owners' batteries, with each asset's telemetry
**Clock:** Monthly revenue and capture-rate reports to owners and annual contract renewals, which are client dates. ISO day-ahead bid deadlines (10:00 a.m. the day before in ERCOT and CAISO, recalled) bind every bid, so they govern production output rather than an analytical deliverable
**Buyer:** Head of Trading or Chief Commercial Officer

**Kill switches:** three regimes, judged separately. Agency and revenue-share services: live — each battery's telemetry, bids and revenue belong to its owner under service agreements whose terms were not checked for product rights, and this half supplies the cross-asset histories that are the moat, so it carries the verdict. Tolls: none — under a toll the bids and revenue are the toller's own book. Software licences: undetermined (terms unread).
**Verdict:** Logged — kill switch (agency and revenue-share half). The software half is undetermined (terms unread) and below threshold; the tolling half carries no switch and is below threshold at the same 37. This tests the storage dispatch angle. The bid is paid for on performance, but bidding is algorithmic, so revenue grows with megawatts rather than people (Q2 2), prices are public (Q3 3), and the day-ahead deadline binds every bid as production rather than setting a clock on analysis (Q4 3, on owners' monthly reports and renewals).

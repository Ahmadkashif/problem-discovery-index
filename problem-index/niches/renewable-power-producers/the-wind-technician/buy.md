# Field Service Management Rebuilt Around the Climb

**Niche:** [[niches/renewable-power-producers/the-wind-technician/profile|The Wind Technician]]
**Industry:** [[industries/renewable-power-producers|Renewable Power Producers]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Field service platforms already dispatch technicians, track parts and capture work orders for industrial equipment; wind adds climb limits, weather windows and a nacelle with no signal.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #optimization-fundamentals #worker-facing #workflow-orchestration #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get a stopped turbine running again on the first climb, and whoever has the highest first-time fix rate on technician callouts takes the account.

## The Problem
Generic field service tools schedule wind crews as if every job were a ground-level visit, and capture work orders in forms nobody fills on a tower.

## What Already Exists
ServiceMax, Salesforce Field Service, IFS and SAP field service modules dispatch technicians by skill and location, reserve parts, run mobile work orders and report first-time fix rates for industrial equipment makers.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs: climb limits per technician per day and two-person crew rules; wind-speed and lightning windows that close towers; grouping of turbines by pad and string so one climb day covers several faults; offline mobile forms that work in a nacelle; OEM fault-code libraries per platform; and parts kits suggested from the fault history rather than from the asset's bill of materials.

## Target Customer
O&M managers at independent service providers and owner-operated fleets.

## Impact If Solved
Scheduling around weather and climb limits could add ~one productive climb per crew per week (estimate), ~10% more faults cleared with the same crews.

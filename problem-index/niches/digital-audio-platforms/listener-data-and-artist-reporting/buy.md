**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn the most detailed record of listening behaviour ever assembled into something an artist can act on — and whoever reports usefully gives artists the decisions they are currently making blind.

## The Problem
Broadcast and media research developed sophisticated audience analysis from panels: reach and frequency, audience composition and flow, loyalty and duplication analysis, and formats designed to support programming and scheduling decisions. It is built on samples of thousands, weighted and modelled. Streaming platforms have a complete census of the actual behaviour of hundreds of millions of people and report a total.

## What Already Exists
Reach, frequency and audience flow analysis; loyalty and duplication measurement; audience composition and segmentation; programming decision support; and standardised reporting conventions.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is from a panel to a census, and from a broadcaster to an artist. It requires: (1) individual-level behaviour rather than modelled panel estimates, which supports far richer analysis and removes the weighting apparatus entirely — this is the substantive advantage; (2) an artist rather than a broadcaster as the reader, so the output must be a decision rather than a table; (3) privacy constraints on individual-level reporting that panel research never had; (4) decisions about touring, releasing and promoting rather than scheduling; and (5) hundreds of thousands of artists rather than a handful of broadcasters, so it must be automatic.

## Target Customer
Artist services leadership, artists and managers, labels, and audience research vendors.

## Impact If Solved
Media research built the analytical vocabulary on panels of thousands, and these platforms have the census. Translating it for an artist making touring and release decisions, under privacy constraints panels never faced, is the adaptation.

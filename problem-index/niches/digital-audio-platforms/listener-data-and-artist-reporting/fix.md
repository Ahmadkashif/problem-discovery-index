# A Map and a Number

**Niche:** [[niches/digital-audio-platforms/listener-data-and-artist-reporting/profile|Listener Data & Artist Reporting]]
**Industry:** [[industries/digital-audio-platforms|Digital Audio Platforms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The artist opens their dashboard, sees a total and a map, and still does not know where to play or what to release.
**Tags:** #quick-win #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #worker-facing #confidence-intervals #automation #revenue-impact #k-means-clustering
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn the most detailed record of listening behaviour ever assembled into something an artist can act on — and whoever reports usefully gives artists the decisions they are currently making blind.

## The Problem
The artist dashboard presents accurate figures that answer nothing. The listener count does not distinguish a returning audience from a one-off crowd. The map shows countries when the decision requires cities. The playlist list does not say which placement actually mattered. The artist looks at it, feels encouraged or discouraged, and makes their touring and release decisions the same way they would have without it.

## Why It's Still Broken
The dashboard reports the aggregates the platform computes for itself, so its structure is the platform's rather than the artist's — a view assembled from internal metrics answers internal questions. Nobody asked what decisions artists make. Interpretation requires a point of view the platform avoids taking. And the same dashboard serves an artist with two hundred listeners and one with twenty million.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Answer the artist's actual questions. Report city-level listening with enough detail to route a tour, which is the fix and is the highest-value decision the data supports. Separate returning listeners from new ones, since that single split changes the interpretation of every other number. Show which playlist placement drove what, because artists currently attribute all growth to whichever placement they know about. Report save and repeat rates, as they indicate intent where a play does not. Show the track that is outperforming, which is directly actionable. Lead with a short written summary of what changed and what it suggests, because that is what will be read. Say plainly when the numbers are too small to conclude anything, since small artists over-read noise. Compare against the artist's own history rather than against absolute levels. Tailor the view to catalogue size, as a new artist and an established one need different things from the same data. And ask artists what they are trying to decide, which has never been done and would reshape the whole product.

## Who Feels the Pain
Artists booking tours on guesswork; managers reconstructing insight from screenshots; labels making release decisions without the platform's view; and a dashboard that is opened constantly and used for nothing.

## Impact If Fixed
A view assembled from internal metrics answers internal questions, so the dashboard reports the platform's aggregates. City-level listening and the new-versus-returning split are available today and are what the artist's two biggest decisions actually need.

## The Problem
Dividing a pooled sum among many claimants is an old problem with developed answers: broadcast royalty distribution with sampled or census usage reporting, insurance pool allocation, settlement systems with published rules and independent audit rights. What those systems have in common is that the rule is published, the inputs are auditable and the claimants can check their share. Streaming has a rule, unpublished inputs, and no claimant can verify anything.

## What Already Exists
Published distribution rules; usage reporting standards; independent audit rights for claimants; dispute and adjustment processes; and governance over rule changes.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a census of usage held by the party doing the dividing. It requires: (1) complete usage data rather than a sample, which removes the main historical excuse for coarse allocation and is the substantive change — the reason for pooling was measurement cost and that reason is gone; (2) the distributing party being a commercial platform rather than a neutral society, so audit rights must be constructed rather than assumed; (3) licensing agreements that constrain disclosure, which is a contractual obstacle rather than a technical one; (4) an adversary manipulating the input, which pooled systems with sampled reporting never faced at this scale; and (5) many platforms with different rules, so a claimant cannot compare.

## Target Customer
Executive and rights leadership, licensors and collecting societies, artists, and policymakers.

## Impact If Solved
Pooled distribution systems publish their rules and grant audit rights, and the historical reason for coarse allocation was measurement cost. That reason no longer exists here, which makes the current settlement a choice rather than a constraint.

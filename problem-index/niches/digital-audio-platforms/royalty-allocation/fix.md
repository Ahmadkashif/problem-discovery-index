# A Statement Nobody Can Derive

**Niche:** [[niches/digital-audio-platforms/royalty-allocation/profile|Royalty Allocation]]
**Industry:** [[industries/digital-audio-platforms|Digital Audio Platforms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** An artist receives a payment and no chain of arithmetic available to them produces that number.
**Tags:** #compliance #quick-win #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #revenue-impact #data-integration #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to decide how a subscription fee is divided and whether the streams it is divided by were real — and the contest splits cleanly enough that it is not terminal.

## The Problem
The payment depends on the total pool, the total stream count across the whole platform, deductions, territory mix, subscription tier mix, and adjustments. An artist knows their own stream count and nothing else. Even their distributor cannot reconstruct the calculation. The result is a number that arrives and is accepted because there is no alternative, in an industry where the central complaint is that nobody trusts the accounting.

## Why It's Still Broken
The inputs are platform-wide aggregates that are treated as commercially sensitive, so the calculation is unverifiable by construction — a formula whose denominator is confidential cannot be checked by anyone holding only a numerator. Statements pass through several intermediaries, each adding opacity. Nobody has been required to make it derivable. And the current arrangement suits the parties with the leverage.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Publish enough for the arithmetic to close. Publish the per-territory, per-tier effective rate and the total pool inputs, which is the fix and is what makes an artist's payment derivable at all. Show the calculation for a given period in a form a rights holder can check, rather than a net figure. Report deductions explicitly, since unexplained deductions are the single largest source of distrust. Standardise statement formats across the chain, as every intermediary currently reformats and loses detail. Show the artist their stream counts alongside the rate applied, so the two connect. Report threshold and policy effects on their specific catalogue, because those policies moved money and artists were told about them in general terms. Preserve the chain of derivation through distributors, which is where most of the loss of detail occurs. Allow an audit route, even a limited one, since the absence of any is the structural complaint. Publish methodology changes in advance rather than discovering them in a statement. And accept that transparency will produce disputes, because the disputes exist already and are currently unanswerable.

## Who Feels the Pain
Artists who cannot check their own income; distributors relaying numbers they cannot derive; rights operations analysts explaining a calculation they do not have; and an industry whose central relationship rests on unverifiable payments.

## Impact If Fixed
A formula whose denominator is confidential cannot be checked by anyone holding only a numerator. Publishing the effective rate and the pool inputs makes the arithmetic close, which is the minimum condition for any of the rest of the argument to be settled.

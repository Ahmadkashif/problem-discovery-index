# A Hundred Thousand Tracks Nobody Will Hear

**Niche:** [[niches/digital-audio-platforms/recommendation-and-discovery/profile|Recommendation & Discovery]]
**Industry:** [[industries/digital-audio-platforms|Digital Audio Platforms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A release goes live, gets no placement, receives almost no algorithmic exposure in its first week, and is thereafter statistically invisible.
**Tags:** #quick-win #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #matrix-decompositions #revenue-impact #automation #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to surface the right track from a catalogue receiving a hundred thousand arrivals a day, against an objective that reliably favours what is already familiar.

## The Problem
A new release has a short window in which the system decides whether it is worth showing to anyone. Without an editorial placement, the algorithmic exposure in that window is minimal, so early engagement is minimal, so the model concludes the track is not engaging, so exposure stays minimal. The outcome is determined within days by a process the artist cannot see, cannot influence and is not told about, and afterwards the track is effectively unreachable.

## Why It's Still Broken
Early exposure is allocated by a model with no prior for a track nobody has heard, so the default is almost none — a system with no information about an item and an engagement objective will not risk showing it. Editorial placement is the only override and is scarce. Artists are not told how the window works. And nobody reports the exposure distribution for new releases.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Give the new release a floor and tell the artist what to expect. Guarantee a minimum exploration exposure to every new release, which is the fix and costs very little across a large listener base. Use the artist's existing audience first, since their own followers are the cheapest and most predictive early signal and are frequently not reached. Extend the evaluation window rather than deciding in days, because a release's audience builds over weeks and the current window is shorter than the behaviour. Publish how the early window works, as artists currently plan releases around folklore. Report a release's actual exposure to the artist, which is data the platform has and the artist is guessing at. Separate no-exposure from low-engagement in the reporting, since they are completely different outcomes and look identical to the artist. Prioritise exploration toward listeners whose taste profile matches, which makes the same exposure budget far more informative. Let an artist see whether their own followers were reached, as that is the most basic and most commonly failed case. Measure the share of new releases that receive meaningful exposure, which will be uncomfortable and is the honest description. And avoid letting the first week's outcome be permanent, because a self-fulfilling verdict from an uninformed prior is the mechanism that needs breaking.

## Who Feels the Pain
Artists whose release was decided before anyone heard it; labels investing in releases with no visibility into the window; listeners never shown things they would like; and a catalogue whose growth does not reach anyone.

## Impact If Fixed
A system with no information about an item and an engagement objective will not risk showing it, so silence becomes a verdict. A guaranteed exploration floor aimed at the artist's own followers breaks the loop at trivial cost across a large base.

# Intake Control From Content Platforms

**Niche:** [[niches/digital-audio-platforms/catalogue-intake-at-scale/profile|Catalogue Intake at Scale]]
**Industry:** [[industries/digital-audio-platforms|Digital Audio Platforms]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Video platforms built content identification and upload-time enforcement at enormous scale, and audio intake validates a file format.
**Tags:** #contrastive-learning #cnns #evaluation-metrics #compliance #confidence-intervals #automation #graph-theory #object-detection
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to ingest well over a hundred thousand tracks a day without letting the bad ones through or losing the good ones — and whoever does it well controls what the catalogue actually is.

## The Problem
Large video platforms built content identification systems that match uploads against reference catalogues at scale, apply rights-holder policies automatically, and handle disputes. It was built because the legal exposure made it necessary and it works at volumes comparable to audio intake. Audio platforms have fingerprinting and use it narrowly, mostly for recording identification rather than for the full range of intake decisions.

## What Already Exists
Reference-matching content identification at scale; rights-holder policy application at upload; dispute and appeal processes; claim and revenue-share mechanics; and transparency reporting on enforcement.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a fixed royalty pool and a delivery chain of distributors. It requires: (1) uploads arriving through distributors rather than directly from users, so intake enforcement acts on a commercial partner rather than an individual — this changes both the leverage and the appeal path; (2) a fixed pool where low-quality uploads take money from other artists rather than creating liability, which changes the motivation entirely; (3) audio similarity rather than video matching, where cover versions and interpolations are legitimate and near-duplicates are the problem; (4) functional and generated audio as a category with no video analogue; and (5) far less legal pressure, so the investment must be justified commercially.

## Target Customer
Catalogue engineering and trust leadership, distributors, rights holders, and content identification vendors.

## Impact If Solved
Video platforms built intake-time identification because legal exposure demanded it, and audio has a fixed pool instead of liability. The motivation must therefore come from protecting other artists' share, which is a different and less automatic pressure.

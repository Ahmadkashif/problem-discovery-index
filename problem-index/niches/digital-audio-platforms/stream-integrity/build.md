# Detecting a Transfer Between Artists

**Niche:** [[niches/digital-audio-platforms/stream-integrity/profile|Stream Integrity]]
**Industry:** [[industries/digital-audio-platforms|Digital Audio Platforms]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Fraud in a fixed pool does not cost the platform anything, which is the entire problem with how seriously it is taken.
**Tags:** #gradient-boosting #graph-theory #change-point-detection #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #compliance #k-means-clustering #contrastive-learning
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to determine whether a stream was a person, against an adversary improving in step — and whoever detects manipulation accurately without penalising legitimate artists protects a pool that every other artist is paid from.

## The Problem
Manipulated streams take money from the pool that every legitimate artist is paid from. The platform's own revenue is unaffected, since the pool is fixed regardless. That structural fact means the party best placed to detect fraud has the least direct financial incentive to detect all of it, and the parties who are harmed — hundreds of thousands of artists losing small amounts each — have no visibility and no standing. Detection exists and improves, and nobody outside the platforms knows how well it works.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The platform bears no direct loss, so integrity competes for investment against features that affect revenue — a harm distributed across third parties with no standing does not generate internal pressure. Publishing a detection rate invites scrutiny and helps the adversary. Enforcement against artists is reputationally costly, which biases toward under-action or unexplained action. And the harmed parties cannot organise because they cannot see the harm.

## What to Build
Detect well and be accountable for how well. Model manipulation behaviourally rather than by rules — listening patterns, account graphs, device and network signatures, playlist structures, temporal signatures — which is the core and is where the adversarial contest actually is. Quantify the redistribution, since fraud in a fixed pool is a measurable transfer and naming the amount changes how seriously it is treated. Publish the detection rate and its methodology, because a self-asserted capability in a contested area is worth little and the disclosure risk is manageable at the right level of abstraction. Distinguish the artist who bought a fraudulent promotion service from the operator running one, as they are treated identically and are not the same. Detect the coordinated network rather than the individual account, which is where the manipulation actually lives. Explain enforcement to the affected artist specifically enough to be contestable, since the current opacity produces injustice and no deterrence. Build an appeal that can succeed, because the innocent case is common. Share signatures with other platforms where lawful, as the adversary operates across all of them. Invite independent audit, which is what would make the numbers credible. And publish what manipulation costs the average artist, because that is what turns a technical problem into an industry priority.

## Target Customer
Trust and integrity leadership, artists losing money to redistribution, artists wrongly penalised, and fraud detection vendors.

## Impact If Built
A harm distributed across third parties with no standing does not generate internal pressure, which is why the platform's incentive is weak. Quantifying the redistribution and publishing the detection rate is what creates the accountability the structure otherwise lacks.

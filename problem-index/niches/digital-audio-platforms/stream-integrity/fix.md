# Nobody Publishes Their Own Rate

**Niche:** [[niches/digital-audio-platforms/stream-integrity/profile|Stream Integrity]]
**Industry:** [[industries/digital-audio-platforms|Digital Audio Platforms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Every platform says it takes manipulation seriously and none of them says how much they catch.
**Tags:** #compliance #quick-win #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #confidence-intervals #revenue-impact #hypothesis-testing #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to determine whether a stream was a person, against an adversary improving in step — and whoever detects manipulation accurately without penalising legitimate artists protects a pool that every other artist is paid from.

## The Problem
Manipulation is acknowledged, policies are announced, penalties are applied, and no platform publishes what proportion of streams it believes are manipulated, how much it detects, or how much money the redistribution moves. Artists are told the problem is being addressed. The entire industry's understanding of the scale of its own central integrity problem consists of occasional research estimates and the confidence of the parties who benefit from the current arrangement.

## Why It's Still Broken
Disclosure risks helping the adversary and inviting criticism, so the default is silence — an organisation with a defensible reason not to disclose will use it indefinitely, and no counterparty has required otherwise. Definitions differ across platforms, which makes a comparable number hard. Nobody is asking with any leverage. And the parties harmed are individually too small to demand it.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Publish at a level that is useful and safe. Report the estimated manipulated share of streams with a methodology note, which is the fix and can be done at an abstraction that does not assist an adversary. Report the amount detected and removed from the pool, since that is the redistribution artists are entitled to know about. Publish the enforcement volumes and the appeal outcomes, as those describe the process rather than the detection. Agree a common definition across the industry, because incomparable numbers are worse than none. Report the average per-artist effect, which is what turns an abstract issue into a personal one for every rights holder. Separate confirmed manipulation from suspected, since the difference matters and is currently elided. Publish periodically so the trend is visible, as the level matters less than the direction. Invite an independent body to verify the figures rather than self-reporting. State plainly what is not being disclosed and why, which is more credible than silence. And commit to it in licensing agreements, since that is where the requirement would actually bind.

## Who Feels the Pain
Artists losing money to a transfer of unknown size; rights holders negotiating without any basis; researchers estimating what the platforms know; and the credibility of the whole royalty arrangement.

## Impact If Fixed
An organisation with a defensible reason not to disclose will use it indefinitely unless a counterparty requires otherwise. A published manipulated share and per-artist effect can be disclosed safely and is what makes the problem an industry priority.

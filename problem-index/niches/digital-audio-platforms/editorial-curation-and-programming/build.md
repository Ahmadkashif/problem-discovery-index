# Getting the Right Pitches Heard

**Niche:** [[niches/digital-audio-platforms/editorial-curation-and-programming/profile|Editorial Curation & Programming]]
**Industry:** [[industries/digital-audio-platforms|Digital Audio Platforms]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** The decision that makes a career is made by people who cannot listen to a fraction of what they are sent.
**Tags:** #contrastive-learning #transformers #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #worker-facing #gradient-boosting #automation #k-nearest-neighbors
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to let a small editorial team decide what tens of millions of people hear, from more pitches than they could listen to in a year — and whoever supports that decision changes who gets a career.

## The Problem
The volume is impossible. A curator receives vastly more pitches than they can hear, so most are filtered by something other than listening — the pitcher's relationship, the label's size, the artist's existing numbers, the order things arrived in. The judgement the curator is there for is applied to whatever survives that filtering, which means the filtering is the decision and it is made by proxies for access rather than by anything about the music.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Curation is understood as taste, so supporting it with tooling reads as automating the thing curators are for — a role defined by judgement resists a system even where the system would only decide what reaches the judgement. The filtering is not visible as a decision because it is an absence. Access-based filtering advantages incumbents who are not motivated to change it. And nobody measures what the filter selects for.

## What to Build
Improve what reaches the ear, not the ear. Triage pitches by musical and contextual fit for the specific playlist rather than by the pitcher's identity, which is the core and changes what the curator hears without touching what they decide. Assemble evidence per pitch — early listener response, audience overlap with the playlist, the artist's own audience behaviour — since a curator judging a track blind is missing information the platform has. Surface the pitch that the data supports and the relationships do not, as that is precisely the case the current filter loses. Report what the pitch filter is selecting for, because if it selects for label size that should be a deliberate choice rather than an emergent one. Measure placement effects properly, so the value of a placement is known rather than assumed. Give the unselected artist a response, since silence is the current answer and the process is opaque. Widen the pitching route beyond established relationships, as the access asymmetry is the structural inequity here. Track which pitches performed after placement, which is the feedback a curator never receives. Keep the curator's judgement decisive, because it is the product and automating it would destroy what editorial is for. And report the composition of what gets placed, as that is the platform's most consequential distributional act.

## Target Customer
Editorial leadership and curators, artists and labels pitching, artist relations teams, and music analytics vendors.

## Impact If Built
A role defined by judgement resists a system even where the system would only decide what reaches the judgement, so access does the filtering. Triaging by fit rather than by pitcher identity changes what the curator hears without touching what they decide.

# More Pitches Than Hours

**Niche:** [[niches/digital-audio-platforms/editorial-curation-and-programming/profile|Editorial Curation & Programming]]
**Industry:** [[industries/digital-audio-platforms|Digital Audio Platforms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The curator's queue would take a year to listen to and refills every week, so most of it is never opened.
**Tags:** #worker-facing #quick-win #workflow-orchestration #automation #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #confidence-intervals #contrastive-learning
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to let a small editorial team decide what tens of millions of people hear, from more pitches than they could listen to in a year — and whoever supports that decision changes who gets a career.

## The Problem
The curator opens the queue and the arithmetic is hopeless. They listen to what they can, which is a small fraction, prioritised by whatever surfaces first — recency, the pitching account's history, a name they recognise. Every unheard pitch is an artist who did everything asked and received nothing, including a response. The curator knows this, which is its own weight, and the system offers no way to make the fraction they hear a better fraction.

## Why It's Still Broken
The queue is a list, so prioritisation defaults to whatever order the interface produces — an inbox with no ranking makes the arrival order into the selection criterion. Triage tooling would need to evaluate audio, which was harder until recently. Nobody measures what proportion of pitches are heard. And the unheard artists have no visibility into any of it.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Make the listened fraction the right fraction. Rank the queue by fit for the specific playlist rather than by arrival, which is the fix and changes the composition of what gets heard without changing anyone's judgement. Report what proportion of pitches are actually opened, since the number is not known internally and is the honest description of the process. Filter out the clearly unsuitable — wrong genre, wrong tempo, wrong language for the playlist — as that is a large share and is mechanical. Surface the artist's own audience response, because a track already resonating with its own listeners is a strong signal and is available. Group similar pitches so a curator can hear a range quickly rather than serially. Give the artist a response, even an automatic one, since silence is experienced as contempt and costs nothing to fix. Tell artists honestly what the odds are, as they are making decisions about promotion budgets in ignorance. Track which unheard pitches later performed, which would reveal what the triage is missing. Rotate the queue so the same submissions are not permanently last. And report pitch volume and open rate as an operating metric, because a process nobody measures cannot be defended.

## Who Feels the Pain
Curators who know most of the queue will never be heard; artists who pitched and received nothing; labels whose relationship is the only reliable route; and a platform whose most consequential editorial act runs on an unreviewed filter.

## Impact If Fixed
An inbox with no ranking makes the arrival order into the selection criterion, so access decides what is heard. Ranking by playlist fit changes the composition of the listened fraction, and an automatic response costs nothing.

# An Account They Can Check

**Niche:** [[niches/digital-audio-platforms/the-independent-artist/profile|The Independent Artist]]
**Industry:** [[industries/digital-audio-platforms|Digital Audio Platforms]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** The people who supply the catalogue are the only party in the system who can verify nothing.
**Tags:** #worker-facing #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #data-integration #revenue-impact #automation #compliance
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to give an artist a defensible account of what happened to their music and their money — and whoever provides it addresses the relationship the entire catalogue depends on.

## The Problem
An artist's income depends on an allocation they cannot audit, exposure decided by a recommender they cannot see, an editorial process they cannot access, matching they cannot verify and enforcement they cannot contest. When any of it goes wrong their only recourse is to post about it publicly. The platforms' relationship with the people who supply everything they sell is therefore structurally adversarial, and the adversarialism is a product of information rather than of interests.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The artist is not the paying customer, so the product was built for listeners and for rights holders with negotiating power — a supplier with no leverage and no contract with the platform does not receive a product. Explanation invites challenge. Much of the information sits behind licensing confidentiality. And the complaints, though loud, do not move revenue.

## What to Build
Give them the account. Show each artist their royalty derivation as far as disclosure allows, which is the core and is the difference between a payment and an assertion. Explain exposure — how much algorithmic and editorial exposure a release received, from whom, and how that compares to their own history — since that is the question behind most complaints. Distinguish a platform change from a demand change in their own data, because artists attribute everything to the algorithm and are sometimes right. Tell them about unmatched money relating to their catalogue, which they currently cannot know about. Explain enforcement decisions specifically enough to be contested. Give them a route to a person for the cases that warrant one, as the absence of any is the core grievance. Publish the rules that affect them before applying them, since discovering a policy change in a statement is what produces the public complaint. Show whether their own followers were reached, which is the most basic and most commonly failed case. Report what the platform can and cannot disclose and why, as honesty about the limit is more credible than silence. And treat artist trust as a measurable asset, because the catalogue depends on people continuing to supply it.

## Target Customer
Artist relations and executive leadership, independent artists, distributors relaying the complaints, and artist services vendors.

## Impact If Built
A supplier with no leverage and no contract with the platform does not receive a product, which is why the artist can verify nothing. A derivable royalty and an exposure account address most of the complaints the category generates.

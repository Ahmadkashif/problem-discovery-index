# Streams Fell and There Is Nobody to Ask

**Niche:** [[niches/digital-audio-platforms/the-independent-artist/profile|The Independent Artist]]
**Industry:** [[industries/digital-audio-platforms|Digital Audio Platforms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** An artist's streams halved in a week and the help centre has an article about how streaming works.
**Tags:** #worker-facing #quick-win #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #automation #confidence-intervals #change-point-detection #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to give an artist a defensible account of what happened to their music and their money — and whoever provides it addresses the relationship the entire catalogue depends on.

## The Problem
Streams drop sharply. The cause could be a playlist removal, an algorithmic change, a seasonal effect, a competitor release, a technical issue or a fraud enforcement action. The artist's dashboard shows the fall. There is no explanation, no contact route, and no way to distinguish between causes that require action and causes that do not. They post about it, speculate with other artists, and often conclude something that is not true.

## Why It's Still Broken
Support is scaled for listeners, so an artist's question has no route — a support function sized for consumers of a subscription has no place for the people supplying the catalogue. The explanation requires joining exposure data the artist cannot see. Nobody measured how often this happens. And the artist's complaint is absorbed as noise.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Explain the drop with the data already held. Show playlist additions and removals affecting the artist's tracks, which is the fix and explains a large share of sudden changes immediately. Break down streams by source — playlist, algorithmic, search, library, external — so the artist can see which component moved. Compare against the artist's own history and against seasonal patterns, since many alarming drops are ordinary. Say when a drop is within normal variation, which prevents unnecessary panic and unnecessary spending. Tell the artist if an enforcement action affected them rather than leaving them to infer it. Provide a contact route for material cases, as a threshold-based escalation is affordable and the absence of any is the grievance. Publish the common causes of sudden changes, so an artist can diagnose their own case. Flag a platform-wide change when one occurs, because artists otherwise attribute a general event to their own work. Report the drop's effect on revenue, which is what they actually need to know. And measure how often artists ask this, since it is almost certainly the most common question in the category and nobody has counted it.

## Who Feels the Pain
Artists making decisions on speculation; distributors relaying questions they cannot answer; platforms whose most common artist interaction is an unanswered complaint; and the credibility of the whole relationship.

## Impact If Fixed
A support function sized for consumers of a subscription has no place for the people supplying the catalogue. Playlist changes and stream source breakdown explain most sudden drops and are data the platform already shows in part.

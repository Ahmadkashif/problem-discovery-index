# The Owner Does Not Know the Money Exists

**Niche:** [[niches/digital-audio-platforms/catalogue-matching-and-metadata/profile|Catalogue Matching & Metadata]]
**Industry:** [[industries/digital-audio-platforms|Digital Audio Platforms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** There is money sitting for a recording that has been streamed for years, and nobody has ever told the person it belongs to.
**Tags:** #compliance #quick-win #data-integration #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #revenue-impact #automation #confidence-intervals
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to match a recording to a rights record cleanly enough that its money reaches an owner — and whoever resolves the unmatched pool releases a real sum to people who do not know it exists.

## The Problem
Unmatched royalties accumulate. The resolution mechanism is a claim, which requires the owner to know the money exists, to know which platform holds it, and to navigate a process built for administrators. Most owners of unmatched money are independent artists who have never heard of the pool. So the money sits, is eventually distributed by whatever the rule says, and goes somewhere other than to the person who made the recording.

## Why It's Still Broken
The resolution mechanism is a claim, so the burden sits with the party who does not know — a process that requires the uninformed party to initiate will resolve almost nothing. Notifying probable owners requires a probable match, which the binary system does not produce. The platform bears no cost from the pool sitting. And nobody publishes its size.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Go and find the owner. Publish the unmatched pool's size and how it is eventually distributed, which is the fix's starting point and creates the pressure for everything else. Produce probable-owner candidates rather than a binary match, since a likely match is enough to make contact. Contact probable owners proactively through the delivery chain, as the distributor who delivered the recording usually knows who the artist is. Provide a searchable route for anyone to check whether unmatched money relates to their catalogue, which is a straightforward lookup nobody offers. Simplify the claim process for an individual artist, because it is currently built for administrators. Report the pool's composition by likely cause — missing identifier, mismatched metadata, disputed ownership — so the systemic fixes are identifiable. Work the pool in order of value, since it is concentrated. Handle the disputed case separately, as two claimants is a different problem from no claimant. Tell distributors which of their deliveries are generating unmatched money, which fixes the cause rather than the instance. And set a target for reducing it, because an unmeasured pool has never shrunk.

## Who Feels the Pain
Artists whose money sits unclaimed for years; distributors blamed for the shortfall; societies holding pools with no claimants; and an industry whose most concrete failure is money that reached nobody.

## Impact If Fixed
A process that requires the uninformed party to initiate will resolve almost nothing, and the owners do not know the pool exists. Producing probable-owner candidates and contacting them through the delivery chain reverses the burden onto the party who can act.

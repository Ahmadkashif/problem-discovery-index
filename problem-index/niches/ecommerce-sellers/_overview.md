## Why These Niches

E-commerce selling is not one market — it fragments by channel model (FBA aggregator vs. DTC brand vs. marketplace arbitrage), geographic scope (domestic vs. cross-border), product type (commodity vs. handmade vs. subscription), and operational function (advertising vs. fulfillment vs. returns). These 8 niches cover the two largest revenue concentrations (Amazon FBA portfolios and Shopify DTC), the two most digitally underserved segments (cross-border sellers navigating customs/VAT complexity and handmade sellers on Etsy/Faire with no operational tools), two specific populations poorly served by existing platforms (subscription box operators who need churn-specific tooling and resellers who need sourcing intelligence), and two high-ROI automation targets (PPC bid management and returns processing). Excluded: Amazon 1P vendors (structurally different), enterprise brands with in-house teams, and dropshippers (low margin, high churn segment).

## Why These Pockets

Four qualifiers, the joint-highest yield of any industry in the sweep alongside accounting, and the reason is a single structural fact: marketplaces publish almost nothing, so every consequential number in e-commerce is somebody's estimate, and the estimating is done by organizations far larger than the sellers.

Three of the four sell an estimate. Share measurement providers tell brands their position in categories that report no data. Marketplace intelligence platforms tell sellers what a product actually sells, which is the number a sourcing decision turns on. Content syndication operations tell brands what a retailer will actually accept, which the published specification does not. The fourth, brand protection, sells a determination rather than an estimate — whether a listing is infringing, a judgment marketplaces will not make and rights holders cannot make at scale.

The gap pattern is unusually consistent and unusually tractable here, because in three of the four cases ground truth is already inside the building. Marketplace intelligence platforms have thousands of subscribers who connect their own seller accounts and thereby disclose true sales — the exact calibration set the estimation models need, used for reporting features instead. Share measurement providers receive actual sales from contributing brands and treat it as delivered product rather than as a standing validation set. Content syndication providers receive a precise statement of what each retailer really enforces every time a submission is rejected, and work it as a ticket. In all three, the correction is available and unused.

Brand protection is the exception and the sharpest single finding in the industry. Enforcement removes listings; the operator relists within days under a new account; and the entire segment reports takedown volume, which cannot distinguish a programme that deters operators from one that briefly inconveniences them. Nobody measures recurrence, in a service sold entirely on the claim that it reduces infringement.

Nine pockets logged without qualifying. The largest insight workforce found — marketplace trust and safety, up to five thousand investigators with complete platform history — scores 41 because there are three possible customers on earth.

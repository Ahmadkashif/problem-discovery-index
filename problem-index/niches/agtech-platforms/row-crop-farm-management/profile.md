# Row Crop Farm Management

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/agtech-platforms|Agtech Platforms]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in row crop software is fighting to tell a grower which of their input decisions actually changed the yield on their own ground — and whoever produces credible on-farm effect estimates takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.5B US row crop farm management and precision agriculture software
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~38% of agtech revenue
**Digital Adoption:** High — machine data collection is near-universal on larger operations
**Target Buyer:** Growers and farm managers of 1,000+ acres; product leads at John Deere Operations Center, Climate FieldView and Trimble
**Automation Potential:** Very High — the field is already an instrumented experiment and nobody is running it as one

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Row crop agriculture is the most thoroughly instrumented production activity in the economy outside a factory. A planter records population and depth at sub-metre resolution; a sprayer records rate and product by location; a combine records yield continuously across the field. The grower makes six-figure input decisions annually — seed genetics and population, nitrogen rate and timing, fungicide, seed treatment — and decides them on a neighbour's experience, a retailer's recommendation and a trial plot run at a research farm in a different soil and a different year. The evidence that would answer the question for this farm is generated every season by the equipment already in the field, and the platforms render it as a yield map. The gap between the evidence available and the evidence used is the widest in the vault.

## Current Tools & Gaps
John Deere Operations Center dominates by installed base and is tied to Deere equipment; Climate FieldView is the largest brand-independent option; Trimble and AGCO serve mixed fleets. All handle data collection, mapping, prescription writing and basic recordkeeping. Variable rate prescriptions are standard practice; measuring whether a prescription worked is not. Yield maps are displayed and not analysed — the variation within a field, which is the signal, is presented as colour. Nothing supports randomised on-farm trials despite every modern planter and sprayer being capable of executing one by simply varying the prescription, and nothing estimates an effect with an interval, which is what a grower would need to act on it.

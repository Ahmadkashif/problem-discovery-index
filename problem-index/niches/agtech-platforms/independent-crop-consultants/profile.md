# Independent Crop Consultants — Acres Per Consultant

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/agtech-platforms|Agtech Platforms]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor selling to independent consultants is fighting to raise the acres one person can cover at quality — and whoever cuts the hours between walking a field and delivering a report takes the firm.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$300M US software spend across independent crop consulting firms
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~8% of agtech revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — consultants carry tablets and write reports at night
**Target Buyer:** Owners of independent consulting firms, from sole practitioners to regional practices
**Automation Potential:** Very High — scouting capture and report generation are the whole cost structure and are manual

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
An independent crop consultant is paid per acre by the grower, sells no products, and their business is entirely bounded by how many acres one person can cover well in a season. That single constraint determines everything: their revenue, their pricing, whether they can take on a new client, and whether the quality of their work holds in July when everything needs scouting at once. The largest consumer of their time after driving is reporting — a day of field observations becomes a set of written reports delivered to growers, produced in the evening, for every field visited. In peak season a consultant may walk fields from dawn until evening and then write for three hours. The reporting is genuinely valuable to the grower and is transcription of what the consultant already recorded, which makes it the clearest automation target in the profession.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Agworld, Agrian, and the scouting-focused products handle field records, observations, photographs and recommendation documentation, and several have improved capture substantially. Imagery is available in most of them. The gaps: report generation from captured observations is partial at best, so the consultant restates in prose what they already entered as observations; route and visit planning is manual, so the decision of where to go tomorrow is made from memory and a map; capture in the field still requires typing on a tablet with dirty hands, which is why many consultants revert to a notebook and re-enter in the evening — the single worst outcome and a common one; and nothing helps a consultant decide which fields need a visit this week, which is the prioritisation that would most directly raise their covered acres.

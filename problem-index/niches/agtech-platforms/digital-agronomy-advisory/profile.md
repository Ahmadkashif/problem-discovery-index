# Digital Agronomy & Advisory

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/agtech-platforms|Agtech Platforms]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is that every competitor is fighting to make agronomic advice more valuable per acre — but a retail agronomist giving free advice attached to input sales and an independent consultant billing by the acre face opposite problems. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$900M US spend across agronomy software, advisory platforms and digital scouting
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~23% of agtech revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Medium-High — agronomists carry tablets and write reports in the evening
**Target Buyer:** Retail agronomy leaders at co-ops and input retailers; owners of independent crop consulting firms
**Automation Potential:** High — scouting capture, report generation and recommendation evidence are all addressable

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Agronomic advice is where most growers' input decisions are actually made. Somebody walks the field, assesses the crop, and recommends what to apply and when. That person works in one of two structurally different businesses. In retail agronomy they are employed by a co-op or an input retailer, the advice is free, and the employer earns on the products recommended — which is the industry's normal arrangement and carries an obvious tension that everyone involved is aware of and nobody measures. In independent consulting they are paid per acre by the grower, sell no products, and their constraint is how many acres one person can cover well in a season. The advice is superficially the same and the businesses have nothing in common, which is why the niche decomposes.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Agworld, Agrian, Proagrica and the platform vendors' own agronomy modules handle scouting records, recommendations and compliance documentation. Satellite imagery is widely available and used for visual scouting. Prescription writing is standard. The gaps shared across both models: recommendations are recorded and their outcomes are not, so an agronomist accumulates decades of experience with no systematic feedback on which of their recommendations paid; scouting observations are captured as notes and photographs that are never analysed across fields or seasons; and pest and disease pressure forecasting, which would let a scouting visit be timed and targeted, is largely generic rather than field-specific. Where the two models diverge is what the binding constraint is, which the sub-niches address.

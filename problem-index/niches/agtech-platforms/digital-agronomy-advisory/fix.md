## The Problem
A senior agronomist retires after thirty years. What they knew — which hybrids handle this soil in a wet spring, which fields flood in the corner, which growers will follow a recommendation and which will not, what actually happened the last three times a particular disease pressure appeared in this county — leaves with them. Their successor begins from published recommendations and their own observation, and spends a decade rebuilding a subset of it. This happens continuously across an ageing profession, and the knowledge in question is precisely the local, field-specific, hard-won kind that no published research produces.

## Why It's Still Broken
Scouting notes and recommendations are captured as free text for compliance and client communication, not as structured observations, so they are unsearchable and unanalysable across fields and seasons. Nobody has asked for structure, because the immediate purpose of a scouting record is to document a visit. And the knowledge itself is largely tacit — the agronomist would struggle to write down what they know, which means capturing it has to be a by-product of the work rather than a separate exercise.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Structure the observation at the point of capture and make the history searchable. Scouting records carry the field, the growth stage, the observation type, the severity and the location within the field, in addition to the note and the photograph — which is a slightly more structured capture and no slower if designed properly, particularly with voice. The field's own history then becomes queryable: what has been observed on this field in previous seasons, at this stage, in comparable weather, and what was done about it. Across the organisation the same history supports the question a departing agronomist takes with them — which fields in this county had this problem, and how did it resolve. Photographs accumulate into a local reference library that is more useful than any published one because it is this region's crops and this region's problems. None of this asks the agronomist to write anything extra; it asks the capture to be structured and the history to be searchable.

## Who Feels the Pain
New agronomists rebuilding local knowledge from scratch; growers whose new adviser does not know their fields; and the profession, which is ageing and transmitting its most valuable knowledge by conversation.

## Impact If Fixed
Structured capture costs nothing at the point of observation and converts a compliance record into a searchable institutional memory. In an ageing profession with high local specificity, the succession problem is the industry's quietest and most consequential, and the record is the only mechanism that addresses it.

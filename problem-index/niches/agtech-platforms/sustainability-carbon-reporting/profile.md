# Sustainability & Carbon Reporting

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/agtech-platforms|Agtech Platforms]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in agricultural sustainability reporting is fighting to let one set of farm records satisfy every programme that wants evidence — and whoever makes a grower's data reusable across programmes takes the enrolment.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$460M US spend across carbon programmes, sustainability reporting and verification in agriculture
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~11% of agtech revenue, in the category's newest area
**Digital Adoption:** Low-Medium — the programmes are new and the infrastructure was never designed for them
**Target Buyer:** Programme administrators, grain buyers and food companies with supply chain commitments; growers as the party doing the work
**Automation Potential:** Very High — report generation from a common record is a solved software problem

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A new class of measurement and verification requirements has arrived in agriculture with real money attached. Carbon programmes pay for practice change and require evidence of the practices and, increasingly, modelled outcomes. Grain buyers and food companies with supply chain emissions commitments require their own evidence from their own suppliers. Conservation programmes have their own documentation. Each wants overlapping but non-identical information, in its own format, through its own portal, with its own verification process. A grower who wants to participate in three programmes does the work three times, and the rational response — which many growers take — is to enrol in one and decline the rest, which is bad for the grower's income and bad for the programmes' coverage. The underlying farm records are largely the same in every case.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Carbon programme operators run their own enrolment and data collection platforms. Farm management systems hold the practice records in principle and are used inconsistently. Soil sampling and modelling providers serve the measurement side. Verification is performed by third parties with their own requirements. The gaps: no common record satisfies multiple programmes, so data is re-entered per programme; historical practice evidence, which most programmes require to establish a baseline, is frequently unavailable because the records were never kept in a usable form; the modelled outcomes that determine payment are produced by programme-specific models the grower cannot inspect or verify; and nobody has quantified the grower's own cost of participation — the data work, the practice change, the verification — against the payment, which is the calculation a grower most needs and least has.

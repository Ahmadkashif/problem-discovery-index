# Nobody Computes What Participation Costs the Grower

**Niche:** [[niches/agtech-platforms/sustainability-carbon-reporting/profile|Sustainability & Carbon Reporting]]
**Industry:** [[industries/agtech-platforms|Agtech Platforms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A grower is offered a payment per acre for a practice change and is given no accounting of what the practice change, the data work and the verification will actually cost them, so the decision is made on the headline rate.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #confidence-intervals #evaluation-metrics #hypothesis-testing #revenue-impact #compliance #quick-win #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in agricultural sustainability reporting is fighting to let one set of farm records satisfy every programme that wants evidence — and whoever makes a grower's data reusable across programmes takes the enrolment.

## The Problem
A programme offers a payment per acre for adopting cover crops and reducing tillage. The grower sees the rate. What they do not see is the seed and establishment cost of the cover crop, the equipment change or custom operation reduced tillage may require, the yield effect in the transition years which the literature suggests can be negative before it is positive, the hours of data assembly and enrolment, the verification visit, and the contract term that commits the acres for several years including the right to terminate. Each of those is knowable and none is presented alongside the rate. Growers enrol and some of them discover the arithmetic afterwards, which is the fastest way to damage a programme's reputation in a community where everyone talks.

## Why It's Still Broken
The programme's interest is enrolment and the enrolment materials are marketing, which is unsurprising. The grower's own costs are farm-specific and require the grower's records to compute, which nobody has assembled — so even a well-intentioned programme could not present a personalised figure. And the transition yield effect is genuinely uncertain and varies by soil and system, which makes it easy to omit rather than to state with its range.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Produce the grower-side arithmetic before enrolment. Direct costs of the practice change from the grower's own cost records and local custom rates. Expected yield effect during transition with its uncertainty, from published evidence for comparable soils and systems, stated as a range rather than omitted. Administrative time estimated honestly. Contract terms summarised in plain language, particularly the commitment period, the termination provisions and what happens if the practice lapses. Net expected return per acre with the uncertainty shown, and a break-even statement — what the yield effect would have to be for participation to lose money. Compare programmes side by side on that basis rather than on headline rate. And record what actually happened for growers who enrol, since after a few seasons the local realised experience is better evidence than any projection and is exactly what the next grower asks about over the fence.

## Who Feels the Pain
Growers enrolling on a headline rate and discovering the arithmetic afterwards; programme operators whose reputation suffers in tight communities when that happens; and the practice change itself, which needs durable participation rather than enrolment followed by regret.

## Impact If Fixed
An honest net-return calculation before enrolment is what makes participation a decision rather than a gamble, and it is computable from the grower's own records plus published evidence. The accumulating record of what participants actually experienced is the more durable asset, because in this community that is the evidence that will decide whether these programmes reach the scale they need.

# Prescription Outcome Measurement as the Product

**Niche:** [[niches/agtech-platforms/retail-agronomy-prescriptions/profile|Retail Agronomy — Prescriptions With a Conflict Attached]]
**Industry:** [[industries/agtech-platforms|Agtech Platforms]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A retailer's agronomic advice is free, its credibility is discounted because the retailer sells the inputs, and the only thing that would resolve the discount — evidence that the prescriptions paid — is the one thing nobody measures.
**Tags:** #causal-inference #hypothesis-testing #confidence-intervals #bayesian-inference #evaluation-metrics #gradient-boosting #revenue-impact #compliance
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in retail agronomy software is fighting to make a recommendation credible when the adviser's employer sells the product — and whoever can evidence that a prescription paid for the grower takes the relationship.

## The Problem
A retail agronomist writes a variable rate nitrogen prescription with a clear agronomic rationale. The grower applies it and pays for the nitrogen. At the end of the season nobody establishes whether the variable rate prescription produced more than a flat rate would have, because no comparison was designed into it. The retailer believes it did, the grower half believes it, and the discount the grower applies to next year's recommendation is unchanged. Over years, the retailer's substantial investment in agronomic capability produces a relationship asset rather than a demonstrable one.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The obvious commercial objection — that measurement might show some prescriptions do not pay — is real and is also the wrong way round: a retailer that can demonstrate which of its recommendations pay has a differentiator no competitor can claim, and one that cannot is competing on price and relationship indefinitely. The deeper obstacle is that measurement requires designing a comparison into the prescription, which means deliberately not applying the recommendation on part of the field, which feels to an agronomist like withholding the right answer. That is a reasonable instinct and it is what keeps the question unanswerable.

## What to Build
Measurement designed into the prescription as standard practice. Check strips at a flat rate, or at an alternative rate, written into every variable rate prescription by default — a small proportion of the field, randomised, positioned to be analysable. At harvest the comparison is computed with the spatial methods the row crop niche describes, and the output is a per-field, per-season statement: this prescription returned this much per acre against the alternative, plus or minus this much. Results accumulate per grower, per field type and across the retailer's whole book, which is what turns a per-field answer into a credible claim. Publish the aggregate honestly, including where prescriptions did not pay, because selective reporting is exactly what the grower already suspects and doing it would be worse than not measuring at all. The by-product is a retailer that knows which of its own recommendations are worth making.

## Target Customer
Co-ops and input retailers with agronomy organisations, the platform vendors serving them, and the growers whose input spend is shaped by this advice.

## Impact If Built
Demonstrable prescription value is the only answer to the credibility discount that the retail agronomy model carries, and it is available from equipment the grower already owns. The retailer that measures first gains a position no competitor can match by hiring more agronomists — and the growers get an answer to a question they have been asking informally for as long as the model has existed.

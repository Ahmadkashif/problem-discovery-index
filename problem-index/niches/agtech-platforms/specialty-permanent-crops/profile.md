# Specialty & Permanent Crops

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/agtech-platforms|Agtech Platforms]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in specialty crop software is fighting to manage a permanent planting at the block and the tree rather than at the field — and whoever makes per-block economics and harvest labour legible takes the operation.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$520M US software spend across orchards, vineyards, and vegetable and specialty crop operations
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~13% of agtech revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Low — the row crop platforms fit badly and most operations run on spreadsheets
**Target Buyer:** Orchard and vineyard managers, vegetable operation managers, and packing and marketing organisations
**Automation Potential:** High — block-level costing, harvest planning and labour management are structured and are manual

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A permanent crop is a capital asset with a thirty-year life that produces annually, which makes its management fundamentally different from an annual row crop. Decisions are per block and sometimes per tree: which blocks to replant, which varieties and rootstocks performed, where the return on a replant is best, how to prune and thin, when to harvest each block. The cost structure is dominated by labour rather than by inputs — harvest alone can be the majority of production cost — and labour is scheduled against a ripeness window measured in days. Vegetables add their own structure: multiple plantings a season, harvest windows measured in hours, and food safety traceability requirements with real regulatory teeth. None of this is what row crop software models, so the segment runs on spreadsheets and the knowledge of long-serving managers.

## Current Tools & Gaps
A handful of specialist products serve vineyards and orchards, with varying depth; vegetable operations use a mix of farm management, food safety and packing software. Row crop platforms are used and fit badly, because their unit of management is the field and the season. The gaps: per-block cost and return accounting, which is the analysis that should drive replant decisions on a thirty-year asset and is done in a spreadsheet if at all; harvest labour planning against ripeness, which is the operation's largest cost and hardest scheduling problem; variety and rootstock performance across blocks and years, which is the most valuable long-run knowledge these operations generate and which lives in a manager's memory; and traceability, which is a compliance requirement handled with paper records in a substantial part of the segment.

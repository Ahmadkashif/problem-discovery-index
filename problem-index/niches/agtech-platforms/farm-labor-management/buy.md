# Piece Counting and Time Capture Off the Shelf

**Niche:** [[niches/agtech-platforms/farm-labor-management/profile|Farm Labour Management]]
**Industry:** [[industries/agtech-platforms|Agtech Platforms]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Barcode scanning, RFID, biometric time clocks and counting from images are all commodity technologies deployed across every labour-intensive industry, and a fruit harvest is counted with a ticket book.
**Tags:** #cnns #object-detection #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #data-integration #automation #compliance #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in farm labour software is fighting to make piece-rate pay, hours and compliance provable to the worker as well as to the regulator — and whoever makes the record trusted by both sides takes the operation.

## The Problem
A crew leader carries a book of tickets and a pen. Each full bin gets a ticket with the worker's number. At the end of the day the tickets are collected, counted and entered into a spreadsheet, and at some point in that chain tickets are lost, numbers are misread and the count for a particular worker is wrong. The equivalent transaction in a warehouse has been scanned since the 1990s.

## What Already Exists
Barcode and QR scanning, RFID tags and readers, rugged handheld terminals, biometric and badge time clocks, and object counting from images are all mature, cheap and deployed at scale in warehousing, logistics and manufacturing. Offline-capable field devices are standard. Multilingual interfaces are ordinary. Every component required for accurate piece and time capture in a field is commodity and has been for years.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a field, a seasonal workforce and a mobile collection point. It requires: (1) capture at the point the crop changes hands, which in a harvest is a moving truck or a collection station rather than a fixed dock — so the reader has to go where the bins are and work without power or connectivity; (2) worker identification that is fast, reliable in gloves and dirt, and respectful — a badge scan is fine and a fingerprint reader in a field is both unreliable and an unnecessary intrusion, and the choice matters for adoption; (3) tolerance for the realities of harvest, including partial bins, rejected fruit and reassignment mid-row, since a system that cannot represent them will be worked around with a ticket book within a week; (4) immediate feedback to the worker at the point of credit — a display, a printed slip, a message — because the credit is only verifiable if the worker sees it when it happens; and (5) a deployment cost proportionate to a seasonal operation, which rules out most of the industrial equipment and points at phones and cheap tags.

## Target Customer
Specialty crop operations with harvest crews, farm labour contractors, and the agricultural labour management vendors serving them.

## Impact If Solved
Accurate individual piece capture removes the most common and least defensible failure in agricultural pay, and the technology has been available and unadopted for two decades. Immediate feedback at the point of credit is the specific design decision that converts a tracking system into a record the worker can rely on.

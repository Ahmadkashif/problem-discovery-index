# Farm Labour Management

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/agtech-platforms|Agtech Platforms]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in farm labour software is fighting to make piece-rate pay, hours and compliance provable to the worker as well as to the regulator — and whoever makes the record trusted by both sides takes the operation.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$340M US software spend across agricultural labour management, payroll and compliance
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~9% of agtech revenue, against the most labour-intensive part of American agriculture
**Digital Adoption:** Low — crews are tracked on paper tickets and payroll is reconciled by hand
**Target Buyer:** Labour-intensive operations and farm labour contractors; the workers are the other party to every record
**Automation Potential:** High — piece counting, hours capture and compliance documentation are all mechanical

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Specialty crop agriculture runs on hand labour and the record of that labour is thin on both sides. Piece-rate pay — so much per bin, per bucket, per tray — is the dominant structure, and the count is made by a crew leader with a ticket book or a punch card. Hours are recorded to establish that piece-rate earnings met minimum wage, which is a legal requirement and is frequently reconstructed rather than measured. H-2A employment adds substantial obligations around wage rates, hours guarantees, housing and transport, all of which must be documented. Workers, many of whom are seasonal and many of whom do not speak English as a first language, have limited ability to verify what they were credited with or what they were paid. Employers face real compliance exposure and manage it with paper. It is a setting where better records serve the worker and the employer at once, and where nearly every product built to date has been designed for the employer alone.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Agricultural payroll providers and a handful of labour management products serve the segment, handling payroll computation and some compliance reporting. Piece counting is increasingly done with scanning at the collection point in larger operations and with paper elsewhere. H-2A compliance is a specialist service area. The gaps: the worker's own view is essentially absent, so a worker cannot check their piece count, their hours or their pay against anything; piece counts attributed at the crew rather than the individual level create disputes nobody can resolve; the minimum wage make-up calculation, which is where compliance failures concentrate, is performed at payroll from reconstructed hours; and heat, rest and sanitation obligations, which are increasingly regulated and genuinely matter, are documented by attestation rather than recorded.

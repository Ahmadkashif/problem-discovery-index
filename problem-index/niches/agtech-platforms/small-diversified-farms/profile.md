# Small & Diversified Farms

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/agtech-platforms|Agtech Platforms]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor selling to small diversified farms is fighting to make recordkeeping and compliance work for an operation with twenty crops, five sales channels and nobody in the office — and whoever gets the record-keeping burden lowest takes the segment.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$290M US software spend across small, diversified and direct-market farms
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~7% of agtech revenue, against a very large number of operations
**Digital Adoption:** Low — spreadsheets, notebooks and a point of sale at the farmers market
**Target Buyer:** The owner, who is also the grower, the marketer and the bookkeeper
**Automation Potential:** Very High — the entire administrative load falls on one person who is outdoors

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Every product in agricultural technology assumes scale and a single commodity. A diversified vegetable operation growing forty crops across succession plantings, selling through a farmers market, a subscription box scheme, two restaurants and a wholesale account, with a handful of seasonal employees, matches none of those assumptions. Its planning problem is a succession schedule across crops and beds; its recordkeeping problem is food safety and organic certification, both of which demand traceability records; its sales problem is five channels with different pricing, packing and delivery; and its labour problem is a small crew doing everything. The person handling all of it is the same person who was harvesting at six in the morning. The available software either serves row crop operations, or serves one of these functions in isolation, so the farm runs on spreadsheets and the owner does the administration at night.

## Current Tools & Gaps
A handful of purpose-built products serve this segment — crop planning tools, subscription box management platforms, farmers market point of sale — generally good at one function. Organic certification and food safety recordkeeping is served by paper templates and a few specialist products. General farm management software fits badly. The gaps are the joins: crop planning does not connect to sales commitments, so a farm plants from last year's plan rather than from what it has actually committed to sell; harvest records are kept separately from traceability records, so the food safety documentation is reconstructed at audit rather than captured; and profitability by crop and by channel — which is the analysis that would tell the farm what to stop growing — requires labour attribution the operation has no mechanism to capture.

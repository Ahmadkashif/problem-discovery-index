# One Record From Seed to Market Stall

**Niche:** [[niches/agtech-platforms/small-diversified-farms/profile|Small & Diversified Farms]]
**Industry:** [[industries/agtech-platforms|Agtech Platforms]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A diversified farm's crop plan, its planting records, its harvest records, its traceability documentation and its five sales channels are five separate systems or five spreadsheets, maintained by the same exhausted person at night.
**Tags:** #data-integration #optimization-fundamentals #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #workflow-orchestration #compliance #automation #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor selling to small diversified farms is fighting to make recordkeeping and compliance work for an operation with twenty crops, five sales channels and nobody in the office — and whoever gets the record-keeping burden lowest takes the segment.

## The Problem
A farm plants a succession of lettuce. The planting goes in a notebook. The harvest goes on a clipboard in the wash-pack shed. The subscription boxes are packed from a list in a different application, the farmers market sales come off a point of sale, the restaurant order is an email, and the organic certification records are a binder updated when the inspection is scheduled. Every one of those concerns the same lettuce, and connecting them — which is what traceability, profitability and next year's plan all require — is a person retyping at eleven at night. The administrative burden is the most commonly cited reason people leave this kind of farming, ahead of the physical work.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The segment is large in number and small in spend, fragmented, and sells to buyers who cannot evaluate software and have no time to implement it. The products that exist address one function well because a single-function product is what a small team can build and sell. Building the whole chain requires modelling a farm whose complexity — many crops, many plantings, many channels — is genuinely higher per acre than a row crop operation's, for a customer who will pay a fraction as much.

## What to Build
One record keyed on the planting. A succession planting is created once, from the crop plan, and everything that happens to it attaches: the seeding, the transplanting, any applications, the harvests with quantities and dates, and where each harvest went — which box run, which market, which wholesale order. From that single spine, traceability falls out automatically, since a lot's history is the record; profitability by crop and by channel falls out once labour is attributed; and next year's plan is informed by what actually happened rather than by what was planned. Capture must be designed for the wash-pack shed and the field: voice, wet hands, a shared device, offline. The crop plan is the anchor and should be the thing the farm interacts with most, because it is the document these operations actually run on and every product treats it as a spreadsheet export.

## Target Customer
Diversified vegetable, fruit and mixed operations selling through direct and wholesale channels, and the specialist vendors currently serving one function each who could integrate rather than compete.

## Impact If Built
Administrative burden is the binding constraint on these operations and a cited reason for people leaving the work. A single planting-keyed record removes most of the duplicate entry, turns certification and food safety documentation into a by-product rather than a project, and produces the by-crop and by-channel profitability that these farms most need and almost never have.

# Grain Marketing & Risk Tools

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/agtech-platforms|Agtech Platforms]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in grower-side grain marketing is fighting to show a grower their own position — bushels priced, basis exposure, storage and interest cost, against production risk — in one place, and whoever makes the grower's position legible takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$380M US software spend across grain marketing, contract management and risk tools
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~10% of agtech revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Low on the grower side — merchandisers are well tooled and growers are not
**Target Buyer:** Growers marketing their own production; elevators and merchandisers as the other side of the trade
**Automation Potential:** High — position aggregation and scenario analysis are computable and are done on paper

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A grower's marketing decisions determine a large share of their income and are made with substantially less information than the party on the other side of the trade. Grain is sold through a mixture of instruments — cash sales, forward contracts, hedge-to-arrive, basis contracts, deferred pricing, futures and options — each with a different exposure, each entered at a different time, some with an elevator and some with a broker. A grower with a dozen positions across three elevators and a futures account frequently cannot state what proportion of expected production is priced, what basis exposure remains open, or what a ten percent yield shortfall would do to their obligations. The merchandiser on the other side knows their own book precisely. It is one of the clearest information asymmetries in American agriculture and the tooling gap is almost entirely on the grower's side.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Merchandising and elevator systems are mature and serve the buy side well. Broker platforms serve futures and options positions. Farm management systems record contracts inconsistently. Grower-facing marketing tools exist and are mostly advisory subscriptions or elevator portals showing that elevator's contracts only. The gaps: no aggregated position view across counterparties, so a grower's own book is assembled mentally; no production risk integration, so the interaction between contracted bushels and a bad yield year — which is the scenario that actually hurts — is not modelled; storage and interest carrying cost is not included in the comparison between selling now and storing; and the contract terms themselves, which differ in important ways between instruments, are not represented well enough to compute exposure from.

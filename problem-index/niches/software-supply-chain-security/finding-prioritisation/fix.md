# The Count Reported as the Posture

**Niche:** [[niches/software-supply-chain-security/finding-prioritisation/profile|Finding Prioritisation]]
**Industry:** [[industries/software-supply-chain-security|Software Supply Chain Security]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Security posture is reported as the number of open findings, which rewards a tool that reports more and a team that suppresses rather than fixes.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #hypothesis-testing #confidence-intervals #evaluation-metrics #logistic-regression #compliance #quick-win #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn thousands of findings into the handful that matter for this application — and whoever does that takes the account, because the ratio between what the category reports and what is worth acting on is its central unresolved problem.

## The Problem
The security dashboard reports eleven thousand open findings across the estate, down from twelve thousand. This is the number reviewed monthly and the one teams are held to. It rewards suppressing findings, downgrading severities and choosing a scanner that reports less, and it is unaffected by whether the three findings that actually matter were fixed. Everybody involved understands this, and the number is reported because it is the number the tools produce.

## Why It's Still Broken
The count is produced automatically and requires no judgement, which is why it became the metric — the same reason downloads, coverage, pass rates and cache hit ratios became metrics in other categories in this vault. A better metric requires prioritisation, which is the unsolved problem. Reporting a smaller, honest number is uncomfortable because it appears to be a reduction in rigour. And the count rises when a new vulnerability class is published, which makes the metric partly a measure of the outside world.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Report what was at risk and what was done about it. Time to remediate for the findings that were genuinely urgent, which is the operational measure of whether the function works and requires only a definition of urgent. Exposure duration — how long an exploitable, reachable, internet-facing vulnerability remained unfixed — which is the number that corresponds to actual risk. Count only the prioritised set as open, with the deferred set reported separately and explicitly as accepted risk, which is honest and prevents the suppression incentive. Report the escaped cases: vulnerabilities that were exploited or nearly exploited and where they sat in the queue, which is the validation of the prioritisation and is the strongest available evidence. Track suppression and dismissal rates, since a rising one is either good triage or bad practice and the distinction matters. Separate new findings caused by newly published vulnerabilities from those caused by new code, since the first is the world changing and the second is the organisation's own introduction rate. And report the introduction rate, because preventing new exposure is more valuable than clearing old and nobody measures it.

## Who Feels the Pain
Security teams held to a number that rewards suppression; engineering teams receiving work generated by a metric; and organisations whose reported posture improved while their actual exposure did not.

## Impact If Fixed
Exposure duration for reachable, exploitable findings is the number that corresponds to risk and is computable once prioritisation exists. Separating world-driven from self-introduced findings distinguishes the two very different problems the single count conflates.

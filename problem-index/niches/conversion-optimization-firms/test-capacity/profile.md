# Test Capacity Allocation

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/conversion-optimization-firms|Conversion Optimization Firms]]
**Category:** 🔵 High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to spend a finite number of tests on the changes most likely to produce a detectable effect, when the selection is currently by ease of building — and whoever allocates it takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$500M US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~20% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Low — by ease
**Target Buyer:** Programme leadership
**Automation Potential:** High — prioritisation and portfolio design

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The programme's value is bounded by test capacity and that capacity is spent badly. Most tests are small variations on low-traffic pages that could never have produced a detectable effect, chosen because they were easy to build rather than because they addressed a plausible mechanism. The number of tests a site can run concurrently is limited by traffic, and the allocation of that scarce resource is the single largest determinant of whether a programme is worth anything.

## Current Tools & Gaps
A backlog of ideas, a prioritisation framework scored by hand and a launch calendar. The gaps: no modelling of expected value per test; no account of traffic as a shared constraint; no measure of whether the effect was detectable; no portfolio view across concurrent tests; and no learning from what has worked.

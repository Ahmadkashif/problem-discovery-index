**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to spend a finite number of tests on the changes most likely to produce a detectable effect, when the selection is currently by ease of building — and whoever allocates it takes the account.

## The Problem
Research portfolio management addresses the same structure: a fixed budget, many candidate projects with uncertain returns, and a need to balance safe incremental work against larger speculative bets. The discipline models expected value, accounts for the probability of a null result, deliberately reserves capacity for higher-variance work, and treats the portfolio rather than the individual project as the unit of decision. Test backlogs are scored on ease, impact guessed and confidence asserted.

## What Already Exists
Expected value modelling under uncertainty; explicit probability of success by project type; portfolio balance between incremental and speculative; capacity constrained allocation; and learning priors from past outcomes.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a constraint that is traffic rather than money. It requires: (1) concurrent tests competing for the same visitors, so capacity is a statistical rather than a financial constraint and adding budget does not add capacity — this is the substantive difference; (2) detectability as a hard gate, since a test that cannot detect its effect has zero expected value regardless of the idea's merit; (3) effect size priors learnable from the programme's own history, which research funding rarely has so directly; (4) implementation cost varying enormously across ideas of similar merit; and (5) a client expecting a steady stream of tests rather than a portfolio outcome.

## Target Customer
Conversion optimisation firms and experimentation teams, programme leadership, testing platform vendors, and optimisation consultancies.

## Impact If Solved
Research portfolios allocate scarce capacity across uncertain bets with expected value and deliberate variance. Traffic as the constraint — where more budget adds no capacity — is what makes detectability a hard gate here.

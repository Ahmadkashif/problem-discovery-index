# The Monthly Report Needs a Win

**Niche:** [[niches/conversion-optimization-firms/the-strategist/profile|The Strategist]]
**Industry:** [[industries/conversion-optimization-firms|Conversion Optimization Firms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Three tests ran this month, none concluded, and the report is due Thursday.
**Tags:** #worker-facing #quick-win #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #descriptive-statistics #workflow-orchestration #compliance #hypothesis-testing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to let the person who knows what day-four significance means say so, when the monthly report is expected to contain a win — and whoever supports that takes the account.

## The Problem
The monthly reporting cycle and the experiment duration do not match. Tests take as long as they take; the report is due on a date. In a month where nothing concluded, the strategist must either report that nothing happened — which reads as no progress — or find something in the data that can be presented as a finding, which is where the post-hoc segmentation comes from. The calendar creates the malpractice.

## Why It's Still Broken
The cadence was never aligned to the work — a reporting rhythm set by a client's calendar rather than by experiment duration guarantees months with nothing to report, and the response is to find something. Nobody set expectations at the start. A null looks like failure. And every firm does the same, so the expectation is industry-wide.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Change what the report contains, since the cadence will not move. Report programme progress — tests launched, running, concluded, and what was learned — rather than wins, which is the fix and makes a month without a conclusion reportable. Set the expectation at kickoff that most tests do not produce a detectable effect, which is true and is never said. Report the results that are still running as running, with their expected conclusion date. Present a rigorous null as a finding, since knowing a change does not help is genuinely useful and is currently discarded. Include the pipeline and the reasoning, which is what the client is actually buying. Report on a cadence matched to test duration where the client will accept it. Give the strategist a firm policy to cite rather than a personal position. Have leadership present the expectation-setting conversation rather than leaving it to the account team. Track the ratio of real effects found to tests run, and share it. And stop presenting a win as the unit of progress, which is the habit the whole problem rests on.

## Who Feels the Pain
Strategists choosing between honesty and the relationship; clients receiving findings that will not replicate; the discipline, whose case studies are full of noise; and the practitioners who report honestly and lose accounts to those who do not.

## Impact If Fixed
A reporting rhythm set by a calendar rather than by experiment duration guarantees months with nothing to report, and the response is to find something. Reporting programme progress rather than wins makes an honest month presentable.

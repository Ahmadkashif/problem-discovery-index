## The Problem
Statistical methodology has well-developed answers to every one of this discipline's failures. Sequential analysis permits valid continuous monitoring with the error rate controlled. Alpha spending functions allow interim looks. Always-valid inference and confidence sequences make peeking legitimate by construction. Multiplicity corrections handle many comparisons. The methods are published, implemented in libraries and taught. Testing platforms implement a fixed-horizon test and display it continuously, which is the one combination that does not work.

## What Already Exists
Sequential analysis and alpha spending; always-valid confidence sequences; multiplicity correction procedures; power analysis and minimum detectable effect calculation; and Bayesian alternatives with explicit decision rules.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to practitioners who are not statisticians and a workflow that must stay fast. It requires: (1) correct methods delivered as defaults rather than as options, since a practitioner choosing between a simple wrong method and a complex right one will choose the simple one — this is the substantive difference and makes the interface the product; (2) effect sizes that are small and traffic that is finite, so the honest answer is frequently that a test is not worth running; (3) a commercial context where declaring no winner has a cost; (4) many simultaneous tests across a site, creating multiplicity nobody accounts for; and (5) platforms whose incentives favour reporting wins.

## Target Customer
Conversion optimisation firms and experimentation teams, testing platform vendors, product and marketing leadership, and statistical tooling providers.

## Impact If Solved
Statistics solved valid continuous monitoring and the methods are published and implemented. Delivering them as defaults to non-statisticians in a fast workflow is what the platforms have not done.

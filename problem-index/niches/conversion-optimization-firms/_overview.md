# Niche Analysis — Conversion Optimization Firms

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/conversion-optimization-firms|Conversion Optimization Firms]]

## Niche Selection

This discipline sells uplift and mostly reports it wrong, and unlike most professional services its validation data is not on the other side of a wall — it runs the experiments and holds the results. The eight niches below follow that: whether the reported numbers are real, whether the test capacity is spent on anything that could produce an effect, what the tests are built from, and who carries the gap between what the statistics say and what the monthly report needs.

## Why These Niches

Uplift validity and capacity allocation carry the money and the discipline's credibility: a year of reported wins that would have doubled the conversion rate, and a test programme spent on variations that could never have produced a detectable effect. Hypothesis generation and variant implementation are the two inputs that determine whether a test is worth running at all — an endless supply of observations with no way to tell which describe a mechanism, and variants injected by script into a page nobody controls. The two underserved audiences are the strategist who knows what day-four significance means and has a monthly report to deliver, and the developer writing JavaScript against a codebase they cannot see. The last two are mechanical: running the experiments, and keeping what was learned.

## Filter Notes

Seven of the eight are terminal — each names one contest that every serious competitor is fighting over, and decomposing further would produce features rather than markets.

**Reported uplift validity** is not. It names a property, and establishing it requires two things at different levels that fail independently. Experiment design and statistical practice asks whether any individual test was run in a way that supports its conclusion — powered for the effect it claims, with a fixed stopping rule, without post-hoc segmentation — which is methodology inside each experiment, fixable by a firm unilaterally, and improves every future result without saying anything about the past. Cumulative holdback validation asks whether the accumulated effect of everything implemented this year matches the sum of what was reported — a permanent randomised holdback at the programme level, run over months, which grades the whole practice including the tests that were run correctly. A firm can fix its statistics and still not know whether its claimed value is real, because good methodology produces a better estimate rather than a verified one; and a firm can run the holdback without fixing anything and learn only that the number is wrong. Each is separately worth buying and neither substitutes for the other.

Two adjacent candidates were rejected as belonging elsewhere: **designing the page being tested** belongs to [[industries/product-design-studios|Product Design Studios]], and **the acquisition spend bringing the traffic** sits with [[industries/performance-marketing-agencies|Performance Marketing Agencies]].

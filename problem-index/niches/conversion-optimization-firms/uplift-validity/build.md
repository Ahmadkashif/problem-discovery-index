# Reporting What Is Real

**Niche:** [[niches/conversion-optimization-firms/uplift-validity/profile|Reported Uplift Validity]]
**Industry:** [[industries/conversion-optimization-firms|Conversion Optimization Firms]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** The reported wins do not add up to the actual change and nobody has been asked to explain the difference.
**Tags:** #hypothesis-testing #confidence-intervals #evaluation-metrics #causal-inference #probability-distributions #descriptive-statistics #revenue-impact #bayesian-inference
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to report uplift that is real, in a discipline whose standard workflow inflates false positives and whose reported wins do not replicate — and whoever can demonstrate validity takes the account.

## The Problem
Tests are run on traffic that cannot detect the claimed effect, monitored continuously with a decision at the moment significance appears, stopped early when favourable, and segmented after the fact until something is significant. Each practice inflates the false positive rate and they are combined routinely. The reported wins are implemented, the uplift is assumed, and nobody returns to check whether the year's reported gains bear any relationship to the actual movement.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The client is satisfied with the reported number and the answer is likely to be uncomfortable. The testing platforms make the bad practices easy and the good ones harder. The discipline's case studies all report wins, so a firm reporting fewer looks worse. And nobody has been asked for validation.

## What to Build
Fix the methodology and validate the programme, and treat them as separate commitments. Establish a methodology that supports the conclusions — power analysis before launch, a fixed stopping rule, pre-registered segments, correction for multiple comparisons — which is the core and improves everything from this point forward. Hold back a randomised fraction from every implemented winner permanently, which is the only way to learn whether the accumulated effect is real. Compare the sum of reported uplifts against the actual change annually, which is the comparison the industry has never made standard. Report effects with intervals rather than as point uplifts, since a wide interval is the honest description of most of these tests. Report null results as results, because a discipline that only publishes wins is selecting on noise by construction. Retire the practices that inflate error, even where the platform encourages them. Publish the firm's own replication rate, which is the most credible thing any competitor in this market could say. Educate the client on what a result means, since the expectation of a monthly win is what drives the bad practice. Design tests for effects worth detecting rather than for whatever is testable. And be willing to report a year with few real wins, which is the commercial courage the whole niche turns on.

## Target Customer
Conversion optimisation firms and in-house experimentation teams, testing platform vendors, marketing and product leadership, and measurement providers.

## Impact If Built
The reported wins do not add up to the actual change and the comparison has never been standard. Methodology that supports the conclusion plus a permanent holdback is what turns a reported number into a demonstrated one.

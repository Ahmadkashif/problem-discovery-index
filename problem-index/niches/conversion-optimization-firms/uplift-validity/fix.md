# The Year That Should Have Doubled Conversion

**Niche:** [[niches/conversion-optimization-firms/uplift-validity/profile|Reported Uplift Validity]]
**Industry:** [[industries/conversion-optimization-firms|Conversion Optimization Firms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Add up the reported uplifts from the last twelve months and compare them to what the conversion rate actually did.
**Tags:** #quick-win #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #hypothesis-testing #revenue-impact #causal-inference #data-integration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to report uplift that is real, in a discipline whose standard workflow inflates false positives and whose reported wins do not replicate — and whoever can demonstrate validity takes the account.

## The Problem
The simplest diagnostic in this discipline has never been run as standard: take the reported uplift from every implemented winner over a year, compound them, and compare against the actual change in the conversion rate. The two are typically wildly apart. The comparison takes an afternoon, requires nothing but data the firm already has, and is not part of any standard reporting because everyone can guess how it comes out.

## Why It's Still Broken
The comparison is nobody's report — a calculation that would embarrass the reporting it checks is never added to the reporting, and no client has asked for it. Reported uplifts are per-test and the actual rate is a business metric held elsewhere. The gap has other explanations available. And the current numbers satisfy everyone.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Run the comparison and say what it shows, with the caveats. Compound the year's reported uplifts and compare against the actual movement, which is the fix and is an afternoon's arithmetic. State the legitimate reasons the two differ — traffic mix, seasonality, other changes — rather than either ignoring them or hiding behind them. Break it down by quarter, which usually shows the pattern more clearly than a year. Look at which tests were stopped early, since those will be where the inflation concentrates. Compare implemented winners' effects before and after implementation where the data allows. Share the finding internally first, which is where the practice change has to start. Use it to justify a holdback programme, which is the fix the comparison points at. Present it to clients as a credibility position rather than an admission, since the firm that raises it first owns the conversation. Repeat it annually as standard reporting. And adjust the expectation that every month produces a win, which is what drives the whole problem.

## Who Feels the Pain
Clients who believe they bought an uplift that did not arrive; strategists who know the numbers do not add up; the discipline's credibility, which erodes as buyers become more sophisticated; and every practitioner doing it properly and competing against inflated case studies.

## Impact If Fixed
A calculation that would embarrass the reporting it checks is never added to the reporting, and no client has asked for it. An afternoon compounding the year's uplifts against the actual change is the whole diagnostic.

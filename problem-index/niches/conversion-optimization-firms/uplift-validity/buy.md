## The Problem
Clinical research confronted every one of these problems and solved them: pre-registration fixing the hypothesis and the analysis before data is seen, formal interim analysis with spending functions for those who must look early, multiplicity correction, pre-specified subgroups, and mandatory reporting of null results. The reforms happened because the literature was full of findings that did not replicate. Online experimentation has the same failures, better data, and none of the discipline.

## What Already Exists
Pre-registration of hypothesis and analysis plan; group sequential designs with alpha spending for interim looks; multiplicity correction; pre-specified subgroup analysis; and mandatory null result reporting.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to fast, cheap, continuous experiments with commercial rather than scientific stakes. It requires: (1) tests run weekly at low cost, so the pre-registration and analysis apparatus must be near-zero-effort or it will be skipped — this is the substantive difference and is why importing trial practice wholesale has failed; (2) sequential designs as the default rather than the exception, since continuous monitoring is how the tools work and cannot simply be prohibited; (3) effect sizes far smaller relative to variance than clinical effects; (4) a commercial client expecting monthly wins rather than an ethics committee expecting rigour; and (5) no regulator, so adoption must be commercial.

## Target Customer
Conversion optimisation firms and in-house experimentation teams, testing platform vendors, marketing and product leadership, and statistical consultancies.

## Impact If Solved
Clinical research fixed early stopping, multiplicity and selective reporting because its literature would not replicate. Weekly cheap tests with a client expecting monthly wins is why the apparatus must be near-zero-effort to survive.

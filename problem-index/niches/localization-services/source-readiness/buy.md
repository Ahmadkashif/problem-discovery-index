# Input Validation From Manufacturing Quality

**Niche:** [[niches/localization-services/source-readiness/profile|Source Readiness]]
**Industry:** [[industries/localization-services|Localization Services]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Manufacturing learned to inspect incoming materials rather than discovering defects at assembly, and localization accepts whatever arrives.
**Tags:** #compliance #automation #evaluation-metrics #data-integration #workflow-orchestration #descriptive-statistics #revenue-impact #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to catch the defects that were created in the source content before they enter thirty language pipelines and multiply — and whoever checks the source takes the account.

## The Problem
Manufacturing established that a defect costs more the further downstream it is found, and responded with incoming inspection, supplier quality requirements and the authority to stop the line. A supplier delivering defective material is told, charged and required to improve. Localisation is a production process fed by a supplier — the content team — with no incoming inspection, no quality requirement and no authority to reject.

## What Already Exists
Incoming material inspection; supplier quality standards and scorecards; cost of poor quality attributed to source; authority to reject non-conforming input; and supplier development programmes.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to an internal supplier who is a colleague rather than a vendor. It requires: (1) a source team inside the same organisation with no commercial relationship, so rejection is a political act rather than a contractual one — this is the substantive difference and is why localisation has no standing; (2) defects that are linguistic and structural rather than physical, needing automated detection to be credible; (3) cost multiplied by the language count, which makes the internal case unusually strong once computed; (4) authors who are not trained in localisation and should not have to be, so the tooling must carry the knowledge; and (5) a content pipeline moving continuously rather than in batches.

## Target Customer
Enterprise content and product teams, language service providers, content platform vendors, and technical writing functions.

## Impact If Solved
Manufacturing made incoming inspection and supplier quality standard because downstream defects cost more. An internal supplier with no commercial relationship is why localisation has no authority, and why the cost attribution has to do the work.

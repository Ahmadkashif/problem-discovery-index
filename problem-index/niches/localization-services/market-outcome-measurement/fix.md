# A High Quality Score and No Conversions

**Niche:** [[niches/localization-services/market-outcome-measurement/profile|Market Outcome Measurement]]
**Industry:** [[industries/localization-services|Localization Services]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The localised page passed review with a strong score and performs a fraction as well as the source market's version.
**Tags:** #quick-win #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #confidence-intervals #causal-inference #revenue-impact #data-integration #word-embeddings
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to show that a translation worked in the market it was made for, when the industry measures the artefact and the outcome sits in the client's systems — and whoever measures it takes the account.

## The Problem
A client localises a set of pages, the translations pass linguistic review comfortably, and the localised pages convert far below the source market's rate. Nobody investigates whether the translation is a cause, because the quality process says the translation is fine and the performance sits with a different team. The gap may be terminology that does not match local search behaviour, a register that is wrong for the market, or nothing to do with language at all — and nobody finds out.

## Why It's Still Broken
The two numbers sit with different teams — a quality score held by localisation and a conversion rate held by marketing are never compared, so a translation problem and a market problem look identical. Nobody owns the joined question. The review process declares success. And investigating implicates the translation everyone signed off.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Put the performance number next to the quality score. Compare localised page performance against the source market as a routine part of the delivery review, which is the fix and is a chart rather than a project. Check the key terms against what people actually search for in that market, since search term mismatch is the commonest and most fixable cause. Ask a local speaker whether the register and terminology are right for the market rather than whether the translation is accurate, which is a different question and is rarely asked. Compare against locally-produced competitor content, which is the honest benchmark. Test one alternative wording where volume allows, as a single test settles more than a review cycle. Look at support volume in the locale, which frequently reveals comprehension problems. Share the performance data with the language team, who currently never see it and can often explain it. Record the finding so the pattern accumulates across locales. Separate translation causes from market causes explicitly rather than assuming either. And treat a large performance gap as a question worth asking rather than as a marketing problem.

## Who Feels the Pain
Clients whose localised markets underperform for unexamined reasons; linguists whose good work is indistinguishable from bad; agencies competing on price because they cannot demonstrate effect; and the markets themselves, served by content that does not land.

## Impact If Fixed
A quality score held by localisation and a conversion rate held by marketing are never compared, so a translation problem and a market problem look identical. Putting the two numbers side by side is a chart, not a project.

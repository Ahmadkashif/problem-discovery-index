# Market Outcome Measurement

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/localization-services|Localization Services]]
**Category:** 🔵 High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to show that a translation worked in the market it was made for, when the industry measures the artefact and the outcome sits in the client's systems — and whoever measures it takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$5.5B US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~20% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Very low — nothing returns
**Target Buyer:** Client and agency leadership
**Automation Potential:** High — outcome linking and comparison

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The industry runs an elaborate apparatus for scoring translations against error typologies and has no idea which translations actually worked in the market they were made for. A translation can score well and convert badly; one that reads awkwardly to a reviewer can outperform a fluent one because it uses the terms buyers actually search for. The internal quality framework describes the artefact; the client is buying a market outcome, and nothing connects the two.

## Current Tools & Gaps
An error typology, a review score and a delivery. The gaps: no access to in-market performance; no baseline before localisation; no comparison between translation variants; no link between linguistic quality scores and market results; and no outcome anywhere in the quality apparatus.

# Did the Translation Work

**Niche:** [[niches/localization-services/market-outcome-measurement/profile|Market Outcome Measurement]]
**Industry:** [[industries/localization-services|Localization Services]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** The quality apparatus describes the translation and the client is buying a result in a market.
**Tags:** #causal-inference #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #hypothesis-testing #data-integration #descriptive-statistics #revenue-impact #word-embeddings
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to show that a translation worked in the market it was made for, when the industry measures the artefact and the outcome sits in the client's systems — and whoever measures it takes the account.

## The Problem
Localisation quality is assessed by linguistic review against an error typology. Whether the localised page converted, whether the product was understood, whether the support article resolved the question, whether the terms match what buyers in that market actually search for — all of it is measurable in the client's systems and none of it returns. So the industry competes on internal scores and price, and cannot demonstrate that a better translation produces a better result.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The outcome data belongs to the client and the engagement ends at delivery. The industry's quality framework is a genuine asset that this would relativise. Comparing translation variants requires running them, which nobody does. And no buyer has demanded outcome evidence.

## What to Build
Link the translation to the market result and compare variants. Negotiate access to in-market performance for localised content as a standard engagement term, which is the core and is a commercial change most clients would accept if asked. Establish baselines before localisation so a comparison is possible, since without one the result is uninterpretable. Compare translation variants in market where volume allows, which is the only way to establish that one translation outperforms another. Measure the outcomes that match the content's purpose — conversion for a landing page, resolution for a support article, comprehension for documentation — rather than one metric for everything. Include search behaviour in the target market, as terminology that matches what people search for is frequently where the largest effect is and is invisible to linguistic review. Relate the outcome back to the linguistic quality score, which is the finding that would reshape the industry's quality apparatus. Accumulate across clients at the pattern level, which is where a language service provider's unique position lies. Report honestly when a high-scoring translation underperformed, since those cases are the informative ones. Offer outcome measurement as a paid service rather than absorbing it. And use the evidence commercially, because an agency that can show its translations perform is selling something nobody else is.

## Target Customer
Language service providers, enterprise localization and marketing leadership, translation technology vendors, and market research providers.

## Impact If Built
The industry scores the artefact and the client is buying a market result, with nothing connecting them. Outcome access plus variant comparison is what would let quality claims be made about performance rather than about fluency.

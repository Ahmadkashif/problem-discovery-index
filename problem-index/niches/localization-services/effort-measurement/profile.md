# Effort Measurement

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/localization-services|Localization Services]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to capture what post-editing a segment actually takes, from an environment that records everything and reports nothing — and whoever captures it takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$3.8B US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~14% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Very low — nothing captured
**Target Buyer:** Agency operations and technology leads
**Automation Potential:** Very high — instrumentation of the translation environment

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
This half is instrumentation. The translation environment already sees every keystroke, every pause, every revision, every segment opened and reopened. Capturing that as effort data is a tooling change, it requires nobody to agree to a rate, and it is valuable to an agency's own operations — for scoping, for scheduling, for engine selection and for knowing which content types are expensive — independently of whether anything is ever renegotiated.

## Current Tools & Gaps
A word count, a match percentage and a timesheet. The gaps: no per-segment timing; no edit distance or revision capture; no relationship between machine output and editing cost; no aggregation by content type or language pair; and no consent framework for measuring linguists' work.

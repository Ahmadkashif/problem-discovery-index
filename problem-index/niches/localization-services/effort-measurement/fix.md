# Word Count as the Only Measure

**Niche:** [[niches/localization-services/effort-measurement/profile|Effort Measurement]]
**Industry:** [[industries/localization-services|Localization Services]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Two jobs of the same word count took three hours and eleven hours, and the records show one number for both.
**Tags:** #quick-win #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #data-integration #confidence-intervals #revenue-impact #worker-facing #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to capture what post-editing a segment actually takes, from an environment that records everything and reports nothing — and whoever captures it takes the account.

## The Problem
Everything in the industry is counted in words. Scoping, pricing, scheduling, capacity planning and payment all use word count adjusted by match band. Two jobs with the same count can differ by a factor of three in the time they take, and nothing in any record distinguishes them. Agencies therefore scope badly, linguists are paid the same for very different work, and nobody can improve because the measure does not vary with the thing that varies.

## Why It's Still Broken
The measure is the unit of trade — a quantity used for pricing, scoping and payment throughout a supply chain will not be supplemented easily, even when everyone agrees it is a poor proxy. Time is not captured. Match bands describe similarity rather than difficulty. And an alternative measure would have to be agreed by many parties.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Record time alongside words, which requires no agreement with anybody. Capture actual time per job and per segment where the tooling allows, which is the fix and is available in most translation environments already. Compare time against word count to establish the spread, which is the finding that makes the case. Segment by content type, language pair and engine, since that is where the variation concentrates and is directly actionable. Use time for scoping and scheduling internally, which improves operations immediately with no external negotiation. Identify the content types whose ratio is consistently poor and price or route them differently. Give linguists their own time data for their own scoping. Track how the ratio moves as engines change, which is a useful signal about supplier quality. Keep word count as the trading unit while managing on time internally, which is the pragmatic path. Publish the spread without client detail, which advances the industry conversation. And treat a job whose ratio is far off as a scoping question rather than a linguist problem.

## Who Feels the Pain
Linguists paid the same for very different work; agencies scoping badly and discovering it late; clients receiving rushed work on underscoped jobs; and everyone planning capacity on a measure that does not track effort.

## Impact If Fixed
A quantity used for pricing, scoping and payment throughout a supply chain will not be supplemented easily, even when everyone agrees it is a poor proxy. Recording time alongside words needs nobody's agreement and improves operations immediately.

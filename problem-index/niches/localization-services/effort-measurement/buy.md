**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to capture what post-editing a segment actually takes, from an environment that records everything and reports nothing — and whoever captures it takes the account.

## The Problem
Translation process research has studied exactly this for decades using keystroke logging, pause analysis and eye tracking to establish where cognitive effort goes during translation and post-editing. The findings — that effort is highly variable, that fluent-but-wrong output is expensive, that pauses indicate problem-solving — are published and consistent. The industry that would be transformed by them prices on word counts, and the research has stayed academic.

## What Already Exists
Keystroke and pause logging methodologies; established effort indicators from process research; post-editing effort taxonomies distinguishing temporal, technical and cognitive effort; validated instruments; and a published literature on what the measures mean.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is from a research laboratory to production at scale with a freelance workforce. It requires: (1) measurement embedded in commercial production tooling rather than in a study environment, with consent, privacy and data ownership handled for people who are not research participants — this is the substantive difference and is the reason the transfer has not happened; (2) scale across millions of segments rather than controlled studies; (3) measures that must be robust to interruption, multitasking and uncontrolled conditions; (4) a use that is commercial rather than scientific, which changes the consent question entirely; and (5) an output that must be defensible in a rate negotiation.

## Target Customer
Language service providers, translation technology vendors, translator associations, and academic and standards bodies.

## Impact If Solved
Process research established what post-editing effort looks like and published consistent findings for decades. Embedding it in commercial tooling used by a freelance workforce, with consent handled properly, is what has never been done.

# Rate & Pricing Structure

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/localization-services|Localization Services]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to design a rate structure that survives a supply chain of parties with opposed interests, once the effort is known — and whoever designs one that holds takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$3.2B US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~12% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Very low — negotiated
**Target Buyer:** Commercial leadership and linguist associations
**Automation Potential:** Medium — pricing design and administration

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
This half is commercial design in a chain where each tier takes a margin and the parties' interests conflict. A rate that reflects measured effort must be agreed by an enterprise buyer, an agency, sometimes a subcontracted vendor and a freelance linguist, each of whom gains or loses from any change. Measurement is an input; the structure that survives the negotiation is a separate problem, and a measured effort figure with no workable pricing model attached changes nothing at all.

## Current Tools & Gaps
A discount percentage, a match band table and an annual rate negotiation. The gaps: no pricing model reflecting effort variation; no mechanism for passing a rate change along the chain; no transparency between tiers; no standard anyone can point to; and no route for linguists to negotiate collectively.

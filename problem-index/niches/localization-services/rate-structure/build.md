# A Rate That Reflects the Work

**Niche:** [[niches/localization-services/rate-structure/profile|Rate & Pricing Structure]]
**Industry:** [[industries/localization-services|Localization Services]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Knowing what the work takes changes nothing unless a rate structure can carry it through four tiers.
**Tags:** #revenue-impact #optimization-fundamentals #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #descriptive-statistics #compliance #worker-facing #data-integration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to design a rate structure that survives a supply chain of parties with opposed interests, once the effort is known — and whoever designs one that holds takes the account.

## The Problem
Suppose effort is measured. Translating that into what anyone is paid requires agreement across a chain: the enterprise buyer purchases from an agency, which may subcontract to a vendor, which contracts freelance linguists. Each tier prices independently and takes a margin. A rate structure that pays for measured effort must survive all of that, and a fairer rate at the bottom has to be funded from somewhere higher up. The measurement is the easy half.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Every party has an interest in the current structure or in a different change. There is no industry body with standing to propose a standard. Transparency between tiers is commercially unwelcome. And linguists, who would gain most, have the least collective leverage.

## What to Build
Design a structure that is administrable and defensible at every tier. Build a pricing model that varies with measured effort characteristics rather than applying a flat discount, which is the core and is what a measurement can actually be spent on. Make it administrable — computable at scoping, verifiable at invoicing, not requiring anyone to trust the other party's data. Allow the structure to pass through tiers so an agency can price to a client on the same basis it pays a linguist, which is what stops the change being absorbed as margin. Provide transparency at the segment level without exposing commercial rates between tiers, which is a design constraint rather than an obstacle. Publish a reference structure that any party can adopt unilaterally, since waiting for industry agreement means waiting indefinitely. Support the buyer's case — a rate that reflects effort produces better output on the hard segments, which is what they are buying. Give linguists a basis for negotiation rather than an assertion. Handle the transition, as a step change in rates is unfundable and a phased one is not. Model the effect on total cost honestly, because a structure that only raises cost will not be adopted. And measure whether output quality improves where the structure is used, which is the evidence that sustains it.

## Target Customer
Enterprise localization buyers, language service providers, translator associations and freelance linguists, and industry standards bodies.

## Impact If Built
A measured effort figure with no workable pricing model attached changes nothing across four tiers. An administrable structure that passes through the chain is what a measurement can actually be spent on.

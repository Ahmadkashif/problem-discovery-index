**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to design a rate structure that survives a supply chain of parties with opposed interests, once the effort is known — and whoever designs one that holds takes the account.

## The Problem
Industries paying by the piece with variable difficulty — garment manufacture, agricultural harvest, medical transcription, court reporting — developed rate structures that account for it: difficulty banding, minimum effective hourly floors, and in several jurisdictions statutory protections ensuring piece rates deliver at least a minimum equivalent. The designs exist because a flat piece rate on variable work transfers all the variance risk to the worker. Localization has the same structure and a flat discount.

## What Already Exists
Difficulty-banded piece rates; minimum effective hourly floors under piece work; time-based fallbacks for exceptional items; published rate schedules; and collective negotiation structures.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a globally distributed freelance workforce across jurisdictions. It requires: (1) linguists in many countries under many employment frameworks, so statutory floors cannot be the mechanism and the structure must be commercial — this is the substantive difference; (2) difficulty that is only knowable after the work begins, unlike a garment's complexity; (3) a multi-tier supply chain rather than an employer and a worker; (4) work that is remote and unsupervised, making time-based fallbacks harder to administer; and (5) no collective bargaining structure in most of the market.

## Target Customer
Enterprise buyers, language service providers, translator associations, and standards and industry bodies.

## Impact If Solved
Piece-rate industries built difficulty banding and effective hourly floors because a flat rate transfers all variance risk to the worker. A globally distributed freelance chain means the mechanism must be commercial rather than statutory.

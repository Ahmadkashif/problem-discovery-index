# The Discount Nobody Can Justify

**Niche:** [[niches/localization-services/rate-structure/profile|Rate & Pricing Structure]]
**Industry:** [[industries/localization-services|Localization Services]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Asked where the post-editing discount percentage came from, nobody at any tier of the chain can say.
**Tags:** #quick-win #revenue-impact #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #compliance #confidence-intervals #worker-facing #hypothesis-testing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to design a rate structure that survives a supply chain of parties with opposed interests, once the effort is known — and whoever designs one that holds takes the account.

## The Problem
The post-editing discount is a number that propagated. It appeared in early contracts, was matched by competitors, became a market expectation, and is now applied by parties who cannot say what it was derived from. It has not moved as machine translation quality has changed substantially in both directions across language pairs and content types. Everyone applies it, nobody defends it, and the negotiation each year is about a percentage point either way.

## Why It's Still Broken
The number is a convention rather than a calculation — a rate that everybody uses and nobody derived is defended by its ubiquity, and the annual negotiation is about moving it rather than about whether it means anything. Nobody has the evidence. Each party assumes the others know. And the convention is administratively convenient.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Write down where the number came from and check it against anything. Establish the discount's provenance, which is the fix and frequently ends the discussion once it is clear nobody knows. Compare it against whatever internal time data exists, even rough timesheets, which is usually enough to show it is wrong in at least some segments. Differentiate the discount by language pair and content type rather than applying one figure, since the underlying reality plainly differs and a single number cannot be right for all of it. Set it from evidence where evidence exists and label it as an assumption where it does not. Review it as engine quality changes rather than annually by habit. Give linguists visibility of what discount applies and why. Handle the exceptional segments separately rather than averaging them in. State the assumption in the contract so it can be revisited on evidence. Ask suppliers and linguists what they observe, which is data nobody collects. And accept that a differentiated structure is more administratively complex, which is the actual reason the flat number survives.

## Who Feels the Pain
Linguists whose income rests on an undocumented convention; agencies defending a number they cannot source; buyers who may be overpaying on easy content and underpaying on hard; and an annual negotiation conducted about nothing.

## Impact If Fixed
A rate that everybody uses and nobody derived is defended by its ubiquity, and the negotiation is about moving it rather than about whether it means anything. Establishing its provenance usually ends the discussion.

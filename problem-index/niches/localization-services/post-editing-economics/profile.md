# Post-Editing Economics

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/localization-services|Localization Services]]
**Category:** 🔵 High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting over a rate structure that assumes editing machine output is proportionally less work than translating, an assumption nobody has measured — and whoever measures it settles the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$7B US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~26% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Very low — assumed
**Target Buyer:** Commercial leadership
**Automation Potential:** High — instrumentation plus pricing design

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Machine output is paid at a fraction of translation rates on the assumption that editing is proportionally less work, and nobody has ever measured whether it is. Linguists widely dispute it, particularly on difficult segments where fixing a fluent-sounding but wrong machine output takes longer than translating from scratch. The rate is therefore a negotiated assumption governing the dominant production model of a global industry, and the data that would settle it is generated continuously and never captured.

### Contested sub-niches
This niche is not terminal. It names a commercial arrangement, and settling it needs two separable things:
- [[niches/localization-services/effort-measurement/profile|🎯 Effort Measurement]] — what post-editing actually takes
- [[niches/localization-services/rate-structure/profile|🎯 Rate & Pricing Structure]] — what that implies for pricing

## Current Tools & Gaps
A discount percentage applied to a translation rate, and a match-band table. The gaps: no measurement of actual effort; no per-segment effort variation; no relationship between machine output quality and editing cost; no rate structure reflecting either; and no evidence in a negotiation conducted entirely on assertion.

# Pricing the Work That Is Actually Done

**Niche:** [[niches/localization-services/post-editing-economics/profile|Post-Editing Economics]]
**Industry:** [[industries/localization-services|Localization Services]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** The dominant production model of a global industry is priced on an assumption nobody has tested.
**Tags:** #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #confidence-intervals #revenue-impact #hypothesis-testing #optimization-fundamentals #worker-facing #data-integration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting over a rate structure that assumes editing machine output is proportionally less work than translating, an assumption nobody has measured — and whoever measures it settles the account.

## The Problem
Post-editing replaced translation as the standard production model and the rates moved with it: a percentage discount, applied uniformly, on the assumption that editing takes proportionally less time. Linguists report that the relationship is not proportional and is highly variable — some segments are nearly free and some cost more than translating, because a fluent but wrong output must first be diagnosed. The dispute is conducted entirely on assertion by parties with opposed interests and no data.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Measuring effort per segment requires instrumenting the translation environment, which nobody has done systematically. The party with the tooling is the party benefiting from the current rate. Linguists have no collective mechanism to commission measurement. And the assumption is embedded in every contract in the industry.

## What to Build
Measure the effort and design the rate from it. Instrument the translation environment to capture actual effort per segment — time, edits, revisions, pauses — which is the core and is the evidence the entire dispute lacks. Relate effort to the machine output's characteristics, so a rate can vary with what was supplied rather than being flat. Show the distribution of effort rather than an average, since the variability is the whole point and an average conceals it. Identify the segment types where post-editing costs more than translation, which is the specific claim linguists make and which is testable. Design a rate structure that pays for the work done rather than for a category, which is the commercial deliverable. Report effort by language pair and content type, as the relationship differs substantially across both. Give linguists their own effort data, which changes the negotiation from assertion to evidence. Let agencies price to clients on the same basis, so the structure holds across the chain. Publish aggregate findings, since an industry-level number would settle a great deal. And treat the measurement as independent of the rate decision, because a measurement produced by an interested party will not be accepted.

## Target Customer
Language service providers, enterprise localization buyers, translator associations and freelance linguists, and translation technology vendors.

## Impact If Built
The dominant production model of a global industry is priced on an untested assumption, disputed by the people doing the work. Instrumented effort per segment is the evidence the negotiation has never had.

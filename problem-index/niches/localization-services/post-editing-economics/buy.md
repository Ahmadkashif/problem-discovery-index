# Work Measurement From Industrial Engineering

**Industry:** [[industries/localization-services|Localization Services]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Industrial engineering measures how long work actually takes before setting a rate, and localization set the rate first.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #hypothesis-testing #optimization-fundamentals #revenue-impact #worker-facing #probability-distributions
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting over a rate structure that assumes editing machine output is proportionally less work than translating, an assumption nobody has measured — and whoever measures it settles the account.

## The Problem
Industrial engineering established how to set a rate for work: measure how long the work actually takes across a representative sample, account for variation and difficulty, allow for fatigue and recovery, and derive a standard time that the rate is built on. The methods are a century old, taught, and used in every setting where piece rates apply. Localization moved to a piece-rate model for post-editing and set the rate by negotiation and analogy.

## What Already Exists
Time and motion measurement; standard time derivation with allowances; difficulty and variation adjustment; piece-rate design from measured standard times; and periodic re-measurement as methods change.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to cognitive work whose difficulty is invisible in the artefact. It requires: (1) effort that varies enormously by segment for reasons not apparent from its length or match percentage, so measurement must be per segment rather than per unit of output — this is the substantive difference and is why word counts have never captured it; (2) a difficulty driver that is the machine output's plausibility rather than the source's complexity, which inverts intuition; (3) a workforce that is largely freelance and distributed, making measurement a consent question; (4) rapidly changing machine output quality, so any standard time has a short life; and (5) a supply chain of several tiers, each taking a margin, so a rate change propagates.

## Target Customer
Language service providers, enterprise buyers, translator associations, and translation technology vendors.

## Impact If Solved
Industrial engineering measures the work before setting the rate, and the methods are a century old. Effort driven by the machine output's plausibility rather than the source's complexity is why word counts never captured it.

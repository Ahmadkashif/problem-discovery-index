# The Segment That Took Longer Than Translating

**Niche:** [[niches/localization-services/post-editing-economics/profile|Post-Editing Economics]]
**Industry:** [[industries/localization-services|Localization Services]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The machine output read perfectly and meant the opposite, and unpicking it took longer than starting from the source.
**Tags:** #quick-win #worker-facing #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #revenue-impact #hypothesis-testing #data-integration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting over a rate structure that assumes editing machine output is proportionally less work than translating, an assumption nobody has measured — and whoever measures it settles the account.

## The Problem
The hardest post-editing segments are the ones where the machine output is fluent and wrong. The linguist must notice that something plausible is incorrect, work out what the source actually meant, and rewrite — which is slower than translating the source directly, because the plausible wrong version has to be diagnosed and set aside first. These segments are paid at the post-editing discount like every other, and the linguist absorbs the difference.

## Why It's Still Broken
The rate is applied per word — a discount applied uniformly across segments cannot reflect that some cost more than full translation, and nothing identifies which ones do. Match bands describe similarity to memory, not editing difficulty. Linguists rarely refuse work. And nobody records which segments were hard.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Let linguists flag the hard segments and count them. Provide a way to flag a segment as harder than translating, recorded with the job, which is the fix and costs a keystroke. Count the flagged proportion by content type and language pair, which is the evidence nobody has assembled. Pay flagged segments at translation rate where the flag is upheld, which is a policy several buyers would accept if it were asked for. Sample-review flagged segments so the mechanism is not gamed, which is what makes it acceptable to the buyer. Report the flagged proportion back to whoever supplies the machine output, since it is a quality signal about the engine. Exclude content types that consistently flag high from post-editing entirely, which is the right answer for some material. Give linguists visibility of their own flagged rate. Feed the flags into engine selection and training where the client controls it. Publish the aggregate rate, which is a useful industry number. And treat a high flag rate on a job as a scoping error rather than a linguist problem.

## Who Feels the Pain
Linguists paid a discount for work that cost more than the full rate; agencies whose quality suffers when linguists rush; clients receiving worse output than they think they bought; and the profession's willingness to take post-editing work.

## Impact If Fixed
A discount applied uniformly across segments cannot reflect that some cost more than full translation, and nothing identifies which. A flag and a count is the smallest evidence-producing change available.

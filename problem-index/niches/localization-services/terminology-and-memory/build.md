# Assets Linguists Can Trust

**Niche:** [[niches/localization-services/terminology-and-memory/profile|Terminology & Memory Health]]
**Industry:** [[industries/localization-services|Localization Services]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** The client's most valuable localisation asset decays because maintaining it is nobody's job.
**Tags:** #data-integration #evaluation-metrics #word-embeddings #automation #descriptive-statistics #k-nearest-neighbors #confidence-intervals #compliance
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep the translation memory and glossary trustworthy, because once linguists stop trusting them they work around them and the assets decay unchecked — and whoever maintains them takes the account.

## The Problem
A translation memory accumulates every approved segment for years, including the ones that were wrong, the ones for a product that no longer exists, and the ones in a brand voice that has since changed. A glossary accumulates entries added by different people at different times, some contradicting others. Linguists learn which matches to distrust and start overwriting by default, at which point the asset is costing time rather than saving it and nobody knows.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Maintenance has no owner: the agency's engagement is per project, the client has no language technology function, and the tooling treats the memory as an append-only store. Removing a segment requires judgement. Nobody measures whether the assets are used. And the decay is gradual.

## What to Build
Score the assets, retire what is wrong, and measure whether they are used. Score every memory segment for reliability using its age, provenance, override history and consistency with current terminology, which is the core and turns an undifferentiated store into a ranked one. Detect contradictory and obsolete glossary entries automatically, since the contradictions are machine-findable and are why linguists stop consulting it. Track how often a proposed match is accepted, edited or discarded, which is the direct measure of whether the asset is working and is available in every environment. Retire or demote segments that are consistently overwritten, rather than leaving them to propose themselves forever. Flag entries tied to products, features or brand language that no longer exist. Attribute segments to their source so provenance can inform trust. Curate continuously rather than in occasional clean-up projects, which is the only cadence that keeps pace with decay. Give the client an owner and a report, since the asset is theirs and its condition is currently invisible to them. Report the assets' leverage — how much they actually save — which is what justifies maintaining them. And make curation part of delivery rather than a separate project nobody funds.

## Target Customer
Language service providers, enterprise localization teams, translation technology vendors, and terminology management providers.

## Impact If Built
The asset that should provide leverage becomes a source of error, and nobody notices because nothing measures whether matches are accepted. Reliability scoring plus acceptance tracking makes the decay visible and reversible.

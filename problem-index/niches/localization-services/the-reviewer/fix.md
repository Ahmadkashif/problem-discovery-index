# Preference Recorded as Error

**Niche:** [[niches/localization-services/the-reviewer/profile|The Reviewer]]
**Industry:** [[industries/localization-services|Localization Services]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The reviewer would have phrased it differently, logged it as a style error, and the linguist's score fell.
**Tags:** #worker-facing #quick-win #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #confidence-intervals #compliance #data-integration #hypothesis-testing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to make the reviewer's judgement count for something, when their scores are the industry's quality currency and nothing validates them — and whoever grounds those scores takes the account.

## The Problem
A large share of recorded errors are preferences. The reviewer would have used a different word, a different structure, a different register — none of which is wrong, all of which is logged against a style or fluency category and counted in the score. The linguist sees their score fall for choices that were defensible. Over time this drives translators toward whatever they think the reviewer wants rather than toward what works in the market, which is the opposite of the intent.

## Why It's Still Broken
The typology has no category for preference — an error taxonomy in which everything must be classified as a defect will classify preferences as defects, because there is nowhere else to put them. Reviewers are paid to find things. Nobody measures what proportion of logged errors are contested. And the linguist has no appeal.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Give preference somewhere to go and count how often it is used. Add a preference category that is recorded and does not count toward the score, which is the fix and is a form change. Require a rule or reference for anything logged as an error, since a defect with no authority behind it is a preference. Track the proportion of preference entries per reviewer, which is immediately diagnostic. Let linguists contest entries with a defined resolution route, which surfaces the disagreements rather than burying them in a score. Record contested entries and their outcomes, so the pattern is visible. Feed genuine preferences into the style guide rather than into the score, which is where they belong and where they help. Calibrate reviewers on a shared sample periodically, which shrinks the divergence. Report scores with and without preference entries, so the client sees both. Share the preference rate with reviewers privately, which most will act on. And be explicit in the process that a preference is not an error, which is a statement the current framework does not make.

## Who Feels the Pain
Linguists whose scores and livelihoods fall on defensible choices; reviewers with nowhere to record a legitimate observation; clients receiving quality scores partly composed of preferences; and the translations themselves, written to please a reviewer.

## Impact If Fixed
An error taxonomy in which everything must be classified as a defect will classify preferences as defects, because there is nowhere else to put them. A preference category that does not score is a form change with real consequences.

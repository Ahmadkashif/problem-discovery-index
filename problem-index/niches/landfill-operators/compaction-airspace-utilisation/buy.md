# Intelligent Compaction From Road Building

**Niche:** [[niches/landfill-operators/compaction-airspace-utilisation/profile|Compaction & Airspace Utilisation]]
**Industry:** [[industries/landfill-operators|Landfill Operators]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Road builders already use intelligent compaction — roller-mounted sensors and GNSS that map stiffness and pass coverage in real time; landfills compact a far less uniform material with no such feedback.
**Tags:** #gaussian-processes #feature-engineering #worker-facing #automation #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to pack more waste into each cubic yard of permitted airspace, and whoever achieves the highest measured in-place density between airspace surveys takes the account.

## The Problem
Compactor operators work by feel and managers learn the result from quarterly surveys.

## What Already Exists
Intelligent compaction systems from roller makers and machine-control vendors map pass counts, temperature and a stiffness measure on asphalt and soil, with targets set by specification and verified by spot tests.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs: a density proxy that works on heterogeneous waste, where stiffness readings are unreliable; calibration to survey-measured density rather than spot tests; deduction of daily cover; waste type from the scale ticket feeding the target; slope and rollover safety alerts; and a link between the machine's map and the landfill's airspace model.

## Target Customer
Landfill owners buying new compactors, and the dealers and makers selling them.

## Impact If Solved
If feedback lets average operators close half the gap to the best operator, ~100 lb per cubic yard on a 1,000-ton-a-day face (estimate), the site saves ~25,000 cubic yards of airspace a year.

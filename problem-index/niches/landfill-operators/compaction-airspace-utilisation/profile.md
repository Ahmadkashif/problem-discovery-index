# Compaction & Airspace Utilisation

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/landfill-operators|Landfill Operators]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to pack more waste into each cubic yard of permitted airspace, and whoever achieves the highest measured in-place density between airspace surveys takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.5B a year of compactors, dozers, operators, cover materials and airspace surveys (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~8% of operating spend (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium-low — drone surveys are spreading, but density is reported per site per quarter and machines rarely feed it
**Target Buyer:** Landfill manager or director of landfill engineering
**Automation Potential:** Medium — measurement and feedback automate; the operator stays in the cab

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Density is the operations half of airspace revenue: the difference between ~1,200 and ~1,800 pounds per cubic yard (search summary) is half as much waste again in the same permit. It is set on the working face by lift thickness, pass count, slope, waste mix and how much soil goes on as daily cover, and it is measured by comparing two surveys against scale tonnage, which trade guidance suggests doing quarterly above ~1,000 tons a day and once or twice a year below ~500 (search summary). Competitors are the compactor makers (Caterpillar, Tana, BOMAG, Al-Jon) and their machine-control partners (Trimble, Carlson), drone survey platforms and service firms (Propeller, Firmatek, DroneDeploy), alternative daily cover suppliers (tarp systems, spray-on covers such as Posi-Shell), and the consultants who write airspace utilisation studies. Each sells density; the winner is whoever can show the highest measured density between surveys.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Compactor telematics and GNSS can map passes; drone software computes volumes and a site-wide density with weighbridge data. Gaps: no density by area, lift or shift; cover soil not separated from waste in the volume; and operators never see results.

# Daily Cover That Eats the Airspace

**Niche:** [[niches/landfill-operators/compaction-airspace-utilisation/profile|Compaction & Airspace Utilisation]]
**Industry:** [[industries/landfill-operators|Landfill Operators]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Six inches of soil spread over the working face every night is required cover, but at many sites it is spread thicker than required, rarely stripped back, and silently consumes airspace that was sold by the ton.
**Tags:** #semantic-segmentation #cnns #descriptive-statistics #quick-win #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to pack more waste into each cubic yard of permitted airspace, and whoever achieves the highest measured in-place density between airspace surveys takes the account.

## The Problem
Federal rules require the active face to be covered at the end of each day, usually with six inches of earth or an approved alternative. Soil cover can take ~10–20% of airspace (estimate) when spread thick and left in place.

## Why It's Still Broken
Cover thickness is not measured; the volume change in a survey mixes waste and soil; and stripping cover before the next lift is extra work nobody is measured on.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Weekly drone surveys segmented into waste and cover, cover-soil haul counts, and a per-face cover ratio reported to the manager, with targets for alternative daily cover use and stripping.

## Who Feels the Pain
Landfill managers whose remaining-life forecasts slip; owners paying for airspace twice — once in the permit, once in soil.

## Impact If Fixed
Cutting soil cover from ~15% to ~10% of airspace (estimate) at a 1,000-ton-a-day site returns ~5% of capacity, roughly 20,000 cubic yards a year.

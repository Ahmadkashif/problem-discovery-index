**Industry:** [[industries/landfill-operators|Landfill Operators]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Sewer utilities already forecast storm inflows and control storage and pumping in real time to avoid overflows; a landfill's leachate system is the same storage-and-outlet problem with PFAS limits attached.

**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep leachate moving off the liner as treatment plants tighten PFAS acceptance, and whoever has the fewest days with leachate head above the 30 cm design limit takes the account.

## The Problem
Landfills manage leachate storage and outlets reactively, by phone, after rain.

## What Already Exists
Real-time control and hydraulic modelling software for combined sewers and stormwater (from vendors such as Xylem and Autodesk's InfoWorks line) forecasts inflow from rainfall, models storage and pumping, and schedules releases to avoid overflows.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs: percolation through waste and cover instead of runoff; head on the liner as the controlled variable; trucked outlets with receiving hours and volume caps alongside piped ones; each outlet's PFAS, ammonia and other limits checked against the landfill's own sample results; recirculation where permitted; and the monthly haul and discharge reports.

## Target Customer
Environmental managers at large landfills and multi-site owners facing outlet restrictions.

## Impact If Solved
Planning outlets against forecast inflow can move ~20% of hauling from emergency to scheduled rates (estimate), ~$100K a year at a site trucking ~10M gallons.

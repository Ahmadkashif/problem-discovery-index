# Leachate Management & PFAS

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/landfill-operators|Landfill Operators]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep leachate moving off the liner as treatment plants tighten PFAS acceptance, and whoever has the fewest days with leachate head above the 30 cm design limit takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.5B a year of leachate hauling, treatment, sewer charges and on-site systems (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~8% (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — sump levels read by hand or a basic SCADA screen, volumes tallied from haul tickets, outlet terms in emails and permits
**Target Buyer:** Environmental manager or director of environmental compliance at a landfill owner
**Automation Potential:** Medium — forecasting, outlet scheduling and reporting automate; pumps, trucks and treatment stay physical

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Federal rules require composite-lined landfills to have collection systems designed to keep less than 30 cm of leachate on the liner, so every gallon that percolates through the waste has to leave the cell. Volumes swing with rainfall, open area and cell age, and outlets are narrowing. Treatment plants are capping volumes, demanding PFAS pretreatment or refusing leachate (search summary), EPA has completed its study and plans to revise the landfill effluent guideline for PFAS, and Republic's 2025 landfill operating costs rose mainly on gas and leachate system maintenance. Competitors are leachate haulers, municipal plants selling capacity, on-site treatment and evaporation vendors (Heartland Water Technology, Veolia), reverse osmosis and PFAS destruction firms (Aclarity, Revive Environmental, 374Water), and engineering consultancies designing treatment. Whoever keeps head off the liner as outlets close takes the account.

## Current Tools & Gaps
SCADA and IoT level sensors at sumps and tanks; haulers' dispatch; treatment plants' pretreatment software; consultant studies of on-site treatment. Gaps: no forecast of volumes against outlet capacity, no continuous estimate of head on the liner, and haul tickets that never reconcile to meters.

# Upstream Production Surveillance for Gas Wellfields

**Niche:** [[niches/landfill-operators/landfill-gas-rng-projects/profile|Landfill Gas Collection & RNG Projects]]
**Industry:** [[industries/landfill-operators|Landfill Operators]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Oil and gas producers already run surveillance software that watches thousands of wells, flags under-performers and recommends interventions; a landfill wellfield is a few hundred low-pressure gas wells with stricter limits.
**Tags:** #gradient-boosting #time-series-forecasting #change-point-detection #automation #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to pull more of the methane a landfill generates into the collection system and the RNG plant, and whoever delivers the most methane per ton of waste in place takes the account.

## The Problem
Landfill wellfields are run from monthly readings and technician judgement.

## What Already Exists
Upstream production surveillance and artificial-lift optimisation platforms ingest well telemetry, detect deviations from expected rates, diagnose causes and queue interventions for field crews.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs: wells under vacuum rather than reservoir pressure; oxygen, balance gas and temperature as constraints, with the NSPS 5-day correction rules for positive pressure and temperature above 55 °C; liquids as a cause of loss; surface emission results as feedback; RNG plant inlet specifications; and decline modelled from waste age rather than reservoir depletion.

## Target Customer
RNG developers and consolidators running dozens of wellfields.

## Impact If Solved
If surveillance lifts field-wide methane recovery by ~3% (estimate) across a 20-site RNG portfolio averaging 2,000 scfm each, that is ~300,000 MMBtu a year, several million dollars of gas and RINs.

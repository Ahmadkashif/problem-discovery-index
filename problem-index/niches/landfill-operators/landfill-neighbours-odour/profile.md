# Landfill Neighbours & Odour Complaints

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/landfill-operators|Landfill Operators]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to stop landfill odour reaching the neighbours, and whoever has the fewest verified odour complaints per month takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$300M a year of odour monitoring, control products, community programmes and complaint response (estimate), alongside host-community fees of ~$1–5 a ton (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~2% (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — complaints go to the air agency by phone or web form; the landfill hears days later
**Target Buyer:** Landfill district manager or community relations manager; air compliance manager
**Automation Potential:** Medium — complaint intake, source attribution and response tracking automate

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The people most affected by a landfill are its neighbours, and no landfill tool is built around them. Odour comes from the working face, leachate handling, cap construction, gas leaks and, at worst, subsurface heating. Complaints drive enforcement and decide expansion hearings and host-community negotiations. Waste Connections' Chiquita Canyon Landfill received ~410 nuisance notices of violation from its air district after May 2023 and operates under an abatement order with 107 conditions. Competitors are odour monitoring networks (Envirosuite), misting and neutraliser suppliers (OMI Industries), cover products and tarps, gas collection contractors, and community engagement consultants. Whoever has the fewest verified complaints per month takes the account.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Fixed odour and hydrogen sulphide sensors and weather stations at some sites; agency complaint portals; manual odour patrols. Gaps: complaints arrive at the regulator before the landfill, are not tied to wind and site activity, and the source is guessed.

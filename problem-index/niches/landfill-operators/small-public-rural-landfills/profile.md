# Small Public & Rural Landfills

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/landfill-operators|Landfill Operators]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep a small public landfill open as long as possible, and whoever adds the most years of remaining permitted life takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~920 publicly owned active landfills (estimate; ~65% of EREF's 1,412) with ~$4B of disposal revenue (estimate); engineering, operations and equipment services sold to them ~$600M a year (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~20% of disposal revenue, ~65% of sites (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — a scale-house PC, one airspace survey a year from the consultant, budgets and cell plans in spreadsheets
**Target Buyer:** County solid waste director or public works director; regional solid waste authority manager
**Automation Potential:** Medium — remaining-life forecasting, reporting and density feedback automate; staffing and politics do not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Most landfills by count are run by counties, cities and regional authorities, many taking a few hundred tons a day or less, with a handful of staff and no engineer on payroll. Their central question is how long the landfill will last and what happens after: expand, build a new cell, or close and haul waste to a distant private landfill through a transfer station. The tools sold to landfills are built for consolidators' networks, and the consultants who serve counties work engagement by engagement. Competitors for the county's budget are engineering consultancies offering airspace studies and expansion permits, operations contractors and consolidators offering to run or buy the site, equipment dealers and trainers (SWANA's landfill operations courses) selling better compaction, and regional transfer and haul-out offers that end the landfill's life. Whoever adds the most years of permitted life takes the account.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Scale software from Paradigm or Carolina Software; consultants' annual reports; state annual tonnage reports. Gaps: remaining life known once a year, no density feedback, and capital plans not tied to airspace.

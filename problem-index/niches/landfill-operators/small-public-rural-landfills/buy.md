# Municipal Capital Planning With Airspace as the Asset

**Niche:** [[niches/landfill-operators/small-public-rural-landfills/profile|Small Public & Rural Landfills]]
**Industry:** [[industries/landfill-operators|Landfill Operators]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Cities already plan roads, pipes and buildings in capital planning and asset management software; a landfill's main asset — permitted airspace — is consumed every day and appears in none of it.
**Tags:** #time-series-forecasting #optimization-fundamentals #data-integration #automation #compliance
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep a small public landfill open as long as possible, and whoever adds the most years of remaining permitted life takes the account.

## The Problem
Counties plan landfill cell construction, closure reserves and equipment replacement in separate spreadsheets from their main capital plan.

## What Already Exists
Municipal capital planning and asset management platforms (OpenGov, Cartegraph and similar) track asset condition, project budgets and multi-year capital plans for public works.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs: airspace as a depleting asset updated from surveys and tonnage; cell construction timed to remaining capacity; closure and post-closure reserves and financial assurance under the local government test; enterprise-fund rate impacts; and state permit and expansion timelines.

## Target Customer
Public works and finance directors in counties and authorities that own landfills.

## Impact If Solved
Timing a ~$3M cell build to measured airspace rather than a fixed calendar can defer it by a year (estimate), saving ~$150K in financing at ~5%.

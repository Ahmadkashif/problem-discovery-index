# One Survey a Year and a Guess in Between

**Niche:** [[niches/landfill-operators/small-public-rural-landfills/profile|Small Public & Rural Landfills]]
**Industry:** [[industries/landfill-operators|Landfill Operators]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A small landfill learns how much airspace it used last year from an annual survey, so a surge in disaster debris or a drop in density goes unnoticed for months.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #semantic-segmentation #quick-win #automation #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep a small public landfill open as long as possible, and whoever adds the most years of remaining permitted life takes the account.

## The Problem
Trade guidance suggests sites under ~500 tons a day survey once or twice a year (search summary). Between surveys, staff extrapolate from tonnage and an assumed density.

## Why It's Still Broken
Ground surveys cost a consultant visit, and in-house staff have no drone programme or software.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A quarterly drone flight by staff or a shared county service, processed automatically into volume used, density and remaining capacity, with an alert when consumption runs ahead of plan.

## Who Feels the Pain
Solid waste directors defending budgets; commissioners surprised by an early closure date.

## Impact If Fixed
Catching a 10% density drop within a quarter instead of a year (estimate) at a 300-ton-a-day site saves ~10,000 cubic yards of airspace, about a month of the site's capacity, that would otherwise be lost before anyone acted.

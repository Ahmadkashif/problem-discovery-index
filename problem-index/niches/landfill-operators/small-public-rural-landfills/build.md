# A Remaining-Life Planner for County Landfills

**Niche:** [[niches/landfill-operators/small-public-rural-landfills/profile|Small Public & Rural Landfills]]
**Industry:** [[industries/landfill-operators|Landfill Operators]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A county commission deciding whether to expand, build a cell or close its landfill gets a single remaining-life number from a consultant once a year, with no way to test what changes it.
**Tags:** #time-series-forecasting #monte-carlo-methods #linear-regression #data-integration #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep a small public landfill open as long as possible, and whoever adds the most years of remaining permitted life takes the account.

## The Problem
Remaining life depends on tonnage, density, cover use and the next cell's timing, all of which the county can influence; but the forecast is a static figure in a report, and decisions on rates, out-of-county waste and capital wait for the next one.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Small public landfills are a fragmented, procurement-bound market with tiny software budgets, and consultants sell the study, not a tool.

## What to Build
A low-cost planner fed by scale exports and an annual or semiannual drone survey: tonnage forecast by source, measured density, cover ratio and cell plan, producing a remaining-life range and scenarios — higher density, diverting organics, refusing or accepting out-of-county tons, building the next cell early — with the cost per year of life for each. Board-ready charts for the commission.

## Target Customer
County solid waste and public works directors and regional authority managers.

## Impact If Built
For a county facing a ~$5M a year haul-out cost once its landfill closes (estimate), finding two extra years of life through density and diversion is worth ~$10M in avoided haul-out.

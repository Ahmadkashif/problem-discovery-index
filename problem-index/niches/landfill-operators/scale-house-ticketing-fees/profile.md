# Scale-House Ticketing, Tonnage & Fee Reporting

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/landfill-operators|Landfill Operators]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn every load across the scale into a ticket that bills, reports and pays fees correctly the first time, and whoever has the smallest share of tickets corrected after invoicing takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$400M a year of scale-house software, hardware, attendants' time and fee reporting (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~2% of costs, but every disposal dollar passes through it (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — tickets are electronic, but material codes, special-waste approvals, host fees and surcharges are applied by hand
**Target Buyer:** Controller or landfill business manager; scale-house supervisor
**Automation Potential:** High — identification, rate selection, fee calculation and reports are rule-bound

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The scale ticket is the landfill's revenue record and the source of every downstream report: host-community fees per ton, state disposal surcharges and tonnage reports, out-of-county rates, and the disposal-site records haulers use to reconcile their own franchise reporting (that hauler-side reconciliation belongs to [[niches/waste-haulers/franchise-tonnage-reporting/profile|⚡ Franchise Tonnage, Diversion & Compliance Reporting]]). Each ticket carries a customer, vehicle, gross and tare weight, material code and rate, and sometimes a special-waste approval. Errors become credit memos, fee under-payments found in audits, and arguments at the window. Competitors are scale software vendors (Paradigm's Compass, Carolina Software's WasteWORKS, AMCS, Soft-Pak), special-waste profile platforms such as Wastebits, plate and RFID hardware suppliers, and consolidators' in-house systems. Whoever gets the most tickets right the first time takes the account.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Scale software prints tickets and invoices and exports reports; RFID and plate readers exist at larger sites. Gaps: no check of the material code against the load or customer history, stale tare weights, and host-fee and surcharge calculations in spreadsheets.

# Airspace Sales & Disposal Revenue

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/landfill-operators|Landfill Operators]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is "Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to earn the most revenue from each cubic yard of permitted airspace," but revenue per cubic yard multiplies two contests with different winners: the price a landfill realises per ton against rival disposal sites, and the tons it packs into each cubic yard. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$18B a year of US landfill disposal revenue including public landfills (estimate; solid waste landfills took $12.76B of employer-firm receipts in 2022)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~85% (estimate; the rest is gas royalties and energy sales, other treatment and services)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — rates and tonnage live in scale software, but airspace is measured by survey a few times a year and the two are joined in spreadsheets
**Target Buyer:** VP of disposal operations or regional landfill manager; solid waste director at a public landfill
**Automation Potential:** Medium — pricing analytics and density measurement are automatable, the decisions on the face and in the contract are not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A landfill's inventory is permitted airspace, and every ton sold uses some of it for good. WM reported 5.1B tons of permitted capacity across its 253 solid waste landfills and took 129M depletable tons in 2025; Republic reported 5.0B in-place cubic yards across 207 active landfills, and third-party tipping fees were ~12% of its revenue. The 2024 EREF survey put the national average tipping fee at $62.28 a ton, up 10%, with private landfills ~34% higher than public ones. Revenue per cubic yard is the number that matters, and it is the product of two things that different people control: the commercial team sets price per ton against rival landfills and transfer stations, and the operations team sets tons per cubic yard on the working face.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Scale software (Paradigm, Carolina Software, AMCS) records tons and rates; drone survey platforms (Propeller, Firmatek, DroneDeploy) measure volume; engineering consultants produce the annual airspace report; the consolidators run internal depletion models for their accounts. Nobody joins the ticket to the cubic yards it consumed, so a landfill knows its revenue per ton by customer but not its revenue per cubic yard by customer or material.

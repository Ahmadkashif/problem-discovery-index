# Bulky Loads Priced by Weight, Paid for in Volume

**Niche:** [[niches/landfill-operators/airspace-disposal-revenue/profile|Airspace Sales & Disposal Revenue]]
**Industry:** [[industries/landfill-operators|Landfill Operators]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Light, bulky loads — mattresses, furniture, foam, shredded tyres — pay the per-ton gate rate while consuming several times the airspace of the waste the rate was set for.
**Tags:** #cnns #object-detection #descriptive-statistics #revenue-impact #quick-win
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is "Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to earn the most revenue from each cubic yard of permitted airspace," but revenue per cubic yard multiplies two contests with different winners: the price a landfill realises per ton against rival disposal sites, and the tons it packs into each cubic yard. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## The Problem
The gate rate is per ton because the scale weighs tons. A trailer of furniture or a roll-off of foam weighs little and compacts poorly, so it pays little and uses a lot. Some sites add bulky-item fees, but the attendant decides at a glance and most loads go through at the standard rate.

## Why It's Still Broken
Volume at the gate is not measured, material codes are coarse, and the airspace cost of a material is not known per site, so surcharges are set by habit rather than evidence.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Bed cameras that classify bulky and low-density loads and estimate their volume, a per-material airspace factor from the site's own surveys, and a rate rule that applies a volume-based or bulky surcharge automatically, with the photo on the ticket.

## Who Feels the Pain
Landfill managers watching airspace disappear faster than tonnage; finance teams; scale attendants arguing surcharges without evidence.

## Impact If Fixed
If 3% of a site's tons are bulky loads using ~3 times average airspace (estimate), they consume ~9% of the airspace for ~3% of revenue, and pricing them for volume adds ~6% to the site's disposal revenue or diverts them.

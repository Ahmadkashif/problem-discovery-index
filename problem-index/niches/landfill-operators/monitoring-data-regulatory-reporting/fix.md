# Surface Readings Retyped From Field Sheets

**Niche:** [[niches/landfill-operators/monitoring-data-regulatory-reporting/profile|Environmental Monitoring Data & Regulatory Reporting]]
**Industry:** [[industries/landfill-operators|Landfill Operators]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Quarterly surface emission readings are logged on an instrument or a sheet, retyped into a spreadsheet and copied into the report, and every transfer is a chance to lose an exceedance or its follow-up date.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #large-language-models #compliance #quick-win #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn a landfill's monitoring data into regulatory reports that survive agency review, and whoever has the fewest deficiencies found by the agency per report takes the account.

## The Problem
Technicians walk a 30-metre traverse with a methane analyser each quarter. Readings above 500 ppm start 10-day and one-month clocks; three in a quarter start a 120-day clock for a new well. Missed follow-ups are reportable deviations.

## Why It's Still Broken
Instruments export in vendor formats without GPS, follow-ups are tracked separately, and the consultant assembles everything at report time.

## What a Fix Looks Like
GPS-tagged readings captured on a tablet or drone, exceedances mapped automatically, follow-up tasks dated by rule and closed with the re-monitoring result, and the report table generated from the same record.

## Who Feels the Pain
Compliance managers, consultants and technicians who discover a missed re-monitoring at report time.

## Impact If Fixed
Eliminating even one missed follow-up deviation a year per site (estimate) avoids a reportable violation that can carry penalties of tens of thousands of dollars (estimate) and saves ~2 days of transcription per quarter.

# Mine Closure Cost Estimating for Landfills

**Niche:** [[niches/landfill-operators/closure-post-closure-assurance/profile|Closure, Post-Closure & Financial Assurance]]
**Industry:** [[industries/landfill-operators|Landfill Operators]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Mines already estimate reclamation bonds with standardised cost estimators and manage closure liabilities as portfolios; landfills price the same kind of obligation in one-off spreadsheets.
**Tags:** #monte-carlo-methods #linear-regression #data-integration #compliance #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to shrink the decades of closure and post-closure spending a landfill owes, and whoever wins regulator approval to reduce or end post-closure care at the most closed units takes the account.

## The Problem
Every landfill closure and post-closure estimate is rebuilt by hand each year.

## What Already Exists
Mining regulators and operators use standardised reclamation cost estimators and closure-liability tools that price earthwork, covers and long-term water treatment from site quantities and published unit costs, and roll them into bonding amounts.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs: the Subtitle D third-party cost basis and largest-open-area rule; 30-year post-closure care with gas, leachate and groundwater line items projected from site data; each state's form and inflation factor; the parallel GAAP estimate with own costs and discounting; and links from each estimate to the assurance instruments that must change with it.

## Target Customer
Corporate environmental engineering teams and the consultancies preparing estimates for public owners.

## Impact If Solved
Standardising estimates across 50 sites saves ~40 hours per estimate (estimate), ~2,000 hours a year, and makes every site's estimate auditable against the same unit costs.

# Disposal Contracts Escalating on CPI While Costs Climb Faster

**Niche:** [[niches/landfill-operators/tip-fee-disposal-pricing/profile|Tip Fee & Disposal Contract Pricing]]
**Industry:** [[industries/landfill-operators|Landfill Operators]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Long-term municipal disposal contracts rise with a consumer price index while leachate, gas-system and closure costs rise faster, and the gap is found only at renewal.
**Tags:** #linear-regression #descriptive-statistics #revenue-impact #compliance #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to raise the price a landfill realises per ton at the gate and in disposal contracts without losing tonnage to rival landfills and transfer stations, and whoever loses the fewest tons per point of price increase takes the account.

## The Problem
Cities and counties sign 5-to-20-year disposal agreements (estimate) with escalators tied to CPI. Republic's 2025 landfill operating costs rose mainly from gas and leachate system monitoring and maintenance, and PFAS pretreatment is coming; none of that is in CPI.

## Why It's Still Broken
Site-level cost growth is not tracked against each contract's escalator, and renewal pricing starts from the old rate plus a negotiation.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A contract register with each escalator, cap and renewal date, compared every quarter with the site's actual per-ton cost growth by category, and a renewal brief that shows the gap and proposes a cost-based index or pass-through clause for leachate treatment and regulatory changes.

## Who Feels the Pain
Disposal sales managers renewing contracts; site managers whose margins erode; public owners on the other side who face a large reset at renewal.

## Impact If Fixed
A 2-point annual gap between CPI and cost growth on a 200,000-ton contract at ~$50 a ton (estimate) is ~$200K a year by year one and compounds; catching it at each renewal keeps the contract priced at cost.

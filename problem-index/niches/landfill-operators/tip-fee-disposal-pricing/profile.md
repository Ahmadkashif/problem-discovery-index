# Tip Fee & Disposal Contract Pricing

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/landfill-operators|Landfill Operators]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to raise the price a landfill realises per ton at the gate and in disposal contracts without losing tonnage to rival landfills and transfer stations, and whoever loses the fewest tons per point of price increase takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$18B of disposal revenue priced each year (estimate); pricing analytics, rate studies and rate-table software ~$100M (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~85% of revenue passes through this decision (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium-low — posted gate rates and contract rates sit in scale software; competitive intelligence on rival rates is phone calls and hauler gossip
**Target Buyer:** VP of pricing or director of disposal sales at a landfill owner; solid waste director setting a public gate rate
**Automation Potential:** Medium — elasticity modelling and rate administration automate; contract negotiation does not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Disposal markets are local. A truck will drive only so far to save on tipping fees, so each landfill competes with the few landfills and transfer stations within haul distance, and integrated haulers send their own trucks to their own sites. EREF's 2024 survey of 351 landfills found the average fee up 10% to $62.28 a ton, large landfills above $70 and private landfills ~34% above public ones. Three kinds of tons are priced differently: gate-rate tons from third-party haulers and the public, long-term municipal disposal contracts with CPI escalators, and special-waste tons quoted job by job. The contest is pricing power against the next-best disposal option for each customer. Competitors include the consolidators' central pricing teams, B2B price-optimisation vendors (PROS, Pricefx, Zilliant), scale-software rate modules, and the rate consultants (Raftelis, Burns & McDonnell, HDR) who set public gate rates.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Rate tables are maintained by hand; consolidators model price increases centrally; public landfills commission rate studies every few years. Gaps: no model of each customer's alternative disposal cost, contract escalators not compared with actual cost growth, and tonnage loss after an increase noticed only in the monthly report.

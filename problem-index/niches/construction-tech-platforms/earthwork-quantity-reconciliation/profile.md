# Earthwork & Sitework — Quantity Reconciliation

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/construction-tech-platforms|Construction Tech Platforms]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in earthwork software is fighting to reconcile the material actually moved against the material paid for, across design surfaces, machine control, survey and truck counts — and whoever makes those four numbers agree takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$400M US software spend across earthwork, excavation, grading and heavy civil sitework contractors
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~4% of construction software revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — GPS machine control is near-universal on larger equipment, and the office systems around it are not
**Target Buyer:** Owners and operations managers at earthwork contractors; project engineers at heavy civil firms
**Automation Potential:** Very High — every source of quantity data is already digital and none of them are compared

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Earthwork is paid by quantity, and quantity is the only thing that matters. A contractor bids to move a volume of material computed from design surfaces, is paid on quantities measured by the owner's engineer, and incurs cost according to how much material its machines and trucks actually handled. Those three numbers are computed by three different parties with three different methods and they never agree exactly. The gap between them is the contractor's margin and the industry's most common source of dispute. Unlike every other trade, the evidence to settle it is abundant and digital: machine control systems record every blade position, GPS on haul trucks records every load, drone survey produces a measured surface weekly, and the design surface is a file. Four independent measurements of the same physical fact, held by a contractor who reconciles them by having an engineer redo a takeoff in a spreadsheet.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Trimble, Topcon and Leica dominate machine control and site positioning, and their office software handles surface modelling and volume calculation competently. Drone survey has become routine through DroneDeploy, Propeller and Pix4D. Haul truck tracking exists through telematics and through purpose-built products. Estimating and takeoff is served by HCSS, B2W and Trimble's own tools. Every individual measurement is well instrumented. What does not exist is the reconciliation: a standing comparison of design quantity, machine-recorded production, surveyed progress and hauled volume, with the discrepancies located in space rather than totalled. So a contractor who has moved more material than the design called for — because the subgrade was worse than shown, which is the single most common cause of an earthwork claim — has the evidence distributed across four systems and assembles it manually, after the fact, under time pressure, if at all.

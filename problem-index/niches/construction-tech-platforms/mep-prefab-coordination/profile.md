# Mechanical & Electrical — Model-to-Fabrication Chain

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/construction-tech-platforms|Construction Tech Platforms]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in MEP contractor software is fighting to carry a coordinated model through to a fabricated, delivered, installed and tracked assembly without anyone redrawing it — and whoever closes that chain with the fewest re-entries takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$700M US software spend across mechanical, electrical and plumbing contractors
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~8% of construction software revenue, concentrated in the larger self-performing contractors
**Digital Adoption:** Medium-High — MEP contractors are the most BIM-literate trades and run VDC departments
**Target Buyer:** VDC managers, prefabrication managers and operations directors at mechanical and electrical contractors
**Automation Potential:** Very High — the chain is a data pipeline that is currently broken by file formats and re-entry

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
MEP contractors are the trades whose economics are transformed by prefabrication. Building a rack of pipe, duct and conduit in a shop rather than overhead on a ladder is faster, safer, and cheaper by a wide margin, and the largest contractors have invested heavily in it. The whole benefit depends on a chain: the coordinated model must yield shop drawings, which must yield fabrication tickets, which must yield labelled assemblies, which must arrive at the right area on the right day, be installed, and have their installation recorded back against the model. Every link in that chain is a place the data is currently re-entered by a person. What makes this sub-niche terminal is that the chain is countable: a contractor can measure how many times a given assembly's identity is retyped between model and installed status, and the number today is typically three or four.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Autodesk Revit and Fabrication CADmep, Trimble SysQue and Stabicad, and the Victaulic and manufacturer-specific tools dominate model authoring; Navisworks and BIM Track handle clash coordination well. Shop floor systems — fabrication management, spool tracking, nesting and cutting — are a separate software category with its own vendors. Field installation status lives in the GC's platform or in a spreadsheet. The gaps are at the joins: a coordinated model exports to a shop system through a file that loses the linkage back to the model element, so an assembly's identity is recreated; delivery and site logistics are managed by phone; and installed status is recorded as a percentage in a pay application rather than against the assembly, which means the model — the most complete record of the work — is never updated to reflect what is actually in the building.

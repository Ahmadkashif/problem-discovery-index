# Specialty Trade Contractor Platforms

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/construction-tech-platforms|Construction Tech Platforms]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is that every competitor is fighting to make a trade contractor's own work measurable on its own terms rather than through the general contractor's schedule — but what "measurable" means differs completely by trade. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.8B US software spend across mechanical, electrical, concrete, earthwork, steel and other specialty trade contractors
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~20% of construction software revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Medium-High — the larger specialty contractors are sophisticated buyers; the mid-market runs on the GC's platform plus spreadsheets
**Target Buyer:** Operations directors and VDC managers at specialty contractors; founders of trade-vertical platforms
**Automation Potential:** High but trade-shaped — the automatable work is the measurement chain, and its physical substrate differs entirely by trade

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Specialty trade contractors are where most of the industry's labour and most of its risk actually sit, and they experience the general contractor's platform as somebody else's system. The GC platform tracks the project; the trade contractor needs to track its own production — how much of its work is installed, at what labour cost, against what it bid. That gap has produced a real market of trade-vertical products. What makes the segment analytically awkward is the same thing that made specialty EHR awkward: the members look alike from outside and contest on completely different things. A mechanical contractor's leverage is in prefabrication and the chain from coordinated model to installed spool. An earthwork contractor's is in reconciling the volume of material it moved against the volume it is paid for. Both are "production tracking" at the level of a category label and neither product would serve the other's customer.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Trimble, Autodesk and Procore all sell trade-facing modules; specialist vendors serve individual trades well (mechanical fabrication, electrical prefab, concrete placement). Estimating and takeoff is a separate fragmented category and connects poorly to production. The consistent gap across every trade is the same and appears in a different physical form in each: the measurement that determines whether the job is making money — installed quantity against budgeted hours — is assembled weekly by a project manager from a foreman's estimate, a timecard export and a spreadsheet, days after the work happened and at a granularity too coarse to act on. Which quantity, and where it should come from, is exactly what separates the sub-niches.

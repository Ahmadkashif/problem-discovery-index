# Craft Workforce Platforms

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/construction-tech-platforms|Construction Tech Platforms]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in craft workforce software is fighting to give a worker a portable, verified record of their own hours, certifications, skills and safety history that follows them between employers — and whoever makes that record trusted by employers takes the market.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$400M US software spend across construction workforce management, training funds, apprenticeship programmes and skilled trade labour platforms
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~4% of construction software revenue, and the lowest per-worker spend of any segment in the industry
**Digital Adoption:** Low — the worker-facing layer barely exists; what exists is employer-facing workforce management
**Target Buyer:** Ambiguous by design — the beneficiary is the worker, the payer is usually an employer, a union training fund, or a workforce board
**Automation Potential:** High — credential verification, hours tracking and skills evidencing are record-keeping problems being solved with paper cards and phone calls

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Construction employs millions of skilled workers whose careers span many employers, and none of them owns a record of their own work. Hours toward an apprenticeship sit with the training fund. Certifications — OSHA cards, welding qualifications, equipment operator credentials, site-specific safety orientations — exist as physical cards, PDFs and entries in employer systems, and every new employer re-verifies them, sometimes re-training the worker in a course they completed last month. Skills, which are the worker's actual asset, are evidenced by whoever remembers them. The consequences fall entirely on the worker: slower mobility between employers, lost credit toward advancement, repeated unpaid orientation, and no way to demonstrate capability to a new employer except by being vouched for. The industry treats this as a workforce management problem for employers, which is why the product category points away from the person it should serve.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Workforce management platforms — Arcoro, Bridgit Bench, LaborChart, Rhumbix and the labour modules of the major platforms — are built for the contractor: who is deployed where, at what cost, with what certifications on file. Union training funds run their own apprenticeship tracking systems. Credentialing bodies issue cards. Safety orientation is tracked per site or per contractor, and the same worker is oriented repeatedly. The consistent gap is ownership and portability: every record about the worker is held by an institution the worker leaves, in a form the next institution does not accept, with no mechanism for the worker to carry it. Verifiable digital credential standards exist and are almost entirely unused in this industry.

# Daily Report & Field Capture

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/construction-tech-platforms|Construction Tech Platforms]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in field capture is fighting to assemble the daily report from the day's own photos, messages, timecards and deliveries so the superintendent confirms rather than types — and whoever gets the accepted-unedited share highest takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$500M attributable to daily reporting and field capture within construction platforms
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~6% of construction software revenue, and the feature with the widest daily usage in the industry
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — daily reports are submitted on platform nearly everywhere and are typed by a person nearly everywhere
**Target Buyer:** Product leads at every field platform; the actual user is a superintendent at the end of a twelve-hour day
**Automation Potential:** Very High — every fact in a daily report already exists somewhere in the platform by the time the report is written

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The daily report is the most universally resented ritual in construction. A superintendent who has been on site since six spends the last forty-five minutes of the day retyping what happened: which crews were present and how many, what work was performed in which areas, weather, deliveries, equipment, visitors, safety observations, and any issues. Nearly every one of those facts is already in the system by the time he starts — timecards were submitted, photos were taken with timestamps and locations, deliveries were logged, the weather is a public record, messages were exchanged about the issues. The report is an act of re-entry performed by the most expensive person on site at the hour he has least left. It is also genuinely important, because it is the contemporaneous record every claim and dispute eventually relies on, which is why nobody is willing to let it lapse.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Every platform has a daily report module — Procore, Autodesk, Raken, Buildertrend, Fieldwire — and Raken in particular has built its business on making the form faster. Voice entry exists in several. Photo management is good. The universal gap is that the form is the model: the products optimise the typing rather than eliminating it, and none of them assembles a draft from what the platform already holds and asks for confirmation. The second gap is that the report's downstream value is unrealised — crew counts, work locations and issues are captured in prose and never used as the structured signal they could be for forecasting, productivity or claim support.

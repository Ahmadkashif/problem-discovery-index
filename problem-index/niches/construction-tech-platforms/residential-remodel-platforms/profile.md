# Residential Remodel & Custom Home

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/construction-tech-platforms|Construction Tech Platforms]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in residential remodelling software is fighting to keep selections, allowances and change orders reconciled to the contract in real time so the homeowner conversation is about a number both sides can see — and whoever keeps that reconciliation current takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$750M US software spend across remodelling contractors and custom home builders
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~8% of construction software revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Low-Medium — the larger remodellers have adopted; the typical three-to-fifteen person firm runs on spreadsheets and email
**Target Buyer:** Remodelling company owners and production managers; product leads at Buildertrend, CoConstruct and their competitors
**Automation Potential:** High — selections tracking, allowance reconciliation and change order generation are bookkeeping tasks currently performed manually under emotional conditions

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Residential remodelling differs from every other construction segment in who the client is. The client lives in the building, has never done this before, is spending their own money rather than an institution's, and is present. That changes the nature of the software problem: the central failure is not schedule or productivity but the conversation about money. A remodel contract includes allowances — budgeted amounts for items the homeowner has not yet chosen — and selections made weeks later against those allowances, plus changes requested midway. Each of those adjusts the contract price. When the reconciliation is not current, the homeowner discovers at the end that the project costs more than they thought, having made each individual decision without seeing its cumulative effect. That moment is the source of most disputes, most bad reviews and most unpaid final invoices in the segment, and it is a bookkeeping failure rather than a construction one.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Buildertrend and CoConstruct (now merged) dominate, with Houzz Pro, JobTread and a fragmented tail below them. All of them have selections and change order modules, and all of them depend on somebody keeping those modules current — which in a firm with no office staff means the owner, in the evening, after the homeowner has already been told a number verbally on site. The gaps are specific: selections are tracked as a list rather than as running arithmetic against the contract; verbal approvals on site, which is how most changes actually happen, have no capture path and become change orders days later or never; and the homeowner's view, where one exists, shows documents rather than the one number they care about. Nothing in the category treats the running contract total as the product.

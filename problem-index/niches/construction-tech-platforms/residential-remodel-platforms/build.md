# The Running Contract Total Both Sides Can See

**Niche:** [[niches/construction-tech-platforms/residential-remodel-platforms/profile|Residential Remodel & Custom Home]]
**Industry:** [[industries/construction-tech-platforms|Construction Tech Platforms]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Every remodelling dispute is the same dispute — the homeowner did not know what the running total was — and every platform in the segment tracks selections and changes as documents rather than as arithmetic against a contract.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #workflow-orchestration #automation #compliance #revenue-impact #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in residential remodelling software is fighting to keep selections, allowances and change orders reconciled to the contract in real time so the homeowner conversation is about a number both sides can see — and whoever keeps that reconciliation current takes the account.

## The Problem
A kitchen remodel is contracted at $94,000 with a $9,000 tile allowance and a $12,000 appliance allowance. Over six weeks the homeowner selects tile at $13,400, appliances at $15,900, adds a pantry cabinet, upgrades the lighting, and agrees to relocate a wall after the framing revealed a problem. Each decision was reasonable and each was discussed. At the end the invoice is $118,000 and the homeowner is shocked, because at no point did anyone show them a running figure — and the contractor is equally uncomfortable, because he knows the conversation is about to be adversarial and he has no document that walks through it.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Keeping the total current requires the contractor to record a price effect at the moment of a decision, which is usually a conversation in a driveway or over a text message, and the tooling has always asked him to do that later at a desk. So the platform's numbers lag reality by days, and a number that is wrong is worse than no number — a homeowner shown $103,000 when the real figure is $110,000 will hold the contractor to it. Vendors have therefore kept the arithmetic advisory and the documents authoritative, which is the opposite of what the segment needs. There is also a sales reason: showing the homeowner a rising total in real time is a feature contractors are ambivalent about, and vendors have followed the buyer's ambivalence.

## What to Build
Make the contract a live ledger and the homeowner a first-class viewer of it. Every allowance has a budgeted amount, a selected amount, and a variance, computed as selections are made. Every change has a price and an approval state. The running total — contract, plus approved changes, plus allowance variances, plus pending items shown separately — is a single figure both parties see in the same place, updated when the decision is made rather than when the paperwork is done. Pending items matter as much as approved ones: a homeowner who can see that three decisions are outstanding with an estimated range is a homeowner who is not surprised. The contractor's obligation reduces to entering a price at the moment of agreement, which is the one thing the product must make almost free, and which is where speech and messaging capture belong.

## Target Customer
Remodelling contractors and custom home builders of three to fifty people, and the residential platform vendors whose selections modules are currently a list rather than a ledger.

## Impact If Built
The end-of-project dispute is the segment's largest destroyer of margin, referrals and reviews, and a shared running total removes the surprise that causes it. Contractors who adopt real-time reconciliation typically collect final invoices faster and discount less, and the reputational effect compounds in a business where referral is the dominant acquisition channel.

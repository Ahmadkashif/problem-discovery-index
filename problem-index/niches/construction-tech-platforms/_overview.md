## Why These Niches

The two large blocks split by who is holding the risk. A general contractor's platform is bought to manage a schedule and a budget it is contractually on the hook for, which makes forecasting the contested capability — and the vendors hold hundreds of thousands of projects' worth of the evidence and render it as a Gantt chart. Specialty trade platforms are bought by contractors whose risk is their own labour and material, and that block **failed the filter as a single niche**: a mechanical contractor and an earthwork contractor are not fighting over the same thing, so it is decomposed below.

Small subcontractors and residential remodellers are the two underdigitised populations, and both are underdigitised because the existing products were designed for someone with an office. A five-person framing crew and a remodeller doing four kitchens at once both fail at the same point — the product assumes somebody will sit down and enter things.

The two underserved buyers are the ones the category's revenue model does not point at. Owners see their project through the contractor's tool and get the contractor's account of it. Craft workers are the largest population in the industry and are the only party whose own record — hours, certifications, safety, skills — is scattered across every employer they have ever had and owned by none of them.

The automation niches are the industry's two most reliably resented tasks: reading a specification to route a submittal, and retyping a day that everyone on site already lived through.

## Filter Notes

**Niche 2 failed the filter as stated and was decomposed.** "Specialty trade contractor platforms" is a segment defined by who the buyer is not — not a general contractor — rather than by a shared contest. A mechanical or electrical contractor's competitive question is whether the coordinated model reaches the fabrication shop and the field as an installable, tracked package without anyone redrawing it; that is a data-chain problem. An earthwork contractor's is whether the dirt it actually moved reconciles against the dirt it gets paid for, across design surfaces, machine control, drone survey and truck counts; that is a measurement-reconciliation problem. Different evidence, different competitors, different products. Both sub-niches are terminal.

**Niches 1, 3–8 are terminal as stated.** Each names a capability testable in a pilot: forecast lead time on a slip, time from site to recorded fact without typing, contract-to-selections reconciliation, independent cost-to-complete, portability of a worker's own record, routing accuracy against the specification, and the share of a generated daily report a superintendent accepts unedited.

# Owner & Developer-Side Project Controls

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/construction-tech-platforms|Construction Tech Platforms]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor selling to owners is fighting to produce a cost-to-complete and a completion date that do not originate with the contractor being measured — and whoever makes the owner's number independent takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$850M US software spend across owners, developers, owner's representatives and institutional real estate
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~9% of construction software revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — large institutional owners run programme management systems; the mid-market developer sees the project through the contractor's platform
**Target Buyer:** Developers, owner's representatives, corporate real estate and facilities capital teams, institutional investors
**Automation Potential:** High — the owner's core questions are forecasts computable from data the owner is already given

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The owner pays for everything and sees the project through a lens ground by the party it is paying. The general contractor's platform produces the schedule, the progress reporting and the cost forecast, and the owner receives a version of each. That is not an accusation of bad faith — a contractor's forecast is genuinely its best view — but it is structurally a self-report, produced by a party with commercial exposure to what it says, at monthly granularity, in a format designed to support a payment application. The owner's actual questions are different from the contractor's: when will I be able to occupy this, what will it finally cost, how much of my contingency is left in reality rather than on paper, and how does this project compare to the last four I built. Nobody's product answers those from the owner's side.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Oracle's Aconex and Primavera Unifier, e-Builder, Kahua and Procore's owner offering serve the institutional end with document control, contract management and capital programme reporting. The mid-market developer typically uses spreadsheets plus guest access to the contractor's platform. Programme-level reporting exists and is descriptive: committed, spent, remaining. What is absent everywhere is independence. Cost-to-complete is the contractor's estimate reformatted. Schedule is the contractor's schedule. Contingency drawdown is reported as a balance rather than forecast against remaining risk. And no owner platform benchmarks a project against the owner's own portfolio history, even though institutional owners build the same building types repeatedly and hold the outcomes.

# Portfolio Benchmarking From the Owner's Own History

**Niche:** [[niches/construction-tech-platforms/owner-side-project-controls/profile|Owner & Developer-Side Project Controls]]
**Industry:** [[industries/construction-tech-platforms|Construction Tech Platforms]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Cost benchmarking data is a purchasable product from RSMeans and the cost consultancies, and it describes the market rather than the owner, while the owner's own twenty completed projects sit in closed files nobody has ever compared.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #hypothesis-testing #linear-regression #confidence-intervals #evaluation-metrics #dimensionality-reduction #data-integration #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor selling to owners is fighting to produce a cost-to-complete and a completion date that do not originate with the contractor being measured — and whoever makes the owner's number independent takes the account.

## The Problem
An owner setting a budget for its next clinic asks what a clinic costs. The answer comes from a cost consultant using published benchmarks adjusted for location and escalation, which describes what clinics cost generally. The owner has built eleven clinics in the last six years, with known final costs, known change histories, known schedule performance and known contractors. That is a far better predictor of what its twelfth clinic will cost than any market benchmark, and it has never been assembled, because each project closed into its own folder.

## What Already Exists
RSMeans, Gordian, Compass International and the major cost consultancies sell structured benchmark data with established classification systems — Uniformat and Masterformat provide the taxonomy, and it is widely used. Programme management platforms hold the owner's own project records. Cost estimating software handles the arithmetic. The market data is purchasable and the owner's data is already owned; nothing needs inventing.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to build the owner's portfolio into a comparable dataset and use the purchased benchmarks as a prior rather than as the answer. It requires: (1) normalising historical projects to a common classification and a common basis — escalated to current dollars, adjusted for scope differences, with the adjustments recorded rather than assumed — which is the unglamorous majority of the work; (2) separating the parts of cost that are genuinely building-type-driven from the parts that are market-driven, since only the first transfers across the owner's portfolio and only the second is what the published benchmarks are good at; (3) blending the owner's thin, highly relevant sample with the market's thick, less relevant one explicitly, with the weighting visible, rather than choosing one; (4) benchmarking outcomes rather than budgets — final cost against original budget, final date against original date, change volume by phase — because the interesting question is not what a clinic costs but how reliably this owner's budgets have held; and (5) tracking by contractor and delivery method, which is where an owner's own data says things no market benchmark can.

## Target Customer
Institutional owners with recurring capital programmes, developers with a repeat product type, and the owner's representative and cost consultancy firms who would rather sell analysis of the client's portfolio than a market average.

## Impact If Solved
An owner that knows its own historical budget reliability sets contingency from evidence instead of convention, which is typically the single largest correction available in a capital programme. Comparing outcomes by contractor and delivery method also answers procurement questions that are currently settled by relationship and impression.

# Submittal & RFI Routing Content

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/construction-tech-platforms|Construction Tech Platforms]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in document workflow is fighting to read the specification and decide who a submittal or RFI should go to, in what sequence, and by when — and whoever routes most accurately against the spec takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$320M attributable to submittal and RFI workflow within construction platforms, plus the project engineering labour it consumes
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~4% of construction software revenue and a far larger share of project engineering hours
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — the workflow engines are universally deployed and the routing decision inside them is manual
**Target Buyer:** Product leads at Procore, Autodesk and the document-workflow vendors; project executives who staff project engineers
**Automation Potential:** Very High — the routing rules are written down in a document the platform already stores

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Every commercial project generates hundreds of submittals and RFIs, and every one of them has to be routed: to which reviewers, in what order, with what turnaround, against which specification section. The answer is in the project manual — a document of several hundred to several thousand pages that the platform holds as a PDF. A project engineer reads the relevant section, builds a submittal register at the start of the job, and routes each item as it arrives. The register-building alone consumes weeks of a project engineer's time per project, is redone from scratch on every project, and is the work that engineers universally describe as the least valuable part of the role. The workflow engines that carry the items are excellent and entirely indifferent to the content of the document that determines where they should go.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Procore, Autodesk Construction Cloud, Newforma and the document control platforms all provide submittal and RFI workflows with configurable approval chains, due dates and reminders, and they do this well. Specification management products exist for the design side. Submittal register creation is offered as a service by some vendors and by third-party firms, performed by people reading specifications. Nothing reads the specification automatically to build the register, determine the required submittal types per section, identify the reviewers a section designates, or apply the contractual turnaround the general conditions specify — despite the specification being a structured, conventionally organised document that follows a published format across the overwhelming majority of commercial projects.

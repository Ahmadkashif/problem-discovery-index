# General Contractor Project Management

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/construction-tech-platforms|Construction Tech Platforms]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in GC project management is fighting to tell a project team which activities will slip weeks before the schedule shows it — and whoever forecasts a slip earliest, with evidence a superintendent believes, takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$3.2B US general contractor project management software
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~36% of construction software revenue and the category's centre of gravity
**Digital Adoption:** High — drawings, RFIs, submittals and daily reports are on platform at most commercial GCs
**Target Buyer:** VPs of Operations and project executives at general contractors; product leads at Procore, Autodesk Construction Cloud and their competitors
**Automation Potential:** Very High — every input a forecast needs is already captured, structured and cross-project

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The general contractor carries the schedule and budget risk, which makes the GC platform the one place in the industry where an early warning has a clear owner and an obvious value. The platforms have won capture completely: baseline schedules and every revision, RFIs with the days they sat unanswered, submittals with turnaround by reviewer, change orders with cause and cost, daily reports with crew counts and weather, and photographs of every area on every day. What they return is a status view — what is late now — which is a report of a decision that has already been made by circumstances. The gap between what the data supports and what the product says is wider here than anywhere else in the vault, and it is wide in a way the customer can feel: project outcomes in this industry have not improved in decades while the software has improved enormously.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Procore is dominant with a deep ecosystem; Autodesk Construction Cloud pairs the same workflows with model authoring; Trimble and Oracle serve the heavy-civil and large-programme end. Scheduling lives in Primavera P6 and Microsoft Project, largely disconnected from the field data that would inform it — the schedule is updated by a scheduler in a weekly ritual, from percent-complete numbers supplied by the people whose performance they describe. Reality capture (OpenSpace, Buildots, Matterport) has made site progress objectively observable and is only beginning to be joined to the schedule. Analytics in the category means dashboards. No platform forecasts a completion date with an interval, and no platform tells a project manager on Tuesday that the activity starting in three weeks will not start, despite RFI latency and submittal turnaround being the leading indicators every superintendent already uses informally.

# Cramming Found by the Customer, Not the Store

**Niche:** [[niches/wireless-retail-agents/store-compliance-sales-practice-audits/profile|Store Compliance & Sales-Practice Audits]]
**Industry:** [[industries/wireless-retail-agents|Wireless Retail Agents]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A rep adds features or lines customers didn't ask for, and the store learns about it when customers come back with their bills.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #hypothesis-testing #compliance #worker-facing #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche (carrier audit programmes, retail-execution apps such as Zenput and the chains' own loss-prevention teams) is fighting to produce, for every line added, feature sold and SIM changed, evidence that the customer authorised it and was authenticated, and whoever makes that evidence automatic takes the dealer's compliance budget before the carrier's next audit or complaint review.

## The Problem
In the Florida case charged in July 2026, management caught on only after customers returned with missing credits and billing problems, and then found that nearly every transaction the employee had processed needed manual review. By that point the chargebacks, refunds and reputational damage were already done.

## Why It's Still Broken
Monthly targets reward add-ons, the signs of cramming are spread across POS data no one reviews per rep, and managers who share in the rep's sales have little reason to look.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A weekly per-rep report comparing add-on rates, feature removals within 30 days, returns, after-hours activations and complaint mentions against peers in the same store and district, with statistical thresholds rather than gut feel. Flags go to loss prevention, not to the store manager whose bonus depends on the rep.

## Who Feels the Pain
Customers, honest reps whose stores get a bad reputation, and dealers who face chargebacks and carrier audit findings.

## Impact If Fixed
Cramming caught in weeks instead of months, from data the dealer already holds, at the cost of one report.

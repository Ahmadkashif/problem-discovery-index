# Store Compliance & Sales-Practice Audits

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/wireless-retail-agents|Wireless Retail Agents]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche (carrier audit programmes, retail-execution apps such as Zenput and the chains' own loss-prevention teams) is fighting to produce, for every line added, feature sold and SIM changed, evidence that the customer authorised it and was authenticated, and whoever makes that evidence automatic takes the dealer's compliance budget before the carrier's next audit or complaint review.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$120M a year in loss-prevention and compliance staff time, audit preparation and tooling (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~0.6% of industry receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — brand-standards checklists, photos texted to district managers, and complaint investigations run by email
**Target Buyer:** Director of Loss Prevention & Compliance
**Automation Potential:** Medium — evidence capture automates; investigation stays human

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A dealer store handles the carrier's customers and their account data, so the carrier audits it: brand standards, sales practices, customer authentication and how complaints are handled (estimate of programme scope; the audits are confidential). The risk has real cases behind it. A former AT&T store employee in Oregon was sentenced to 16 months for SIM-swapping customers for a hacker. The Manhattan DA indicted a SIM-swap ring that included AT&T and T-Mobile retail employees. In July 2026 a former T-Mobile retailer employee in Florida was charged after opening unauthorized lines on customer accounts. The FCC's November 2023 SIM-swap and port-out order (WC Docket 21-341) requires carriers to authenticate customers before a SIM change or port-out and to notify them immediately, but a July 2024 bureau order (DA 24-649) deferred compliance with all of its rules until OMB approval and a Federal Register compliance-date notice, and no such notice had appeared by October 2026. Cramming, where reps add features customers didn't ask for, turns into chargebacks, complaints and terminations.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Carrier audit portals and mystery-shop programmes, retail-execution apps (Zenput, now part of Crunchtime, and FORM), video surveillance, and POS user logs. The gaps: no per-transaction record that the customer approved each add-on; authentication steps logged in carrier systems the dealer can't query; complaint investigations rebuilt from memory.

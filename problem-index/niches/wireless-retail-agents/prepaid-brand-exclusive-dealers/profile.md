# Prepaid Brand-Exclusive Dealers

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/wireless-retail-agents|Wireless Retail Agents]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche (master agents, the brands' dealer portals and prepaid POS vendors) is fighting to tell an owner-operator, store by store and day by day, what the month's activation commissions, bill-pay fees and first-recharge chargebacks will net to, and whoever makes that cash figure trustworthy takes the dealer's stores.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$300M a year in POS, payment processing, bookkeeping and back-office services across the dealer-run share of 16,000+ prepaid brand stores (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~1.5% of industry receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low-Medium — activations run through brand portals; the owner's view of what the stores earned comes from statements, deposits and memory
**Target Buyer:** Owner-operator of 1–50 stores
**Automation Potential:** High for the ledger; medium for cash handling

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Metro by T-Mobile, Cricket, Boost and Total Wireless together had more than 16,000 stores by mid-2026 (Wave7 Research), most of them run by independent dealers under exclusive agreements, often through a master agent. The economics differ from postpaid. Commissions are smaller per activation, many depend on the customer recharging, and stores also take cash bill payments and sell accessories. Dealers are not franchisees: NWIDA said in 2020 that Metro dealers "are NOT franchises" and lack franchise-law protection, so terms can change abruptly, as they did when Metro ended non-exclusive dealers in 2020. The owner is the finance department.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Brand dealer portals, master agents' statements and payout reports, prepaid POS systems (iQmetrix RQ is used by Cricket, among others), bill-pay terminals, QuickBooks, and spreadsheets. The gaps: commissions, bill-pay fees, residuals and chargebacks arrive on different reports at different times, so the owner can't see the month's true net until weeks later; and there is no early warning of which activations will fail the first-recharge condition.

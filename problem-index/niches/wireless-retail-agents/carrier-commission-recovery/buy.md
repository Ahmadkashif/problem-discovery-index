# Recovery Audit Methods Applied to Carrier Statements

**Niche:** [[niches/wireless-retail-agents/carrier-commission-recovery/profile|Carrier Commission Recovery]]
**Industry:** [[industries/wireless-retail-agents|Wireless Retail Agents]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Recovery audit firms have spent decades finding overpayments and missed credits in retailers' supplier accounts; carrier commission statements are the same problem in a different format.
**Tags:** #random-forests #feature-engineering #evaluation-metrics #data-integration #automation #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche (iQmetrix ReconcilePro, outsourced reconciliation services and spreadsheet-run back offices) is fighting to match every carrier statement line to the activation that earned it and file the dispute before the carrier's window closes, and whoever recovers the most short-paid dollars per month-end takes the dealer's reconciliation.

## The Problem
Dealers lack the staff to review every line every month, and the outsourced reconciliation shops that serve them mostly bill for hours.

## What Already Exists
Recovery audit firms such as PRGX review large retailers' accounts payable and supplier deals for missed allowances, duplicate payments and pricing errors, usually paid a share of what they recover, using matching rules and analytics built over many clients.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs: statement parsers for each carrier and master agent; expected-commission rules from monthly comp plans; identifier stitching across exchanges, ports and SIM changes; carrier dispute windows and evidence rules; and, because comp plans and statements are confidential to each dealer, models that run within one client's data rather than pooling.

## Target Customer
CFOs at mid-sized chains who would rather pay a share of recovery than add reconciliation staff.

## Impact If Solved
A contingency-priced recovery service with no up-front cost, reaching dealers too small for a software purchase.

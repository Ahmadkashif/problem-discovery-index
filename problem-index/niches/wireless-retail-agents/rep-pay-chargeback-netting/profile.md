# Rep Pay & Chargeback Netting

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/wireless-retail-agents|Wireless Retail Agents]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche (incentive-compensation platforms such as Xactly, Spiff and QCommission, payroll providers and in-house spreadsheets) is fighting to net each carrier chargeback against the right rep's advanced commission under a written plan with an audit trail that holds up under state wage law, and whoever makes every clawback explainable to the rep takes the chain's commission payroll.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$100M a year in commission payroll staff time, incentive-compensation software and wage-dispute cost (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~0.5% of industry receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low-Medium — payroll is digital; the calculation feeding it is often a spreadsheet
**Target Buyer:** VP Human Resources or Director of Sales Compensation
**Automation Potential:** High

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
This is the dealer facing its own reps. The legal and human stakes differ from carrier recovery: a clawback is a deduction from wages, defensible only if the plan defines when commission is earned, as in Deleon v. Verizon Wireless, where commissions were unearned until a 120-day-to-one-year chargeback period ended. California also requires commission plans to be in writing (Labor Code §2751). The winner is whoever makes each clawback traceable and explainable, not whoever recovers the most from carriers.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Xactly, Spiff, CaptivateIQ and QCommission for plan administration; ADP, Paylocity or UKG for payroll; in-house spreadsheets everywhere else. The gaps: clawbacks entered as lump-sum deductions without the line behind them; recovered disputes not reversed to the rep; and plans rewritten monthly as carrier plans change, with version control by email.

# Clinical Review and Expert Panel Practice

**Niche:** [[niches/ai-model-evaluation-firms/the-expert-rater/profile|The Expert Rater]]
**Industry:** [[industries/ai-model-evaluation-firms|AI Model Evaluation Firms]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Medicine, law and peer review already run structured expert panels with adjudication, calibration and appropriate compensation, and evaluation firms rebuilt none of it.
**Tags:** #worker-facing #hypothesis-testing #confidence-intervals #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #compliance #tacit-knowledge-ml
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep scarce practising specialists willing to do this work — and whoever does that takes the supply, because the entire domain evaluation business depends on a pool that is currently being spent down.

## The Problem
Organising specialists to render structured judgements at scale is what clinical chart review, utilisation review, medical-legal expert panels, grant review and academic peer review all do. Each has developed calibration procedures, adjudication for disagreement, conflict of interest management, documentation standards and — crucially — compensation norms that reflect what a specialist's time is worth. Evaluation firms recruited specialists onto annotation platforms.

## What Already Exists
Clinical chart review methodology with dual review and adjudication; expert panel consensus procedures with documented disagreement handling; grant and peer review processes with conflict management and structured forms; medical-legal expert practice with established rate structures; and inter-rater reliability practice with appropriate agreement statistics for expert judgement.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to high volume and to a subject that is a model output rather than a patient record. It requires: (1) adjudication as standard rather than majority vote, since expert disagreement on a hard case is information and chart review methodology treats it as such — adopting dual review with adjudication would raise both quality and retention; (2) calibration sessions before production rating, which every clinical review programme runs and no evaluation firm does, and which reduces the disagreement that later gets misread as rater error; (3) compensation benchmarked against the specialist's professional rate rather than against annotation rates, which is the norm in medical-legal work and is the honest comparison; (4) conflict disclosure, since a specialist rating a model their employer is evaluating is a conflict nobody currently asks about; and (5) volume adapted to sustainable review — the clinical review world knows how many charts a reviewer can assess in a day before quality falls, and this industry has no equivalent number and exceeds it routinely.

## Target Customer
Evaluation firms, the specialists doing the rating, regulated industry buyers, and the clinical review and expert panel organisations for whom this is adjacent work.

## Impact If Solved
Structured expert review is a mature practice with calibration, adjudication and honest rate structures. Dual review with adjudication would improve both the measurement and the retention, and the clinical world's sustainable-volume knowledge has no equivalent here.

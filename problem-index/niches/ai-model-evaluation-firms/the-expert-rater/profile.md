# The Expert Rater

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/ai-model-evaluation-firms|AI Model Evaluation Firms]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep scarce practising specialists willing to do this work — and whoever does that takes the supply, because the entire domain evaluation business depends on a pool that is currently being spent down.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$150M US in expert rating spend
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~19% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** None — piece rates against rubrics that do not fit
**Target Buyer:** The firms staffing this work; the constituency is the specialists themselves
**Automation Potential:** Medium — the tooling around the judgement, not the judgement

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A practising physician, attorney or engineer rating model outputs is doing genuinely hard cognitive work — reading a clinical vignette, reconstructing what the right answer would be, and evaluating a response against it — at piece rates set as though it were annotation. The rubric frequently does not cover the case in front of them, there is no way to record that the question itself was malformed, and the time budget assumes a speed that the work does not support. These people have alternatives, their supply is the binding constraint on the entire domain evaluation business, and the industry treats them as a labour input rather than as the scarce asset they are.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Annotation interfaces adapted from generic labelling, piece-rate compensation, rubric documents, and quality scoring by agreement. The gaps: no channel to flag a bad item, so experts grade nonsense questions and their frustration goes unrecorded; time budgets set by volume rather than by case difficulty; compensation that does not reflect the credential required; no feedback on whether their input mattered; and no visibility into how their judgement is aggregated or used.

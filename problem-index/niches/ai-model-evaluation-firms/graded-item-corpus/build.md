# The Material to Fix Measurement, Unexamined

**Niche:** [[niches/ai-model-evaluation-firms/graded-item-corpus/profile|Graded Item Corpus]]
**Industry:** [[industries/ai-model-evaluation-firms|AI Model Evaluation Firms]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** The firms hold millions of graded items with judge, rater, model and prompt attached — the empirical basis for fixing the industry's validity problems — and the incentive to publish uncomfortable findings about measurement is weak.
**Tags:** #hypothesis-testing #confidence-intervals #bayesian-inference #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #logistic-regression #causal-inference #cross-validation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn millions of graded items into an empirical account of how well this industry's own measurements work — and whoever publishes it sets the standard the field is judged by, which is worth more than any evaluation contract.

## The Problem
Every validity question this industry faces is empirically answerable from data it already holds. How much do judge models and domain experts actually agree, and on what kinds of items do they diverge? How much of a reported model difference survives switching the grader? How many items in a typical bank discriminate between models at all? How do rater populations differ from each other? Which published benchmark items show the signature of contamination? Millions of graded items with every relevant variable attached sit in these firms' storage, and the analysis has not been run, because the answers would make every number the industry sells look softer.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The findings are commercially unwelcome: a firm publishing that grader choice moves results by eight points has just told its customers that its own reports carry an eight-point uncertainty. Customer contracts restrict use of engagement data, drafted broadly. Cross-firm pooling requires a convener nobody has appointed. And the market currently buys reassurance, so the first firm to sell honesty is selling a worse-sounding product to buyers who have not yet been taught to ask.

## What to Build
Run the analysis and publish it. Start with judge-human agreement at scale, since every firm has the labels, the finding is immediately actionable for everybody, and it is the question on which the industry's dominant method rests — a published empirical account of it would be cited immediately and has no competitor. Quantify grader sensitivity: how much a reported difference between two models changes when the grader changes, which is the number that tells a buyer how much to trust any single evaluation and which nobody has ever put in front of them. Analyse item discrimination across the corpus and characterise what makes an item informative, which directly improves item construction and is the fix note's subject. Characterise rater population differences, since preference is always somebody's and the field reports it as though it were universal. Detect contamination signatures empirically across benchmarks and models, which the corpus supports and individual studies do not. Establish an aggregation basis with customers that is specific and inspectable, since the contractual barrier is real and is solvable by asking a narrow question rather than a broad one. Publish as a standing reference with a methodology anyone can check. And accept that the findings make the industry's numbers look softer, because they are softer, and the firm that says so first is the one that defines the standard.

## Target Customer
Evaluation firms, standards bodies and regulators, enterprise buyers with no reference for interpreting scores, and the measurement research community.

## Impact If Built
Every validity question the industry faces is answerable from data it already holds and has not examined. A published account of judge-human agreement at scale would be cited immediately, and grader sensitivity is the number that tells a buyer how much any single evaluation is worth.

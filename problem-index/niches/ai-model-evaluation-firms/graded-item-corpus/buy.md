## The Problem
Educational measurement treats every test item as an object with measured properties: difficulty, discrimination, how it functions across subgroups, and how much information it contributes at each ability level. Items are banked, calibrated, analysed and retired on evidence. Adaptive testing uses those properties to select the next item, reaching a precise measurement in a fraction of the questions. Benchmark suites are assembled by hand, used unchanged for years, and never analysed at the item level at all.

## What Already Exists
Item response theory with difficulty, discrimination and guessing parameters; item information functions and test information curves; differential item functioning analysis; computerised adaptive testing with item selection algorithms; item banking and calibration infrastructure; and reliability estimation from item statistics.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to test-takers that are models and to items graded on a scale. It requires: (1) calibration across models rather than across people, where the model population is small and heterogeneous and the standard estimation assumes a large sample — which is the main statistical adaptation and is tractable with hierarchical methods; (2) polytomous and continuous item models, since many evaluation items are graded on a rubric rather than marked right or wrong, and the extensions exist and are unused here; (3) differential functioning across model families rather than demographic groups, which would reveal when an item advantages a particular training approach rather than measuring the intended capability — a genuinely novel and valuable application; (4) adaptive item selection, which would let an evaluation reach a given precision in far fewer model calls and is the most direct cost saving available to the whole category; and (5) handling non-stationarity, since item difficulty for models changes as the field advances in a way that human test-taker populations do not.

## Target Customer
Evaluation firms, benchmark maintainers, standards bodies, and the psychometrics community for whom this is a large and unclaimed application.

## Impact If Solved
A century of item analysis exists and benchmark suites are assembled by hand. Adaptive item selection would reach a given precision in far fewer model calls, and differential functioning across model families would reveal items that measure training approach rather than capability.

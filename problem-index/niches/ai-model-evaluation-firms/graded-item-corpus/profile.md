# Graded Item Corpus

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/ai-model-evaluation-firms|AI Model Evaluation Firms]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn millions of graded items into an empirical account of how well this industry's own measurements work — and whoever publishes it sets the standard the field is judged by, which is worth more than any evaluation contract.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$140M US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~18% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** None — the material to fix measurement, unexamined
**Target Buyer:** The firms themselves, and the standards bodies that should exist
**Automation Potential:** Very High — the corpus is structured and complete

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
These firms hold millions of graded items with the judge, the rater, the model, the prompt, the rubric and the outcome all attached. That corpus answers the questions the industry's own validity problems turn on: how much judges and humans actually agree and where they diverge, which items discriminate between models and which every model passes, how much of a reported difference survives a change of grader, how rater populations differ, which benchmark items show contamination signatures. Every firm sits on this and none has published the analysis, because a market that buys reassurance does not reward a vendor for demonstrating how loose its own measurements are.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Per-engagement reporting, internal quality dashboards, and published research on individual effects using small academic datasets. The gaps: no cross-engagement analysis of measurement validity; no item-level discrimination analysis, so item banks are full of items that separate nothing; no empirical account of judge-human agreement at scale, despite every firm being able to compute one; and no shared reference that a regulator or buyer could use to interpret any published score.

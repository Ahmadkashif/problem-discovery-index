# Frontier Lab Assurance

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/ai-model-evaluation-firms|AI Model Evaluation Firms]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this sub-niche is fighting to find capability or risk that the lab's own evaluation team did not — and whoever does that takes the contract, because a lab with a large internal team buys external evaluation for exactly one reason.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$180M US, the least visible revenue pool in the category
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~23% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Low — the valuable part is judgement, the tooling is thin
**Target Buyer:** A small number of frontier and near-frontier labs
**Automation Potential:** Medium — elicitation is automatable, novelty is not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A handful of organisations contract external firms to evaluate models before release, under strict confidentiality, frequently on unreleased checkpoints. These labs have large internal evaluation teams and considerable expertise, so the external firm is not being bought for capacity. It is being bought for what the internal team cannot produce: an outside view, an adversarial posture, a capability elicitation approach the lab did not think of, or a finding whose discovery inside the lab would have been institutionally uncomfortable. One non-obvious finding justifies an engagement. Contract size is large, the customer count is small, and almost nothing about the business resembles selling a harness to product teams.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Bespoke evaluation design, expert teams, elicitation techniques, private harnesses, and reporting under embargo. The gaps: elicitation effort is unmeasured, so a negative result cannot be distinguished from insufficient effort; findings are not tracked across engagements, so the same firm rediscovers the same classes; no methodology is published, which makes the field's assurance claims unauditable; and the firm's independence is structurally compromised by depending on a handful of customers.

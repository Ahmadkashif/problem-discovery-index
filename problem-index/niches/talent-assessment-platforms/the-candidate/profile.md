# The Candidate

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/talent-assessment-platforms|Talent Assessment Platforms]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Whether a person assessed for an hour gets to know what was measured, how they did, or why they were rejected.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$520M — 13% of US pre-employment assessment
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~13%
**Digital Adoption:** Very low — an hour in, nothing out
**Target Buyer:** Candidates; regulators; employers competing on candidate experience
**Automation Potential:** Very high — every output is already computed

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche

An hour of unpaid assessment, a score they never see, criteria they are never told, a rejection with no reason, and no way to ask.

The candidate is the only party in this transaction who contributes labour and receives nothing. They complete the instrument, generating the data the employer buys and the vendor's product depends on, and their access to the results is zero.

The consequence is measurable. Completion rates suffer, and the candidates who abandon are not a random sample — they skew toward those with other options, those with less time, and those who have been through the process before and learned it produces nothing. So the instrument is administered to a population shaped by who was willing to tolerate it, which is a selection effect nobody accounts for.

## Current Tools & Gaps

Assessment delivery interfaces with progress indicators and time estimates of varying accuracy. Rejection emails, generally templated and reasonless. Some vendors and employers provide feedback reports; the practice is rare and usually reserved for late-stage candidates. Regulatory regimes are beginning to require disclosure of what is being assessed and how.

The gaps are everything on the candidate's side. No statement of what is being measured, no time honesty, no results, no reason for rejection, no route to contest an error, no accessibility accommodation path that works, and no reuse — the same candidate takes an equivalent instrument for the next employer a fortnight later.

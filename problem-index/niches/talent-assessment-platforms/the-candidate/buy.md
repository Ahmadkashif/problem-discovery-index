# Buy: Candidate Experience Tooling Adapted to a Scored Encounter

**Niche:** [[niches/talent-assessment-platforms/the-candidate/profile|The Candidate]]
**Industry:** [[industries/talent-assessment-platforms|Talent Assessment Platforms]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Candidate experience products measure how applicants felt about the process; the problem here is what they were not told and did not receive.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #compliance #workflow-orchestration #large-language-models #confidence-intervals #worker-facing #automation
**Contested on:** Whether experience tooling built around surveys and communication can address an information asymmetry.

## The Problem

Candidate experience is an established concern with real products. Application status communication, automated updates, interview scheduling, feedback surveys, employer brand measurement and candidate NPS are all available and widely deployed.

They address communication and sentiment. The candidate's problem in assessment is informational and procedural: they do not know what is being measured, do not receive their result, do not get a reason, and have no route to raise an error. A better-worded rejection email and a satisfaction survey do not touch any of it.

## What Already Exists

Candidate experience platforms with communication automation and survey tooling. Candidate NPS measurement. Status portals. Application tracking with candidate-facing views. Employer brand monitoring. Assessment delivery platforms with progress indicators.

## The Customization Gap

**The gap is disclosure, not tone.** What is measured, how it is used, what the result was, and why the outcome occurred. These are content requirements the vendor and employer must decide to meet, and no experience product supplies the content.

**Returning a score is a product surface nobody has built.** A candidate-facing results view with the score, its interval, the construct descriptions and the reference point. It is a rendering of existing data and it exists essentially nowhere.

**Contestation needs a workflow, not a survey.** A route to report a technical failure, an accessibility problem or a data error, with a human, a timeline and an outcome. Experience platforms collect sentiment; this needs a case process.

**Accessibility accommodation is a request path that mostly does not work.** Candidates needing an adjustment frequently cannot find how to ask, or ask and receive no response before the deadline. A functioning accommodation workflow, with the request visible to whoever can grant it and a deadline that pauses, is both a legal requirement and a product gap.

**Abandonment analytics are the employer-facing half.** Where candidates drop out, who they are, and how that shapes the applicant pool. Experience platforms measure satisfaction among completers, which is precisely the wrong population.

## Target Customer

Employers competing on candidate experience who have deployed an experience platform and find assessment is the worst-rated stage and the one the platform cannot fix. Also assessment vendors, for whom candidate-facing output is a differentiator as regulation moves toward disclosure, and the experience platform vendors themselves.

## Impact If Solved

The communication automation, status portals and survey machinery get reused, and the disclosure content, candidate results view, contestation workflow, working accommodation path and abandonment analytics get built. Concretely: a candidate who knows what happened to them, which no amount of email tone achieves.

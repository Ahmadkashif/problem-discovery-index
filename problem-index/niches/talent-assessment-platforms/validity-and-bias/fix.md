# Fix: The Validation Study Belongs to Someone Else's Population

**Niche:** [[niches/talent-assessment-platforms/validity-and-bias/profile|Validity & Bias Measurement]]
**Industry:** [[industries/talent-assessment-platforms|Talent Assessment Platforms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The technical manual reports validity coefficients from a population and a role that resemble the client's only loosely, and nobody in the procurement reads the manual.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #hypothesis-testing #confidence-intervals #evaluation-metrics #compliance #workflow-orchestration #quick-win #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Whether the buyer will read what the validity claim actually says before deploying the instrument.

## The Problem

An assessment is purchased. Somewhere in the vendor's technical documentation is a validity claim: a coefficient, a sample, a criterion, a population, a role family. Frequently it is a meta-analytic argument that this class of instrument predicts performance generally.

The buyer deploys it for a specific role in a specific organisation with a specific applicant population, none of which appears in the study. Sometimes the fit is reasonable and the validity generalisation argument is defensible. Sometimes the study was on graduate applicants in a different country for a different job family, and the instrument is being used to screen warehouse supervisors.

Nobody checks. The procurement reviewed pricing, integration and candidate experience. The technical manual, where one exists, was not read by anyone in the process — and for several newer entrants there is no technical manual, only marketing claims about validity from vendor-run studies.

## Why It's Still Broken

Procurement has no assessment expertise. The buyer is a talent acquisition leader choosing between vendors on demo quality, integration, cost and candidate experience, and validity is asserted by every vendor so it does not differentiate.

The documentation is also written for psychologists and is impenetrable to the person deciding. A validity coefficient with a confidence interval and a range restriction correction is not a format that reaches a procurement committee.

And the professional standards that would require this review exist — the psychometrics community has published guidance for decades — and are not binding on anyone in the purchase.

## What a Fix Looks Like

Make the claim legible at the point of purchase and check the fit.

Require a structured validity summary from every vendor in a fixed format: what was measured, against what criterion, on what population, in what roles, with what sample size, what coefficient, what interval, and whether the study was independent or vendor-run. Same fields from every vendor, comparable side by side. Procurement can evaluate that; it cannot evaluate forty pages of psychometrics.

Check the fit explicitly. How does the client's role and applicant population compare to the study's, on the dimensions that matter — job family, seniority, applicant education and experience, geography. A structured comparison with a stated verdict on whether validity generalisation is defensible here.

Ask for the negative results. Studies where the instrument did not predict, and roles where the vendor does not recommend it. A vendor unwilling to name where their instrument does not work is telling you something.

Distinguish independent from vendor-run evidence and weight accordingly. This is a single field and it changes how a claim should be read.

Write the local validation into the contract. A commitment to validate at this client within eighteen months, with the vendor supporting the analysis and a stated consequence if the instrument does not predict. Buyers have far more leverage at purchase than afterwards and almost never use it here.

And publish the summary to candidates. A candidate told what the instrument measures, on what evidence, is being treated as a person rather than as a score input — and it costs nothing that the vendor has not already written.

## Who Feels the Pain

Candidates screened out by an instrument that may not predict anything about their ability to do the job. Employers making hiring decisions on a purchased claim they did not examine, with the legal exposure that follows. I-O psychologists inside vendors and employers who know the fit is poor and are not in the purchase decision. And the established publishers with genuine validation traditions, who compete against claims that nobody checks.

## Impact If Fixed

The validity claim becomes legible and comparable at the point where the decision is made. Poor fits between a study's population and a client's get caught before deployment. And the local validation commitment gets into the contract, which is the only moment the buyer has the leverage to ask for it.

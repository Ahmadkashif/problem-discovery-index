# Score Interpretation by Hiring Managers

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/talent-assessment-platforms|Talent Assessment Platforms]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Whether the person acting on the score understands what it does and does not say.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$400M — 10% of US pre-employment assessment
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~10%
**Digital Adoption:** Very low — a number, a band and a colour
**Target Buyer:** Employers' recruiting leadership; vendors
**Automation Potential:** High — the presentation and the guardrails are both product work

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche

An instrument's validity is a statement about a population and a correlation. The person acting on it is a recruiter or hiring manager looking at one candidate's number, with no training in what a moderate correlation implies about an individual.

So the score gets over-read. A candidate at the 61st percentile is treated as meaningfully better than one at the 54th, when the instrument cannot distinguish them. A band boundary becomes a hard cut, excluding people a point below it. A personality profile gets read as a character description. A composite score is compared across roles it was never normed for. And a score is combined with an interview impression in a way that double-counts the same signal.

The niche is distinct because the instrument can be perfectly valid and the decision still wrong, and because the failure is entirely in the presentation layer — which is the vendor's to design.

## Current Tools & Gaps

Score reports with percentiles, bands, colour coding and narrative descriptions. Recruiter dashboards with sortable score columns. Some vendors provide interpretation guidance and training; uptake is limited. Integration into applicant tracking systems that surfaces the score next to the candidate.

The gaps are uncertainty, guardrails and combination. Scores are reported as points with no error, which invites distinctions the instrument cannot support. Nothing prevents a manager sorting a list by score and taking the top ten. Nothing warns when a score is being used outside its validated range. And no guidance exists on how to combine a score with an interview, which is where most of the practical error occurs.

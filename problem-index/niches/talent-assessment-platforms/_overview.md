# Niche Analysis — Talent Assessment Platforms

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/talent-assessment-platforms|Talent Assessment Platforms]]

## Niche Selection

This industry sells prediction of job performance and almost never measures whether its predictions came true where they were used. The eight niches follow from that: the measurement that is not made, the instruments themselves, the two operational failures, the two people, and the mechanical layers.

## Why These Niches

Validity and bias are the two properties an instrument must have, and the industry measures one of them. Design and content are where the instruments come from. Item leakage and manager misinterpretation are the two failures that quietly destroy an instrument's value in deployment. The two underserved people are the candidate scored by something they cannot see and the psychologist who knows the configuration is wrong and watches it ship. Delivery and integration are mechanical, and the first is where accessibility failures exclude people entirely.

## Filter Notes

Seven of the eight are terminal — each names one contest and dividing further produces features rather than markets.

**Validity and bias measurement** is not, and its halves separate on the fairness of the distribution versus the truth of the claim. Adverse impact auditing asks whether the instrument passes different groups at different rates. It is now a legal requirement in some jurisdictions — New York City's Local Law 144 mandates annual independent bias audits of automated employment decision tools with published results, the first regime of its kind — it is a bounded exercise on pass-rate data the vendor already holds, it produces an artefact procurement can file, and every vendor in the market now offers it. Local criterion validity asks whether the score predicts anything about the people this employer actually hired for this role. It requires the client's own performance, tenure and progression data joined to assessment scores; it requires eighteen months before the sample exists; performance ratings are themselves noisy and biased, so the analysis is genuinely hard to do well; and a negative finding invalidates an instrument the client has bought and deployed, which is a result nobody in the transaction wants written down. So the industry does the audit and skips the validation — and the consequence is exactly what the analysis names: an instrument can be perfectly balanced across groups and predict nothing at all, and there is currently more pressure to demonstrate the first than the second.

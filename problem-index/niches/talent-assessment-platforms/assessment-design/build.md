# Build: Job Analysis and Item Development at Market Speed

**Industry:** [[industries/talent-assessment-platforms|Talent Assessment Platforms]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Compress job analysis and item development from months to weeks without abandoning the psychometric standards that make an instrument defensible.

**Contested on:** Whether item development can be accelerated without degrading the psychometric properties that justify the instrument.

## The Problem

Items take months to develop and psychometrically validate, and circulate publicly within weeks of deployment. That arithmetic is the design function's central problem: the production cost of a valid item is high and its useful life is short.

Underneath it sits a slower problem. A defensible instrument starts from a job analysis — a structured procedure establishing what the role actually requires, which is the evidentiary foundation for claiming the instrument measures something job-related. Job analysis is expensive, and in much of the market it is skipped or reduced to a conversation with a hiring manager, which means the construct's relevance to the job is asserted rather than established.

So instruments are built quickly on thin foundations, and then deployed for roles the foundation never covered.

## Why Nobody Has Built This

The established publishers do this properly and slowly, at a cost structure that supports it. The newer entrants compete on speed and format novelty and have not built the foundation, which is why their validity claims frequently rest on vendor-run studies that would not survive independent scrutiny.

Nobody has built the middle: rigorous foundations at market speed. The tooling to attempt it — generative item drafting, automated job analysis from job descriptions and incumbent data, rapid psychometric calibration — has only recently become good enough.

And the market does not currently reward it, because buyers cannot distinguish a well-founded instrument from a well-marketed one.

## What to Build

Acceleration of the slow parts with the standards intact.

**Automate the first draft of job analysis.** Job descriptions, incumbent task data, performance criteria, organisational documents and role-family libraries, read together to produce a draft competency and task profile for expert review. The expert's time moves from producing the draft to critiquing it, which is where their value is and which compresses the timeline substantially.

**Ground the construct choice in the analysis, visibly.** Every construct the instrument measures traced to a task or competency the job analysis established, with the evidence attached. This is what makes an instrument defensible and it is currently a document produced after the fact if at all.

**Generate items at volume, calibrate them properly.** Generative drafting can produce large item pools quickly; the psychometric work of calibrating difficulty, discrimination and differential functioning remains necessary and cannot be skipped. The gain is in the drafting, not in the validation, and being clear about that distinction is what separates a legitimate acceleration from a shortcut.

**Screen for differential item functioning early.** Items that behave differently across groups should be identified during calibration rather than discovered in a bias audit two years later. This is standard psychometric practice, it is cheap, and it is the point where adverse impact is most efficiently prevented.

**Build for rotation from the start.** Given the leakage problem, the design objective is a large calibrated pool with items retired and replaced continuously, rather than a fixed form. That changes the economics of item development and is the only structural answer to circulation.

**Document to an evidentiary standard.** The job analysis, the construct mapping, the calibration, the DIF screening and the version history, assembled as the technical documentation that a challenge would examine. Producing it as a by-product rather than as a retrospective exercise is both cheaper and more credible.

## Target Customer

The established test publishers, for whom the acceleration is a cost structure improvement on a process they already do properly. Also the newer entrants under regulatory and scrutiny pressure, who need foundations they do not have, and employers commissioning custom instruments for specific roles.

## Impact If Built

The slow, expensive foundation of a defensible instrument gets built at something closer to market speed, which is what would let rigour compete with novelty. Differential item functioning gets caught during development rather than in an audit. And continuous item rotation becomes economically feasible, which is the only real answer to leakage.

# Assessment Design & Content

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/talent-assessment-platforms|Talent Assessment Platforms]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Whether what the instrument measures is what the job requires.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$720M — 18% of US pre-employment assessment
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~18%
**Digital Adoption:** Mature in part of the market, very thin in the rest
**Target Buyer:** Test publishers; assessment vendors; employers commissioning custom instruments
**Automation Potential:** Moderate — item generation automates; construct definition does not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche

An assessment is a claim that a measurable construct predicts job performance. Two things have to be right: the construct has to matter for the job, and the items have to measure the construct. The established publishers do both with genuine rigour; much of the rest of the market does neither carefully.

The distinguishing pressure is speed. Items take months to develop and psychometrically validate, and the market wants new instruments quickly, for new roles, in new formats — game-based, video-based, simulation-based — where the psychometric tradition has thin foundations and the claims run ahead of the evidence.

The niche is distinct because it is about the instrument itself rather than about whether it works in deployment. A badly designed instrument cannot be rescued by validation or auditing; it can only be found out by them.

## Current Tools & Gaps

Item banking and test assembly software. Item response theory calibration. Job analysis methodology for establishing what a role requires. Adaptive testing engines. Content development workflows at the established publishers. Generative tooling for item drafting, arriving quickly.

The gaps are job analysis and novel formats. Job analysis — the procedure establishing what a role actually requires — is the foundation of a defensible instrument and is frequently skipped or done superficially, so instruments measure constructs whose relevance to the role is asserted. And the newer formats have real measurement questions that the enthusiasm has outrun, with construct validity for game-based and video-based assessments considerably less established than their marketing suggests.

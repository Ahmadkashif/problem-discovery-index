# Item Security & Content Leakage

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/talent-assessment-platforms|Talent Assessment Platforms]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Whether an item that took months to develop is still measuring anything after it has circulated for a fortnight.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$440M — 11% of US pre-employment assessment
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~11%
**Digital Adoption:** Low — leakage is known, tracked informally and rarely measured
**Target Buyer:** Test publishers; employers relying on the instrument's integrity
**Automation Potential:** High — exposure detection and rotation both automate

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche

Items take months to develop and psychometrically validate, and circulate publicly within weeks of deployment. Forums, coaching sites, shared documents and video walkthroughs collect and distribute assessment content, and for high-volume instruments the effective exposure is close to total within a hiring season.

A leaked item measures preparation rather than the construct, and its psychometric parameters — difficulty, discrimination — silently change. The instrument continues to produce scores that look the same and mean something different, and the validity claim attached to it was established before the leak.

The niche is distinct because it is the mechanism by which a valid instrument quietly becomes an invalid one, and because it is measurable — a leaked item's statistics move in a detectable way — and mostly not measured.

## Current Tools & Gaps

Item pools with some rotation. Randomised item selection and form assembly. Proctoring for higher-stakes tests. Legal action against content-sharing sites, which is slow and partially effective. Informal monitoring of forums by content teams.

The gaps are detection and economics. Nobody systematically monitors the public channels where items circulate, though they are public. Nobody watches item statistics for the drift that indicates exposure, though it is computable from the response data. And item rotation is constrained by the cost of producing calibrated replacements, which makes the structural answer expensive.

# Consigned Stock Nobody Has Touched in a Year

**Niche:** [[niches/industrial-distributors/vmi-and-industrial-vending/profile|Vendor-Managed Inventory & Industrial Vending]]
**Industry:** [[industries/industrial-distributors|Industrial Distributors]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Bins and vending slots filled at installation keep slow movers on the distributor's books for years, and nobody is measured on clearing them.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #survival-analysis #feature-engineering #quick-win #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep the customer's bins, cribs and vending machines in stock at the lowest consigned inventory and the fewest replenishment visits, and whoever replenishes from actual usage signals rather than walk-around counts takes the account.

## The Problem
When a programme is installed, the customer's wish list goes into the bins. Usage settles within months, but many slots hold items that are dispensed rarely or never. On consignment, the distributor owns that stock, carries its cost, and writes it off when the programme ends or the customer changes products.

## Why It's Still Broken
Account managers are rewarded for programme count and sales, not for inventory turns per programme. Removing an item means a conversation with the customer, and the dispense data that would justify it is buried in usage reports nobody reads for this purpose.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A quarterly review per programme listing slots with no or minimal dispense, with time since last use, the carrying cost and a proposed swap or removal, sent to the account manager and shared with the customer as a savings item.

## Who Feels the Pain
Distributor finance carrying the inventory, account managers facing write-offs at programme end, and customers whose cribs are cluttered.

## Impact If Fixed
Clearing dead slots can release a meaningful share of consigned inventory per programme (estimate ~10–20%), and the customer counts the cleaner crib as part of the programme's value.

# Vendor-Managed Inventory & Industrial Vending

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/industrial-distributors|Industrial Distributors]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep the customer's bins, cribs and vending machines in stock at the lowest consigned inventory and the fewest replenishment visits, and whoever replenishes from actual usage signals rather than walk-around counts takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$15B a year of US distributor sales through vending, managed bins and onsite storerooms (estimate); Fastenal alone put $3,712.7M of 2025 sales through its managed-inventory programme
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~4% of the two codes' receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** High at the leaders (badge-controlled vending, scanned or sensor bins), Medium elsewhere, where replenishment reps still count bins by eye
**Target Buyer:** VP of Inventory Solutions or Onsite Programmes at the distributor; plant operations and procurement at the customer
**Automation Potential:** High — counting, forecasting, reorder and routing are all mechanisable; installing and stocking stay physical

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Point-of-use inventory moves the distributor's stock into the customer's plant and makes replenishment a continuous service. Fastenal (136,638 weighted bin and vending installations at end-2025, 44.7% of sales through managed inventory), Grainger KeepStock, MSC and many regionals compete on it, with crib and vending technology from firms such as AutoCrib and CribMaster. The customer measures the programme on two things — no stockouts on the line, and lower spend and inventory — and the distributor pays for every unnecessary visit and every slow-moving item it owns on consignment.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Vending machines and crib software logging every dispense, weight and RFID bins, scanner-based replenishment apps, and ERP min/max. The gaps: min/max set at installation and rarely revisited, intermittent slot-level demand forecast poorly or not at all, visit schedules fixed rather than driven by need, and consignment carrying cost invisible to the account manager.

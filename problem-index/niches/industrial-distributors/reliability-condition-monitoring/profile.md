# Reliability & Condition Monitoring Services

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/industrial-distributors|Industrial Distributors]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this sub-niche is fighting to call a bearing, gearbox or coupling failure weeks before it stops the line, with few enough false alarms that planners keep acting on the alerts, and whoever posts the best caught-to-false-alarm record on the plant's own critical assets takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$3B a year of US spend on rotating-equipment condition monitoring, vibration and oil analysis routes, and reliability services sold to plants (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~1% of the two codes' receipts (estimate); most of the spend goes to specialist vendors rather than distributors
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — wireless sensors and cloud dashboards are spreading, while route-based vibration collection and failure analysis remain manual
**Target Buyer:** Plant reliability or maintenance manager; the distributor's reliability services manager
**Automation Potential:** High for detection and triage; low for diagnosis on difficult machines and for root-cause work on site

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Reliability is sold on a track record built over months, not on speed on the day. BPT distributors (Motion and Applied run reliability groups), manufacturers' own services (SKF, Schaeffler, Timken), sensor-and-analytics firms (Augury, Tractian and others) and independent vibration contractors all compete for the same reliability budget. The buyer judges them on one thing: did alerts arrive early enough to plan a repair, and were there few enough false ones that the planners kept trusting them. The distributor's angle is that it also stocks the part, but that only matters if its detection holds up.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Wireless vibration and temperature sensors, route-based data collectors, analyst-reviewed diagnostics, and oil analysis labs. The gaps: alerts rarely get labelled with what was actually found when the component came out; thresholds are generic; and the alert seldom triggers the supply side — the part staged and the fix recommended. Independent failure-analysis labs are profiled elsewhere (see the Pass 2 notes).

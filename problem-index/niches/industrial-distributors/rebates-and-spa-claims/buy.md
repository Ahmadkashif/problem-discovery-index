# Rebate Platforms Turned Toward the Distributor's Claim Desk

**Niche:** [[niches/industrial-distributors/rebates-and-spa-claims/profile|Supplier Rebates & SPA Claims]]
**Industry:** [[industries/industrial-distributors|Industrial Distributors]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Rebate management platforms model programmes well; industrial distributors need them to file SPA claims line by line against each manufacturer's rules.
**Tags:** #logistic-regression #feature-engineering #large-language-models #compliance #data-integration #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to file every claim the distributor has earned under each manufacturer's agreement terms before the claim window closes, and whoever recovers the most claim dollars at the lowest denial rate takes the account.

## The Problem
A distributor that licenses a rebate platform gets programme tracking, accruals and dashboards, but still builds SPA claims in spreadsheets, because the platform's model of an agreement doesn't match how industrial manufacturers write SPAs or run their portals.

## What Already Exists
Enable, Vistex and similar platforms for volume rebates and incentive programmes; distributor-focused newcomers such as Band and Claro; ERP contract-pricing modules.

## The Customization Gap
Industrial SPA logic: end-customer and ship-to matching, order-date versus ship-date rules, manufacturer part numbers mapped to distributor SKUs and interchanges, quantity caps consumed across branches, claim-file formats per manufacturer, and resubmission rules for denials. Plus integration with Prophet 21, Infor CSD and Eclipse contract tables, so terms are entered once.

## Target Customer
Controllers and rebate analysts at distributors already paying for a rebate platform who still file SPAs by hand.

## Impact If Solved
If one system ends ~2–4 days a month of parallel spreadsheet work at a claim desk (estimate) and recovers ~5% more claim dollars by covering SPAs and rebates together (estimate), the CFO sees recovery and leakage in one report.

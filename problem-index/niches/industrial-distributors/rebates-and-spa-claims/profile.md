# Supplier Rebates & SPA Claims

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/industrial-distributors|Industrial Distributors]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to file every claim the distributor has earned under each manufacturer's agreement terms before the claim window closes, and whoever recovers the most claim dollars at the lowest denial rate takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$6–10B a year of special-pricing and rebate money owed by manufacturers to US industrial distributors (estimate); the labour and software spent claiming it is ~$300M (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~2% of the two codes' receipts in claimable money (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — agreements arrive as PDFs and emails, claims are matched in spreadsheets, and 28% of distributors in Enable's 2024 vendor survey tracked rebate performance in Excel
**Target Buyer:** CFO or controller at the distributor
**Automation Potential:** High — agreement extraction, line matching and rule checks are all mechanical once terms are structured

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Special pricing agreements let a distributor sell to a named customer below its normal cost and claim the gap back from the manufacturer; volume rebates and buying-group programmes add more claimable money on top. For many distributors this money is a large share of net profit, so a missed claim is lost profit, not lost sales. The work is a back-office function with hard external clocks: each agreement has a claim window, and each manufacturer has its own denial rules. Enable's 2024 survey found 52% of distributors believe they don't receive everything they earn. The same contest exists in electrical distribution, where SPAs are even more central; this niche covers the industrial side and leaves electrical to its own build.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Rebate and incentive platforms (Enable, Vistex), newer distributor-side tools (Band, Claro), ERP contract-pricing tables, and manufacturer claim portals. The gaps: agreement terms keyed by hand, part-number aliasing between manufacturer and distributor numbers, denial rules known only to the person filing, and no list of lines sold below cost that were never claimed. The foodservice equivalent is logged as [[niches/restaurant-suppliers/deviated-pricing-rebate-administration/profile|Deviated Pricing & Rebate Administration]].

# National-Account & Integrated MRO Supply

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/industrial-distributors|Industrial Distributors]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to prove, site by site, savings that the customer's procurement and finance teams will sign off at re-bid, and whoever brings the largest audited documented-savings total to the contract review takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$70B a year of US distributor sales to multi-site manufacturers under national, strategic or integrated-supply contracts (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~18% of the two codes' receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium to High — punchout, e-procurement and vending are standard; the savings evidence that wins renewals is assembled by hand
**Target Buyer:** VP of National or Strategic Accounts at the distributor; the customer's category manager for indirect spend
**Automation Potential:** High for evidence assembly and contract compliance; low for the onsite relationship

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A multi-site manufacturer buys maintenance, repair and operating supplies under a contract that its procurement team re-bids every ~3–5 years (estimate). Grainger, Fastenal, MSC, Applied and Motion compete for those contracts alongside integrated-supply operators that run the customer's storeroom. Price lists converge at bid time, so the decision turns on proof: how much the incumbent saved through consolidation, standardisation, inventory reduction, vending and avoided downtime. Applied Industrial built its Documented Value-Added reports for exactly this; every serious competitor has an equivalent. The contest is not selling more items, but producing savings claims that survive the customer's finance review.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Distributor savings programmes (Applied's DVA, vending usage reports, onsite reports), e-procurement punchout through Ariba and Coupa, and the customer's own spend analytics. The gaps: savings are logged by reps as sales collateral, with inconsistent baselines; downtime-avoidance claims lack evidence; site-level contract compliance is measured late; and the customer's procurement team discounts what it cannot audit.

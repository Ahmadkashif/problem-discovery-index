# No Fit Standard Is Ever Measured Against What Happened Next

**Niche:** [[niches/alterations-tailoring/body-scan-fit-standards/profile|Body Scan & Fit Standard Consultancies]]
**Industry:** [[industries/alterations-tailoring|Alterations & Tailoring]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The firm delivers a size chart and a block library, the brand cuts two years of product against it, and the return rates that follow are never routed back — so the standard's actual performance is unknown to the people who wrote it.

## The Problem
A fit standard is a set of consequential predictions: that this size chart covers the target consumer, that these grade rules hold across the size run, that this block produces garments people keep. The brand then finds out whether that was true, in the form of fit-related return rates, alteration rates, size-run sell-through, and customer complaints — and none of it comes back. The consultancy delivers, invoices, and moves to the next engagement, learning about performance only through the coarsest possible signal: whether the client rehires. Across hundreds of engagements the firm has generated an enormous body of natural experiments in fit standard design and retained the outcome of none of them. The recommendations it makes today rest on anthropometric evidence and senior judgment, with no empirical link to which prior recommendations actually worked.

## Why It's Still Broken
The outcome data belongs to the brand, arrives twelve to twenty-four months after delivery, and lives in retail systems the consultancy has no relationship with. Nothing in a standard engagement contemplates a follow-up, so there is no clause, no channel, and no budget for it. There is also a straightforward disincentive: a firm that systematically measures its own recommendations creates a record that can show some of them failed, and the traditional consulting posture is to let the work speak through the client's satisfaction rather than through data. The result is that the industry's central question — does a well-constructed fit standard measurably reduce fit failure — has never been answered by the people best positioned to answer it.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A follow-back layer written into the engagement rather than bolted on. The delivered standard carries a durable identifier and a defined set of outcome measures — fit-related return rate by style and size, alteration incidence, size-run sell-through — agreed with the client up front, reported in aggregate at agreed intervals, with no individual customer data crossing the boundary. Over engagements this accumulates into the asset the firm most conspicuously lacks: an outcome record linking specific fit standard decisions to what happened in market. That supports retrospective analysis — which grade rule choices are associated with failure at the top of the size run, whether shape-based segmentation outperforms girth-based in practice — and prospective use, where a proposed standard is checked against the outcomes of structurally similar prior standards before it ships. The client gets something of their own out of it too, which is what makes the ask reasonable: an ongoing read on whether an expensive investment is working, which they currently do not have either.

## Who Feels the Pain
Consultants making recommendations they will never see tested; the managing director who cannot demonstrate return on a fit standard investment beyond anecdote; brands paying six figures for a standard with no mechanism to verify it worked; and ultimately the consumer, who absorbs a bad grading decision as a return or a trip to an alterations shop.

## Impact If Fixed
Turns a judgment-based advisory practice into an evidence-based one, which is both a better product and a more defensible price. It also produces the only proof point that matters in a market where phone-based scanning is commoditizing data capture: not that the firm holds the largest body dataset, but that standards built from it demonstrably reduce fit failure. No competitor can assert that without the same outcome loop, and the loop only exists if someone builds it into the engagement.

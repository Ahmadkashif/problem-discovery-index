## The Problem
Software and consumer subscription businesses routinely identify which product behaviours predict retention, which features drive expansion, and which usage patterns precede churn, and they use that to direct product investment. The methods — cohort analysis, engagement scoring, feature-level retention attribution — are standard. A publisher's articles are its features, its readers are its users, and its analytics stack counts sessions.

## What Already Exists
Product analytics with feature-level engagement; retention driver analysis; cohort and lifecycle measurement; engagement scoring; and the practice of directing investment from usage evidence.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a product that is thousands of one-off items. It requires: (1) a catalogue where every unit is used once and never again, so feature-level retention analysis has no repeated usage to work with — this is the substantive difference and forces a different model; (2) huge heterogeneity, since two articles are less alike than two features; (3) selection bias where the articles that attract the most readers attract different readers, which product analytics rarely faces so acutely; (4) editorial values that are not reducible to retention, so the measurement must inform rather than dictate; and (5) a large free audience alongside the subscribers, which most subscription analytics does not model.

## Target Customer
Data and editorial leadership, subscription and audience teams, publisher analytics vendors, and product analytics vendors with no publishing presence.

## Impact If Solved
Subscription businesses direct investment from usage evidence and publishers have none for their central product decision. Adapting retention attribution to a catalogue of one-off items is the real work and applies to any content business.

# Ranked by Pageviews

**Niche:** [[niches/digital-native-publishers/content-value-measurement/profile|Content Value Measurement]]
**Industry:** [[industries/digital-native-publishers|Digital Native Publishers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The weekly content report ranks articles by traffic, and everyone in the room knows the ranking is wrong.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #quick-win #evaluation-metrics #revenue-impact #confidence-intervals #worker-facing #data-integration #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to say what a piece of journalism is actually worth to the business — and the contest splits cleanly enough that it is not terminal.

## The Problem
The report circulated every Monday lists the week's articles by pageviews. Everyone reading it knows a high-traffic aggregation piece that brought a thousand passing visitors is worth less than an investigation that produced forty subscriptions, and the report says the opposite. Editorial planning, praise, and sometimes compensation follow the ranking anyway, because it is the only ranking there is.

## Why It's Still Broken
The report came with the analytics tool, so the ranking is whatever that tool makes easy — a default view becomes the management instrument when nothing else exists. Better metrics require joining analytics to subscription data across systems. Nobody has defined what should replace it. And traffic targets are embedded in contracts and expectations.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Add the columns that exist. Add subscriptions started and retained by article to the report, which is the fix and is a join the publisher can run today. Show engaged reading time rather than only pageviews, since it is available and is a better proxy for value. Report the reader's second and third visit, because repeat reading is the leading indicator of a relationship and is the closest available substitute for causation. Break out by acquisition source, as traffic from a search result and from a newsletter behave completely differently. Report per desk and per beat, so resourcing conversations have evidence. Show the distribution rather than a top ten, because the top ten is always the same shape and hides everything. Flag the high-traffic, low-value pattern explicitly, since naming it changes how the room reads the report. Keep pageviews in the report rather than removing them, as an abrupt switch loses credibility and both numbers are informative. Say what the report cannot yet show, which is the causal question. And change what is celebrated in the weekly meeting, because that is where the metric actually operates.

## Who Feels the Pain
Reporters ranked by a metric the business has abandoned; editors planning from it; subscription teams whose contributors are invisible; and a business whose management instrument contradicts its strategy.

## Impact If Fixed
A default view becomes the management instrument when nothing else exists, so the ranking came with the tool. Adding subscription and repeat-reading columns is a join the publisher can run today and reorders the report immediately.

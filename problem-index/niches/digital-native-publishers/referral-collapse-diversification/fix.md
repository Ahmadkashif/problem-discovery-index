# Still Writing for the Search Result

**Niche:** [[niches/digital-native-publishers/referral-collapse-diversification/profile|Referral Collapse & Diversification]]
**Industry:** [[industries/digital-native-publishers|Digital Native Publishers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The publication is still producing the explainer content whose entire purpose was to capture a query that is now answered upstream.
**Tags:** #quick-win #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #revenue-impact #confidence-intervals #automation #worker-facing #time-series-forecasting
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to manage a structural decline in referral supply deliberately rather than reactively — and whoever forecasts it and converts the remaining traffic fastest arrives at the other side with an audience.

## The Problem
A whole genre of content exists because it captured search queries: the explainer, the definition page, the roundup, the how-to. Those queries are increasingly answered without a click. The content still gets commissioned, still consumes editorial capacity, and increasingly returns nothing — and the decision to keep producing it is made by inertia rather than by anyone examining whether it still works.

## Why It's Still Broken
The content format has a production process and a team attached, so it continues under its own momentum — a workflow that runs weekly does not stop because its rationale expired, it stops when someone measures it. The traffic decline is gradual and blends into the general decline. Nobody looks at performance by content format. And stopping a format means reassigning people, which is harder than continuing.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Measure by format and stop what has stopped working. Report traffic and conversion by content format over the last two years, which is the fix and will show which genres have decayed and which have held. Distinguish declining impressions from declining click-through, since a query that is now answered upstream shows a specific signature. Report conversion to direct relationship by format, because a format with falling traffic that converts well is worth keeping and one with the opposite is not. Compute the editorial cost per format, as the decision needs both sides and the cost is rarely computed. Stop the formats that fail on both measures, which is the decision the analysis exists to support. Reassign the capacity to formats that convert rather than absorbing it, since the point is redeployment and not reduction. Check whether the format still serves the direct audience, as some search-origin formats are genuinely useful to loyal readers and should be kept for that reason. Review formats quarterly rather than annually, given how fast the supply is moving. Tell the newsroom why, because a format stopped without explanation reads as a cut. And track what the redeployed capacity produced, so the decision can be evaluated.

## Who Feels the Pain
Reporters producing content that returns nothing; editors commissioning by inertia; audience teams watching a format decay; and publishers spending scarce editorial capacity on a channel being withdrawn.

## Impact If Fixed
A workflow that runs weekly does not stop because its rationale expired — it stops when someone measures it. Reporting traffic and conversion by content format over two years identifies the genres to stop and frees the capacity to redeploy.

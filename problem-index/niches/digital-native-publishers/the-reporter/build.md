# Measured on What Actually Matters

**Niche:** [[niches/digital-native-publishers/the-reporter/profile|The Reporter]]
**Industry:** [[industries/digital-native-publishers|Digital Native Publishers]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** The reporter's target is a number the business has publicly moved away from, and nobody has told them what replaced it.
**Tags:** #worker-facing #evaluation-metrics #causal-inference #confidence-intervals #revenue-impact #large-language-models #workflow-orchestration #descriptive-statistics
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to stop measuring reporters on a metric the business has already abandoned — and whoever replaces it with something that reflects contribution changes what the newsroom produces.

## The Problem
A reporter is asked for a number of stories a week and judged on the traffic they attract, in a business whose revenue has moved to subscriptions and whose leadership says so publicly. The reporter knows the deep piece that produced forty subscribers matters more than the aggregation that produced twenty thousand passing visits, and the system rewards the second. The incoherence is the demoralising part, and it is producing exactly the journalism the business says it does not want.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The traffic metric is the only one that exists at the article level, so it remained the target after the strategy changed — an organisation cannot stop measuring something until it has something else to measure. The replacement requires the causation work nobody has done. Traffic targets are in contracts and management routines. And changing how journalists are measured is politically fraught, which delays it further.

## What to Build
Replace the metric and support the work. Give reporters a contribution view — subscriptions influenced, retention contribution, engaged reading — which is the core and requires the causation work to be credible before it can be adopted. Report at the beat level as well as the article level, since individual attribution is noisy and beat-level is robust and actionable. Show the reporter their own effect rather than only reporting it upward, because that is what changes behaviour and what the role is currently denied. Measure the cost side too, so an expensive investigation can be evaluated properly rather than assumed to be indulgent. Support the research work — records, transcription, archive search, source management — as those hours are real and produce nothing visible. Make the publication's own archive usable to its own reporters, which is a small fix with a daily return. Set volume expectations against the actual supply rather than against the historical one, since asking for more stories into a shrinking referral base is the definition of a treadmill. Give reporters visibility of what their work earns, which is the connection to the business that the role has never had. Handle the uncertainty honestly, because a wrong contribution number will be rejected instantly and permanently. And change the target deliberately rather than running both, since two targets means the old one wins.

## Target Customer
Editorial leadership, reporters, publisher executives changing strategy, and newsroom analytics vendors shipping traffic dashboards.

## Impact If Built
An organisation cannot stop measuring something until it has something else to measure, so the traffic target outlived the strategy. A credible contribution view at beat level is what lets the newsroom be managed toward what the business actually needs.

# Investigative Tooling From Research Practice

**Niche:** [[niches/digital-native-publishers/the-reporter/profile|The Reporter]]
**Industry:** [[industries/digital-native-publishers|Digital Native Publishers]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Legal discovery and academic research built serious tooling for working through large document sets, and reporters use a folder and a text search.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #bert #workflow-orchestration #worker-facing #data-integration #automation #evaluation-metrics #graph-theory
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to stop measuring reporters on a metric the business has already abandoned — and whoever replaces it with something that reflects contribution changes what the newsroom produces.

## The Problem
Legal discovery platforms handle millions of documents with review workflows, entity extraction, relationship mapping and privilege logging. Academic research tooling manages sources, citations and notes across years of work. Both fields built this because working through large bodies of material is the job. Reporters do the same work — records requests, document sets, interview transcripts, source relationships — with a folder structure and whatever they can remember.

## What Already Exists
Discovery platforms with document review and entity extraction; relationship and network mapping; reference and source management; transcription with speaker identification; and structured note-taking with retrieval.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a reporter with no budget and an urgent deadline. It requires: (1) cost and setup near zero, since discovery platforms are priced for litigation and a newsroom will not buy one — this is the binding constraint; (2) source protection as a first-class requirement, where confidentiality is an ethical and sometimes legal obligation stronger than anything in academic practice; (3) work that must produce a published story on a deadline rather than a review or a paper; (4) material that arrives continuously in small amounts rather than as a production; and (5) reuse across stories over years, since a beat reporter's material compounds and nothing currently carries it forward.

## Target Customer
Editorial leadership and reporters, investigative units, newsroom tooling vendors, and discovery and research platform vendors with no media offering.

## Impact If Solved
Two adjacent fields built serious tooling for exactly this work, priced for litigation and academia. Source protection as a first-class requirement and near-zero cost are what a newsroom version must add.

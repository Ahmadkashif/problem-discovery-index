# The Traffic Target Nobody Believes In

**Niche:** [[niches/digital-native-publishers/the-reporter/profile|The Reporter]]
**Industry:** [[industries/digital-native-publishers|Digital Native Publishers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Everyone in the meeting knows the number on the board is the wrong number, and it is still on the board.
**Tags:** #worker-facing #quick-win #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #revenue-impact #confidence-intervals #compliance #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to stop measuring reporters on a metric the business has already abandoned — and whoever replaces it with something that reflects contribution changes what the newsroom produces.

## The Problem
Leadership tells the newsroom the business is now about subscriptions and durable audience. The weekly report still ranks by traffic, the quarterly review still cites pageview targets, and the reporters whose work is praised are the ones who hit them. The stated strategy and the operating metric contradict each other every week, which teaches everyone that the metric is what counts. The result is journalism optimised for a supply that is shrinking.

## Why It's Still Broken
Nobody has produced a replacement number, so the old one stays by default — a metric is not removed by a strategy announcement, only by another metric. Traffic is in contracts, targets and habits. The better measurement is genuinely harder. And leadership is reluctant to change how journalists are evaluated without being confident in the replacement.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Stop celebrating the wrong thing while the better measure is built. Add the available proxies — engaged reading time, return visits, subscription starts — to the same report, which is the fix and changes what the room discusses immediately. Stop ranking by traffic in the weekly meeting, since what is celebrated is the real metric regardless of what is written down. Say explicitly which numbers are directional and which are targets, because ambiguity means everyone assumes the familiar one. Report at beat level, which is robust enough to act on today. Remove traffic from individual evaluation before removing it from reporting, as the evaluation is where the harm is. Set volume expectations from the actual supply, since asking for more against a shrinking referral base is an unwinnable instruction. Tell reporters what the business needs from them in plain terms, which is currently communicated through a contradiction. Show reporters the subscription effect of their own work as it becomes available, because it is motivating and is the connection the role lacks. Commit to the timeline for the better measurement, so the interim is understood as interim. And be honest that the measure is imperfect, since a newsroom will forgive imperfection and not pretence.

## Who Feels the Pain
Reporters producing journalism the business says it does not want; editors managing to a contradiction; subscription teams whose contributors go unrecognised; and a newsroom whose morale erodes on the incoherence rather than the workload.

## Impact If Fixed
A metric is not removed by a strategy announcement, only by another metric, so traffic stayed by default. Adding available proxies and changing what the weekly meeting celebrates closes the contradiction before the harder measurement arrives.

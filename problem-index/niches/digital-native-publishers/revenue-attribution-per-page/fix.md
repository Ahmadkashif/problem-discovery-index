# Three Reports, Three Numbers

**Niche:** [[niches/digital-native-publishers/revenue-attribution-per-page/profile|Revenue Attribution per Page]]
**Industry:** [[industries/digital-native-publishers|Digital Native Publishers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The ad server, the exchange and the payment all report different impression counts and nobody reconciles them.
**Tags:** #data-integration #quick-win #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #revenue-impact #automation #confidence-intervals #compliance
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to say what a page actually earns after a chain of intermediaries has taken undisclosed margin — and whoever can follow the money gives publishers the first true unit economics they have ever had.

## The Problem
The publisher's ad server records a number of impressions. The exchange reports a different, lower number. The remittance corresponds to a third. Each discrepancy has a plausible technical explanation — timing, counting methodology, invalid traffic filtering — and each is accepted individually. In aggregate the discrepancies are a meaningful share of revenue, they are systematically in one direction, and nobody tracks whether they are growing.

## Why It's Still Broken
Discrepancies are explained away individually as technical differences, so they never aggregate into a finding — a gap that always has a plausible reason never becomes a question. Reconciliation requires pulling several reports into one place monthly and nobody owns it. The publisher's leverage to challenge is limited. And the amounts per partner look small.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Reconcile monthly and track the gap. Pull every partner's reported numbers alongside the ad server's and compute the discrepancy per partner per month, which is the fix and is a monthly spreadsheet at worst. Track the trend, since a stable discrepancy is a methodology difference and a growing one is a problem. Rank partners by discrepancy rate, which will produce a short list and a set of conversations. Ask for the explanation, because many publishers never have and the request alone changes behaviour. Reconcile the payment to the report as well as the report to the log, as that second gap is separate and also unexamined. Quantify the total in money rather than in percentage points, since the aggregate is what makes it worth someone's attention. Check invalid traffic deductions specifically, as they are the most common explanation and the least verified. Include the fee disclosure where contractually available, which many publishers have a right to and do not exercise. Assign the reconciliation to someone, because it is nobody's job today. And use the result in renewal negotiations, which is where the analysis pays.

## Who Feels the Pain
Finance teams accepting numbers they cannot verify; revenue teams negotiating without evidence; publishers whose margin leaks invisibly; and an industry where opacity is the default because nobody checks.

## Impact If Fixed
A gap that always has a plausible reason never becomes a question, so discrepancies are explained away one at a time. Computing them per partner per month and tracking the trend converts an accepted cost into a negotiating position.

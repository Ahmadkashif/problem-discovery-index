# Following the Money to the Page

**Niche:** [[niches/digital-native-publishers/revenue-attribution-per-page/profile|Revenue Attribution per Page]]
**Industry:** [[industries/digital-native-publishers|Digital Native Publishers]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** The publisher cannot say what any individual page earned, which means it cannot say whether any individual page was worth making.
**Tags:** #data-integration #evaluation-metrics #revenue-impact #descriptive-statistics #confidence-intervals #automation #graph-theory #compliance
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to say what a page actually earns after a chain of intermediaries has taken undisclosed margin — and whoever can follow the money gives publishers the first true unit economics they have ever had.

## The Problem
A page generates advertising revenue through a chain of intermediaries whose fees are partly undisclosed, contributes to subscriptions in ways nobody attributes, may be licensed, and may drive commerce. The publisher receives aggregate statements that do not reconcile with each other or with its own ad server logs, and computes revenue per thousand pageviews at the site level. Whether a given article, beat or format earns more than it costs is not a question the finance system can answer.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The supply chain was designed to be opaque and the publisher is the least powerful party in it, so aggregate reporting was accepted as the terms of trade — a counterparty who benefits from opacity will not supply the data voluntarily. Page-level attribution requires joining ad server logs to exchange reports to content records, which nobody funded. Non-advertising revenue is not allocated at all. And the cost side has never been measured either.

## What to Build
Reconcile the chain and allocate everything to the page. Join ad server logs, exchange and intermediary reports and content records to produce page-level advertising revenue, which is the core and is the first honest unit number. Measure the intermediary take by comparing what buyers report spending to what the publisher receives, since that gap is the supply chain's undisclosed margin and publishers have run this analysis rarely and found it large. Reconcile the discrepancies between reports rather than accepting whichever is convenient, because the differences are material and systematic. Allocate subscription contribution to pages using the causation work, so the two halves combine into one number. Include licensing and commerce earnings, which are growing and are never attributed. Compute the cost per page including editorial time, as unit economics requires both sides and nobody has the denominator. Report contribution by beat and format, which is the actionable level. Detect the pages and formats that lose money, since they exist in volume and are invisible. Use the reconciliation to negotiate with intermediaries, as evidence of leakage is commercial leverage. And report the supply chain's total take as a line item, because a cost nobody measures is never managed.

## Target Customer
Revenue and finance leadership, publisher trade bodies, advertisers concerned about supply chain leakage, and supply path optimisation vendors.

## Impact If Built
A counterparty who benefits from opacity will not supply the data voluntarily, so aggregate reporting became the terms of trade. Reconciling logs against intermediary reports produces the first page-level unit economics and quantifies the take nobody measures.

# Dependency Risk From Channel Management

**Niche:** [[niches/digital-native-publishers/the-audience-editor/profile|The Audience Editor]]
**Industry:** [[industries/digital-native-publishers|Digital Native Publishers]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Businesses that depend on a single channel treat it as a concentration risk with a mitigation plan, and publishers treat it as a job title.
**Tags:** #confidence-intervals #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #revenue-impact #time-series-forecasting #compliance #worker-facing #change-point-detection
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to make someone accountable for traffic from platforms that change without notice and are actively reducing supply — and whoever gives that person evidence instead of folklore changes what the role can do.

## The Problem
Distribution dependency is a known business risk with known management practice: measure the concentration, model the loss scenario, build alternative channels before they are needed, and hold the exposure at a board level rather than in an operating team. Retailers, manufacturers and software companies all do it. Publishers concentrated most of their audience acquisition in two or three platforms and managed it as an operational responsibility assigned to one editor.

## What Already Exists
Concentration risk measurement; scenario and stress analysis for channel loss; diversification programme management; channel economics comparison; and board-level risk reporting.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a channel that is free, involuntary and deliberately shrinking. It requires: (1) a counterparty with no commercial relationship and no obligation to notify, unlike a retail buyer or a distributor — this is the substantive difference and removes every normal mitigation; (2) supply that is being reduced by design rather than lost to competition, so scenario planning must assume continued decline; (3) an alternative channel that must be built from the existing audience rather than bought, which takes years; (4) the exposure measured in audience rather than in revenue directly, requiring a translation nobody makes; and (5) an industry where every participant has the same exposure, which makes collective action relevant and rare.

## Target Customer
Publisher executives and boards, audience leadership, trade bodies, and strategy and risk advisors serving media.

## Impact If Solved
Channel dependency is a managed risk everywhere else and here it is a job title. Treating a deliberately shrinking, non-contractual channel as a board-level exposure is the adaptation, and it changes who owns the problem.

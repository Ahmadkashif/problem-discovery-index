# Evidence Instead of Forum Posts

**Niche:** [[niches/digital-native-publishers/the-audience-editor/profile|The Audience Editor]]
**Industry:** [[industries/digital-native-publishers|Digital Native Publishers]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** The person accountable for platform traffic diagnoses the platform from rumours and a dashboard that reports the fall after it happened.
**Tags:** #change-point-detection #causal-inference #time-series-forecasting #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #worker-facing #gradient-boosting #hypothesis-testing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to make someone accountable for traffic from platforms that change without notice and are actively reducing supply — and whoever gives that person evidence instead of folklore changes what the role can do.

## The Problem
Traffic drops thirty percent. Was it a ranking update, a change in what the platform shows, a seasonal effect, a competitor, a technical problem on the publisher's own site, or a shift in what people are searching for? The audience editor answers from a dashboard that shows the fall, industry chatter, and whatever they can piece together. They are accountable for the outcome and have no instrument for diagnosing it, in a supply that is structurally shrinking.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The platforms do not explain and the analytics vendors report what the platforms return, so the whole toolchain stops at description — an ecosystem where the upstream party discloses nothing produces downstream tools that describe rather than diagnose. Attribution requires comparing against a baseline nobody constructs. The role is staffed rather than tooled. And each publisher sees only its own traffic, which is the wrong unit for detecting a platform change.

## What to Build
Give the role diagnosis and forecasting. Detect changes against a modelled baseline rather than against last week, which is the core and separates an ordinary fluctuation from an event. Attribute a change to a plausible cause — platform, seasonality, competition, own-site technical, content mix — since the diagnosis determines the response and is currently guesswork. Compare against a peer set where data can be pooled, because a change affecting many publishers simultaneously is a platform change and that is only visible collectively. Monitor own-site technical health as a candidate cause, as a meaningful share of mystery traffic drops turn out to be self-inflicted. Forecast the declining base honestly, so targets are set against reality rather than against last year. Detect query and topic demand shifts, since what people search for changes and the publisher notices only through the traffic. Track which content is being answered upstream rather than clicked, which is the defining change of the era and nobody measures it directly. Quantify the dependency explicitly, as a channel supplying most of the traffic is a board-level exposure. Support the diversification decision with evidence about what each channel is actually worth. And give the role a defensible account of what happened, because being accountable without an explanation is the job's central indignity.

## Target Customer
Audience leadership and audience editors, publisher executives setting targets, analytics vendors reporting platform traffic, and trade bodies who could pool the data.

## Impact If Built
An ecosystem where the upstream party discloses nothing produces downstream tools that describe rather than diagnose. Change detection against a modelled baseline, with a peer comparison, is what separates a platform event from the publisher's own doing.

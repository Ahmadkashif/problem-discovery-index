# Performance Engineering Practice

**Niche:** [[niches/digital-native-publishers/advertising-stack-operations/profile|Advertising Stack Operations]]
**Industry:** [[industries/digital-native-publishers|Digital Native Publishers]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Ecommerce established that page performance moves revenue and built a discipline around it, and publishers ship an ad stack that fights it.
**Tags:** #evaluation-metrics #causal-inference #confidence-intervals #hypothesis-testing #revenue-impact #optimization-fundamentals #automation #descriptive-statistics
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to run a deep, leaky ad stack that funds the newsroom without destroying the page experience that retains readers — and whoever manages that trade with evidence rather than instinct keeps both revenue and audience.

## The Problem
Ecommerce demonstrated conclusively that page speed affects conversion and revenue, and built a performance engineering discipline in response: budgets, monitoring, regression gates in deployment, and a culture in which shipping a slow page is a defect. Publishers have the same evidence available about return visits and subscription conversion, and ship third-party scripts whose performance nobody gates.

## What Already Exists
Performance budgets and regression gates; real user monitoring; third-party script impact measurement; conversion-to-performance elasticity studies; and deployment practices that block regressions.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to third-party code the publisher does not control and needs. It requires: (1) scripts the business depends on for revenue, so the remedy cannot be removal and must be a measured trade — this is the substantive difference from a first-party performance programme; (2) the outcome being return visits and subscription rather than immediate conversion, which is measurable but over weeks rather than in a session; (3) ad operations as the owner of the regressions, a team with no performance mandate and an opposing metric; (4) partners added for revenue reasons with no performance review, so the gate must be commercial as well as technical; and (5) revenue attribution to performance that no publisher has established, which is the missing evidence the whole discipline rests on.

## Target Customer
Revenue operations, engineering and audience leadership, header bidding vendors, and performance monitoring vendors with no publisher specialisation.

## Impact If Solved
Ecommerce made performance a discipline by proving it moves revenue, and publishers have never established the equivalent for return visits. Gating third-party additions on a measured audience cost is what the practice would look like here.

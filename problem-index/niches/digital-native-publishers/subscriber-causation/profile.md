# Subscriber Causation

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/digital-native-publishers|Digital Native Publishers]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to identify which journalism causes a reader to become a subscriber and stay one — and whoever separates that from the articles that merely attract readers who were already going to subscribe changes where every publisher spends its editorial budget.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$2.2B US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~16% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Very Low — not attempted
**Target Buyer:** Data and editorial leadership
**Automation Potential:** Very High — causal inference over a complete record

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
This contest is causal inference about readers. Given the full reading history of everyone who subscribed, lapsed or disappeared, determine which articles actually contributed. The difficulty is that popular articles attract different readers, that subscription follows a chain of reading rather than a single piece, and that the last article before a conversion takes credit it did not earn. It is a methodological problem over the publisher's own data and has nothing in common with tracing advertising revenue through intermediaries.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Last-touch attribution where anything exists at all, conversion funnels and subscription counts. The gaps: selection bias unaddressed; the reading chain collapsed to a last touch; retention contribution never measured; and no counterfactual of any kind.

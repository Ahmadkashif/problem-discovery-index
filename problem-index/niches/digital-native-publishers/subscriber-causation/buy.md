# Attribution From Marketing Science

**Niche:** [[niches/digital-native-publishers/subscriber-causation/profile|Subscriber Causation]]
**Industry:** [[industries/digital-native-publishers|Digital Native Publishers]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Marketing science spent twenty years learning that last-touch attribution is wrong and building alternatives, and publishers attribute subscriptions to the last article.
**Tags:** #causal-inference #hypothesis-testing #confidence-intervals #evaluation-metrics #monte-carlo-methods #gradient-boosting #descriptive-statistics #survival-analysis
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to identify which journalism causes a reader to become a subscriber and stay one — and whoever separates that from the articles that merely attract readers who were already going to subscribe changes where every publisher spends its editorial budget.

## The Problem
Marketing attribution went through this exact argument and largely settled it: last-touch systematically over-credits the final interaction, multi-touch models are better but still correlational, and incrementality experiments are the only reliable evidence. The field produced methods, tooling and a professional consensus. Publishers attributing subscriptions to journalism are at the beginning of the same road and are using the method the other field abandoned.

## What Already Exists
Multi-touch attribution models; incrementality and holdout testing; media mix modelling; conversion path analysis; and the methodological critique of last-touch.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to touchpoints that are editorial rather than promotional. It requires: (1) touchpoints the reader chose rather than ones the business placed, which makes selection bias far more severe than in advertising where exposure is assigned — this is the substantive difference and is the whole methodological problem; (2) a holdout that means withholding journalism, which raises editorial objections a marketing holdout does not; (3) thousands of heterogeneous touchpoints rather than a handful of channels, so channel-level models do not transfer; (4) a conversion window measured in months of habit formation; and (5) an outcome including retention rather than only acquisition, which marketing attribution usually stops short of.

## Target Customer
Data and subscription leadership, editorial leadership, publisher analytics vendors, and marketing attribution vendors with no publishing presence.

## Impact If Solved
Marketing settled the last-touch argument twenty years ago and publishers are still using it. The adaptation is that readers choose their own exposure, which makes selection bias the central problem rather than a footnote.

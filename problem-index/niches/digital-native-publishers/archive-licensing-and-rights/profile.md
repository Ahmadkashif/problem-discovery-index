# Archive Licensing & Rights

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/digital-native-publishers|Digital Native Publishers]]
**Category:** 🟠 Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to know what its archive is worth, who is using it, and on what terms — and whoever can enumerate and price that asset negotiates from evidence rather than from a position of ignorance.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.5B US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~11% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Very Low — no rights infrastructure
**Target Buyer:** Business development and legal
**Automation Potential:** High — inventory, provenance and detection

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The archive turned out to be an asset. It is training data for the systems now intermediating the publisher's audience, it is licensable to several kinds of buyer, and it is being used in ways the publisher cannot enumerate. Meanwhile the publisher frequently cannot say what it owns — which pieces have freelance rights limitations, which images are licensed for one use, which content was syndicated — and is negotiating a valuable asset without an inventory of it.

## Current Tools & Gaps
A content management system, contracts in a drive, and an ad hoc licensing conversation. The gaps: no rights inventory; freelance and image terms unrecorded in usable form; usage undetected; the archive not valued; and no basis for pricing a licence.

# Paywall & Access Policy

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/digital-native-publishers|Digital Native Publishers]]
**Category:** 🟠 Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to decide who sees what for free, when, and at what price — and whoever measures the readers a paywall turns away as well as the subscribers it converts optimises the whole thing rather than half of it.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.8B US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~13% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Low — a rule tuned on conversion
**Target Buyer:** Subscription leadership
**Automation Potential:** Very High — a per-reader decision

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The paywall is a rule about how many free articles a reader gets, tuned on conversion rate, with no visibility into the readers it turns away or the subscribers it acquires who leave in three months. It is the single highest-leverage policy in a subscription publisher, it applies uniformly to a wildly heterogeneous audience, and both of its costs — lost readers and low-quality subscribers — are invisible in the metric it is optimised on.

## Current Tools & Gaps
A meter, a set of rules, some segment exceptions and a conversion dashboard. The gaps: turned-away readers never measured; subscriber quality by paywall path unexamined; the meter uniform across a heterogeneous audience; pricing rarely tested; and access changes made without experiments.

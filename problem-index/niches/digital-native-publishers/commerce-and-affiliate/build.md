# A Commerce Line Run as a Business

**Niche:** [[niches/digital-native-publishers/commerce-and-affiliate/profile|Commerce & Affiliate]]
**Industry:** [[industries/digital-native-publishers|Digital Native Publishers]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A growing revenue line is managed with a link plugin and a spreadsheet, and it trades against the only asset the publisher has.
**Tags:** #gradient-boosting #evaluation-metrics #revenue-impact #confidence-intervals #causal-inference #automation #data-integration #descriptive-statistics
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to run a commerce line that earns without costing the trust the journalism depends on — and whoever measures both sides of that trade runs it as a business rather than as a side project.

## The Problem
Commerce content earns meaningfully and is operated casually. Links rot, products are discontinued, commission rates change without anyone noticing, and recommendations written three years ago still sit at the top of search results advising readers to buy things that are no longer sold. Meanwhile every commerce placement spends a small amount of reader trust, and nobody has measured whether the aggregate is costing more in subscription and return visits than the commissions are worth.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Commerce started as an experiment that worked, so it never acquired an operating model — a revenue line that grew by accident is run by whoever started it and inherits no discipline. Affiliate networks report commission and nothing else. The trust cost is diffuse and unmeasured, which means it loses every argument. And the editorial and commerce teams have different incentives and no shared metric.

## What to Build
Operate it properly and price the trust. Monitor link health and product availability continuously, which is the core and is the most basic operational failure — a recommendation pointing at a discontinued product is worse than no recommendation. Measure conversion by placement, format and content type, since the variation is large and nothing is optimised. Refresh recommendations on a schedule, because the top-ranked buying guide from three years ago is an active liability. Reconcile commission against tracked conversions, as affiliate reporting is opaque in the same way advertising reporting is and is checked even less. Measure the trust cost experimentally — a holdout on commerce placement, measured on return visits and subscription conversion — because that is the trade and it is currently assumed to be free. Keep the recommendation quality genuinely high, since the business depends on readers believing the advice and the incentive runs the other way. Separate editorial and commercial recommendation clearly, which is both an ethical and a commercial necessity. Report revenue net of the estimated trust cost, so the trade is visible to whoever decides. Track which products readers actually bought and were satisfied with where possible, as that is the feedback loop the recommendations need. And review the commerce content inventory as an asset with a maintenance requirement rather than as a set of published pages.

## Target Customer
Commerce and editorial leadership, publisher executives balancing revenue and trust, affiliate networks, and readers taking the advice.

## Impact If Built
A revenue line that grew by accident is run by whoever started it and inherits no discipline. Link and availability monitoring is the basic fix, and a holdout on commerce placement is the only way to price the trust it spends.

# The Link to a Product That No Longer Exists

**Niche:** [[niches/digital-native-publishers/commerce-and-affiliate/profile|Commerce & Affiliate]]
**Industry:** [[industries/digital-native-publishers|Digital Native Publishers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The top-ranked buying guide recommends a model discontinued two years ago and earns nothing while telling readers to buy it.
**Tags:** #quick-win #automation #data-integration #evaluation-metrics #revenue-impact #descriptive-statistics #confidence-intervals #workflow-orchestration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to run a commerce line that earns without costing the trust the journalism depends on — and whoever measures both sides of that trade runs it as a business rather than as a side project.

## The Problem
Commerce content ranks well and persists. The products in it do not. A guide published two years ago sits at the top of the results, recommends items that are discontinued, links that redirect to a homepage or a dead page, and prices that are wrong. It earns little because the links no longer convert, and it costs a reader's trust every time someone follows the advice and finds it stale. Nobody is checking, because checking is nobody's job.

## Why It's Still Broken
Commerce content is published like editorial and editorial does not require maintenance, so the format inherited a publish-and-forget model — an article is finished when it ships and a product recommendation never is. Link checking is not part of any workflow. The decay is invisible in aggregate revenue. And the affiliate network reports commission rather than link health.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Check the links and schedule the refresh. Monitor every commerce link for availability and redirect behaviour, which is the fix and is a scheduled job that catches most of the problem. Check product availability and price where the retailer's data permits, since a discontinued product is the worst case and is detectable. Report the top-traffic commerce pages against their revenue, as the pages that rank and earn nothing are the priority list and it will be short. Schedule refreshes for high-traffic evergreen guides, because they are assets with a maintenance requirement and are treated as finished articles. Flag pages whose conversion has collapsed, which usually means the links or the products have. Show the publication date and last review date to the reader, as that is honest and reduces the trust cost of an out-of-date recommendation. Remove or update recommendations that are no longer good, since continuing to recommend a discontinued product is the clearest possible failure. Reconcile commission against expected conversions so silent tracking failures are caught. Assign ownership of the commerce inventory, because the absence of an owner is the root cause. And measure revenue per commerce page over time, which makes the decay visible.

## Who Feels the Pain
Readers following stale advice; publishers earning nothing from their best-ranked commerce pages; editorial teams whose credibility is spent by unmaintained content; and commerce teams who cannot see where the revenue went.

## Impact If Fixed
An article is finished when it ships and a product recommendation never is, so commerce content inherited a publish-and-forget model. Scheduled link and availability checking catches most of it, and ranking traffic against revenue produces a short, high-value fix list.

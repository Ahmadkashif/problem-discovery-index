## The Problem
Retail merchandising is a developed practice: assortment selection, availability management, placement and performance analysis, margin management, and a discipline about removing products that no longer sell or no longer exist. Publisher commerce content is functionally a store — a set of product recommendations with placement and conversion — and it is merchandised by nobody.

## What Already Exists
Assortment planning; availability and out-of-stock management; placement and conversion analysis; margin and mix management; and product lifecycle and retirement practice.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a store whose shelves are articles. It requires: (1) inventory that is editorial content with an independent reason to exist, so a low-converting recommendation may still be good journalism — this is the substantive tension and merchandising has no concept of it; (2) products the publisher does not stock and cannot control, so availability must be monitored externally; (3) placement determined by editorial judgement and search ranking rather than by a merchandiser; (4) a trust constraint that caps how commercial the assortment can become, which no retailer faces in the same way; and (5) commission rates set by counterparties and changed without notice, unlike owned margin.

## Target Customer
Commerce and editorial leadership, affiliate networks, publisher executives, and retail merchandising vendors with no publisher offering.

## Impact If Solved
Publisher commerce is a store nobody merchandises. Importing availability management and placement analysis is straightforward; the new constraint is that a low-converting recommendation may still be the right editorial answer.

# The Referrer Who Always Means an Arthrogram

**Niche:** [[niches/diagnostic-imaging-centers/exam-protocolling/profile|Exam Protocolling]]
**Industry:** [[industries/diagnostic-imaging-centers|Diagnostic Imaging Centers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Every senior protocoller knows which referrers mean something other than what they write; the knowledge lives in their heads and in hallway notes.
**Tags:** #k-nearest-neighbors #large-language-models #tacit-knowledge-ml #quick-win #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to attach the right protocol to every order before the patient arrives, and whoever cuts radiologist protocolling minutes per order most, with no rise in protocol-attributable repeats or recalls, takes the account.

## The Problem
An orthopaedic surgeon orders "MRI shoulder" and always wants an arthrogram. An oncology practice writes "pain" and means a staging CT. A new radiologist or a weekend technologist does not know this, protocols as written, and the patient comes back.

## Why It's Still Broken
Referrer preferences are never recorded as data. They pass by word of mouth and leave with the people who know them.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Show, at protocolling, the referrer's last 20 orders of the same type and how they were protocolled and amended. Let radiologists pin a preference note that appears on every future order from that referrer.

## Who Feels the Pain
New radiologists and technologists; referrers whose patients are re-scanned; patients making a second trip.

## Impact If Fixed
A simple retrieval view could prevent a quarter or more of referrer-preference callbacks from day one (estimate) and keeps senior protocollers' knowledge when they leave.

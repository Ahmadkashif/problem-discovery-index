# Aviation Checklists and Remote Supervision for the Scan Room

**Niche:** [[niches/diagnostic-imaging-centers/mri-ct-technologists/profile|The MRI & CT Technologist]]
**Industry:** [[industries/diagnostic-imaging-centers|Diagnostic Imaging Centers]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Cockpit checklists and remote expert support let one pilot or field technician handle rare situations safely; a lone evening technologist needs the same.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #workflow-orchestration #worker-facing #compliance #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to let one technologist run a scanner safely through a full shift, and whoever lets a single technologist complete the most exams per shift with no rise in repeat scans or safety events takes the account.

## The Problem
Technologists working alone face uncommon events (contrast reactions, unclear implants, unusual protocols) with only a binder and a phone.

## What Already Exists
Electronic checklists and remote-expert video support in aviation and field service, and the imaging OEMs' remote-scanning cockpits that let a senior technologist operate or guide scanners at other sites.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs checklists built around ACR contrast and MR safety guidance and the centre's own protocols. It must route to a radiologist or senior technologist with the patient's screening form, implant sheet and current images in view. Events have to be documented for accreditation and incident review. It must work across mixed scanner fleets, not one OEM's.

## Target Customer
Operators running evening and weekend scanners with single technologist staffing.

## Impact If Solved
Remote support lets centres keep more evening and weekend scanners open with one technologist each. Each added hour of scanner time is worth several exams a week (estimate).

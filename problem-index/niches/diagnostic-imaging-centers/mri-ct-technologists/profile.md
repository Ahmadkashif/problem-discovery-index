# The MRI & CT Technologist

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/diagnostic-imaging-centers|Diagnostic Imaging Centers]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to let one technologist run a scanner safely through a full shift, and whoever lets a single technologist complete the most exams per shift with no rise in repeat scans or safety events takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$0.8B a year of technologist-facing tools: MR safety and implant software, scan-assist and remote-scanning products, protocol libraries, training and credentialing support (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~4% of centre revenue (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — the scanner console is sophisticated, but safety screening, implant research, contrast checks and documentation around it are paper forms, binders and phone calls
**Target Buyer:** Director of imaging operations or chief technologist at a multi-site operator
**Automation Potential:** Medium — screening, implant lookup and scan set-up are automatable; positioning, IV access and patient care are not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Technologists are the centre's binding labour constraint. Vacancy rates reached 19.4% for CT and 17.4% for MRI in 2025 (ASRT). BLS counts 7,940 MRI technologists and 16,650 radiologic technologists in medical and diagnostic laboratories, which include imaging centres (May 2023). Yet the AI and software spend in radiology goes overwhelmingly to the radiologist's read. The technologist screens each patient, researches implants, starts IVs, chooses and adjusts sequences, repeats views that moved, and documents it all, often alone on evenings and weekends. OEMs now sell remote scanning, where a senior technologist operates scanners at several sites from one cockpit (recalled). That signals a market for multiplying scarce technologist skill. The contest is exams per technologist shift without more repeats or safety events.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Scanner consoles with OEM protocol trees and AI reconstruction, remote-scanning products from the OEMs, MR safety reference sites and manufacturers' labelling, paper screening forms, RIS technologist notes. Gaps: implant clearance done by hand at the scanner; repeats caught only by the radiologist later; no record of in-scan protocol changes or their reasons.

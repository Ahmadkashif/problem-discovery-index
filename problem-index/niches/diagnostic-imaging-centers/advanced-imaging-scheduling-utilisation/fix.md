# The Afternoon Gap Nobody Phoned to Fill

**Niche:** [[niches/diagnostic-imaging-centers/advanced-imaging-scheduling-utilisation/profile|Advanced Imaging Scheduling & Scanner Utilisation]]
**Industry:** [[industries/diagnostic-imaging-centers|Diagnostic Imaging Centers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A cancellation at 11:00 leaves a 3:00 MRI slot open, and it stays open because the waitlist is a sticky note and the scheduler is on the phone with new orders.
**Tags:** #gradient-boosting #logistic-regression #large-language-models #automation #quick-win #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn each MRI and CT referral into a completed, paid exam in the next open scanner slot, and whoever completes the most paid exams per scanner hour takes the account.

## The Problem
Same-day cancellations and no-shows open slots every day. Patients who would come at short notice exist, people booked weeks out, but nobody knows who they are, whether their authorization is valid today, or whether they have already been called. The slot is lost.

## Why It's Still Broken
Waitlists live outside the RIS, and authorization status lives in another screen. Calling down a list is the lowest-priority task for staff who are also answering new-order calls.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A ranked same-day fill list built from future bookings. Each patient is scored on willingness to move up (distance, past acceptance, flexibility noted at booking) and filtered to valid authorization and completed screening. Texts or voice-agent calls go out automatically, and the first to accept is booked into the gap.

## Who Feels the Pain
Schedulers juggling new orders and gaps; technologists standing idle and then running late; patients waiting weeks for a slot that opens every afternoon.

## Impact If Fixed
Filling half of a centre's same-day gaps, often one or two per magnet per day, is worth about $100,000 a year per scanner (estimate). It also shortens wait times referrers notice.

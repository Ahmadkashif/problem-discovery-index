# The Peer-Review Results the Centre Never Sees

**Niche:** [[niches/diagnostic-imaging-centers/teleradiology-reading-coverage/profile|Teleradiology & Outsourced Reading Coverage]]
**Industry:** [[industries/diagnostic-imaging-centers|Diagnostic Imaging Centers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A reading firm runs peer review on its own radiologists, but the centre buying the reads never sees discrepancy rates for its own studies, so a quality threshold in the contract cannot be enforced.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #hypothesis-testing #workflow-orchestration #quick-win #compliance
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to read the evening, weekend, subspecialty and overflow studies a centre's own radiologists cannot cover, and whoever covers the highest share of the centre's after-hours and overflow studies with peer-review major-discrepancy rates under the contract threshold takes the account.

## The Problem
When the centre's own radiologists open a prior that an outside reader reported overnight, they sometimes disagree. Those disagreements are logged, if at all, in the reading firm's internal peer-review system or in an email. The centre cannot tell whether its outsourced reads are better or worse than its own.

## Why It's Still Broken
Peer review is run for accreditation and internal quality, and the results are treated as protected by each group. Contracts state a quality expectation but name no shared measure.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A per-contract discrepancy log. Every disagreement found on comparison or sampled re-read is graded major or minor and attributed to study type and reader. A monthly report shows the centre its coverage share and major-discrepancy rate against the contract threshold, with corrective actions listed.

## Who Feels the Pain
Centre medical directors answering referrers about missed findings; reading firms losing contracts over cases they cannot put in context.

## Impact If Fixed
A shared log turns quality from anecdote into a rate within one quarter. If a few major discrepancies a month concentrate in one subspecialty (estimate), the report shows exactly which coverage to change before renewal.

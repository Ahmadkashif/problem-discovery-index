# Field-Service Route Optimisation for Scanner Trailers

**Niche:** [[niches/diagnostic-imaging-centers/mobile-imaging-operators/profile|Mobile Imaging Operators & Rural Shared Units]]
**Industry:** [[industries/diagnostic-imaging-centers|Diagnostic Imaging Centers]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Field-service software already routes technicians and equipment across sites under time windows; a scanner trailer is a technician with a very large, very fragile toolbox.
**Tags:** #optimization-fundamentals #time-series-forecasting #automation #data-integration #workflow-orchestration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep each mobile MRI, CT or PET unit scanning on the days it is parked at a host site, and whoever fills the highest share of booked unit-days with completed, paid scans takes the account.

## The Problem
Mobile operators plan routes in spreadsheets and rebook by phone when a unit breaks down or snow closes a road.

## What Already Exists
Field-service management platforms schedule crews and equipment across customer sites with time windows, skills and travel, analysed under [[industries/field-service-software|Field Service Software]].

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs a pad and power hook-up at each host and set-up time per modality. Staff skills have to be expressed as state technologist licences and modality credentials. Helium, cryogen and quality-control schedules need to be modelled as maintenance windows. It needs host patient bookings as the demand to protect, and IDTF billing status (operator-billed or under arrangement) per host.

## Target Customer
Mobile imaging operators with multi-state fleets.

## Impact If Solved
Configuring an existing routing engine replaces spreadsheet planning in weeks. Faster rebooking after a breakdown can save a day of scans per incident (estimate).

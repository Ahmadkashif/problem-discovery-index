# A Licensing Room for Cost-Shared Project Shoots

**Niche:** [[niches/commercial-photographers/architectural-interiors-photography/profile|Architectural & Interiors Photography]]
**Industry:** [[industries/commercial-photographers|Commercial Photographers]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A finished building has eight firms who want its photographs and one photographer coordinating them by email, so most of them never join the cost-share and many use the images anyway.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #contrastive-learning #k-nearest-neighbors #workflow-orchestration #revenue-impact #compliance
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to license one shoot to every party on a project (architect, contractor, interior designer, product makers) on terms each can see and none can stretch, and whoever makes the cost-share easy to join and hard to leak takes the account.

## The Problem
The architect commissions the shoot and invites the contractor and the interior designer to share costs. The lighting maker, the façade supplier, the furniture brand and the engineer hear about it later, or simply see the images in a magazine. Each has to be approached separately, sent a licence and invoiced. Shot priorities conflict: the lighting maker wants its fixtures featured, the contractor wants the structure. Most parties never formally join.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The market is small and fragmented, a few thousand architectural photographers (estimate), and the coordination problem spans several companies with no shared system. Gallery platforms are built for one photographer and one client.

## What to Build
A project room the photographer opens before the shoot. The project's parties are identified from the architect's credits list and invited automatically. Each sees the planned shot list, can request shots and joins at a published price per tier. After delivery, each party downloads only what it licensed, with terms embedded in the files. Image matching flags project images appearing on the websites and social accounts of parties who haven't joined, with a one-click licence offer.

## Target Customer
Architectural and interiors photographers, and the marketing directors at architecture and design firms who commission them.

## Impact If Built
More parties pay their share of each shoot, the commissioning firm's cost falls, and the photographer's revenue per project rises, plausibly by a third (estimate), without a single awkward email.

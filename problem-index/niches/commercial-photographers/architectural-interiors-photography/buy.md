# Client Gallery Platforms Adapted to Multi-Party Licences

**Niche:** [[niches/commercial-photographers/architectural-interiors-photography/profile|Architectural & Interiors Photography]]
**Industry:** [[industries/commercial-photographers|Commercial Photographers]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Photographers already deliver through galleries that handle proofing, downloads and payments; those galleries assume one client per job, while an architectural shoot has five.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #data-integration #workflow-orchestration #compliance #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to license one shoot to every party on a project (architect, contractor, interior designer, product makers) on terms each can see and none can stretch, and whoever makes the cost-share easy to join and hard to leak takes the account.

## The Problem
The photographer delivers a project gallery to the architect, who forwards the link to the contractor, who downloads everything, including images the contractor did not license and images the architect licensed only for awards.

## What Already Exists
Client gallery and delivery platforms (PhotoShelter, Pixieset, ShootProof, Pic-Time) offer branded galleries, download permissions, watermarking and e-commerce for print sales or image purchases.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs: per-party access within one project gallery, each party seeing the full set but downloading only what it licensed; licence tiers by use (marketing, awards, publication, advertising) with prices; embedded rights metadata (licensee, uses, credit line) written into each downloaded file; credit-line requirements for publications; a request flow for additional parties and uses; and an audit trail of who downloaded what.

## Target Customer
Architectural, interiors and hospitality photographers who run cost-shared projects.

## Impact If Solved
Forwarded links stop leaking unlicensed downloads, extra parties license through a self-serve page, and the photographer has a record to point to when an image turns up where it shouldn't.

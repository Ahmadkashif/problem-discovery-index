# Small Brands Commissioning Product Content

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/commercial-photographers|Commercial Photographers]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to prove that a set of images lifts the listing's conversion on the seller's own marketplace data, and whoever can show the lift per dollar spent takes the seller.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$400M in US product photography bought by small ecommerce brands and marketplace sellers, with fewer than ~200 SKUs each (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~9% of commercial photography revenue (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** High — ordered online, samples shipped in, images delivered as downloads
**Target Buyer:** Founder or ecommerce manager at a small brand; productised studio's head of growth on the selling side
**Automation Potential:** High — briefs, shot templates and testing are all automatable

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Small brands selling on Amazon, Shopify or Etsy (the businesses analysed under [[industries/ecommerce-sellers|E-Commerce Sellers]] and [[industries/d2c-brand-operators|D2C Brand Operators]]) need marketplace-compliant main images, a few lifestyle shots and infographics. They can't afford an agency shoot and don't know how to brief one. Productised studios (Soona from ~$39 a photo, Squareshot from ~$50 an image, often with unlimited usage included) sell to them online. Generative tools offer free alternatives for secondary images. This buyer does not judge images by craft. They judge by whether sales move, and they can measure it: marketplaces offer split tests of main images and listing content. The commercial photographer who can show the lift wins. The one selling "beautiful images" competes on price with software.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Productised studio ordering portals, freelance marketplaces, AI image generators, marketplace experiment tools such as Amazon's Manage Your Experiments, and image scoring products such as Vizit at the enterprise end. The gaps: briefs written as adjectives; no link between the shoot and a measured test; and no record of what worked for similar products.

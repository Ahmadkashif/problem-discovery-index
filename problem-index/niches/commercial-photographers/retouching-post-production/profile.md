# Retouching & Post-Production

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/commercial-photographers|Commercial Photographers]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to return brand-consistent, spec-compliant edits overnight at a defect rate the client's QA never sees, and whoever automates the routine edit without letting errors escape takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$600M in US spend on commercial photo retouching, in-house and outsourced, including offshore houses serving US studios (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~13% of commercial photography revenue (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** High — entirely on screen
**Target Buyer:** Post-production manager at a studio or retouching house; brand content-operations lead
**Automation Potential:** Very high for routine edits, medium for high-end beauty and advertising retouching

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Almost every commercial image is retouched before it is delivered. Ecommerce work is a production line: background removal and clipping paths, ghost-mannequin composites, dust and lint, wrinkle reduction, shadows to spec, colour matched to the sample, crops and exports per channel, all overnight. Outsourced services price it per image (Pixelz lists ~$0.95–1.45 for standard work), and AI background removal has absorbed the easy cases. High-end advertising and beauty retouching is a separate craft priced by the hour. What's left in the production tier is the hard tail (hair, fringe, transparency, jewellery, white on white), colour, and the risk of a defect escaping to the client's QA.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Photoshop with actions and scripts, Capture One for batch colour, AI background removal APIs (Photoroom, remove.bg), outsourced houses with their own tooling, and client QA portals. The gaps: automated edits with no confidence measure, so everything is checked or nothing is; defects found by the client rather than before delivery; and capture problems blamed on post-production.

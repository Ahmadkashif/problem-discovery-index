# Packshot & Marketplace-Spec Production

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/commercial-photographers|Commercial Photographers]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to deliver every SKU's images right first time against each retailer's specification and the physical sample's true colour before the listing date, and whoever posts the lowest reshoot and rejection rate per SKU takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$700M in US commissioned packshot and marketplace image production (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~15% of commercial photography revenue (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** High — automated rigs, tethered capture and batch processing are common
**Target Buyer:** Studio director at a high-volume ecommerce studio; brand content-operations manager
**Automation Potential:** Very high — the most mechanical photography in the industry

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Packshots are the standard set of product images on a plain background: front, back, side, detail, sometimes a 360° spin. They exist to satisfy a retailer's or marketplace's specification. Amazon requires the main image to be a real photograph on pure white (RGB 255) with the product filling at least 85% of the frame, and other retailers set their own margins, shadows, crops and file rules. The creative brief is fixed. What varies is whether the image passes ingest first time and whether the colour matches the sample in the shopper's hand. Automated rigs, CGI rendering from CAD files and generative tools all compete for this work, so studios win on rejection and reshoot rates, not on look.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Automated capture rigs (Orbitvu, PhotoRobot) with templated lighting, Capture One styles, batch background removal, Pixelz-style outsourced retouching, and retailer ingest portals that reject non-compliant files. The gaps: specifications checked by eye or at ingest rather than at capture; colour judged against a swatch on a monitor, not measured; and rejections arriving days after the sample has been returned.

# Food & Beverage Photography

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/commercial-photographers|Commercial Photographers]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get the real product looking its best through brand, legal and retailer approval on a perishable one-day set without crossing truth-in-advertising lines, and whoever gets an approved hero shot of the actual product fastest takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$300M in US food, beverage and restaurant photography fees, including styling (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~7% of commercial photography revenue (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — the set is physical and perishable; approvals happen by people standing at the tether or by screenshots in chat
**Target Buyer:** Food photographer and producer; packaging or brand manager at a food or beverage company; restaurant chain marketing director
**Automation Potential:** Low-Medium — approvals, records and spec checks automate; styling and timing do not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Food photography is a team craft run against the clock. A food stylist builds a hero burger or a pour shot that looks right for minutes; the photographer has a narrow window to light and capture it before it wilts, melts or separates. Packaging and advertising shots carry legal weight. In advertising the product shown should be the actual product, a principle that dates to the Campbell's soup case, in which marbles at the bottom of the bowl pushed the solids up in late-1960s print ads. Packs carry "serving suggestion" language, and claims are policed by competitors and by self-regulation, analysed under [[niches/marketing-agencies-smb/advertising-self-regulation/profile|Advertising Self-Regulation & Enforcement]]. Brand, regulatory and retailer stakeholders often have to approve on the day, because the set cannot be rebuilt cheaply tomorrow.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Capture One tethering with clients watching on set or remotely through screen sharing, proofing tools afterwards, styling notes in notebooks, and receipts for every ingredient. The gaps: no structured record of what in the frame was the real product and what was a stand-in or styling aid; live approvals lost in chat threads; and no reusable record of how a hero shot was built.

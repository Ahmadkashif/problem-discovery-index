# The Stand-In Nobody Wrote Down

**Niche:** [[niches/commercial-photographers/food-beverage-photography/profile|Food & Beverage Photography]]
**Industry:** [[industries/commercial-photographers|Commercial Photographers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A competitor challenges a pack image's portion size, and the brand cannot show which elements were its actual product and which were styled substitutes.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #compliance #workflow-orchestration #quick-win #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get the real product looking its best through brand, legal and retailer approval on a perishable one-day set without crossing truth-in-advertising lines, and whoever gets an approved hero shot of the actual product fastest takes the account.

## The Problem
Food styling legitimately uses aids (supports, glycerine sprays, undercooked vegetables, hand-picked pieces), and some sets use stand-ins for components that aren't the product being sold. The line between styling the real product and misrepresenting it is the line advertising law cares about, but the record of what was done lives in the stylist's memory and a pile of receipts.

## Why It's Still Broken
Stylists work at speed with their hands full, there is no standard form, and nobody downstream asks until there's a challenge, often months later, when the stylist has moved on to other clients.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A voice-first styling log: the stylist dictates as they build ("product lot 4471 patty, real; bun from client batch; cheese re-melted with heat gun"), a language model structures it, and it attaches to the frames captured during that build. Retouching edits are logged against the same frames. Brands receive the log with the delivery.

## Who Feels the Pain
Food stylists asked to reconstruct what they did; brand legal and regulatory teams defending claims; photographers whose images are questioned.

## Impact If Fixed
Claim challenges are answered with a record rather than recollection, and the styling team's craft is documented rather than suspected.

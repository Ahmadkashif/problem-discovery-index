# Registration Filed Too Late for Statutory Damages

**Niche:** [[niches/commercial-photographers/image-rights-enforcement/profile|Image Rights Enforcement & Licence Compliance]]
**Industry:** [[industries/commercial-photographers|Commercial Photographers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A photographer discovers a clear infringement, then discovers the image was never registered, and the most they can recover drops from a statutory award to a modest licence fee.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #compliance #workflow-orchestration #automation #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to prove which online uses of an image are unlicensed (checked against the licences actually granted) and convert them into paid licences without a lawsuit, and whoever keeps claims against licensed users near zero takes the photographer's archive.

## The Problem
US statutory damages and the higher Copyright Claims Board caps depend on registering before the infringement began or within three months of first publication. Commercial photographers publish constantly, often through clients, and rarely know the publication date of each image. Group registration allows up to 750 published photographs per application but requires grouping by publication period and preparing deposit files, so most photographers register irregularly or never.

## Why It's Still Broken
Registration is paperwork with a deferred, uncertain benefit, done by people with no administrator. Publication dates are controlled by clients. The Copyright Office's application process is unfamiliar and easy to get wrong, and errors invite refusals.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Track publication from delivery dates and client launch dates; batch delivered images into group applications inside the three-month window automatically; prepare deposit files and the application fields; and remind the photographer before each window closes. Registration status is attached to each image in the licence ledger, so enforcement decisions know the available remedy.

## Who Feels the Pain
Self-employed commercial photographers; small studios with large archives; enforcement services whose cases are weakened by late registration.

## Impact If Fixed
Every new commercial image is registered in time for full remedies at a cost of minutes a month, which makes every later enforcement decision stronger.

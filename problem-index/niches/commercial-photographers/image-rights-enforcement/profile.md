# Image Rights Enforcement & Licence Compliance

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/commercial-photographers|Commercial Photographers]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to prove which online uses of an image are unlicensed (checked against the licences actually granted) and convert them into paid licences without a lawsuit, and whoever keeps claims against licensed users near zero takes the photographer's archive.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$100M a year in US recovered licence fees and enforcement fees for commissioned and archive photography (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~2% of commercial photography revenue (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium-High — matching is automated; licence checking and case handling are manual
**Target Buyer:** The photographer or studio owner; head of operations at an enforcement service
**Automation Potential:** High — matching, licence lookup, registration and first-contact letters are mechanical

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Commercial photographs are copied constantly, by clients' partners, by distributors and resellers, by bloggers who take them from a client's website. Enforcement is a pipeline: find the use, decide whether it is licensed, decide whether it is worth pursuing, and resolve it with a licence offer, a demand, a Copyright Claims Board filing or a lawsuit. Services such as Pixsy, ImageRights and Copytrack run the pipeline on contingency (Pixsy takes 50% of recoveries). The remedy depends on registration timing: the Board pays up to $15,000 per work registered before the infringement or within three months of publication, half that otherwise, and caps each proceeding at $30,000. Pictorial, graphic and sculptural works made up ~36% of claims filed with the Board through March 2026, and most claims were dismissed, many for procedural failures.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Reverse image search (Google Lens, TinEye), contingency enforcement services with their own crawlers, the Copyright Office's group registration for up to 750 published photographs per application, and contract PDFs in the photographer's inbox. The gaps: matches checked against nothing, so licensed users get demands; registration left too late; and Board claims dismissed for avoidable errors.

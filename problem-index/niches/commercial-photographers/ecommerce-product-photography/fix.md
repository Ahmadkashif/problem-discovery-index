## The Problem
Merchandising decisions keep changing after samples have left the warehouse: a colourway cancelled, a SKU re-coded, a launch pulled forward for a retailer. The changes reach the studio piecemeal through emails to different people, so sets shoot cancelled products while new priorities wait, and the brand is billed for both.

## Why It's Still Broken
The style list is a snapshot, not a live feed. Merchandisers communicate changes to whoever they know at the studio, and nobody at the studio owns reconciling the list against the samples on the shelf. Per-SKU pricing means the studio absorbs the waste unless it can prove the change came late.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A single change log per client season: a language model reads incoming emails and updated exports, proposes the change against the current list, and the producer confirms in one click. Shot lists and set schedules update, samples affected are flagged on the shelf, and every late change is time-stamped for billing.

## Who Feels the Pain
Studio producers re-planning sets mid-day; photographers shooting work that gets thrown away; brand ecommerce managers paying for wasted SKUs and missing launch dates.

## Impact If Fixed
Wasted set time from late changes drops to near zero, and the studio can bill for the changes it can't avoid, which matters for margins measured in single dollars per SKU.

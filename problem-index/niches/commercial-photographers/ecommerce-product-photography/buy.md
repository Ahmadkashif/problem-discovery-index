# Product Information Management Extended Into the Studio

**Niche:** [[niches/commercial-photographers/ecommerce-product-photography/profile|Ecommerce Product Photography]]
**Industry:** [[industries/commercial-photographers|Commercial Photographers]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Brands already keep every SKU's attributes, colourways and launch dates in a product information system; the studio receives them as a spreadsheet export and re-keys them.

**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is that every competitor is fighting to deliver more usable images per SKU per dollar, but packshot production and on-model or lifestyle content are won by different studios on different measures: right first time against retailer specifications and the sample's true colour on one side, one consistent brand look across talent, sets and seasons on the other. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## The Problem
Every season the brand exports a style list from its product information system and emails it to the studio. The studio turns it into shot lists, file-naming rules and delivery folders by hand, and the finished images are uploaded back and matched to SKUs by file name, which is where most mismatches start.

## What Already Exists
Product information management platforms (Akeneo, Salsify, Syndigo, inRiver) hold SKU attributes, digital assets and channel specifications, and some integrate with enterprise studio platforms such as Creative Force.

## The Customization Gap
An independent studio serving many brands needs a light connector, not an enterprise integration project: read each brand's style list and launch dates from its product information system; generate shot lists from category and brand rules; push finished images back to the correct SKU and colourway with shot-type metadata; and report coverage gaps (colourways not yet shot) against the launch calendar. It must work across several systems at once, because the studio's clients use different ones.

## Target Customer
Studio directors and producers at independent ecommerce studios serving several brands, and the brand content-operations managers who receive their files.

## Impact If Solved
Re-keying and file-name mismatches disappear, and coverage gaps surface while the samples are still in the studio rather than on launch day.

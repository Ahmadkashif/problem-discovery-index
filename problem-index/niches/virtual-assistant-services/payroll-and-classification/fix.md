# Fix: The FX Spread Nobody Mentions

**Niche:** [[niches/virtual-assistant-services/payroll-and-classification/profile|Payroll, Classification & Cross-Border Pay]]
**Industry:** [[industries/virtual-assistant-services|Virtual Assistant Services]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The rate is quoted in dollars, the payment arrives in local currency, and the difference between the two rates is never stated.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #data-integration #compliance #worker-facing #quick-win #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Whether the assistant will be shown the exchange rate applied to their own wages.

## The Problem

An assistant is paid $6 an hour. The money arrives in pesos, shillings or another currency, converted at a rate the payment provider applied and nobody disclosed. The spread against the mid-market rate is typically one to three percent, sometimes more on less-served corridors, and a transfer fee sits on top.

Over a year, on a modest wage, this is a meaningful sum to the recipient and an invisible one to everyone else. The assistant sees a local-currency amount and no way to check it. They frequently do not know a better payout path exists, and they have no standing to ask for one.

The agency, which selects the rail and has volume, is the only party positioned to negotiate a better rate and has no pressure to.

## Why It's Still Broken

Nobody asks. The assistant does not know the arithmetic is checkable; the client never sees it; and the agency has no reason to raise it. Where an agency receives a rebate or a preferential rate from a payout provider, disclosing the spread would raise a question about where that goes.

Payment providers also present FX as a rate rather than as a fee, which is a category-wide practice that makes the cost genuinely hard to see even for someone looking.

And the amounts per payment are small, which makes each instance easy to dismiss and the annual total easy to ignore.

## What a Fix Looks Like

State the arithmetic on every payment.

Show gross in the quoted currency, the FX rate applied, the mid-market rate on the same date, the spread as an amount, the transfer fee, and the local-currency total that arrives. Six lines on a payment statement. The comparison to mid-market is the line that matters and the one no provider includes.

Compare the corridors and route on landed amount. Payout rails differ by several percent on the same corridor and the difference is stable enough to measure. The agency, paying hundreds of assistants in a handful of markets, has both the volume to negotiate and the data to choose, and neither is currently used for the recipient's benefit.

Let the assistant choose where there is a real choice. Bank transfer, local wallet, or a multi-currency account, each with a stated landed amount and arrival time. Some will prefer speed and some will prefer amount, and both are legitimate.

Disclose any rebate. If the agency receives a preferential rate or a volume rebate from the payment provider, say so and say what happens to it. This is the part most likely to be resisted and it is the part that most affects whether the rest is believed.

Report annually. Total paid, total in FX spread and fees. An assistant seeing that a few hundred dollars went to conversion over a year is seeing something they were entitled to know, and an agency that has optimised the corridor has a number worth showing.

And where a client is billed in dollars and the assistant paid in local currency, be clear about who carries the currency risk between invoicing and payment — a question that arises in every volatile quarter and is usually answered in the assistant's disfavour by default.

## Who Feels the Pain

Assistants, losing a few percent of a modest wage to a cost they cannot see and did not agree to — and most acutely those in corridors with the worst rates, who are generally in the lowest-wage markets. Clients, who assume more of what they pay reaches the worker. And agencies, whose relationship with their workforce rests on trust that opacity like this steadily erodes.

## Impact If Fixed

The assistant can check what happened to their own wages. The payout path gets chosen on landed amount rather than on whichever rail was set up first, which is worth several percent to every recipient. And an agency that shows the arithmetic is demonstrating something about how it treats its workforce that no recruiting claim can.

# Payroll, Classification & Cross-Border Pay

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/virtual-assistant-services|Virtual Assistant Services]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Whether an assistant in another country is paid correctly, classified defensibly, and without the value eroding on the way.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$250M — 5% of US remote assistance spend
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~5%
**Digital Adoption:** Moderate — employer-of-record and payout rails exist and are unevenly used
**Target Buyer:** Agency finance and compliance
**Automation Potential:** Very high — this layer is largely solved by purchasable services

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche

The assistant is in the Philippines, Kenya, Colombia or elsewhere; the agency is in the US or UK; the client is a third party. Money has to cross borders regularly, reliably and cheaply, with the right tax treatment and a defensible answer to what kind of worker this is.

Contractor classification is the live question. An assistant working set hours in the client's timezone, exclusively for one client, directed day to day, using the client's systems, over a period of years, has several of the features that classification tests examine — and the analysis is governed by the assistant's own jurisdiction, several of which have tightened in recent years. The industry's default position is contractor everywhere, applied without much jurisdiction-specific analysis.

The niche is distinct because it is the most purchasable layer in the industry — employer-of-record providers and cross-border payout rails both exist and work — and because the risk is concentrated in a decision most agencies have made by default.

## Current Tools & Gaps

International payout through Wise, Payoneer, Deel and similar, used widely and working reasonably. Employer-of-record providers covering most relevant markets, used by the larger and more careful agencies. Contractor agreements, generally templated from a single jurisdiction. Local tax obligations handled by the assistant.

The gaps are classification analysis and cost transparency. The contractor position is asserted rather than assessed per jurisdiction, and the arrangement's features are not examined against local tests. FX spread and fees on payouts erode a meaningful share of a modest wage and are not presented to the assistant. And local obligations — social contributions, mandatory benefits, termination requirements — are frequently unaddressed.

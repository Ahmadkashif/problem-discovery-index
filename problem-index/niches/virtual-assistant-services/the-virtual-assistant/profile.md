# The Virtual Assistant

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/virtual-assistant-services|Virtual Assistant Services]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Whether the person delivering the service has a career, a record of their work, or any knowledge of what they are billed at.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$650M — 13% of US remote assistance spend
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~13%
**Digital Adoption:** Very low — a set of logins and a timezone
**Target Buyer:** Assistants directly; worker organisations; agencies competing for talent
**Automation Potential:** High — the record and the accounting are derivable

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche

Working a client's business hours from twelve time zones away, billed at several times what you are paid, with no career path beyond the next placement.

The labour arbitrage is the economic core of this industry and is rarely discussed openly. An assistant in a lower-cost market may be billed at several times what they are paid, which is a normal agency structure, and it becomes uncomfortable in combination with the rest: contractor classification, work performed in the client's timezone rather than the assistant's, no visibility of the billing rate, and a replacement guarantee that treats the end of a placement as a routine event.

The niche is distinct because the subject is the assistant's own working life. It is also the part of this industry where the gap between what the work requires and what the arrangement provides is widest: the job demands judgement, trust and accumulated context, and the structure provides none of the security, progression or recognition that normally accompanies work of that kind.

## Current Tools & Gaps

Agency-provided training at onboarding, generally brief. Payout through international transfer or an employer-of-record arrangement. A profile in the agency's system. Assistant communities on Facebook and Discord where rates, agencies and clients are discussed candidly, which is the main information source this workforce actually has.

The gaps are record, progression and rate transparency. An assistant with four years and six placements has no portable evidence of any of it. There is no progression beyond being placed with a better-paying client, and no defined skill ladder. Health, time-off and the effects of permanently working another continent's hours are unaddressed. And the billing rate is unknown, which removes any basis for negotiating the split.

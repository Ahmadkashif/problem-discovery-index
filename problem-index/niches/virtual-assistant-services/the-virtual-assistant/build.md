# Build: A Portable Record and a Rate Reference

**Niche:** [[niches/virtual-assistant-services/the-virtual-assistant/profile|The Virtual Assistant]]
**Industry:** [[industries/virtual-assistant-services|Virtual Assistant Services]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Give assistants verifiable evidence of the work they have done and a reference for what agencies pay and bill, so experience compounds into something they can carry.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #confidence-intervals #evaluation-metrics #data-integration #word-embeddings #compliance #worker-facing #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Whether a verifiable professional record can be assembled for workers whose employers have no reason to verify it.

## The Problem

An assistant with four years of experience supporting executives across three agencies has no way to prove any of it. Their evidence is a CV claim and whatever reference a former agency chooses to provide. The agencies hold the record of what they did, for how long, at what quality, and it is not theirs.

The consequence is that experience does not compound into bargaining power. An assistant with four years applies alongside one with six months and is differentiated mainly by how they interview. Rates are set by the agency, benchmarked against what other agencies pay, in a market where the supply side has no way to demonstrate they are worth more.

The rate information gap is equally structural. Assistants do not know what they are billed at, and mostly do not know what other agencies pay for comparable work in their market. The information that exists circulates informally in community groups, unverified and patchy.

## Why Nobody Has Built This

Agencies have no incentive to verify a record that makes their workers portable, and they are the party holding it. Rate transparency runs directly against the margin structure.

Third parties face a credibility problem: a self-reported record is a CV, which already exists and persuades nobody. Verification requires either the agency's cooperation, or a structure where the record is built from primary evidence the assistant can demonstrate — task systems, platform logins, payment records — which is harder but possible.

And the market has been treated as one of interchangeable low-cost labour rather than a professional workforce, which is both inaccurate and self-fulfilling.

## What to Build

A verifiable record and a rate reference, built on the assistant's own evidence.

**Build the record from primary evidence.** Payment records showing duration and continuity. Task system exports showing volume and type of work. Systems and tools used, evidenced by account access. Client references where offered. Tenure per placement. None of this requires the agency's cooperation and all of it is stronger than a CV claim.

**Structure it by capability, not by job title.** Calendar and travel management, inbox triage, research, bookkeeping support, CRM administration, project coordination, vendor management — with volume, duration and tooling for each. This is the vocabulary an agency or a direct client actually matches on and it does not exist as a standard anywhere.

**Separate the confidential rigorously.** The record must evidence capability without disclosing anything about any client — no names, no content, no context. This is a design requirement and the whole thing is unusable without it.

**Build the rate reference.** Pay rates by market, experience and capability, crowdsourced from assistants and cross-checked, alongside billing rates where they can be established from published agency pricing. The gap between the two is the arbitrage and publishing it is the most consequential single act available here. It is also the piece that makes the tool worth opening.

**Add the practical layer that drives adoption.** Multi-currency income tracking across placements and agencies, tax obligations in the assistant's own jurisdiction, and hours worked including the timezone reality. This is the utility; the record is the value.

**Serve the direct-engagement path honestly.** Some assistants will use a portable record to go direct. That is the point of portability, it is what gives the workforce any leverage, and a tool that quietly prevents it is not serving them.

## Target Customer

Assistants directly — a large, connected, motivated workforce that already organises in communities and shares exactly this information informally. Also the agencies that pay above market, for whom verified records and published rates are an advantage rather than a threat.

## Impact If Built

Experience becomes evidence, which is the precondition for it being worth anything. The rate gap becomes visible, which is what gives a supply side any basis for negotiating. And a workforce currently treated as interchangeable acquires the professional record that every other skilled occupation takes for granted.

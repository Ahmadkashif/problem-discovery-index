# Context Capture

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/virtual-assistant-services|Virtual Assistant Services]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Whether what an assistant knows can be extracted from the work rather than written down by hand.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$810M — 60% of the context and continuity niche
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~16% of US remote assistance spend
**Digital Adoption:** Very low — SOP documents written unpaid by the conscientious
**Target Buyer:** Agencies; clients; assistants — all three want it
**Automation Potential:** Very high — the work traces are digital and dense

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche

This is the half everyone benefits from. An assistant's accumulated knowledge — preferences, relationships, rules, procedures, standing permissions — is buildable from the traces the work already leaves: messages between executive and assistant, calendar actions and their corrections, documents and revisions, escalations, forms submitted.

The distinguishing property is unanimity. The agency wants it because it makes the replacement guarantee deliverable and reduces the cost of churn. The client wants it because their delegation stops resetting. The assistant wants it because it removes the unpaid documentation burden and because handing over well is what a professional wants to do. Nobody in the arrangement has a reason to resist.

It is also self-sufficient. A captured context is useful the day it exists, as a reference for the assistant doing the job now, whether or not anyone ever leaves.

## Current Tools & Gaps

Documentation tools and shared drives, filled in by hand at variable quality. Screen-recording process capture tools, which handle repeatable procedures reasonably and say nothing about judgement. Email and calendar systems holding the raw material. Generative assistants that can read it and are not pointed at this.

The gap is that nobody has built the extraction. The raw material is unusually good — a year of instructions, corrections and explanations between two people in a written channel is close to an ideal corpus for inferring how one of them wants things done — and it sits unexamined in a shared inbox.

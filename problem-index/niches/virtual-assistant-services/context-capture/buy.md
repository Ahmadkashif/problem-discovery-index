**Contested on:** Whether procedure-capture tooling can hold the exceptions and judgement that constitute the actual knowledge.

## The Problem

Process documentation has decent tooling. Screen-recording products capture a task being performed and generate a step-by-step guide with screenshots; SOP platforms manage the resulting library; task management systems hold the recurring work. An agency wanting to capture how an assistant works will find these and deploy them.

They capture procedure, which is the part of the job that matters least. How to submit an expense report is documented in three minutes and learned in five. What an assistant actually knows is when the procedure does not apply — which expense goes to the other approver, which meeting request from this person is always accepted regardless of the calendar, when "handle it" means handle it and when it means check first.

## What Already Exists

Scribe, Tango, Loom and the process capture category. SOP libraries and documentation platforms. Task and workflow tools. Automation platforms for the genuinely repeatable parts. Generative assistants over document corpora. All of it works for what it does.

## The Customization Gap

**The valuable content is exceptions, and procedure tools have no place for them.** A captured process is a linear sequence. The knowledge is conditional and exception-laden, and it needs a structure that holds rules with their conditions and their known exceptions rather than a list of steps.

**The source is conversation, not screen recording.** An assistant learns from being told and corrected, in a message thread. Screen capture records what was clicked, which is the least informative part. The ingest has to be the correspondence.

**Capture cannot be an extra activity.** Recording a process requires deciding to record it, which happens for the tasks someone thought to document and never for the accumulated judgement. Extraction from work that was happening anyway is the only approach that captures the long tail.

**The content is about people and is sensitive.** Process tools hold neutral procedures. Here the record includes characterisations of named colleagues, board members and family, inferred from private correspondence. The permission model, the scope limits and the executive's visibility are requirements these products have never had to consider.

**Currency matters more than completeness.** A procedure documented once stays roughly true. A preference recorded eighteen months ago may be wrong, and a stale context record is actively dangerous because it will be trusted. Confirmation cycles and staleness marking are essential and absent from documentation tooling.

## Target Customer

Agencies deploying process documentation and finding their assistants dutifully record the parts nobody needs. Also the process capture vendors, for whom conversation-derived tacit knowledge is a natural extension now that reading a year of messages is cheap.

## Impact If Solved

The capture, storage and presentation machinery gets reused, and the exception-holding structure, conversational ingest, zero-effort extraction, personal-data posture and staleness management get built. Concretely: a record of the judgement rather than a library of procedures nobody needed written down.

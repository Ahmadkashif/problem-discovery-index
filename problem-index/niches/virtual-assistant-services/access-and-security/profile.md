# Access, Credentials & Data Security

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/virtual-assistant-services|Virtual Assistant Services]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Whether a contractor overseas gets the access they need to do the job without getting access to everything.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$350M — 7% of US remote assistance spend
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~7%
**Digital Adoption:** Low — shared logins and a password sent over chat
**Target Buyer:** Client IT and security functions; agency operations
**Automation Potential:** Very high — the tooling exists and is not deployed

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche

The service is a person and a set of shared logins. An assistant needs the executive's calendar, their inbox or parts of it, travel and expense systems, the CRM, banking portals in some arrangements, and whatever else the work touches. The normal way this is arranged is that the executive shares their own credentials.

The risk this creates is out of proportion to the niche's size. A contractor in another jurisdiction, engaged through an intermediary, operating with an executive's full credentials on their own personal device, with no logging distinguishing their actions from the executive's, is a security posture most organisations would not knowingly accept. Many have accepted it without knowing, because executive assistant access is arranged personally rather than through IT.

The niche is distinct because the answer is almost entirely deployment of existing tooling — delegated access, password management, device policy, logging — into a context where nobody has applied it.

## Current Tools & Gaps

Delegated access features in the major mail and calendar platforms, which are good and widely unused in this context. Password managers with sharing, used by the better agencies. Single sign-on and conditional access at larger clients. Device policy where the assistant uses agency hardware, which is not the norm.

The gaps are attribution, scope and offboarding. Shared credentials mean no log distinguishes the assistant's actions from the executive's, which defeats audit and makes any incident unattributable. Access is granted broadly because scoping it takes effort. And offboarding is frequently a message asking the assistant to stop using the account, with no revocation, which leaves credentials outstanding indefinitely.

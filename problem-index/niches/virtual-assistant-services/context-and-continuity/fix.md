# Fix: Three Months to Rebuild What Was Already Known

**Niche:** [[niches/virtual-assistant-services/context-and-continuity/profile|Context & Continuity]]
**Industry:** [[industries/virtual-assistant-services|Virtual Assistant Services]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** An assistant leaves, the replacement guarantee sends someone new, and the executive spends a quarter explaining things the last person learned two years ago.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #workflow-orchestration #large-language-models #confidence-intervals #worker-facing #quick-win #data-integration
**Contested on:** Whether the departing assistant is given time and a structure to hand over what they know.

## The Problem

An assistant gives notice, or is replaced, or moves to another client. The agency invokes its replacement guarantee and provides a new person, generally quickly, which is presented as the guarantee working.

What actually happens is that the executive restarts. Preferences re-explained. Rules rediscovered by getting them wrong. Relationships relearned. The judgement calls that the previous assistant made correctly without asking now arrive as questions, dozens of them, at exactly the point where the executive was expecting relief.

The departing assistant knew all of it. In most cases they were given no structured handover time, or a day, or told to "write some notes", unpaid, at the end of an engagement they may be leaving unhappily.

## Why It's Still Broken

Handover time is an unbilled cost to the agency at the end of a placement that may already be a commercial problem. The incentive is to move the departing assistant onto the next client and the replacement into this one as quickly as possible, which is exactly what the guarantee promises.

There is also no structure, so even a willing assistant given a day produces a document of variable quality organised by whatever occurred to them. Blank-page handovers are reliably bad.

And when a placement ends badly — which is common, since replacements are frequently triggered by dissatisfaction — the departing assistant has little goodwill to spend on a thorough handover and nobody has given them a reason to.

## What a Fix Looks Like

Make handover a defined, paid, structured part of every placement ending.

Pay for it. A defined number of hours, billed to nobody or absorbed as a cost of the guarantee, in which the departing assistant's only job is handover. An agency that treats this as a cost of the replacement guarantee rather than as an unbilled favour gets a completely different quality of output.

Structure it against a checklist rather than a blank page. People and how to handle them. Recurring calendar rules and exceptions. Preferences by task type. Standing permissions — what can be done without asking. Systems and where things live. Things that have gone wrong and why. In-flight items with their state. Each prompted, each answerable in a few sentences.

Generate the draft from the work, where the capability exists. A model reading the message history and calendar actions produces most of the checklist for confirmation, which turns a day of writing into two hours of review and produces something more complete than memory would.

Overlap the assistants where possible. A week of the two working together transfers more than any document, and where the departure is planned it costs one week of double-running against a quarter of degraded service.

Debrief the executive too. The client knows things about how the arrangement worked that the assistant does not, and a short structured conversation at transition captures preferences the executive has never articulated.

And measure the ramp. Time to independent operation for each new placement, and the number of clarifying questions in the first month. An agency that tracked this would discover what its replacement guarantee actually costs its clients.

## Who Feels the Pain

Executives, who bought recovered time and get a quarter of training work instead, and who mostly conclude the service does not work rather than that the handover failed. Incoming assistants, who start blind and appear incompetent while learning things that were already known. Departing assistants, asked to document a year of judgement in an afternoon for free. And agencies, whose churn costs them clients through a mechanism they treat as a feature.

## Impact If Fixed

The replacement guarantee starts delivering continuity rather than a warm body. A new assistant reaches competence in weeks instead of months, which is the difference between the service compounding and resetting. And the agency finds out what its own churn costs the client, which it currently does not measure and which is the number that would change how it handles replacements.

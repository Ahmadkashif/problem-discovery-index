**Contested on:** Whether knowledge management tooling can hold knowledge that was never authored.

## The Problem

Knowledge management is a long-established category. Wikis, documentation platforms, SOP tools, internal search and now retrieval-augmented assistants over a company's documents all work reasonably well, and an agency looking to solve the context problem will reach for one.

Every one of them starts from documents someone wrote. The context that matters here was never written and never will be by hand — it is an accumulation of small judgements, corrections and preferences that nobody would think to document and that would take longer to write than to re-learn. A wiki with a template for "client preferences" gets filled in once, at onboarding, by someone who does not yet know the answers.

## What Already Exists

Notion, Confluence, Guru, Slab and the documentation category. SOP tools aimed at operations teams. Retrieval-augmented assistants over document corpora. Personal knowledge tools. CRM systems for relationship records. Process documentation and screen-recording tools that capture how a task is performed.

## The Customization Gap

**The source is work traces, not documents.** The adaptation that matters is an extraction layer over messages, calendar actions and document revisions that proposes context items. Without it the product is a blank template, which is what every previous attempt in this industry has been.

**The unit is one executive, not an organisation.** Knowledge products are built for many contributors and many readers, with permissions, review workflows and taxonomies. Here there is one subject, one or two authors and one or two readers, and all that machinery is overhead. The right shape is closer to a personal record than a knowledge base.

**Maintenance has to be confirmation, not authoring.** These tools rot because updating requires editing a document. A record that surfaces "is this still true" against inferred items keeps itself current at a cost of seconds. This is a fundamentally different interaction model.

**Much of the content is about named people and is sensitive.** How to handle a particular board member, which colleague is difficult, an executive's personal habits. Knowledge products have permissions; they do not have a posture for personal information about third parties assembled by a contractor. Scope, visibility and the executive's control over it are product requirements rather than settings.

**The handover is the moment of use.** These products are designed for continuous reference. Here the highest-value moment is a replacement's first week, which calls for an ordered, guided introduction rather than a searchable corpus — a different surface over the same content.

## Target Customer

Agencies building or buying a knowledge layer for placements, who will find the documentation products assume an author. Also the knowledge management vendors, for whom trace-derived context is a capability their document-centric products lack and generative retrieval makes feasible.

## Impact If Solved

The storage, search, permissions and retrieval infrastructure gets reused, and the trace extraction, single-subject shape, confirmation-based maintenance, personal-data posture and handover surface get built. Concretely: a context record that exists because it assembled itself, rather than a template nobody filled in.

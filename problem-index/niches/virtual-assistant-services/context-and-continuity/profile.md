# Context & Continuity

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/virtual-assistant-services|Virtual Assistant Services]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Whether what an experienced assistant knows survives their departure.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.35B — 27% of US spend on remote executive and operational assistance
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~27%
**Digital Adoption:** Very low — the asset is a person's memory
**Target Buyer:** Agency leadership; clients; assistants
**Automation Potential:** High for capture; the ownership question is not a technical one

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche

Everything that makes an experienced assistant valuable lives in their head, and the agency model replaces assistants routinely.

An assistant's usefulness depends on knowing how a particular executive works — which meetings can be moved and which cannot, who gets a response the same day, what the executive means by "sort it out", which supplier is difficult, how the expense process actually runs versus how it is documented, the relationships and the unwritten rules. That knowledge takes months to build and is worth more than any individual task.

It is destroyed on every replacement. The agency treats replacement as a routine service feature — a guarantee, a benefit — while it is in fact the periodic destruction of the only asset the arrangement accumulates. This is the reason the service so rarely compounds: a client three years in is frequently no better served than a client three months in, having rebuilt the same context twice.

## Current Tools & Gaps

Standard operating procedure documents, written by conscientious assistants in their own time. Shared drives with inconsistent conventions. Handover documents produced hurriedly at departure, if at all. Some agencies mandate documentation; compliance is variable because it is unpaid and competes with the work.

The gaps are capture and entitlement. Nothing extracts context from the work itself, though the work is almost entirely digital — messages, calendar actions, documents, forms — and carries most of what a procedure document would say. And there is no settled answer to who owns the result, which is why nobody builds the capture.

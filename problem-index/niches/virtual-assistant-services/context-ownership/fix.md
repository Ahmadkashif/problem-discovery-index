# Fix: The Contract Says Nothing About It

**Niche:** [[niches/virtual-assistant-services/context-ownership/profile|Context Ownership]]
**Industry:** [[industries/virtual-assistant-services|Virtual Assistant Services]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Agency agreements cover confidentiality, non-solicitation and data security, and are silent on who owns the procedures and preferences the assistant wrote down.
**Tags:** #compliance #descriptive-statistics #workflow-orchestration #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #quick-win #data-integration #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Whether the parties will state, in advance, who gets the documentation when the engagement ends.

## The Problem

Even without any capture software, context artefacts already exist in every engagement: the SOP documents an assistant wrote, the templates they built, the process notes, the spreadsheet of supplier contacts, the checklist for the monthly close. Real, written, useful.

Nobody has said whose they are. The agency contract covers confidentiality, data security and non-solicitation. It does not say whether the client gets the documents when the engagement ends, whether the agency may reuse the templates for another client, or whether the assistant may take their own checklists to a new placement.

So it resolves by accident. Documents in the client's drive stay with the client. Documents in the agency's system stay with the agency. Documents in the assistant's personal tools leave with them. The allocation is determined by where files happened to be saved.

## Why It's Still Broken

Contracts are templated and inherited, drafted for a simpler version of the service, and nobody has revisited them because the issue only surfaces at termination — by which point the relationship is ending and nobody wants a fight over documents.

Ambiguity also suits the agency slightly, and the client has not asked because they have not thought about it until the moment it matters.

And the assistant, whose documents many of these are, is not a party to the discussion.

## What a Fix Looks Like

Say it in the contract, before it matters.

Define the categories. Client-specific documentation — procedures, preferences, contacts, templates containing the client's information — goes to the client on termination, in full. Agency methodology — generic checklists, training materials, the agency's own frameworks — stays with the agency. The assistant's own professional materials — their personal systems, their generic templates, a record of the work they did — stay with them. Three sentences that remove nearly all the ambiguity.

Say where things live. Client-specific documentation is created in the client's systems, or synchronised there continuously, rather than in an assistant's personal drive or an agency tool. Allocation by storage location only works if the storage location is deliberate.

Make termination handover a defined deliverable with a list. What the client receives, in what format, within how many days. Written into the contract rather than negotiated during a departure.

Give the assistant an explicit right to a professional record — task types, systems used, tenure, scope, without client confidential content. This costs the agency nothing, is the only credential this workforce can hold, and is currently denied by silence rather than by intent.

And give the client a standing export right rather than a termination one. A client who can retrieve their documentation at any time never has to have the conversation at all.

## Who Feels the Pain

Clients who end an engagement and discover the procedures documenting their own operations are in an agency system they no longer have access to. Assistants who leave a placement and lose the templates they built, and who have no way to evidence four years of work. Agencies drawn into disputes at termination over files whose status nobody ever defined. And account managers, who handle those disputes with no contractual basis for a position.

## Impact If Fixed

The allocation stops being determined by where files were saved. Clients get the documentation of their own operations as a defined deliverable. Assistants get a professional record they can carry. And a question that currently surfaces at the worst possible moment gets answered at the best one, in three sentences.

# Context Ownership

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/virtual-assistant-services|Virtual Assistant Services]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Who holds the context record — the client whose business it describes, the agency that placed the worker, or the assistant who accumulated it.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$540M — 40% of the context and continuity niche
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~11% of US remote assistance spend
**Digital Adoption:** None — the question has not been asked because the artefact does not exist
**Target Buyer:** Agency leadership and counsel; clients negotiating terms; assistant organisations
**Automation Potential:** Low — this is a contractual and commercial question with almost no engineering in it

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche

The moment context stops being one person's memory and becomes an artefact, it becomes property, and the three parties' interests diverge completely.

The client has the strongest claim in principle — it is a description of how their business runs, assembled while someone worked for them — and the strongest interest, because holding it means a replacement from any source is productive quickly. The agency's commercial interest runs the other way: a client whose context is portable can change agency or hire directly with little friction, and the switching cost that portable context destroys is a meaningful part of what makes the business defensible. The assistant has a real claim too, since it is the record of a professional capability they built, and a portable version of it is the only credential this workforce could ever have.

That is why this is a distinct market rather than a feature of capture. It is zero-sum in a way capture is not, it is settled by contract rather than by software, and it is the reason the capture keeps not getting built.

## Current Tools & Gaps

Standard agency contracts covering confidentiality and work product, generally drafted before any context artefact existed and silent on it. Client data ownership clauses that may or may not reach an inferred record. Non-solicitation clauses preventing direct hiring, which is the commercial mechanism currently substituting for context lock-in.

The gaps are that no standard terms exist, that clients do not ask, and that the assistant is not a party to the negotiation at all despite having assembled the thing.

# Buy: Data Ownership and Portability Frameworks Adapted to a Three-Party Service

**Niche:** [[niches/virtual-assistant-services/context-ownership/profile|Context Ownership]]
**Industry:** [[industries/virtual-assistant-services|Virtual Assistant Services]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Data ownership and portability have well-developed frameworks in SaaS and in privacy law, all built for two parties; here there are three and the contested asset is inferred rather than supplied.
**Tags:** #compliance #data-integration #workflow-orchestration #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #automation #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Whether two-party data ownership frameworks can allocate an asset that three parties helped create.

## The Problem

Data ownership and portability are well-charted territory. SaaS contracts routinely allocate customer data, derived data and aggregated insights; privacy regulation gives individuals access and portability rights; and standard clauses exist for return and deletion on termination. A lawyer drafting an agency contract has plenty of precedent.

None of it fits. The frameworks assume a customer who supplied data to a provider. Here the asset was not supplied by anyone — it was inferred from the interaction between a client's executive and a worker the agency placed, it describes the client's operations, it constitutes the assistant's professional capability, and it sits in the agency's system. Three parties with genuine claims and a two-party template.

## What Already Exists

Standard SaaS data ownership and derived-data clauses. Privacy law access and portability rights, which reach personal data about the executive and about third parties in the record. Work-for-hire and intellectual property allocation in services contracts. Non-solicitation and confidentiality clauses. Data processing agreements and cross-border transfer mechanisms. Employer-of-record arrangements with their own data positions.

## The Customization Gap

**Three parties, and one of them has no seat at the table.** The assistant assembled the context, has a real professional claim to a version of it, and is a contractor in another jurisdiction with a contract they did not negotiate. Standard frameworks have no representation for a third party with a partial claim, and constructing one requires deciding deliberately that the assistant gets something.

**The asset is inferred, not supplied.** Derived-data clauses in SaaS allocate insights generated from customer data. Here the "customer data" is the client's own correspondence and calendar, the inference is about the client's own operations, and the deriving party is a service provider whose worker is inside the client's systems. Whether existing derived-data language reaches this at all is genuinely unclear, which is why it needs writing rather than borrowing.

**Portability has to be splittable, not whole.** The client's copy, the assistant's professional record and the agency's operating copy are three different subsets of one artefact. The technical and contractual structure has to support a clean split — confidential content on one side, capability evidence on the other — which no portability framework contemplates.

**Personal data about third parties sits inside it.** Characterisations of named colleagues, board members and family, inferred from correspondence, processed across borders. The privacy analysis is real, the data subjects are not parties to any of the contracts, and the standard DPA templates do not anticipate an artefact of this shape.

**Cross-border is the default, not the exception.** Assistants predominantly in other jurisdictions, clients in the US, agencies anywhere. Transfer mechanisms, retention and deletion obligations have to work across all three, which the templates handle for supplied data and not for inferred records about individuals.

## Target Customer

Agency counsel drafting terms for a context product and finding the precedent does not fit. Also client procurement and legal functions at larger buyers, who will ask the question once the artefact exists, and employer-of-record providers whose data positions interact with it.

## Impact If Solved

The contractual precedent, privacy analysis and transfer mechanisms get reused, and the three-party allocation, the inferred-asset characterisation, the splittable portability and the third-party personal data posture get constructed. Concretely: terms that can be published rather than an ambiguity that resolves in favour of whoever wrote the software.

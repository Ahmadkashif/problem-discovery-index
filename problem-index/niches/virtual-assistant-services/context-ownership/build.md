# Build: Portable Context With Defined Rights

**Niche:** [[niches/virtual-assistant-services/context-ownership/profile|Context Ownership]]
**Industry:** [[industries/virtual-assistant-services|Virtual Assistant Services]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Hold the context record where the client can take it with them, with the agency's and the assistant's rights defined, and compete on service rather than on the switching cost.
**Tags:** #compliance #data-integration #workflow-orchestration #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #confidence-intervals #revenue-impact #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Whether an agency will give up the lock-in that an unwritten context provides.

## The Problem

The context record, once it exists, is the most valuable artefact in the arrangement, and there is no settled answer to whose it is.

Left undefined, it resolves by default to whoever holds the system. If the agency builds the capture into its own platform, the agency holds a description of the client's operations and the client's switching cost rises sharply. If the client holds it, the agency loses the friction that makes churn survivable. If the assistant holds it, they can take a client's operating knowledge to a competitor, or to direct engagement, which the agency's non-solicitation clause exists to prevent.

Nobody has written terms for this, and the absence is not neutral — it means the party who builds the software wins by default, and that party is the agency.

## Why Nobody Has Built This

Because the agency, which is the only party positioned to build the capture, has a commercial interest in the ambiguity. Portable context weakens the relationship in a market where clients already churn and where the alternative — hire directly through a marketplace — is one step away.

Clients have not asked because the artefact does not exist yet, so there is nothing to negotiate about. Assistants have no negotiating position at all, being contractors placed by the agency, frequently in another jurisdiction, with a contract they did not draft.

And there is no template. Data ownership provisions in service contracts were written for deliverables and customer data, not for an inferred description of how a client works assembled by a third party's worker.

## What to Build

A rights structure, with the software built to implement it, offered as a competitive position rather than extracted by negotiation.

**Define the three rights separately.** The client's right to the record — full, exportable, in a usable format, at any time and on termination. The agency's right to use it for delivering service while engaged, and to retain nothing afterwards. The assistant's right to a professional record of their own — capabilities, systems, task types and duration, without the client's confidential content — which is the portable credential this workforce lacks entirely. These three do not conflict once stated separately; they conflict only while everything is one undifferentiated blob.

**Build export as a first-class function, not a legal promise.** Structured, readable, complete, one action, available to the client at all times rather than on request at termination. A portability right that requires a support ticket is not one.

**Separate the confidential from the professional.** The assistant's record needs to carry what they can do without carrying what they know about this client. That separation is a data modelling decision — task types, systems, volumes and tenure on one side; names, preferences and relationships on the other — and making it cleanly is what allows all three rights to coexist.

**Write the terms and publish them.** An agency that offers full client portability and says so is making a claim about competing on quality, and it is an unusually credible one in a market where every competitor's retention rests partly on friction. It also pre-empts the day a large client's counsel asks the question.

**Handle the cross-border reality.** Assistants in several jurisdictions, client data subject to the client's own obligations, and an agency in a third place. The record contains personal data about the executive and about third parties, and the processing, transfer and retention positions need to be actually correct rather than assumed.

## Target Customer

Agencies choosing to compete on portability rather than friction — a smaller set than the market, and the one for whom this is a strategy rather than a concession. Also large clients whose procurement and legal functions will eventually ask the question, and assistant organisations, for whom a portable professional record is the single most consequential thing available to this workforce.

## Impact If Built

The context artefact acquires defined rights instead of resolving to whoever built the software. Clients get the continuity they are paying for without acquiring a dependency. Assistants get a portable record of their own capability, which is the only credential this workforce could ever hold. And agencies that choose portability compete on the service rather than on how expensive it is to leave.

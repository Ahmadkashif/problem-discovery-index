# Fix: The Guarantee Is the Only Tool

**Niche:** [[niches/virtual-assistant-services/the-account-manager/profile|The Account Manager]]
**Industry:** [[industries/virtual-assistant-services|Virtual Assistant Services]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Between a check-in call and replacing the assistant there is nothing, so every problem that is not solved by a call is solved by ending someone's placement.
**Tags:** #workflow-orchestration #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #hypothesis-testing #quick-win #worker-facing #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Whether the agency will build responses proportionate to the problems it actually encounters.

## The Problem

A client is dissatisfied. The account manager calls, listens, and reassures. If that does not resolve it, the next and only option is the replacement guarantee.

The guarantee is sold as a benefit and functions as a default. It satisfies the client immediately, which is what the account manager is measured on. It also destroys the accumulated context, costs the client a quarter of rebuilding, moves an assistant off an account often through no fault of theirs, and leaves the underlying cause untouched — so the same complaint frequently reappears with the next assistant, at which point the client concludes the agency is the problem.

The problems that arrive are mostly not "this assistant is incapable". They are briefing problems, scope problems and expectation problems, and none of them has a remedy in the toolkit.

## Why It's Still Broken

Because the guarantee is cheap for the agency and expensive for everyone else, and the account manager is measured on client retention rather than on placement stability. Replacing is the locally optimal move every time.

Intermediate interventions also require capabilities the agency does not have: someone able to coach an executive on how they brief, a structured re-scoping process, a way to diagnose which problem this is. Building them is an investment in a role that is treated as relationship maintenance.

And nobody counts the cost. Replacement rate is known; the client-side cost of a replacement, the assistant-side cost, and the recurrence rate after replacement are not.

## What a Fix Looks Like

Build the ladder and change what the account manager is measured on.

Diagnose before acting. A structured conversation with both parties, separately, against a checklist: what is being delegated, how it is briefed, what comes back, what happens then, what the expectation was. Fifteen minutes each and it separates the four causes more often than not. Currently the conversation is unstructured and reaches whichever account the account manager heard second.

Add briefing coaching as a service. A short session with the executive on how to hand over a task, with examples from their own recent tasks. This is the most common cause and the cheapest fix, it is entirely absent from every agency's offering, and clients accept it readily when it is framed as getting more from the arrangement.

Add re-scoping as a defined move. Move a task type back to the executive, or change how it is handled, or add a checkpoint. Many failing arrangements are failing at two of twelve task types and succeeding at ten.

Add a structured improvement period with stated expectations and a review date, for genuine execution problems. Uncomfortable, fair, and far better than a surprise replacement for the assistant involved.

Then measure what happens. Recurrence after replacement — how often the same complaint arrives about the next assistant — is the number that proves the guarantee is not solving anything, and it is computable from existing records.

And change the account manager's metrics to include placement stability alongside client retention, or the ladder will not be used.

## Who Feels the Pain

Assistants, removed from placements for problems that were not theirs, with no process and no appeal. Clients, who get a new person and the same difficulty, and lose a quarter of context each time. Account managers, who know a replacement is the wrong answer and have nothing else. And agencies, whose replacement rate is a cost they treat as a feature.

## Impact If Fixed

Problems get diagnosed before they get a remedy, which means briefing problems get briefing fixes. The context stops being destroyed to resolve issues that were never about the assistant. Recurrence after replacement becomes a measured number, which is what makes the case internally. And the guarantee returns to being what it should be — a last resort rather than the only tool in the drawer.

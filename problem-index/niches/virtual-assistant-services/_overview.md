## Niche Selection

## Why These Niches

Context is what makes an experienced assistant valuable and the agency model discards it routinely. Delegation value is the thing being sold and the thing nobody measures. Matching and handoff are where placements fail. The two underserved people are the assistant working another continent's hours at a fraction of the billed rate, and the account manager whose only tool for an unhappy client is ending someone's placement. Access and payroll are mechanical, and the first carries risk far out of proportion to its size.

## Filter Notes

Seven of the eight are terminal — each names one contest and further division produces features rather than markets.

**Context and continuity** is not, and its halves separate on a line this vault has not drawn before: producing the asset versus deciding whose it is. Context capture asks whether what an assistant knows — an executive's preferences, their relationships, the calendar logic, the unwritten rules, the procedures nobody wrote down — can be extracted and structured from the work itself. It is an engineering problem over messages, calendar actions and documents; it serves every party at once, because a captured context makes the replacement guarantee actually work, makes the assistant's job easier, and makes the client's delegation survive a departure; and nobody in the arrangement opposes it. Context ownership asks who holds the result. The captured context is a description of how a client's business runs, assembled by a worker the agency placed, and whoever holds it holds the switching cost — which is exactly why it is contested. An agency that lets the context sit with the client loses the lock-in that makes replacement guarantees commercially bearable; one that lets it sit with the assistant makes that assistant portable to a competitor or to direct engagement; one that holds it itself is claiming a description of the client's own operations as its asset. It is a contractual and commercial question with almost no engineering in it, and it is the reason capture keeps not being built even though everyone would benefit — because building it forces the ownership question, and nobody wants to ask it.

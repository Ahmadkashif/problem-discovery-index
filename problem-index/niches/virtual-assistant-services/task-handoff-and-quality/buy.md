**Contested on:** Whether task tooling can work when the person assigning the work refuses to leave their inbox.

## The Problem

Task management is a crowded, mature category. Boards, lists, assignments, due dates, comments, attachments, templates and recurring tasks are all available, cheap and familiar, and every agency in this industry has tried to put one in place.

They fail for a specific reason. The executive will not use it. Delegation happens in a message, a forwarded email or a passing remark in a call, because that is where the executive already is and because opening a separate tool to assign a task is more work than doing the task. The assistant then maintains the board alone, which makes it an assistant's tracking system rather than a shared workspace, and the briefing quality problem is untouched.

## What Already Exists

Asana, Trello, ClickUp, Monday, Notion and the task management field. Email-to-task integrations. Shared inboxes and delegation features in mail clients. Template and recurring task support. Time tracking integrations. Slack and Teams integrations. The tooling is not the constraint.

## The Customization Gap

**The handoff surface has to be the executive's inbox or chat.** Anything requiring the executive to visit a tool will fail, consistently, as it has repeatedly. The task object must be created from a forwarded email or a message, with the assistant's tooling behind it and nothing new for the executive to learn.

**The parties are in different tenants.** Cross-organisation task collaboration means guest access, separate identities, and documents living somewhere both can reach — with the added feature that the assistant frequently operates through the executive's own account, which makes attribution and audit messy in a way no task tool anticipates.

**Briefing quality is the value and no tool addresses it.** These products capture that a task exists; they do nothing about whether it was described well enough to execute. Prompting the missing constraint at creation is the adaptation that matters and it does not exist anywhere in the category.

**The round-trip count is the metric and none of them reports it.** Task tools report cycle time and throughput. The quantity that governs whether delegation works is how many exchanges a task required and how often it came back — derivable from comment threads and status changes, and reported by nobody.

**Recurring tasks carry accumulated specifics.** A monthly report has preferences, exceptions and gotchas learned over a year. Task templates hold steps; they do not hold the accumulated context, and linking a recurring task to the context record is the connection that makes both useful.

## Target Customer

Agencies deploying task management into client engagements and watching the executive never log in. Also the task management vendors, for whom cross-boundary delegation from an inbox is a recognisable pattern beyond this industry — every executive-assistant relationship has it.

## Impact If Solved

The task object, assignment, tracking and template machinery gets reused, and the inbox-native handoff, cross-tenant identity, briefing prompts, round-trip metric and context-linked recurring tasks get built. Concretely: a system the executive never has to open and which still improves how they brief.

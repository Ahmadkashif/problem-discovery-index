# Buy: Recruiting and Assessment Tools Adapted to a Working Relationship

**Industry:** [[industries/virtual-assistant-services|Virtual Assistant Services]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Recruiting technology matches a candidate to a role specification; here the role is one person and the thing being matched is how two people will work together.
**Tags:** #gradient-boosting #matrix-decompositions #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #compliance #data-integration #automation #descriptive-statistics
**Contested on:** Whether role-based recruiting tooling can match a person to a person.

## The Problem

Recruiting technology is abundant. Applicant tracking, candidate sourcing, skills assessment, structured interviewing and matching algorithms are all mature, and an agency placing assistants uses some of it.

The category matches candidates to roles: a specification of required skills and experience against a profile of held skills and experience. Here the role is one specific executive with their own communication style, tolerance for error, briefing habits and expectations about autonomy, and the failures are almost never about skill. An assistant who can do everything on the task list and cannot work with this particular person is a failed placement, and nothing in recruiting technology has a representation for that.

## What Already Exists

Applicant tracking systems, sourcing platforms, skills assessment products, structured interview tooling, work-sample testing, and matching algorithms in staffing and freelance platforms. Personality and working-style assessments, with their well-documented validity debates. Reference checking services.

## The Customization Gap

**The counterparty is an individual, not a role, and nobody profiles them.** Matching requires characterising the executive as carefully as the assistant — communication style, autonomy granted, briefing quality, error tolerance — and the intake process profiles the tasks instead. Building the executive-side instrument is the central adaptation and it is a ten-minute structured conversation nobody has designed.

**The outcome label is relationship duration, not hire success.** ATS analytics report time to fill and offer acceptance. What matters here is whether the placement lasted and worked, which arrives months later, is attributed to neither party cleanly, and has to be captured deliberately with a reason code.

**Skills are close to irrelevant above a threshold.** Assessment products discriminate on capability, which in this market is largely a floor rather than a differentiator. Predicting fit means weighting working-style interaction over skill overlap, which inverts the model's emphasis.

**Working-style assessment has to be used carefully.** Personality instruments are commercially available and their predictive validity for this purpose is not established. Using the agency's own outcome data to determine which dimensions actually predict survival is far better than adopting a vendor's framework, and it is the only defensible basis given that the results affect people's livelihoods.

**The population is cross-border contractors.** Assessment localisation, language, and the fairness implications of instruments validated on different populations all matter here in ways the domestic recruiting products do not address, and getting it wrong systematically disadvantages exactly the workforce this industry depends on.

## Target Customer

Agency recruiting and placement leadership running an ATS and finding it says nothing about why placements fail. Also the staffing and assessment vendors, for whom person-to-person matching in placed-assistant and executive-support markets is a distinct and unserved pattern.

## Impact If Solved

The sourcing, tracking, assessment and interview machinery gets kept, and the executive-side profile, the duration outcome label, the style-over-skill weighting, the locally-validated dimensions and the cross-border fairness posture get built. Concretely: matching that predicts whether these two people will work together, which is what the placement actually turns on.

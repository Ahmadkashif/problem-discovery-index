# Matching & Placement Fit

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/virtual-assistant-services|Virtual Assistant Services]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Whether the assistant placed with an executive is the right one, established before three months of context-building are spent finding out.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$600M — 12% of US remote assistance spend
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~12%
**Digital Adoption:** Low — a recruiter reading a profile and a brief
**Target Buyer:** Agency placement and recruiting teams
**Automation Potential:** High — the agency holds every placement it has ever made

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche

Matching is done by a recruiter reading a profile and a client brief, and a mismatch costs three months of context-building before anyone admits it.

The cost structure makes this the highest-leverage decision in the business. A good match compounds: context accumulates, supervision falls, the executive delegates more. A bad match consumes a quarter of executive time in supervision and correction, ends in a replacement, and destroys whatever context was built. The two outcomes are separated by a judgement made in an afternoon from two documents.

The niche is distinct because the agency holds the data that would improve it — every placement it has made, the client characteristics, the assistant characteristics, and whether it lasted — and uses none of it. A firm that has made two thousand placements has a genuine dataset and a recruiter's intuition.

## Current Tools & Gaps

Applicant tracking systems holding assistant profiles. Client intake forms capturing requirements, generally a task list and a timezone. Recruiter judgement. Some agencies run a trial period or a paid test task; a few offer a choice of candidates.

The gaps are the data and the dimensions. Placement outcomes are not fed back into matching, so the agency learns nothing from two thousand attempts. The intake captures tasks and misses the dimensions that actually predict fit — how the executive communicates, how much autonomy they grant, how they react to errors, how much structure they provide. And nothing detects a failing placement early, so it runs until the client complains.

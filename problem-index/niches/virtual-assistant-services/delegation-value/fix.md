# Fix: No Baseline Was Ever Taken

**Niche:** [[niches/virtual-assistant-services/delegation-value/profile|Delegation Value Measurement]]
**Industry:** [[industries/virtual-assistant-services|Virtual Assistant Services]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Nobody recorded how the executive spent their week before the assistant started, so no comparison is possible afterwards at any price.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #hypothesis-testing #workflow-orchestration #quick-win #data-integration #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Whether two weeks of measurement at the start of an engagement will be spent.

## The Problem

An engagement begins. The assistant is onboarded, tasks are handed over, and the arrangement settles into a rhythm. Six months later the client wonders whether it is worth what they are paying.

There is no way to answer. Nobody recorded what the executive's week looked like before — how many hours went to scheduling, to inbox, to travel booking, to the recurring report — so there is nothing to compare against. The assistant's hours are known, the executive's are not, and the counterfactual is unrecoverable.

The onboarding conversation that would have captured it happens anyway. The client is asked what they want to delegate, they list the tasks, and nobody asks how long those tasks currently take or how often they occur. Two extra questions, at a moment when everyone is already talking about exactly this.

## Why It's Still Broken

Onboarding is optimised for starting quickly, because a client who has decided to buy wants the assistant working. A measurement period reads as delay.

The agency also has little reason to establish a baseline that will later be used to evaluate them, and no client has asked for one, because the thought occurs six months later when it is too late.

And the measurement sounds heavier than it is. People imagine time-tracking software and a study; what is actually needed is a structured conversation and two weeks of light calendar categorisation.

## What a Fix Looks Like

Capture the baseline at onboarding, cheaply, as part of the conversation that is already happening.

Ask the two extra questions per task. For each thing being delegated: how often does this happen, and roughly how long does it take you. The client can answer both, they are already listing the tasks, and the answers are the entire baseline. Write them down.

Add two weeks of light calendar categorisation before handover where the client will tolerate it — tagging existing calendar entries and blocking rough time estimates for the non-calendar work. This is much better data than recall and costs the executive a few minutes a day.

Record the goal explicitly. What does the executive want their recovered time for — deep work, more client meetings, leaving at six. The point of the measurement is whether that happened, and it is rarely articulated.

Then review against it at defined points. At thirty, ninety and one hundred and eighty days: which tasks are being handled, how much executive time each still consumes, what the error and rework rate is, and how the recovered time compares to the baseline estimate. The comparison is crude and it is infinitely better than nothing.

Re-scope rather than continue or cancel. Most engagements have some task types working well and some not, and the review's purpose is to move the failing ones back or change how they are briefed. Agencies fear the review because they imagine cancellation; in practice it usually saves the account.

## Who Feels the Pain

Clients, who cannot tell whether they are getting value and eventually cancel on instinct rather than evidence — frequently cancelling arrangements that were working. Assistants, whose good work is invisible and who are replaced for a failure of scoping. Account managers, defending value with a task list. And agencies, who lose accounts to a question they could have answered with two questions asked at the start.

## Impact If Fixed

A crude baseline exists, which makes every later evaluation possible and costs two questions in a conversation that is already happening. Engagements get re-scoped on evidence instead of cancelled on feeling. And the industry's central claim becomes checkable, which is the precondition for anyone competing on it.

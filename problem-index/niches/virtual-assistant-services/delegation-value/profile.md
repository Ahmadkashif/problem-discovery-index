# Delegation Value Measurement

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/virtual-assistant-services|Virtual Assistant Services]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Whether the delegation saved the executive any time.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.0B — 20% of US spend on remote executive and operational assistance
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~20%
**Digital Adoption:** None — hours delivered is the only number anyone produces
**Target Buyer:** Clients; agency leadership willing to be measured
**Automation Potential:** High — the evidence sits in the client's calendar and messaging

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche

The service is sold as recovered executive hours, billed as delivered assistant hours, and the difference between those two is measured by nobody.

These are different quantities that can move in opposite directions. An assistant delivering thirty hours a week may recover twenty of the executive's, or five, or none — and a delegation that requires more supervision than the task saves is a common and undiagnosed failure. The back-and-forth to brief a task, the review, the rework, the interruptions to clarify: all of these consume executive time and none is counted against the hours delivered.

The niche is distinct because the measurement crosses the boundary between the vendor and the customer. The evidence is in the client's calendar and communication systems, which the assistant already has access to, and the finding may be that the client should buy less.

## Current Tools & Gaps

Time tracking on the assistant's side, producing hours delivered by category. Client satisfaction surveys. Task management tools where used. Some agencies produce a monthly report of tasks completed, which is a list rather than a measurement.

The gaps are the executive's side entirely. Nobody measures the briefing and review time the delegation consumes, the rework rate, the error rate on delegated work, or what happened to the executive's own calendar. There is no baseline taken before the engagement starts, so even a crude before-and-after is impossible after the fact. And nothing identifies which task types are net positive for this executive and which are not, which is the most actionable finding available.

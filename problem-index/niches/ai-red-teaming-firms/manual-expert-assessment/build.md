# Novelty That Cannot Be Demonstrated

**Niche:** [[niches/ai-red-teaming-firms/manual-expert-assessment/profile|Manual Expert Assessment]]
**Industry:** [[industries/ai-red-teaming-firms|AI Red Teaming Firms]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A client pays for findings their own team would not have reached, and nothing in a report distinguishes a genuinely novel discovery from something already in a public dataset.

**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this sub-niche is fighting to produce the finding the client's own team and every automated suite would have missed — and whoever does that takes the engagement, because a client with an internal red team is paying for exactly one thing.

## The Problem
A firm delivers eleven findings. Two are genuinely novel — approaches nobody has published and the client's team had not considered. Nine are variations on techniques in public datasets that the client's own automated suite would have caught had it been run more thoroughly. The report presents all eleven identically. The client, who is paying premium rates for novelty, cannot tell which two justified the engagement, and the firm cannot demonstrate it. Renewal is decided on impression and relationship, which is how a judgement business with no measure of its own output ends up competing on price.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Demonstrating novelty requires a comprehensive record of what is already known, which means maintaining the technique catalogue that nobody has built. It also means admitting that nine of eleven findings were not novel, which is uncomfortable in a document the client is paying for. The research community moves fast enough that today's novel finding is public in a month. And the firms' value proposition has never had to be evidenced because the market is young and demand exceeds supply.

## What to Build
Make novelty a measured property of a finding. Maintain the technique catalogue with provenance, so every finding can be checked against what is publicly known and against what the firm has found before — which is the precondition and is the same asset the parent niche identifies. Classify each finding as novel, a variant, or a known technique, with the reference attached, which is the honest presentation and is the one that lets a client see what they bought. Report novel findings separately and prominently, since burying two novel findings among nine known ones understates the engagement's value to the firm's own detriment. Track novelty rate per researcher and per engagement as an internal measure, since it is the output metric this business does not have and cannot improve without. Feed known-technique findings back to the client as a coverage gap in their own automated testing, which is a genuinely useful secondary deliverable and reframes a weak finding as a process observation. Accumulate novel techniques into the catalogue, so the firm's capability compounds rather than living in individuals. Timestamp and register novel findings, since the field moves fast and priority matters for the firm's reputation. And publish the non-sensitive ones, since a firm that visibly advances the field is buying the thing this business runs on.

## Target Customer
Labs and enterprises buying expert engagements, the firms competing for them, and the researchers whose original work is currently indistinguishable from a catalogue run.

## Impact If Built
A judgement business with no measure of its own output competes on relationship and price. Classifying findings by novelty against a maintained catalogue lets a client see what they bought, and it gives the firm the output metric it cannot improve without.

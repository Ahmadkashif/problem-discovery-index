# One Document for Two Audiences

**Niche:** [[niches/ai-red-teaming-firms/the-report-writer/profile|The Report Writer]]
**Industry:** [[industries/ai-red-teaming-firms|AI Red Teaming Firms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A single report tries to serve an engineering team who need something to change and a compliance function who need something to file, and gives each of them a document mostly written for the other.
**Tags:** #compliance #worker-facing #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #automation #data-integration #quick-win #workflow-orchestration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get a finding from a researcher's head into two audiences' hands without the researcher writing documents for a week — and whoever does that takes the account, because that week is the industry's most expensive time spent on its least skilled task.

## The Problem
The engineer opens the report looking for what to change and finds forty pages of methodology, scope, regulatory context and executive summary, with the actionable content distributed through it. The compliance officer opens the same report looking for evidence that testing was performed against their obligations and finds technical detail they cannot evaluate and no explicit mapping to their framework. Both skim. The engineer works from a verbal summary in a call; the compliance officer files the document and cites its existence. The week the researcher spent writing produced something that neither audience actually used.

## Why It's Still Broken
One document is one deliverable, which is simpler to scope and price. The two audiences are frequently represented by one buyer who did not specify either need precisely. Writing two documents doubles the effort under the current manual process, which makes it obviously unaffordable and forecloses the question. And neither audience complains, because both have adapted.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Produce the artefact each audience needs. Generate an engineering deliverable that is a prioritised, actionable work list with reproductions attached and nothing else, which is what the engineer will actually use and is a different document rather than a section. Generate a compliance deliverable that maps findings and coverage to the client's obligations, states methodology and scope, and carries the evidence a filing needs — which is what the compliance officer needs and is currently buried. Push the engineering findings directly into the client's tracker rather than delivering a document to be retyped, since the deliverable's job is to cause a change and a document is a step away from that. Give the compliance deliverable a stable structure so it can be compared year on year, which is what a regulator reads it for. Keep one underlying record so the two never disagree, which is what generation from structured findings makes possible and hand-writing does not. Add an executive summary that states what was examined and what was not, since that is the only part a board reads and it is currently the least precise part of the document. Ask each audience whether their artefact was usable, which nobody does. And measure whether findings get closed, because that is the only test of whether the engineering deliverable worked.

## Who Feels the Pain
Engineers working from a call because the report is unusable; compliance officers filing a document they cannot evaluate; and researchers spending a week producing something neither audience reads.

## Impact If Fixed
Two generated artefacts from one structured record cost little more than one hand-written document and give each audience something they will use. Pushing engineering findings straight into the client's tracker removes the step between a report and a change.

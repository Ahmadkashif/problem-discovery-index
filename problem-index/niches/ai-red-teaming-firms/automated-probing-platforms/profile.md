# Automated Probing Platforms

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/ai-red-teaming-firms|AI Red Teaming Firms]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this sub-niche is fighting to cover broad ground cheaply and stay current as models are updated against known techniques — and whoever does that takes the account, because the alternative is a public dataset that goes stale.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$70M US and the faster-growing half
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~12% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Moderate — adopted for compliance breadth
**Target Buyer:** Engineering and security teams with many deployed systems
**Automation Potential:** Very High — the product is the automation

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Automated probing runs a catalogue of known techniques against a system continuously, which is cheap breadth and is what a team with twenty deployed applications and no specialists actually needs. Its competitor is a public jailbreak dataset and an afternoon of scripting, which is genuinely available and genuinely works for a while. What a product must add is currency — incorporating new techniques as the field moves and retiring ones that models have been updated against — plus the operational surface of running continuously across many systems, and a result that a team can act on. A probing platform whose catalogue is six months old is selling reassurance at the moment it is least warranted.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Probe suites, open attack datasets, scheduled scanning, and reporting. The gaps: catalogue currency is unstated, so a stale suite looks identical to a fresh one; results are reported as pass or fail without success rates; no adaptation to the system under test, so effort is spent on irrelevant categories; findings are not prioritised by what the client can change; and there is no measure of what the automated ground covers relative to the whole risk surface.

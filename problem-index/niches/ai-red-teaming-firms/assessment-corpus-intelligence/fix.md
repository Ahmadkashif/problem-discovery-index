# Paid by the Party Who Would Be Embarrassed

**Niche:** [[niches/ai-red-teaming-firms/assessment-corpus-intelligence/profile|Assessment Corpus Intelligence]]
**Industry:** [[industries/ai-red-teaming-firms|AI Red Teaming Firms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The findings most useful to the field are the ones least welcome to the clients funding the work, and no mechanism exists that lets a firm publish them without losing the account.
**Tags:** #compliance #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #confidence-intervals #worker-facing #hypothesis-testing #quick-win #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn thousands of assessments into an empirical account of which defences actually work — and whoever publishes it defines the field's standard of evidence, which is worth more than the clients it would embarrass.

## The Problem
A firm establishes across forty engagements that a widely-sold class of guardrail product reduces failure rates far less than its marketing claims, and that a simple prompt structure change outperforms it. That finding would materially improve the security of every deployment in the field. The guardrail vendor is a client. Publishing costs the firm a relationship, and possibly several, since vendors talk. The firm keeps it internal, advises its own clients informally, and the rest of the field continues buying on marketing. This dynamic operates on every uncomfortable finding the corpus would produce.

## Why It's Still Broken
The economics are simple and adverse: the firms are paid by the parties a comparative finding would embarrass, and no individual firm can absorb the cost of publishing first. There is no convening body to publish on the field's behalf. Contracts cover engagement findings without distinguishing the client-specific from the general. And the absence is invisible, since nobody knows what is not being published.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Change who publishes and what the contract covers. Separate client-specific findings from general technical findings in every engagement contract, reserving the right to publish the latter in abstracted form — which is a one-clause change, is usually acceptable when asked at contracting time rather than at publication time, and is the practical route through the whole problem. Publish through a neutral convener where one can be established, since an aggregate finding attributed to an industry body costs no single firm a relationship and is the structural answer. Publish aggregate efficacy without naming vendors first, since the finding that a class of defence underperforms is most of the value and names little. Give vendors advance notice and a right of reply, which is standard disclosure practice and converts an ambush into a process. Report methodology fully, since an uncomfortable finding will be attacked on method and only a published method survives that. Build the corpus and publish the analysis even where individual findings cannot be shared, because the aggregate is the point. Start with findings that embarrass nobody, establishing the practice before testing it. And be explicit that the field's current evidence base is vendor marketing, since naming that is what creates demand for the alternative.

## Who Feels the Pain
Defenders buying mitigations on marketing; the firms sitting on findings that would improve everyone's security; and the field, whose standard of evidence is set by whoever is selling.

## Impact If Fixed
A one-clause separation of general technical findings from client-specific ones, agreed at contracting time rather than at publication time, is the practical route through. Aggregate efficacy findings carry most of the value and name almost nobody.

# Assessment Corpus Intelligence

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/ai-red-teaming-firms|AI Red Teaming Firms]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn thousands of assessments into an empirical account of which defences actually work — and whoever publishes it defines the field's standard of evidence, which is worth more than the clients it would embarrass.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$50M US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~8% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** None — the only cross-model failure record, unused
**Target Buyer:** The firms themselves, and the defenders who would benefit
**Automation Potential:** Very High — the corpus is structured if it is recorded

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
These firms accumulate the only cross-model, cross-deployment record of what actually fails: thousands of assessments with the probe, the model, the configuration, the defence in place and the outcome. That corpus answers questions the field cannot otherwise settle — which defensive measures actually work across deployments, which failure classes generalise across models, and how much of a model's robustness is specific to it rather than general. The obstacle is commercial rather than technical: publishing comparative defensive efficacy would make some clients' vendors look bad, some findings would be uncomfortable, and the firms are paid by the parties who would be embarrassed.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Per-engagement records, internal knowledge, and published research on individual techniques. The gaps: probes and outcomes are not recorded in a common schema across engagements; no analysis of defensive efficacy across deployments; no account of which failure classes transfer between models; no measure of technique decay; and nothing published, which leaves defenders choosing mitigations on marketing.

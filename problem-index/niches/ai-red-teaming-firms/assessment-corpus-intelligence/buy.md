## The Problem
Sharing what you have learned about attacks with people who need it, without exposing the victims or the sources, is the founding problem of threat intelligence, and the security industry built institutions for it: sector sharing organisations, a traffic light protocol for disclosure scope, standard exchange formats, coordinated vulnerability disclosure with timelines, and anonymisation conventions that let a finding travel without its client. The same conflict of interest exists there and was resolved by institutional design, and this field is treating it as a reason not to act.

## What Already Exists
Sector-based information sharing and analysis organisations with membership rules; the traffic light protocol for scoping disclosure; structured threat information exchange formats; coordinated vulnerability disclosure with agreed timelines; attack technique knowledge bases maintained collectively; and anonymisation conventions for sharing incident detail.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to findings that are about a model's behaviour rather than about a software defect. It requires: (1) a technique knowledge base for model attacks, maintained collectively rather than by each firm privately, which is the most obviously transferable institution and does not yet exist in a maintained form; (2) abstraction conventions suited to prompts, since a technique can frequently be described structurally without the specific text, which both protects the client and reduces the risk of publishing something directly usable — this distinction is the key design question; (3) disclosure timelines adapted to a target that can be patched by a model update rather than a code fix, where the remediation path and its duration are different; (4) a convening institution, since the firms will not coordinate spontaneously and every successful sharing regime has one; and (5) explicit handling of the dual-use question, which the security community has long practice with and which is sharper here because the technique and its description are the same artefact.

## Target Customer
Assessment firms, model providers, defenders, standards bodies, and the threat intelligence community whose institutional design is the template.

## Impact If Solved
The identical conflict of interest was resolved in security by institutional design and this field treats it as a reason for inaction. A collectively maintained technique knowledge base is the most transferable institution, and structural abstraction of prompts is the design question that makes sharing safe.

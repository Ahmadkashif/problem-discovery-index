# Government Cost Accounting & Indirect Rates

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/contract-research-labs|Contract Research Labs]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get a research firm's books through a DCAA accounting-system review and its incurred-cost submission accepted on time, and whoever makes a small firm audit-ready without a full-time government accountant takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$350M a year in government-contract accounting software, outsourced accounting and rate-advisory services bought by contract research labs (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~17% of contract research labs' tooling and services spend (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low at small firms — QuickBooks plus spreadsheets for pools and rates; Medium-High at mid-size firms on Unanet or Costpoint
**Target Buyer:** Founder or controller at a 5–100-person research firm
**Automation Potential:** High — allowability coding, pool allocation, rate forecasting and submission schedules are rule-based

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Research firms move onto cost-type contracts earlier in their life than most government contractors, because Phase II SBIR and agency research awards are often cost-reimbursement. That brings FAR Part 31 cost principles, indirect pools and provisional billing rates, DCAA audits and an incurred-cost submission due six months after each fiscal year end under FAR 52.216-7 — at a firm whose finance function may be one part-time bookkeeper. Getting this wrong blocks the next cost-type award.

## Current Tools & Gaps
QuickBooks or Xero with DCAA-oriented timekeeping add-ons; Unanet GovCon and Deltek Costpoint for firms that can afford implementation; outsourced government-contract accountants. Gaps: no running forecast of year-end rates against billing rates; timesheet errors found at audit rather than at entry; incurred-cost schedules rebuilt in Excel every year.

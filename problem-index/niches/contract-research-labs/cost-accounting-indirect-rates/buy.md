# Government-Contract ERP Sized for a Ten-Person Lab

**Niche:** [[niches/contract-research-labs/cost-accounting-indirect-rates/profile|Government Cost Accounting & Indirect Rates]]
**Industry:** [[industries/contract-research-labs|Contract Research Labs]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Government-contract ERPs handle DCAA compliance well and are built for contractors with finance departments, not for a research firm whose controller is the founder.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #feature-engineering #compliance #data-integration #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get a research firm's books through a DCAA accounting-system review and its incurred-cost submission accepted on time, and whoever makes a small firm audit-ready without a full-time government accountant takes the account.

## The Problem
A small research firm needs exactly what Unanet GovCon and Deltek Costpoint do — compliant timekeeping, pool allocation, indirect-rate calculation, incurred-cost schedules — but at a fraction of the configuration and price, and with the accounting knowledge built in rather than supplied by staff.

## What Already Exists
Unanet GovCon (indirect rates, DCAA timesheet rules, incurred-cost support; quote-only pricing aimed at mid-sized contractors), Deltek Costpoint, and QuickBooks add-ons for DCAA timekeeping.

## The Customization Gap
A research-firm edition needs: a pre-built chart of accounts and pool structure typical of SBIR firms (fringe, overhead with lab and facilities costs, G&A with bid-and-proposal and internal R&D); allowability coding suggested from vendor and description; firm-fixed-price SBIR Phase I tracked alongside cost-type Phase II without double rules; guided setup by someone who knows DCAA adequacy reviews; and an incurred-cost submission generated straight from the ledger.

## Target Customer
Founders and bookkeepers at research firms moving from fixed-price Phase I awards to their first cost-type contracts.

## Impact If Solved
An adequate accounting system becomes a weeks-long setup rather than a consultant project, removing the barrier between a small lab and cost-type awards.

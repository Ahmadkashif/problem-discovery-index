# Experimental Records & Research Data

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/contract-research-labs|Contract Research Labs]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to capture each experiment's conditions and raw data at the bench without slowing the scientist, and whoever makes that record complete enough to defend an invention date, a data-rights claim and a final report takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$300M a year in electronic notebooks, research data management and instrument-integration tooling and services at contract research labs (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~15% of contract research labs' tooling and services spend (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — paper notebooks and instrument PCs still hold much of the primary record, and electronic notebooks are often bought and half-used
**Target Buyer:** Director of research operations or chief scientist
**Automation Potential:** High for capture from instruments, low for the scientist's interpretation

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
In a contract lab the experimental record does three jobs at once: it is the evidence behind every deliverable, the proof of when an invention was conceived and reduced to practice, and the basis for asserting which funding generated which data — which decides the legend on a government deliverable. Records must also be segregated by sponsor and, for defense work, by export-control status. A university lab can tolerate a messy notebook; a contract lab pays for it in disputed rights and irreproducible reports.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Paper notebooks witnessed by colleagues; Benchling, LabArchives and Revvity Signals electronic notebooks; raw data on instrument PCs, network drives and USB sticks. Gaps: instrument outputs not linked to the notebook entry that produced them; electronic notebooks configured for biology workflows rather than physical and engineering science; no sponsor or export-control segregation at the record level.

# The Process That Works Only on One University's Tool

**Niche:** [[niches/contract-research-labs/nanotech-spinouts-shared-facilities/profile|Nanotech Spinouts on Shared Facilities]]
**Industry:** [[industries/contract-research-labs|Contract Research Labs]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A startup's process is tuned to one shared tool, and when that tool is down, retired or booked solid, the startup's schedule stops.
**Tags:** #gaussian-processes #transfer-learning #feature-engineering #quick-win #data-integration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor serving these firms is fighting to give a nanotech startup production-grade fabrication and characterisation capability without its own cleanroom, and whoever turns rented tool time into reproducible process recipes the startup owns takes the account.

## The Problem
Processes drift into dependence on one tool's quirks. A maintenance outage or a facility's decision to retire an old deposition system leaves the startup with a recipe nobody can run elsewhere, often in the middle of a sponsor milestone.

## Why It's Still Broken
Nobody qualifies a second tool until the first one fails, because each qualification costs runs and money the startup does not have.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A cheap cross-tool qualification routine: a small designed set of calibration runs on a second tool, with a model that maps the known-good recipe to the new tool's settings and confirms equivalence on the key measured properties, kept current as part of the process record.

## Who Feels the Pain
Process engineers; founders facing a milestone; sponsors waiting on samples.

## Impact If Fixed
A qualified second source for each critical step turns a tool outage from a stopped schedule into a one-week detour.

# A Transition Map from Prototype to Program Element

**Niche:** [[niches/contract-research-labs/defense-applied-research/profile|Defense & Intelligence Applied R&D]]
**Industry:** [[industries/contract-research-labs|Contract Research Labs]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Research firms finish prototypes without knowing which budget line could fund the next phase, because nobody maps their results to the program elements and budget exhibits where follow-on money is planned two years out.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #word-embeddings #k-nearest-neighbors #graph-theory #revenue-impact #workflow-orchestration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn a funded prototype into a funded next phase with a transition sponsor, and whoever puts the evidence a program office needs — test data at a stated readiness level, a cost to field, a sponsor commitment — in front of it before the budget is built takes the follow-on.

## The Problem
A Phase II SBIR or a DARPA seedling ends with a working prototype and a final report. The follow-on depends on a program office that has money in a future year's budget for exactly this capability, and that money is planned in budget justification documents long before the prototype is finished. PIs learn of the right program office late, often after the budget window in which they could have been written in has closed.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The budget exhibits are public but enormous, written in program-element prose, and updated annually; matching them to a small firm's technology is reading work no one is paid to do. Opportunity-intelligence vendors focus on solicitations, which come after the budget decision, and transition offices inside the services track their own portfolios rather than a firm's.

## What to Build
A map that reads the firm's prototypes, final reports and test data alongside the services' RDT&E budget justification books and program-office announcements, links each technology to the program elements whose stated goals it matches, and tracks those lines year over year. For each match it lists what evidence the program office would need — readiness level, test conditions, cost to field — and what the firm already has, and schedules the conversations before the budget is built.

## Target Customer
Business development leads and CTOs at defense-focused SBIR firms and research institutes with several Phase II efforts nearing completion.

## Impact If Built
A firm with ten Phase II efforts that converts one or two more into Phase III or program-of-record funding adds millions in follow-on revenue a year (estimate), and stops discovering the right sponsor a budget cycle too late.

# Defense & Intelligence Applied R&D

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/contract-research-labs|Contract Research Labs]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn a funded prototype into a funded next phase with a transition sponsor, and whoever puts the evidence a program office needs — test data at a stated readiness level, a cost to field, a sponsor commitment — in front of it before the budget is built takes the follow-on.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$700M a year in capture, transition, compliance and research-data tooling and services bought by contract research labs doing defense and intelligence work (estimate); the underlying market is DoD's $56.1B of FY2024 R&D obligations to businesses (NSF NCSES), most of which goes to large primes rather than research labs
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~35% of contract research labs' tooling and services spend (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — cost systems and portals are digital; transition planning lives in slide decks and the PI's relationships
**Target Buyer:** Vice President of Business Development or Chief Technology Officer at an SBIR-active firm or institute
**Automation Potential:** Medium — evidence assembly and budget-exhibit tracking are automatable; the sponsor relationship is not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Defense R&D is funded in phases by program managers at AFRL, ONR, Army DEVCOM, DARPA and their peers, and a research result has value to the firm only if it moves into a funded next phase: a Phase II, a Phase III, an option, or adoption by a program of record. The gap between a working prototype and a program office that has budgeted for it is the industry's "valley of death", and it runs on the defense budget calendar, roughly two years ahead of execution (estimate). Work here also carries export control, CUI handling and CMMC requirements that commercial research does not.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Deltek GovWin IQ and HigherGov for opportunities; Costpoint or Unanet for cost; CRM in Salesforce or a spreadsheet; budget justification books read by hand; test reports in Word. The gaps: no record linking a prototype's test evidence to the program elements that could fund it; readiness-level claims unsupported by test records; and transition contacts held in one PI's inbox.

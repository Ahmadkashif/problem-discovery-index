# Contract Lifecycle Tools Fitted to Research Agreements

**Niche:** [[niches/contract-research-labs/single-client-contract-rd/profile|Single-Client Contract R&D]]
**Industry:** [[industries/contract-research-labs|Contract Research Labs]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Contract lifecycle software reviews sales and procurement contracts well, and misses the clauses that matter in a research agreement: background IP, publication rights, sample retention and results ownership.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #bert #transformers #compliance #workflow-orchestration #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this sub-niche is fighting to answer one sponsor's technical question inside a fixed scope and hand over the results and IP with no ownership dispute, and whoever delivers a result the sponsor can act on with the cleanest handover gets the next statement of work.

## The Problem
Every bilateral study starts with a research agreement negotiated against the sponsor's template. Sponsors' procurement teams send purchase-order terms written for buying parts; the lab's contracts staff must spot the clauses that would give away background IP, bar publication, or make the lab liable for the sponsor's product.

## What Already Exists
Contract lifecycle management platforms such as Ironclad, DocuSign CLM and Agiloft, with AI clause extraction and playbooks.

## The Customization Gap
A research playbook needs: background versus foreground IP definitions and carve-outs; licence-back rights for the lab's own methods; publication and confidentiality windows; specimen, sample and data retention; export-control representations; limits on warranties for research results; and linkage to the lab's ledger of existing background IP so negotiators know what to protect.

## Target Customer
Contracts managers and general counsel at independent institutes and private research labs.

## Impact If Solved
Agreements close faster and with fewer give-aways, and ownership disputes at handover — the most damaging failure in bilateral work — become rare.

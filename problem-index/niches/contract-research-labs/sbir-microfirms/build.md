# Which Topics a Firm Like Ours Actually Converts

**Niche:** [[niches/contract-research-labs/sbir-microfirms/profile|SBIR-Funded Microfirms]]
**Industry:** [[industries/contract-research-labs|Contract Research Labs]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Once agencies cap proposals per firm, a ten-person lab needs to know which topics firms like it actually win and carry to Phase II, and nothing tells it.
**Tags:** #word-embeddings #transformers #logistic-regression #k-nearest-neighbors #evaluation-metrics #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor serving these firms is fighting to turn a capped number of proposal slots into Phase II awards — agencies set per-firm proposal limits from FY2027 — and whoever helps a firm pick the topics it can convert and set up Phase II from its Phase I results takes the account.

## The Problem
A founder scans a topic list of hundreds and bids on whatever looks close to the firm's expertise. Some topics have an obvious incumbent, some are written for hardware the firm cannot build, some agencies rarely fund Phase II from that office. The founder learns this by losing, one cycle at a time, and from FY2027 every losing bid also uses up a capped slot.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Award data is public but shows winners only; opportunity vendors sell coverage and alerts, not conversion odds; and SBA data cannot reliably link a Phase I to its Phase II, which makes the most useful label hard to build.

## What to Build
A triage tool that embeds each new topic and finds similar past topics and their awards, estimates whether an incumbent exists, profiles the topic office's Phase II follow-through, and scores the fit against the firm's own past awards and reports. It recommends where to spend the firm's allowance per agency and records the decision so outcomes can be graded.

## Target Customer
Founder-PIs at SBIR firms of 1–25 people submitting across several agencies.

## Impact If Built
Concentrating a capped allowance on winnable topics could lift a microfirm's win rate from roughly one in five toward one in three (estimate), the difference between a funded year and a layoff.

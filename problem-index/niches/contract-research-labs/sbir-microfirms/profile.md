# SBIR-Funded Microfirms

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/contract-research-labs|Contract Research Labs]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor serving these firms is fighting to turn a capped number of proposal slots into Phase II awards — agencies set per-firm proposal limits from FY2027 — and whoever helps a firm pick the topics it can convert and set up Phase II from its Phase I results takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$250M a year in proposal help, opportunity tracking, compliance and accounting services bought by research firms of 1–25 people living mainly on SBIR and STTR awards (estimate); the underlying programme awarded $4.4B in SBIR and $662M in STTR in FY2022 (CRS)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~12% of contract research labs' tooling and services spend (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium-Low — agency portals are digital; everything around them is Word, email and the founder's memory
**Target Buyer:** Founder and principal investigator
**Automation Potential:** Medium — topic triage, compliance and reuse are automatable; the technical idea is not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A microfirm is usually a founder-scientist and a few staff, often spun out of a university or a national lab, whose revenue is SBIR and STTR awards. DoD made 34,704 SBIR/STTR awards to 6,093 small businesses over FY2012–2023 (National Academies). These firms are too small for capture staff, government-contract consultants or enterprise tools, and they were hit hardest by the programme's six-month lapse from October 2025 to April 2026. The FY2027 proposal caps change their strategy from submitting widely to choosing well. The grant-writing side of this market is covered under [[niches/grant-writers/sbir-sttr/profile|SBIR/STTR Grant Specialists]]; this niche is the performing firm itself.

## Current Tools & Gaps
SBIR.gov and agency portals (DSIP, Research.gov, eRA Commons); free topic searches and paid alerts from HigherGov or GovWin; state SBIR assistance offices and accelerators; QuickBooks. Gaps: no view of which topics a firm like this actually wins; Phase I reports written to close a contract rather than to win Phase II; compliance requirements (foreign-affiliation disclosures, NIST SP 800-171 self-assessment) handled ad hoc.

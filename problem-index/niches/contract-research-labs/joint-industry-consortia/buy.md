# Data-Sharing Platforms Fitted to Member Tiers and Embargoes

**Niche:** [[niches/contract-research-labs/joint-industry-consortia/profile|Joint Industry Programmes & Research Consortia]]
**Industry:** [[industries/contract-research-labs|Contract Research Labs]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Cloud data-sharing platforms can publish datasets to many organisations; they do not know that a consortium's access depends on membership year, tier and an embargo date.
**Tags:** #feature-engineering #data-integration #compliance #automation #workflow-orchestration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this sub-niche is fighting to produce pre-competitive test data no single member could afford to generate alone, and whoever turns each year's results into data members can drop straight into their own design and qualification decisions gets the renewals.

## The Problem
Consortium access rules are intricate: founding members see everything, later joiners see results from their entry year, some tiers see summaries only, and results stay embargoed until a member review. Labs enforce this by emailing different PDFs to different people.

## What Already Exists
Snowflake and Databricks data sharing, SharePoint extranets, and scientific data portals.

## The Customization Gap
A consortium portal needs: entitlement rules by member, tier and year of participation; embargo and review workflows before release; watermarking and download logs per member; separation of member-confidential results from public summaries; and a schema for test conditions and specimens that survives a decade of programme changes.

## Target Customer
Programme directors and IT leads at institutes running several consortia.

## Impact If Solved
Access rules are enforced by the system rather than by inbox discipline, and delivering data instead of PDFs becomes safe enough to do.

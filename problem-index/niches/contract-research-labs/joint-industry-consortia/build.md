# A Member Data Product Instead of an Annual Report

**Niche:** [[niches/contract-research-labs/joint-industry-consortia/profile|Joint Industry Programmes & Research Consortia]]
**Industry:** [[industries/contract-research-labs|Contract Research Labs]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Consortia spend millions generating test data and deliver it as a PDF, so members rebuild the dataset by hand before they can use it.
**Tags:** #gaussian-processes #linear-regression #feature-engineering #confidence-intervals #data-integration #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this sub-niche is fighting to produce pre-competitive test data no single member could afford to generate alone, and whoever turns each year's results into data members can drop straight into their own design and qualification decisions gets the renewals.

## The Problem
A member engineer wants to know how a material behaves at a condition the programme did not test exactly. The answer is somewhere in five annual reports, as plotted points. They digitise the figures, fit their own curves and hope they read the axes right.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Programme budgets go to testing; data delivery is an afterthought owned by whoever writes the report. Labs worry that releasing raw data weakens their hold on members, when the opposite is more likely.

## What to Build
A members' data product: every test result with conditions, specimen metadata and uncertainty in a consistent schema across years; fitted response surfaces (Gaussian-process or regression models) members can query at untested conditions with intervals; and downloads in the formats members' design tools accept. Usage analytics show which results members rely on, which feeds next year's agenda.

## Target Customer
Programme directors running multi-year joint industry programmes with ten or more paying members.

## Impact If Built
Members get engineering-ready data on day one, renewal conversations rest on visible usage rather than goodwill, and a programme that retains two or three more members a year at typical fees of tens of thousands of dollars each funds its own data platform (estimate).

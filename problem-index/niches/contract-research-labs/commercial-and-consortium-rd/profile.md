# Commercial & Consortium Contract R&D

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/contract-research-labs|Contract Research Labs]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is that every competitor is fighting to be the outside lab a company trusts with a technical problem it cannot solve in-house — but single-client work is won by solving one sponsor's problem inside a fixed scope and handing over results and IP cleanly, while consortium work is won by producing shared pre-competitive data that members keep paying for year after year. Different buyers, clocks and deliverables, so it is decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$450M a year in project management, IP and agreement, data-delivery and analysis tooling and services bought by labs doing industry-funded research (estimate); the underlying work is several billion dollars of industry-sponsored research at independent institutes and private labs (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~22% of contract research labs' tooling and services spend (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — project accounting is digital; scoping, IP tracking and results delivery are documents and email
**Target Buyer:** Division director or commercial program manager at an independent institute or private research lab
**Automation Potential:** Medium — scoping, agreement review and results packaging are automatable; the experimental work is not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Industry sponsors — engine and vehicle makers, energy companies, chemical and materials firms, device makers — buy research from independent institutes such as Southwest Research Institute (about $966M research volume in FY2025) and SRI International, and from private labs, when they lack the facilities or specialists. The money is the sponsor's, the IP is usually the sponsor's, and there is no federal cost-accounting regime, so the work behaves differently from government R&D. Within it sit two businesses: bespoke projects for one company, and joint industry programmes where many companies fund a shared research agenda.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Professional services automation or the institute's ERP for project accounting; contract-management tools for research agreements; SharePoint extranets and PDF reports for results. Gaps: background IP not tracked across sponsors; effort estimates for statements of work made from memory; results delivered as documents rather than usable data.

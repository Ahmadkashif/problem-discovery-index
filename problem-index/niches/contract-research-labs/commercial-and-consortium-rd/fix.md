# The Proposal That Promises What Another Sponsor Owns

**Niche:** [[niches/contract-research-labs/commercial-and-consortium-rd/profile|Commercial & Consortium Contract R&D]]
**Industry:** [[industries/contract-research-labs|Contract Research Labs]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A new proposal reuses a method or dataset that an earlier sponsor's agreement made that sponsor's property, and nobody notices until the earlier sponsor does.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #bert #k-nearest-neighbors #compliance #quick-win #workflow-orchestration
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is that every competitor is fighting to be the outside lab a company trusts with a technical problem it cannot solve in-house — but single-client work is won by solving one sponsor's problem inside a fixed scope and handing over results and IP cleanly, while consortium work is won by producing shared pre-competitive data that members keep paying for year after year. Different buyers, clocks and deliverables, so it is decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## The Problem
Engineers write proposals from what they know works, and what they know works was often developed on someone else's money. A test procedure refined for one automaker appears in a proposal to another; a consortium dataset is cited as the lab's own baseline in a bilateral bid. Each instance risks a breach claim and, worse, a sponsor's trust.

## Why It's Still Broken
Proposal writers are engineers, not lawyers; agreements are filed by contracts staff who never see proposals; and nothing compares the two before a proposal goes out.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A pre-submission check that compares each proposal's described methods, datasets and prior-results claims against the lab's agreements and final reports, flags passages that rely on another sponsor's foreground or a consortium's member-only results, and routes them to contracts staff with the relevant clause.

## Who Feels the Pain
Division directors and general counsel managing sponsor relationships; engineers who did not know; sponsors whose results resurface elsewhere.

## Impact If Fixed
A cheap check before submission prevents the disputes that cost a lab its best repeat sponsors.

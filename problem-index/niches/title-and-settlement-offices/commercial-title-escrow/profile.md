# Commercial Title & Escrow

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/title-and-settlement-offices|Title & Settlement Offices]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to deliver a marked pro forma policy, with every exception reconciled to the ALTA/NSPS survey and the endorsements lender's counsel demands, before the loan's closing deadline, and whoever negotiates exceptions and endorsements fastest across states takes the national accounts.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.5B annual staff time, software and outsourced services spent on commercial title and escrow files (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~12% — the size figure as a share of the industry's ~$12.5B receipts; commercial files account for ~25–30% of revenue (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — documents are digital, but exception review and endorsement negotiation run on marked-up PDFs and calls
**Target Buyer:** Commercial title officer or national commercial services manager
**Automation Potential:** Medium — document review automates; negotiation with lender's counsel does not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A commercial file is few in number and large in premium. An office building, a portfolio of retail sites or an industrial park carries liability in the tens or hundreds of millions, often across several states, with a lender's counsel who reviews every exception and demands a list of ALTA endorsements (zoning, contiguity, access, same-as-survey, non-imputation). The work is reading: dozens of recorded easements, covenants and leases reconciled against the ALTA/NSPS survey (see [[niches/land-surveyors/alta-nsps-surveys/profile|ALTA/NSPS Surveys]] under Land Surveyors), the pro forma policy marked up through several rounds, and local agents coordinated in each state. The buyer is a commercial real estate lawyer or a lender who sends repeat business to whoever turned the last deal's pro forma fastest.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Production systems handle the order and escrow; national commercial desks of the large underwriters and multi-state agencies run the coordination; legal contract-review tools serve the lawyers on the other side. Gaps: no tool links each Schedule B exception to the instrument and to its plotted location on the survey; endorsement availability and pricing by state are tracked in memory and rate manuals; and the redline history of a pro forma is a chain of emails.

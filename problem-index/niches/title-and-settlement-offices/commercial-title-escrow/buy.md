# Contract-Review AI Pointed at the Pro Forma

**Niche:** [[niches/title-and-settlement-offices/commercial-title-escrow/profile|Commercial Title & Escrow]]
**Industry:** [[industries/title-and-settlement-offices|Title & Settlement Offices]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Law firms already use AI contract review to read leases and loan documents; the title side of the same deal still reads covenants and easements by hand.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #bert #evaluation-metrics #compliance #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to deliver a marked pro forma policy, with every exception reconciled to the ALTA/NSPS survey and the endorsements lender's counsel demands, before the loan's closing deadline, and whoever negotiates exceptions and endorsements fastest across states takes the national accounts.

## The Problem
Recorded declarations and easement agreements are long, old and inconsistent. The title officer must find use restrictions, rights of first refusal, reverter clauses and maintenance obligations that matter to the buyer and lender, and summarise them for counsel.

## What Already Exists
Legal contract-review platforms extract clauses from leases, credit agreements and M&A documents and flag deviations from a playbook.

## The Customization Gap
Title needs a playbook built around recorded instruments: clause types that matter to insurability (forfeiture and reverter, use restrictions violated by the current use, access and parking rights, options to purchase), OCR tuned to recorder scans, links from each clause to the Schedule B exception it supports, and a map from clause type to the ALTA endorsement that can cover it.

## Target Customer
Commercial title officers and underwriting counsel at agencies with national commercial practices.

## Impact If Solved
Covenant review moves from a day of reading to an hour of checking, and the officer's comments to counsel arrive with clause citations.

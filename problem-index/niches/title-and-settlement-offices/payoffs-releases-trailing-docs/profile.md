# Payoffs, Lien Releases & Trailing Documents

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/title-and-settlement-offices|Title & Settlement Offices]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is that every competitor is fighting to make sure every existing lien is paid and then released of record — but before closing the contest is getting an accurate, good-through payoff statement from the lienholder in time to balance the Closing Disclosure, and after closing it is proving months later that the release was recorded and the final policy issued. Different counterparties, clocks and vendors win each half. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$600M annual staff time and outsourced services spent on payoffs, releases, recordings and final policies (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~5% of industry receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — payoff requests by fax, phone and servicer web forms; release follow-up by letter and phone
**Target Buyer:** Escrow manager (payoffs); post-closing manager (releases and policies)
**Automation Potential:** High for tracking and requests; medium for exception handling

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
ALTA's 2024 study named loan payoffs, with property taxes and HOA dues, among the most common curative actions in title work. Every lien that existed before closing must be paid from the proceeds and then released of record, and every closing creates trailing work: recorded documents returned, a final policy issued, a premium remitted. The work is unglamorous, counterparty-driven and mostly conducted with servicers' payoff and release departments, which treat title offices as a low-priority queue. It is the part of the file where a perfectly examined title can still go wrong.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Production systems hold the payoff figure and the disbursement; e-recording platforms record outgoing documents; servicer payoff portals exist for the largest servicers; post-closing tracking is often a spreadsheet. Gaps: no single ledger following one lien from payoff request through release recording; HELOC freeze letters and private-lender payoffs handled ad hoc; no servicer-level performance data.

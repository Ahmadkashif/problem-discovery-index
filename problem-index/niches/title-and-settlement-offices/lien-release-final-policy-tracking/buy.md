# E-Recording Platforms Watching for Inbound Satisfactions

**Niche:** [[niches/title-and-settlement-offices/lien-release-final-policy-tracking/profile|Lien Release & Final Policy Tracking]]
**Industry:** [[industries/title-and-settlement-offices|Title & Settlement Offices]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** E-recording platforms already connect title offices to most county recorders, but only for documents the office sends.
**Tags:** #data-integration #workflow-orchestration #automation #compliance #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to prove that every paid lien was released of record and every final policy issued within the statute's and the underwriter's deadlines, and whoever closes the post-closing loop with the fewest open items at audit takes the agency's back office.

## The Problem
The office records the new deed and mortgage electronically and receives confirmation, but the release it needs is recorded by someone else, and the platform does not tell it.

## What Already Exists
E-recording submitters with county connections, recording confirmations and returned images.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is a watch list: the office registers each paid lien, and the platform reports when a satisfaction referencing it is recorded in that county, using the county connection it already holds. Add statutory due dates, servicer-level reporting and export to the underwriter's audit format.

## Target Customer
Agencies already e-recording most of their documents through one platform.

## Impact If Solved
Automatic satisfaction alerts would remove an estimated 70–80% of manual index checks — the share of releases that record on time without chasing (estimate) — leaving the post-closer the late minority, on an integration the office already pays for.

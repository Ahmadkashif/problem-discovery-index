# The Unreleased Mortgage Found at the Next Sale

**Niche:** [[niches/title-and-settlement-offices/lien-release-final-policy-tracking/profile|Lien Release & Final Policy Tracking]]
**Industry:** [[industries/title-and-settlement-offices|Title & Settlement Offices]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A mortgage paid at one closing and never released becomes another office's curative problem when the property sells again years later.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #workflow-orchestration #compliance #quick-win #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to prove that every paid lien was released of record and every final policy issued within the statute's and the underwriter's deadlines, and whoever closes the post-closing loop with the fewest open items at audit takes the agency's back office.

## The Problem
The next examiner finds an open mortgage, the seller swears it was paid, and someone must find the old payoff evidence, the old servicer and the old title office's file. The release that was never chased costs weeks on someone else's closing.

## Why It's Still Broken
The office that closed the first sale had no automatic signal that the release never recorded, and by the time the problem surfaces its post-closer has moved on.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Keep every payoff's evidence — wire confirmation, payoff statement, release demand letters — attached to the lien record, and close the item only when the satisfaction is found in the index; escalate to the underwriter's release-tracking process when the statute lapses.

## Who Feels the Pain
Future examiners and escrow officers; sellers whose sale is delayed; underwriters.

## Impact If Fixed
The release gap closes at the office that created it, not years later at someone else's.

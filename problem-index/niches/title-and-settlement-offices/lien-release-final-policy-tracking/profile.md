# Lien Release & Final Policy Tracking

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/title-and-settlement-offices|Title & Settlement Offices]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to prove that every paid lien was released of record and every final policy issued within the statute's and the underwriter's deadlines, and whoever closes the post-closing loop with the fewest open items at audit takes the agency's back office.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$350M annual post-closing staff time and outsourced post-closing services (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~3% of industry receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — demand letters, phone follow-up and periodic county index checks
**Target Buyer:** Post-closing manager or agency owner
**Automation Potential:** High

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
After funding the clock changes. Release statutes in most states give the lienholder a fixed window, often 30 to 90 days, to record a satisfaction (estimate; varies by state), and underwriters set deadlines for issuing final policies and remitting premium; ALTA's time study treats final policy issuance as a task of its own on every file. The counterparty is the servicer's release department and the county recorder, the evidence is a recorded satisfaction, and the audience is the underwriter's auditor and the next examiner who searches the property. The winner is whoever has the fewest open items when someone looks.

## Current Tools & Gaps
E-recording platforms, county online indexes, production-system post-closing modules and spreadsheets. Gaps: no automatic check that a release was recorded; no servicer-level escalation; final policies assembled by hand from commitments.

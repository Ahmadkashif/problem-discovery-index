# Historical Handwriting Recognition for Deed Books

**Niche:** [[niches/title-and-settlement-offices/courthouse-abstracting/profile|Courthouse Abstracting in Non-Plant Counties]]
**Industry:** [[industries/title-and-settlement-offices|Title & Settlement Offices]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Genealogy and archive projects already transcribe 19th-century handwriting at scale; title searchers still read the same books by eye.
**Tags:** #cnns #transformers #seq2seq #evaluation-metrics #data-integration #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to return a complete, correctly indexed search from a county with no title plant and no reliable online index inside the agent's turn time, and whoever knows each courthouse's indexing quirks and gaps takes that county's orders.

## The Problem
Many counties have scanned their old deed and index books but left them as images. A searcher pages through them on a screen or microfilm reader, one name at a time.

## What Already Exists
Handwritten text recognition platforms used by archives and genealogy services, trainable on a collection's hand, with search across the transcribed text.

## The Customization Gap
Title needs field extraction on top of transcription — grantor, grantee, instrument type, date, consideration, legal description, book and page — plus models fine-tuned per county clerk's hand and era, legal-description parsing into calls and distances, and a confidence score so an abstractor reviews only the uncertain entries.

## Target Customer
Abstract companies and agencies with recurring volume in counties whose older records are imaged but not indexed.

## Impact If Solved
The back-chain search moves from paging through books to querying a transcribed index, with the abstractor checking flagged entries rather than reading every page.

# Courthouse Abstracting in Non-Plant Counties

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/title-and-settlement-offices|Title & Settlement Offices]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to return a complete, correctly indexed search from a county with no title plant and no reliable online index inside the agent's turn time, and whoever knows each courthouse's indexing quirks and gaps takes that county's orders.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$400M annual fees paid to independent abstractors and in-house courthouse searchers (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~3% of industry receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — grantor-grantee index books, deed books on microfilm or paper, and partial online images with short index histories
**Target Buyer:** Agency production manager ordering searches by county; abstract company owner
**Automation Potential:** Medium — transcription and indexing automate; the searcher's knowledge of each courthouse does not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Where no title plant exists, the search is done where the records are: an abstractor in the county courthouse, working the grantor-grantee indexes name by name, back through each owner, then pulling the deed books, judgment dockets, probate files and tax records. Many counties have imaged recent decades but not the older books, or index online only from a recent start date, so a full search still means a trip. Abstractors are mostly one-person shops or small firms covering a handful of rural counties, paid per search by agencies hundreds of miles away. Mineral title work in oil and gas counties uses the same books for runsheets, which is why abstract offices sit in this NAICS code.

## Current Tools & Gaps
County e-records portals (coverage and start dates vary by county), national search-ordering platforms that route orders to local abstractors, and the abstractor's own notes. Gaps: no shared record of each county's indexing start dates, known misindexing patterns or missing volumes; handwritten books untranscribed; and quality is judged only when a missed instrument becomes a claim.

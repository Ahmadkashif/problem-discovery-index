# Genealogy Research Tools Turned to Heirship Affidavits

**Niche:** [[niches/title-and-settlement-offices/heirs-property-title-clearing/profile|Heirs' Property & Estate Title Clearing]]
**Industry:** [[industries/title-and-settlement-offices|Title & Settlement Offices]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Consumer genealogy platforms already build family trees from records; title needs the same tree, with the legal weight of each link spelled out.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #k-nearest-neighbors #data-integration #workflow-orchestration #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to assemble a path to insurable title that the underwriter will accept — affidavit of heirship, probate, quiet title or a partition under the heirs' property act — for a family with no will and many heirs, and whoever locates every heir and gathers the signatures fastest closes the sale.

## The Problem
Curative specialists already use genealogy sites informally to trace heirs, then retype what they find into affidavits.

## What Already Exists
Genealogy platforms with record search, hint matching and family-tree building across census, vital, obituary and military records.

## The Customization Gap
Title needs: evidence grading (a death certificate versus a family hint), intestacy calculations by state and date of death, fractional-interest tables, export into the state's affidavit-of-heirship form, tracking of which heirs have signed, and a record the underwriter can review link by link.

## Target Customer
Title curative departments and real estate attorneys handling several heirship files a year.

## Impact If Solved
Informal genealogy research becomes documented curative work the underwriter can rely on.

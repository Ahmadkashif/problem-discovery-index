# Heirs' Property & Estate Title Clearing

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/title-and-settlement-offices|Title & Settlement Offices]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to assemble a path to insurable title that the underwriter will accept — affidavit of heirship, probate, quiet title or a partition under the heirs' property act — for a family with no will and many heirs, and whoever locates every heir and gathers the signatures fastest closes the sale.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$150M annual curative spend by title offices, closing attorneys and families on estate and heirship title clearing (estimate); the underlying asset base is far larger — a 2023 study counted ~444,000 heirs' parcels covering ~9.25M acres worth ~$41B
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~1% of industry receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — family interviews, courthouse probate files, obituaries and handwritten family trees
**Target Buyer:** Title office curative specialist or closing attorney; heirs' property legal-aid centres
**Automation Potential:** Medium — heir location and document assembly automate; family negotiation does not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Heirs' property is land passed down without a will or probate, so title is held in common by every descendant with a share. It is concentrated among Black landowners in the South, in Appalachia and among rural families. A sale or refinance needs every heir, or a court, and the family usually arrives at the title office with a buyer, a deadline and no idea how many cousins hold an interest. The same problem blocks disaster aid and conservation programmes. The title office cannot insure until the heirship path is one the underwriter accepts, and most offices handle a few such files a year, each from scratch.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Probate and court records, obituaries and genealogy services, affidavit-of-heirship forms that vary by state and underwriter, legal-aid centres in some states. Gaps: no tool builds the heirship tree with evidence for each link; underwriter acceptance rules are not written in one place; and families cannot see what is needed or how long it will take.

# Which Heirship Proof This Underwriter Accepts

**Niche:** [[niches/title-and-settlement-offices/heirs-property-title-clearing/profile|Heirs' Property & Estate Title Clearing]]
**Industry:** [[industries/title-and-settlement-offices|Title & Settlement Offices]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Whether an affidavit of heirship is enough, or probate is required, depends on the state, the years since death and the underwriter, and the office finds out by asking.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #compliance #workflow-orchestration #quick-win #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to assemble a path to insurable title that the underwriter will accept — affidavit of heirship, probate, quiet title or a partition under the heirs' property act — for a family with no will and many heirs, and whoever locates every heir and gathers the signatures fastest closes the sale.

## The Problem
An office prepares an affidavit of heirship, the underwriter's counsel asks for probate instead, and the family's buyer walks away while the court date is set.

## Why It's Still Broken
Acceptance rules sit in underwriting bulletins, state title standards and underwriters' counsel's experience, not in one searchable rule set.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A rule set by state and underwriter — time since death, estate size, number of heirs, presence of a will, whether a partition act applies — that tells the office at order open which path is acceptable, with the underwriter's bulletin cited.

## Who Feels the Pain
Curative specialists; families trying to sell; underwriters' counsel answering the same question repeatedly.

## Impact If Fixed
The office chooses the right path on day one rather than after a rejected affidavit.

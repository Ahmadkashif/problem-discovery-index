# The HELOC Paid but Never Frozen

**Niche:** [[niches/title-and-settlement-offices/payoff-statement-procurement/profile|Payoff Statement Procurement]]
**Industry:** [[industries/title-and-settlement-offices|Title & Settlement Offices]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A home equity line paid off at closing stays open unless the borrower's freeze-and-close request goes with the payoff, and a later draw leaves a lien nobody expected.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #compliance #workflow-orchestration #quick-win #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to obtain an accurate, good-through payoff statement from every lienholder — servicers, HELOC lenders, private noteholders, judgment creditors — before the Closing Disclosure is final, and whoever gets the right number first with no post-funding shortage takes the agency's payoff desk.

## The Problem
Paying a HELOC's balance does not close it. Without a signed instruction to freeze and close the line, the lender will not release the lien, and the seller can still draw on it. The new owner's title is clouded and the agency faces a claim.

## Why It's Still Broken
The freeze letter is a separate document with lender-specific wording, obtained from the seller at or before signing, and its absence is noticed only at release time.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Detect open-end liens at examination, require the lender-specific freeze-and-close instruction as a B-I requirement, attach it to the payoff request automatically, and confirm closure in the payoff statement before disbursement.

## Who Feels the Pain
Post-closers chasing releases that never come; buyers inheriting a clouded title; agencies paying claims.

## Impact If Fixed
A known, preventable claim type is closed off at the payoff step.

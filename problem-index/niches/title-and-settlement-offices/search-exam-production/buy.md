# Underwriter Title AI Tuned to the Agent's Own Practice

**Niche:** [[niches/title-and-settlement-offices/search-exam-production/profile|Title Search & Examination Production]]
**Industry:** [[industries/title-and-settlement-offices|Title & Settlement Offices]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Underwriters are rolling out AI review of search packages; agents need it to reflect their county, their plant and their examiners' standards rather than a national default.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #evaluation-metrics #compliance #data-integration #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn a clean chain into an underwriter-acceptable commitment in minutes and send only the genuinely defective chains to an examiner, and whoever draws that line most accurately takes the lender channel's refinance and purchase volume.

## The Problem
An agent licensing an underwriter's AI review gets flags tuned to the underwriter's national book. Its own examiners know that one county's index routinely double-lists releases, or that a particular subdivision's plat restrictions always appear as exceptions.

## What Already Exists
Underwriter and vendor AI review tools launched in 2026 that read search packages and flag chain breaks and liens, with human determination; automated plant searches.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs agent-level feedback loops (examiner overrides captured and fed back), county-specific rule sets, the agent's own requirement library and wording, and reporting on how often the tool's flags were acted on — so the agent can trust it on clean files and the underwriter can see where it fails.

## Target Customer
Agents licensing an underwriter's or vendor's AI review, and the underwriters' agency-technology teams.

## Impact If Solved
The AI review stops generating noise the examiner ignores and starts removing work the examiner trusts it with.

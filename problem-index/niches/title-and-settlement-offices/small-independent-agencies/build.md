# Compliance Evidence as a By-Product of Daily Work

**Niche:** [[niches/title-and-settlement-offices/small-independent-agencies/profile|Small Independent Title Agencies]]
**Industry:** [[industries/title-and-settlement-offices|Title & Settlement Offices]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A three-person agency spends days before each assessment proving things its systems already know.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #feature-engineering #compliance #data-integration #workflow-orchestration #automation #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep a one-to-four-person agency on its underwriters' and lenders' approved lists — Best Practices evidence, daily escrow reconciliation, cyber and E&O cover, vetting questionnaires — without a compliance hire, and whoever turns that evidence into a by-product of daily work takes the shop.

## The Problem
Best Practices assessments and underwriter audits ask for evidence: daily and monthly three-way escrow reconciliations, policies issued and premiums remitted on time, privacy and information-security controls, wire verification on every disbursement, complaint handling. The owner gathers it from the production system, the bank portal and email, the week before the auditor arrives.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Vendors sell to the larger agencies that have compliance staff; small agencies buy production software and treat compliance as a periodic chore.

## What to Build
A connector layer over the production system, bank feeds and email that records each control as it happens — reconciliation completed and balanced, wire verified, policy issued, premium remitted, access reviewed — maps it to the Best Practices framework and the underwriter's audit checklist, and shows a live readiness score with the exceptions to fix.

## Target Customer
Owners of agencies with one to ten staff; underwriters who would rather audit evidence than files.

## Impact If Built
Assessment preparation shrinks from days to an hour, and the owner learns about a control lapse when it happens rather than at audit.

# Walk Sheets Typed In Days Later, or Never

**Niche:** [[niches/self-storage-operators/unit-audits-overlocks/profile|Unit Audits, Overlocks & Facility Walks]]
**Industry:** [[industries/self-storage-operators|Self-Storage Operators]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The manager finds a lock on a vacant unit, writes it on the walk sheet, and the sheet sits under the counter while the unit goes another month unbilled.
**Tags:** #workflow-orchestration #worker-facing #quick-win #automation #data-integration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to make every door match the ledger, and whoever recovers the most rent lost to ledger-door mismatches per store takes the account.

## The Problem
Audit findings start on paper. Typing them into the facility software is a second job that loses to customers and phone calls. A vacant-but-locked unit needs follow-up: cut the lock, inventory, re-rent or trace the occupant. That follow-up waits for the data entry. District managers see the result only at their next visit.

## Why It's Still Broken
The facility software has no mobile audit screen at many stores, or one that needs a connection the back hallways lack. Nothing tracks whether a finding was resolved, so unresolved findings are invisible.

## What a Fix Looks Like
An offline-capable phone checklist in door order. Each finding is recorded with one tap and a photo, creates a follow-up task with an owner and a due date, and syncs when the phone reconnects. A district dashboard shows open findings by store and age.

## Who Feels the Pain
Store managers doing the walk twice, once on foot and once at the keyboard; district managers auditing blind; owners of managed stores paying for rent that was never billed.

## Impact If Fixed
If each unresolved vacant-but-locked unit waits ~3–4 weeks longer than it should (estimate), at ~$100–150 a month, a 100-store operator with ~3 such units per store a month recovers ~$30,000–45,000 a month.

# Apartment Renewal Pricing Adapted to Month-to-Month Units

**Niche:** [[niches/self-storage-operators/existing-customer-rate-increases/profile|Existing-Customer Rate Increases (ECRI)]]
**Industry:** [[industries/self-storage-operators|Self-Storage Operators]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Multifamily revenue management already prices renewals for millions of apartments. Storage needs the same machinery without lease expiry dates, and without the pooled competitor data that drew antitrust suits against apartment pricing.
**Tags:** #gradient-boosting #survival-analysis #feature-engineering #compliance #revenue-impact #data-integration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to size each existing tenant's increase so the added rent outweighs the move-outs it causes, and whoever earns the most net rent per increase notice after twelve months of induced move-outs takes the account.

## The Problem
A storage operator wants renewal-style pricing but has no renewal date. A tenant can be raised in any month, leave with a few days' notice, and the unit is worth much less to them than an apartment is. Off-the-shelf tools from the apartment world assume a lease expiration calendar and pooled market data.

## What Already Exists
Multifamily revenue-management systems recommend renewal increases unit by unit from demand, competitor rents and lease-expiration exposure. They are profiled at [[niches/property-management/revenue-management-rent-pricing/profile|Revenue Management & Rent Pricing Systems]] (47; ~45 under the current clock ruling, pending Stage E), a pocket under federal and state antitrust litigation over pooling competitors' pricing.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs: an anniversary clock per tenant instead of a lease expiry; move-out hazard modelled monthly, because storage tenants leave in days, not at lease end; segments that matter for storage (business tenants, vehicle storage, climate-controlled units, protection-plan holders); and the gap to the store's own street rate for the same unit type as the main price signal. It also needs each state's notice period and any emergency cap built in, plus a design that uses only the operator's own ledger and public street rates. Pooling private rent rolls across competing operators would import the litigation risk that hangs over apartment pricing.

## Target Customer
Third-party managers and regional operators already running a multifamily-style revenue team, and vendors adapting apartment pricing products to storage.

## Impact If Solved
Reusing a proven renewal engine cuts build time from ~12–18 months to ~4–6 (estimate). Keeping it on own-ledger and public data avoids the pooled-data design behind the apartment-pricing suits.

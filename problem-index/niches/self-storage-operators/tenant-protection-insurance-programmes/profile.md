# Tenant Protection & Tenant Insurance Programmes

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/self-storage-operators|Self-Storage Operators]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to attach tenant protection or tenant insurance at move-in under each state's insurance rules, and whoever earns the most protection margin per occupied unit takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.2–1.8B a year of tenant insurance premiums and protection-plan fees (estimate); Public Storage booked $250.7M of tenant reinsurance premiums and Extra Space $352.9M of tenant reinsurance revenue in 2025
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~6–8% of the industry's receipts-equivalent revenue (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — enrolment is a checkbox in the facility software; pricing, coverage levels and claims are run by administrators on older systems
**Target Buyer:** VP of ancillary revenue at an operator; programme head at an administrator
**Automation Potential:** High for enrolment, coverage sizing and claims triage; Low for state filings

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
After rent, protection is the biggest line most operators earn. It comes in two legal forms. Tenant insurance is a policy from a carrier, sold under a limited-lines licence: Public Storage holds "limited lines self-service storage insurance agent licenses" and reinsures the policies through a captive. A tenant protection plan is a lease addendum in which the operator itself agrees to cover losses. California's Supreme Court held in Heckart v. A-1 Self Storage (2018) that such a plan is not insurance. Trade sources report moratoriums on protection plans in Louisiana and New Mexico (search summary). Which form is allowed, how it is priced and who carries the loss differ by state, which makes this a distinct contest from rent.

## Current Tools & Gaps
REIT captives (Public Storage, Extra Space); Storable Tenant Insurance (the StorSmart and Bader programmes, administered by Property First Group) inside Storable's software; MiniCo (since 1974), SBOA and other administrators; operator-run protection plans (search summaries). Gaps: coverage levels set by default rather than by what is in the unit; claims assessed without the store's gate and lock logs; no shared view of which facilities and units generate losses; state-by-state form choices made by memory.

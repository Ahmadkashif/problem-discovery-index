# The Unit Listed With Three Dark Photos

**Niche:** [[niches/self-storage-operators/lien-auction-execution/profile|Lien Auction Execution & Sale Proceeds]]
**Industry:** [[industries/self-storage-operators|Self-Storage Operators]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The manager cuts the lock at dusk, takes three phone photos into an unlit unit and lists it, and bidders pass on what they cannot see.
**Tags:** #cnns #object-detection #worker-facing #quick-win #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this sub-niche is fighting to sell each lien unit for as much of the unpaid balance as its contents will fetch, and whoever recovers the highest share of the balance owed per auctioned unit takes the account.

## The Problem
Listing photos are taken by store managers with no photography guidance, often in poor light and from rules-limited angles. Units whose contents bidders cannot make out draw fewer bids. The manager does not know the photos were weak until the sale closes low.

## Why It's Still Broken
Marketplaces accept any upload. Managers photograph lien units a few times a month at most, so they never build the habit. Nobody connects photo quality to final price where the manager can see it.

## What a Fix Looks Like
An in-app photo guide that checks each shot as it is taken. It flags darkness, blur and missing angles (left, right, rear, ceiling height), prompts the manager to use the phone's light, and confirms the set covers the unit before upload. It can also suggest a short, factual description of visible categories for the listing.

## Who Feels the Pain
Store managers judged on lien recovery; operators recovering less of each balance; tenants whose surplus (if any) shrinks.

## Impact If Fixed
If guided photos lift average hammer prices by ~5–10% (estimate), an operator selling 2,000 units a year at a few hundred dollars each recovers ~$25,000–60,000 more, for a feature that costs the marketplace little.

# Single-Facility Independent Owners

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/self-storage-operators|Self-Storage Operators]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to lift a one-store owner's rent roll without a revenue manager on staff, and whoever grows the owner's same-store rental income most over twelve months takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$6.9B of receipts at the 8,619 firms with under 20 employees (Census SUSB 2022); their software, pricing and management spend is ~$300–500M a year (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~33% of receipts and ~98% of employer firms (Census SUSB 2022)
**Digital Adoption:** Low–Medium — most use facility software for billing; rates and increases are set by the owner by hand
**Target Buyer:** Owner-operator
**Automation Potential:** High for pricing and collections, Low for the decision to sell or hire a manager

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The owner of one or two facilities, often a family that built a drive-up site decades ago, competes with REITs that reprice daily. Public Storage says the four largest owners hold ~22% of US storage square footage and regional and local operators the remaining 78%. The REITs are buying: Public Storage closed National Storage Affiliates in 2026 and acquired 48 more facilities in the first nine months alone. The owner's alternatives are to run the store alone, hire a third-party manager (Extra Space manages 1,856 stores for owners, CubeSmart 862, Public Storage 417), or sell. Every vendor competing for this owner is really competing against those exits, measured in same-store rental income. Stores this size often skip increases for years or apply one flat increase to everyone.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Storable's Sitelink, Storable Edge and Storable Easy; OpenUnit's free software with "price & revenue management tools"; Storeganise; third-party management for a fee based on a percentage of revenue. Gaps: no comp-set rate board; no ECRI logic beyond a flat percentage; no forecast of what a third-party manager or a sale would actually add; pricing tools priced for portfolios.

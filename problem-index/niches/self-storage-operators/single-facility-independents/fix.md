# Increases Skipped for Years Because Nobody Had Time

**Niche:** [[niches/self-storage-operators/single-facility-independents/profile|Single-Facility Independent Owners]]
**Industry:** [[industries/self-storage-operators|Self-Storage Operators]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** At many family-owned stores, tenants who moved in five years ago still pay their move-in rate, because sending increases is a job nobody got round to.
**Tags:** #survival-analysis #workflow-orchestration #compliance #quick-win #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to lift a one-store owner's rent roll without a revenue manager on staff, and whoever grows the owner's same-store rental income most over twelve months takes the account.

## The Problem
The owner knows increases are standard but dreads the phone calls, lacks the time to write letters, and does not know how big an increase is safe. So nothing happens, then a big catch-up increase lands on everyone at once, and some long-term tenants leave.

## Why It's Still Broken
Facility software lets owners change rates but does not suggest who, when or how much. Notice rules differ by state. The owner's fear of move-outs is unmeasured.

## What a Fix Looks Like
An "increase due" list each month in the owner's software. It shows tenants past their anniversary, their in-place rent against the current street rate for the same unit, and a suggested modest increase from pooled move-out curves. Notices are pre-written and dated to the state's notice rule (30 days in California), and one click sends them with delivery proof. A quarterly result shows the money earned and the move-outs that followed.

## Who Feels the Pain
Owners leaving income on the table; store managers taking the calls after a catch-up increase; long-term tenants hit by a single large jump instead of small regular ones.

## Impact If Fixed
If ~40% of a 400-unit store's tenants are ~15% below street rate (estimate) at ~$100 a month, modest regular increases closing half that gap add ~$14,000 a year. The single large catch-up increase that drives move-outs is avoided.

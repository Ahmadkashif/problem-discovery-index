# Vehicle, RV & Boat Storage Lots

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/self-storage-operators|Self-Storage Operators]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to fill outdoor and covered vehicle spaces with the vehicles that pay most for the space they take, and whoever earns the most rental income per acre takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.0–1.5B a year of vehicle, RV and boat parking rent at storage operators and dedicated lots (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~5–7% of receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — spaces are tracked as if they were units, lengths on paper, waitlists in spreadsheets
**Target Buyer:** Owner of a dedicated RV and boat storage facility; operations lead for parking at a storage operator
**Automation Potential:** Medium

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A storage operator selling parking for RVs, boats, trailers and work vans sells length and clearance, not cubic feet. A 40-foot fifth-wheel and a 16-foot bass boat both take "a space". Covered and enclosed spaces sell at multiples of open gravel, and demand peaks before and after the season. The audience is underserved because storage software models every space as a unit with a size code. Vehicle tenants are also different. They visit to take the vehicle out, so access patterns are seasonal, and a lien on a vehicle needs title and lienholder steps that a unit of boxes does not. Storable now runs separate Marine and RV/Campground product lines, and Neighbor runs a peer-to-peer marketplace for parking and storage space. The boundaries: marinas' dry-stack boat storage belongs to the queued marinas build, RV parks selling nightly sites to the queued RV parks and campgrounds build, and RV sales to [[industries/rv-dealerships|RV Dealerships]]. Boat sales are analysed under [[industries/powersports-and-boat-dealers|Powersports & Boat Dealers]].

## Current Tools & Gaps
Storage facility software with parking "units"; spreadsheets of vehicle lengths; gate keypads, sometimes plate-reader cameras; marketplace listings on Neighbor. Gaps: no space assignment by length and turning room; no seasonal price curve; no waitlist pricing; vehicle liens run as unit liens.

# Cost Per Lead With No Denominator

**Niche:** [[niches/lending-marketplaces/acquisition-economics/profile|Acquisition Economics]]
**Industry:** [[industries/lending-marketplaces|Lending Marketplaces]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The marketing team hits its cost per lead target every month and nobody can say what those leads were worth.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #quick-win #evaluation-metrics #revenue-impact #automation #data-integration #confidence-intervals #workflow-orchestration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to bid on a borrower's expected funded value rather than on their click probability — and whoever gets the funded signal into the bid takes the traffic everyone else is overpaying for.

## The Problem
Acquisition is managed to a cost per lead target. The target is met. Whether the leads acquired at that cost were sold at good rates, accepted by lenders, or funded is reported separately, later, in aggregate, by a different team. A channel that produces cheap leads nobody will buy looks like a success and gets more budget. The two halves of the same economic question are measured in different reports on different cadences.

## Why It's Still Broken
Cost per lead is available immediately and funded value is not, so the fast metric became the managed one — and an organisation running weekly optimisation will always steer by the number it has on Monday. The two datasets live in marketing and finance systems that were never joined. Channel-level outcome reporting does not exist. And nobody asked for cost per funded loan, so nobody built it.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Join the two reports. Report funded rate and revenue by acquisition channel and campaign, which is the fix and is a join between systems the company already operates. Publish cost per funded loan alongside cost per lead, since showing both makes the divergence undeniable within a month. Break it down by segment, because the average across a channel hides that some keywords fund at several times the rate of others. Backfill historical data, as the answer exists for the last two years and would immediately redirect budget. Accept the reporting delay rather than waiting for a real-time answer, since a six-week-old funded rate is enormously better than none. Use lead sale price as an interim proxy where funded data is thin, because it is available immediately and correlates. Give marketing access to the outcome data directly, since asking another team for a report every month guarantees it stops. Flag the channels with good cost per lead and poor funded rate, which is where the budget is being wasted and is a short list. Set the target on the outcome metric once it is trusted, as the target is what actually drives behaviour. And review the divergence monthly, because channel quality shifts and a one-off analysis decays.

## Who Feels the Pain
Marketing teams hitting a target that does not correspond to value; finance reconciling acquisition spend against revenue; lenders receiving the volume cheap channels produce; and a business optimising its largest cost against a proxy.

## Impact If Fixed
The fast metric became the managed one because an organisation optimising weekly steers by the number it has on Monday. Reporting cost per funded loan beside cost per lead exposes the divergence from data both teams already hold.

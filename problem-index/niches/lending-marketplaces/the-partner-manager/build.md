# Arming the Lead Quality Conversation

**Niche:** [[niches/lending-marketplaces/the-partner-manager/profile|The Partner Manager]]
**Industry:** [[industries/lending-marketplaces|Lending Marketplaces]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** The lender says the leads are bad and the partner manager has no way to find out whether that is true.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #worker-facing #confidence-intervals #data-integration #revenue-impact #gradient-boosting #hypothesis-testing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to give a partner manager evidence that answers the lender's actual question when the answer lives in the lender's own systems — and whoever arms that conversation changes what the relationship can be negotiated on.

## The Problem
The lender's complaint is specific and the marketplace's response cannot be. Which leads were bad? Bad how — declined, unreachable, wrong product, wrong state? Compared to what? The partner manager has volume delivered and price paid, and that is the whole evidentiary basis for a negotiation about a multi-million dollar relationship. They end up conceding on price because they cannot argue on quality, and the underlying question goes unresolved for years.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The evidence depends on outcome data the lender holds, so the role was defined around relationship management instead — and a job scoped as relationship management never gets analytical tooling. Partner managers are measured on bid rates and volume retained. No internal team produces lender-level quality analysis. And the missing data is treated as an unchangeable fact rather than as the thing the role should be working to change.

## What to Build
Assemble the case from what the marketplace does know, and make the ask for the rest. Produce lender-level quality views from internal data — profile mix, eligibility match, duplicate and resale exposure, contact rates, funding where known — which is the core and is more evidence than the role has ever had. Segment by borrower profile, since "your leads are bad" almost always means one segment and identifying it converts a dispute into a targeting change. Compare a lender's mix against peers on similar products, which contextualises the complaint immediately. Make the data ask part of the relationship, as the partner manager is the person positioned to trade for it and connects directly to outcome data recovery. Show the lender what better matching would require, because a specific request is far more persuasive than a defence. Detect disengagement early from bid, volume and response patterns, since a lender withdrawing is visible weeks before they say so. Model the value of a lender relationship properly, so concessions are made deliberately rather than reflexively. Track complaints as a structured record, since they recur and are currently handled as individual emails. Prepare the negotiation with a value estimate rather than a volume report, which is what changes the outcome. And measure quality-adjusted revenue per lender, as it is the metric the role should be managed on.

## Target Customer
Partner leadership, partner managers, lender partnership teams on the other side of the table, and partner management platform vendors with no quality analytics.

## Impact If Built
A job scoped as relationship management never gets analytical tooling, so the role argues price because it cannot argue quality. Segment-level quality views from internal data give the conversation its first evidence and make the data ask concrete.

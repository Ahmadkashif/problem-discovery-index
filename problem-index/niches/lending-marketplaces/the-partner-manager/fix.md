# The Quarterly Deck Built by Hand

**Niche:** [[niches/lending-marketplaces/the-partner-manager/profile|The Partner Manager]]
**Industry:** [[industries/lending-marketplaces|Lending Marketplaces]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Every quarter, for every lender, someone exports volume data into a spreadsheet and rebuilds the same twelve slides.
**Tags:** #worker-facing #quick-win #automation #workflow-orchestration #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #data-integration #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to give a partner manager evidence that answers the lender's actual question when the answer lives in the lender's own systems — and whoever arms that conversation changes what the relationship can be negotiated on.

## The Problem
The quarterly business review with each lender needs a deck: volume delivered, mix, pricing, trend, upcoming changes. There are dozens of lenders. The structure is identical every time and the data comes from the same three systems. Partner managers spend a substantial part of every quarter exporting, pasting and reformatting, and the slides that result say less than the data could because building anything more analytical by hand is not feasible.

## Why It's Still Broken
The deck is a meeting artefact owned by the person attending the meeting, so it was never treated as a reporting system — each manager built their own and the duplication is invisible. Data lives in systems with no reporting layer aimed at this. Nobody counts the hours. And the deck's weakness is attributed to the missing outcome data rather than to the manual assembly.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Generate the deck and spend the time on the argument. Generate the standard sections automatically from the source systems, which is the fix and returns most of a week per quarter to every partner manager. Standardise the structure across lenders, since bespoke decks per manager multiply the work and reduce comparability. Refresh continuously rather than quarterly, so the conversation can happen whenever it needs to. Add the segment-level quality views once they exist, as that is the content the meeting actually needs. Keep a record of what was presented and agreed, because commitments made in these meetings are currently remembered rather than recorded. Flag the metrics that moved materially, so the manager walks in knowing what will be raised. Let the lender see a live view rather than a quarterly snapshot, which changes the relationship's tempo and is a differentiator. Reuse the same generation for internal reviews, since the same numbers are assembled twice today. Track preparation hours, which is the number that justifies the work. And ask partner managers which slides the lenders actually engage with, since half the deck is probably unread.

## Who Feels the Pain
Partner managers losing a week a quarter to assembly; lenders receiving thin decks; and leadership with no comparable view across the portfolio.

## Impact If Fixed
The deck is a meeting artefact owned by whoever attends, so the duplication across dozens of relationships is invisible. Generating the standard sections returns the preparation time to the argument the meeting is actually about.

# Four Applications, Four Inquiries

**Niche:** [[niches/lending-marketplaces/inquiry-protection/profile|Inquiry Protection]]
**Industry:** [[industries/lending-marketplaces|Lending Marketplaces]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The borrower is encouraged to apply to several lenders at once and is never told what that does to their credit file.
**Tags:** #compliance #quick-win #automation #evaluation-metrics #worker-facing #descriptive-statistics #workflow-orchestration #confidence-intervals
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to stop spending the borrower's credit file on applications that will be declined — and whoever treats the inquiry as a cost rather than as free takes the trust the category has never had.

## The Problem
The interface presents several lenders and invites the borrower to apply to all of them to compare offers. That is reasonable advice in one sense and expensive in another, and the page does not mention hard inquiries, score impact, or the rate-shopping window that would make multiple applications within a short period count as one. The borrower learns about it later, from their credit report, and concludes the marketplace was working against them.

## Why It's Still Broken
Disclosure was scoped to what is legally required, so anything discouraging an application was left out — and more applications is the revenue-maximising default, which means nobody argued for adding it. The rate-shopping window is genuinely complicated to explain. Consumer understanding is not measured. And the harm is diffuse and delayed.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Tell the borrower what applying costs. State plainly that an application is a hard inquiry and what that means, which is the fix and is a sentence of copy. Explain the rate-shopping window, since applying to several lenders within it is usually treated as one inquiry and knowing that changes behaviour for the better in both directions. Label which lenders offer prequalification without a hard pull, as borrowers will choose them and lenders will notice. Show how many applications the borrower has already made through the marketplace, because they frequently do not remember. Suggest a sensible number rather than encouraging all of them, since the interface currently pushes toward the maximum. Time the disclosure to the moment of application rather than burying it in terms, which is where it changes a decision. Test comprehension with real users, as this is a communication problem and the current language was never tested. Report the number of inquiries generated per borrower, which is the diagnostic that will make the case internally. Give consultants the same language, so the phone channel matches. And monitor complaints about credit impact, since they are the ground truth on whether the disclosure works.

## Who Feels the Pain
Borrowers whose scores fall at the worst moment; consultants asked about credit impact with no approved answer; and a category whose trust deficit is built from experiences exactly like this.

## Impact If Fixed
Disclosure was scoped to the legal minimum and more applications is the revenue-maximising default, so nobody argued for the sentence. Explaining the inquiry and the rate-shopping window at the moment of application is copy, not engineering.

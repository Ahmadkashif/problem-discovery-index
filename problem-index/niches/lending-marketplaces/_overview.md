# Niche Analysis — Lending Marketplaces

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/lending-marketplaces|Lending Marketplaces]]

## Niche Selection

A lending marketplace runs first-rate acquisition and ranking infrastructure optimised against the only signal that reliably returns to it, which is the click. It routes a borrower to lenders and, in most partnerships, never learns the decision, the terms, the acceptance or the performance. So lenders complain about lead quality, borrowers absorb credit inquiries from lenders who decline them, and the marketplace's answer to both is more volume, because volume is the only lever that works without the missing data. Meanwhile it sees what no lender sees: the same borrower shopping across many lenders, repeatedly, for years. The eight niches below split the category by what competitors actually fight over.

## Why These Niches

Borrower routing takes the largest share because it is the product, and it is trained on the wrong signal. Acquisition economics is second because paid acquisition is where the money goes and it is optimised to the same wrong signal one step earlier. The two low-digitized niches are the ones maintained by hand: hundreds of lender products with their own eligibility rules and state restrictions, and a consent record that is simultaneously the permission to operate and the largest litigation exposure in the business. The two underserved audiences are the licensed consultant calling borrowers whose quality was priced before anyone knew anything about them, and the partner manager defending lead quality with reports that cannot answer the lender's question. The two automatable niches are the cross-lender view nobody exploits and the borrower's credit file, which the industry treats as free.

## Filter Notes

Seven of the eight level-1 niches are terminal. Borrower Routing is not: the label names the function the product performs rather than a contest, and writing the contested statement produces two sentences whose winners are different companies. Approval prediction asks which lender will approve this borrower and on what terms — an inference problem under heavily censored outcomes, where the winner is whoever predicts best from partial and biased feedback. Outcome data recovery asks how to get the decision back from the lender at all — a commercial and contractual problem about what participation in a marketplace should require, where the winner is whoever changes the terms of the relationship. One is modelling under missing data and the other is negotiating the missingness away, and the second is explicitly commercial before it is technical. It therefore decomposes into **Approval Prediction** and **Outcome Data Recovery**.

Two candidates were considered and rejected. **Underwriting and credit decisioning** is the decision the marketplace is trying to predict, but the contest over making it belongs to lenders and is analysed in [[industries/bnpl-providers|BNPL Providers]]. **The brokered mortgage relationship** shares the routing shape but is the contest of [[industries/mortgage-brokers|Mortgage Brokers]], where origination and the borrower relationship are analysed on their own terms.

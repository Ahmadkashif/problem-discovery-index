**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn the view of the same borrower shopping across many lenders over years into answers no individual lender can produce — and whoever organises it owns the only empirical picture of how credit is actually priced and granted.

## The Problem
Credit bureaus built substantial businesses on aggregated lender data: origination trend reports, benchmark products, market share analytics and published indices. The practice — governance, aggregation thresholds, product packaging, regulatory framing — is well established. Marketplaces hold something bureaus do not, which is the shopping side: who approached whom, in what order, and with what stated need. It has never been turned into a product.

## What Already Exists
Bureau benchmark and trend products; aggregation and suppression standards; market share analytics; published credit indices; and the regulatory framing around consumer credit data products.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to demand-side rather than supply-side data. It requires: (1) the shopping record rather than the origination record as the base asset, which is complementary to bureau data and is the substantive contribution; (2) self-reported profile data rather than bureau-verified attributes, which is richer in intent and weaker in accuracy; (3) partial outcome coverage, so products must be honest about what is inferred; (4) consumer consent scoped for lead generation rather than for data products, which is a real constraint requiring deliberate handling; and (5) lender relationships that are commercial customers, so a product characterising their appetite must be designed not to damage them.

## Target Customer
Data and commercial leadership, lender strategy teams, researchers and regulators, and bureau and market data vendors lacking the shopping view.

## Impact If Solved
Bureaus productised the origination side and the shopping side has no equivalent. The consent and accuracy constraints are the real design work, and the resulting view is complementary rather than competitive.

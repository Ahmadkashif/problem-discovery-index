# Player Charting & Scouting Data Vendors

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/sports-teams|Sports Teams & Clubs]]
**Category:** Insight Layer
**Value-Chain Position:** Data & benchmark vendors

## What They Do
Firms that chart every play from game film into event data and grades and sell it to clubs' front offices and coaching staffs. Teamworks now owns PFF's enterprise business, which it says is used by every NFL team and 240+ Division I programmes, alongside Telemetry Sports and Sportlogiq (acquired before March 2026). Hudl (with StatsBomb), Sports Info Solutions and Stats Perform sell comparable event data in their sports. Clubs pay annual subscriptions; the data and grades are the product.

## Insight Function
**Size:** ~20–60 data scientists, analysts and product researchers per firm (estimate); the per-game charters who log and grade each play (PFF uses ~30 analysts and ~90 combined hours a game, search summary) are production and are not counted
**Output:** Play-by-play event data, player grades, draft and free-agency boards, opponent reports
**Proprietary data:** Years of charted event data and grades built from game and coaching film; the film itself is licensed from leagues and broadcasters
**Clock:** Game-by-game delivery deadlines (production) and the league's draft and free-agency calendar, which the clubs work to
**Buyer:** Chief product or data officer at the vendor

**Kill switches:** none on the charted data, which the vendors create and own. The film it is charted from is used under league and broadcaster licences whose terms were not read, so that input is undetermined; it would not lift the score.
**Verdict:** Logged — below threshold (41). The data and grades are what clubs pay for, and the archive is unique. But growth comes from adding per-game charters, who are production, rather than analysts. The clock is a per-game production deadline, and after Teamworks' consolidation the field is a handful of firms (Q5 2).

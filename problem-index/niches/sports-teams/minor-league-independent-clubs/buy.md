# Small-Business CRM Fitted to a Ballpark

**Niche:** [[niches/sports-teams/minor-league-independent-clubs/profile|Minor-League & Independent Clubs]]
**Industry:** [[industries/sports-teams|Sports Teams & Clubs]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** A minor-league club can afford a small-business CRM, but not the big-league fan-data platform built on top of Salesforce.
**Tags:** #logistic-regression #k-means-clustering #data-integration #workflow-orchestration #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to run a small club's ticketing, sponsorship, promotions and fan data from one system that a front office of twenty to forty people will actually use, and whoever lifts a club's total revenue per home date the most takes the account.

## The Problem
Clubs have ticket buyers in their ticketing system, group leaders in a spreadsheet, sponsors in another, and email subscribers in a marketing tool. Nobody sees one fan or one business across them.

## What Already Exists
Small-business CRMs and email platforms; big-league fan-data platforms (KORE, StellarAlgo) priced and staffed for larger clubs.

## The Customization Gap
A ballpark data model out of the box: ticket buyers, group leaders, sponsors and kids' club members as linked records; connectors to the club's ticketer and point-of-sale; segment templates (lapsed group leaders, businesses near the park, families who attended a giveaway night); and simple propensity scores. Setup must take days, not a consulting project.

## Target Customer
Affiliated, independent and lower-division clubs without a data analyst.

## Impact If Solved
Re-contacting lapsed group leaders alone can bring back ~5–10% of a club's group volume (estimate), a few hundred thousand dollars at a busy club.

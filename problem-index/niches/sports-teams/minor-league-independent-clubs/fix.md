# The Promotion Nobody Costed

**Niche:** [[niches/sports-teams/minor-league-independent-clubs/profile|Minor-League & Independent Clubs]]
**Industry:** [[industries/sports-teams|Sports Teams & Clubs]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Clubs repeat bobblehead and fireworks nights every season without knowing how many extra fans each one brought for what it cost.
**Tags:** #causal-inference #linear-regression #hypothesis-testing #quick-win #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to run a small club's ticketing, sponsorship, promotions and fan data from one system that a front office of twenty to forty people will actually use, and whoever lifts a club's total revenue per home date the most takes the account.

## The Problem
A giveaway costs the club items, staff and sometimes a sponsor's contribution. Fireworks cost thousands of dollars a night. Promotions are usually put on the dates that would have drawn anyway (Fridays and Saturdays in summer), so their raw attendance flatters them.

## Why It's Still Broken
Nobody has the time or the method to separate a promotion's effect from the date's, and last year's schedule is the template for this year's.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Estimate each promotion type's incremental attendance and revenue against comparable dates without it, pooling across clubs for rarer promotions, and show it beside the promotion's cost when the season is planned.

## Who Feels the Pain
Promotions and marketing managers defending their budget, and general managers allocating sponsor dollars to theme nights.

## Impact If Fixed
Moving five promotions a season from dates that sell out anyway to soft dates, at ~1,000–2,000 extra fans each (estimate), adds ~5,000–10,000 paid fans, or ~$70–140K of ticket revenue at ~$14 a ticket (estimate), and pays for itself the same season.

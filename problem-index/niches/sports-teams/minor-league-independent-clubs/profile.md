# Minor-League & Independent Clubs

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/sports-teams|Sports Teams & Clubs]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to run a small club's ticketing, sponsorship, promotions and fan data from one system that a front office of twenty to forty people will actually use, and whoever lifts a club's total revenue per home date the most takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$250M a year of ticketing, CRM, sponsorship, marketing and front-office software and services at ~120 affiliated MiLB clubs and a few hundred independent-league, AHL, ECHL, USL and other lower-division clubs (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** The 917 firms under 20 employees took ~1.4% of the industry's 2022 receipts (Census SUSB); most affiliated clubs sit in the 20–499 range (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — clubs sell tickets online, but pricing, groups, sponsorship fulfilment and promotions are run by a small staff from spreadsheets
**Target Buyer:** Club president or general manager, often the owner's operating partner
**Automation Potential:** High — pricing, promotions planning, group outreach and sponsor recaps are repeatable across seventy home dates

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A minor-league club sells an evening out, not a pennant race. Players are assigned by the major-league affiliate, the roster changes weekly, and ~60–70 home dates (estimate) must each be sold on promotions, price and weather. The same 20–40 people sell sponsorship in winter, run game day in summer and do the books in between (estimate). Big-league tools assume a department for each job. Ownership is consolidating: Diamond Baseball Holdings owns ~42–49 of the 120 affiliated MiLB clubs (search summary), which brings central buying. Independent leagues, hockey and lower-division soccer remain fragmented.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Primary ticketing from a handful of ticketers, CRMs and email tools sized for small business, sponsorship tracked in spreadsheets, social tools, and the parent club's or league's shared services. Gaps: no per-game pricing, no view of which promotions earn their cost, group sales worked from a binder of past buyers, and sponsor recaps rebuilt every September.

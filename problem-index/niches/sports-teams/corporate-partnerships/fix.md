# The Deal Value Nobody Can Reconstruct

**Niche:** [[niches/sports-teams/corporate-partnerships/profile|Corporate Partnerships & Sponsorship]]
**Industry:** [[industries/sports-teams|Sports Teams & Clubs]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Multi-asset deals are signed at one price for a bundle, so nobody can later say what the jersey patch, the signage and the hospitality were each worth.
**Tags:** #linear-regression #regularization #feature-engineering #quick-win #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is that every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to grow a club's partnership revenue, but that hides two contests with different winners: pricing unsold inventory from what comparable deals close for, won on the most contracted partnership revenue per available inventory asset, and proving to existing partners what their money produced, won on net partnership-dollar retention at renewal. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## The Problem
Most partnerships bundle several assets for one annual fee. When a new prospect asks for signage alone, or a partner asks to drop hospitality at renewal, the club has no record of what each piece was priced at. Sellers re-derive values from memory and the rate card, and two sellers quote the same asset differently.

## Why It's Still Broken
Allocating a bundle price to its parts is never done at signing, because it does not affect the invoice. The question only matters later, and by then the seller who built the deal may have moved on.

## What a Fix Looks Like
At signing, allocate the fee across assets using a simple model fitted on the club's own single-asset deals and SponsorUnited-style comparables, record the allocation with the contract, and show it to the next seller who touches the partner.

## Who Feels the Pain
Partnership sellers quoting à-la-carte requests, finance teams reporting revenue by asset, and renewal leads negotiating a partner down without knowing what to give up.

## Impact If Fixed
A recorded allocation turns every past bundle into a price comparable. A club with ~100 partners (estimate) gains a library of several hundred priced assets within one season.

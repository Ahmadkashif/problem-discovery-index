# Local Media Rights & Team Streaming

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/sports-teams|Sports Teams & Clubs]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to replace the rights fee a collapsing regional sports network used to pay, through streaming subscriptions, over-the-air reach and local advertising, and whoever delivers a club the most local media revenue per in-market household takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.5B a year of production, distribution, streaming technology, ad sales and rights advisory spent on local game telecasts by MLB, NBA and NHL clubs (estimate), on local rights revenue that was ~$4–5B a year before the RSN collapse (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** Local media was "more than 20% of industry revenue" in MLB (Manfred, February 2026); lower in the NBA and NHL (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — streaming platforms are digital; territorial rights, blackout rules and carriage deals are contracts negotiated one by one
**Target Buyer:** Club president, chief revenue officer or head of media
**Automation Potential:** Medium — subscriber analytics, pricing and ad yield automate; rights and carriage negotiations do not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Until 2023 most clubs sold their local rights to a regional sports network for a fixed annual fee and never dealt with a viewer. Diamond Sports filed for bankruptcy in March 2023 holding rights to 42 teams. Its successor, Main Street Sports Group, now holds 20 NBA and NHL teams and stopped paying several MLB clubs. By February 2026 MLB was producing local broadcasts for at least 13 clubs, and the Braves, Tigers and Angels had also left Main Street without, at that date, announcing where their games would go. Each club must now decide between league-run media, a team-owned network, an over-the-air partner and a direct-to-consumer stream, and it bears the subscriber, churn and ad risk itself. NFL clubs are outside this niche because their games are on national contracts.

## Current Tools & Gaps
League-run production and distribution (MLB's local media operation), team-owned networks, local TV groups for over-the-air games, streaming platforms and app vendors, and the remaining RSNs. Gaps: no club-level view of in-market households reached across cable, over-the-air and streaming; subscription prices set without demand data; ad inventory sold without audience measures the club controls.

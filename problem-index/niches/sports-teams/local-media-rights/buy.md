# Streaming Subscriber Analytics for a Single Team

**Niche:** [[niches/sports-teams/local-media-rights/profile|Local Media Rights & Team Streaming]]
**Industry:** [[industries/sports-teams|Sports Teams & Clubs]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Subscription analytics built for streaming services assume a year-round catalogue; a team's stream carries ~150 local games in a six-month season and then goes quiet.
**Tags:** #survival-analysis #gradient-boosting #time-series-forecasting #data-integration #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to replace the rights fee a collapsing regional sports network used to pay, through streaming subscriptions, over-the-air reach and local advertising, and whoever delivers a club the most local media revenue per in-market household takes the account.

## The Problem
A team stream gains subscribers in the spring, loses them during a losing streak and after the trade deadline, and goes dark in winter. Churn and price questions are seasonal and tied to on-field results.

## What Already Exists
Subscription analytics and churn tools sold to streaming services, covered from the platform side at [[niches/streaming-video-platforms/churn-and-lifecycle/profile|Churn & Lifecycle]]; the streaming technology vendors' own dashboards.

## The Customization Gap
Team-specific logic: churn models with the club's win probability, standings and star availability as covariates; season-pass versus monthly plan design; in-market verification and blackout rules; bundles with tickets and memberships (season-ticket holders who get the stream free); and off-season retention offers.

## Target Customer
Clubs running their own or a league-run stream and the vendors operating those streams.

## Impact If Solved
Cutting mid-season churn by a few points on a ~100,000-subscriber stream at ~$20 a month (estimate) keeps ~$1M or more a season.

# The Stream Priced Without a Demand Curve

**Niche:** [[niches/sports-teams/local-media-rights/profile|Local Media Rights & Team Streaming]]
**Industry:** [[industries/sports-teams|Sports Teams & Clubs]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Clubs launched direct-to-consumer streams at prices copied from each other, with no test of what their own fans would pay.
**Tags:** #hypothesis-testing #linear-regression #confidence-intervals #quick-win #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to replace the rights fee a collapsing regional sports network used to pay, through streaming subscriptions, over-the-air reach and local advertising, and whoever delivers a club the most local media revenue per in-market household takes the account.

## The Problem
When an RSN deal ends weeks before a season, the club or the league picks a monthly and a season price quickly, usually matching another club's. The price then stays all season.

## Why It's Still Broken
There was no time to test, the league may set prices across several clubs, and nobody owns a demand estimate.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Run controlled price and offer tests within league rules (season-pass discounts by date, bundles for ticket holders, a lower mid-season price) and estimate the price response by segment. Reset next season's price from the evidence and record the reasoning.

## Who Feels the Pain
Club presidents answering to owners for lost RSN revenue, and fans priced out of watching at home.

## Impact If Fixed
A price set from tested demand rather than copied can move subscription revenue by ~5–15% (estimate) in the first season it is applied.

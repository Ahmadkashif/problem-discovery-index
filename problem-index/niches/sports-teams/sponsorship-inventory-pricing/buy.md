# Deal Databases Tuned to One Club's Inventory

**Niche:** [[niches/sports-teams/sponsorship-inventory-pricing/profile|Sponsorship Inventory Pricing & Deal Valuation]]
**Industry:** [[industries/sports-teams|Sports Teams & Clubs]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** A deal database shows what other properties sold; a club needs it translated into a price for its own building, audience and broadcast reach.
**Tags:** #k-nearest-neighbors #linear-regression #feature-engineering #data-integration #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this sub-niche is fighting to price a club's unsold inventory (patches, naming rights, signage, digital, hospitality) from what comparable deals actually signed for, and whoever's clubs earn the most contracted partnership revenue per available inventory asset takes the account.

## The Problem
Partnership sellers search a deal database for "bank, arena naming rights, mid-size market", get a list of deals and estimated values, and then adjust by feel for their own market size, attendance, broadcast reach and the length of the term.

## What Already Exists
SponsorUnited's database, AI analyst and proposal evaluator; valuation services from Nielsen Sports and Two Circles; agency benchmarks.

## The Customization Gap
The adjustment step: a comparable-matching layer that weights each comparable by market, attendance, local TV or streaming reach, term length and category exclusivity. It applies the club's own exposure measurements and returns a range with the comparables shown, so a seller can defend it in a negotiation. It should run on the club's own data inside its CRM, not as a separate login.

## Target Customer
Clubs and college departments already subscribing to a deal database.

## Impact If Solved
Cutting proposal preparation from days to hours and narrowing quoted ranges lets a seller run ~2x the proposals a season (estimate) on the same inventory.

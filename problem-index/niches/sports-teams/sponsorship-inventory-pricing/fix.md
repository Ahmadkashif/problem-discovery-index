# The Rate Card Nobody Updated

**Niche:** [[niches/sports-teams/sponsorship-inventory-pricing/profile|Sponsorship Inventory Pricing & Deal Valuation]]
**Industry:** [[industries/sports-teams|Sports Teams & Clubs]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A club's sponsorship rate card is set once, inflated by a percentage each year and ignores what its inventory has actually sold for.
**Tags:** #linear-regression #descriptive-statistics #quick-win #automation #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this sub-niche is fighting to price a club's unsold inventory (patches, naming rights, signage, digital, hospitality) from what comparable deals actually signed for, and whoever's clubs earn the most contracted partnership revenue per available inventory asset takes the account.

## The Problem
Rate cards list a price for each asset. Most were built years ago and are raised by a flat percentage. Some assets have not sold at card in years, while others sell above it every time. Sellers discount from a number everyone knows is wrong.

## Why It's Still Broken
Nobody owns the rate card between seasons, and resetting it means admitting that some assets are worth less than last year's number.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Each off-season, recompute card prices from the last three seasons of signed deals per asset (allocated from bundles), sell-through and current audience measures. Flag assets that sold below card every time and assets that always sold out, and publish the new card with the evidence beside each line.

## Who Feels the Pain
Sellers negotiating from a discredited card, partnership VPs explaining unsold inventory, and finance teams budgeting on card values.

## Impact If Fixed
Repricing the ~20% of assets furthest from their realised price (estimate) shortens negotiations and lifts sell-through on the assets that were overpriced.

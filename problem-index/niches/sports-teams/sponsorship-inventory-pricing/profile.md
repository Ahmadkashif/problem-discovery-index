# Sponsorship Inventory Pricing & Deal Valuation

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/sports-teams|Sports Teams & Clubs]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this sub-niche is fighting to price a club's unsold inventory (patches, naming rights, signage, digital, hospitality) from what comparable deals actually signed for, and whoever's clubs earn the most contracted partnership revenue per available inventory asset takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$400M a year of deal-intelligence subscriptions, valuation projects and partnership-strategy staff time spent on pricing at North American clubs and college departments (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~1% of club revenue goes on it; it governs ~15–20% (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — deal databases are online, rate cards are spreadsheets
**Target Buyer:** Vice president of partnership strategy or chief revenue officer
**Automation Potential:** High for comparables and valuation ranges; the asking price stays a negotiation

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Before a deal signs, the question is what the inventory is worth to the market. New inventory classes keep appearing: NBA and NHL jersey patches, MLB uniform patches since 2023, helmet decals, streaming overlays, college venue rights after revenue sharing pushed departments to sell more. Their price is set from comparables, audience and exposure estimates. The winner is the club that turns each available asset into the most contracted revenue, measured on signed deals, not on the elegance of the valuation method. The data is largely the vendor's own: observed deals, venue audits, estimated values.

## Current Tools & Gaps
SponsorUnited's deal database and proposal evaluator, which it says rest on $45B of transacted deals and audits by 3,000 venue scouts; KORE (Two Circles) and Nielsen Sports valuation work; agencies on the brand side. Gaps: estimates of deal values are not graded against signed fees in public; club-specific audience and exposure are not joined to comparables; bundled deals hide what each asset sold for.

# League Tracking & Official Data Providers (Non-Betting Work)

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/sports-teams|Sports Teams & Clubs]]
**Category:** Insight Layer
**Value-Chain Position:** Suppliers selling into the industry

## What They Do
Companies that install optical and sensor tracking in league venues and deliver player and ball tracking, official event data and fan-engagement content to leagues, clubs and media. Hawk-Eye (Sony) supplies MLB's Statcast and NBA tracking, and Genius Sports owns Second Spectrum (both recalled, not checked). Genius Sports reported FY2025 revenue of $669.5M: $471.5M from betting, which is a revenue fact here and not profiled; $144.5M from media technology including fan engagement; and $53.5M from sports technology and services. This pocket covers only the work for leagues, clubs and fans. Two data regimes sit inside it: tracking captured as a service for the leagues, and official-data licences that let a provider redistribute league data to media and fan-engagement clients.

## Insight Function
**Size:** ~20–100 data scientists and engineers on the non-betting side of the larger providers (estimate); venue operators and live data loggers are production
**Output:** Tracking feeds and derived metrics for leagues and clubs, broadcast and app graphics, fan-engagement and advertising products
**Proprietary data:** Tracking and event data captured in league venues under league service contracts, plus official data held under licences that may include redistribution rights (terms not read)
**Clock:** Every game's live delivery (production)
**Buyer:** League chief technology or media officer; at the vendor, the head of sports technology

**Kill switches:** none on the official-data-licence half: where a provider holds an official-data licence that grants it redistribution rights to media and fan-engagement clients, that is a product right. Undetermined on the tracking-service half: tracking captured in league venues for the leagues rests on league contracts that were not read (whether, for example, MLB owns Statcast data with Hawk-Eye as its contractor was not checked). The tracking-service half carries the verdict, and the score is 29 on either half, because the licences are exclusive per league and the product is a per-game feed.
**Verdict:** Logged — below threshold (29). Large engineering operations and the richest physical data in sport, but the leagues control access to it, the product is a feed delivered per game rather than an analysis, and four or five leagues buy it.

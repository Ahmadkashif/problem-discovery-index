# Sponsor Outcome Proof & Renewal

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/sports-teams|Sports Teams & Clubs]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this sub-niche is fighting to show a partner what its sponsorship produced in the partner's own business before the renewal date, and whoever's clubs keep the highest net partnership-dollar retention at renewal takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$500M a year of activation and fulfilment staff, exposure measurement, research and recap production at North American clubs and college departments (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~1% of club revenue goes on it; it protects the ~15–20% that is sponsorship (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — exposure is measured digitally, delivery and outcome proof are assembled by hand
**Target Buyer:** Vice president of partnership activation or chief revenue officer; on the brand side, the sponsorship director whose finance team signs the renewal
**Automation Potential:** High for delivery tracking and recaps; medium for outcome lift, which needs the partner's data

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
After signing, the question changes from what the inventory is worth to the market to what it did for this partner. Exposure and media value are measured by vendors, but partners' finance teams increasingly discount them. In Bain's 2026 survey only 41% of marketers felt equipped to tie spend to business outcomes. The outcome data that would answer the question sits inside the partner's business, so the contest is won by whoever gets that data shared and turns it into a credible lift estimate before the renewal. The same boundary was drawn for esports at [[niches/esports-organizations/sponsor-outcome-attribution/profile|Sponsor Outcome Attribution]].

## Current Tools & Gaps
Exposure and media value from Relo Metrics, Nielsen Sports and KORE's Hookit; fulfilment in KORE or spreadsheets; recap decks built in slides; occasional brand-lift surveys. Gaps: delivery not reconciled to contract lines, no control group for outcomes, partner data rarely contracted for, and media value treated as proof when finance teams no longer accept it.

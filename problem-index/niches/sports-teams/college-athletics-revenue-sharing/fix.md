# Revenue Share Paid Before the Portal Opens

**Niche:** [[niches/sports-teams/college-athletics-revenue-sharing/profile|College Athletics Departments Under Revenue Sharing]]
**Industry:** [[industries/sports-teams|Sports Teams & Clubs]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Departments pay much of an athlete's revenue share early in the year, and when he enters the transfer portal the money already paid leaves with him.
**Tags:** #survival-analysis #descriptive-statistics #compliance #quick-win #workflow-orchestration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to help an athletic department spend its revenue-sharing cap on athletes who will stay through their agreements, and whoever's schools lose the fewest revenue-share dollars to athletes who leave before their agreement ends takes the account.

## The Problem
Revenue-share agreements were drafted quickly in 2025, and departure terms vary from agreement to agreement. Where an agreement pays in a lump sum or front-loads payments to win a recruit, and the athlete enters the portal after the season, the department finds that most of his year's money is already paid, and nobody can say quickly what each agreement provides if he leaves.

## Why It's Still Broken
Agreements are drafted by different staff and outside counsel for each sport, payments run through the university's payroll or accounts-payable systems, and nobody has lined up payment dates against the portal windows across the roster.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Abstract every agreement's term, payment schedule and departure provisions into one register. Show, for each upcoming portal window, how many cap dollars have been paid to athletes who could still leave. Default new agreements to payment schedules that track the season, within what the rules and the athlete's counsel accept.

## Who Feels the Pain
General managers and business offices explaining to the athletic director where the cap went, and coaches who lose both the player and the money.

## Impact If Fixed
Moving even a third of a ~$20M cap (estimate) from up-front to in-season payment cuts the dollars exposed when the portal opens by ~$2–3M a year (estimate).

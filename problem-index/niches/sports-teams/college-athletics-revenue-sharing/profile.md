# College Athletics Departments Under Revenue Sharing

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/sports-teams|Sports Teams & Clubs]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to help an athletic department spend its revenue-sharing cap on athletes who will stay through their agreements, and whoever's schools lose the fewest revenue-share dollars to athletes who leave before their agreement ends takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$300M a year of general-manager and compliance staff, revenue-share and NIL software, payments and advisory spent by revenue-sharing Division I departments (estimate), governing payments of up to ~$20M a school in 2025-26 rising to $32.9M by 2034-35
**Share of Parent Industry:** College departments run as ticket-and-sponsorship businesses sit mostly outside the Census count of this code (estimate); they are included here for the business they run, not the education they provide
**Digital Adoption:** Low — cap tracking, payment schedules and roster budgets are spreadsheets built in 2025
**Target Buyer:** Athletic director, deputy AD for revenue sharing, or the football or basketball general manager
**Automation Potential:** Medium — cap ledgers, payment schedules and retention risk automate; the conversation with a player and his family does not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Since 1 July 2025, under the House settlement, schools can pay athletes directly up to a cap of 22% of Power Five average athletic revenue, ~$20M a school in the first year. Every third-party NIL deal over $600 goes to NIL Go, run by Deloitte, for a fair-market-value review, with disputes going to the College Sports Commission. The commission had 29 full-time staff in September 2026 and cleared more than $227M of deals in July and August 2026. Departments now run a capped payroll across sports beside a deal clearinghouse and the transfer portal, with tools built for a world in which athletes were not paid. NIL Go and the commission decide whether a third-party deal clears, a pass or fail the department does not own. What the department and its vendors compete on is the money: how little of the cap is paid to athletes who then leave before their agreements end.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Teamworks (which lists Compliance, GM, Influencer and Wallet products), NIL marketplaces, multimedia-rights holders' NIL programmes such as Learfield's, collectives, and law and advisory firms. Gaps: no single cap ledger across sports, payment schedules not lined up against the portal windows, agreement terms on departure not tracked, and retention risk judged by coaches' feel. The parent university's other businesses sit under [[industries/colleges-and-universities|Colleges & Universities]], which has no athletics niche.

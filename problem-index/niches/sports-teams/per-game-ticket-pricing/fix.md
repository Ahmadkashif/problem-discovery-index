# The Price Change Nobody Tested

**Niche:** [[niches/sports-teams/per-game-ticket-pricing/profile|Per-Game Ticket Pricing & Demand Forecasting]]
**Industry:** [[industries/sports-teams|Sports Teams & Clubs]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Clubs change prices on gut and never measure the effect, so the elasticity in every pricing model is a guess.
**Tags:** #hypothesis-testing #causal-inference #confidence-intervals #quick-win #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to set each game's price by seat tier before the resale market sets it for the club, and whoever raises a club's ticket revenue per available seat against its own prior season takes the account.

## The Problem
A ticket manager raises the lower-bowl price for a weekend series and attendance holds. Was demand insensitive, or would it have sold out anyway? Without a comparison, the next decision repeats the guess.

## Why It's Still Broken
Clubs worry that visible price experiments upset fans, and nobody owns the analysis between games.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Small, planned variations across comparable dates and sections (within a few dollars, within community price floors), with a pre-written comparison and a log of every price change and its result. Feed the measured elasticities back into the forecast.

## Who Feels the Pain
Ticket operations managers who cannot defend a price, general managers choosing between a pricing vendor's claim and their own feel.

## Impact If Fixed
Ten logged and measured price changes a season (estimate) give a club its own elasticity estimates. If acting on them moves ticket revenue by 2–3% on ~$3.9M (estimate), that is ~$80–120K a year, more than most pricing tools cost a small club.

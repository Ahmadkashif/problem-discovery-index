# Premium Seating, Suites & Club Memberships

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/sports-teams|Sports Teams & Clubs]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep a building's suites, loge boxes and club seats sold on multi-year contracts, and whoever's clubs open each season with the highest share of premium inventory already under contract takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$700M a year of premium sales and service staff, premium sales outsourcing, CRM, inventory and hospitality software at North American big-league clubs and Power Four departments (estimate), on premium revenue of ~25–40% of a big-league club's ticket revenue (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~5–10% of club revenue is premium seating (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — suite inventory and contracts live in spreadsheets and PDFs, and selling is done in person with tours and dinners
**Target Buyer:** Vice president of premium sales and service, or chief revenue officer
**Automation Potential:** Medium — prospect targeting, renewal risk and inventory pricing automate; the hospitality relationship does not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Premium inventory is a different product from a season ticket. Suites, loge boxes, field clubs and all-inclusive memberships are sold to companies and wealthy individuals on three- to ten-year contracts, often with food and beverage bundled, and each new or renovated building adds new classes of it. Buyers are corporate controllers and executives who review the spend each year. The work is relationship selling and service, recorded in spreadsheets of suite numbers, contract terms and contact names. Season-ticket renewal tooling, sold by ticketers and CRM vendors, is a separate contest at [[niches/ticketing-agencies/season-ticket-renewals/profile|Season-Ticket & Membership Ticketing]].

## Current Tools & Gaps
Ticketing platforms' suite modules, KORE (Two Circles) and Salesforce for pipeline, premium-sales outsourcing firms that sell inventory for new buildings, and hospitality systems for in-suite food. Gaps: no record of how each suite is actually used (empty games, resold nights, guest counts), renewal risk judged by the account rep's feel, and inventory prices set by building tier rather than by demand.

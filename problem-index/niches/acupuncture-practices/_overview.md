## Why These Niches

Insurance-billing clinics and integrative groups dominate revenue but face wildly inconsistent payer rules across states. Solo community acupuncture and TCM herbalists remain almost entirely paper-based, representing the largest digitization gap. Veterans acupuncture is growing rapidly via VA expansion but providers struggle with federal billing requirements, while rural practitioners face geographic isolation and limited referral networks. The two automation niches target the highest-volume manual workflows — claims processing and intake documentation — where error rates directly erode already-thin margins.

## Why These Pockets

Acupuncture's operator layer is the smallest in the vault — 38,000 practitioners, three quarters of them solo — so nothing at that altitude survives the gate. What the sweep finds instead is that the industry's economics are set almost entirely by other people's research. Whether an acupuncture course gets paid for is decided by a treatment guideline written at MCG or ODG; whether a herbal formula can be sold is decided by a monograph at a publisher the practitioner has never heard of. Those two pockets qualify because in both cases the written analytical artefact *is* the product, produced by staffs far larger than any practice, against clocks nobody in the chain controls.

The third qualifier sits on the herbal supply side rather than the clinical one, and the adjacency is worth stating plainly: natural products channel data vendors sell to the manufacturers who supply dispensaries, which is one hop out from the operator. It is included because the shape is exact — a proprietary taxonomy over data nobody else holds, sold as insight — but it is a supply-chain finding, not a clinical one.

Ten pockets logged without qualifying, and two patterns in the failures generalize. First, the payer-side pockets with the best data score badly on Q1: CAM network managers hold an unmatched CAM claims corpus and workers' comp reviewers hold an unmatched repricing corpus, but both invoice for administration or savings rather than for insight, which caps price and kills procurement. Second, every association and regulatory position in this industry fails the gate outright — the credentialing body, the accreditor, and the federal center are all under thirty people, and the accreditor's actual review labour is volunteer-supplied. In a profession this small, the association layer never reaches research scale.

# Deduction & Chargeback Recovery

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/consumer-products-makers|Consumer Products Makers]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn invalid retailer deductions into credits before each retailer's dispute window closes, and whoever recovers the most invalid deduction dollars per maker takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$400M a year of retailer deductions at makers in the four codes, of which a median 5–10% of dollars is invalid (CRF 2023 survey, as compiled by Overdeduct; the dollar pool is an estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~2% of receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — claims sit in portals and EDI; research and filing are manual
**Target Buyer:** Controller or CFO; at small makers, the owner
**Automation Potential:** High — retrieval, matching and filing are repetitive and rule-bound

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Recovery is a race against each retailer's dispute window, worked claim by claim with documents the maker already has but cannot find fast enough. CRF's 2023 survey puts the median automatic write-off threshold at $50–$100, and 91% of companies write off small deductions automatically (CRF 2023 survey, as compiled by Overdeduct), which is where most of the invalid money sits at a maker with thousands of small claims. The competitors are funded CPG deduction platforms (Confido and Glimpse, both hiring engineers and forward deployed engineers in New York on 2026-10-24; SPS Revenue Recovery, built from SupplyPike and Carbon6), order-to-cash suites such as HighRadius, contingency recovery firms and the maker's own clerk. The maker keeps whichever recovers the most. Wholesalers face the same claims with a second ledger, re-billing valid supplier-caused deductions to the makers and importers they buy from, profiled at [[niches/consumer-goods-wholesalers/two-sided-deductions/profile|Retailer Deductions on Both Sides]].

## Current Tools & Gaps
Those platforms grew on food and beverage brands. The gaps for general merchandise: home-centre defective allowances, seasonal return-to-vendor and markdown claims, Amazon Vendor Central shortage claims, and makers with no promotion system where the agreed allowance is an email.

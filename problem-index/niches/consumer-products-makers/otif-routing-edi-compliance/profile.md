# OTIF, Routing-Guide & EDI Compliance

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/consumer-products-makers|Consumer Products Makers]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep a maker's shipments inside each retailer's routing guide and on-time, in-full targets, and whoever keeps the maker's compliance fines lowest as a share of shipments takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$200M a year of compliance fines and the labour to prevent them at makers in the four codes (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~1% of receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium-High — EDI documents and appointment systems are digital; rule checking is manual
**Target Buyer:** VP of Operations or Supply Chain
**Automation Potential:** High — rules are written down and checkable before the truck leaves

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Prevention is an operations contest. Each retailer's routing guide sets carton labels, pallet builds, advance ship notice timing, appointment rules and fill-rate targets, and fines the supplier when a shipment misses: Walmart charges 3% of the cost of goods on cases that miss its on-time or in-full targets (search summary). The work is fought over by EDI providers, warehouse and transport software, 3PLs and compliance consultants; the maker keeps whoever brings its fines down. The retailer's side of the same rules is profiled as [[niches/contract-packagers/retailer-supplier-compliance-programmes/profile|🔍 Retailer Supplier Compliance & Packaging Programmes]].

## Current Tools & Gaps
EDI providers (SPS Commerce, TrueCommerce, Cleo), warehouse and transport systems, 3PL portals. The gaps: no pre-shipment check against each retailer's current guide; fines analysed months later; root causes split between maker, 3PL and carrier.

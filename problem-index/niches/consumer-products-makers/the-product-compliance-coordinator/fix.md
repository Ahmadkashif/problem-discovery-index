# The Prop 65 Notice Answered From a Shared Drive

**Niche:** [[niches/consumer-products-makers/the-product-compliance-coordinator/profile|The Product Compliance Coordinator]]
**Industry:** [[industries/consumer-products-makers|Consumer Products Makers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** When a 60-day notice or a retailer query arrives, the coordinator spends days finding the formula, the test and the warning decision for one SKU.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #quick-win #compliance #workflow-orchestration #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to have every SKU's certificate, test report and warning ready before listing and import, and whoever clears the most SKUs to listing without a compliance hold takes the account.

## The Problem
Prop 65 produced 1,535 settlements and about $66M in payments in 2025, most of it attorney's fees. A 60-day notice, or a retailer's query about a SKU, starts a search: the formula, the supplier declarations, the test report, and the reasoning behind the original warning decision. Most of the 60 days go to finding documents.

## Why It's Still Broken
Warning decisions were made SKU by SKU, often by email with outside counsel, and nobody recorded why. Documents sit in shared drives named by supplier or date, not by SKU, and the coordinator who made the decision may have moved on.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Index existing certificates, test reports, safety data sheets, IFRA certificates and warning emails by SKU; extract each warning decision and its basis; and assemble a response pack for counsel in an hour, with gaps flagged. New decisions are recorded with their reasons as they are made.

## Who Feels the Pain
Compliance coordinators, owners paying counsel by the hour, and retailers waiting on answers.

## Impact If Fixed
A response pack takes about an hour instead of ~2–3 days (estimate), cutting the counsel hours billed for document gathering, and the decision record makes the next notice faster.

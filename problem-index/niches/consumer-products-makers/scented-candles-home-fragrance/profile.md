# Scented Candles & Home Fragrance

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/consumer-products-makers|Consumer Products Makers]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn each season's fragrance brief into candles that pass burn testing before the retailer's holiday set ships, and whoever puts the most passing new scents on the holiday shelf takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$3.5B US manufacturer receipts from scented candles, wax melts and related home fragrance (estimate; US retail candle sales were ~$3.14B a year in a 2013 Mintel estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~16% of the four codes' ~$21.4B receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low-Medium — orders, EDI and costing are digital; formulation and burn testing run on notebooks and spreadsheets
**Target Buyer:** VP of Product Development or Head of Lab & Quality at a candle maker
**Automation Potential:** Medium — burn measurement and first-wick choice automate; the safety sign-off stays with a person

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Candles are the largest product group in 339999 and the only one where a physical combustion test decides whether a product can ship. Branded makers such as Newell's Yankee Candle, private-label pourers for mass and specialty retailers, gift-trade brands and importers all compete for the same seasonal programmes: the National Candle Association puts ~35% of candle sales in the holiday season. Each programme needs a dozen or more new scents (estimate), and each scent in each vessel needs its own wick, found by test burns judged by eye. Retailers ask for third-party reports against ASTM F2417 and F2058, and CPSC has recalled high-flame candles in 2024 and 2025 (search summary). The account goes to the maker that gets the most new scents through testing on time.

## Current Tools & Gaps
ERPs and costing tools hold the recipe; wick and wax suppliers' application labs run burns for customers; third-party labs certify to ASTM F2417; fragrance houses supply IFRA certificates. The gaps: burn tests recorded as free text; no video or measurement; no searchable history of which wick worked with which fragrance and vessel; production lots never compared with the approved burn.

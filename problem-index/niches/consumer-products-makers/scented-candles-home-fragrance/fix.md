# The Burn Notebook Nobody Can Search

**Niche:** [[niches/consumer-products-makers/scented-candles-home-fragrance/profile|Scented Candles & Home Fragrance]]
**Industry:** [[industries/consumer-products-makers|Consumer Products Makers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Years of burn tests sit in paper notebooks and free-text spreadsheets, so every new scent starts from the chandler's memory.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #k-nearest-neighbors #tacit-knowledge-ml #quick-win #data-integration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn each season's fragrance brief into candles that pass burn testing before the retailer's holiday set ships, and whoever puts the most passing new scents on the holiday shelf takes the account.

## The Problem
"Fig & cedar, 8%, CD 10, 3 in jar, tunnelled hr 2, up one." The record exists, but in a form nobody can query. Years of burn tests sit in paper notebooks, photographed pages and free-text spreadsheets, with fragrance names that are sometimes the supplier's code and sometimes the marketing name. When a similar fragrance arrives in a new brief, only the chandler who wrote the note remembers it, and if that chandler has left, the lab starts from scratch.

## Why It's Still Broken
Lab time goes to pouring and burning, not typing. Fragrance names change between supplier catalogues and retailer briefs, wick suppliers renumber series, and nobody owns the archive or has time to clean it. The value appears only once the history is searchable, so nobody starts.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Photograph or export the old notebooks, extract recipe, wick, vessel and outcome with a language model, normalise fragrance and wick names against supplier catalogues, and let the chandler search "closest past recipes" for any new brief, with the wick that finally passed and how many rounds it took. New tests go straight into the same structured log.

## Who Feels the Pain
Chandlers and lab technicians; product managers waiting on test results before a programme deadline; and the next technician when the senior one retires.

## Impact If Fixed
If a searchable history makes the first wick guess right for about a third of new scents (estimate), the lab saves one test round, roughly a week, on each of them: ~10–15 lab-weeks a year at a maker launching ~40 scents (estimate), for a few weeks of digitising.

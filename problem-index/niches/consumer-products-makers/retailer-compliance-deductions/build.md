# One Ledger for Every Dollar a Retailer Keeps

**Niche:** [[niches/consumer-products-makers/retailer-compliance-deductions/profile|Retailer Compliance, Deductions & Chargebacks]]
**Industry:** [[industries/consumer-products-makers|Consumer Products Makers]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Deductions sit in accounts receivable and fines in operations, so no one at a maker sees, by shipment, the full cost of selling to each retailer.
**Tags:** #gradient-boosting #large-language-models #causal-inference #feature-engineering #revenue-impact #data-integration
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is that every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to stop retailers keeping money a maker earned, but it splits into two contests with different winners: recovering deductions already taken, won by whoever recovers the most invalid dollars before each dispute window closes, and preventing compliance fines before they are charged, won by whoever keeps fines lowest as a share of shipments. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## The Problem
A maker can say what Walmart deducted last quarter. It cannot say which carrier lane, label template or promotion caused it, or what each retailer really costs to serve after deductions, fines and returns.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Deduction tools serve finance; EDI and warehouse tools serve operations; and the join needs shipment-level data from both.

## What to Build
A ledger that links every deduction and fine to the purchase order, shipment, carrier, label and promotion behind it, classifies cause, and ranks fixes by the money they would have saved. Finance gets disputes; operations gets prevention tasks; sales gets the true net margin by retailer.

## Target Customer
CFOs and COOs at makers with three or more big-box accounts.

## Impact If Built
At a $50M maker with deductions and fines of ~2–5% of sales (~$1–2.5M, estimate), fixing the top three causes could remove ~20–30% of the total (estimate), ~$200K–$750K a year, plus the clerk's time spent disputing it.

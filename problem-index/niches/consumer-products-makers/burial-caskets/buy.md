# Distribution Routing and Inventory Tools Fitted to Next-Day Casket Delivery

**Niche:** [[niches/consumer-products-makers/burial-caskets/profile|Burial Caskets]]
**Industry:** [[industries/consumer-products-makers|Consumer Products Makers]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Route planning and distribution inventory software is mature; it needs casket-specific service-date rules, vehicle constraints and funeral-home delivery windows.
**Tags:** #optimization-fundamentals #dynamic-programming #time-series-forecasting #workflow-orchestration #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get the casket a family chose into the funeral home before the service, and whoever fills the highest share of orders before the service takes the account.

## The Problem
Dispatchers at a casket service centre build next-day delivery runs by hand from the orders taken that day, balancing service times at funeral homes across a region, distance, and the few vehicles fitted to carry caskets and outer burial containers safely. A late order or a substitution reshuffles the day, and special runs for a single casket are common when the plan misses. Stock decisions are made separately, with no link to the routes.

## What Already Exists
Fleet routing and dispatch tools and distribution inventory modules used across wholesale and field service, with time windows, vehicle constraints and live re-planning.

## The Customization Gap
Hard service-time windows taken from the funeral home's schedule rather than a generic delivery slot; vehicle and handling constraints for caskets and vaults; substitution rules when a model is out (same gauge, colour family or wood); collection of display units and returns on the same run; and integration with the maker's order intake, including after-hours phone orders, rather than a generic order file. It is configuration and rules, not new routing science.

## Target Customer
Service-centre managers and dispatchers at casket makers and regional distributors.

## Impact If Solved
Planned routes cut special single-casket runs and delivery miles by ~5–10% (estimate) while holding the share of caskets delivered before the service at ~99% or better (estimate).

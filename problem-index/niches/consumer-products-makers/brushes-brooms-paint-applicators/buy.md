# Digital Travellers and Machine Data for a Brush Plant

**Niche:** [[niches/consumer-products-makers/brushes-brooms-paint-applicators/profile|Brushes, Brooms, Mops & Paint Applicators]]
**Industry:** [[industries/consumer-products-makers|Consumer Products Makers]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Low-code manufacturing apps already replace paper travellers in small plants; a brush plant needs them shaped around blends, heads and trims.
**Tags:** #feature-engineering #descriptive-statistics #data-integration #workflow-orchestration #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep professional painters and cleaning crews reaching for its brush, roller or broom at the paint store and home centre, and whoever keeps the largest share of pros' repeat purchases takes the account.

## The Problem
Each run follows a paper traveller listing the blend, ferrule, handle, epoxy and trim profile; the operator notes changes and problems in pen. Pull and shed tests are written on a clipboard, if at all. When a paint store returns a batch of brushes that shed bristles, nobody can find what changed on that run, which machine made it or which filament lot went in.

## What Already Exists
Frontline manufacturing app platforms and small-plant MES and inventory tools used across discrete manufacturing, with digital work instructions, station data capture and lot traceability.

## The Customization Gap
Blend recipes as versioned objects (natural bristle and synthetic filament types, diameters, ratios and tipping); machine settings captured per run; pull and shed tests logged at the station with photos; lot codes on finished goods linked back to runs and filament lots; retailer returns and pro complaints tied to those lots; and simple tablets that setters will actually use with gloves on.

## Target Customer
Plant managers at brush, broom and mop makers with one to three plants.

## Impact If Solved
A quality complaint traces to its run in minutes instead of ~1–2 days (estimate), and scrap from repeated faults falls by ~10–20% once runs are comparable (estimate).

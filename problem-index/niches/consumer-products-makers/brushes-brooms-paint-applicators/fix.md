# Machine Settings That Live in the Setter's Head

**Niche:** [[niches/consumer-products-makers/brushes-brooms-paint-applicators/profile|Brushes, Brooms, Mops & Paint Applicators]]
**Industry:** [[industries/consumer-products-makers|Consumer Products Makers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Two or three setters know how to bring each brush machine back to spec after a changeover, and when one is off sick the line runs slow or makes scrap.
**Tags:** #tacit-knowledge-ml #large-language-models #worker-facing #quick-win #workflow-orchestration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep professional painters and cleaning crews reaching for its brush, roller or broom at the paint store and home centre, and whoever keeps the largest share of pros' repeat purchases takes the account.

## The Problem
Changeovers between brush sizes, blends and handle types need adjustments the machine manuals do not cover: feed tension, cut length, setting depth, trim angle. Two or three setters know how to bring each machine back to spec, learned over decades and done by look and touch. When one is off sick or retires, the line runs slow, makes scrap or waits.

## Why It's Still Broken
Nobody has time to write it down, the setters are not asked to, and a written manual would not capture the parts done by feel anyway. Training is shadowing, which takes years.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Film and voice-record each changeover at the machine, turn it into a short step list with photos, the settings that worked and the checks that show it is right, and keep it on a tablet at the machine. Each new fix is added as it happens, and newer setters mark which steps they needed.

## Who Feels the Pain
Setters, line leads, and the plant manager planning for retirements.

## Impact If Fixed
Changeovers run ~20–30% shorter when the senior setter is away (estimate), and the plant has a succession plan for its scarcest skill.

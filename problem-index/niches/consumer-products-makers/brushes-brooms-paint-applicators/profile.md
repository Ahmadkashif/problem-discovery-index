# Brushes, Brooms, Mops & Paint Applicators

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/consumer-products-makers|Consumer Products Makers]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep professional painters and cleaning crews reaching for its brush, roller or broom at the paint store and home centre, and whoever keeps the largest share of pros' repeat purchases takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$3.0B in 2022 receipts across 138 employer firms (Census SUSB 2022, NAICS 339994)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~14% of the four codes' receipts
**Digital Adoption:** Low — filament blends, machine settings and trim specifications live with setters and on paper travellers
**Target Buyer:** Plant manager or VP of Operations at a family-owned brush maker
**Automation Potential:** Medium — the machines are automated; setting them to match a feel is not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
This is a small, old trade: 138 firms, nine of which (500+ employees) hold ~52% of receipts (SUSB 2022). Paint brushes are built from blends of natural bristle and synthetic filament, flagged and tipped, set in epoxy and trimmed to a profile; rollers, brooms, mops and toothbrushes have their own versions of the same choices. Professional painters judge a brush in seconds by how it picks up and releases paint and holds a line, and they buy from paint-store chains and home centres that decide which makers get the shelf. Makers compete with each other and with imported applicators, and the pro's repeat purchase, which the paint-store chains track through pro accounts, is what keeps the shelf.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Brush-making machines from specialist builders, ERPs and paper travellers, and in-house paint pickup tests. The gaps: no record linking filament blend and machine settings to measured performance; settings held by a few setters; returns and pro feedback not tied to production runs.

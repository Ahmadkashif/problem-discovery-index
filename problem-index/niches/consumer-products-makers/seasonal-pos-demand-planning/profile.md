# Seasonal Demand Planning From Retailer POS

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/consumer-products-makers|Consumer Products Makers]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to set seasonal and new-item quantities from retailer point-of-sale data before the last re-order date, and whoever leaves a maker with the least unsold seasonal stock at season's end takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$900M a year of seasonal markdowns, returns, excess stock and planning labour at makers in the four codes (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~4% of receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — the data is in retailer portals; planning is in spreadsheets
**Target Buyer:** VP of Supply Chain or the demand planning lead
**Automation Potential:** High — data retrieval, mapping and baseline forecasts are mechanical

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
These makers live on seasons: the holiday candle programme (about 35% of candle sales fall in the holiday season, per the National Candle Association), back-to-school pens and markers, spring cleaning tools, Christmas décor. Each season brings new items with no history, a last re-order date set by the retailer, and post-season returns or markdowns for whatever is left. The direct-to-consumer version of the same decision is [[niches/d2c-brand-operators/inventory-and-demand-planning/profile|Inventory & Demand Planning]]; here the data is the retailer's POS, pulled from portals, and the customer is the retailer's buyer. Retail data platforms, planning suites, consultants and in-house planners compete for it, and the maker keeps whoever leaves the least seasonal stock unsold.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Retailer portals (Walmart's Scintilla, Target's partner portal, Amazon Vendor Central), retail data platforms (Alloy.ai, Crisp), planning suites, and spreadsheets. The gaps: new-item forecasts made by analogy in the planner's head; in-season signals read a week late; plant build plans not tied to the forecast's uncertainty.

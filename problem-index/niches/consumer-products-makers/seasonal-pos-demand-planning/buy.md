# Retail POS Platforms Fitted to Seasonal Programmes

**Niche:** [[niches/consumer-products-makers/seasonal-pos-demand-planning/profile|Seasonal Demand Planning From Retailer POS]]
**Industry:** [[industries/consumer-products-makers|Consumer Products Makers]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Retail data platforms already pull and harmonise POS from retailer portals; they are tuned for always-on replenishment, not one-shot seasonal programmes.
**Tags:** #time-series-forecasting #exponential-smoothing #data-integration #workflow-orchestration #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to set seasonal and new-item quantities from retailer point-of-sale data before the last re-order date, and whoever leaves a maker with the least unsold seasonal stock at season's end takes the account.

## The Problem
Retail data platforms forecast steady sellers well and treat a holiday item that sells for ~10 weeks (estimate) like a short history. Planners export the platform's numbers back into spreadsheets to plan seasonal programmes, losing the connectors and mapping they paid for.

## What Already Exists
Alloy.ai, Crisp and EDI providers' retail analytics modules pull and harmonise POS, inventory and orders from retailer portals, with dashboards, alerts and replenishment forecasts; planning suites add supply plans.

## The Customization Gap
Programme objects with set dates, last re-order dates and return or markdown terms per retailer; season-over-season analogue items; sell-through curves for seasonal items rather than steady-state models; end-of-season exposure (units likely left on shelf); and a link from the forecast to the plant's build plan and labour schedule, so the seasonal build can start earlier.

## Target Customer
Planning teams at makers already licensing a retail data platform.

## Impact If Solved
Seasonal forecast error falls by ~10–20% (estimate) inside the platform the planner already pays for, worth ~$100K–$200K a year in avoided returns and markdowns on ~$15M of seasonal sales (estimate).

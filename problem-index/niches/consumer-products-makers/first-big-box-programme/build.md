# A First-Season Margin Model Before the PO Is Signed

**Niche:** [[niches/consumer-products-makers/first-big-box-programme/profile|Small Makers Entering Big-Box Retail]]
**Industry:** [[industries/consumer-products-makers|Consumer Products Makers]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A small maker accepts its first big-box order on gross margin and discovers its real margin months later, after allowances, fines, returns and freight it did not model.
**Tags:** #gradient-boosting #monte-carlo-methods #large-language-models #feature-engineering #revenue-impact #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to carry a first-time supplier through onboarding and its first retail season, and whoever keeps the most first-season makers net-positive after deductions and fines takes the account.

## The Problem
The owner sees a purchase order worth more than last year's whole wholesale business. The vendor agreement sets defective allowances, co-op, payment terms and return rules; the routing guide sets fines; freight terms shift costs. The net margin is unknown until the deductions arrive.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Deduction vendors serve makers already in retail; first-timers are small, scattered and hard to sell to before they need help.

## What to Build
A tool that reads the vendor agreement and routing guide with a language model, takes the order, costs and freight, and simulates first-season net margin with ranges for deductions, fines, returns and markdowns based on what similar makers experienced with that retailer. It flags the terms worth negotiating and the operational steps that prevent the largest fines.

## Target Customer
Owners of makers with under ~$20M in sales weighing a first or second big-box programme.

## Impact If Built
If the model helps an owner negotiate allowances and avoid the largest fine types, first-season net margin improves by ~2–4 points of sales (estimate), ~$40K–$80K on a $2M first programme.

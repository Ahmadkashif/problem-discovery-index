# The Vendor Agreement Nobody Read Before the First PO

**Niche:** [[niches/consumer-products-makers/first-big-box-programme/profile|Small Makers Entering Big-Box Retail]]
**Industry:** [[industries/consumer-products-makers|Consumer Products Makers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The allowance percentages, fine schedules and return rights that decide a first programme's profit are buried in a supplier agreement and manual the owner signed without reading closely.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #quick-win #compliance #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to carry a first-time supplier through onboarding and its first retail season, and whoever keeps the most first-season makers net-positive after deductions and fines takes the account.

## The Problem
Supplier agreements, vendor manuals and routing guides run to hundreds of pages. The owner signs to keep the order and learns the terms one deduction at a time: a defective allowance taken off every invoice, co-op advertising at a percentage of purchases, payment terms longer than expected, a fine schedule for labels and late deliveries, and return rights on unsold seasonal goods.

## Why It's Still Broken
Lawyers are expensive for a first order, the owner is busy getting product made, and the terms look standard enough that owners assume they cannot change them. Some can.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A plain summary of the agreement, manual and routing guide that lists every term that costs money (allowances, fines, payment terms, returns, chargeback rules), with the expected cost for this order in dollars, the operational steps that avoid each fine, and the clauses other suppliers have negotiated.

## Who Feels the Pain
Owners of small makers, and the bookkeeper who reconciles short payments months later.

## Impact If Fixed
Owners price the ~2–5% of sales that allowances and fines typically take (estimate) into the deal before shipping, ~$40K–$100K on a ~$2M first programme, rather than finding it in the first quarter's remittances.

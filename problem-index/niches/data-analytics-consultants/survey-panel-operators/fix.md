# Every Study Is Delivered and the Firm Learns Nothing From It

**Niche:** [[niches/data-analytics-consultants/survey-panel-operators/profile|Survey Research & Panel Operators]]
**Industry:** [[industries/data-analytics-consultants|Data Analytics Consultants]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Thousands of studies a year ship as datasets and decks and are archived by client, so the firm's accumulated knowledge about how populations answer questions is stored as a filing system.

## The Problem
A large research operation runs thousands of studies a year, each producing a dataset, a set of methodological decisions, and a finding. All of it is archived by client and project, which is the index that serves contracts and no analytical purpose. So the firm cannot answer questions about its own body of work that would be genuinely valuable: how has response to this construct moved across a decade of studies, which populations are hardest to reach and how has that changed, how do results differ by mode for this kind of question, which methodological choices are associated with problems downstream. The methodological expertise that justifies a research house's fees is being generated continuously and stored write-only.

## Why It's Still Broken
Studies are commissioned, delivered, and closed, and client confidentiality is read expansively — most contracts protect the client's findings, which is not the same as prohibiting methodological learning across studies, but nobody has done the work of separating the two. The archive was built for retention obligations. And the operating model is project-based with no role owning the corpus, so the effort has no natural sponsor even where the value is obvious.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A cross-study research asset built on the methodological layer rather than on client findings. Each study contributes its instruments, populations, modes, quotas, weighting approach, fieldwork performance, and response distributions on non-proprietary constructs, with a rights model recording what each contract permits so analysis runs on the entitled portion rather than on none of it. That yields several things the firm currently buys or guesses at: normative distributions for common constructs across populations and time, which is the reference every researcher wants and none has; incidence and reachability estimates for hard-to-find populations, which currently get quoted from experience and drive real cost risk in feasibility; and mode and context effect evidence drawn from the firm's own fieldwork rather than from published literature. It also makes fieldwork estimation empirical, replacing the experience-based feasibility guess that determines whether a study is profitable.

## Who Feels the Pain
Researchers re-deriving methodological judgments the firm has already made hundreds of times; the methodology function whose expertise is generated by fieldwork it cannot examine; account teams quoting feasibility from instinct and absorbing overruns; and the firm's positioning, which rests on accumulated expertise it cannot demonstrate.

## Impact If Fixed
Converts thousands of delivered projects into a compounding methodological asset. Feasibility and normative data alone change the economics of bidding and delivery, and the normative reference is a saleable product in its own right — clients pay for benchmarks the firm could construct from work it has already been paid to do.

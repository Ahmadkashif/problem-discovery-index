# Reporter Market Knowledge Is the Asset and Lives in Contacts

**Niche:** [[niches/metal-fabrication/metals-price-reporting-agencies/profile|Metals Price Reporting Agencies]]
**Industry:** [[industries/metal-fabrication|Metal Fabrication]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A reporter's value is knowing who to call and how to weigh what they say, and both leave with the reporter.
**Tags:** #tacit-knowledge-ml #graph-ml #anomaly-detection #worker-facing #data-integration

## The Problem
A metals reporter covering a grade for eight years knows the market in a way no methodology document captures. Which contributors report accurately and which shade. Which trader will explain what is actually happening off the record. Which mill's announced price increase will stick and which is posturing. How this market behaves in the two weeks before a trade case decision. What a widening bid-offer spread in this grade usually precedes.

That is the product. The published assessment is the visible output; the judgment behind it is what makes it credible enough to index contracts to.

None of it is written down. The assessment record holds submissions and published prices. Contact relationships live in personal phones and notebooks. The reasoning behind a judgment call — why the reporter weighted one submission down, why the assessment moved less than the submissions implied — is not captured in any structured form.

Reporters move between agencies regularly, and each departure takes a market's coverage quality with it.

## Why It's Still Broken
The discipline is journalistic and sources are personal. Reporters protect contacts because contacts are their professional capital, and there is a legitimate confidentiality dimension to source relationships.

The methodology also, deliberately, does not capture judgment. Benchmark regulation pushed agencies toward transparent, rules-based processes, and anything that looks like unrecorded discretion is uncomfortable — which has produced a system that documents the rules and not the reasoning applied within them.

And the market coverage model is individual. One reporter owns a grade, which is efficient and makes the knowledge structurally personal.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Capture the market-facing knowledge as institutional, with the source layer protected.

**Contributor reliability profiles, held internally.** Submission accuracy against later-confirmed trades, responsiveness, and consistency — as data rather than as reporter opinion. This is the highest-value capture and it is entirely defensible under benchmark governance, which expects contributor assessment.

**Structured judgment records.** When an assessment departs from the arithmetic of submissions, the reason, in typed categories. This is exactly what a benchmark regulator wants to see documented, and it is currently the least documented part of the process.

**Market context notes attached to grades.** Recurring seasonal behaviour, how the grade responds to trade actions, typical spread relationships to related grades. Institutional market knowledge that today exists only as reporter experience.

**Source relationships recorded at organization level, not individual.** Which companies participate, in what capacity, with what history — without exposing individual source identities where confidentiality applies. That distinction is the whole design problem and it is solvable.

**Retrieval at assessment time.** A reporter covering an unfamiliar grade should see the agency's accumulated knowledge about it, rather than starting with a methodology document and a phone.

## Who Feels the Pain
Reporters, especially new ones, rebuilding market understanding from scratch. Editorial leadership, whose coverage quality is a set of individual tenures in a mobile profession. Contributors, who receive inconsistent treatment depending on who covers them. And every party to a contract indexed to an assessment whose quality depends on who happened to be covering that week.

## Impact If Fixed
The agency's asset is credibility, and credibility rests on judgment that currently walks between competitors. Making contributor reliability and market context institutional protects coverage quality through turnover, gives benchmark governance the documentation it is increasingly asking for, and compresses the years it takes a new reporter to cover a market well.

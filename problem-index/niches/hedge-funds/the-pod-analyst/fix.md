# The Analyst Who Never Learns Which Calls Were Right

**Niche:** [[niches/hedge-funds/the-pod-analyst/profile|The Pod Analyst]]
**Industry:** [[industries/hedge-funds|Hedge Funds]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Analysts see pod P&L, not the factor-neutral outcome of their individual recommendations, so they cannot tell where their judgement is good.
**Tags:** #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #linear-regression #worker-facing #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor serving the pod analyst is fighting to make a coverage list transferable — so the analyst who inherits forty names gets the previous analyst's models, theses and management reads in usable form — and whoever solves the handover becomes the analyst's daily workspace.

## The Problem
An analyst's recommendations are mixed with the PM's sizing and timing and with market moves. Without a factor-neutral record of each recommendation's outcome, analysts cannot learn whether they are better at longs or shorts, at some sectors, at earnings or at long-horizon calls.

## Why It's Still Broken
Recommendations are made verbally and not recorded, and feedback is seen as the PM's job.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Record each recommendation with direction, horizon and conviction, and show the analyst a private, factor-neutral scorecard with uncertainty intervals, broken down by type of call.

## Who Feels the Pain
Analysts trying to improve and to make the case for promotion to PM.

## Impact If Fixed
Analysts learn where their edge is and PMs get evidence for promotions, using data the fund already has.

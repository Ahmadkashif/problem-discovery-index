# The Pod Analyst

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/hedge-funds|Hedge Funds]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor serving the pod analyst is fighting to make a coverage list transferable — so the analyst who inherits forty names gets the previous analyst's models, theses and management reads in usable form — and whoever solves the handover becomes the analyst's daily workspace.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$600M global spend on analyst-level productivity tooling at hedge funds (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~5% of hedge fund research and data spend (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low for the analyst's own workflow — Excel, email and personal notes
**Target Buyer:** Portfolio Manager, Director of Research
**Automation Potential:** High for the mechanical half of the job

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Tools are bought for funds by CIOs and CTOs; the analyst's own day is mostly unserved. At multi-manager platforms the analyst works inside a pod that can be shut down within weeks, moves platforms every few years, and inherits or abandons coverage lists repeatedly. The person whose judgement generates the fund's returns is the one whose working memory is least supported and least portable.

## Current Tools & Gaps
External search (AlphaSense), data terminals, Excel, research management systems used inconsistently. Gaps: no structured handover when coverage changes; personal knowledge kept in personal files; and no feedback on which of their calls were right.

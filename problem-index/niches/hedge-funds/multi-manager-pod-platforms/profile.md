# Multi-Manager Pod Platforms

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/hedge-funds|Hedge Funds]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to separate a pod's skill from its factor and crowding exposure fast enough to move capital before the drawdown — and whoever measures pod skill first decides how the platform's risk budget is allocated.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$3B global spend on central risk, data and analytics at multi-manager platforms (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~25% of hedge fund research and data spend (estimate), concentrated in fewer than twenty firms
**Digital Adoption:** Very High — central teams are among the best-equipped in finance
**Target Buyer:** Chief Risk Officer, Head of Portfolio Construction, Head of Central Research/Data
**Automation Potential:** High — allocation decisions rest on measurable inputs

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Multi-manager platforms such as Millennium, Citadel, Balyasny, Point72 and ExodusPoint allocate capital to dozens or hundreds of semi-independent pods, each run by a PM with tight drawdown limits. The centre's job is to decide how much capital each pod gets, hedge out unwanted factor exposure, and cut pods that stop working. The contest is distinct from the analyst's: it is about measuring PMs, not companies, and doing so with noisy, short track records.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Factor risk models (MSCI Barra, Axioma), in-house risk systems, centralised alternative data and data science teams, and crowding analytics from prime brokers. The gap: pod evaluation leans on realised P&L and drawdown rules over short windows, which cannot separate skill from luck on the sample sizes involved, and does not use the research process itself as evidence.

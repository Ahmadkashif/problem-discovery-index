# Twenty Years of Covenant Reviews and No Record of Which Loopholes Got Used

**Niche:** [[niches/hedge-funds/credit-intelligence-services/profile|Credit & Distressed Intelligence Services]]
**Industry:** [[industries/hedge-funds|Hedge Funds]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Credit intelligence services have reviewed thousands of documents and covered hundreds of liability management exercises, and the two archives are not joined to say which flagged provisions were actually exploited.
**Tags:** #gradient-boosting #survival-analysis #large-language-models #evaluation-metrics #feature-engineering #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this pocket is fighting to publish a reliable reading of a new credit document or restructuring filing before the market trades on it — and whoever reads fastest without losing legal accuracy takes the subscription.

## The Problem
Covenant analysts flag provisions that could let a borrower move value away from creditors. Years later some of those borrowers execute exactly those transactions; most never do. The service holds both the original review and the subsequent restructuring coverage, in different products and teams.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Covenant review and restructuring coverage grew as separate products. Past reviews are stored as documents, not structured records. And the services sell analysis, not predictions, so grading has not been part of the product.

## What to Build
Join every structured covenant review to the borrower's subsequent history — amendments, LMEs, defaults, recoveries — and model which provisions and combinations predict use, conditioned on sponsor and financial stress. Feed it back as a risk score on new reviews, with the precedents that support it.

## Target Customer
Heads of research and product at credit intelligence services.

## Impact If Built
Covenant reviews gain an evidence-based risk ranking only the archive-holder can produce, and analysts learn which of their concerns matter.

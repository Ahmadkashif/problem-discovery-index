# Research Compliance & MNPI Control

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/hedge-funds|Hedge Funds]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to clear a research input — an expert call, a dataset, a management meeting — in minutes with a record an SEC examiner will accept, and whoever makes clearance both fast and defensible takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$800M global spend on research-related compliance tooling and staff time at hedge funds (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~7% of hedge fund research and data spend (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — surveillance is digital, research clearance is largely manual
**Target Buyer:** Chief Compliance Officer
**Automation Potential:** High for triage, low for final judgement

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Insider-trading law and the Investment Advisers Act require advisers to maintain policies reasonably designed to prevent misuse of material non-public information, and SEC enforcement history around expert networks (the 2010s cases) and alternative data has made research inputs a standing examination focus. The compliance work here is not trade surveillance — it is clearing the inputs to research before and after they are used. The tooling for it lags far behind the tooling for surveillance.

## Current Tools & Gaps
E-communications surveillance (Global Relay, Smarsh, Behavox), code-of-ethics and restricted-list platforms (ComplySci, StarCompliance), and expert networks' own compliance screens. The gaps: call pre-clearance done by email and spreadsheet; transcript review by reading; dataset provenance reviewed against questionnaires one at a time; and no consistent record of why something was cleared.

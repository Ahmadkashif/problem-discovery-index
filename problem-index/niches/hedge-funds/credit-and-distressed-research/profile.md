## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Credit research runs on documents rather than models: indentures, credit agreements, amendments, intercreditor agreements and bankruptcy filings, each hundreds of pages long. Since the late 2010s, loose covenants and liability management exercises (LMEs) — uptiers, drop-downs and similar transactions that move value between creditor classes — have made document terms a direct source of gains and losses. That is why the niche is low-digitized and valuable at once.

It is **not terminal**. Two contests live here. For a performing credit book, the question is what each borrower is permitted to do under its documents and which loopholes expose the fund — a document-reading contest at portfolio scale. In stressed and distressed situations the question is which creditor group is organising, how a transaction or plan will treat each tranche, and what the recovery is — a contest of speed on dockets, sources and legal analysis. Different inputs, different clocks, different vendors.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Covenant analysis and credit research from Octus (formerly Reorg), CreditSights (Fitch Solutions, which absorbed Covenant Review), 9fin and Debtwire; court dockets via PACER and specialist services; and in-house legal reading. The gap: document reading still scales with lawyer and analyst hours, and the fund's own interpretations are not structured or reusable.

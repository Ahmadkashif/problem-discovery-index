# A Permission Map for Every Borrower in the Book

**Niche:** [[niches/hedge-funds/covenant-and-document-monitoring/profile|Covenant & Document Monitoring]]
**Industry:** [[industries/hedge-funds|Hedge Funds]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A credit PM cannot ask which holdings would allow a drop-down transaction tomorrow, because the answer lives in hundreds of unread pages.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #graph-theory #evaluation-metrics #feature-engineering #data-integration #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn every credit agreement and amendment in a portfolio into a structured, comparable record of what the borrower is permitted to do — and whoever does it fastest after a document drops sees liability-management risk first.

## The Problem
Liability management exercises have repeatedly moved value away from creditors who did not see them coming, and in most cases the enabling terms were in the documents all along. A fund holding two hundred credits needs to know which documents permit what, and where its own position sits in each capital structure if those permissions are used.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Covering every instrument in a fund's book, including private loans, was uneconomic with lawyer-hours; vendors therefore cover the liquid universe. The structural representation — permissions as a graph over entities, liens and baskets — has no standard.

## What to Build
A permission map per borrower: entities and guarantors, lien priorities, basket capacities computed from current financials, and the specific transactions each document permits, represented as a graph so a PM can see where value could move. Extracted with clause links, updated on every amendment, annotated by the fund's lawyers, and queryable across the book.

## Target Customer
Credit and multi-strategy funds with broadly syndicated and private credit exposure.

## Impact If Built
Document risk becomes a portfolio-level metric reviewed like any other exposure, rather than a surprise discovered in a press release.

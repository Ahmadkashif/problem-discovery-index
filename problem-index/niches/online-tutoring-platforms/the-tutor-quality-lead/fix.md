# Fix: The Only Intervention Is Removal

**Niche:** [[niches/online-tutoring-platforms/the-tutor-quality-lead/profile|The Tutor Quality Lead]]
**Industry:** [[industries/online-tutoring-platforms|Online Tutoring Platforms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Between a guidance email nobody reads and removing a tutor from the platform there is nothing, so most quality concerns produce no action at all.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #workflow-orchestration #hypothesis-testing #compliance #quick-win #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Whether the platform will build responses proportionate to a moderate concern about a tutor.

## The Problem

A quality lead has a concern about a tutor: two mediocre complaints, a pattern in the feedback, something noticed in a sampled recording. Not enough to remove them. More than enough to want something to happen.

Nothing happens. The available actions are a generic guidance email, a warning that reads as a threat, or removal. The first is ignored, the second damages the relationship without helping, and the third is disproportionate. So the concern is noted and the tutor continues, and the quality lead accumulates a list of tutors they are worried about and cannot act on.

The same gap runs the other way. A tutor who is clearly excellent generates no action either, because there is no mechanism to do anything with that.

## Why It's Still Broken

Intermediate actions require intermediate infrastructure — a way to give specific feedback, a coaching path, a review period, a matching adjustment — and none of it was built because quality was scoped as complaint handling.

The contractor relationship is the stated obstacle and it is genuinely constraining, though less than it is used to justify. A platform cannot require a tutor to attend training. It can offer resources, provide feedback, set standards for remaining on the platform, and adjust how it matches — all of which are things a business does with its suppliers routinely. The caution has been over-applied because nobody wanted to be the one to test it.

And the de-weighting option specifically has never been built because it is invisible. Removing a tutor is a decision with a record; quietly matching them less is a ranking change nobody has to own, which makes it both the most useful intermediate action and the one most in need of being made explicit rather than informal.

## What a Fix Looks Like

Build a response ladder and state it.

**Specific private feedback**, referencing an observed pattern rather than a general principle: in three sessions the student spoke less than 20% of the time, here is why that matters and here is one thing to try. This is the most-used rung and requires the observation layer, which is the build in this niche.

**Offered resources and coaching**, tied to the specific pattern, voluntary, ideally delivered by strong tutors on the platform rather than by the platform itself — which is both more effective and cleaner in contractor terms.

**A structured improvement conversation** with a stated concern, a stated standard and a review date. Uncomfortable and fair, and far better than a warning with no path attached.

**Matching de-weighting during a review period**, explicitly and with the tutor told. Doing this openly is better for everyone than the informal version, which happens anyway and which tutors suspect without being able to confirm.

**Removal**, last, with the evidence stated and an appeal.

Then two things that make the ladder work. Write it down and publish it to tutors, so the standards are knowable and the process is not arbitrary. And record every step, so the quality lead can say what they did, at what volume, and whether it changed anything — which is the evidence that gets the function resourced.

Build the positive ladder too: identify strong tutors, tell them, feature them, pay them to mentor or to contribute exemplar sessions. It costs little and it is the only quality lever that improves the platform rather than merely policing it.

## Who Feels the Pain

Students taught by tutors everyone had concerns about and nobody acted on. Tutors, who get either silence or a threat and no path between, and who cannot find out what they are doing wrong. Quality leads, who carry a list of unresolved concerns and a role with no instruments. And the platform, whose quality claims rest on a function that can only remove people.

## Impact If Fixed

Moderate concerns produce proportionate action instead of nothing, which is where nearly all of the quality opportunity sits. Tutors get specific feedback and a route to improve rather than a threat or silence. And the quality function acquires a record of what it did, which is the first evidence anyone in the role has ever had.

# The Tutor Quality Lead

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/online-tutoring-platforms|Online Tutoring Platforms]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Whether the person accountable for teaching quality across thousands of tutors has any instrument that measures teaching.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$480M — 6% of US online tutoring
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~6%
**Digital Adoption:** Low — star ratings, rebooking rates and a complaints queue
**Target Buyer:** Platform quality and operations leadership
**Automation Potential:** High — the instruments they need are computable from recordings

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche

Someone at the platform is responsible for tutor quality and the only instruments they have are star ratings, rebooking rates and complaints. They are asked to raise standards across a workforce of thousands of independent contractors they cannot observe, train or direct, using three metrics that measure satisfaction and one that measures failure.

The role is real and is usually one or two people per platform. Their week consists of reviewing complaints, sampling a handful of recordings manually, writing guidance nobody reads, and defending quality claims to leadership on the basis of a rating average that has been stable for two years.

The niche is distinct from effectiveness measurement because it is about the function rather than the metric: even a perfect effectiveness signal would need an intervention system, a coaching path and a standards process around it, and none of those exist either.

## Current Tools & Gaps

Rating dashboards, rebooking reports, a complaints queue, sampled manual review, and a set of guidelines. Some platforms run an onboarding assessment or a demo lesson review at intake, which is the one point where teaching is actually observed. A few offer optional webinars as professional development.

The gaps are observation, intervention and evidence. Quality is inferred from parent sentiment rather than observed. There is nothing between doing nothing and removing a tutor. And the quality lead cannot tell leadership whether their programme has improved anything, because the only metric available moves with marketing and pricing as much as with teaching.

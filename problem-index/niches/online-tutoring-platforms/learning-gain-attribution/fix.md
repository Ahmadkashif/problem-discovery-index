# Fix: Nobody Asks the Family for the Report Card

**Niche:** [[niches/online-tutoring-platforms/learning-gain-attribution/profile|Learning Gain Attribution]]
**Industry:** [[industries/online-tutoring-platforms|Online Tutoring Platforms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The one piece of outcome evidence that is freely available — the grade the family already receives — is never requested, so the platform measures nothing because it asked nobody.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #hypothesis-testing #compliance #data-integration #quick-win #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Whether the platform will simply ask families to share the outcome they already have.

## The Problem

Outcome measurement in consumer tutoring is treated as impossible because the platform does not hold assessment data. Meanwhile every family using the service receives, several times a year, a report card or grade report containing exactly the information in question, for the subject they are paying for tutoring in.

Nobody asks for it. There is no upload, no prompt, no exchange offered. The platform's position that outcomes are unmeasurable rests on never having made the request.

A meaningful share of families would share it. Parents paying for tutoring are engaged by definition, they want to know whether it is working as much as anyone, and a progress report that put their child's grades alongside their tutoring history is something they would value. The exchange is fair and nobody has proposed it.

## Why It's Still Broken

Partly because the answer might be bad. A platform holding grade data can compute whether tutored students improved, and the finding could be unflattering — which is a reason to prefer not holding it.

Partly it is genuine caution about student data. Grades for minors carry privacy obligations, and collecting them means consent, retention policy, access control and security review. That work is real and is far smaller than it is treated as being.

And partly nobody owns it. Outcome measurement is not product, not marketing, not support. In the consumer segment there is no contractual requirement forcing the question, so it does not get asked.

## What a Fix Looks Like

Ask, and give something back for the answer.

Add a simple prompt at natural moments — the start of tutoring, the end of a term, after a reported exam. Let a parent upload a report card or enter grades directly. Keep it optional, brief, and clear about what it is for.

Offer the exchange honestly: a progress view showing the student's grades over time alongside their tutoring history, sessions, topics covered and tutor notes. That view is worth having for its own sake and is the reason a parent participates. It also gives the platform its first longitudinal student record.

Capture a baseline at intake regardless. Current grade, target grade, and what the family is trying to achieve — three fields, asked once, at the point where the parent is already telling a tutor this in the first message. Without a baseline nothing later is interpretable, and it costs nothing to collect.

Handle the data properly. Explicit consent, stated purpose, defined retention, access restricted, and aggregated analysis separated from identified records. Say plainly that it will be used to understand whether tutoring is working — parents respond well to that framing.

Then report what you find, at the aggregate level, publicly. A platform that can say tutored students in its programme improved by a stated amount, with a stated method and its limitations, has the only outcome claim in a market where everyone else has testimonials. That is worth considerably more in marketing than the current silence.

Start with the subset that opts in and be honest that it is selected. A selected sample with a stated caveat is enormously more informative than no sample at all.

## Who Feels the Pain

Families, who spend thousands of dollars over a year with no idea whether it worked and no instrument offered to find out. Effective tutors, whose results are invisible and who cannot distinguish themselves from tutors who are merely pleasant. Districts and schools evaluating providers, who find no outcome evidence in the consumer market. And the platform, which claims outcomes cannot be measured without having tried the obvious thing.

## Impact If Fixed

The platform acquires outcome evidence for the cost of a prompt and a progress screen. Families get a view of their child's progress that nobody currently provides. And the industry's central excuse — that learning cannot be measured here — is tested rather than assumed, which is the first step in the whole chain.

# Learning Gain Attribution

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/online-tutoring-platforms|Online Tutoring Platforms]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Whether the student learned, and whether it was because of this tutor.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$900M — 40% of the tutor effectiveness niche
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~11% of US online tutoring
**Digital Adoption:** Essentially zero in the consumer segment; partial in district contracts
**Target Buyer:** Districts and schools buying tutoring; platforms selling into them; families
**Automation Potential:** Moderate — the modelling is tractable; the data acquisition is not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche

This is the half that reaches outside the product. Whether a student learned is not observable in a session recording. It requires assessments, grades or test scores that belong to a school, a family, or an instrument someone has to administer — and it requires a claim about causation, since a student who improved may have improved anyway.

Three things make it a distinct market rather than a continuation. The evidence has to be acquired from parties outside the transaction, which is a business development problem before it is a modelling one. The horizon is months, so the feedback loop is far too slow to coach on. And the finding is consequential in a way in-session analysis is not: an attribution study can conclude that a substantial, well-rated segment of the tutor base produces no measurable gain, which is a result about named individuals with income at stake.

It is also the half with the strongest external demand. Districts spending public money on tutoring are increasingly required to demonstrate impact, and that requirement — not consumer preference — is what funds this work where it happens at all.

## Current Tools & Gaps

District-contracted tutoring programmes frequently include pre- and post-assessment and sometimes independent evaluation. A research literature on high-dosage tutoring exists and is reasonably strong at the programme level. Platform-administered diagnostics exist on some services.

The gaps are at the individual level and in the consumer segment. Programme-level evidence that tutoring works says nothing about whether a particular tutor works. Consumer tutoring has no outcome measurement at all — families buy on rating and hope. And the causal question is rarely addressed properly even where data exists, because the comparison group is hard to construct and the selection into tutoring is strong.

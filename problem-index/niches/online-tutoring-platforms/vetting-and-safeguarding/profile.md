# Tutor Onboarding, Vetting & Safeguarding

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/online-tutoring-platforms|Online Tutoring Platforms]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Whether a platform placing adults in unsupervised one-to-one contact with children knows that its checks are adequate and its monitoring is real.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$240M — 3% of US online tutoring
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~3%
**Digital Adoption:** Moderate — background checks are automated, ongoing monitoring is thin
**Target Buyer:** Platform trust & safety leadership; schools and districts as procurers
**Automation Potential:** Very high — the checks and the monitoring are both automatable

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche

A tutoring platform places an adult in a one-to-one video session with a child, unsupervised, repeatedly, and frequently allows private messaging between them. That is a safeguarding responsibility of a specific and serious kind, and it is the one area of this industry where the failure mode is not commercial.

The niche is distinct because it is the only part of the business where the platform's obligations are external and largely non-negotiable — to families, to districts under their own policies, and to the law — and because the standards are clearest in the district segment and weakest in the consumer one, where a parent has only the platform's word about what checking was done.

## Current Tools & Gaps

Identity verification and criminal background checks at onboarding, typically bought from the established screening vendors. Some platforms run qualification verification, reference checks or a demo lesson. Session recording is common and is generally cited as the safeguarding measure. In-platform messaging with some keyword filtering.

The gaps are what happens after onboarding and whether anyone checks the checks. Point-in-time screening goes stale, and continuous monitoring — re-screening on new records — is standard in some adjacent industries and inconsistent here. Recording is a deterrent and an evidence source and is not monitoring, since nobody watches it. Off-platform contact is prohibited and largely undetected. And no platform publishes its screening standard, its coverage, or what its monitoring actually consists of, so a parent comparing two services cannot tell which is more careful.

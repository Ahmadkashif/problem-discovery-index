# Fix: The Recording Nobody Watches

**Niche:** [[niches/online-tutoring-platforms/vetting-and-safeguarding/profile|Tutor Onboarding, Vetting & Safeguarding]]
**Industry:** [[industries/online-tutoring-platforms|Online Tutoring Platforms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Session recording is cited as the safeguarding measure and nobody watches the recordings, so it is an evidence store described as a safeguard.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #compliance #workflow-orchestration #confidence-intervals #large-language-models #quick-win #automation
**Contested on:** Whether the platform will be accurate about what its recording actually provides.

## The Problem

Ask a tutoring platform how children are kept safe in one-to-one sessions and the answer includes that sessions are recorded. Parents hear this as monitoring. It is not monitoring. The recordings are stored, viewed only when a complaint arrives, and nobody looks at them otherwise.

Recording is genuinely valuable — it deters, it provides evidence, and it makes an investigation possible. Those are real benefits and they are all retrospective. Presented as an ongoing safeguard, it creates a false impression of supervision that nobody is providing, and it has substituted for building monitoring that would be prospective.

The gap widens where recording is not universal. Some platforms record by default, some by opt-in, some only certain session types. Parents rarely know which applies to them.

## Why It's Still Broken

Watching recordings at scale was impossible and nobody said so plainly. The claim that sessions are recorded was true and the inference parents drew was convenient, so it was left uncorrected.

Building actual monitoring raised the surveillance objection — watching teaching sessions involving children, at scale, is uncomfortable and easy to get wrong — and that objection, applied to content monitoring, was allowed to block the structural monitoring that does not involve watching teaching at all.

And there is no forcing function in the consumer segment. Districts audit; parents accept the description they are given.

## What a Fix Looks Like

Be accurate about what exists, and build the cheap parts of what does not.

**Say plainly what recording provides.** Sessions are recorded and retained for a stated period, reviewed if a concern is raised, and not watched routinely. That is an honest and reasonably reassuring statement, and it is far better than an implication that collapses the first time a parent asks a direct question.

**Make recording universal and non-disableable** for sessions involving minors, with clear consent at signup. A safeguard that can be turned off by the party it constrains is not one, and requests to disable recording are themselves a signal worth flagging.

**Add the structural monitoring that needs no watching.** Off-platform contact attempts in messages, contact outside sessions, unusual scheduling, requests to move to another video service. These are metadata checks, they cover the pattern prohibited-contact rules exist for, and they involve no analysis of teaching content whatsoever.

**Give students and parents a visible reporting route** inside the session interface, with a stated response time and a process behind it. This is the channel through which almost all genuine concerns actually arrive, and making it findable during a session matters more than any detection system.

**Tell families what is retained, for how long, who can access it, and how to request deletion.** Specificity here is what makes recording acceptable; vagueness is what makes it feel intrusive while providing less than people assume.

**Publish the whole standard** — checks performed, refresh interval, monitoring in place, reporting route, response process. A parent choosing between two platforms currently has no basis for comparison, and the platform doing more has no way to say so.

## Who Feels the Pain

Parents, who believe their child's sessions are supervised in a way they are not. Children, in the rare and serious cases where a prospective measure would have mattered. Careful platforms, who do more and cannot demonstrate it against competitors who describe the same recording in the same words. And tutors, whose sessions are recorded without a clear statement of who may watch them and when.

## Impact If Fixed

Families get an accurate account of what protects their child rather than an implication. Universal recording plus structural monitoring closes the gap between what is claimed and what exists, at low cost. And a published standard creates the comparison that lets careful platforms compete on it — which is how the floor in this industry rises.

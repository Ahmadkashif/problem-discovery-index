# Buy: Teacher Coaching Analytics Adapted to One-to-One Tutoring

**Niche:** [[niches/online-tutoring-platforms/session-instruction-analysis/profile|Session Instruction Analysis]]
**Industry:** [[industries/online-tutoring-platforms|Online Tutoring Platforms]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Classroom analytics products already score teaching from recordings; their rubrics measure classroom management and whole-class discourse, neither of which exists in a one-to-one video session.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #bert #descriptive-statistics #worker-facing #compliance
**Contested on:** Whether classroom observation analytics can be re-specified for a two-person online session.

## The Problem

Automated teaching analytics is a real, working category. Products serving K-12 schools record classroom audio, transcribe it, and report measures like teacher talk ratio, question types, wait time and student participation, feeding teacher coaching programmes with research backing behind the constructs.

The constructs were defined for a classroom of thirty. Much of what they measure — distribution of participation, classroom management, cold-calling patterns, group work transitions — has no analogue in a one-to-one session. What transfers is the questioning and feedback dimension, which is a fraction of the rubric and the fraction that matters most here.

## What Already Exists

TeachFX, Edthena and the classroom discourse analytics category. Observation rubrics — CLASS, Danielson, and subject-specific frameworks — with published rater reliability. Transcription with diarisation. A substantial research literature linking specific instructional moves to learning. Coaching platforms for teacher development.

## The Customization Gap

**Most of the rubric does not apply.** The dimensions have to be re-specified for a dyad: questioning, wait time, error diagnosis, cognitive load management, scaffolding and release of responsibility, and continuity across sessions with the same student. That last one is unique here and valuable — a classroom product has no concept of the same learner returning weekly for months, which a tutoring platform can analyse and which is where the most interesting signal lives.

**The subject content matters more.** In a one-to-one session the tutor is working through specific problems, and whether they diagnosed the specific misconception is a subject-matter judgement. Classroom analytics stay largely content-agnostic; here a maths-specific or reading-specific analysis is substantially more useful and requires subject models the general products do not have.

**The measured party is a contractor.** School products are deployed by an employer into a professional development programme with teachers who have training, contracts and often union protection. Tutors are independent, may work across platforms, and a scoring system that affects their income raises consent and fairness questions the classroom vendor never faces.

**Scale and cost are different by orders of magnitude.** A school product analyses a few lessons per teacher per term. A marketplace could analyse every session for every tutor — hundreds of thousands a week — which changes the per-session cost budget completely and makes the cheap structural measures the backbone rather than the supplement.

**Minors, recordings and consent.** Classroom recording sits under school policy with institutional consent. Here the platform must obtain family consent for recording and analysis of sessions involving children, define retention, and restrict access — a compliance layer that is the platform's responsibility and is not part of any bought product.

## Target Customer

The classroom analytics vendors themselves, for whom online tutoring is an adjacent market with a much larger session volume and no incumbent. Also tutoring platforms that will evaluate these products, find the rubric mismatch in the pilot, and need to know what re-specification actually requires.

## Impact If Solved

The transcription, discourse-measure and coaching-delivery machinery gets reused, and the dyadic rubric, subject specificity, contractor fairness, per-session economics and minors' consent get built deliberately. In practice: every session analysed rather than a sample, on measures that apply to what tutoring actually is.

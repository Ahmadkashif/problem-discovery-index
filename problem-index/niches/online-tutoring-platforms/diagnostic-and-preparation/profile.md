# Diagnostic & Session Preparation

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/online-tutoring-platforms|Online Tutoring Platforms]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Whether a tutor can find out what a student does not know before the session rather than during it.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.04B — 13% of US online tutoring
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~13%
**Digital Adoption:** Very low — unpaid, unsupported and invisible
**Target Buyer:** Tutors directly; platform product teams
**Automation Potential:** High — diagnosis from prior sessions and student work is well-posed

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche

A tutor's first session is spent working out what the student does not know, and the platform that has watched a million such sessions offers no help with it.

Diagnosis is the foundation of effective tutoring. A student failing algebra may have an algebra problem, or a fractions problem from four years earlier, or a reading problem that means they cannot parse the question. Which of these it is determines everything about what should happen next, and finding out takes a skilled tutor most of a first session — an hour the family paid for that produced no teaching.

Preparation compounds it. Between sessions, a tutor who prepares reviews the student's work, plans a sequence, finds or makes materials and thinks about the misconception. None of it is paid.

The niche is distinct because it is the largest single pool of unpaid skilled work in the industry, and because both halves are substantially automatable from data the platform already holds.

## Current Tools & Gaps

Some platforms offer a placement or diagnostic quiz, generally coarse and grade-level rather than misconception-level. Curriculum-producing platforms supply materials for their own content. Tutors otherwise rely on asking the parent, asking the student, and working it out live. Preparation is done in a tutor's own time with whatever they have assembled personally.

The gaps are diagnosis granularity and continuity. A grade-level placement does not tell a tutor which specific misconception is blocking the student, which is the useful unit. And nothing carries forward: a tutor picking up a student who previously worked with someone else on the platform starts from nothing, though the platform holds the entire history of those sessions.

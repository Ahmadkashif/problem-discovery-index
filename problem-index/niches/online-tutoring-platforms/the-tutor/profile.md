# The Tutor

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/online-tutoring-platforms|Online Tutoring Platforms]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Whether the person delivering the service can see their own economics, hold their own client relationships, or influence the terms they work under.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.04B — 13% of US online tutoring
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~13%
**Digital Adoption:** Low — a calendar, a payout screen and a commission they did not set
**Target Buyer:** Tutors directly; tutor organisations; platforms competing for supply
**Automation Potential:** High — the accounting is derivable from records the tutor holds

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche

The platform sets the economics and holds the relationship. It determines the commission, ranks tutors in search, controls whether a tutor is surfaced to a student, and in most cases prohibits taking the relationship off-platform. Commission rates in this sector have moved against tutors over time in several well-publicised cases, and a tutor has no leverage in that change.

The tutor is running a small teaching practice with no instruments. They do not know their realised hourly rate once preparation, marking and messages are counted. They cannot forecast next month's income. They cannot tell whether their rate is competitive. They have no portable record of their experience or their results, so leaving means starting over.

The niche is distinct because the subject is the tutor's own practice rather than the platform's mechanics, and because much of it is buildable from the tutor's own data across whichever platforms they work on.

## Current Tools & Gaps

Platform dashboards show booked hours, earnings and ratings. Tutors keep their own spreadsheets, use general invoicing tools if they work independently as well, and exchange extensive advice in forums about which platforms pay what and which policies to watch.

The gaps are the same three that recur across platform labour. Realised rate including unpaid work is never computed, so the rate a tutor quotes bears no relation to what they earn per hour worked. Income is unforecastable despite being highly seasonal and predictable from a booking calendar. And a tutor's professional record — hours taught, subjects, students' outcomes, feedback — is the platform's data, not theirs, which is what makes the switching cost high and the commission unopposable.

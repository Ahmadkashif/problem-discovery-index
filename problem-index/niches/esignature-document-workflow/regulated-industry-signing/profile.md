# Regulated Industry Signing

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/esignature-document-workflow|E-Signature & Document Workflow]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor here is fighting to finish a regulated transaction without a room — and that contest splits by transaction type rather than by regulation, which is why this niche is not terminal and is decomposed below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.9B US across financial services, healthcare, real estate and public sector signing
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~27% of category revenue, and the large majority of the remaining paper
**Digital Adoption:** Low — this is where the residual wet ink is concentrated
**Target Buyer:** Varies sharply by transaction: lenders and title agents, health systems, government agencies
**Automation Potential:** Medium-High, gated by statute and by third-party coordination rather than by technology

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Electronic execution became legally routine in the United States through ESIGN and UETA more than two decades ago, and yet a substantial share of high-value, consequential transactions still ends in a room with paper. The reasons are not that the technology is unavailable. They are that certain instruments carry statutory formalities — notarisation, witnessing, recording with a county office, original-document requirements — that the general-purpose signature product does not address; that the transaction involves four or five parties with different systems, different regulators and different incentives; and that the assurance level a clicked email link provides is visibly inadequate to the value at stake. This is where the category's remaining growth actually is, and it is underdigitised for structural reasons rather than for want of adoption.

The filter fails here. "Regulated" describes which customers buy the product; it does not describe what the competition is about, and the competition is about entirely different things inside it. A mortgage closing is a package assembly and multi-party coordination problem with a county recorder at the end. A clinical consent is a point-of-care capture problem with a chart write-back at the end. They share a compliance posture and nothing else — not the buyer, not the incumbent, not the failure mode, not the definition of a win. The two contested sub-niches below carry the terminal statements.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Vertical point solutions in each corner — closing platforms in lending, consent modules inside electronic health records, agency-specific portals in the public sector — alongside general signature platforms with regulated-industry configurations. The gaps: the general platforms treat regulation as a settings problem, which handles audit trails and retention but not the statutory formalities; the vertical solutions are strong in their corner and do not generalise; and every one of these transactions involves parties outside the buying organisation, which is the coordination problem no signature product owns.

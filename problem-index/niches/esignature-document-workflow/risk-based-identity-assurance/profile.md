# Risk-Based Identity Assurance

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/esignature-document-workflow|E-Signature & Document Workflow]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in signing assurance is fighting to set the verification level per transaction from its actual risk rather than per account from a default — and whoever does that gives customers both lower fraud and less friction, which are currently traded against each other.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$890M US identity assurance attached to electronic signing
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~13% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — the options exist and the decision is made once
**Target Buyer:** Fraud, trust and legal risk owners; in practice set by whoever configured the account
**Automation Potential:** Very High — transaction attributes and outcomes are all available

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Every platform offers a ladder of identity assurance: an emailed link, an access code, knowledge-based authentication, government identity document verification, a qualified certificate, a notarial act. Companies pick one rung during implementation and apply it to everything, because nothing tells them which transactions warrant more and every step up costs money and adds friction. The result is the worst of both: a two-hundred-dollar service agreement gets the same verification as a property transfer, which means either the small transaction is over-verified and completes worse, or the large one is under-verified and is the one that gets attacked. The assurance decision is a per-transaction risk decision being made as a per-account configuration decision, and that mismatch is the entire niche.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Assurance options across the full ladder, configurable per template or per envelope in the more capable products, integrated identity verification vendors, and authentication event records in the audit trail. The gaps: nothing recommends a level, so the ladder is a menu nobody has a basis for choosing from; the outcomes that would inform the choice — disputed signatures, repudiation claims, fraud losses — are not fed back to any platform, so nobody learns; the friction cost of each rung is unmeasured, so the trade-off is discussed without either number; and signature repudiation risk is not modelled at all, despite being the reason the ladder exists.

# The Signer Side

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/esignature-document-workflow|E-Signature & Document Workflow]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor that takes the counterparty seriously is fighting to make the person receiving an agreement able to understand it, trust it and ask about it without leaving the envelope — and whoever does that raises completion, which is the only number the sender buys on.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$540M US attributable to signer-side experience, delivery and trust
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~8% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Low — the signer has no account, no history and no product built for them
**Target Buyer:** The sender pays; the beneficiary is the counterparty
**Automation Potential:** High — comprehension, context and question-handling are all tractable

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Everyone in this category builds for the sender. The sender has an account, a dashboard, templates, reporting and a customer success manager. The signer — who is the single point of failure for the industry's headline metric — receives an email from an unfamiliar domain, containing a link, asking them to agree to something they have not read, often on a phone, sometimes in a language they read with difficulty, with no way to ask a question and nobody to ask. A large share of non-completion originates entirely on this side: the email looked like phishing, the document was unreadable on the device it arrived on, the signer had a question with no route to an answer, or the signer was the wrong person and had no way to say so. The category has built a two-sided workflow and productised one side.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Branded envelope experiences, mobile-responsive signing views, guided field navigation, and in some products a comment or decline-with-reason path. The gaps: nothing explains the agreement in terms the signer can act on, so the honest choice is between reading a contract they cannot parse and clicking through; the sender-verification problem is unaddressed, and phishing awareness training has correctly taught people to distrust exactly this email; there is no route to a question that does not require composing an email to a stranger; the signer has no durable copy or record of what they have signed across senders; and declining or redirecting to the right person is either absent or buried, so the signer's most useful possible action is the one the product discourages.

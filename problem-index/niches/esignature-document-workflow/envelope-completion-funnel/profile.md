# Envelope Completion Funnel

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/esignature-document-workflow|E-Signature & Document Workflow]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to know why an envelope has stalled while it can still be saved and to act on that reason automatically — and whoever raises completion rate takes the account, because completion is the number the buyer already reports.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$2.4B US attributable to transactional signature volume and the agreement workflow around it
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~34% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** High for sending and tracking, effectively zero for diagnosis
**Target Buyer:** Revenue operations, legal operations and the platform vendors themselves
**Automation Potential:** Very High — the routing, timing and outcome of every envelope is already recorded

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The signature itself is solved and commoditised; what remains contested is the funnel around it. A meaningful share of agreements sent for signature never complete, and the reasons are almost never exotic. They were sent to a person who does not have authority to sign. They were routed to someone on holiday. They arrived with no context, from a sender the recipient does not recognise, and looked like a phishing attempt. They stalled on a term that nobody flagged before sending and that now requires a renegotiation nobody has started. Every one of those is visible or inferable in data the platform holds — the routing order, the open-without-sign events, the time since last activity, the reminder history, the sender's other envelopes to the same counterparty — and every platform reports the same thing: outstanding. The distinct capability is diagnosis and intervention inside the window where the agreement is still recoverable, and it is the capability the industry's own headline metric is defined around.

## Current Tools & Gaps
All major platforms provide status tracking, automatic reminders on a fixed schedule, and dashboards of outstanding envelopes. Some offer bulk send and basic reporting on time-to-complete. The gaps: reminders are scheduled rather than diagnostic, so a wrongly-routed envelope receives four polite nudges to a person who cannot sign it; no platform distinguishes the stall types, which are the actionable unit; nothing predicts a stall before it happens, though the signals appear early; and the escalation that would actually resolve it — routing to the correct signer, alerting the sender to a specific blocker, surfacing the term under dispute — is left to a human who finds out on the thirty-first.

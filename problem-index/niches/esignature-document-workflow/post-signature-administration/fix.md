# The Renewal Nobody Wanted

**Niche:** [[niches/esignature-document-workflow/post-signature-administration/profile|Post-Signature Administration]]
**Industry:** [[industries/esignature-document-workflow|E-Signature & Document Workflow]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** An agreement auto-renews for a year because the notice window closed while the calendar reminder sat in the mailbox of someone who left in March.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #hypothesis-testing #logistic-regression #automation #quick-win #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor moving from signature to agreement platform is fighting to turn the executed document into structured obligations that drive a calendar and a system of record — and whoever does that stops being a signature vendor, which is the whole strategic question in the category.

## The Problem
A software agreement renews automatically unless cancelled ninety days before the anniversary. The person who signed it set a personal calendar reminder for sixty days before, which was already too late, and left the company in March. The reminder fires into a disabled mailbox. In August the invoice arrives for another year of a product the team stopped using in January. This happens routinely, at every size of organisation, and the terms that caused it were written down in a document the company holds.

## Why It's Still Broken
Renewal tracking lives in personal calendars because no system owns it — the signature platform's job ended at execution, the finance system knows about the invoice rather than the notice window, and contract lifecycle management is priced above most of the companies affected. Notice periods are also frequently misread, since ninety days before the anniversary, ninety days before the end of the term and ninety days before the renewal date are three different dates in agreements that use the phrases interchangeably. And the failure has a delay of a year between cause and consequence, so nobody connects them.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Make renewal an organisational obligation rather than a personal reminder. Extract or retain the renewal terms and compute the actual notice deadline explicitly, showing the calculation, since the calculation is where the errors are. Own the deadline at the organisation level with a named role and an escalation path, so a departure does not silently remove the only person watching. Alert well ahead of the deadline rather than at it — a notice window is the last date to act, and the decision needs weeks before that. Attach the usage or value evidence to the alert, because the question at renewal is whether the thing is still worth having and nobody wants to answer that cold. Route notice correctly, to the address and in the manner the agreement specifies, which is a requirement people miss even when they remember the date. And report the portfolio: every agreement, its renewal type, its next deadline and its annual value, on one page — a view most organisations cannot produce and which typically surfaces several agreements nobody knew were still live.

## Who Feels the Pain
Contract administrators holding a set of personal reminders they inherited; finance teams paying for renewed agreements nobody chose; and small organisations without a legal function, where this is a real and recurring cost.

## Impact If Fixed
The failure is fully preventable from information the organisation already holds, and the portfolio view alone typically finds live agreements that had been forgotten. Computing the deadline explicitly rather than by hand removes a misreading that is as common as the missed reminder.

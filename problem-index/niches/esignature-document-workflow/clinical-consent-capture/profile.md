# Clinical Consent Capture

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/esignature-document-workflow|E-Signature & Document Workflow]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in clinical consent is fighting to capture the right consent form for the procedure actually being performed, bound to the correct patient and encounter, and land it in the chart before the patient goes to theatre — and whoever does that reliably takes the health system.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$410M US clinical consent, intake and authorization capture
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~6% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Low-Medium — intake has digitised faster than procedural consent
**Target Buyer:** Health system perioperative, risk management and health information leadership
**Automation Potential:** High — procedure codes, scheduling and form libraries are all structured

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Clinical consent looks like signature and behaves like clinical documentation. The signer is present rather than remote, so the completion problem the rest of the category obsesses over barely exists. What matters instead is whether the form is the right one — matched to the procedure actually scheduled, including laterality and any additional procedures the surgeon may add — whether it is bound to the correct patient and encounter rather than to an email address, whether the clinician who obtained it is recorded, whether the patient could understand it, and whether it reaches the chart in a form that is retrievable at the moment someone needs it. A consent that is missing, wrong-sided, for a superseded procedure, or unfindable is one of the reliable contributors to a cancelled case and to a claim. The contest here is clinical correctness and integration, not execution.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Consent modules inside major electronic health record systems, perioperative and intake platforms, digital front-door products handling registration paperwork, and paper in a substantial share of settings. The gaps: form selection is done by a person choosing from a library, which is where the wrong-procedure and wrong-laterality errors enter; the form does not update when the scheduled procedure changes, which happens routinely; comprehension is unaddressed despite consent forms being written far above the reading level of the population signing them; the record often lands as a scanned image rather than as structured data, so nobody can query which consents are missing before tomorrow's list; and interpretation and translation for patients with limited English proficiency is handled ad hoc at the point of signing.

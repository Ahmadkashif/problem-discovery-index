# Identity Proofing Beyond the Breached Credit File

**One-liner:** Knowledge-based authentication asks questions answerable from data that has been breached repeatedly, and it remains the default identity proofing standard for remote notarization in much of the country.
**Tags:** #cnns #transformers #object-detection #logistic-regression #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #compliance #data-integration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in remote notarization is fighting to produce an act that will be accepted — by the recipient county, registry, court or counterparty — in every jurisdiction the customer transacts in, and whoever has the widest accepted footprint takes the volume.

## The Problem
The dominant remote identity-proofing method asks a signer to identify a former address, a car loan amount or a mortgage holder — all of which are in commercially available and repeatedly breached data. It fails legitimate signers regularly, particularly those with thin files, recent immigrants and young adults, and it is weak against a determined impersonator. Meanwhile document-and-biometric verification, presentation attack detection and credential-based digital identity have all matured substantially and are used routinely in account opening at far lower transaction values.

## What Already Exists
Identity document authentication with liveness and presentation attack detection is a mature commercial category with several credible providers and open research behind it. Face matching between a document portrait and a live capture is commodity. Mobile driver's licence credentials with cryptographic verification are being issued by a growing number of states. Device and behavioural signals for fraud detection are standard in financial services. The identity verification industry treated as a separate market in this vault supplies all of this.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a legally consequential act with prescribed standards. It requires: (1) alignment to the specific state rules on permissible proofing methods, which sometimes prescribe the technique and sometimes a standard, and some of which still require knowledge-based authentication regardless of its weakness — a constraint the product must live with rather than argue away; (2) evidentiary capture, since the act may be challenged years later and what matters is a retained, tamper-evident record of the evidence considered rather than a pass or fail; (3) fairness measurement across demographic groups, which is both a documented weakness of face-matching systems and a specific problem here because a false rejection denies access to a legal act rather than to a discretionary service — this needs measuring and publishing, not assuming; (4) a supervised fallback path, because any automated method will fail legitimate signers and a notarial act that cannot be completed has no acceptable failure mode; and (5) graduated assurance matched to the instrument's value and revocability, which connects directly to the risk-based assurance niche elsewhere in this industry.

## Target Customer
Remote notarization platforms, the state commissioning authorities setting the standards, and the identity verification vendors for whom this is an adjacent regulated application.

## Impact If Solved
The current default is weak against exactly the threat it exists to counter and simultaneously excludes legitimate signers, which is the worst combination available. Every replacement component is mature; the work is the rule alignment, the evidentiary record and the fairness measurement, and the third is the one most likely to be skipped.

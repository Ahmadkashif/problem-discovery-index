# Notarization & Witnessing

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/esignature-document-workflow|E-Signature & Document Workflow]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in remote notarization is fighting to produce an act that will be accepted — by the recipient county, registry, court or counterparty — in every jurisdiction the customer transacts in, and whoever has the widest accepted footprint takes the volume.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$620M US notarial and witnessed execution services
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~9% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Low — remote online notarization is legal in most states and used in a minority of eligible acts
**Target Buyer:** Title and escrow, estate planning, lending, legal operations and corporate secretarial functions
**Automation Potential:** Medium-High — identity proofing and session recording automate; acceptance does not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The notary is the reason a large class of documents cannot be finished electronically. The act itself is simple — verify identity, witness the signature, apply a seal, keep a journal — and the difficulty is entirely in its legal reception: the notary must be commissioned in a specific state, the signer's location may matter, the permissible technology and identity-proofing standard are prescribed by state rule, the recording county or receiving registry may or may not accept the resulting electronic act, and a defective act can invalidate an instrument years later when nobody involved is available to fix it. Witnessing is the same problem with a different formality: some documents require witnesses who are not parties, some require them to be physically present, and the rules vary by state and by instrument type, with wills and certain estate documents the most restrictive. This is a compliance-and-acceptance market wearing the clothes of a technology market.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Remote online notarization platforms with identity proofing and session recording; notary marketplaces matching signers to commissioned notaries; e-notary tooling inside closing platforms; and mobile notary services, which remain a substantial part of the market. The gaps: acceptance is not knowable in advance, which is the same acceptance-matrix problem the closing sub-niche describes and is the binding constraint here too; identity proofing relies heavily on knowledge-based authentication, which is weak against anyone holding a breached credit file and is being deprecated in several states; notary availability at the moment of need is a scheduling problem nobody has solved well, so an urgent act at eight in the evening is a phone hunt; and journal and recording retention obligations run for years with no product built for the retrieval that matters when the act is later challenged.

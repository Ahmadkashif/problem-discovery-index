## Why These Niches

The category's own headline metric is completion, and a meaningful share of envelopes never reach it — for ordinary reasons that the platform observes and does not act on. That funnel is the largest contested surface and it is unworked: every vendor reports that an envelope is outstanding and none says why or does anything about it. Workforce documents are the other volume pool, and their contest is different in kind — not completion but provable currency, since an offer letter is signed once and a policy acknowledgement has to be true of the whole workforce against the current version.

Regulated signing **failed the filter as one niche** and is decomposed below. Mortgage and title closing contests on assembling and executing a hundred-document package across lender, title, escrow and notary, with recording at the end. Clinical consent contests on capturing consent at the point of care, binding it to the right patient and writing it back into the record. They share the word "regulated" and share no buyer, no incumbent and no definition of success.

Notarization and witnessing is the genuinely underdigitised remainder — legal in most states only recently, varying state by state, and the reason certain documents still cannot be finished electronically.

The two underserved constituencies are the counterparty and the administrator. The signer is the single point of failure for the industry's headline metric, has no account, no context and no way to ask a question, and is not anybody's user. The contract administrator is downstream of the whole category, typing effective dates and notice periods off documents the platform just produced and holds in full.

The automation niches are the category's two standing failures: clause libraries that accumulate contradictory and retired terms exactly as support macro libraries do, and identity assurance set once at the account level when it is a per-transaction risk decision.

## Filter Notes

Seven of the eight level-1 niches are terminal. **Regulated Industry Signing** is not: "regulated" names which customers buy it rather than what the competition is about, and the contest inside it splits cleanly. A mortgage closing is a package assembly and coordination problem — a hundred documents, four parties, a notary, wet-sign carve-outs that survive, and a county recorder at the end; the winner is whoever can get a closing to fund without a room. Clinical consent is a point-of-care capture problem — the patient is present, the identity binding is to a medical record rather than to an email address, the form has to reflect the procedure actually being performed, and the executed consent has to land in the chart. Different buyers, different incumbents, different failure modes. Decomposed into two contested sub-niches.

Two candidates were considered and rejected. *Sales agreement execution* was dropped because its contest belongs to contract lifecycle management rather than to signature, and is covered in that industry. *SMB and solo signing* was dropped because the contest there is price and simplicity rather than a capability anyone is competing to build — a distribution question, not a product one.

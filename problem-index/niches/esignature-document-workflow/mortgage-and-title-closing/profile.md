# Mortgage & Title Closing

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/esignature-document-workflow|E-Signature & Document Workflow]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in closing technology is fighting to get a loan to fund and an instrument to record without the parties being in a room — and whoever can do that across the widest set of counties, investors and lender overlays takes the volume.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$740M US closing technology, excluding title production systems
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~11% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Low-Medium — fully digital closings remain a minority of transactions
**Target Buyer:** Lenders, title agents, escrow companies and settlement service providers
**Automation Potential:** High for package assembly and coordination; gated by county recording and investor acceptance

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A residential closing is a hundred-odd documents executed by four to six parties — borrower, lender, title agent, escrow, notary, sometimes a seller and their agent — inside a compressed window with a disclosure regime that imposes its own timing, ending with an instrument that must be accepted by a specific county recorder in a specific format. The contest is not signature. It is whether the whole choreography can complete without a table: whether the county accepts electronic recording, whether the investor who will buy the loan accepts an electronic note, whether the lender's own overlay permits it, whether a notary can appear remotely under that state's law on that date, and whether the package was assembled correctly in the first place. A single failure at any of those points sends the entire closing back to paper, which is why the digital share has grown far more slowly than the underlying legality would suggest.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Digital closing platforms, e-vaults for electronic notes, remote online notarisation providers, title production systems and lender origination systems, with point-to-point integrations between them. The gaps: acceptance data — which counties, which investors, which lenders permit what — is tracked privately and incompletely by every participant; package assembly and completeness checking is manual and is where most delays originate; the borrower's experience is assembled from three or four separate portals; and nothing predicts which closings are at risk of falling back to paper, though the predictors are all known in advance.

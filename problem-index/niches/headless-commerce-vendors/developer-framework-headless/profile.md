# Developer-Framework Headless

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/headless-commerce-vendors|Headless Commerce Vendors]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this sub-niche is fighting to get a developer from nothing to a working storefront faster than they could write it themselves, and to still be worth using in month six — and whoever does that takes the adoption, because the decision is made before anybody is paid.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$320M US, much captured through hosting and adjacent services
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~8% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** High
**Target Buyer:** Developers choosing a stack, before any commercial conversation
**Automation Potential:** High

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A developer framework is adopted, not sold. An engineer evaluating options reads the documentation, follows a quickstart, and decides within an afternoon whether this is better than writing the integration themselves — and that decision, made without a salesperson, determines everything downstream. What wins is documentation quality, ergonomics, a working storefront quickly, and the framework still being worth having when the requirements get specific. What loses is the same pattern as every framework: an abstraction that makes the first hour fast and the third month impossible, at which point the developer writes it themselves and tells their colleagues to skip it.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Storefront frameworks, hosting, starter templates, component libraries and documentation. The gaps: the escape hatch to the underlying commerce interface is incomplete; performance characteristics are demonstrated on a starter and diverge at real catalogue sizes; upgrade paths break storefronts; the local development experience diverges from production; and the framework's own overhead is undocumented.

# Checkout Compliance

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/headless-commerce-vendors|Headless Commerce Vendors]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to deliver a checkout that is correct in every jurisdiction the retailer sells into, from components that each solve one part — and whoever does that takes the risk off the retailer, because the assembly is where all of it lands.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$420M US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~10% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Low — mature parts, unassembled
**Target Buyer:** Whoever built the checkout, and the legal function above them
**Automation Potential:** High — verification is checkable, the rules change

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Tax engines, payment authentication and accessibility tooling are all mature bought components, and assembling them into a checkout that is correct in every jurisdiction the retailer sells into is left to whoever built the checkout. That assembly carries obligations that change constantly and independently: tax rules and thresholds, strong customer authentication and its exemptions, accessibility standards with enforcement behind them, consumer protection requirements about price display, cancellation rights and pre-contractual information, and data protection consent. Each component vendor covers their part and disclaims the composition. A developer who built a checkout flow is carrying a regulatory obligation across a dozen regimes, which nobody described to them.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Tax calculation services, payment providers handling authentication, accessibility testing tools, and a legal review at launch. The gaps: no continuous verification that the assembled checkout satisfies the obligations; regulatory change is not tracked against the checkout that implements it; accessibility is tested once and regresses with every release; jurisdiction-specific requirements are discovered rather than enumerated; and no component vendor accepts responsibility for the assembly.

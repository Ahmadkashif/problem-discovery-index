# The Solutions Architect

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/headless-commerce-vendors|Headless Commerce Vendors]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to let an architect find out whether a composition works before the programme commits to it — and whoever does that takes the delivery quality, because the failure modes currently appear in production eighteen months later.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$340M US in architecture and rework
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~8% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** None — designing for failure modes that appear in production
**Target Buyer:** Vendor and integrator delivery organisations
**Automation Potential:** Medium — the evidence is buildable, the judgement is theirs

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Solutions architects live inside multi-year replatforming programmes, designing compositions whose failure modes only appear in production, held responsible for outcomes that depend on a dozen parties. They make binding decisions — which services, which boundaries, where state lives, how consistency is handled — at the moment they know least, against vendor documentation that describes intended behaviour rather than observed behaviour, with no way to test the composition before committing to it. When it goes wrong eighteen months later the decision is theirs and the evidence that would have informed it existed at other implementations they had no access to.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Vendor documentation, reference architectures, their own experience, and conversations with peers. The gaps: no way to test a composition before committing; no record of how similar compositions performed elsewhere; vendor behaviour under load and at scale is undocumented; the decisions are not recorded with their reasoning, so nothing accumulates; and the feedback from production never reaches the person who designed it.

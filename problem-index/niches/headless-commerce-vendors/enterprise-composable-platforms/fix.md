# The Partner Who Determines the Outcome

**Niche:** [[niches/headless-commerce-vendors/enterprise-composable-platforms/profile|Enterprise Composable Platforms]]
**Industry:** [[industries/headless-commerce-vendors|Headless Commerce Vendors]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The quality of a headless deployment depends more on the integrator than on the platform, everybody in the category knows it, and integrator quality is represented by a certification badge.
**Tags:** #evaluation-metrics #compliance #descriptive-statistics #confidence-intervals #hypothesis-testing #revenue-impact #quick-win #workflow-orchestration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this sub-niche is fighting to be the commerce core a large retailer can build twelve business units and fifteen markets on for a decade — and whoever does that takes the licence, because the buyer is choosing what they will be unable to leave.

## The Problem
Two retailers implement the same platform with the same scope. One goes live in nine months with a maintainable composition; the other takes two years, ends with fifteen interceptor services, and cannot upgrade. The difference is the integrator. The retailer chose theirs from a partner directory where every firm carries the same certification badge, awarded for training courses completed rather than for outcomes delivered. The vendor has visibility into every implementation on their platform and publishes a directory sorted alphabetically.

## Why It's Still Broken
Partner programmes are commercial relationships that generate pipeline, and ranking partners by quality means telling most of them they are worse. Certification measures training because training is easy to verify. Outcome data is visible to the vendor and publishing it would strain the relationships the vendor depends on for distribution. And the retailer bearing the consequence has no way to see what the vendor sees.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Measure partners on outcomes and let buyers see it. Score partners on the implementations they delivered — time to go live against scope, extension footprint, upgrade currency of their deployments, incident rate, and whether the retailer is still on the platform — which the vendor can compute from their own telemetry and account records and is the fix. Publish the score, or at minimum band it, since the retailer is making a decade-long decision partly on the partner and currently has a badge. Report to partners with specifics, since most will improve and none currently receive anything actionable. Tie referral and certification tiers to measured outcomes rather than to training completion. Publish a reference implementation and measure deviation from it, which gives partners a standard and gives retailers a comparison. Track which partners' deployments upgrade and which stall, since upgrade currency is the clearest long-run quality signal and is entirely visible to the vendor. Give the retailer a health assessment of their own deployment against the benchmark, which is useful mid-programme rather than only in hindsight. And accept the partner attrition, because a platform whose implementations succeed attracts better partners than one whose directory is complete.

## Who Feels the Pain
Retailers making a decade-long commitment partly on a badge; good integrators indistinguishable from poor ones; and vendors whose platform is blamed for outcomes their partners produced.

## Impact If Fixed
The vendor can compute partner quality from telemetry they already hold and publishes an alphabetical directory. Upgrade currency across a partner's deployments is the clearest long-run quality signal and is entirely visible to the vendor and to nobody else.

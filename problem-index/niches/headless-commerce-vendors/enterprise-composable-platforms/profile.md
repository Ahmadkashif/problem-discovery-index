# Enterprise Composable Platforms

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/headless-commerce-vendors|Headless Commerce Vendors]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this sub-niche is fighting to be the commerce core a large retailer can build twelve business units and fifteen markets on for a decade — and whoever does that takes the licence, because the buyer is choosing what they will be unable to leave.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$780M US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~19% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** High
**Target Buyer:** Architecture committees at large retailers, via integrators
**Automation Potential:** Medium — the product is flexibility, not automation

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
An enterprise composable platform is bought by a committee making a decade-long decision for an organisation with many brands, markets, currencies, tax regimes, business models and legacy systems. The evaluation is about whether the data model can express what this organisation actually does, whether it scales at their peak, whether the partner ecosystem can staff a programme, and what happens when the requirements change in three years. The competitor is another enterprise platform or the legacy monolith they are replacing, the sale runs for a year through an integrator, and the buyer knows they are choosing something they will not realistically be able to leave — which makes flexibility and longevity the whole contest.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Extensible data models, multi-tenant and multi-market capability, partner certification programmes, and reference architectures. The gaps: the data model's limits are discovered during implementation rather than during evaluation; migration from a monolith has no mapped path; the partner ecosystem's quality varies enormously and is not measured; and the retailer's ability to exit is never discussed despite being the decision's largest risk.

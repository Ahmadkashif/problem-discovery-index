**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Emergency services and large-scale operations built incident command for exactly the case where several organisations must respond together, and a six-vendor commerce outage is a conference call.

**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to let the person holding the pager see across the vendors whose systems they are responsible for — and whoever does that shortens the incidents, because the engineer currently owns an outcome and can see one component of it.

## The Problem
Coordinating a response across organisations that do not report to each other is a solved problem with a formal structure. Incident command systems define roles, establish a single commander, structure communication, and are used wherever several agencies must act together. Large technology operations adapted it into their own incident management practice. A commerce outage spanning six vendors is precisely a multi-organisation incident and is handled by an engineer opening tickets and dialling numbers.

## What Already Exists
Incident command structures with defined roles and a single commander; multi-agency coordination protocols; major incident management practice with declared severities and mobilisation; mutual aid agreements establishing obligations before an event; and post-incident review across participating organisations.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to parties bound by commercial contracts rather than by public duty. It requires: (1) the commander's authority established contractually in advance, since the retailer's engineer has no authority over a vendor's responders and mutual aid agreements are the closest analogue — writing that into the contract before peak is the specific adaptation; (2) a pre-established joint channel with named responders per vendor, since assembling one during an incident is where the first hour goes; (3) severity definitions agreed across parties, since a retailer's critical and a vendor's critical are different and the mismatch determines the response; (4) joint post-incident review, which crosses a commercial boundary and is where the systemic causes live; and (5) rehearsal before peak, which every serious incident practice insists on and which this category does not do across vendors.

## Target Customer
Retail engineering and procurement, vendor support organisations, and the incident management community for whom multi-vendor commercial incidents are a growing case.

## Impact If Solved
Multi-organisation incident response is a formal discipline and this is handled by opening tickets. Establishing the commander's authority and a named joint channel contractually before peak is the adaptation, and it removes the hour that currently goes into assembling the response.

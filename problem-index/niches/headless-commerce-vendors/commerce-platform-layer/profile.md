**Parent Industry:** [[industries/headless-commerce-vendors|Headless Commerce Vendors]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Not terminal — the contest differs by whether the buyer is an architecture committee or a developer, and the decomposition is recorded below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.1B US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~27% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** High
**Target Buyer:** Large retailers via integrators; separately, developers
**Automation Potential:** High

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
This is what the category licenses: the commerce engine — carts, orders, catalogue, pricing, promotions — exposed through an interface rather than through a storefront. It is the largest revenue line and the surface the vendors compete on.

It is **not terminal**, because two unrelated businesses sell it. An enterprise composable platform is a multi-year licence bought by an architecture committee at a large retailer, delivered through a systems integrator, and won on data model flexibility, scale characteristics, extensibility and the depth of the partner ecosystem — a long, consultative sale against another enterprise platform or a legacy monolith. A developer framework is adopted by an engineer, frequently free at the point of adoption, and won on documentation, ergonomics, time to a working storefront and how well the abstraction survives production — a decision made before anybody talks to a salesperson, against the option of writing the integration directly. The buyer, the motion, the evidence and the economics have nothing in common, and vendors strong at one are structurally weak at the other. Filter Notes in the overview records the two rejected alternatives; the sub-niches below are the split.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Commerce APIs, extensibility mechanisms, storefront frameworks, hosting and developer tooling. The gaps are specific to each side and are stated in the sub-niches.

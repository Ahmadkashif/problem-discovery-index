# Niche Analysis — Performance Marketing Agencies

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/performance-marketing-agencies|Performance Marketing Agencies]]

## Niche Selection

These agencies are skilled operators on other people's automation. The buying craft that justified the fee has been absorbed into the platforms' automated campaign types, the measurement the allocation decision rests on comes from the platforms grading their own work, and the fee is a percentage of the spend the agency recommends. Meanwhile they sit on the one vantage point nobody else has — the same measurement question asked across hundreds of advertisers with the clients' actual revenue visible — and discard it at the end of each engagement. The eight niches below split the category by what competitors actually fight over.

## Why These Niches

Measurement and allocation take the largest share because allocation is the agency's core deliverable and it is made against numbers that double-count, use incompatible windows and cannot be added together. Creative is second because paid social now consumes concepts faster than any team can produce them and is one of the few remaining differentiators the platforms have not absorbed. The two low-digitized niches are the business model itself: a fee that pays more for recommending more spend and nothing for finding a channel is not incremental, and a new business process that consumes an expensive share of the firm's capacity. The two underserved audiences are the account director whose week is six decks and six calls, and the media buyer left feeding assets into a box they cannot see inside. The two automatable niches are the discarded portfolio corpus and the weekly ritual of writing what happened.

## Filter Notes

Seven of the eight level-1 niches are terminal. Creative Operations is not: the label names a function rather than a contest, and writing the contested statement produces two sentences describing different winners. One fight is throughput — producing, versioning, localising and trafficking enough assets to feed platforms that exhaust a concept in days, which is a production and pipeline contest won on cost per asset and cycle time. The other is selection — knowing which concepts are worth producing before spending on them, which is a prediction contest won on a corpus of creative and its measured performance. A competitor can lead decisively on either while being ordinary at the other, and agencies are visibly split between production shops and strategy shops on exactly this line. It therefore decomposes into **Creative Production Throughput** and **Concept Selection & Prediction**.

Two candidates were considered and rejected. **Platform buying tooling** — the automated campaign products themselves — is a real contest but belongs to [[industries/programmatic-ad-platforms|Programmatic Ad Platforms]] and the platforms that build it. **Affiliate and influencer channel management** is work these agencies do, but the contests within it belong to [[industries/affiliate-networks|Affiliate Networks]] and [[industries/influencer-marketing-platforms|Influencer Marketing Platforms]], where they are already analysed.

# The Experiment Nobody Kept

**Niche:** [[niches/performance-marketing-agencies/portfolio-measurement-priors/profile|Portfolio Measurement Priors]]
**Industry:** [[industries/performance-marketing-agencies|Performance Marketing Agencies]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The geo holdout cost sixty thousand pounds of foregone spend, produced a clear finding, was presented once, and exists now only in a deck on someone's laptop.
**Tags:** #compliance #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #workflow-orchestration #quick-win #hypothesis-testing #confidence-intervals #data-integration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn a portfolio of hundreds of advertisers into calibrated priors on how much each platform overstates — and whoever does that is selling the only thing in this market that cannot be copied from a platform's documentation.

## The Problem
An agency persuades a client to run a proper geographic holdout. It costs real money in suppressed spend, takes six weeks, and produces a clean result. The finding is presented, acted on, and the analysis lives in a spreadsheet and a deck belonging to the analyst who ran it. Eighteen months later that analyst has left, the client has changed agency, and a colleague running the same test for a similar client in the same vertical starts from nothing. This is the most expensive data the agency ever generates and it has no home.

## Why It's Still Broken
There is no repository because nobody's job includes maintaining one, and experiments are treated as client deliverables rather than as firm assets — a classification error with a large cumulative cost. The analysis is bespoke, so its outputs are not in a standard shape. Confidentiality is invoked loosely as a reason not to retain anything. And the loss is only felt by whoever needed the finding and could not get it.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Keep the results. Record every experiment in a structured registry — hypothesis, design, platform, vertical, dates, effect size, uncertainty, and what was decided — which is the fix, takes twenty minutes per experiment, and is the difference between a firm that accumulates knowledge and one that does not. Register before running, which improves the designs, prevents post hoc rationalisation and makes the record honest. Standardise effect reporting so results are comparable, since a registry of incomparable findings is a filing cabinet. Store abstracted results rather than client data, which resolves the confidentiality question properly rather than using it as a reason to keep nothing. Make it searchable by vertical, platform and cycle, because the value is entirely in retrieval at the moment of a new engagement. Include the failures and the inconclusive tests, which are as informative as the clean results and are the first things discarded. Attach the designs so future tests are not re-derived. Review the registry quarterly to extract the emerging priors, which is how a pile of records becomes an asset. Make registration a condition of the experiment being run, since anything optional will not happen. And measure how often the registry is used in new engagements, because a repository nobody consults is a cost rather than an asset.

## Who Feels the Pain
Analysts re-running experiments the firm already paid for; clients funding tests whose answers the agency already had; and agencies whose most expensive knowledge leaves with individuals.

## Impact If Fixed
The firm's most expensive data is classified as a client deliverable rather than a firm asset, so it has no home. A twenty-minute structured record per experiment, registered before running, is the difference between a firm that accumulates knowledge and one that starts over.

**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get paid for the finding that a channel is not incremental — and whoever builds a commercial model that rewards that is selling something no percentage-of-spend competitor can match.

## The Problem
Aligning a supplier's incentives with a buyer's outcome is a worked commercial problem across several industries. Gainsharing in consulting, target-cost contracts with pain and gain shares in construction, outcome-based reimbursement in healthcare, and shared savings arrangements in energy all exist because paying for effort produces more effort. The mechanisms, the baseline-setting practices and the failure modes are documented. Media agency contracts remain a percentage of the spend the agency recommends.

## What Already Exists
Gainsharing and shared savings contract structures; target-cost and pain-gain mechanisms; outcome-based reimbursement models; baseline setting and independent verification practice; and risk-sharing arrangements with caps and collars.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to an outcome that is contested and partly unobservable. It requires: (1) a baseline that both parties accept in a domain where measurement itself is disputed, which is the central difficulty and the reason every previous attempt collapsed — construction can measure a cost and media cannot agree on a conversion; (2) an outcome influenced heavily by factors outside the agency's control, including the client's own product and pricing, requiring careful attribution of what the agency is accountable for; (3) short contract cycles against effects that unfold over longer periods, which the healthcare and energy models handle with multi-year terms unavailable here; (4) a measurement function that must be independent of both parties to be credible, which nobody currently provides; and (5) agency cost structures dominated by salaries, which limits how much variance a firm can absorb without a floor.

## Target Customer
Agency and client leadership, procurement functions, and the measurement providers who could serve as the independent party these contracts need.

## Impact If Solved
Every other industry that pays for outcomes solved baseline-setting first, and media cannot agree on the measurement. An independent measurement party is the missing role that makes the whole structure possible and that nobody currently plays.

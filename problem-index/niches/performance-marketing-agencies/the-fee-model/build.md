# Paid More for Recommending More Spend

**Niche:** [[niches/performance-marketing-agencies/the-fee-model/profile|The Fee Model]]
**Industry:** [[industries/performance-marketing-agencies|Performance Marketing Agencies]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Most agencies charge a share of media spend, which pays them more for recommending more spend and pays them nothing for the finding that a channel is not incremental.
**Tags:** #compliance #evaluation-metrics #causal-inference #revenue-impact #confidence-intervals #hypothesis-testing #descriptive-statistics #workflow-orchestration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get paid for the finding that a channel is not incremental — and whoever builds a commercial model that rewards that is selling something no percentage-of-spend competitor can match.

## The Problem
An agency runs an incrementality test and finds that a channel taking a fifth of the budget produces almost nothing. Telling the client costs the agency income immediately and permanently. Not telling them is a breach of the relationship's spirit and everyone's stated values. The contract has created a situation where the single most valuable thing the agency could discover is the thing it is paid to avoid discovering. Clients understand this, which is why retention has shortened and why every pitch now includes a question about incentive alignment that nobody answers convincingly.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Percentage of spend is simple, universally understood and easy to sell, and the alternatives require a shared measurement both parties trust — which the measurement niche shows does not currently exist. Outcome-based fees founder on attribution disputes. Agencies fear the variance. And a firm that prices differently must explain why, which is a harder sale than matching the market rate.

## What to Build
Build a commercial model on a measurement both sides accept. Establish a shared incrementality measurement first, since no outcome-based arrangement survives without an agreed number and every previous attempt has failed on this point — the measurement is the prerequisite and not a detail. Price on incremental outcome rather than on spend, which aligns the agency to the client's actual objective and makes the non-incremental finding a paid deliverable. Pay explicitly for efficiency findings, so a recommendation that reduces spend while holding outcomes is rewarded rather than penalised — this single clause is what the whole niche turns on. Separate the fee for management from the fee for measurement, since measurement paid as a percentage of what it measures is inherently compromised. Use a base and variable structure so the agency's fixed costs are covered and the variance is bearable. Define the baseline rigorously with independent verification, because the disputes in outcome contracts are almost always about the baseline. Handle the long tail of effects, since a change made now may pay off after the contract period and a naive structure punishes the right decision. Publish the model, because transparency about incentives is itself a competitive position in a market where clients have learned to distrust the standard one. Provide contract templates and measurement standards, since each firm negotiating alone is why the market has not moved. And report the agency's own recommendations that reduced spend, which is the credential nobody currently has.

## Target Customer
Agency leadership seeking differentiation, client procurement and marketing leadership, and the industry bodies that could standardise a model.

## Impact If Built
The contract makes the most valuable discovery the one the agency is paid to avoid, and clients have noticed. Paying explicitly for efficiency findings is the clause the whole thing turns on, and an agreed incrementality measurement is the prerequisite every previous attempt failed on.

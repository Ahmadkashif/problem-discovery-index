**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Manufacturing has a century of flow, bottleneck and batch-size theory, and creative production is a queue nobody has measured.

**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to produce assets at the rate the platforms consume them, at a cost that survives the fee — and whoever wins on cost per asset and cycle time takes the volume work the whole category depends on.

## The Problem
Getting work through a process at rate is one of the oldest solved problems in industry. Flow analysis, bottleneck identification, batch size reduction, work-in-progress limits, queueing theory and cycle time measurement are all mature, taught everywhere, and have been applied successfully to software development, healthcare and services as well as to factories. Creative production for performance marketing is an unmeasured queue with unlimited work in progress and a review step nobody has timed.

## What Already Exists
Theory of constraints and bottleneck analysis; queueing theory and batch size optimisation; work-in-progress limits and flow metrics; value stream mapping; and cycle time measurement and reduction practice.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a process whose most expensive step is a subjective judgement by a client. It requires: (1) the bottleneck being approval by an external party who does not work for the agency and cannot be scheduled — which is where flow theory's assumption of a controllable resource breaks and is the central adaptation; (2) work items of highly variable size, from a resize to an original concept, so the flow model must handle a mixed workload rather than a standard unit; (3) quality that is judged rather than specified, meaning rework rates depend on taste and cannot be engineered out entirely; (4) demand set by measured creative fatigue rather than by an order book, which makes production planning a control problem; and (5) an industry that regards its work as craft and will resist manufacturing language, so the practice must be introduced through outcomes rather than through vocabulary.

## Target Customer
Performance agencies and production studios, in-house creative operations, and operations consultancies for whom creative throughput is an unserved application.

## Impact If Solved
Flow theory assumes a controllable bottleneck resource and here it is a client's subjective approval. Handling a mixed workload and demand driven by measured fatigue are what make a century-old practice fit a continuous creative feed.

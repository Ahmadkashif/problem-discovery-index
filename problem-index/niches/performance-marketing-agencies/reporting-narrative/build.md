# The Pipeline Ends Where the Work Starts

**Niche:** [[niches/performance-marketing-agencies/reporting-narrative/profile|Reporting Narrative]]
**Industry:** [[industries/performance-marketing-agencies|Performance Marketing Agencies]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Pipeline vendors solved getting the data into a dashboard, and left untouched the part that consumes the week — agreeing what the numbers mean and writing what happened.

**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to write what happened rather than to move data into a chart — and whoever automates the part that consumes the week takes the value the pipeline vendors left on the table.

## The Problem
The data pipeline runs perfectly. Every metric from every platform lands in a warehouse and renders in a dashboard on schedule. Then a person opens the dashboard, looks at fourteen charts, decides which three matter, works out why they moved, writes four paragraphs, adds a recommendations slide, and repeats for five more clients. The engineering problem was solved and sold; the human problem it was supposed to relieve was untouched. Agencies bought reporting automation and their reporting takes the same amount of time it always did.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Writing what data means was not tractable software until recently, so vendors correctly scoped to the pipe and the scope became the category definition. Interpretation requires the causal work the category has not built, so a narrative generator without it produces descriptions rather than explanations. Agencies bill the hours. And narrative quality is hard to demonstrate in a sales process, unlike a connector count.

## What to Build
Generate the interpretation. Detect what is worth commenting on rather than narrating everything — a change outside the metric's normal variation, a threshold crossed, a trend broken — which is the first step and immediately removes most of the writing. Explain rather than describe, drawing on the diagnostic decomposition so the narrative says why and not merely what, which is the difference between a useful report and a caption. Draft the recommendation with its evidence, leaving acceptance to the director, which keeps judgement human while removing composition. Maintain continuity with previous reports, since a client notices when last week's explanation contradicts this week's and consistency is a genuine quality problem in manual reporting. Match the client's own vocabulary and reporting conventions, because a generic narrative reads as generic. Assemble the deck or the document in the format the client expects, since the last mile is where the time actually goes. Flag where the data cannot support an explanation, which is honest and is what a careful director does. Learn the account's normal patterns so seasonality is not reported as a finding. Keep the narrative record searchable, which becomes the institutional memory of what was said and recommended. And measure time to produce a report, since that is the metric the whole build exists to move and most agencies have never measured it.

## Target Customer
Agency operations and account teams, in-house marketing teams producing internal reporting, and the pipeline vendors whose products stop at the dashboard.

## Impact If Built
The engineering problem was solved and sold while the human problem it was meant to relieve went untouched, so reporting takes as long as it ever did. Detecting what is worth commenting on and explaining rather than describing is what the pipeline vendors declared out of scope.

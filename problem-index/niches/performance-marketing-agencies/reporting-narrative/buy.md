## The Problem
Turning structured data into readable prose is an established application. Financial results summaries, sports match reports, weather narratives and business intelligence commentary have been generated automatically for years, first with templates and now with language models, and the outputs are routinely indistinguishable from human writing for these purposes. Marketing performance reporting is the same shape of task — take a structured result, identify what is notable, explain it in prose — and is performed manually across the whole industry every week.

## What Already Exists
Natural language generation from structured data; notable-event detection in time series; templated and model-based narrative systems; business intelligence commentary features; and tone and audience adaptation.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is from describing what happened to explaining why and recommending what next. It requires: (1) causal explanation rather than description, since a sports report needs only to say what occurred and a marketing report must say why — this dependency on a diagnostic layer is what separates a useful product from a caption generator and is why the obvious version disappoints; (2) recommendations with consequences, which no narrative generation application has had to produce and which raises the bar on correctness sharply; (3) continuity across weekly reports, so the narrative is a running account rather than an isolated summary; (4) per-client vocabulary, conventions and sensitivities, which requires configuration a general system does not expose; and (5) an accountable human in the loop, since the agency's name is on the interpretation and an unreviewed generated explanation is a commercial risk.

## Target Customer
Agency operations and account teams, in-house reporting functions, and business intelligence and pipeline vendors for whom interpretation is unclaimed.

## Impact If Solved
Narrative generation is proven for describing what happened, and a marketing report must explain why. The dependency on a diagnostic layer is what separates the useful product from the caption generator that disappoints.

# Unified Measurement Practice

**Industry:** [[industries/performance-marketing-agencies|Performance Marketing Agencies]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Large advertisers built unified measurement combining mix models, experiments and attribution, and the agencies serving everyone else add up what the platforms say.
**Tags:** #causal-inference #bayesian-inference #time-series-forecasting #hypothesis-testing #confidence-intervals #evaluation-metrics #monte-carlo-methods #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to produce one allocation decision from platform numbers that double-count and cannot be added — and whoever does that credibly is selling the only judgement a platform cannot supply about itself.

## The Problem
Unified marketing measurement is an established practice at the largest advertisers: a marketing mix model provides the top-down view, incrementality experiments calibrate it, attribution provides tactical granularity, and the three are reconciled into one allocation. It requires a measurement team and takes months to stand up. Everyone below that scale — which is most of the market and almost all of an agency's client base — uses platform-reported numbers added together, because the unified approach has never been packaged for them.

## What Already Exists
Marketing mix modelling platforms; incrementality experimentation frameworks; multi-touch attribution; unified measurement reconciliation methodologies; and budget optimisation against modelled response curves.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is from an enterprise measurement function to an agency serving many mid-market clients. It requires: (1) working with a fraction of the data history, since a mid-market client may have two years of inconsistent data where mix modelling assumes a long clean series — pooling across the agency's portfolio is the only way to make this tractable and is the central adaptation; (2) cost and time reduced from a months-long project to something deliverable inside a retainer; (3) experiments run on small budgets, which limits power and demands careful design rather than the generous holdouts a large advertiser can afford; (4) the measurer being paid a share of the spend being measured, which is a conflict enterprise measurement teams do not have and which must be structurally addressed for the output to be credible; and (5) delivery to a marketing manager rather than to a measurement scientist.

## Target Customer
Performance agencies, mid-market advertisers, and measurement vendors for whom the sub-enterprise segment is unserved.

## Impact If Solved
Unified measurement exists and is packaged for advertisers with measurement teams, leaving everyone else adding platform numbers. Pooling across an agency's portfolio substitutes for the long clean data history mix modelling assumes.

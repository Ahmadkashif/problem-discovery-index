# More Conversions Than Orders

**Niche:** [[niches/performance-marketing-agencies/cross-platform-measurement/profile|Cross-Platform Measurement & Allocation]]
**Industry:** [[industries/performance-marketing-agencies|Performance Marketing Agencies]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The weekly report says the platforms produced eleven hundred conversions, the client took eight hundred orders, and the meeting proceeds as though both numbers are fine.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #hypothesis-testing #confidence-intervals #quick-win #revenue-impact #compliance #causal-inference
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to produce one allocation decision from platform numbers that double-count and cannot be added — and whoever does that credibly is selling the only judgement a platform cannot supply about itself.

## The Problem
The report shows platform-reported conversions by channel, totalled. The client's own system recorded substantially fewer orders. Nobody reconciles the two, because doing so raises a question about the whole report, and the report is what the meeting runs on. Instead everyone applies a private mental discount and carries on. The practice is universal and openly acknowledged in the industry, and it means every allocation decision is made on a figure that the person presenting it does not believe. It persists because correcting it is uncomfortable for the agency and confusing for the client.

## Why It's Still Broken
The platform numbers are what the client sees in their own accounts, so a reconciled figure looks like the agency marking down its own results — the optics of honesty are bad and nobody wants to go first. Deduplication needs the client's order data joined to platform data, which is a project nobody has scoped. The discrepancy benefits both the platforms and the agency's reported performance. And no client has made reconciliation a contractual requirement.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Reconcile to the client's own orders. Put actual orders next to the platform total in every report, which is the fix, requires one number the client already has, and makes the gap impossible to ignore — most of the industry's tolerance for this rests on the two figures never appearing on the same page. Report a deduplicated figure as the headline and the platform figures as diagnostics, which is the correct hierarchy and reframes the conversation permanently. Explain the gap's composition — double counting, view-through, window differences, modelled conversions — since a named gap is manageable and an unnamed one is alarming. Agree definitions with the client up front, so the reconciliation is a shared method rather than an agency adjustment. Show the gap's trend, because a widening gap is a signal about platform behaviour and is currently invisible. Apply the same method across clients, which is what makes the agency's numbers comparable and is the basis of the portfolio work. Prepare the client's leadership for smaller headline numbers, since the change is presentational as much as analytical and is lost without that conversation. Use the reconciled figure for allocation even while reporting both, because the decision matters more than the presentation. Make it standard in the agency's own reporting templates so it is not a per-client negotiation. And publish the practice, since an agency that reconciles openly acquires a credibility position competitors who do not cannot match.

## Who Feels the Pain
Clients allocating budget on totals that exceed their own sales; agencies presenting numbers they privately discount; and the category, whose core deliverable rests on an invalid sum.

## Impact If Fixed
The industry's tolerance rests on the platform total and the client's order count never appearing on the same page. Putting them side by side uses a number the client already has and makes every downstream reconciliation unavoidable.

# The Media Buyer After Automation

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/performance-marketing-agencies|Performance Marketing Agencies]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to give the buyer visibility and leverage inside a black box they are still accountable for — and whoever does that restores a profession the platforms hollowed out while keeping its responsibilities.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$2B US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~8% of category fees
**Digital Adoption:** Low — assets in, results out
**Target Buyer:** Agency channel leadership
**Automation Potential:** High — inference and control can both be rebuilt

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Automated campaign types removed most of the controls a media buyer's expertise operated, kept all of the accountability, and left a skilled professional feeding assets into a box and explaining its output. The platforms took the levers and the visibility together, which is an unusual combination: the buyer cannot act and cannot see, and is still the person in the meeting when performance declines. This is a distinct niche because the contest is over reconstructing visibility and leverage from the outside, which is a technical problem quite separate from measurement or creative.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Automated campaign types with a handful of settings, platform-reported breakdowns of limited granularity, asset feeds, and budget controls. The gaps: no visibility of where budget went; no diagnosis when performance drops; controls that are indirect and undocumented; no way to test what the box responds to; and accountability with no corresponding lever.

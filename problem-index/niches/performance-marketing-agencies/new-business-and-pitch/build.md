# Giving the Analysis Away to Win the Work

**Niche:** [[niches/performance-marketing-agencies/new-business-and-pitch/profile|New Business & Pitch Economics]]
**Industry:** [[industries/performance-marketing-agencies|Performance Marketing Agencies]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Winning an account requires producing the audit and the strategy for free, for a prospect who may take both and stay where they are, and retention cycles have shortened so it happens constantly.
**Tags:** #automation #workflow-orchestration #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #revenue-impact #gradient-boosting #quick-win #data-integration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to win work without giving away the analysis for free — and whoever changes that economics releases the capacity the whole category burns on unpaid pitching.

## The Problem
A prospect invites four agencies to pitch. Each spends two senior people for two weeks producing an account audit, a measurement critique, a creative direction and a ninety-day plan. One wins. The other three have given a competitor's client a free consultancy engagement, and the winner has spent a meaningful share of the account's first-year margin before starting. With retention cycles of eighteen months, every agency runs this constantly, and the capacity consumed is the same senior capacity that would otherwise be doing the analytical work that differentiates the firm.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Free pitching is a market norm that no single agency can refuse without losing opportunities, which is a collective action problem rather than an individual choice. The audit work is genuinely bespoke as currently practised, so its cost is accepted as unavoidable. Nobody measures the total cost of pitching against the revenue it produces. And prospects have no reason to change a process that gets them free analysis.

## What to Build
Make the audit cheap and the differentiation defensible. Automate the account audit — structure, waste, measurement configuration, creative volume, competitive position — which is largely mechanical, is redone by hand every pitch, and once automated turns two weeks into an afternoon. Draw on portfolio benchmarks so the audit says something the prospect cannot get elsewhere, which is the piece competitors cannot copy and is what a firm with a portfolio corpus can uniquely offer. Qualify opportunities analytically before committing, since a large share of pitches are unwinnable and the cost is spent anyway on an intuition. Demonstrate capability with prior outcomes rather than with free work, which requires the track record the measurement niches produce and is the durable escape from the norm. Give the diagnosis and hold the prescription, since the audit wins the meeting and the plan is what gets taken. Measure cost per pitch and win rate by opportunity type, which most firms do not know and which would change what they chase. Package a paid diagnostic as a first engagement, which is the practical route out of free pitching and converts a cost into a small revenue line and a qualification step. Reuse the analytical assets across pitches so the marginal cost falls. Publish benchmarks openly, since credible published analysis attracts opportunities that do not require a pitch at all. And track the pitch pipeline's true economics, because the capacity spent here is the same capacity the firm needs for the work that differentiates it.

## Target Customer
Agency leadership and new business teams, procurement functions running pitch processes, and the industry bodies that could change the norm.

## Impact If Built
The winner spends a meaningful share of first-year margin before starting and the losers fund a competitor's client. Automating the audit turns two weeks into an afternoon, and portfolio benchmarks make the cheap version say something the prospect cannot get anywhere else.

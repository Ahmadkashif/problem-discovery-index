# The Audit That Took Two Weeks and Went Nowhere

**Niche:** [[niches/performance-marketing-agencies/new-business-and-pitch/profile|New Business & Pitch Economics]]
**Industry:** [[industries/performance-marketing-agencies|Performance Marketing Agencies]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The same account audit is rebuilt from scratch for every prospect, by hand, by the firm's most expensive people, and thrown away when the pitch is lost.
**Tags:** #automation #workflow-orchestration #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #quick-win #data-integration #revenue-impact #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to win work without giving away the analysis for free — and whoever changes that economics releases the capacity the whole category burns on unpaid pitching.

## The Problem
Every pitch audit does the same things: examine account structure, find obvious waste, check measurement configuration, assess creative volume and refresh rate, compare against category benchmarks, and identify the three most impactful changes. The specifics differ by account; the method is identical every time. A senior strategist does it manually, in a spreadsheet and a deck, over two weeks. When the pitch is lost the work is deleted. The firm has done this three hundred times and has neither a tool nor a template that makes the three hundred and first faster.

## Why It's Still Broken
Pitch work is treated as bespoke because the output is bespoke, which obscures that the method is not — the conflation of a custom output with a custom process is the whole mechanism. The work is done under deadline, which is exactly when nobody stops to systematise. Losing pitches are not reviewed, so the repetition is never noticed as repetition. And senior people doing it manually is read as commitment rather than as waste.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Systematise the method. Write down the audit as a defined procedure with a fixed set of checks, which is the fix and is a one-day exercise that most firms have never done despite performing the audit hundreds of times. Automate every check that reads from an account or from public data, which is most of them and turns days into minutes. Maintain benchmark tables from the firm's own portfolio so comparisons are instant and are better than any competitor's. Template the output so the deck assembles itself from the findings. Keep every audit, won or lost, since the accumulated set is a benchmark asset and is currently deleted. Review lost pitches for what the audit missed, which is the only learning available and is skipped because losing is unpleasant. Separate the mechanical findings from the strategic judgement, so senior time goes only to the latter — which is the actual differentiator and is currently crowded out by the spreadsheet work. Qualify before auditing, using a short automated version to decide whether to invest the rest. Time-box the manual portion explicitly, because it expands to fill whatever is available. And measure hours per pitch, since the figure is usually much larger than leadership believes and is what justifies fixing any of this.

## Who Feels the Pain
Senior strategists spending weeks on repeated mechanical analysis; firms burning delivery capacity on unpaid work; and clients receiving audits whose quality depends on how tired the team was.

## Impact If Fixed
A custom output was conflated with a custom process, and the method has been identical across three hundred audits. Writing it down and automating the mechanical checks turns two weeks into an afternoon and reserves senior time for the judgement that actually differentiates.

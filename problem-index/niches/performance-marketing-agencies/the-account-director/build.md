# Six Decks on Thursday

**Niche:** [[niches/performance-marketing-agencies/the-account-director/profile|The Account Director]]
**Industry:** [[industries/performance-marketing-agencies|Performance Marketing Agencies]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Thursday is for building six weekly decks, Friday is for the calls about them, and the thinking that would actually help the client happens in gaps that keep not appearing.
**Tags:** #worker-facing #large-language-models #automation #workflow-orchestration #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #revenue-impact #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to take the deck-building off the account director so the thinking happens — and whoever does that changes what an agency is actually able to deliver.

## The Problem
The director manages six accounts. Each expects a weekly report. The data arrives automatically into a dashboard — the pipeline vendors solved that — and then a person exports it into a deck, writes a paragraph explaining each chart, adds a recommendations slide, and does it again five times. Thursday is gone. Friday is the six calls. The strategic work each account actually needs — the measurement question, the channel that is not incremental, the creative direction — requires uninterrupted analysis and is scheduled into a gap that never opens. The agency's fee is justified by that thinking and the week is structured to prevent it.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Reporting automation stopped at the dashboard, because getting data into a chart is a tractable engineering problem and writing what it means was assumed to need a person — that assumption is now wrong and nobody has revisited it. The deck is a client expectation with its own momentum. Agencies bill time, so hours spent are not obviously a loss. And the strategic work is not scheduled by anyone, so it loses every contest for the same hours.

## What to Build
Automate the reporting ritual end to end. Generate the deck from the data, including the narrative — what changed, why it plausibly changed, what it means and what is recommended — which is now achievable and is the fix for most of the week; the pipeline vendors solved the pipe and stopped at the part that consumes the time. Detect what deserves comment rather than narrating every chart, since most weeks most metrics did nothing and the ritual of explaining them is the bulk of the writing. Draw the explanation from the causal work so the narrative is a diagnosis rather than a description. Rank the six accounts by what needs attention this week, which is the triage that currently does not happen and which determines where the director's remaining hours go. Prepare the call rather than the deck, since the deck is a means and the conversation is the deliverable. Draft recommendations with evidence for the director to accept or reject, which keeps the judgement human while removing the composition. Protect analysis time structurally in the week, because it will otherwise be consumed regardless of how much reporting is automated. Record what was recommended and what happened, which is the agency's institutional memory and is currently in decks nobody rereads. Report the ratio of reporting hours to analysis hours, which is the state of the role and is not measured anywhere. And measure the outcome of the recommendations, since that is the thing a client is actually buying.

## Target Customer
Agency operations and account leadership, the directors themselves, and the reporting vendors whose products stop at the dashboard.

## Impact If Built
Pipeline vendors solved getting data into a chart and stopped at writing what it means, on an assumption that is no longer true. Generating the narrative and triaging which accounts need attention returns the week to the thinking the fee is justified by.

# The Account Nobody Looked At Until It Churned

**Niche:** [[niches/performance-marketing-agencies/the-account-director/profile|The Account Director]]
**Industry:** [[industries/performance-marketing-agencies|Performance Marketing Agencies]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The quiet account performed steadily worse for four months without complaining, and the first signal anyone acted on was the notice email.
**Tags:** #survival-analysis #change-point-detection #gradient-boosting #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #quick-win #revenue-impact #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to take the deck-building off the account director so the thinking happens — and whoever does that changes what an agency is actually able to deliver.

## The Problem
Six accounts, and attention goes to the two that complain and the one that is growing. The fourth has been drifting: performance down gently, the client engaging less on calls, a marketing director replaced two months ago, questions about the fee raised once and not pursued. Every one of those is a signal. None triggered anything, because the director's week is full and the account was not making noise. The notice period email arrives and the post-mortem concludes the client went in-house. The decline was visible in the agency's own data for four months.

## Why It's Still Broken
Attention is allocated by inbox, which means the accounts that get looked at are the ones already in trouble loudly and the quiet decline is invisible — a triage mechanism that systematically selects the wrong accounts. Churn signals span performance data, engagement behaviour and client-side changes that nobody joins. The director has no capacity to review accounts that are not demanding attention. And churn is attributed after the fact to reasons that absolve everyone.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Watch the quiet accounts. Score every account weekly on performance trend, engagement and relationship signals, which is the fix and replaces inbox triage with something that can see a silent decline. Detect the performance drift against the account's own baseline rather than against a target, since gentle erosion below a target nobody is missing is the specific invisible pattern. Track client-side engagement — call attendance, response times, who is on the call — which is a strong and freely observable churn predictor that nobody records. Flag client-side personnel changes, because a new marketing director is the single highest-risk event for an agency relationship and is usually known and not acted on. Alert early enough to act, since a four-month decline is recoverable at week three and not at month four. Prompt a specific intervention rather than a general concern, because a director given a score does nothing and a director given this account's return has fallen for six weeks makes a call. Review the quiet accounts on a schedule, which is the simple structural fix underneath all of this. Record churn reasons honestly rather than accepting the client's polite one, since the post-mortems are currently useless. Measure churn against warning signals to calibrate what actually predicts it. And report portfolio health to leadership weekly, because revenue that disappears silently is the category's largest avoidable loss.

## Who Feels the Pain
Directors blindsided by churn they could have prevented; clients who drifted away while their concerns went unnoticed; and agencies losing accounts whose decline was visible in their own dashboards.

## Impact If Fixed
Inbox triage systematically selects the loud accounts and misses the silent decline, which is where churn comes from. Weekly scoring against each account's own baseline, plus flagging client-side personnel changes, turns an unrecoverable month four into an actionable week three.

# Lifeline Wireless Subscribers

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/telecom-resellers|Telecom Resellers]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche (Lifeline modules in MVNO BSS platforms, enrolment and verification tools, and compliance outsourcers) is fighting to keep each claimed subscriber both eligible and actively using service through National Verifier checks, annual recertification and non-usage rules, and whoever keeps claims clean without dropping eligible subscribers takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1B a year in Lifeline support, most of it claimed by wireless resellers (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~2% of a ~$60B resale economy (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low-Medium — enrolment runs through the National Verifier, but outreach to subscribers is by text to phones that change hands, run out of minutes or go unused
**Target Buyer:** Lifeline Compliance Director or Head of Subscriber Operations at a wireless eligible telecommunications carrier
**Automation Potential:** Medium — eligibility and duplicate checks automate; reaching people with unstable contact doesn't

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Lifeline had ~8.12 million subscribers in mid-2025. It pays up to $9.25 a month for qualifying broadband service, $5.25 for voice-only, and up to $25 more on Tribal lands, and most subscribers get it as a free or cheap mobile plan from a reseller acting as an eligible telecommunications carrier. The subscribers are the people the rest of the industry's tools ignore: no credit history, shared or second-hand phones, frequent number and address changes, limited English, and disability. The provider is paid per eligible, active subscriber, and every rule (one benefit per household, annual recertification, de-enrolment after a period of non-use) depends on reaching a person who is hard to reach. The wired side of affordability, post-ACP broadband households, is [[niches/wired-broadband-providers/low-income-households/profile|Low-Income & Post-ACP Households]].

## Current Tools & Gaps
The National Verifier and NLAD, MVNO BSS platforms with Lifeline modules such as Telgoo5, and outbound texts and calls. The gaps: no prediction of who will miss recertification; notices sent to numbers subscribers don't check; usage rules applied mechanically; and enrolment agents paid per sign-up, which invites the ineligible and duplicate claims the program audits for.

**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche (STIR/SHAKEN signing vendors, call-analytics firms, customer-vetting tools and compliance consultants) is fighting to give a provider records that show the FCC and downstream carriers no illegal traffic originates on or passes through its network, and whoever catches the bad customer before the first traceback takes the account.

## The Problem
A voice provider must vet customers before granting service, monitor their traffic for illegal patterns, act on what it finds, and document all of it for the regulator. That is the anti-money-laundering pattern: customer due diligence, transaction monitoring, case management and regulatory reporting.

## What Already Exists
AML and financial-crime platforms used by banks and payment companies. They cover identity and business verification at onboarding, risk rating, rules and models over transactions, alert triage and case management, link analysis across accounts, and audit trails built for examiners.

## The Customization Gap
Telecom objects replace financial ones. The customer is an account with requested capacity and caller-ID permissions, and its ownership evidence for those caller IDs must be verified. Transactions are calls, monitored on short-duration share, answer rates, caller-ID churn, dialling patterns and destinations. The upstream carrier plays the correspondent bank's role. Cases must produce traceback responses and the evidence the RMD mitigation plan describes, and the time limits are telecom's: hours for traceback, minutes for live traffic.

## Target Customer
Compliance officers at wholesale and CPaaS providers large enough to run a compliance function, often with staff who came from banking.

## Impact If Solved
Providers adopt a proven control framework rather than inventing one, and examiners and downstream carriers see a recognisable, evidenced process.

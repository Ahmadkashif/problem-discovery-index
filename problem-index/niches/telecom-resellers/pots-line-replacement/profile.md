# Legacy POTS Line Replacement

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/telecom-resellers|Telecom Resellers]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to inventory every copper line at a multi-site customer and replace each with a service that passes the attached device's own test (fire-alarm signalling, the elevator emergency phone, the fax), and whoever certifies each line working takes the portfolio.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$2B a year in POTS-replacement service, devices and site work sold by resellers to businesses (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~3% of a ~$60B resale economy (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — line inventories come from old bills, site surveys are clipboards and photos, and nobody knows what half the lines connect to
**Target Buyer:** VP of Operations at a POTS-replacement reseller; on the customer side, a facilities or telecom manager at a multi-site business
**Automation Potential:** Medium — inventory and test verification automate well; the site visit doesn't

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Retailers, banks, property managers, schools and hospitals carry thousands of copper lines that aren't for talking: fire-alarm communicators, elevator emergency phones, gate and door entry, fax machines, point-of-sale and ATM dial-up, building automation. As telcos retire copper and raise its price, resellers sell replacement services (devices with LTE and wired paths and battery backup, such as Ooma AirDial) on fixed monthly fees. The work fails at the device, not the dial tone. An elevator service firm chose AirDial because its customers were failing annual elevator inspections when the copper lines behind in-car phones went dead. The boundary with [[niches/wired-broadband-providers/copper-retirement-migration/profile|Copper Retirement & Customer Migration]] is the record each side works from. The telco runs a wire-center campaign over its own line records. The reseller here inventories and certifies replacement lines across one enterprise's sites, whichever telcos' wire centers they sit in.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Replacement devices with management portals, carrier bills and CSRs, site survey forms and field technicians. The gaps: no reliable map of line to device to purpose; local fire-code and elevator-inspection acceptance that varies by jurisdiction; and no recorded proof that each replaced line passed its device's test after cut-over.

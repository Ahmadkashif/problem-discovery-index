# SMB Hosted Voice & UCaaS

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/telecom-resellers|Telecom Resellers]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to move a small business's numbers, call flows and handsets onto its platform with no missed calls on cut-over day, and whoever makes the switch invisible wins the seats.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$12B a year in US hosted PBX, UCaaS and SIP-trunk revenue from businesses under ~250 seats (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~20% of a ~$60B resale economy (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** High for the product, Low for migration (spreadsheets, PDFs of the old phone system's settings, implementation calls)
**Target Buyer:** VP of Onboarding or Implementation at a hosted-voice or UCaaS provider
**Automation Potential:** High for discovery, configuration and porting; medium for on-site handset swaps

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The FCC counted 63 million interconnected VoIP subscriptions at June 2025, and two-thirds of wireline connections are business lines. Feature lists across providers are near-identical, and RingCentral's $2.5B of 2025 revenue sits alongside thousands of small resellers on white-label platforms. A small business switches rarely, and when it does the decisive moment is cut-over: the main number must ring the right phones, the auto-attendant must say the right thing, the after-hours route must reach the on-call person, and every phone must have a dispatchable 911 location under RAY BAUM's Act. A bad cut-over is remembered for years. A smooth one wins referrals.

## Current Tools & Gaps
White-label platforms (Cisco BroadWorks, NetSapiens), provider admin portals, implementation project trackers and porting desks. The gaps: the old system's call flows are rediscovered by interview; number inventories are incomplete; 911 addresses are entered by hand per user; and weak default security (handset passwords, open international calling) makes new accounts targets for toll fraud.

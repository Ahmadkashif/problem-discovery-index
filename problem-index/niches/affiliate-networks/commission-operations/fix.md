# The Rate Agreed in an Email Thread

**Niche:** [[niches/affiliate-networks/commission-operations/profile|Commission Operations]]
**Industry:** [[industries/affiliate-networks|Affiliate Networks]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A partner's commission rate was raised for a seasonal push two years ago in an email nobody kept, and it has been running ever since.
**Tags:** #workflow-orchestration #compliance #evaluation-metrics #automation #quick-win #descriptive-statistics #revenue-impact #data-integration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to run validation, clawback and rate changes as a system rather than as a person with a spreadsheet — and whoever does that removes the administrative weight that caps how many partners a programme can have.

## The Problem
A partner asked for a higher rate to support a launch. The manager agreed, for that quarter, by email, and set it in the console. The manager left. The rate is still elevated. Nobody knows why, nobody can find the agreement, and nobody wants to cut it without knowing what was promised. Across a programme with three hundred partners this happens repeatedly, and the result is a rate card that is a sediment of forgotten negotiations, several of which are costing serious money for reasons nobody can reconstruct. The record exists only in departed people's inboxes.

## Why It's Still Broken
Rates are a field in a console rather than an agreement with terms, which means there is nowhere for the reason, the duration or the approver to live — the data model has no slot for the thing that matters. Negotiation happens in email because that is where commercial conversations happen and nothing pulls it into a system. Nobody audits the rate card, because it produces no error. And the cost is a margin leak with no owner.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Make the rate an agreement rather than a setting. Record every rate with its effective dates, its rationale, its approver and its link to the agreement, which is the fix, is a handful of fields, and makes the rate card auditable for the first time. Default temporary rates to expire, since almost every elevated rate was intended to be temporary and permanence is an accident of the interface. Audit the current rate card against partner performance, which reliably surfaces several partners earning elevated rates for reasons that no longer apply and typically pays for the whole exercise immediately. Require approval above a threshold, with the record attached, so the commercial decision is visible. Capture the negotiation itself, even as a note, because the reasoning is what future managers need and is what is always lost. Alert before a temporary rate expires, so the decision to continue is deliberate. Compare rates across partners of similar type and performance, which exposes inconsistency that is both costly and a genuine partner grievance. Model the margin impact of a rate change before agreeing it, which managers currently estimate mentally. Hand over the record when a manager changes, since that transition is when most of this knowledge is lost. And report the weighted average commission rate over time, because a rate card that drifts upward by accident is a margin problem that nothing currently reports.

## Who Feels the Pain
Merchants paying elevated rates for expired reasons; managers inheriting a rate card they cannot explain; and partners treated inconsistently for no reason either side can articulate.

## Impact If Fixed
A rate is a console field with no slot for the reason, the duration or the approver, so the record lives in a departed person's inbox. Expiring temporary rates by default and auditing the card against performance typically pays for the work immediately.

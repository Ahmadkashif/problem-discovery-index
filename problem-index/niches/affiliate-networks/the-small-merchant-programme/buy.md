# Self-Service Onboarding Practice

**Niche:** [[niches/affiliate-networks/the-small-merchant-programme/profile|The Small Merchant Programme]]
**Industry:** [[industries/affiliate-networks|Affiliate Networks]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Payment processors and commerce platforms took businesses from signup to live in minutes, and affiliate networks still run an onboarding process with a salesperson in it.
**Tags:** #automation #workflow-orchestration #compliance #evaluation-metrics #data-integration #revenue-impact #descriptive-statistics #confidence-intervals
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to run a working affiliate programme for a merchant with nobody to run it — and whoever does that opens a merchant base the networks priced themselves out of.

## The Problem
Payment processing was once a sales-led, underwriting-heavy, weeks-long onboarding process available to established businesses. It became a signup form and an integration snippet, and the market expanded enormously. The same transformation happened in commerce platforms, business banking and email marketing. Affiliate networks, whose product is arguably simpler to onboard, still run a sales conversation, a contract, a setup fee and a minimum commitment.

## What Already Exists
Self-service signup with automated underwriting; instant integration through platform plugins and snippets; automated risk and compliance screening; usage-based pricing with no minimums; and in-product onboarding that reaches first value without human contact.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a two-sided product where a merchant alone is not a working programme. It requires: (1) supply as part of onboarding, since a merchant with a live programme and no partners has nothing — this is the structural difference from payments, where the merchant is immediately functional, and it is why self-service alone has not worked here; (2) fraud exposure that is shared across the network rather than borne by the merchant, which changes underwriting from a per-merchant assessment to a portfolio one; (3) tracking integration verified continuously rather than at setup, because a broken pixel makes the programme silently useless in a way a broken payment integration never is; (4) commission terms that must be set sensibly without the merchant understanding the market, which requires a defaulting mechanism the category has never needed; and (5) an economics with no minimum, which requires support cost to be genuinely near zero.

## Target Customer
Affiliate networks and platforms, commerce platform vendors bundling the channel, and small merchants currently excluded.

## Impact If Solved
Payments made the same transition and the market expanded; affiliate kept the salesperson. The structural difference is that a merchant with no partners has nothing, so supply must be part of onboarding rather than a later step.

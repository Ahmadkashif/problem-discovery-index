# Pay Only on a Sale, If You Can Get In

**Niche:** [[niches/affiliate-networks/the-small-merchant-programme/profile|The Small Merchant Programme]]
**Industry:** [[industries/affiliate-networks|Affiliate Networks]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Affiliate is the one channel a small merchant can afford because it only charges on a sale, and running it requires a person they do not have.
**Tags:** #automation #workflow-orchestration #evaluation-metrics #revenue-impact #data-integration #gradient-boosting #confidence-intervals #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to run a working affiliate programme for a merchant with nobody to run it — and whoever does that opens a merchant base the networks priced themselves out of.

## The Problem
A merchant doing a few million a year in sales has no advertising budget to risk and would happily pay fifteen percent on sales that would not otherwise happen. That is precisely what affiliate offers. To get it they must pay a network setup fee and a monthly minimum, integrate tracking, write a programme description, recruit partners from a directory, negotiate rates, validate transactions monthly, watch for fraud, and handle payouts. There is nobody to do any of that. So they do not have a programme, the channel best suited to their economics is unavailable to them, and the networks' addressable market is a fraction of what it could be.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Network economics were built around service, and service does not scale down — the minimums exist to cover the support the model assumes, which makes the exclusion structural rather than incidental. Recruitment and validation were assumed to require judgement. Fraud policing needs scale to be economic. And the large merchants produce the revenue, so the tail has never been anyone's priority.

## What to Build
Make the programme run itself. Set it up from the merchant's catalogue and category, choosing an initial rate structure from what comparable merchants pay, which removes the first barrier and is a lookup rather than a decision the merchant should make. Recruit partners automatically by matching the catalogue to publishers who already cover the category, which connects to the discovery work and is the part the merchant is least able to do. Validate transactions against the merchant's own order system rather than asking anyone to approve anything, which is the same integration the operations niche needs and is what removes the monthly workload entirely. Police fraud centrally across all small programmes, since the patterns are shared and the scale that makes detection economic exists at the network rather than the merchant. Handle payouts, tax and reconciliation without the merchant touching it. Remove minimums and setup fees, which are the formal exclusion and are only defensible while support is manual. Install tracking through the merchant's existing commerce platform with continuous verification, which is the fix note's subject and is where most small programmes silently die. Report in sales terms rather than affiliate terms, since the merchant has no vocabulary for the channel and will judge it on revenue. Set expectations honestly at the start, because an unmanaged programme that generates nothing in month one loses the merchant permanently. And measure activated and retained programmes rather than merchant count, since the failure mode is programmes that exist and do nothing.

## Target Customer
Small and mid-size merchants without affiliate staff, commerce platform vendors whose merchants want the channel, and networks seeking a larger addressable base.

## Impact If Built
The minimums exist to cover service the model assumes, which makes the exclusion structural, and the channel best suited to a small merchant's economics is the one they cannot access. Validating against their own order system removes the monthly workload that the minimums were pricing for.

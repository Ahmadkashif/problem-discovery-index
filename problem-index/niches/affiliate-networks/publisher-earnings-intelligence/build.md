# Which Article Made the Money

**Niche:** [[niches/affiliate-networks/publisher-earnings-intelligence/profile|Publisher Earnings Intelligence]]
**Industry:** [[industries/affiliate-networks|Affiliate Networks]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** The network knows the click and the sale, the creator knows the content and the traffic, and nobody joins the two, so the creator decides what to make next by feel.
**Tags:** #data-integration #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #revenue-impact #worker-facing #gradient-boosting #confidence-intervals #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to tell a creator which piece of their work earned the money — and whoever attributes earnings back to content changes what a creator makes next.

## The Problem
The statement says link 8842f produced four hundred dollars. The creator has no idea which of their nine hundred pages that link is on, whether it was the in-text mention or the comparison table at the bottom, which audience segment converted, or whether the piece that earned most is the one they would have guessed. Every decision they make about what to write next, which products to cover, where to place recommendations and which merchants to work with is made without the one piece of information that would inform it — which is sitting, complete, split across two systems that do not talk.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The network sees the click and not the page it came from, and the creator's analytics stop at the outbound click, so neither party has both halves and neither has a reason to go and get the other — this structural split is the entire cause. Sub-identifier tagging exists as a workaround and requires discipline creators do not have. Networks are not measured on publisher outcomes. And no product owns the boundary.

## What to Build
Join the two halves. Map every link to the content and position it occupies, which is the foundation and can be done by crawling the creator's own site rather than by asking them to tag anything — this is what makes it work for the creators who never configured sub-identifiers. Attribute earnings to content, section and placement, so the reporting answers the question the creator actually has. Combine with the creator's own traffic data to give earnings per reader, which is the only comparable measure across content of different reach and is the number that changes decisions. Report by audience segment and source, since a piece earning well from search and poorly from social implies something specific about what to make. Analyse placement position within a page, which is directly testable and is usually the largest single controllable factor. Track the back catalogue's earnings over time, because most creator income comes from older content and the decay pattern determines whether to refresh or replace. Identify the content earning nothing despite traffic, which is the largest recoverable opportunity in most catalogues. Recommend what to make next from the creator's own performance and the network's merchant data, which is the product's eventual form. Work across networks, since a creator's catalogue mixes them and a single-network view misattributes by construction. And measure earnings per hour of work, because that is the creator's real economics and nothing reports it.

## Target Customer
Content creators and publishers, creator analytics vendors, and the networks whose publisher tooling ends at a link identifier.

## Impact If Built
Neither party has both halves, so the join is nobody's job and the creator decides by feel. Crawling the creator's own site to map links to content works without any prior tagging, and earnings per reader is the comparable measure that changes what gets made.

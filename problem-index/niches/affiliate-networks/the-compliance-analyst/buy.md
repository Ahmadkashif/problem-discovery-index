## The Problem
Payment fraud detection made this transition long ago. Rule engines gave way to behavioural models, device and graph signals, network analysis linking related accounts, real-time scoring with calibrated thresholds, and case management that presents assembled evidence to an analyst. The move happened because rules provably could not keep pace with adversaries. Affiliate compliance faces the same adversarial dynamic with the same economics and runs on rules and manual review.

## What Already Exists
Behavioural fraud models with continuous learning; device fingerprinting and graph-based account linking; real-time risk scoring with calibrated thresholds; case management with assembled evidence; and analyst feedback loops into model training.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is from a transaction to a partner relationship. It requires: (1) the unit of assessment being a partner over time rather than a transaction in a moment, which changes the model's shape entirely and means the relevant signal is a trajectory rather than an event; (2) cross-merchant behaviour as the primary signal, which is the network's structural advantage and has no payment fraud analogue where each merchant sees its own traffic; (3) violations that are contractual rather than criminal — trademark bidding and incentivised traffic are breaches of terms, not fraud, so the threshold and the remedy differ and the framing matters commercially; (4) consequences that suspend a legitimate business's income, requiring an explainability and appeal standard higher than a declined transaction; and (5) commercially sensitive subjects, since the largest partners are the network's own revenue and investigating them is a business decision as well as a compliance one.

## Target Customer
Network compliance and risk teams, merchant programme managers, and fraud detection vendors for whom partner-level contractual abuse is unserved.

## Impact If Solved
Payment fraud left rules behind because rules cannot outpace adversaries, and affiliate faces the same dynamic with the same economics. Assessing a partner's trajectory across merchants rather than a transaction in a moment is the model change, and the network is the only party with that view.

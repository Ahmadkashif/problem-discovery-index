# Five Schedules and a Spreadsheet

**Niche:** [[niches/affiliate-networks/cross-network-payments/profile|Cross-Network Payments & Operations]]
**Industry:** [[industries/affiliate-networks|Affiliate Networks]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A creator's income arrives from five networks on five schedules with five sets of terms, and reconciling it into a business is a monthly spreadsheet exercise nobody designed.
**Tags:** #data-integration #workflow-orchestration #automation #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #revenue-impact #worker-facing #time-series-forecasting
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn five sets of terms, schedules and statements into one predictable income — and whoever does that makes affiliate a business a creator can run rather than a set of payments that arrive.

## The Problem
One network approves transactions after thirty days and pays on the fifteenth with a fifty-dollar minimum. Another approves after ninety and pays monthly in arrears. A third holds until the merchant confirms. Clawbacks from returns appear as negative lines against future months with no explanation. Tax documents arrive in different formats at different times. A creator earning a real income across these has no consolidated ledger, no idea what they are owed, no forecast of when it will arrive, and a monthly reconciliation exercise that exists purely because five companies each designed their own settlement independently.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Each network's settlement process was built for its own operations and there has never been any pressure to converge, because the friction lands entirely on publishers who have no leverage. A consolidator must integrate with networks that have no obligation to provide access. Publishers are individually small, so the revenue per customer is low. And the problem is boring, which keeps it unaddressed despite being universal.

## What to Build
Build one ledger over all of them. Consolidate every network's transactions, approvals, adjustments and payments into a single account, which is the foundation and is the thing every creator assembles by hand today. Normalise the statuses, since pending, locked, approved and validated mean different things at each network and a creator cannot reason across them. Forecast earnings and payment dates from each network's observed behaviour rather than its stated terms, because the stated terms and the actual timing differ and the observed pattern is what a creator needs to plan against. Explain every clawback with its original transaction, which is the fix note's subject. Handle tax documentation and reporting across sources, which is genuinely onerous and is where an individual creator most needs help. Advance earnings against approved-but-unpaid balances, which is a real product for people whose income is locked in ninety-day cycles and which the consolidated ledger makes underwritable. Sweep balances trapped below minimum thresholds, which is money creators simply never receive. Reconcile automatically and flag only the discrepancies, since the monthly exercise is almost entirely mechanical. Report effective terms by merchant — real approval rate, real delay, real clawback rate — which should determine which programmes a creator works with and is invisible today. And measure days from sale to money received, because that single number is the creator's actual working capital position and nobody reports it.

## Target Customer
Creators and publishers earning across networks, creator finance and business tooling vendors, and the networks whose settlement friction costs them supply.

## Impact If Built
Five companies each designed settlement independently and the friction lands entirely on publishers with no leverage. A consolidated ledger with forecast timing from observed behaviour makes the income plannable and makes advancing against approved balances underwritable.

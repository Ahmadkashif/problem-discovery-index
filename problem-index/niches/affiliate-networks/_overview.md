# Niche Analysis — Affiliate Networks

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/affiliate-networks|Affiliate Networks]]

## Niche Selection

An affiliate network sees the entire click path across thousands of merchants and hundreds of thousands of publishers, joins it to confirmed transactions, and uses that dataset to execute one rule: whoever was last gets paid. Every contest in the category is downstream of that rule — enforcing it, disputing it, defrauding it, or working around the administrative weight it creates. The eight niches below split the category by what competitors actually fight over.

## Why These Niches

Attribution takes the largest share because the rule decides where twelve billion dollars of commission lands, and the network holding both the touch sequence and the outcome is the only party who could measure it properly. The publisher side is second because it is half the marketplace and has been treated as a supply pool rather than as a customer. The two low-digitized niches are the parts still done in email and spreadsheets: the monthly validation, clawback and rate work, and the merchant too small for any network to onboard properly. The two underserved audiences are the manager whose month is consumed by approvals and the compliance analyst policing tactics that evolve faster than the rulebook. The two automatable niches are where the network's data would directly answer the question and does not: which publishers could actually sell a given merchant's products, and whether the channel produced anything at all.

## Filter Notes

Seven of the eight level-1 niches are terminal. The Publisher Side is not: the label names who the audience is rather than what the contest is, and writing the contested statement produces two sentences describing different winners. One fight is over telling a creator which of their work actually earns — an attribution and analytics problem over content, sessions and conversions that the networks hold and never surface. The other is over consolidating five dashboards, five payment schedules and five sets of terms into one operational surface — a payments, reconciliation and workflow contest with a different buyer and a different build. A competitor can lead decisively on either while being ordinary at the other. It therefore decomposes into **Publisher Earnings Intelligence** and **Cross-Network Payments & Operations**.

Two candidates were considered and rejected. **Tracking and redirection infrastructure** is commodity plumbing, and the contest that exists over redirect latency and reliability belongs to [[industries/edge-cdn-providers|Edge & CDN Providers]]. **Creator representation and talent management** is a real contest but it belongs to [[industries/creator-talent-agencies|Creator Talent Agencies]] and [[industries/influencer-marketing-platforms|Influencer Marketing Platforms]], where it is already analysed.

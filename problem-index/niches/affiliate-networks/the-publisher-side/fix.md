# The Link That Broke in March

**Niche:** [[niches/affiliate-networks/the-publisher-side/profile|The Publisher Side]]
**Industry:** [[industries/affiliate-networks|Affiliate Networks]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A link in an article that still gets thousands of readers a month has pointed at a dead product since March, and nobody told the creator or the merchant.

## The Problem
A creator's best-performing article contains fourteen affiliate links. Over two years, products get discontinued, merchants leave the network, programmes change terms, and links quietly stop working or stop paying. The article still gets traffic. The readers still click. The revenue went to zero months ago and nobody noticed, because the creator has hundreds of articles and thousands of links and no system tells them when one dies. Both the merchant and the network can see the failed redirect and neither has any mechanism to mention it.

## Why It's Still Broken
Links are generated and then forgotten, because the tooling's job ends at generation — there is no concept of a link having a lifecycle. A dead link produces no revenue and therefore no line in a report, so it is invisible in exactly the place a creator would look. Networks have no view into where a link was placed. And the loss is silent and gradual rather than an event.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Treat the link as something with a lifecycle. Monitor every link for destination health, programme status and commission terms, which is the fix, is mechanical, and is the single highest-return maintenance task available to a creator with a back catalogue. Alert the creator when a link dies, with the content it sits in, since telling them the identifier alone is useless and the mapping is the part they cannot do. Suggest a replacement automatically — the same product at another merchant, or the successor product — which turns an alert into a repair. Detect terms changing rather than only breaking, as a commission rate cut to a fraction is a quieter and larger loss than a dead link. Flag high-traffic content with degraded links first, because the value is concentrated and a creator will not work through an undifferentiated list. Report the revenue at risk, which is what makes the maintenance worth doing and is currently invisible. Give merchants the same view, since they are losing sales from dead links in content that still ranks and they do not know it. Handle programme departures gracefully with notice and a migration path, rather than links simply ceasing. Support bulk repair, since a discontinued product may appear in two hundred pieces of content. And report catalogue health as a standing number, because a back catalogue is most creators' largest asset and it decays continuously with nobody watching.

## Who Feels the Pain
Creators losing income from content that still performs; merchants losing sales from links in ranking articles; and networks whose supply side quietly decays.

## Impact If Fixed
The tooling's job ends at link generation, so a link has no lifecycle and a dead one produces no line in any report. Monitoring health and terms, mapped back to the content a link sits in, turns the most valuable maintenance task a creator has from impossible into automatic.

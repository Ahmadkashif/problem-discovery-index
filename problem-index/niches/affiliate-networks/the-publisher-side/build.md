# Half the Marketplace Treated as Supply

**Niche:** [[niches/affiliate-networks/the-publisher-side/profile|The Publisher Side]]
**Industry:** [[industries/affiliate-networks|Affiliate Networks]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A creator earning across five networks has five dashboards, five payment schedules and no way to know which piece of their work produced the money, so they optimise by guess.
**Tags:** #worker-facing #data-integration #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #revenue-impact #workflow-orchestration #automation #quick-win
**Contested on:** This niche is not terminal — telling a creator which of their work earns and consolidating five networks into one operational surface are different contests with different winners, and they are stated separately in the sub-niches.

## The Problem
A creator publishes two hundred pieces of content a year and earns from five networks. Their monthly income arrives as five statements on five schedules, each listing link identifiers and amounts. To learn which article, video or newsletter produced the money they would have to map hundreds of link identifiers to content by hand, which nobody does. So they make more of whatever they believe worked, on no evidence, and the networks — who could tell them precisely — send a dashboard of link performance and a payment. The supply side of a marketplace worth twelve billion dollars operates on guesswork.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Networks compete for merchants, whose spend is the revenue, and publishers are recruited rather than served — the investment follows the competition. Publisher tooling has no obvious return for a single network because a creator's needs span networks by definition. Nobody owns the cross-network view, and each network has a reason not to build one. And publishers are individually small and have never had collective leverage.

## What to Build
Serve the supply side as a customer. Attribute earnings to content rather than to links, which is the first sub-niche's contest and is the single thing every creator asks for and no network provides. Consolidate the operational reality — dashboards, terms, schedules, minimums, tax handling and reconciliation — into one surface, which is the second sub-niche and is the difference between a business and a set of hobbies. Forecast earnings and payment timing, since creators manage cash flow on income they cannot predict and the networks have the data to project it. Detect broken and expired links, which is the fix note's subject and is a silent and continuous revenue loss. Report what a merchant's terms actually mean in practice — the real approval rate, the real payment delay, the real clawback rate — which is the information that should drive which programmes a creator joins and is entirely absent. Give creators negotiating evidence, since their performance data is their only leverage and they cannot currently assemble it. Surface the merchant programmes that fit a creator's audience, which connects to the discovery work from the other side. Handle the tax and compliance burden of multi-network income, which is genuinely onerous for an individual. Build for the creator as the buyer, because a product funded by networks will serve networks. And measure creator earnings growth, since that is the outcome and the thing no dashboard is judged on.

## Target Customer
Content creators and publishers earning across multiple networks, creator business tooling vendors, and the networks whose supply side is undifferentiated.

## Impact If Built
The supply side of a twelve-billion-dollar marketplace operates on guesswork because earnings are reported by link identifier and nobody maps them to content. Attributing to content and consolidating the operational surface are the two halves, and both are data the networks already hold.

# The Top Twenty Partners and Everyone Else

**Niche:** [[niches/affiliate-networks/the-affiliate-manager/profile|The Affiliate Manager]]
**Industry:** [[industries/affiliate-networks|Affiliate Networks]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Attention goes to the twenty partners already producing revenue, which is exactly the twenty who need it least, and the programme's growth comes from partners nobody is talking to.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #gradient-boosting #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #revenue-impact #quick-win #worker-facing #survival-analysis
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to give the affiliate manager back the partnership work by taking the queue off them — and whoever does that changes how many partners one person can genuinely develop.

## The Problem
The manager's attention follows the revenue report, which is sorted descending. The top twenty partners get calls, rate reviews, early access to promotions and creative support. They are already performing and would mostly continue without any of it. The partner who joined six months ago, produced a promising first quarter and then went quiet is on page eleven. So is the publisher whose audience is a perfect fit and who has never been given a reason to prioritise this merchant. Programme growth comes from exactly the partners the sorting hides, and the sorting is the only prioritisation anyone has.

## Why It's Still Broken
Revenue descending is the default sort in every console, and a default is a decision most people never revisit — this is the whole mechanism. Potential is harder to measure than current revenue and nobody has tried. Managers are reviewed on programme revenue, which the top twenty supply. And the partners who would grow are invisible precisely because they are not yet producing.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Sort by opportunity instead of by revenue. Score partners on potential rather than current output — audience fit, performance for comparable merchants, trajectory, and the gap between what they do here and what they do elsewhere — which is the fix and is computable from data the network holds about every publisher. Flag the partner whose performance has dropped against their own baseline, since that is a specific and recoverable conversation and is invisible in a descending revenue list. Identify the fast-growing partner early, because attention at that moment compounds and attention later does not. Detect the good-fit partner who has never activated, which is the largest untapped group in most programmes. Time the outreach to the moment it matters — a first sale, a first quiet month, a seasonal window — rather than to a quarterly review calendar. Serve the long tail with automated touchpoints so that not-talking-to becomes a lighter-touch programme rather than nothing. Measure the return on manager attention by partner segment, which nobody does and which would end the argument about where it should go. Protect the top partners with maintenance rather than development, since their needs are different and lower. Report programme growth by partner cohort, which shows plainly that growth comes from partners nobody was managing. And change the default sort, because the sort order is doing more to allocate a manager's time than any strategy document.

## Who Feels the Pain
Promising partners who were never contacted and drifted; managers whose time is allocated by a sort order; and merchants whose programmes plateau while the top twenty look healthy.

## Impact If Fixed
The default descending-revenue sort is the only prioritisation anyone has, and it hides exactly the partners growth comes from. Scoring potential from cross-merchant performance and flagging drops against a partner's own baseline are both computable from the network's existing data.

# Paying Whoever Was Closest to the Checkout Button

**Niche:** [[niches/affiliate-networks/commission-attribution/profile|Commission Attribution]]
**Industry:** [[industries/affiliate-networks|Affiliate Networks]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A commission rule written before smartphones pays whoever touched the shopper closest to checkout, which is reliably a coupon extension firing on a payment page the shopper had already reached.
**Tags:** #causal-inference #hypothesis-testing #confidence-intervals #evaluation-metrics #revenue-impact #bayesian-inference #graph-theory #gradient-boosting
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to pay partners for what they contributed rather than for who was last — and whoever measures that moves a substantial share of twelve billion dollars from extensions and trademark bidders to content and creators.

## The Problem
A shopper reads a review three weeks ago, thinks about it, searches the merchant's name today, arrives at the site, adds the item, and reaches the payment page. A browser extension activates looking for a code, drops a tracking parameter, and collects the commission. The publisher who introduced the product receives nothing. The merchant pays for a sale it had already made. This is not an edge case; it is the default path for a large share of the category's commission, it is well documented, and the rule producing it has not changed since it was written for a web that no longer exists.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The networks' largest publishers are frequently the extensions and coupon sites that benefit from last touch, which makes reform a direct attack on their own biggest partners — this is the whole explanation and it is commercial rather than technical. Content publishers are individually small and cannot force the change. Merchants accept network-reported figures because the alternative is running their own measurement. And a rule that is simple and enforceable has a real operational value that a measurement does not.

## What to Build
Replace the rule with a measurement. Model each partner's contribution from the full touch sequence the network already holds, which is the core and is uniquely possible here because the same party sees the path and the confirmed sale — the rest of advertising would need a consortium to assemble what one network has. Validate with experiments rather than with a model's assertion, suppressing partner types for shopper samples and observing what happens to conversion, which is the only credible evidence and is entirely runnable. Distinguish introduction from interception explicitly, since a partner who created demand and one who appeared after it are the two roles the whole argument turns on. Treat extension activation as what it is rather than as a click, which is the fix note's subject. Pay on contribution with a stated method, so partners can understand and plan against it, and publish the method because an unexplained reallocation will be fought rather than accepted. Let merchants set the policy, since it is their money and the network's conflict makes it inappropriate for the network to choose alone. Handle the transition, because an abrupt change removes income from partners who built businesses on the current rule and a phased path is the difference between reform and a revolt. Support role-based commission tiers, which most platforms cannot express at all. Measure the effect on total merchant sales rather than on commission distribution, since a reallocation that grows the channel is a different proposition from one that merely redistributes it. And report the gap between last-touch and contribution-based allocation, because that single figure is the category's real state.

## Target Customer
Merchants paying for intercepted sales, content publishers and creators receiving nothing for demand they created, and the networks willing to take a defensible position against their own largest partners.

## Impact If Built
One party holds the full path and the confirmed sale, which is a dataset the rest of advertising would need a consortium to assemble, and it runs a rule from 2004 over it. Suppression experiments per partner type produce credible evidence and are entirely runnable today.

# Commission Attribution

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/affiliate-networks|Affiliate Networks]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to pay partners for what they contributed rather than for who was last — and whoever measures that moves a substantial share of twelve billion dollars from extensions and trademark bidders to content and creators.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$3.4B US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~28% of category spend
**Digital Adoption:** Low — a rule from 2004, still running
**Target Buyer:** Network product leadership and merchant partnership heads
**Automation Potential:** Very High — the sequence and the outcome are held together

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A commission rule written before smartphones pays whoever touched the shopper closest to checkout, which is reliably a coupon extension firing on a payment page the shopper had already reached. This is the largest concentration of contested value in the category because the rule determines where every commission dollar lands. The network holds the entire touch sequence and the confirmed outcome in one place — a dataset the rest of advertising does not have — and executes a last-touch rule over it. The contest is whether anyone will replace the rule with a measurement.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Last-click cookie attribution, fixed cookie windows, occasional multi-touch reporting shown but not paid on, and manual partner-type commission tiers. The gaps: no contribution measurement; no distinction between introduction and interception; browser extension activation treated as a click; no experimentation to validate any attribution choice; and commission rules that cannot express partner role.

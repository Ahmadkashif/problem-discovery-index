# Attribution Modelling Practice

**Niche:** [[niches/affiliate-networks/commission-attribution/profile|Commission Attribution]]
**Industry:** [[industries/affiliate-networks|Affiliate Networks]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Multi-touch attribution and causal measurement are well-developed practices, and affiliate pays on the single rule every one of those practices exists to replace.
**Tags:** #causal-inference #bayesian-inference #hypothesis-testing #confidence-intervals #evaluation-metrics #gradient-boosting #descriptive-statistics #monte-carlo-methods
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to pay partners for what they contributed rather than for who was last — and whoever measures that moves a substantial share of twelve billion dollars from extensions and trademark bidders to content and creators.

## The Problem
Multi-touch attribution, marketing mix modelling and incrementality experimentation all exist because last-touch attribution is known to be wrong, and the digital marketing industry has spent twenty years and considerable money building alternatives. The methods are documented, the tooling is available, and most large advertisers use some version of them for their other channels. Affiliate, where the attribution decision is not a reporting choice but the mechanism by which money is actually paid, uses last touch exclusively.

## What Already Exists
Multi-touch attribution models from heuristic to data-driven; Shapley-value and Markov-chain contribution methods; incrementality experimentation frameworks; marketing mix modelling; and attribution reconciliation across methods.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is from a reporting allocation to a payment obligation. It requires: (1) the output being a payment to a third party rather than a credit in a report, which raises requirements for stability, explainability and disputability that no attribution product has ever had to meet — this is the central and underappreciated difference; (2) adversarial partners who will optimise against whatever is measured, unlike a company's internal channels, which means the method must be robust to gaming by design; (3) complete path data held by one party, which is a far better input than any attribution vendor works from and makes the modelling easier than the general case; (4) partner types with genuinely different roles, so the model must distinguish demand creation from interception rather than allocating credit uniformly along a path; and (5) contractual and transitional design, since changing the rule changes livelihoods and the commercial mechanics matter more than the statistics.

## Target Customer
Affiliate networks and platforms, merchant partnership teams, and attribution vendors for whom payment-grade attribution is an unserved problem.

## Impact If Solved
Twenty years of attribution work exists to replace the rule affiliate still pays on. Being a payment rather than a report raises the bar on stability, explainability and gaming resistance in ways no attribution product has faced.

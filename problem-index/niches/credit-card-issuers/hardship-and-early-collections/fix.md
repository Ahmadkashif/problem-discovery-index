# Re-Ages Granted Outside the Limits

**Niche:** [[niches/credit-card-issuers/hardship-and-early-collections/profile|Hardship & Early-Stage Collections]]
**Industry:** [[industries/credit-card-issuers|Credit Card Issuers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Bringing a delinquent account back to current after a run of payments is governed by interagency limits, and issuers find breaches in audits rather than at the moment of granting.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #compliance #workflow-orchestration #quick-win #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to match each stretched or delinquent cardholder to the treatment — payment plan, APR reduction, re-age or settlement — that returns the account to paying rather than postponing its charge-off, and whoever predicts cure best takes the account.

## The Problem
A re-age makes a delinquent account current after qualifying payments; supervisory policy limits how often an account may be re-aged and requires demonstrated willingness and ability to repay. Re-ages are granted by agents, by programme rules and by workout plans in different systems.

## Why It's Still Broken
No single view counts every re-age, extension and workout per account across systems and time.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A pre-grant check that counts all prior re-ages and workouts for the account and blocks or escalates breaches, plus a monthly report of re-aged accounts' subsequent performance.

## Who Feels the Pain
Collections leaders facing audit findings, and finance teams whose delinquency figures are flattered by re-ages.

## Impact If Fixed
Re-ages used as a cure, not as a way to move losses into a later quarter.

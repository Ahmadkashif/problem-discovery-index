# Hardship & Early-Stage Collections

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/credit-card-issuers|Credit Card Issuers]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to match each stretched or delinquent cardholder to the treatment — payment plan, APR reduction, re-age or settlement — that returns the account to paying rather than postponing its charge-off, and whoever predicts cure best takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$6B a year in US issuer in-house collections staff, dialers, collections platforms and hardship programme operations from first missed payment to charge-off (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~10% of issuer spend on data, decisioning and operations (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — the core of the work is a phone conversation
**Target Buyer:** Head of Collections & Recoveries, Collections Strategy Director
**Automation Potential:** Medium — contact and arrangement handling automate; hardship conversations do not fully

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Between the first missed payment and charge-off at about 180 days, a card account belongs to the issuer's own collections function, not to an agency. The treatments are issuer-specific — APR reductions, closed-card payment plans, re-ages within the interagency limits, settlements — and so are the constraints: the CARD Act, UDAAP, state creditor-collection rules and internal call caps of one to four a day (CFPB). Placement with agencies and debt sale after charge-off belong to [[industries/collections-agencies|Collections Agencies]]; the decision of how to treat a still-recoverable account belongs here, and minimum-only payers and persistent debt both rose in 2024 (CFPB).

## Current Tools & Gaps
Collections platforms (FICO Debt Manager, C&R Software, Experian), dialers and digital-collections tools, and hardship programme rules coded into the processor. Gaps: treatment assignment rarely tested against cure; hardship reasons sit in notes; and outreach starts at delinquency rather than when stress first shows.

# A Devaluation Tested Before It Is Announced

**Niche:** [[niches/credit-card-issuers/rewards-liability-and-redemption/profile|Rewards Liability & Redemption Cost]]
**Industry:** [[industries/credit-card-issuers|Credit Card Issuers]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Issuers change earn rates, redemption values and transfer ratios on a forecast of savings and discover the run on redemptions, the spend shift and the cancellations only afterwards.
**Tags:** #causal-inference #survival-analysis #bayesian-inference #time-series-forecasting #evaluation-metrics #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to know what each issued point will actually cost — by redemption channel, transfer partner and breakage — and to change earn and burn rules without triggering a redemption run, attrition or a regulator's attention, and whoever forecasts cost per point best takes the account.

## The Problem
A programme change is announced weeks ahead, as cardholder notice and good practice require. In that window, informed cardholders redeem or transfer at the old value; afterwards, some shift spend to another card or close the account. The saving booked in the business case is rarely the saving realised.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Programme changes are infrequent, each one is different, and the issuer's history of past changes and their aftermath has never been assembled into a dataset.

## What to Build
A simulator calibrated on the issuer's own past changes and on observable events elsewhere in the market: for a proposed change it estimates the pre-effective redemption surge by cohort, the post-change spend and attrition response, the net liability effect and the complaint risk, and recommends notice length, grandfathering and targeted retention offers.

## Target Customer
Heads of rewards and card CFOs planning programme changes.

## Impact If Built
Programme changes whose realised saving matches the plan, and fewer that end in cardholder backlash.

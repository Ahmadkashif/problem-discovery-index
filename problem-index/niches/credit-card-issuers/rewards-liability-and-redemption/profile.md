# Rewards Liability & Redemption Cost

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/credit-card-issuers|Credit Card Issuers]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to know what each issued point will actually cost — by redemption channel, transfer partner and breakage — and to change earn and burn rules without triggering a redemption run, attrition or a regulator's attention, and whoever forecasts cost per point best takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$5B a year in rewards platform, fulfilment, travel-portal and rewards-finance spend on proprietary currencies (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~8% of issuer spend on data, decisioning and operations (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — the ledger is digital; the liability estimate is a spreadsheet
**Target Buyer:** Head of Rewards / Rewards Finance Director
**Automation Potential:** High for forecasting and monitoring

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
On an issuer's own currency — Chase Ultimate Rewards, American Express Membership Rewards, Capital One miles and their peers — the issuer bears the full cost of every point. That cost depends on behaviour the issuer influences but does not control: whether a cardholder takes cash back, books through the portal or transfers to a partner, and when. This is a forecasting and programme-design contest, not a partner-pricing one, and the winner is whoever knows its cost per point well enough to change the programme without surprises. The CFPB's attention to rewards devaluation and forfeiture (an issue spotlight in 2024, recalled) adds a conduct dimension.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Loyalty engines (Kobie, Ascenda, in-house), travel-portal providers, transfer-partner integrations, and spreadsheet breakage models in rewards finance. Gaps: no cardholder-level cost forecast, no pre-announcement test of programme changes, and forfeiture rules applied by system defaults.

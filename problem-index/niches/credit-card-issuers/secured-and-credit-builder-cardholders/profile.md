# Secured & Credit-Builder Cardholders

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/credit-card-issuers|Credit Card Issuers]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to graduate secured and starter cardholders to unsecured lines and increases at the moment their behaviour earns it — not on a fixed six- or twelve-month review — so that they stay as they become prime, and whoever reads early payment and cash-flow behaviour best takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.5B a year in issuer spend on secured and starter card acquisition, servicing, deposit handling and graduation analytics (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~2% of issuer spend on data, decisioning and operations (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — applications are digital, graduation is a scheduled batch review
**Target Buyer:** Head of Starter and Secured Products, Credit Builder Product Manager
**Automation Potential:** High

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Students, recent immigrants and people rebuilding after a default usually start with a secured card — a line backed by their own deposit — or a low-line starter card. They are underserved twice over: acquisition models cannot score them, and once they are booked, graduation to an unsecured line follows a calendar rather than their behaviour. The cardholder who pays in full for five months waits for the twelve-month review and opens a card elsewhere; the one who graduates too early becomes a loss. Fintech credit-builder products, covered under [[industries/neobanks|Neobanks]], compete for the same customer with different mechanics.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Processor secured-card modules, bureau scores (often absent or unstable for this group), cash-flow data for some issuers, and periodic graduation reviews in the decision engine. Gaps: graduation timing by rule; deposit refunds slow; and score movement after graduation not used as feedback.

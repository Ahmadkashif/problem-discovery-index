# Score Simulators Fitted to the Issuer's Own Graduation Rules

**Niche:** [[niches/credit-card-issuers/secured-and-credit-builder-cardholders/profile|Secured & Credit-Builder Cardholders]]
**Industry:** [[industries/credit-card-issuers|Credit Card Issuers]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Credit-score simulators tell consumers what generally moves a score; a secured cardholder wants to know what will get this card graduated.
**Tags:** #gradient-boosting #large-language-models #worker-facing #evaluation-metrics #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to graduate secured and starter cardholders to unsecured lines and increases at the moment their behaviour earns it — not on a fixed six- or twelve-month review — so that they stay as they become prime, and whoever reads early payment and cash-flow behaviour best takes the account.

## The Problem
Cardholders building credit are motivated and anxious, and they have little visibility into what the issuer is looking for.

## What Already Exists
Score simulators and credit-education tools from the bureaus, Credit Karma and similar services, often embedded in issuer apps.

## The Customization Gap
The issuer version needs its own graduation criteria expressed as progress, the bureau reporting calendar (when a payment will show), utilisation advice tied to statement dates rather than due dates, and Reg B care that nothing reads as a promise of credit.

## Target Customer
Product owners of secured and starter cards.

## Impact If Solved
Cardholders who know how to graduate tend to do it, and stay: lifting the share who meet graduation criteria by ~5 percentage points (estimate) would move ~10,000 more cardholders a year onto unsecured lines in a 200,000-account secured portfolio.

# Disputes & Billing-Error Resolution

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/credit-card-issuers|Credit Card Issuers]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to decide each disputed charge correctly — genuine fraud, merchant error or first-party misuse — inside Reg Z's two-billing-cycle limit and the network's chargeback window, and whoever cuts write-offs without wrongly denying genuine claims takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$4B a year in US issuer dispute operations staff, case software, network fees and outsourced back-office work, excluding write-offs (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~7% of issuer spend on data, decisioning and operations (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — intake is digital, adjudication is reading documents and making calls
**Target Buyer:** Head of Disputes / Card Operations Executive
**Automation Potential:** High for intake, evidence assembly and deadline control; medium for adjudication

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Cardholders disputed $9.8B of charges in 2024, producing $5.9B of chargebacks (CFPB). The issuer sits between a cardholder protected by Reg Z 1026.13 — acknowledgment in 30 days, resolution within two billing cycles and never more than 90 days, no collection or adverse reporting meanwhile — and a merchant who can rebut under network rules. What the issuer cannot charge back, it writes off; what it wrongly denies becomes a complaint. Every competitor, in-house team or vendor, is judged on the same three numbers: write-off, recovery and deadline compliance.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Network portals (Visa Resolve Online, Mastercom), processor dispute modules, specialist software (Quavo, which publishes an annual benchmark with Auriemma Roundtables) and general case management. Gaps: merchant representment packages read by hand; first-party misuse judged by gut; and outcome data rarely fed back into intake.

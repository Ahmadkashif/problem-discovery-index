# Representment Automation Turned Around for the Issuer

**Niche:** [[niches/credit-card-issuers/disputes-and-billing-errors/profile|Disputes & Billing-Error Resolution]]
**Industry:** [[industries/credit-card-issuers|Credit Card Issuers]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Merchants already buy software that writes representment packages automatically; issuers still read those packages by hand to decide whether to go to pre-arbitration.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #evaluation-metrics #workflow-orchestration #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to decide each disputed charge correctly — genuine fraud, merchant error or first-party misuse — inside Reg Z's two-billing-cycle limit and the network's chargeback window, and whoever cuts write-offs without wrongly denying genuine claims takes the account.

## The Problem
When a merchant rebuts a chargeback, the issuer's analyst reads its evidence — receipts, delivery confirmations, logs, terms of service — and decides whether to accept the loss, go back to the cardholder or escalate.

## What Already Exists
Merchant-side chargeback automation from vendors such as Chargebacks911, Justt and Chargeflow, and the networks' evidence-exchange services, covered from the seller's side in [[industries/payment-fraud-vendors|Payment Fraud Vendors]].

## The Customization Gap
The issuer side needs: evaluation of evidence against the specific reason code's remedy conditions; comparison with what the cardholder said; the Reg Z clock as well as the network clock; a recommendation with its rationale for the analyst; and a record of outcomes by merchant to spot those whose evidence is boilerplate.

## Target Customer
Dispute operations leaders at issuers and the processors that run disputes for small issuers.

## Impact If Solved
Faster, more consistent pre-arbitration decisions and fewer losses accepted by default.

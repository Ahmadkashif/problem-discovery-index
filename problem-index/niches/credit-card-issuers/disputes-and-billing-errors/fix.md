# The Re-Bill Letter That Becomes a Complaint

**Niche:** [[niches/credit-card-issuers/disputes-and-billing-errors/profile|Disputes & Billing-Error Resolution]]
**Industry:** [[industries/credit-card-issuers|Credit Card Issuers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** When a dispute is lost, the provisional credit is reversed with a form letter the cardholder does not understand, and the next contact is a complaint.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #compliance #workflow-orchestration #quick-win #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to decide each disputed charge correctly — genuine fraud, merchant error or first-party misuse — inside Reg Z's two-billing-cycle limit and the network's chargeback window, and whoever cuts write-offs without wrongly denying genuine claims takes the account.

## The Problem
Reg Z requires a written explanation and, on request, the documentation behind a decision that no error occurred. Form letters cite the reason code, not the merchant's evidence.

## Why It's Still Broken
Letters are templated by reason code and approved once by legal; tailoring each one was never affordable.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A draft letter per case that explains in plain language what the merchant showed, attaches the evidence, states the re-billing date and the cardholder's rights, and is checked by the analyst before sending.

## Who Feels the Pain
Cardholders re-billed without explanation, contact-centre agents, and complaint teams answering through the CFPB portal.

## Impact If Fixed
Fewer repeat contacts and complaints on disputes the issuer decided correctly.

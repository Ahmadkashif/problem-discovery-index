# Acquisition & Underwriting Decisioning

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/credit-card-issuers|Credit Card Issuers]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to approve, and set the first line for, applicants the bureau file describes poorly — thin files, recent immigrants, near-prime rebuilders — at a loss rate it can predict in advance and explain in an adverse action notice, and whoever does that best takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$8B a year in US issuer spend on bureau data, prescreen lists, scores, alternative data, decision engines and acquisition analytics, excluding marketing media (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~13% of issuer spend on data, decisioning and operations (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** High — applications are decisioned in seconds, but the data behind the decision is mostly the bureau file
**Target Buyer:** Chief Credit Officer, Head of Acquisition Risk
**Automation Potential:** Very high for the decision; low for the policy that sets what the model may use

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Acquisition is where an issuer decides who it lends to and on what first line, and it is the one decision made without any history on the customer. New account originations fell 19% to 89 million in 2024 (CFPB) as issuers tightened, which made the contest sharper: the prime applicant is over-served and over-solicited, so growth comes from applicants a standard score cannot separate. The rules that govern it — ECOA and Reg B on reasons and fair lending, FCRA on prescreen and permissible purpose, the CARD Act's ability-to-pay test — are specific to credit decisions, and the vendors that sell into it (bureaus, score developers, cash-flow data providers, decision engines) compete on the applicant segment where their data adds lift.

## Current Tools & Gaps
FICO and VantageScore scores and bureau attributes; prescreen lists from the bureaus; cash-flow and bank-transaction data from aggregators such as Plaid and from score developers built on it; decision engines from FICO, Experian, Provenir and Taktile; ML underwriting vendors such as Zest AI, used mostly by credit unions. The gaps: lift on thin files is claimed by every vendor and rarely tested on the issuer's own through-the-door population; reasons for declines from complex models are hard to keep accurate; and first-party fraud mislabelled as default contaminates the training data.

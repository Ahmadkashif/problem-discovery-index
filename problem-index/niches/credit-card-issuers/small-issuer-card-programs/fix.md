# The Agent-Bank Decision Made on a Revenue-Share Quote

**Niche:** [[niches/credit-card-issuers/small-issuer-card-programs/profile|Community Bank & Credit Union Card Programmes]]
**Industry:** [[industries/credit-card-issuers|Credit Card Issuers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Small institutions decide whether to keep their card portfolio or move it to an agent-bank programme by comparing a provider's revenue-share quote with a rough in-house P&L.
**Tags:** #survival-analysis #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #revenue-impact #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to give an issuer with a few thousand to a hundred thousand card accounts underwriting, line and rewards strategies calibrated on pooled peer-portfolio performance rather than on its own thin history, and whoever's pooled performance data is deepest takes the account — whether the institution keeps the balances or rents its brand to an agent-bank programme.

## The Problem
The comparison usually leaves out credit losses through a cycle, rewards cost, fraud, servicing and the value of the card in the wider customer relationship.

## Why It's Still Broken
The decision comes up every few years, and the providers bidding for the portfolio supply most of the analysis.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A standard keep-or-rent model that projects the in-house portfolio through a stress scenario, values relationship effects from the institution's own data, and compares like for like with each provider's terms.

## Who Feels the Pain
Chief lending officers and boards of small institutions, and their members.

## Impact If Fixed
A portfolio decision made on the institution's own numbers rather than the bidder's.

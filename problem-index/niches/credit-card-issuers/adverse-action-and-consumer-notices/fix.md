# The Six-Month Rate Review Run From a Spreadsheet

**Niche:** [[niches/credit-card-issuers/adverse-action-and-consumer-notices/profile|Adverse Action & Regulatory Notices]]
**Industry:** [[industries/credit-card-issuers|Credit Card Issuers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Accounts whose rate was raised must be re-evaluated at least every six months, and many issuers run that review as a batch extract and a spreadsheet.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #compliance #workflow-orchestration #quick-win #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn each model-driven decline, line decrease or rate increase into a notice whose stated reasons are the model's actual reasons and which goes out inside the Reg B and Reg Z clocks, and whoever makes reasons both faithful to the model and defensible to an examiner takes the account.

## The Problem
The review must consider the factors behind the original increase and reduce the rate where they have improved. Accounts drop out of extracts, factors are recomputed inconsistently and decisions are not recorded per account.

## Why It's Still Broken
The rule applies to a modest share of accounts, so it never got a system of its own.

## What a Fix Looks Like
An automated review population maintained from the rate-change history, the original factors re-scored on schedule, reductions applied automatically and a per-account record of each review.

## Who Feels the Pain
Compliance teams facing exam findings and restitution, and cardholders left on a raised rate.

## Impact If Fixed
A recurring exam risk closed with a small piece of automation.

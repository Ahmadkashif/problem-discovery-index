# One P&L per Rewards Currency

**Niche:** [[niches/credit-card-issuers/rewards-and-co-brand-economics/profile|Rewards & Co-Brand Economics]]
**Industry:** [[industries/credit-card-issuers|Credit Card Issuers]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** An issuer can report rewards cost to the cent and still not say which of its rewards currencies, products or cardholder segments makes money once interest, interchange, fees, partner payments and losses are joined.
**Tags:** #gradient-boosting #survival-analysis #feature-engineering #evaluation-metrics #data-integration #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is that every serious competitor is fighting to make rewards pay for themselves — but that splits into two contests with different winners: pricing a partner portfolio well enough to win or keep a co-brand contract, and forecasting and managing what an issued point will cost. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## The Problem
Interchange is booked by the payments team, interest and losses by credit, rewards cost by rewards finance, partner payments by partnerships and annual fees by product. Each ledger is accurate. The profitability of a rewards currency — and of the cardholders who earn it — is assembled once a year for planning, by hand, from aggregates.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The data sits with different owners on different systems, and the answer is politically uncomfortable: some flagship products and segments lose money and are kept for acquisition or partner reasons nobody wants to restate.

## What to Build
A cardholder-month ledger that attributes interest, interchange, fees, rewards accrual at expected cost, partner payments, funding cost and expected loss to each account, rolled up by currency, product, partner and segment, with forecast redemption cost from survival models rather than a flat rate. It should answer "what does this cardholder earn us over three years under the current programme, and under the proposed change?"

## Target Customer
CFOs of card businesses and heads of rewards at issuers with several rewards currencies or co-brand programmes.

## Impact If Built
Programme changes, retention offers and partner negotiations decided on per-cardholder economics instead of annual aggregates.

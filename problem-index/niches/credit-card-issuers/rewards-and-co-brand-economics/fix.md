# Statement Credits Nobody Tracks Until Year-End

**Niche:** [[niches/credit-card-issuers/rewards-and-co-brand-economics/profile|Rewards & Co-Brand Economics]]
**Industry:** [[industries/credit-card-issuers|Credit Card Issuers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Premium cards justify a high annual fee with dining, travel, streaming and lounge credits, and the issuer learns how many were used only when the year's accounts are closed.
**Tags:** #time-series-forecasting #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #data-integration #revenue-impact #quick-win
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is that every serious competitor is fighting to make rewards pay for themselves — but that splits into two contests with different winners: pricing a partner portfolio well enough to win or keep a co-brand contract, and forecasting and managing what an issued point will cost. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## The Problem
Each credit has its own enrolment, merchant list, monthly or annual cadence and posting logic, often run by a different partner. Utilisation is reported late, in aggregate, so the economics of a premium product depend on a number the product team cannot see during the year.

## Why It's Still Broken
Credits were added one partnership at a time, each wired into posting separately, and no single team owns the full set.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A credit-utilisation feed per account and per credit, forecast to year-end from early-month behaviour, with alerts when a credit's cost runs ahead of plan and with the cardholder-level view retention teams need when a customer threatens to cancel over the fee.

## Who Feels the Pain
Premium product managers, rewards finance and retention agents arguing about a fee without knowing what the cardholder used.

## Impact If Fixed
Premium card economics managed monthly rather than discovered annually.

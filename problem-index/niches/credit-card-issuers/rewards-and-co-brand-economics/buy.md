# Customer Lifetime Value Fitted to Rewards Cards

**Niche:** [[niches/credit-card-issuers/rewards-and-co-brand-economics/profile|Rewards & Co-Brand Economics]]
**Industry:** [[industries/credit-card-issuers|Credit Card Issuers]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Lifetime-value models from retail and subscription businesses assume one revenue stream and no credit risk; a card customer pays through four streams and can default.
**Tags:** #survival-analysis #gradient-boosting #bayesian-inference #evaluation-metrics #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is that every serious competitor is fighting to make rewards pay for themselves — but that splits into two contests with different winners: pricing a partner portfolio well enough to win or keep a co-brand contract, and forecasting and managing what an issued point will cost. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## The Problem
Retention offers, product changes and acquisition bids all need a lifetime value per cardholder, and issuers mostly use a revenue-based proxy.

## What Already Exists
Customer lifetime value and churn tooling in customer-data and marketing platforms (Salesforce, Adobe, Braze) and survival-based CLV libraries built for retail and subscription businesses.

## The Customization Gap
The card version needs: revenue split into interchange (spend-driven), interest (revolve-driven) and fees; rewards cost netted at expected redemption cost by currency; credit loss and funding cost as negative cash flows that rise for exactly the revolvers who generate interest; attrition that often follows a redemption or an annual-fee posting; and co-brand partner payments that differ by product.

## Target Customer
Product and retention leaders at rewards issuers.

## Impact If Solved
Retention offers sized to what the cardholder is actually worth, rather than to how much they spend.

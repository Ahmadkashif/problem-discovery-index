# Rewards & Co-Brand Economics

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/credit-card-issuers|Credit Card Issuers]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is that every serious competitor is fighting to make rewards pay for themselves — but that splits into two contests with different winners: pricing a partner portfolio well enough to win or keep a co-brand contract, and forecasting and managing what an issued point will cost. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$12B a year in US issuer spend on rewards platforms and fulfilment, partner management, partnership finance and rewards analytics, excluding the cost of the rewards themselves (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~20% of issuer spend on data, decisioning and operations (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — accrual and redemption are fully digital; the economics are run in spreadsheets
**Target Buyer:** Head of Rewards, Head of Partnerships, CFO of the card business
**Automation Potential:** Medium — forecasting and reconciliation automate; partner negotiation does not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Rewards cards carry 92% of general-purpose purchase volume (CFPB), so the rewards programme is the product most cardholders choose on. Its economics are unlike anything else in the issuer: a liability created at every swipe, a cost that depends on how and when the point is redeemed, and, in co-brand programmes, a partner that owns the currency and is paid per mile — Delta took $8.2B from American Express in 2025. When a large partner rebids its programme, the portfolio itself moves between issuers, as Apple Card is doing from Goldman Sachs to JPMorgan Chase.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Loyalty engines (Kobie, Ascenda, Augeo, in-house builds), travel portals, transfer-partner integrations, partner revenue-share spreadsheets and the advisory firms that run co-brand requests for proposal. No issuer-side tool joins interchange, interest, rewards cost and partner payments into one view per product and per cardholder.

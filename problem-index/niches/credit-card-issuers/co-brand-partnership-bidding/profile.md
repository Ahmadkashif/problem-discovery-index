# Co-Brand Partnership Bidding & Renewal

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/credit-card-issuers|Credit Card Issuers]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to price a partner's portfolio — remuneration per mile or point, the spend and revolve the partner's customers will generate, acquisition through the partner's own channels, and the balances that move at contract expiry — closer to its true lifetime value than the rival bidder, and whoever prices it best takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$3B a year in partner-facing analytics, partnership teams, advisory fees and marketing commitments negotiated in co-brand and private-label programmes, excluding the remuneration itself (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~5% of issuer spend on data, decisioning and operations (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — bids are built in spreadsheets from data rooms
**Target Buyer:** Head of Partnerships / Co-Brand General Manager
**Automation Potential:** Medium for valuation and contract tracking; low for negotiation

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A co-brand contract — an airline, hotel group, retailer or technology company lending its brand and customers to an issuer — runs five to ten years and is rebid at expiry. The incumbent knows the portfolio's true performance; the challenger sees only what the partner's data room shows. The winner is whoever prices the portfolio's future closest to reality, because over-bidding loses money for a decade and under-bidding loses the portfolio, as Goldman Sachs's exit from Apple Card and the Delta–American Express agreement running to 2029 show at the top of the market.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Advisory firms run the request for proposal; issuers build bid models in spreadsheets from the data room; partner payments are reconciled monthly in finance systems. Gaps: no standard portfolio-value model that both the partner and the bidders can test; contract obligations tracked in documents; post-signing performance rarely compared against the bid.

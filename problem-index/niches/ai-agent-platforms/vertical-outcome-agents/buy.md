# Outsourcing and Contact Centre Management

**Niche:** [[niches/ai-agent-platforms/vertical-outcome-agents/profile|Vertical Outcome Agents]]
**Industry:** [[industries/ai-agent-platforms|AI Agent Platforms]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** The outsourcing industry spent thirty years building quality monitoring, service level agreements and workforce management for exactly this buyer, and agent vendors sell a dashboard.
**Tags:** #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #time-series-forecasting #confidence-intervals #revenue-impact #compliance #hypothesis-testing #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this sub-niche is fighting to resolve a higher share of one bounded domain's tasks without a human than an outsourcer would at the same cost — and whoever does that takes the account, because the buyer is comparing against a labour contract, not against a framework.

## The Problem
The buyer of a vertical agent is the buyer of an outsourcing contract, and that market has a mature apparatus: service level agreements with penalties, quality monitoring by sampled review against a scorecard, calibration sessions so reviewers agree, workforce management for volume forecasting, and defined escalation paths. Agent vendors arrive with a resolution percentage and a monthly report, into a procurement function that has run contact centre contracts for two decades and knows what is missing.

## What Already Exists
Contact centre service level agreements with measured penalties; quality monitoring with sampled review, scorecards and calibration; workforce management with volume forecasting and staffing models; first contact resolution and repeat contact metrics; escalation and severity frameworks; and customer satisfaction instrumentation tied to interactions.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a workforce that is software. It requires: (1) service levels that are contractible, since an outsourcer commits to a resolution rate with a penalty and agent vendors commit to nothing — adopting this norm is the single biggest credibility move available to a vendor confident in their agent; (2) quality scorecards applied to agent interactions by human reviewers, which is exactly the existing practice and needs almost no adaptation, and which no vendor volunteers; (3) volume forecasting that accounts for an agent's capacity being elastic, which changes the staffing model rather than removing it; (4) a defined escalation contract, since escalation is where the agent's failures land on the buyer's own staff and is currently informal; and (5) calibration between the vendor's automated grading and the buyer's reviewers, which is how the two sides come to agree on what resolved means.

## Target Customer
Support and operations leaders, procurement functions comparing against outsourcing, and the contact centre management industry whose practice transfers almost unchanged.

## Impact If Solved
The buyer already runs this playbook for human outsourcers and the agent vendors arrive without it. A contractible service level with a penalty is the biggest credibility move available, and sampled quality scorecards transfer with almost no adaptation.

# Merge Engines Adapted to Two Score Models on One Report

**Niche:** [[niches/credit-bureaus/mortgage-merged-reports/profile|Mortgage Merged Reports & Score Transition]]
**Industry:** [[industries/credit-bureaus|Credit Bureaus]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Reseller merge engines were built around one score model selected by a fixed rule; the transition needs two models, two licensing paths and the lender's choice carried through to the investor.
**Tags:** #decision-trees #evaluation-metrics #feature-engineering #compliance #data-integration #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to deliver the merged report and whichever score set each lender's investors now accept — Classic FICO, VantageScore 4.0, later FICO 10T — straight into the lender's pricing and underwriting engines, and whoever re-plumbs the score transition without breaking a lender's pricing or pull-through takes the account.

## The Problem
A merged report today carries three Classic FICO scores and a representative-score rule. Under the rollout a lender may order VantageScore 4.0 instead, FICO 10T is coming, scores may be licensed directly from FICO or through the bureaus, and the choice must be recorded consistently from order to delivery to the agency.

## What Already Exists
Reseller merge and delivery platforms with established bureau connections, and loan origination system credit interfaces.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs: score-model selection per loan and per investor; direct-licence and bureau-licence billing with funded-loan fees reconciled to closings; representative-score logic for each model; the score set carried consistently into the pricing engine, underwriting submission and loan delivery data; and audit trails showing which model was used and why.

## Target Customer
Mortgage credit resellers and the loan origination system vendors they integrate with.

## Impact If Solved
The transition happens without mispriced loans or deliveries rejected for score inconsistency, and resellers turn a disruption into a reason to switch.

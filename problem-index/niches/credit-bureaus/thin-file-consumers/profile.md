# Thin-File & Credit-Invisible Consumers

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/credit-bureaus|Credit Bureaus]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get rent, utility, telecom or cash-flow data into the lender's production underwriting model rather than a side score, and whoever clears the lender's model validation and fair-lending review first takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$500M a year of alternative-data reports, scores and rent and utility furnishing services sold for thin-file decisions (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~3% (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — the data exists digitally in bank accounts and rent ledgers but reaches bureaus selectively and in non-standard forms
**Target Buyer:** Head of Credit Strategy / Chief Risk Officer at fintech, community and card lenders
**Automation Potential:** High for data assembly, low for model acceptance

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The CFPB's corrected estimate puts ~7 million adults (2.7%) with no credit record in 2020, and a larger group with a record too thin or stale to score — revised to 12.7% of adults for 2010. These consumers are the subject of a file that does not exist, so no buyer represents them. The products that try — Experian Boost, Plaid Check's Consumer Report with Prism Data's CashScore, rent reporting through Experian RentBureau and services such as Esusu, VantageScore's claim to score more consumers, now accepted in a limited agency-mortgage rollout — all hit the same wall: lenders use them as a second look rather than inside the model that sets price and approval. The consumer-side counterpart is [[niches/credit-card-issuers/secured-and-credit-builder-cardholders/profile|Secured & Credit-Builder Cardholders]]; the lender-side counterpart is [[niches/credit-unions/underbanked-community-cus/profile|Underbanked Community CUs]].

## Current Tools & Gaps
Consumer-permissioned cash-flow reports, opt-in score boosts, rent and utility furnishing, telecom and utility exchange data, and alternative scores. The gaps: validation evidence a model-risk team will accept; adverse-action reasons that work for cash-flow features; and rent data reported selectively.

# FCRA Dispute Reinvestigation

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/credit-bureaus|Credit Bureaus]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is that every competitor is fighting to close disputes inside the FCRA clock with an investigation that would survive a reasonableness challenge — but the bureau taking in millions of letters and the furnisher answering the ACDVs they generate are different buyers, on different clocks, judged on different evidence. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.2B a year of bureau and furnisher spend on dispute handling staff, outsourcing and tooling (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~7% of credit bureau revenue equivalent (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — e-OSCAR moves the ACDV electronically, but intake is scanned letters and investigation is reading
**Target Buyer:** VP of Consumer Relations at a bureau; Head of Credit Reporting Compliance at a furnisher
**Automation Potential:** High for intake and case preparation, low for the investigation decision

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
FCRA §611 gives a bureau 30 days to reinvestigate a dispute (45 if the consumer adds information) and five business days to notify the furnisher; §623 obliges the furnisher to investigate what the bureau sends. The channel between them is e-OSCAR, which the nationwide bureaus built in 1993. The volume is now extreme: the CFPB received ~5.8 million credit reporting complaints in 2025, 88% of its total, and blamed part of the rise on credit repair organisations and AI-generated submissions. Litigation over "parroting" — a bureau accepting the furnisher's answer without its own look — makes the quality of each investigation a liability question, not just a service level.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Bureaus' in-house dispute platforms and outsourced centres; e-OSCAR; furnishers' servicing platforms and e-OSCAR's web interface (used by 86% of furnishers in a 2025 survey for indirect disputes); generic case-management tools. Neither side sees the whole lifecycle: the bureau sees the furnisher's response code, the furnisher sees a dispute code and a short narrative, and nobody joins the outcome back to the file error that caused it.

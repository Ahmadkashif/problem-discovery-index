# Case Management Adapted to the FCRA Clock

**Niche:** [[niches/credit-bureaus/fcra-dispute-reinvestigation/profile|FCRA Dispute Reinvestigation]]
**Industry:** [[industries/credit-bureaus|Credit Bureaus]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Customer service case platforms track tickets against service levels; a credit dispute runs on statutory clocks with their own notices, extensions and exceptions.
**Tags:** #decision-trees #evaluation-metrics #compliance #workflow-orchestration #automation #data-integration
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is that every competitor is fighting to close disputes inside the FCRA clock with an investigation that would survive a reasonableness challenge — but the bureau taking in millions of letters and the furnisher answering the ACDVs they generate are different buyers, on different clocks, judged on different evidence. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## The Problem
Specialty bureaus — tenant screening, verification, check and deposit screening — face the same §611 duties as the nationwide bureaus with far smaller teams, and many run disputes in a general case tool or a shared inbox. The clock logic is easy to get wrong: when the 15-day extension applies, when a frivolous determination must be sent, when a reinsertion requires notice.

## What Already Exists
General case-management and service platforms (Salesforce Service Cloud, Zendesk, Pega) with configurable service levels and workflows.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs: the §611 timeline with the 5-business-day furnisher notice, the 30/45-day rule and its exceptions, frivolous and reinsertion notices; an ACDV-equivalent channel to the specialty bureau's own data sources (court record vendors, contributors); identity theft block handling under §605B; linking CFPB complaints to the underlying dispute; and records structured for litigation review.

## Target Customer
Compliance and operations leads at specialty consumer reporting agencies without a nationwide bureau's dispute platform.

## Impact If Solved
Missed statutory deadlines — the easiest FCRA claim to prove — disappear from smaller bureaus' exposure.

# Furnisher ACDV Response

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/credit-bureaus|Credit Bureaus]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to answer each ACDV inside the bureau's response window with the account records actually checked, and whoever lets a furnisher's dispute team clear its e-OSCAR queue on time with an investigation record that holds up in an FCRA §623 suit takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$500M a year of furnisher spend on dispute staff, servicing-platform modules and outsourced dispute handling (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~3% (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — ACDVs arrive electronically; the investigation is a person comparing screens and documents
**Target Buyer:** Head of Credit Reporting Compliance / Dispute Operations Manager at a lender, servicer, collection agency or credit union
**Automation Potential:** High for evidence retrieval and drafting; the response decision stays human

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The furnisher's half of the dispute has a different buyer, clock and evidence from the bureau's. A furnisher answers ACDVs within the response date the bureau sets, which is shorter than the consumer's 30 days because the bureau keeps time for itself (recalled practice), and also handles direct disputes under §623. Volumes are uneven: a February 2025 survey found 53% of furnishers handle 250 or fewer disputes a month and 34% more than 1,000. The legal test is whether the furnisher reasonably investigated, so the winning product is the one that makes the check real and the record complete at that volume. The bureau's half is [[niches/credit-bureaus/bureau-dispute-intake/profile|Bureau Dispute Intake & Triage]].

## Current Tools & Gaps
e-OSCAR's web interface and batch files; dispute modules in loan servicing and collection platforms; spreadsheets for direct disputes. The gaps: evidence pulled by hand from several systems; "verified as reported" returned without a field-by-field comparison; recurring disputes never traced to the Metro 2 mapping that caused them.

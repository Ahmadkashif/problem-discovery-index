# Employment & Income Verification

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/credit-bureaus|Credit Bureaus]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to hold a current, employer-sourced record on the applicant at the moment a lender, landlord or benefits agency asks, and whoever answers the most applicants instantly without a paystub upload takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$3B a year of US verification revenue across lending, government benefits, pre-employment and rental use (estimate; Equifax Workforce Solutions alone reported $2.58B in 2025, including employer services)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~18% (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** High — a hit returns in seconds by API; a miss falls back to paystubs, tax forms and phone calls to employers
**Target Buyer:** Head of verification operations at lenders; state eligibility system directors; background-screening operations leads
**Automation Potential:** Very high on a hit, low on the no-hit fallback

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Verification bureaus are specialty consumer reporting agencies: their report is a consumer report under the FCRA, but its content is payroll — employer, dates, pay rate, hours and earnings — rather than credit. The contest is coverage. Equifax's The Work Number held ~209 million active and ~813 million total employment records at end-2025, contributed by employers and payroll processors; challengers such as Truv, Argyle and Pinwheel connect to payroll accounts with the consumer's permission, and Experian Verify builds a second contributed database. The lender-side view of the same workflow, where the contest is agency rep-and-warrant relief, is [[niches/mortgage-lenders/income-asset-verification/profile|Income, Asset & Employment Verification]] under mortgage lending. A new buyer arrives on a statutory date: states must verify Medicaid work hours from 1 January 2027.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Contributed-record databases, consumer-permissioned payroll and bank connectors, document extraction for paystubs and W-2s, and manual employer calls. The gaps: gig and multi-job income; hours rather than income; records that still show an employee who has left; and per-verification prices that push buyers to mix sources (estimate).

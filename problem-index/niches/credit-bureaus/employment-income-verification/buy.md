# Payroll Connectors Adapted to Benefits Eligibility

**Niche:** [[niches/credit-bureaus/employment-income-verification/profile|Employment & Income Verification]]
**Industry:** [[industries/credit-bureaus|Credit Bureaus]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Verification products were built to answer a lender's question about income; Medicaid now asks whether someone worked 80 hours in each month.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #decision-trees #feature-engineering #evaluation-metrics #compliance #data-integration #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to hold a current, employer-sourced record on the applicant at the moment a lender, landlord or benefits agency asks, and whoever answers the most applicants instantly without a paystub upload takes the account.

## The Problem
Under the 2025 reconciliation law, expansion-state Medicaid adults must show at least 80 hours a month of work or qualifying activity, and states must start by 1 January 2027 (HHS may extend to the end of 2028). States must verify at application and at least twice a year, checking data sources before asking the enrollee. Low-wage, hourly, multi-job and gig workers — the population affected — are exactly where contributed databases are thinnest.

## What Already Exists
Contributed-record verification databases already sold to state agencies for income eligibility; consumer-permissioned payroll connectors (Truv, Argyle, Pinwheel) sold to lenders; document extraction for paystubs.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs: hours by calendar month rather than annualised income; aggregation across several employers and platforms; the minimum-wage income equivalence the law allows; exemption evidence alongside work evidence; integration with state eligibility systems and their notice rules; and an audit record per determination, because a wrong "no record" can end someone's coverage.

## Target Customer
State Medicaid and eligibility system directors and their systems integrators; verification bureaus extending into government.

## Impact If Solved
States verify from data instead of paperwork for the people who can be verified, and the people who cannot are identified early enough to be asked, rather than dropped.

# Adverse Action Notices Written From the Actual Decision Rule

**Niche:** [[niches/credit-bureaus/tenant-screening-decisions/profile|Tenant Screening Reports & Decisions]]
**Industry:** [[industries/credit-bureaus|Credit Bureaus]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Lenders have mature adverse-action tooling; landlords send a generic notice that names the screening company and not the reason.
**Tags:** #decision-trees #gradient-boosting #evaluation-metrics #compliance #workflow-orchestration #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to return a screening decision in minutes that is matched to the right applicant and holds up under FCRA accuracy rules and fair-housing law, and whoever gives landlords instant decisions with the fewest wrong-person and disparate-impact findings takes the account.

## The Problem
When an applicant is declined or asked for a larger deposit on the basis of a screening report, the landlord must send an FCRA adverse action notice. Most notices are templates generated by the screening platform; when the decline came from a score or a combination of rules, the applicant cannot tell which item to dispute.

## What Already Exists
Lending decision platforms generate adverse-action notices with principal reasons tied to model features, under Reg B and FCRA requirements.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs: reasons drawn from the property's actual screening criteria and the score's drivers; the specific report items (eviction case, collection, criminal record) cited so they can be disputed; local fair-chance and source-of-income ordinances reflected in the notice; and a record of which rule declined which applicant for the landlord's fair-housing defence.

## Target Customer
Screening bureaus and property-management platforms; compliance leads at large property managers.

## Impact If Solved
Applicants can contest the item that actually cost them the apartment, and landlords can show why each decision was made.

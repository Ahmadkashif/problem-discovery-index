# Tenant Screening Reports & Decisions

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/credit-bureaus|Credit Bureaus]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to return a screening decision in minutes that is matched to the right applicant and holds up under FCRA accuracy rules and fair-housing law, and whoever gives landlords instant decisions with the fewest wrong-person and disparate-impact findings takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.5B a year of US tenant screening reports, scores and decision services (estimate; much of it booked by firms coded outside 561450)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~8% (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** High — applications screened online in minutes
**Target Buyer:** VP of Leasing or Operations at a property manager; product head at a screening bureau
**Automation Potential:** Very high for assembly and recommendation; the legal exposure sits in the decision rule

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Tenant screening bureaus assemble a credit report, eviction filings, sometimes criminal records and rental payment history into a report and, increasingly, an approve/decline recommendation. The work is mechanical and fast, and its failures are expensive: the FTC and CFPB settled with TransUnion's rental screening business for $15M in 2023 over inaccurate eviction records, and SafeRent paid ~$2.3M in 2024 and agreed to stop scoring voucher holders after claims its score disadvantaged Black and Hispanic applicants. The screening data business is profiled as an insight pocket at [[niches/property-management/tenant-screening-data/profile|🔍 Tenant Screening Data & Scoring]] (54, indexed). Criminal-record research for employers belongs to the queued investigation-and-screening-firms industry and is left there.

## Current Tools & Gaps
TransUnion SmartMove and ResidentScore, RealPage, Yardi, SafeRent and many smaller bureaus, fed by court-record vendors and the nationwide bureaus. The gaps: eviction filings reported without their outcome; adverse-action notices that do not reflect the rule that actually declined the applicant; income rules that misread vouchers and benefits.

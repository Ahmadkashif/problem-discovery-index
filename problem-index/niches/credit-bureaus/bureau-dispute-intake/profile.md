# Bureau Dispute Intake & Triage

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/credit-bureaus|Credit Bureaus]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to separate the genuine error from the templated flood at intake — routing identity theft, mixed-file and real inaccuracy claims to investigation inside the five-business-day furnisher notice — and whoever cuts the bureau's cost per dispute without wrongly dismissing a genuine claim takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$700M a year of bureau spend on dispute intake, preparation and handling, in-house and outsourced (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~4% (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — mail, uploads and phone notes keyed by agents into the dispute platform
**Target Buyer:** VP of Consumer Relations / Head of Dispute Operations at a nationwide or specialty bureau
**Automation Potential:** High for preparation and routing; the determination stays human

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The bureau's half of the dispute is an intake problem at a volume no other part of the industry faces: millions of submissions a year (estimate), a rising share of them templated by credit repair firms or generated by language models, mixed with identity theft victims and consumers with real errors. The clock starts at receipt. The winner is whoever classifies and prepares each case fastest without the one failure regulators and courts punish — treating a genuine claim as noise. The furnisher's side of the same dispute is [[niches/credit-bureaus/furnisher-acdv-response/profile|Furnisher ACDV Response]].

## Current Tools & Gaps
Bureaus' proprietary dispute platforms, mailroom scanning, outsourced and offshore agent centres, and e-OSCAR for transmission. The gaps: classification by agents reading scans; generic dispute codes on ACDVs; no consistent treatment of templated submissions; identity theft and mixed-file cases buried in the general queue.

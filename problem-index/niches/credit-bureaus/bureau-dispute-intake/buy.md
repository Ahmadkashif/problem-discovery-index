# Document Processing Adapted to Dispute Letters

**Niche:** [[niches/credit-bureaus/bureau-dispute-intake/profile|Bureau Dispute Intake & Triage]]
**Industry:** [[industries/credit-bureaus|Credit Bureaus]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Intelligent document processing reads invoices and claims forms; a dispute letter must be read against the consumer's own credit file.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #cnns #evaluation-metrics #automation #compliance #data-integration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to separate the genuine error from the templated flood at intake — routing identity theft, mixed-file and real inaccuracy claims to investigation inside the five-business-day furnisher notice — and whoever cuts the bureau's cost per dispute without wrongly dismissing a genuine claim takes the account.

## The Problem
Disputes arrive as handwritten notes, typed letters, annotated copies of the credit report, identity theft affidavits and police reports. Agents read each one, find the items it refers to and choose a dispute code. Most of the handling time goes on this preparation (estimate).

## What Already Exists
Intelligent document processing platforms (ABBYY, Hyperscience and the cloud providers' document AI services) that classify documents and extract fields from scans.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs: extraction keyed to the consumer's file — matching "the Capital account I never opened" to a specific tradeline; the FCRA dispute taxonomy and ACDV dispute codes as outputs; recognition of identity theft documentation for the §605B path; detection of third-party authorship; handling of annotated report copies; and confidence thresholds below which the agent reads the original.

## Target Customer
Dispute operations at bureaus already running document capture in the mailroom.

## Impact If Solved
Agents confirm prepared cases rather than building them from scans, and more of the 30 days is left for the investigation.

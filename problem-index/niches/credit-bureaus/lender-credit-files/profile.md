# Consumer Credit Files for Lending

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/credit-bureaus|Credit Bureaus]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to win the lender's retro study — the archive test in which each bureau's file and attributes are scored against the lender's own booked and declined applicants — and whoever shows the most lift on that population takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$9B a year of US lender spend on consumer credit reports, scores, attributes and account monitoring (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~50% of credit bureau revenue (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Very High — inquiries run by API in milliseconds; the lender's choice of bureau is still made in bespoke analytical projects
**Target Buyer:** Chief Credit Officer / Head of Credit Risk Analytics at a lender
**Automation Potential:** High for delivery and monitoring, medium for evaluation

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
This is the core of the industry: the three nationwide bureaus selling the same kind of product — a consumer's file, a score and a library of derived attributes — to card issuers, auto lenders, personal loan fintechs, credit unions and banks. The files differ because not every furnisher reports to every bureau, reporting dates differ, and each bureau adds its own trended, alternative and identity data. Lenders decide which bureau to pull first, for which product and region, by running retro studies on archived files of their own past applicants and measuring which file separates good from bad best. TransUnion's US financial services revenue reached $1.68B in 2025 and Equifax's USIS $2.08B; the volume behind those figures is allocated in those tests.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Bureau archive-pull services, proprietary attribute libraries, FICO and VantageScore scores, lenders' own modelling stacks and model-monitoring tools. The gaps: retro studies are bespoke projects of several weeks (estimate) run differently by every lender; the bureau cannot explain in advance why its file will or will not lift a given population; and archive pulls and attribute versions are rarely kept in a form that lets anyone reproduce the test later.

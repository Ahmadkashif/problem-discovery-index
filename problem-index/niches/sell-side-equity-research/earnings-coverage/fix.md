# The Preview Rewritten From Zero Every Quarter

**Niche:** [[niches/sell-side-equity-research/earnings-coverage/profile|Earnings Coverage]]
**Industry:** [[industries/sell-side-equity-research|Sell-Side Equity Research]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Earnings previews repeat the same structure for every company every quarter and are still assembled by hand from the model, consensus and last quarter's note.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #k-nearest-neighbors #evaluation-metrics #automation #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get a correct read of the print — which number the stock will trade on and whether the beat or miss is real — to clients before the open, and whoever does that best owns the morning call and the vote.

## The Problem
Before reporting season the team writes a preview per company: our estimates versus consensus, what to watch, the key debates, how the stock has reacted historically. Most of the content is a mechanical comparison and a recap of last quarter's debates. For an analyst with twenty names it consumes the week before the season starts.

## Why It's Still Broken
Templates exist but the content is per-company and the numbers move until the last day. Generic drafting tools do not know the department's estimates, the analyst's prior framing or the company's history.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A preview drafted from the model, consensus, guidance history and the analyst's last two notes on the company, with every number linked to its source and the "key debates" seeded from what the analyst flagged last quarter. The analyst edits the view; the scaffolding is already there.

## Who Feels the Pain
Analysts and associates in the week before reporting season; clients who receive generic previews.

## Impact If Fixed
Preview week becomes a week of thinking about the debates rather than assembling tables, and every preview inherits the department's own history of what mattered.

# Special-District Infrastructure Finance

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/land-developers|Land Developers]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn a developer's infrastructure spend into district bond reimbursement, and whoever recovers the largest share of eligible cost in each bond series takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$800M a year of engineering, legal, financial-advisory, accounting and management fees around district creation, bond issuance and developer reimbursement (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~5% of receipts in fees, against several billion dollars a year of district bonds (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — reimbursement packages are assembled from paper and PDF support in Excel
**Target Buyer:** Developer CFO, with the district engineer and district accountant
**Automation Potential:** Medium-High for classification and package assembly; low for the bond structuring

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Texas municipal utility districts and Florida community development districts finance the public infrastructure under new subdivisions with tax-exempt bonds repaid by the future residents' taxes or assessments. The developer fronts the money and is reimbursed when the district can sell bonds, which in Texas waits on assessed value and TCEQ approval (search summary). Florida had 1,067 CDDs in August 2025, part of a group of development-style districts that grew by more than half since 2020, and one underwriter, FMSbonds, closed 119 CDD deals worth $1.3B in 2024. Forestar's 10-K names district bonds as a source of reimbursement. The work is a paper contest. Each eligible dollar must be documented to the standard the engineer, auditor and bond counsel require, or it waits for a later series. Bond pricing and credit sit with municipal finance, profiled under Municipal Services at [[niches/municipal-services/municipal-advisory-firms/profile|Municipal Advisory Firms]] and [[niches/municipal-services/municipal-credit-rating-analysis/profile|Municipal Credit Rating & Public Finance Analysis]].

## Current Tools & Gaps
Construction accounting (Sage 300 CRE, Sage Intacct, Yardi), Procore for contracts and pay applications, document storage for invoices and waivers, and Excel at the district engineer and accountant. Gaps: no eligibility classification per cost line, no running reimbursement exhibit, and no forecast of which costs fit the next series.

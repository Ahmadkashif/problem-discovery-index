# Raw-Land Feasibility & Lot Yield Screening

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/land-developers|Land Developers]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn a parcel into a lot count and cost per lot that a land committee will underwrite before the feasibility period ends, and whoever's screened lot counts land closest to the final recorded plats takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$600M a year of land-team, civil-engineering concept and due-diligence labour spent screening tracts (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~4% of receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — parcel data, GIS layers and layout tools exist, but each screen is assembled by hand
**Target Buyer:** VP of land acquisition at a developer or builder division
**Automation Potential:** High for constraint screening and conceptual layouts; medium for cost

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A land team screens many tracts for each one it closes. For each it repeats the same steps: pull the parcel, zoning, floodplain, wetlands inventory, slopes, soils and utility locations; sketch a layout with streets, detention and open space; count lots by product width; and rough out the horizontal cost. The output is the first-look page for the land committee and, later, the basis of the land price. It is the most repeatable judgement work in the industry, and its error is measurable when the final plat records. The civil engineer's concept and later design work is at [[niches/engineering-consultants/civil-site-engineering/profile|Civil/Site Engineering Firms]], and the landscape architect's site design at [[niches/landscape-architecture-firms/private-development-site-design/profile|Private Development Site Design]].

## Current Tools & Gaps
TestFit and TraceAir's layout generator produce conceptual layouts and lot yields. Acres, Regrid and Land id supply parcels and layers, and Acres has launched an AI research agent (search summary). Civil engineers draw concept plans in Civil 3D. Gaps: screens do not account for detention, lift stations and street standards consistently, nobody grades yields against final plats, and cost is a rule of thumb.

# Generative Site Layout Adapted to Single-Family Subdivisions

**Niche:** [[niches/land-developers/raw-land-feasibility-lot-yield/profile|Raw-Land Feasibility & Lot Yield Screening]]
**Industry:** [[industries/land-developers|Land Developers]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Generative layout tools produce fast site plans; subdivision screening needs them to respect drainage, utilities and local street standards, or the yield is fiction.
**Tags:** #optimization-fundamentals #feature-engineering #evaluation-metrics #automation #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn a parcel into a lot count and cost per lot that a land committee will underwrite before the feasibility period ends, and whoever's screened lot counts land closest to the final recorded plats takes the account.

## The Problem
Concept layouts from generative tools often over-count lots because they place streets and lots before detention, lift stations, buffers and local standards are applied.

## What Already Exists
TestFit, TraceAir's layout generator and Autodesk Forma generate site layouts and yields; Civil 3D is the engineer's design tool.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs: local street, cul-de-sac and block-length standards by jurisdiction; detention sizing from the drainage criteria manual; gravity-sewer depth and lift-station triggers from lidar; floodplain and buffer setbacks; the developer's lot products and mix; and the yield interval learned from past plats.

## Target Customer
Land teams already licensing a layout tool.

## Impact If Solved
A layout that already includes drainage and utilities brings the first-look yield closer to the engineer's and saves a round of concept revisions per tract, ~$5–15K of engineering time (estimate).

# The Concept Plan That Ignored the Detention Pond

**Niche:** [[niches/land-developers/raw-land-feasibility-lot-yield/profile|Raw-Land Feasibility & Lot Yield Screening]]
**Industry:** [[industries/land-developers|Land Developers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The lot count in the land committee memo assumed no detention pond, and the engineer's first drainage design took a block of lots.
**Tags:** #optimization-fundamentals #numerical-methods #evaluation-metrics #quick-win #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn a parcel into a lot count and cost per lot that a land committee will underwrite before the feasibility period ends, and whoever's screened lot counts land closest to the final recorded plats takes the account.

## The Problem
Detention and water-quality ponds often take a material share of a site (estimate), and their size depends on local criteria and the site's slope and soils. Quick screens leave them out or guess.

## Why It's Still Broken
Sizing detention properly needs a drainage calculation the land team does not run, and the engineer is engaged only after the land is tied up.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A rough detention estimate at screening from the jurisdiction's criteria, the tract's lidar slopes and soils, and the impervious cover of the lot product, with the pond placed at the low point before lots are counted.

## Who Feels the Pain
Land managers whose yields shrink, land committees, and engineers asked to find the missing lots.

## Impact If Fixed
A first-pass detention estimate removes the most common single source of yield loss between screen and design. If ponds take ~10% of a site, leaving them out overstates a 300-lot screen by ~30 lots, ~$3.3M at $108,400 a lot (estimates).

# Lot-Level Takedown Ledger From the Contracts

**Niche:** [[niches/land-developers/lot-takedown-contract-administration/profile|Lot Takedown & Option Contract Administration]]
**Industry:** [[industries/land-developers|Land Developers]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Developers and land bankers compute every takedown's price, deposit credit and quota from spreadsheets rebuilt for each contract, when the contracts themselves can generate the ledger.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #seq2seq #evaluation-metrics #workflow-orchestration #automation #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to close every scheduled takedown on its date with the right lots, escalated price and deposit credit, and whoever posts the fewest takedown closings needing correction takes the account.

## The Problem
Each takedown requires the right lots, the price escalated from the right date, the right slice of deposit credited, and the quota checked. Errors are small per closing and add up across thousands of lots and several builders.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Lot options were a niche until land-light builders and land-banking platforms made them a large asset class. Each contract is bespoke, and the market of administrators is small.

## What to Build
Extract each agreement's terms into a schema, build the lot ledger from plat and inventory data, compute each takedown's price and credit, generate closing statements, and track quotas and cure periods. Add a takedown forecast per builder from its sales pace, so a quota miss shows before the notice date.

## Target Customer
Controllers at developers selling to several builders, and asset managers at land-banking platforms and lot bankers.

## Impact If Built
Computed takedowns remove reconciliation at each closing and the year-end discovery of escalator and deposit errors. On ~3,000 lots a year, avoiding errors on 1% of closings at ~$5,000 each saves ~$150K a year (estimates).

# Infill Lot-Split & Small-Lot Subdividers

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/land-developers|Land Developers]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to identify the urban parcels that current zoning and state lot-split laws let it divide and get each split approved without a variance, and whoever gets the most splits approved per hundred parcels it pursues takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1B a year of infill lot sales from lot splits, short plats and small-lot subdivisions (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~6% of receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low-Medium — parcel and zoning viewers are used, but eligibility is worked out parcel by parcel with a surveyor and a planner
**Target Buyer:** Owner of a small infill developer or owner-builder
**Automation Potential:** Medium-High for screening; low for the approval and site work

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
In built-up cities the land developer is often a two- or three-person firm buying one or a few lots at a time and splitting them into two to ten smaller lots (estimate) for small builders or its own homes. State and local reforms have widened what can be split. California's SB 9 (2021) allows urban lot splits on many single-family parcels, and several states have cut minimum lot sizes (recalled). The constraint is parcel-level: lot width and depth, frontage, easements, utility laterals, trees, slopes and the exact wording of the rule. The firm's edge is finding the parcels that qualify before others, then getting the split through a short-plat process without a variance. Greenfield tools ignore this segment. The surveyor's and reviewer's side is at [[niches/land-surveyors/plat-subdivision-review/profile|Plat & Subdivision Review]] and [[niches/land-surveyors/boundary-surveys/profile|Boundary Surveys]].

## Current Tools & Gaps
Parcel and zoning platforms (Regrid, Zoneomics, Gridics, city GIS viewers), the city's code, and a surveyor's boundary survey. Gaps: no screen of a city's parcels against the split rules with easements and utilities included, and no record of which splits the city approved and why others failed.

# The Split Denied on an Easement Found at Survey

**Niche:** [[niches/land-developers/infill-lot-split-subdividers/profile|Infill Lot-Split & Small-Lot Subdividers]]
**Industry:** [[industries/land-developers|Land Developers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A developer buys a parcel for a split and the survey finds a utility or access easement that makes the second lot unbuildable.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #feature-engineering #workflow-orchestration #quick-win #compliance
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to identify the urban parcels that current zoning and state lot-split laws let it divide and get each split approved without a variance, and whoever gets the most splits approved per hundred parcels it pursues takes the account.

## The Problem
Easements are recorded in deeds and old plats, not on parcel maps. The survey that reveals them comes after the purchase, so the developer finds out too late.

## Why It's Still Broken
Title commitments list recorded easements as exceptions, but nobody reads them against the proposed split line before closing.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Read the title commitment's easement exceptions and referenced plats during the contract period, map them against the proposed split, and flag any that cross a proposed lot's building area or access.

## Who Feels the Pain
Small infill developers, their surveyors and lenders.

## Impact If Fixed
A ~$500 document check during due diligence avoids buying a parcel whose split will fail, often a ~$100K+ loss of expected lot value (estimates).

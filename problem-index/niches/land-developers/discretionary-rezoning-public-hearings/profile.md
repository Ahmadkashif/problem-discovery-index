# Discretionary Rezoning & Public Hearings

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/land-developers|Land Developers]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to carry rezoning and planned-development cases from filing to a council approval at the density the deal needs, and whoever gets the highest share of filed cases approved at the requested lot count takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.5B a year of land-use legal, planning, traffic-study and in-house entitlement spend on discretionary cases (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~9% of receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — agendas and minutes are online, case history is not structured anywhere
**Target Buyer:** VP of entitlements or land-use attorney
**Automation Potential:** Medium; the forecast and preparation can be automated, the vote cannot

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Where the zoning has to change, the developer is asking elected and appointed bodies for a discretionary decision in public. The case runs through staff review, a neighbourhood meeting, planning commission and council, with continuances whenever a commission wants changes or opposition organises. The competitors for the developer's mandate are land-use attorneys, planning consultants and in-house entitlement teams. The client measures them on how many of its cases get through at the lot count the deal needs; a fast approval at reduced density can still kill the land price. The record that predicts the next vote (how this commission treated comparable requests, which conditions staff attach, how often cases are continued) is public in agendas and minutes but unassembled. Phoenix's and Austin's Legistar APIs, for example, return current rezoning cases without a key, and San Antonio's (records to 2021) shows "continued from" status in case titles.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Legistar, Granicus and CivicClerk publish agendas, staff reports and minutes. Hamlet transcribes and summarises meetings across 3,000+ bodies (search summary). Attorneys keep case files. Gaps: no structured case history by jurisdiction, no forecast of approval odds or months, and staff-comment rounds tracked by hand.

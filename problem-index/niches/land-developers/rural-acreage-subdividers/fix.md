# The Tract Sold Without Legal Access

**Niche:** [[niches/land-developers/rural-acreage-subdividers/profile|Rural Acreage Subdividers]]
**Industry:** [[industries/land-developers|Land Developers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A buyer pays for a back tract and then learns the access easement was never recorded or does not meet the county's road standard.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #workflow-orchestration #compliance #quick-win #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to carve large rural acreage into tracts a buyer can legally reach, build on and finance, and whoever sells the highest share of the tracts it carves within a year takes the account.

## The Problem
Interior tracts depend on private road easements across neighbouring tracts. When the easement is drawn on the survey but not recorded correctly, or the road does not meet the county's standard for a building permit, the buyer cannot build or sell.

## Why It's Still Broken
Small subdividers close many tracts with minimal legal review, sometimes without title insurance on seller-financed sales, and the county's access requirement only bites when the buyer applies for a permit.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A pre-closing checklist per tract: recorded access easement matched to the survey, road width and surface against the county's permit standard, and an access endorsement or title commitment where available.

## Who Feels the Pain
Buyers of rural tracts, subdividers facing rescission claims, and county permit staff handling the dispute.

## Impact If Fixed
About an hour of checks per tract removes the most common and costly rural tract dispute, which can run ~$25–100K in rescission and legal costs (estimates).

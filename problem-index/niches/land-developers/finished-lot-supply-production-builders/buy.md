# Construction Scheduling Adapted to Horizontal Phases

**Niche:** [[niches/land-developers/finished-lot-supply-production-builders/profile|Finished-Lot Supply to Production Builders]]
**Industry:** [[industries/land-developers|Land Developers]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Scheduling tools built for buildings track tasks and trades; a lot developer needs lots, inspections, acceptance and utility installs tracked against a takedown contract.
**Tags:** #workflow-orchestration #data-integration #time-series-forecasting #evaluation-metrics #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to deliver finished lots into each builder's takedown schedule, phase by phase, and whoever delivers the highest share of contracted lots by the contract delivery date takes the account.

## The Problem
Superintendents and development managers keep phase schedules in spreadsheets or in P6 files nobody updates. Builders ask for lot-delivery dates by email. Management has no portfolio view of which phases will hit their takedown dates.

## What Already Exists
Procore, Autodesk Construction Cloud and Oracle Primavera P6 schedule and track construction work. Smartsheet and Microsoft Project are common in smaller developers. Drone platforms such as TraceAir report earthwork progress, and builders run takedown tracking in their own land systems.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs the lot as the unit of progress, not the task. It needs municipal milestones (inspection and test results, acceptance of public improvements, plat recording, performance bonds) and third-party dependencies (electric, gas and fibre design and install) modelled explicitly. Each phase's lots must link to the takedown contract's quantities and dates, and the builder needs a shared view. City and utility durations should come from history, not defaults.

## Target Customer
Development managers and superintendents at regional lot developers and MPCs who already license Procore or a scheduling tool.

## Impact If Solved
Extending a tool the developer already pays for to the horizontal workflow gives builders a reliable delivery view and puts every phase's takedown risk on one screen. It saves ~2–4 hours a week of status calls per superintendent (estimate).

# Finished-Lot Supply to Production Builders

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/land-developers|Land Developers]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to deliver finished lots into each builder's takedown schedule, phase by phase, and whoever delivers the highest share of contracted lots by the contract delivery date takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$9B a year of finished-lot sales to production builders (estimate, from the ~$16.8B of 2022 receipts in Census SUSB and Forestar's $1,544.3M of residential lot sales in fiscal 2025)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~55% of receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low-Medium — contracts and schedules live in spreadsheets, field progress in drone scans, photos and subcontractor reports
**Target Buyer:** VP of land development or division president at a lot developer
**Automation Potential:** Medium for delivery tracking and forecasting; low for the field work itself

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The developer's customer here is a production builder, and the product is a finished lot delivered by a date. Builders have moved toward land-light models that buy lots just in time on option instead of holding raw land: Lennar moved $5.5B of land and $1.0B of cash into Millrose Properties in 2025, and D.R. Horton bought 11,751 of Forestar's 14,240 lots in fiscal 2025 under a supply agreement. That moves the delivery risk onto the developer. A phase that is not paved, accepted, platted and energised by the takedown date leaves the builder without lots to start, and the developer carrying finished inventory it cannot bill. The contest is not the land deal or the price; it is hitting delivery dates across many phases at once.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Drone platforms (TraceAir, Propeller) measure earthwork and verify lot grades. Procore and spreadsheets track subcontracts and pay applications, and P6 or Microsoft Project hold schedules where anyone keeps one. Builders track takedowns in their own land systems. No tool forecasts delivery per lot from the real dependencies (earthwork, city inspections and acceptance, plat recording, and the electric, gas and fibre providers' installs) or ties that forecast to the takedown contract.

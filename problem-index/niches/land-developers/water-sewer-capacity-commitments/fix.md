# The Will-Serve Letter That Expires Before the Plat

**Niche:** [[niches/land-developers/water-sewer-capacity-commitments/profile|Water & Sewer Capacity Commitments]]
**Industry:** [[industries/land-developers|Land Developers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A capacity commitment with a 12- or 24-month expiry (estimate) lapses while the rezoning drags on, and the developer goes back to the end of the queue.
**Tags:** #workflow-orchestration #survival-analysis #large-language-models #quick-win #compliance
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to secure binding water and sewer service commitments for a tract's planned lots before the land closes, and whoever obtains binding commitments covering the highest share of planned lots before closing takes the account.

## The Problem
Will-serve letters and capacity reservations often expire unless the developer pays fees, records a plat or starts construction by a date. When entitlement or financing slips, the letter lapses, and capacity may by then be committed to someone else.

## Why It's Still Broken
The expiry sits in a letter in a project folder. The entitlement team tracks hearings, the engineering team tracks plans, and nobody tracks the utility commitment's conditions.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Extract each commitment's conditions and dates into the project record, link them to the entitlement and plat forecasts, and alert months ahead when a forecast date passes an expiry, with the extension options in the agreement.

## Who Feels the Pain
Land development VPs, utility engineers renegotiating lost capacity, and builders whose lots wait.

## Impact If Fixed
One tracked date per commitment prevents the rare but severe loss of capacity that can stall a whole community for a year, ~$1M of carry on ~$10M invested at ~10% a year (estimate).

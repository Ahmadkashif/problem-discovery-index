# Utility Hydraulic Models Opened for Developer Feasibility

**Niche:** [[niches/land-developers/water-sewer-capacity-commitments/profile|Water & Sewer Capacity Commitments]]
**Industry:** [[industries/land-developers|Land Developers]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Utilities already model their systems; a developer-facing service on those models would answer capacity questions in days rather than months.
**Tags:** #numerical-methods #optimization-fundamentals #workflow-orchestration #data-integration #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to secure binding water and sewer service commitments for a tract's planned lots before the land closes, and whoever obtains binding commitments covering the highest share of planned lots before closing takes the account.

## The Problem
Each developer capacity request makes the utility or its consultant run the hydraulic model for that tract, on the utility's schedule.

## What Already Exists
InfoWater, WaterGEMS and SewerGEMS hydraulic models, maintained by utilities and their engineering consultants; some cities publish capacity guidance or fee schedules.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs a request portal where a developer submits a tract, lot count and phasing; a standard model run that returns available capacity, required off-site improvements and their rough cost; a queue of commitments so capacity is not promised twice; and a public summary of headroom by area that the utility is willing to publish.

## Target Customer
Utility directors and their consulting engineers at fast-growing cities and private utilities facing a stream of developer requests; developers would pay through fees.

## Impact If Solved
Standard capacity answers in ~2–3 weeks instead of ~3–6 months (estimate) help the utility as much as the developer, and turn utility planning into a published constraint the land market can price.

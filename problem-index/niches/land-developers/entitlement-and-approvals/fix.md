# Approval Dates Promised to the Land Committee From Memory

**Niche:** [[niches/land-developers/entitlement-and-approvals/profile|Entitlement & Approvals]]
**Industry:** [[industries/land-developers|Land Developers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Approval dates in land-committee memos come from the entitlement manager's experience, and nobody records how far they slipped.
**Tags:** #survival-analysis #evaluation-metrics #large-language-models #quick-win #workflow-orchestration
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is "every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get a tract from contract to approved lots inside the purchase contract's feasibility and extension periods," but that fight splits two ways. Where the zoning must change, it is a public, political contest at planning commission and council, won by whoever gets the highest share of filed cases approved at the requested lot count. Where the land is already zoned, or sits in an unzoned county, the binding constraint is water and sewer, and the contest is won by whoever secures binding commitments covering the highest share of planned lots before closing. Different counterparties, consultants and clocks, so it is decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## The Problem
The land committee approves a purchase on a promised approval date, and the builder's takedown schedule is built on it. When the approval slips, the extension payment, carry and takedown penalties follow, and the lesson is not recorded.

## Why It's Still Broken
There is no field for the promised date that anyone checks later. The manager who made the promise moves on to the next case, and the project team that inherits it measures from the new date.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Record the promised approval date and its assumptions (hearing count, staff rounds, utility agreement) in the deal record at contract, and report each case's actual against it. Over a year that becomes a per-jurisdiction slippage table the next memo can use.

## Who Feels the Pain
Land committees, builders planning starts, and entitlement managers whose estimates are judged without a record.

## Impact If Fixed
A one-field habit turns approval dates from promises into calibrated estimates. A team filing ~30 cases a year (estimate) has a per-jurisdiction slippage table within ~12 months, and each slip priced in at contract avoids ~$0.25M of unplanned carry on a ~$5M tract held six months longer (estimate).

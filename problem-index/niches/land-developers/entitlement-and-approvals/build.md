# A Case Record Joining Every Approval to Its Timeline

**Niche:** [[niches/land-developers/entitlement-and-approvals/profile|Entitlement & Approvals]]
**Industry:** [[industries/land-developers|Land Developers]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A land team cannot say how long its last twenty approvals took or why, because the history is spread across city portals, agenda systems, consultants' emails and the entitlement manager's notes.

**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is "every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get a tract from contract to approved lots inside the purchase contract's feasibility and extension periods," but that fight splits two ways. Where the zoning must change, it is a public, political contest at planning commission and council, won by whoever gets the highest share of filed cases approved at the requested lot count. Where the land is already zoned, or sits in an unzoned county, the binding constraint is water and sewer, and the contest is won by whoever secures binding commitments covering the highest share of planned lots before closing. Different counterparties, consultants and clocks, so it is decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## The Problem
Each case generates a trail: pre-application notes, the application, staff comment letters by round, resubmittals, neighbourhood meeting notes, staff reports, agendas, minutes, votes, ordinances with conditions, and utility agreements. The developer needs that trail to forecast the next case and to defend its dates to the land committee and to builders.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The pieces sit in systems the developer does not own (city portals, agenda platforms), in documents consultants produce, and in people's heads. Each jurisdiction's process differs, and the market of land teams is fragmented.

## What to Build
A case record per tract, assembled automatically from city portals and agenda systems plus the team's own documents, with every step dated and every staff comment tracked to its resolution. On top of it: time-to-approval statistics by jurisdiction and case type, and a forecast for each live case. The two sub-niches below carry the specialised versions for hearings and for utility capacity.

## Target Customer
VPs of entitlements at regional developers and national builders' divisions running dozens of live cases.

## Impact If Built
An entitlement history the whole team can use turns approval dates into estimates with ranges and lets a new manager inherit a case list with its context. Each six-month slip that is priced at contract rather than discovered later is worth ~$0.25M on a tract carried at ~$5M and ~10% a year (estimate).

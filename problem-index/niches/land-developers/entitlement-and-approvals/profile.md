# Entitlement & Approvals

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/land-developers|Land Developers]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is "every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get a tract from contract to approved lots inside the purchase contract's feasibility and extension periods," but that fight splits two ways. Where the zoning must change, it is a public, political contest at planning commission and council, won by whoever gets the highest share of filed cases approved at the requested lot count. Where the land is already zoned, or sits in an unzoned county, the binding constraint is water and sewer, and the contest is won by whoever secures binding commitments covering the highest share of planned lots before closing. Different counterparties, consultants and clocks, so it is decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$2.5B a year of entitlement consultant, legal, study and in-house staff spend by land developers and builders' land teams (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~15% of receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — applications are filed on city portals, but case tracking, staff-comment responses and hearing preparation run on email, PDFs and memory
**Target Buyer:** VP of entitlements, or the land-use attorney who coordinates the case
**Automation Potential:** Medium for tracking, drafting and forecasting; low for the hearing itself

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Entitlement is the step that turns a tract's price from raw-land value into lot value, and it is where most land deals die or slip. It runs on public clocks the developer cannot set: application deadlines, staff review cycles, agenda posting, hearings, continuances, and in some places referenda. The purchase contract's feasibility and extension periods give the developer a fixed window, often with non-refundable extension payments (estimate). Every serious land team competes on getting through faster and with more of the plan intact. But the work divides by what the jurisdiction requires, which is why this niche decomposes.

## Current Tools & Gaps
City permitting portals (Accela, Tyler EnerGov and others) take applications. Legislative agenda systems (Legistar, Granicus, CivicClerk) publish agendas and minutes. Developers track cases in Smartsheet, Excel or a land system, and the land-use attorney keeps the history. Hamlet monitors meetings. The gap is a case record joined across all of it: filing, comments, hearings, vote, conditions, and the time each step took.

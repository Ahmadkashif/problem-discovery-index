# Master-Planned Communities

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/land-developers|Land Developers]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep its builders selling homes faster than the competing new-home communities around it, and whoever posts the most new-home sales a year in its submarket takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$4B a year of lot and commercial-parcel sales by master-planned community developers (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~25% of receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — MPC developers license market data and run builder programmes, but lot pricing and release pacing sit in spreadsheets
**Target Buyer:** MPC president or VP of builder sales
**Automation Potential:** Medium; pricing and release decisions can be modelled, builder relationships cannot

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A master-planned community sells lots to many builders over twenty or thirty years, alongside amenities, schools, commercial parcels, a homeowners' association and, in Texas and Florida, district debt. The developer's revenue is the lot price times the pace at which builders take lots, and builders take lots when their homes sell. So the MPC competes with every other new-home community in its submarket for buyers, through amenities, product mix, lot pricing and release timing, and it publishes its sales rank because builders choose communities by it. Large operators include Howard Hughes (Summerlin, The Woodlands, Bridgeland) and Lakewood Ranch (recalled). RCLCO's annual ranking of top-selling MPCs is the industry's scoreboard (recalled). Developer control of the HOA until turnover links this niche to [[industries/hoa-management|HOA Management]], and new-home demand research is profiled under Building Material Dealers at [[niches/building-material-dealers/building-products-demand-research/profile|Building-Products Channel & Housing Demand Research]].

## Current Tools & Gaps
Zonda (now part of CoStar Group) and John Burns supply competing-community sales and pricing. Builders report their sales to the MPC monthly. CRM and marketing tools run the buyer funnel. The gap is the decision the MPC owns: which lots, in which product, at what price, to which builder, released when, given what competing communities are selling this month.

# Builder Sales Reports That Arrive a Month Late

**Niche:** [[niches/land-developers/master-planned-communities/profile|Master-Planned Communities]]
**Industry:** [[industries/land-developers|Land Developers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** An MPC learns how its builders sold last month from emailed spreadsheets in different formats, weeks after a competitor's price cut has already taken buyers.
**Tags:** #data-integration #large-language-models #time-series-forecasting #quick-win #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep its builders selling homes faster than the competing new-home communities around it, and whoever posts the most new-home sales a year in its submarket takes the account.

## The Problem
Builders report contracts, cancellations, closings and traffic to the MPC monthly, each in its own format, usually by email. The MPC's marketing and sales staff retype them into a tracker. By the time a slowdown in one product band is visible, it has lasted a month.

## Why It's Still Broken
Builders are independent companies with their own systems and reasons to report late. MPCs have no standard and little leverage beyond the contract's reporting clause.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A standard weekly feed in the lot contract, accepted in any format and normalised automatically (spreadsheets and PDFs read by a language model into one schema), with traffic, contracts, cancellations and closings by product. Alerts when a builder's pace falls below its takedown requirement or a competitor's pace rises.

## Who Feels the Pain
MPC sales and marketing staff retyping reports; executives deciding releases on stale data; builders who later miss takedowns.

## Impact If Fixed
Weekly, comparable sales data cuts the lag on a slowdown from about a month to about a week (estimate) and removes the manual consolidation.

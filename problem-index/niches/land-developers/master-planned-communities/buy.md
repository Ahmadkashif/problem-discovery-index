# Revenue Management Adapted to Lot Releases

**Niche:** [[niches/land-developers/master-planned-communities/profile|Master-Planned Communities]]
**Industry:** [[industries/land-developers|Land Developers]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Revenue management software prices perishable inventory to consumers daily; an MPC prices lots to builders once a phase, and the logic has to change accordingly.
**Tags:** #time-series-forecasting #gradient-boosting #causal-inference #evaluation-metrics #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep its builders selling homes faster than the competing new-home communities around it, and whoever posts the most new-home sales a year in its submarket takes the account.

## The Problem
MPC lot pricing reacts slowly to the market. Builders' home prices and incentives move monthly, while lot prices are set per phase and escalated by formula, so the developer either lags the market or renegotiates.

## What Already Exists
Revenue management for apartments (Yardi Revenue IQ and similar) and hotels prices units daily from demand forecasts. Builders run their own home-price and incentive analytics. Zonda and John Burns provide competing-community data.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs: a buyer that is a builder with its own margin, not a consumer; contracts with base prices and escalators rather than daily prices; long cycles (phases, not nights); product mix as a lever (lot widths, price bands); builder mix (how many builders per product); and the MPC's amenity and district-assessment burden in the home buyer's price.

## Target Customer
VPs of builder sales and finance at large MPCs already licensing competitor data.

## Impact If Solved
Lot prices that follow the market on evidence reduce mid-phase renegotiations with builders and capture appreciation the escalator formula misses. Capturing 1% of lot price on ~500 lots a year is ~$0.5M a year at ~$108,400 a lot (estimate).

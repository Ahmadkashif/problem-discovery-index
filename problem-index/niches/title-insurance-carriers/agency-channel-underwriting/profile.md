# Agency-Channel Residential Underwriting

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/title-insurance-carriers|Title Insurance Carriers]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep independent agents writing residential files on its paper by answering their approval and insure-over requests inside the closing window on terms it can stand behind, and whoever approves fastest without the approvals turning into claims takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$400M a year of underwriting counsel, agency-support staff and agent-portal spend serving the residential agency channel (estimate; a slice of the ~$2.9B of industry personnel cost derived from NAIC's 16.1% of $18.2B operating expenses)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~2% of title premium (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — jackets, CPLs and rate quotes run through agent portals; approvals and bulletins run through phone, email and PDF manuals
**Target Buyer:** SVP of Agency Operations, Chief Underwriting Counsel
**Automation Potential:** High for routine approvals and manual lookups, low for novel insure-over risks

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Residential agency business is most of the industry's policy count: 12.5M residential policies in 2025, written mostly by agents who keep the larger part of each premium. Agents can and do move between underwriters, so what an underwriter sells them, beyond capacity and a split, is underwriting support. That means a manual they can follow, an authority limit that lets them close without calling, and an underwriting counsel who says yes on a Friday afternoon when the facts allow it. The underwriter keeps the claim if the yes was wrong. This is the carrier side of the agent's examination work described under [[industries/title-and-settlement-offices|Title & Settlement Offices]]: the agent examines the chain, and the underwriter decides what risk the agent may take on its behalf.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Underwriter portals (First American's AgentNet and its peers), state underwriting manuals and public bulletin libraries, and AI review of search packages arriving from the largest underwriter. The gaps: approval requests arrive unstructured, precedents live in counsel's inboxes, and approvals are not joined to the claims they produce, so no underwriter knows which categories of approval are safe to delegate to agents.

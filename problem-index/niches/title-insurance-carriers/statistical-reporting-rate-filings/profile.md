# Title Statistical Reporting & Rate Filings

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/title-insurance-carriers|Title Insurance Carriers]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn thousands of agents' production records into statistical-plan submissions and rate-filing exhibits that trace to source, and whoever's numbers the regulator and the opposing actuaries cannot impeach at the hearing takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$40M a year of actuarial, statistical reporting, filing and hearing spend across underwriters and their associations (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~0.2% of premium (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — data is digital but assembled from agents' extracts, spreadsheets and statutory statement software
**Target Buyer:** Chief Actuary, VP of Regulatory Affairs; executive director of a state land title association
**Automation Potential:** High for collection, coding and reconciliation; low for the contested actuarial judgement at a hearing

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Title rate regulation takes three forms. Texas promulgates rates after hearings in which TDI staff, the Texas Land Title Association and the Office of Public Insurance Counsel each present calculations built on statistical data from agents and underwriters. The February 2025 order cut rates 10% and went to appeal, and Order 2025-9697 cut them 6.2% from 1 March 2026. New York and Pennsylvania rates are filed by licensed rating bureaus, TIRSA and TIRBOP, whose manuals bind their members. Elsewhere underwriters file their own manuals. Everywhere, statutory annual statements require policy-year loss development and premium by state. The work is mechanical in collection and coding, which is why it sits on this axis, and contested at the end, where a few points of indicated rate change are worth tens of millions of dollars in Texas (estimate). The P&C counterpart is [[niches/property-casualty-insurers/statistical-reporting-data-calls/profile|⚡ Statistical Reporting & Regulatory Data Calls]].

## Current Tools & Gaps
Statutory statement software, actuarial reserving tools, spreadsheets, agents' production-system extracts and consulting actuaries. Gaps: inconsistent fee coding across agents' systems, agent expense data collected by survey, and exhibits that cannot be traced back to the underlying records when challenged.

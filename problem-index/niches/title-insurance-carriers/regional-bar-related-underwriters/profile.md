# Regional & Bar-Related Underwriters

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/title-insurance-carriers|Title Insurance Carriers]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep its independent agents and attorney members from moving to a national underwriter by matching national underwriting support, claims service and agent technology on a regional budget, and whoever keeps the most agents through a national underwriter's recruiting push takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$250M a year of staff, systems and outsourced actuarial, claims and audit spend at regional and bar-related underwriters (estimate), which write ~$1.7B of premium (derived: the ~9% of ALTA's $18.5B left after the four families' ~80% and the three national independents' 10.8%)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~1.4% of premium in spend (estimate); ~9% of premium written (derived)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — agent portals and production integrations exist, often licensed; analytics, claims and audit tooling are thin
**Target Buyer:** President or COO of a regional underwriter; executive director of a bar-related fund
**Automation Potential:** High, because these underwriters cannot staff the work any other way

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Four families write about four-fifths of US title premium, and three national independents (Westcor at 4.2%, Title Resources Guaranty at 4.0% and WFG at 2.6% in ALTA's 2025 ranking) write another tenth with national staffs of their own, so they are left out of this niche. The remaining ~9% is written by regional underwriters and by bar-related underwriters founded by real estate lawyers in attorney-closing states (estimate). These compete for the same independent agents with a fraction of the staff: a handful of underwriting counsel, a small claims team, one or two auditors. They carry the same regulatory load (statutory statements, rate filings, audits, claims-handling rules) and do not own the title plants or data science teams the nationals use. Vendors build for the four families, which can also build for themselves. The national independents just above them show where the competition is going: Doma's instant-underwriting engine now sits inside Title Resources Group, and Westcor joined Fannie Mae's title acceptance pilot in July 2025. The regionals have no comparable budget.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Licensed agent portals, production-system integrations and rate calculators, outsourced actuarial opinions and reinsurance. Gaps: no claims analytics, audit scheduled by calendar, approvals by phone, and no affordable way to give agents the instant decisions the nationals and the digital underwriters offer.

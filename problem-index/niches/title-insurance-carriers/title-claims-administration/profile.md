# Title Claims Administration & Litigation

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/title-insurance-carriers|Title Insurance Carriers]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is that every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to resolve title claims at the lowest total cost of loss and expense. That splits into two contests with different winners: deciding coverage and setting the first reserve correctly inside the statutory clock at intake, and curing or defending covered claims through counsel for less than paying them. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$810M of losses and loss adjustment expense incurred in 2025 (NAIC: $807.7M), of which claims staff, systems and legal spend are perhaps $250–350M (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~4% of premium (derived from NAIC)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — claims are reported by letter and email and worked in payment-oriented claim systems, with reasoning held in notes and correspondence
**Target Buyer:** Chief Claims Officer, Chief Claims Counsel
**Automation Potential:** Medium for intake, document assembly and precedent retrieval; low for coverage and litigation decisions

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Title claims are unlike other insurance claims. The insured event happened before the policy was issued, and the defect sits in the recorded history. The insurer can often fix the title instead of paying. Claims emerge slowly: First American says 65–75% of claim amounts are known within six years. The mix is shifting toward expensive fraud: Milliman's study for ALTA put fraud and forgery claims at over $143,000 on average against about $26,000 for other claims, and 21% of claim dollars. Claims work is done by lawyer-counsel in house, supported by panel law firms. The P&C equivalent, bodily-injury claim escalation, is profiled as [[niches/property-casualty-insurers/casualty-claims-litigation-reserves/profile|🔵 Casualty Claims Litigation & Reserve Adequacy]]; title differs in that cure is often the cheapest path and the evidence is documentary.

## Current Tools & Gaps
In-house and general claims platforms, document management, panel-counsel billing through legal spend tools, and recorded-document retrieval from the underwriters' plants. The gaps: no record that joins the intake read to the resolution path and its final cost, root cause coded inconsistently or not at all, and no feedback from claims to the underwriting approvals and agents that produced them.

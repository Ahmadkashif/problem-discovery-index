# Home Equity & Refinance Lenders

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/title-insurance-carriers|Title Insurance Carriers]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to become the lender's default title answer on low-risk refinance and home-equity loans, whether a limited policy, an attorney opinion letter or a GSE-accepted waiver priced from an automated decision, and whoever proves its automated decisions lose no more than a full policy at a fraction of the price takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.5B of refinance and home-equity title premium and property-report fees (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~8% of premium (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — lenders order by API and expect instant answers; most underwriters still examine a refinance like a purchase
**Target Buyer:** Head of home equity or settlement services at a lender; underwriter's head of lender products
**Automation Potential:** High, since the product is the automated decision

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A refinance or a home-equity line carries less title risk than a purchase, because the owner is not changing. The buyer is a lender that wants an answer in minutes at a low price, not a full examination. Many home-equity lenders take a property report or a limited-coverage product rather than a full lender's policy (estimate). The GSEs are pressing from the other side. Fannie Mae has accepted attorney opinion letters on limited loans since 2022 and reported buying more than 10,000 of them. In July 2025 FHFA added Westcor (with X1 Analytics) to Fannie's title acceptance pilot beside Doma: for refinances under 80% LTV from selected lenders, an automated review decides whether a policy or an attorney opinion letter is needed, and Fannie estimates $500–1,500 saved per loan. The full-policy product was not built for this audience, and the incumbents have defended it rather than redesigned it; 14 state attorneys general asked FHFA to end the pilot in 2024. The vendors behind the engines are logged as [[niches/title-insurance-carriers/instant-decision-underwriters/profile|🔍 Digital Title Underwriters & Instant Decision Engines]], and the alternative providers as [[niches/title-insurance-carriers/aol-title-waiver-providers/profile|🔍 Attorney Opinion Letter & Title Waiver Providers]].

## Current Tools & Gaps
Instant decision engines (Doma within Title Resources Group, WFG DecisionPoint, ANTIC REalSearch), limited-coverage and residential lender products from the large underwriters, property and O&E reports, and attorney opinion letter providers. Gaps: little published loss evidence on instantly decided files beyond vendor claims, and no common way for a lender to compare a waiver, an AOL and a limited policy on the same loan.

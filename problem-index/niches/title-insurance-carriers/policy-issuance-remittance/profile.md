# Policy Issuance, CPLs & Premium Remittance

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/title-insurance-carriers|Title Insurance Carriers]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to make every policy and closing protection letter an agent issues arrive reported, correctly rated and remitted without rekeying, and whoever closes the gap between CPLs issued and policies remitted fastest takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$300M a year of policy processing, remittance staff, agent-portal and integration spend across underwriters (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~1.6% of premium (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — larger agents issue jackets and CPLs through portals integrated with their production systems; reporting, rating checks and exceptions are still manual
**Target Buyer:** VP of Agency Operations, Controller
**Automation Potential:** Very high, because rating and matching are rule-bound

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
NAIC counted 13.6M title policies in 2025, up 8.0%, most issued by agents on the underwriter's paper. Each one starts as a CPL or commitment, becomes a policy at closing and must be reported with the underwriter's share of premium remitted. Every report is checked against a state rate manual with brackets, reissue credits, simultaneous-issue discounts and endorsement charges. The work is mechanical. Its failures are expensive, because an unreported policy becomes a claim on a risk the underwriter never booked, and a mis-rated premium becomes an audit or regulatory finding. Production software vendors on the agent side are profiled as [[niches/title-and-settlement-offices/production-software-vendors/profile|🔍 Title & Escrow Production Software Vendors]]; this niche is the underwriter's intake of what those systems produce.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Underwriter agent portals (AgentNet and peers), production-system integrations with SoftPro, Qualia and RamQuest, rate calculators and agency accounting systems. Gaps: reports from smaller agents still arrive as spreadsheets and PDFs, rate verification is partly manual, and CPL-to-policy matching runs as a periodic chase rather than continuously.

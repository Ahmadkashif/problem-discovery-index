# Agency Audit & Escrow Oversight

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/title-insurance-carriers|Title Insurance Carriers]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to find an agent's escrow shortage before the money is gone, and whoever detects shortages earliest per audit day spent takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$150M a year of underwriter audit staff, travel, monitoring tools and CPL and defalcation losses (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~0.8% of premium (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — audits are on-site or remote tick-and-tie against bank statements and the agent's own production system
**Target Buyer:** VP of Agency Risk, Chief Audit Executive
**Automation Potential:** High for reconciliation testing and risk ranking; low for the on-site judgement of a struggling agency

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The underwriter does not hold the escrow money, but it can end up owing it. Closing protection letters cover lenders and buyers against the closing agent's dishonesty, some states' defalcation statutes make the insurer directly liable, and First American's 10-K names agent defalcations among its risk factors. Oversight is therefore a core underwriter function. Agency managers appoint and keep agents, and field auditors reconcile escrow accounts and test disbursements. CPL issuance is the lever the underwriter pulls when an agent looks shaky. The Pass 2 view of these teams is [[niches/title-and-settlement-offices/underwriter-agency-audit-teams/profile|🔍 Underwriter Agency Audit & Agent Risk Teams]] (31, below threshold); this niche is the operational work itself. Wire-fraud verification at the closing table belongs to the agent and is profiled as [[niches/title-and-settlement-offices/wire-identity-verification-vendors/profile|🔍 Closing Wire & Seller Identity Verification Vendors]].

## Current Tools & Gaps
Audit programmes built on spreadsheets and the agent's reconciliation reports; escrow reconciliation services the agent may buy; lender vetting databases; ALTA Best Practices assessments. Gaps: audits scheduled by calendar rather than risk, the underwriter's own CPL and remittance data not used as warning signals, and audit findings stored as narrative.

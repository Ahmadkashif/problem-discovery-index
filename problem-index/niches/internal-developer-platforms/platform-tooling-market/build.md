# Most Platforms Are Built Rather Than Bought

**Niche:** [[niches/internal-developer-platforms/platform-tooling-market/profile|Platform Tooling Market]]
**Industry:** [[industries/internal-developer-platforms|Internal Developer Platforms]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A software market of this size in which most customers build rather than buy is telling the vendors something specific, and none of them has established what.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #k-means-clustering #logistic-regression #graph-theory #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #revenue-impact #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor here is fighting to be the tooling an organisation builds its platform on — and that contest is a portal decision in one market and an abstraction decision in the other, which is why this niche is not terminal and is decomposed below.

## The Problem
A platform team evaluates the available tooling and decides to build. Their reasons are specific: the portal framework would take two engineers to operate, the abstraction layer does not express a pattern they rely on, the catalogue's data model does not fit their service topology, and the commercial options assume an organisation shaped differently from theirs. They build something narrower that fits, spend three years maintaining it, and eventually reach the same problems the products were built to solve. The vendors record this as a lost deal with no reason attached, and the next organisation reaches the same decision for the same reasons.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Lost-to-build is recorded as no decision rather than as a competitive loss, so the reasons are not collected systematically. The build option is also genuinely rational in many cases — a platform is deeply shaped by an organisation's existing estate, and a product that assumes a different shape imposes a migration the organisation did not want. And the vendors compete with each other in the deals they are in, which is a small subset of the market, and the decisions they lose to building are made before an evaluation starts.

## What to Build
The layer both sub-niches need: understanding and reducing the build decision. Characterise the build decision properly by asking the organisations that made it, which is research nobody has conducted and would produce the requirement list the category is missing. Reduce the assumption load, since the most common build reason is that the product assumes a shape the organisation does not have — which argues for tooling that adapts to an existing estate rather than requiring one, and is a design principle rather than a feature. Make partial adoption viable, because an organisation that has built something will not replace it wholesale and will adopt a component, which means the products must be useful as parts rather than only as platforms. Measure the operational cost honestly and publish it, since the open framework's cost is the category's most consistent complaint and the commercial alternatives' claim rests on it being lower — a claim nobody has quantified. Address the actual reasons rather than the feature list, which requires knowing them. And give the organisations that built something a migration path that does not require abandoning it, since that is the population and they are currently unaddressable.

## Target Customer
Platform tooling vendors, and the platform teams currently building because nothing available fits.

## Impact If Built
A market where most customers build is telling the vendors something they have not collected, and lost-to-build is recorded as no decision rather than as a loss with a reason. Adapting to an existing estate rather than requiring a shape is the design principle the build decisions point at.

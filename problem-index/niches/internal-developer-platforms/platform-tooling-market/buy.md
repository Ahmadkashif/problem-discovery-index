# Total Cost of Ownership Nobody Computes

**Industry:** [[industries/internal-developer-platforms|Internal Developer Platforms]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Total cost of ownership analysis is standard procurement practice, and the build-or-buy decision for a platform is made on licence price against a build estimate that omits the maintenance entirely.

**Contested on:** Every serious competitor here is fighting to be the tooling an organisation builds its platform on — and that contest is a portal decision in one market and an abstraction decision in the other, which is why this niche is not terminal and is decomposed below.

## The Problem
Total cost of ownership methodology is ordinary procurement practice, applied routinely to infrastructure and enterprise software. The platform build-or-buy decision compares a licence cost against an internal build estimate that includes the initial construction and omits the operation, the maintenance, the upgrades, the on-call, the support burden and the opportunity cost of the engineers doing it — which are collectively far larger and are the reason these builds are regretted.

## What Already Exists
Total cost of ownership frameworks from procurement and technology business management; software maintenance cost research, which establishes that maintenance dominates construction; internal cost allocation practice; and the cost attribution capability from the cloud cost management industry in this vault. The methodology is entirely standard.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to internal engineering effort as the dominant cost. It requires: (1) the operating cost of the open framework quantified from real deployments rather than estimated, which is the single most contested number in the category and is measurable by surveying organisations running it — and is the evidence the commercial alternatives' entire pitch depends on; (2) maintenance modelled as a function of the estate's rate of change rather than as a fixed percentage, since a platform in a fast-changing organisation costs far more to maintain and that is the variable nobody includes; (3) the support burden included, since the platform engineer support niche describes a load that is a large share of the team's capacity and appears in no build estimate; (4) opportunity cost stated, because the engineers building the platform are not doing something else and that is the real comparison; and (5) an honest treatment of the build option's genuine advantages, since a narrower platform that fits is legitimately cheaper to operate than a general one that does not, and a total cost analysis that always favours buying will be recognised as a sales artefact.

## Target Customer
Platform tooling vendors, platform engineering leadership making this decision, and the technology business management functions who own cost comparison methodology.

## Impact If Solved
Standard procurement methodology is not applied to the category's defining decision, which is therefore made on licence price against an estimate that omits most of the cost. Quantifying the open framework's operating cost is the contested number the whole market argues about with no evidence.

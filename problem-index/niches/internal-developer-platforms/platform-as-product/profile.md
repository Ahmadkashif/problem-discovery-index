# Platform as Product

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/internal-developer-platforms|Internal Developer Platforms]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to make a platform team behave like a product team — measuring adoption, abandonment and what developers do instead — and whoever does that takes the platform account, because the category error explains most of its failures.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$520M US attributable to platform product management and adoption
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~26% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** None — the signals are available and nobody collects them
**Target Buyer:** Platform engineering leadership
**Automation Potential:** Very High — every product analytics signal exists in the platform's own systems

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
An internal platform team is a product team whose users sit in the same building and whose usage it does not measure. Every signal a consumer product company would consider essential — activation, funnel drop-off, feature usage, retention, support contact rate — is available from the platform's own systems and is almost never collected, because the team thinks of itself as building infrastructure. The consequences follow directly: paved paths are built without knowing what developers need, adoption stalls at the teams who were consulted, developers route around the platform for reasons nobody learns, and the platform team's response to poor adoption is usually more communication rather than a product change. This is the category's defining failure, it repeats at organisation after organisation, and it is correctable with instrumentation nobody has to invent.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Platform portals with basic page analytics, adoption counted as services onboarded, and occasional developer surveys. The gaps: adoption is measured as a count of services rather than as sustained use, so a team that onboarded and reverted looks the same as one that stayed; abandonment within a golden path is not instrumented, so nobody knows at which step developers give up; what developers do instead is invisible, although it is observable in their repositories and pipelines; the platform's own support channel has no metrics; and the team has no product management practice, which is a staffing and framing issue rather than a tooling one.

# Portals & Catalogues

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/internal-developer-platforms|Internal Developer Platforms]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor here is fighting to give an organisation a portal and catalogue that stays accurate without a team operating it — and whoever does that takes the decision, because operational cost and catalogue accuracy are the two reasons these deployments fail.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$430M US portal and service catalogue tooling
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~22% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Medium-High — widely adopted and widely regarded as expensive
**Target Buyer:** Platform teams weighing operational cost against building nothing
**Automation Potential:** Very High — catalogue population and freshness are both derivable

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The portal is the platform's visible surface and the catalogue is the data underneath it, and the two failures are related. The open framework established the pattern and is widely adopted and widely regarded as expensive to operate, which is the complaint the commercial alternatives exist to answer. The catalogue is the deeper problem: it is the foundation everything else depends on, it is populated by asking teams to maintain metadata, and it is accurate on the day it is written and never again. A portal built on a stale catalogue shows wrong owners, missing services and dead links, and developers stop trusting it — which is the specific mechanism by which these deployments become abandoned software rather than the general adoption problem. The contest is a portal that stays accurate without a team maintaining either it or its data.

## Current Tools & Gaps
The open portal framework with its plugin ecosystem, managed commercial portals with maturity scorecards, and service catalogues populated from metadata files. The gaps: catalogue population depends on humans maintaining files, which fails predictably; ownership goes stale as teams reorganise and nothing detects it; services that exist and are not in the catalogue are invisible by construction, which is the same shadow inventory problem as everywhere else in this vault; operating the open framework is the dominant complaint and is not quantified; and the portal's own usage is not measured, so nobody knows which parts of it anybody opens.

# Annotation Delivery Platforms

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/data-labeling-services|Data Labeling Services]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor here is fighting to be how annotation actually gets done — and that contest is a tooling problem for teams doing it themselves and a workforce problem for those buying a result, which is why this niche is not terminal and is decomposed below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.4B US annotation platforms and delivery infrastructure
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~35% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** High — the tooling is genuinely good within its established modalities
**Target Buyer:** In-house annotation teams; separately, labs buying a delivered dataset
**Automation Potential:** High — pre-labelling and workforce matching are both optimisable

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
This is how the work gets done: the interfaces annotators use, the task distribution, the review flow and the workforce behind it. The tooling is genuinely excellent for images, video, text spans and audio, having been refined over a decade, and it is thin for anything involving multi-step reasoning, tool use or comparison of long-form outputs — which is where the demand has moved.

The filter fails here. "Delivery platforms" names the product rather than a contest, and two different businesses sit underneath. Tooling is bought by a team annotating their own data, and the contest is whether a new task type can be supported without a solutions engineer building a bespoke interface first — a product extensibility problem. Workforce marketplaces are bought by a customer purchasing a delivered result, and the contest is sourcing, credentialing and retaining contributors who can actually do the work — an operations and matching problem. The competitors, the buyers and the economics differ completely. Decomposed below.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Mature annotation interfaces per modality, task routing and review workflow, model-assisted pre-labelling, and managed workforces of varying composition. The gaps: every genuinely new task type requires a bespoke interface, which is the tooling contest; pre-labelling anchors annotators toward the model's answer, which is a known effect and is not measured; task routing assigns by availability rather than by demonstrated capability on this task type; review is sampled uniformly rather than targeted where error is likely; and the platform's own data about who does well at what is not used to route.

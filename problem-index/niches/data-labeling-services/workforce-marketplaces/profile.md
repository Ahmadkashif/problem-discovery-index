# Workforce Marketplaces

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/data-labeling-services|Data Labeling Services]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor here is fighting to put the right capable contributor on the right task within days of a contract being signed — and whoever does that takes the delivery, because sourcing and matching at speed is now the binding constraint rather than labour supply.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$780M US workforce sourcing and managed delivery
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~20% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — sourcing is industrialised and matching is crude
**Target Buyer:** Laboratories and enterprises buying a delivered dataset
**Automation Potential:** Very High — capability signals exist and routing ignores them

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A customer buying a delivered dataset is buying a workforce they never see. The vendor's contest is entirely operational: source contributors with the required capability, verify that they have it, match them to the tasks they are actually good at, keep them long enough to become good, and do all of it within the weeks a contract allows. The constraint has shifted — for volume work the binding constraint was labour supply and for expert work it is verified capability at speed, which is a completely different sourcing problem. Retention has become equally consequential, because an expert contributor who leaves after two weeks costs more to replace than they delivered, and the industry's piece-rate structures were designed for a workforce it no longer primarily employs.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Contributor marketplaces with profile and test-based onboarding, qualification tests, reputation scores, and gig-work payment infrastructure. The gaps: qualification tests are built per project and measure a narrow proxy for capability; routing assigns by availability and by a coarse reputation score rather than by demonstrated performance on this task type, although the platform has the performance record; capability is treated as a static property rather than something that develops, so a contributor who is improving is routed as though they were not; retention is not modelled, so the most capable contributors leave for reasons nobody has examined; and the payment structure rewards speed on tasks where care is the requirement.

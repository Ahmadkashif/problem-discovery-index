# Annotation Corpus Intelligence

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/data-labeling-services|Data Labeling Services]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor that gets here is fighting to turn millions of annotation events with their outcomes into answers about who is reliable, what agreement is achievable and whether the data helped — and whoever does that holds the empirical basis for questions the whole field guesses at.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$280M US, largely latent
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~7% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** None — the corpus ships labels
**Target Buyer:** The vendors themselves; secondarily the laboratories buying data
**Automation Potential:** Very High — the corpus is large, structured and outcome-labelled

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The vendors hold something no customer and no academic group has: millions of annotation events with the annotator identity, the time taken, the revision history, the reviewer verdict, the eventual customer acceptance, and — occasionally — downstream model performance attributable to a specific data batch. That is the empirical basis for the questions the whole field guesses at: which annotators are actually reliable on which task types, how much agreement is achievable on a genuinely ambiguous task, whether a guideline change improved anything, and whether more annotators per item is worth the money. The category sells the output and analyses none of it. Every other niche in this industry depends on this one: the quality framework needs ability estimates, the routing needs capability profiles, the guideline work needs revision effects, and the pricing needs the relationship between effort and quality.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Per-project dashboards, agreement statistics, and contributor reputation scores. The gaps: the corpus is treated as operational exhaust rather than as a dataset; the downstream link — whether a batch improved the customer's model — is the only real quality validation and is occasionally available and never used; achievable agreement per task type is unknown, which leaves every project's quality target arbitrary; the effect of an additional annotator per item is a pure cost decision made without evidence despite the evidence existing; and no vendor has articulated a governance position, though the analysis concerns their own operations rather than customer content.

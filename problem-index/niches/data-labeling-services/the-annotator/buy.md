## The Problem
Designing a dispute process that is fast, fair and cheap is a studied problem: online marketplaces, payment platforms and gig platforms have all built them, and the procedural fairness literature establishes what makes a process acceptable to the person subject to it — explanation, a genuine opportunity to respond, consistency, and a decision-maker who is not the original one. Annotation rejection appeals have none of these properties and are described as an appeals process.

## What Already Exists
Platform dispute resolution systems with tiered escalation; the procedural justice literature, which identifies the specific properties that make a process acceptable; automated triage for disputes; consistency monitoring across decision-makers; and the gig economy's own accumulated practice, including its well-documented failures.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to disputes about expert judgement with small amounts at stake. It requires: (1) economic viability at low value, since a dispute over a small payment cannot support a substantial process — which argues for automated first-pass resolution on clear cases and human escalation only where the disagreement is real, and is the design constraint everything else follows from; (2) independence of the escalation, since the second opinion must come from somebody other than the original reviewer and the pool is frequently small, which the low-resource niche makes acute; (3) recognising that both parties may be right, because on genuinely ambiguous items the contributor and the reviewer can both be defensible and a binary overturn model does not fit — the correct outcome is frequently to pay and reclassify the item as ambiguous; (4) consistency monitoring across reviewers, which the procedural literature identifies as central to perceived fairness and which is measurable here and unmeasured; and (5) transparency of outcomes, since a published overturn rate is what tells contributors the process is real and its absence is why they do not use it.

## Target Customer
Annotation marketplaces and delivery organisations, the platform dispute resolution vendors, and the contributor communities whose experience this determines.

## Impact If Solved
An established practice with a clear account of what makes a process acceptable exists and is not applied, in a setting where the affected parties are the business's scarce resource. Automated resolution of clear cases is what makes a real process affordable at small amounts, and consistency monitoring is both measurable and central to whether the process is believed.

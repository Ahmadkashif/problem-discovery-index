# The Qualification Test That Circulates

**Niche:** [[niches/data-labeling-services/expert-credential-verification/profile|Expert Credential Verification]]
**Industry:** [[industries/data-labeling-services|Data Labeling Services]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A fixed qualification test is used for months, its questions and answers circulate in contributor communities within weeks, and nobody monitors whether the pass rate has become meaningless.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #change-point-detection #hypothesis-testing #confidence-intervals #evaluation-metrics #compliance #quick-win #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor here is fighting to establish that a contributor can actually do the work, at the speed a contract demands — and whoever does that takes the expert tier, because identity verification is commodity and capability verification is the constraint.

## The Problem
A qualification test of twenty fixed items is administered to every applicant for six months. Contributors discuss it in the communities they use to share work opportunities, and the answers circulate. The pass rate rises steadily, which is read as improving applicant quality. The correlation between passing and subsequently producing accepted work falls to nothing, which nobody measures. The vendor continues to describe its contributors as qualified, and the customer continues to pay for a verification that has stopped verifying.

## Why It's Still Broken
The test is fixed because building one was already an unplanned effort and building a bank of them is a larger one. Exposure is invisible without monitoring the pass rate against outcomes, which nobody does. The rising pass rate is a comfortable statistic. And the contributor communities that share the answers are outside the vendor's view and are a normal feature of any gig workforce, not a conspiracy.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Monitor exposure and rotate. Track the pass rate over time and the correlation between passing and subsequent delivery quality, which is the direct measure of whether the test still works and is a straightforward analysis over data the vendor holds — a rising pass rate with a falling correlation is compromise and is unmistakable. Build an item bank rather than a fixed test, drawing each administration from a pool, which is standard practice in assessment and slows exposure enormously. Retire items on exposure evidence — an item whose success rate rises sharply is circulating — which is detectable per item and is the mechanism that keeps the bank current. Use work samples with fresh material, since a task drawn from the live project cannot be circulated in advance and is the strongest instrument anyway. Verify with a live component where the stakes justify it, since a short supervised exercise is much harder to outsource or look up than an asynchronous test. Re-verify periodically rather than only at onboarding, since capability and identity can both drift. And treat compromise as expected rather than as misconduct, because designing for it is the only approach that works and treating it as a contributor integrity problem misdirects the response.

## Who Feels the Pain
Customers paying for qualification that no longer qualifies; capable contributors competing against people who obtained the answers; and delivery organisations whose quality problems originate in a screening step that stopped working months ago.

## Impact If Fixed
Tracking the pass rate against subsequent delivery quality detects compromise directly and is a query over existing data. An item bank with exposure-based retirement is standard assessment practice and slows the compromise enormously at modest cost.

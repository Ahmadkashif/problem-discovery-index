# Expert Credential Verification

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/data-labeling-services|Data Labeling Services]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor here is fighting to establish that a contributor can actually do the work, at the speed a contract demands — and whoever does that takes the expert tier, because identity verification is commodity and capability verification is the constraint.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$380M US attributable to expert sourcing, screening and capability verification
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~10% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Very Low — screening establishes who somebody is, not what they can do
**Target Buyer:** Vendors staffing expert contracts; ultimately the labs who require them
**Automation Potential:** High — capability assessment is designable and is currently ad hoc

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Identity verification and background screening are commodity services, and none of them can tell you whether this person can actually do organic chemistry — which is the only question that matters when a contract requires four hundred chemists next week. The distinction is sharp and consequential: a verified identity, a claimed degree and a plausible employment history are all obtainable in minutes and are jointly almost uninformative about whether somebody will produce assessments an expert reviewer accepts. The industry's answer is a qualification test built per project, which is a small unvalidated instrument constructed under time pressure by somebody who is not an assessment designer. The consequence is that a meaningful share of onboarded experts turn out to be unable to do the work, which is discovered in week two, at which point the contract is already late.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Identity verification and background screening services, credential lookup where registries exist, per-project qualification tests, and reference checking. The gaps: credential verification establishes a claim about the past and capability is about performance now; qualification tests are built per project, are short, are unvalidated, and are frequently answerable by somebody who cannot do the actual work; the tests are also leaked and shared, which nobody monitors; capability in one sub-domain does not transfer to another and the screening does not distinguish; and nothing accumulates — an expert verified for one project is re-screened for the next.

# The Notice of Sale the Small Lender Never Sent

**Niche:** [[niches/repossession-services/direct-lender-clients/profile|Credit Union, Community Bank & Buy-Here-Pay-Here Clients]]
**Industry:** [[industries/repossession-services|Repossession Agencies]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The agency recovers the car and delivers it to auction on the lender's instruction, the lender forgets the notice of intent to sell, and the deficiency — and the relationship — is lost.
**Tags:** #decision-trees #large-language-models #compliance #workflow-orchestration #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to be the recovery department a credit union, community bank or buy-here-pay-here dealer does not have, and whoever runs the file from assignment to sale — notices, property, redemption and auction delivery — so the lender never has to touch it takes the account.

## The Problem
Most states require a secured party to send the borrower notice before disposing of repossessed collateral, with prescribed content and timing, and a defective notice can bar the deficiency and invite statutory damages. Large lenders calendar this; a small lender may not, and the agency, which knows the recovery date and the auction date, watches the car sell without the step having happened.

## Why It's Still Broken
The notice is the lender's obligation, so agencies stay out of it; the small lender assumes the agency or the auction will remind it.

## What a Fix Looks Like
The agency's system generates the state's post-recovery timeline for each direct-client file on the day of recovery and holds auction delivery until the lender confirms the notice went out — a reminder and a gate, not legal advice — with a draft notice in the state's form for the lender to send.

## Who Feels the Pain
Credit union and dealer staff who lose deficiencies; borrowers who never get their redemption window; agencies whose clients blame them.

## Impact If Fixed
Fewer barred deficiencies at small lenders, and a reason for the lender to stay with the agency that caught it.

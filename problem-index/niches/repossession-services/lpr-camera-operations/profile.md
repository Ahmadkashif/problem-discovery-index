# Licence-Plate Camera Operations & Hit Conversion

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/repossession-services|Repossession Agencies]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn camera hours into confirmed hits on the hot list before the car moves, and whoever converts the most scans into verified, recoverable hits per driving hour takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$150–250M a year in camera hardware and subscriptions, camera-car driver pay and fuel at agencies (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~10–15% of agency cost base (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** High — scans, hot lists and hit alerts are fully digital; route choice and hit confirmation are manual
**Target Buyer:** Agency owner or camera-fleet manager; the camera networks' affiliate programmes
**Automation Potential:** High — route planning and visual hit confirmation are model problems

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Licence-plate recognition is now how much of the industry finds cars. Agencies buy camera kits (DRN lists ~$13,000 for two cameras and ~$16,000 for four) or subscribe to lighter services, mount them on trucks or ordinary cars, and drive apartment complexes, employer lots and streets at night. Every plate read is checked against hot lists of vehicles up for repossession; a match alerts the driver, who confirms the vehicle by eye, and the reporting shows mismatched alerts being rejected. DRN, part of Motorola Solutions since 2019, claims 4.6M+ vehicles recovered through its network. For an agency the contest is efficiency: which streets at which hours yield hot-list cars, how fast a hit becomes a truck at the car, and how few false confirmations slip through. Competitors are DRN and its affiliate agencies, MVTRAC, subscription services such as RecoveryNet, agencies' own camera fleets, and forwarders that steer assignments to camera-equipped agencies.

## Current Tools & Gaps
DRN and MVTRAC camera apps and hit alerts; Clearplan for dispatch after a hit. Gaps: routes planned by habit; agencies see only their own hits, not where scan coverage is thin; visual confirmation is the driver's glance at a small image at night.

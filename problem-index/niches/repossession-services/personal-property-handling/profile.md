# Personal Property Inventory & Return

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/repossession-services|Repossession Agencies]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to inventory and return a borrower's belongings inside each state's clock with a record that ends the complaint, and whoever produces the complete, timestamped property file fastest takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$40–80M a year in property handling fees, storage space and clerk time (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~3–5% of agency revenue, and a far larger share of complaints (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — carbon-copy inventory forms, labelled bags, phone appointments
**Target Buyer:** Agency office or lot manager; forwarder compliance staff
**Automation Potential:** Medium-High for inventory drafting and notices, low for the handling itself

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A recovered car usually contains things that are not collateral: child seats, work tools, medication, documents, laptops, sometimes cash or a firearm. Each state sets its own rules. California's BSIS guidance calls for an inventory to the debtor within ~48 hours and storage for at least 60 days; Florida treats failing to safeguard and inventory property as a prohibited act for licensed agencies; the CFPB has said that holding belongings until a fee is paid is unfair regardless of contract terms. Lenders and forwarders add release-appointment, photo and fee rules. The work is low-value per car and high-risk per complaint, which is why it has its own contest: the agency that can show exactly what was in the car, when the borrower was told, and when it was collected ends the dispute. Competitors are agencies, RDN's Cleardata module, forwarders' property desks and general inspection apps.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Cleardata for agencies on RDN; SafetyCulture-style checklist apps; paper forms. Gaps: inventories written by hand from memory of what was photographed; state deadlines tracked by the office; regulated items with no procedure; release signatures on paper.

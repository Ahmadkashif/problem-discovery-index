# Storage Days Nobody Authorised

**Niche:** [[niches/repossession-services/post-recovery-storage-transport/profile|Post-Recovery Storage, Transport & Release]]
**Industry:** [[industries/repossession-services|Repossession Agencies]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A recovered car waits twelve days for a transport order the lender says it sent on day three, and the storage invoice becomes an argument the agency cannot document.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #data-integration #workflow-orchestration #evaluation-metrics #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn a recovered car into a redeemed or auction-delivered unit with the fewest days on the lot and no damage or storage-fee dispute, and whoever clears units fastest with a documented chain of custody takes the account.

## The Problem
Storage is billed per day, and client schedules cap the days they will pay or require the agency to request instructions within a set time. When an order arrives late, sits unread in a portal, or the carrier no-shows, the days accrue and the client disputes them.

## Why It's Still Broken
Requests and orders travel through portals, email and phone; the agency's lot log and the client's system disagree about dates; nothing timestamps who was waiting on whom.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Log every storage-relevant event on the unit's record as it happens — recovery, instruction requested, order received, carrier booked, carrier arrived — drawing from portal messages and emails automatically, and generate the storage invoice from that timeline with the client's cap applied.

## Who Feels the Pain
Agency owners writing off storage; lot managers chasing orders; lender coordinators reconciling invoices.

## Impact If Fixed
Storage invoices paid at first submission, and a timeline that shows the client exactly which days were theirs.

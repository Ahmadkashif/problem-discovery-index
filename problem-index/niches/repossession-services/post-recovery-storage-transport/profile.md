# Post-Recovery Storage, Transport & Release

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/repossession-services|Repossession Agencies]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn a recovered car into a redeemed or auction-delivered unit with the fewest days on the lot and no damage or storage-fee dispute, and whoever clears units fastest with a documented chain of custody takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$300–500M a year in storage, transport, key, redemption-release and condition-report fees (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~20–25% of agency revenue (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low-Medium — transport orders and releases run through portals and phone calls; the lot itself is a key board and a clipboard
**Target Buyer:** Agency owner or lot manager; lender remarketing coordinators who choose where cars go
**Automation Potential:** Medium — scheduling, fee calculation and release paperwork are rule-bound

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Once the car is recovered, the agency becomes its custodian. It must store it securely, photograph and document it, release it to a borrower who redeems or reinstates under state law, or deliver it to the auction the lender chooses — often an OPENLANE, Manheim or ACV location or a lender's transport carrier — and bill daily storage until it leaves. Storage and transport are a large share of what agencies earn beyond the recovery fee, and the place where fee disputes cluster: who authorised the extra days, whether the car left the lot in the condition recorded, and whether the redemption release followed the lender's instructions. The lender's choice of channel is analysed under [[niches/sales-finance-companies/repossession-and-remarketing/profile|Repossession, Remarketing & Deficiency]]; the auctions themselves belong to [[industries/wholesale-auto-auctions|Wholesale Auto Auctions]]. Competitors are agencies with secure lots, regional transport carriers, auction-run recovery and transport services, and forwarders that control transport orders.

## Current Tools & Gaps
RDN's Cleardata schedules transports and debtor appointments; towing yard software (TOPS, Towbook) tracks impound stock; auctions issue their own arrival condition reports. Gaps: no forecast of when each unit will leave; storage days accrue while a release or transport order waits in a portal; condition at check-in and at hand-off are recorded by different parties and rarely reconciled.

# Cores Rejected Months After They Shipped

**Niche:** [[niches/auto-parts-distributors/core-and-warranty-credits/profile|Core Returns & Warranty Credit Recovery]]
**Industry:** [[industries/auto-parts-distributors|Auto Parts Distributors & Stores]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A store credits the shop for a core on the spot, and learns months later that the supplier rejected it as cracked, incomplete or the wrong family, by which time the shop's credit cannot be reversed.
**Tags:** #cnns #transfer-learning #logistic-regression #feature-engineering #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn every core and warranty return into the credit the supplier owes, and whoever recovers the highest share of the credits a distributor is owed takes the account.

## The Problem
Counter staff accept cores in seconds while the next customer waits. They check that it looks like the right unit; they don't check the casting number against the supplier's core family or look for the crack that voids credit. The rejection comes back on a supplier report long after the shop was credited.

## Why It's Still Broken
Acceptance rules sit in supplier programme documents nobody reads at the counter, and rejections arrive in lump reports that aren't traced back to the store or the person who accepted the unit.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A counter check on a phone or tablet: scan the part number or photograph the casting, confirm the core family for the supplier, and flag visible damage before credit is given. Rejections are traced back to the store, the counter person and the shop, so training and shop conversations target the real sources.

## Who Feels the Pain
Store managers whose shrink includes rejected cores; returns clerks reconciling rejections; controllers writing them off.

## Impact If Fixed
If supplier core rejections fell from ~4% to ~1.5% of units returned (estimate), a distributor returning ~100,000 cores a year at ~$40 average credit (estimate) would keep ~$100K a year.

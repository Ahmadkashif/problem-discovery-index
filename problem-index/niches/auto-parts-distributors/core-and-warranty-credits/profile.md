# Core Returns & Warranty Credit Recovery

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/auto-parts-distributors|Auto Parts Distributors & Stores]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn every core and warranty return into the credit the supplier owes, and whoever recovers the highest share of the credits a distributor is owed takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$4–6B a year of core and warranty credits passing back through U.S. parts distributors and stores (estimate); ~2–5% of it never recovered (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~1.3% of the three codes' receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — paper core tags, a returns cage, supplier portals and spreadsheets
**Target Buyer:** CFO or controller at a warehouse distributor; director of returns at a chain
**Automation Potential:** High — matching, eligibility checks and claim chasing are rule- and document-driven

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Cores make auto parts unusual among distributed goods: a large share of hard parts are sold with a deposit on the old unit, and the old unit flows back up the chain to a remanufacturer (AutoZone's 10-K describes the charge and credit at the point of sale). Warranty returns, defect claims and supplier credits ride the same reverse flow. The money is the distributor's, owed by suppliers, and it is at risk when a supplier is under stress: Advance took a $28M charge in 2025 on receivables from a supplier in Chapter 11. The contest is among the tools and services a distributor could use (ERP return modules, reverse-logistics providers, deduction and recovery services) on one outcome: recovery rate. The closest analogues elsewhere in the vault are [[niches/plumbing-and-hvac-distributors/warranty-claims-returns/profile|Warranty Claims & Defective-Part Returns]] and [[niches/industrial-distributors/rebates-and-spa-claims/profile|Supplier Rebates & SPA Claims]]; cores add a physical unit with an acceptance test.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Business systems log returns and issue credit memos; remanufacturers run acceptance portals; reverse-logistics providers move the units. Nobody tracks a core at unit level from the counter tag to the supplier's credit, splits lump credits back to units, or warns before a core ages out of eligibility.

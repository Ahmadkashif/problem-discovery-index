# Account Health Scoring Adapted to Shop Share of Wallet

**Niche:** [[niches/auto-parts-distributors/commercial-shop-supply/profile|Commercial Parts Supply to Independent Repair Shops]]
**Industry:** [[industries/auto-parts-distributors|Auto Parts Distributors & Stores]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Customer-success platforms already score B2B account health; they need parts-specific signals to see a repair shop moving its first call.
**Tags:** #gradient-boosting #survival-analysis #logistic-regression #feature-engineering #revenue-impact #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to be the independent shop's first call for the part a car on the lift needs, and whoever fills the largest share of the shop's first requests with the correct part takes the account.

## The Problem
Outside reps and commercial managers learn that a shop is drifting from monthly sales reports, weeks after the shop decided. The early signs exist in the data the store already has, but nobody assembles them per shop.

## What Already Exists
Customer-success and CRM platforms such as Salesforce and Gainsight score account health from usage, tickets and renewals, and churn models are standard in subscription businesses. Distributor business systems produce sales-by-customer reports.

## The Customization Gap
A repair shop does not churn; it reallocates. The useful signal is share of the shop's spend by category against its own history (brakes steady, steering gone), together with parts-specific causes: wrong-part returns on comeback jobs, lookups that found no stock, late deliveries, price-matrix changes, a new service writer. The model needs category-level baselines per shop, seasonality, and a cause attribution a rep can act on, delivered as a weekly visit list rather than a dashboard.

## Target Customer
Commercial sales managers and outside reps at chains, warehouse distributors and larger jobbers.

## Impact If Solved
Catching a drifting shop ~4–6 weeks earlier (estimate) and retaining ~2 more accounts per rep a year at ~$40K each (estimate) is ~$80K of retained sales per rep.

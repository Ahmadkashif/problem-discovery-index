# Commercial Parts Supply to Independent Repair Shops

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/auto-parts-distributors|Auto Parts Distributors & Stores]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to be the independent shop's first call for the part a car on the lift needs, and whoever fills the largest share of the shop's first requests with the correct part takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$70B a year of parts sold to repair shops through chains' commercial programmes, NAPA, warehouse distributors, jobbers and import specialists (estimate); the four public chains account for ~$26B of it (AutoZone ~$5.7B domestic commercial, O'Reilly $8.65B professional, Advance ~$4.3B, NAPA ~$7.6B; derived from their filings)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~19% of the three codes' receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — shops order through AutoZonePro, OReillyPro, Nexpart and PartsTech as well as by phone, but the supplier never sees the requests it lost
**Target Buyer:** VP of commercial sales or regional commercial director
**Automation Potential:** Medium — ordering, matching and dispatch automate; inventory near the bay and the shop relationship do not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The buyer is the independent repair shop's service writer or owner ([[industries/auto-repair-shops|Auto Repair Shops]]), and the shop keeps a short list of suppliers, ranked. The first call goes to whoever has most often had the right part close by; the others get what the first call couldn't fill. Every competitor fights for that rank: AutoZone runs commercial programmes in 6,443 U.S. stores and grew domestic commercial sales 10.6% in fiscal 2026; O'Reilly sells about half its volume to professionals; NAPA's automotive sales are ~80% do-it-for-me; warehouse distributors, jobbers and import specialists hold the shops the chains don't; and franchised dealers sell OEM parts wholesale ([[industries/franchised-car-dealers|Franchised Car Dealers]]). Delivery speed matters, but it is a means; the outcome the shop remembers is whether the right part came on the first try.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Shop ordering sites, e-catalog networks (Nexpart claims 370,000 active buyers, search summary; PartsTech connects 30,000+ distributors) and shop management integrations carry a growing share of orders, and the chains' hub growth (172 AutoZone mega hubs, 399 O'Reilly hubs) brings stock closer. The gap is measurement. A supplier sees what it sold, not the lookup that found no stock, the e-catalog quote another supplier won, or the wrong part that came back, so no one can state its first-call fill rate per shop.

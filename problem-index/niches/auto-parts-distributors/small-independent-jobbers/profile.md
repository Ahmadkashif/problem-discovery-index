# Small Independent Jobbers & Programme-Group Stores

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/auto-parts-distributors|Auto Parts Distributors & Stores]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep the jobber's best shop accounts from moving their first call to a chain hub, and whoever keeps the largest share of its top accounts' parts spend year on year takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$33.3B of 2022 receipts at parts firms with fewer than 20 employees: 13,964 parts-store firms with $16.8B and 5,898 parts-wholesale firms with $16.5B (Census SUSB 2022, 441310 and 423120)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~9% of the three codes' receipts
**Digital Adoption:** Low — one business system, Nexpart or PartsTech for shop orders, and the owner's phone
**Target Buyer:** Owner-operator of an independent store or small warehouse distributor
**Automation Potential:** Medium — ordering, assortment and account monitoring can be delivered as services; the owner's relationships carry the business

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Most parts firms are small and most belong to a programme: Auto Value and Bumper to Bumper stores under the Aftermarket Auto Parts Alliance ("over 50 shareholders"), members of Automotive Parts Associates (~60 warehouse distributors, search summary), Federated and Pronto stores, 809 independently owned Carquest stores served by Advance, and independent NAPA owners, many of whom Genuine Parts is now buying (50+ acquisitions in 2025, mostly independent NAPA stores). They compete with chain hubs that carry ~63,000–110,000 SKUs a few miles away. What they have is the owner's relationship with twenty or thirty core shops and knowledge of the local trade; what they lack is any view of how much of each shop's spend they still hold. AI vendors' integration work and pricing are aimed at the chains and the large distributors, so these firms get last year's tools through their programme group, if at all.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Programme groups supply the catalog, the business-system relationship, marketing and supplier programmes; e-catalog networks bring shop orders. The gaps: no share-of-wallet view by shop, assortment set from the group's recommendation rather than the local parc, and no visibility into fellow members' shelves in the same metro.

# Share-of-Wallet Tracking From the Jobber's Own Records

**Niche:** [[niches/auto-parts-distributors/small-independent-jobbers/profile|Small Independent Jobbers & Programme-Group Stores]]
**Industry:** [[industries/auto-parts-distributors|Auto Parts Distributors & Stores]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A jobber owner knows which shops buy from them; nothing tells them how much of each shop's work they still get, so the chain hub takes it a category at a time.
**Tags:** #bayesian-inference #gradient-boosting #survival-analysis #feature-engineering #revenue-impact #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep the jobber's best shop accounts from moving their first call to a chain hub, and whoever keeps the largest share of its top accounts' parts spend year on year takes the account.

## The Problem
A small store's top 20–30 shops are most of its commercial sales. When a chain hub opens nearby, shops rarely leave outright; they move brakes, then steering, then filters. The owner notices when a monthly total falls, too late to win back the categories already gone.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The store sees only its own sales, so it cannot observe a shop's total spend directly, and small firms have no analyst to build estimates. Programme groups hold pooled data but use it for supplier programmes, not member account health.

## What to Build
An estimate of each shop's expected parts spend by category from its bay count, the vehicles it services (from the VINs and vehicles on its orders) and its own past peaks, compared with what the store sells it; plus early-warning signals from lookups that found no stock, returns and late deliveries. Delivered as a weekly one-page list for the owner, inside the business system or by text.

## Target Customer
Owners of independent stores and small warehouse distributors, sold through programme groups or business-system vendors.

## Impact If Built
If an owner kept one more top shop a year worth ~$60K in parts (estimate), at a ~35% gross margin (estimate) that is ~$21K, often more than the firm's annual software spend.

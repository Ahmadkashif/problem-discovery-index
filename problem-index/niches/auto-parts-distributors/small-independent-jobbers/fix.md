# Member Stores Can't See Each Other's Shelves

**Niche:** [[niches/auto-parts-distributors/small-independent-jobbers/profile|Small Independent Jobbers & Programme-Group Stores]]
**Industry:** [[industries/auto-parts-distributors|Auto Parts Distributors & Stores]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Three independent stores in one city, all in the same programme group, together hold a chain hub's assortment, but none can see what the others have on the shelf.
**Tags:** #optimization-fundamentals #graph-theory #data-integration #workflow-orchestration #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep the jobber's best shop accounts from moving their first call to a chain hub, and whoever keeps the largest share of its top accounts' parts spend year on year takes the account.

## The Problem
When a jobber lacks a part, the counter checks its warehouse distributor's next delivery and tells the shop "this afternoon." A fellow member store ten minutes away may have it on the shelf, but there is no shared view, so the shop calls the chain hub instead.

## Why It's Still Broken
Members compete for the same shops, run different business systems, and fear exposing their customers and margins to each other. Groups have not built a neutral exchange with rules everyone trusts.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A neutral inventory-visibility service run by the group or warehouse distributor: members publish on-hand stock for chosen lines (not customers or costs), a lookup shows nearby member stock, and transfers settle at an agreed price with a small fee. Each member sees only availability, never the other's shop data.

## Who Feels the Pain
Jobber counter staff who lose the call; shop service writers waiting on an afternoon delivery; owners watching the chain hub take categories.

## Impact If Fixed
If member stores filled ~5% more of their no-stock lookups from a neighbour (estimate), a store writing ~150 commercial lines a day would keep ~1–2 more orders daily, and the shop that would have called the hub.

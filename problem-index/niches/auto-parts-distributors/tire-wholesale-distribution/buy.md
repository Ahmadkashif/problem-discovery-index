# Allocation Planning Adapted to Brand-Authorisation Shocks

**Niche:** [[niches/auto-parts-distributors/tire-wholesale-distribution/profile|Replacement Tire Wholesale Distribution]]
**Industry:** [[industries/auto-parts-distributors|Auto Parts Distributors & Stores]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Supply-chain planning suites already rebalance inventory across warehouses; they need tire-specific logic for the day a manufacturer pulls its brand.
**Tags:** #optimization-fundamentals #time-series-forecasting #bayesian-inference #feature-engineering #workflow-orchestration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep independent tire dealers' size-and-brand orders filled from the nearest warehouse, and whoever fills the largest share of a dealer's tire orders as ordered, without substitution, takes the account.

## The Problem
When a manufacturer ends a wholesaler's authorisation, as Michelin did with ATD from July 2025 (search summary), the wholesaler must re-plan its whole assortment: which brands replace the lost sizes, which warehouses get them, and how to tell dealers. Seasonal changeovers and tariff-driven price moves cause smaller versions of the same shock every year.

## What Already Exists
Supply-chain planning suites (Blue Yonder, Kinaxis, o9 and similar) forecast and rebalance inventory across a network, and warehouse systems execute transfers.

## The Customization Gap
Tires need equivalence logic the suites don't carry: which brands and lines a dealer will accept in place of another by size, load index, speed rating, tread category and price tier; dealer-specific brand commitments; manufacturer allocation caps and programme volume tiers; and seasonal sizes that must be in place before the first frost. The plan has to be expressed as dealer-facing substitution offers, not just warehouse moves.

## Target Customer
Planning and category leaders at tire wholesalers and parts distributors with tire programmes.

## Impact If Solved
Cutting the recovery from a lost brand from ~6 months to ~2 (estimate) would protect several points of as-ordered fill rate across a season, worth ~1–2% of annual sales.

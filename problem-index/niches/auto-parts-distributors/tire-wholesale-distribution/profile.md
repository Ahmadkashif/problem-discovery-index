# Replacement Tire Wholesale Distribution

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/auto-parts-distributors|Auto Parts Distributors & Stores]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep independent tire dealers' size-and-brand orders filled from the nearest warehouse, and whoever fills the largest share of a dealer's tire orders as ordered, without substitution, takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** $58.1B of 2022 receipts in NAICS 423130, Tire and Tube Merchant Wholesalers (Census SUSB 2022; includes tire makers' own sales branches); 50 enterprises with 500+ employees took ~72%
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~16% of the three codes' receipts
**Digital Adoption:** Medium-High — dealers order through wholesaler portals and e-catalog networks (PartsTech lists 50+ tire suppliers; WHI lists tires among Nexpart's markets, search summary)
**Target Buyer:** VP of sales or distribution operations at a tire wholesaler
**Automation Potential:** Medium — allocation and forecasting automate; warehouse handling and route delivery stay physical

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Tires are the aftermarket's widest assortment problem in the narrowest product: thousands of size, load-index, speed-rating and brand combinations, seasonal swings (winter tires in the north), and assortment decided by which manufacturers authorise the wholesaler. The customers are tire dealers (the queued tire dealers row, which covers the stores that install) and general repair shops; the competitors are national wholesalers, regional distributors, manufacturers' own distribution and the parts chains' tire programmes. The contest moved in 2024–2026: American Tire Distributors went through a second Chapter 11 and Michelin stopped using it as an authorised distributor from July 2025 (search summary), so dealers re-sorted their first calls among the remaining wholesalers. The tire makers themselves are the queued tire manufacturers row.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Wholesaler portals show availability by size and brand; warehouse management and routing systems run the daily deliveries; e-catalog networks put tires next to parts. The gaps: forecasts are built from shipments, not from dealer searches that found nothing, and when a brand's authorisation changes, re-planning allocation across warehouses is done in spreadsheets. Substitutions (a different brand or load rating) are made to protect fill rate and erode dealer trust when unasked.

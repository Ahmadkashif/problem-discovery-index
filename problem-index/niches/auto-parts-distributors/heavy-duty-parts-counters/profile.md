# Heavy-Duty Truck & Trailer Parts Counters

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/auto-parts-distributors|Auto Parts Distributors & Stores]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get a down truck or trailer the right part from an OE number, a component tag or a photo, and whoever fills the largest share of fleet breakdown orders with the correct part on the first call takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$25B a year of heavy-duty truck and trailer parts sold through independent distributors and their programme groups (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~7% of the three codes' receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — trailers and many components carry no usable VIN-level parts data, and cross-references live in binders and veterans' memories
**Target Buyer:** Regional or branch manager at a heavy-duty parts distributor
**Automation Potential:** Medium — identification from tags and photos is learnable; the counter relationship with fleet mechanics is not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Heavy-duty parts sit in the same NAICS code (423120) as car parts but run on different data and customers. A Class 8 tractor's brakes, air system, axles and suspension are identified by the component maker's tag and part number (Bendix, Meritor, Haldex and others, named from recall), not by the vehicle; a trailer may have no useful VIN-level parts data at all; and fleets use their own part numbers and VMRS repair codes. The customers are fleet maintenance shops ([[industries/fleet-managers|Fleet Managers]]), owner-operators and truck repair shops, for whom a down truck costs a load and a delivery window. The competitors are national heavy-duty distributors and programme groups (FleetPride, TruckPro, HDA Truck Pride and Vipar, named from recall), truck dealers' parts counters and component makers' own channels. Construction equipment parts belong to the queued construction equipment dealers row, and trailer lessors' maintenance to the queued truck, trailer and RV lessors row.

## Current Tools & Gaps
E-catalog networks cover heavy-duty (Nexpart lists it among its markets, search summary), and component makers publish cross-reference tools for their own lines. The gaps are the in-between cases: a worn tag, a fleet's internal number, a superseded part from a maker that was acquired, a photo of an air valve. Those land on the counter veteran, and the resolution is not recorded.

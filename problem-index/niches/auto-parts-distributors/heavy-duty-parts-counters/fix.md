# Cross-References Kept in a Veteran's Binder

**Niche:** [[niches/auto-parts-distributors/heavy-duty-parts-counters/profile|Heavy-Duty Truck & Trailer Parts Counters]]
**Industry:** [[industries/auto-parts-distributors|Auto Parts Distributors & Stores]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The branch's best interchange knowledge sits in a binder, a spreadsheet and one person's head, and every retirement takes some of it away.

**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get a down truck or trailer the right part from an OE number, a component tag or a photo, and whoever fills the largest share of fleet breakdown orders with the correct part on the first call takes the account.

## The Problem
Heavy-duty counters accumulate their own cross-references: handwritten notes on supersessions, a spreadsheet of fleet numbers, a veteran's memory of which aftermarket brand fits a discontinued trailer axle. None of it is in the business system, and it differs from branch to branch.

## Why It's Still Broken
Component makers publish cross-references only for their own lines, distributors' systems have no place for unofficial interchanges, and nobody has time to type up the binder.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Photograph the binder and export the spreadsheets; an LLM extracts candidate interchanges into a shared table, each flagged for confirmation by a senior counter person and checked against order and return history. Confirmed entries appear in the lookup screen at every branch, with the source noted.

## Who Feels the Pain
Newer counter staff, fleet mechanics waiting on calls back, and branch managers facing a veteran's retirement.

## Impact If Fixed
Capturing ~2,000–5,000 informal interchanges per branch network (estimate) would let a new hire resolve a large share of the requests that now wait for the veteran, cutting callbacks by perhaps a third (estimate).

# Hot-Shot Delivery Runs

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/auto-parts-distributors|Auto Parts Distributors & Stores]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get each shop order from the nearest stock to the bay, and whoever loses the fewest shop orders to cancellation because the part arrived after the shop's window takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$3–4B a year of commercial delivery cost across U.S. parts stores and distributors: drivers, vehicles, fuel and insurance (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~1% of the three codes' receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low-Medium — some chains track drivers by app; many stores dispatch by ticket and shout
**Target Buyer:** Commercial operations or store operations leader; owner at an independent
**Automation Potential:** High for dispatch and batching; the drive and hand-off stay human

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
This is the delivery half of shop order intake. The shop gives a window (often ~30–60 minutes, estimate) set by the car on its lift; when the part misses it, the shop cancels and calls another supplier, or the customer's car leaves the bay unrepaired. The competitors in this sub-niche (in-house dispatch at the chains, routing and dispatch software vendors, same-day courier platforms) are judged on that outcome: how many orders the store loses to cancellation because the part came late. Batching runs, folding in core pickups and cutting miles are means, and they are only worth having when they don't push an order past its window. Hub growth raises the stakes: AutoZone added 39 mega hubs in fiscal 2026, and O'Reilly says more than 95% of its stores get multiple same-day deliveries from distribution centres and hubs, so more shop orders travel farther from fewer large stores. The driver's own day is set out in [[problems/auto-parts-distributors/worker-life-1|The Hot-Shot Driver's Forty Runs]].

## Current Tools & Gaps
Last-mile routing software, driver apps and courier platforms handle stop sequencing and proof of delivery. They treat each order as independent; they do not know each shop's real window, predict that the same shop will order again in ten minutes, or record which orders were cancelled because the part arrived late, so no store can state its late-cancellation rate.

# The Coverage Report That Misses Half the Coverage

**Niche:** [[niches/public-relations-agencies/multicultural-in-language-media/profile|Multicultural & In-Language Media Relations]]
**Industry:** [[industries/public-relations-agencies|Public Relations Agencies]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Multicultural agencies earn coverage their monitoring cannot see, so their reports undercount the work and clients compare them unfavourably with general-market results.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #quick-win #worker-facing #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to prove to a public-health, utility or government client that its message was actually picked up and heard in in-language communities where outlets have no audited audiences and commercial monitoring sees little, and whoever can evidence that reach credibly takes the account.

## The Problem
Staff screenshot radio station social posts, photograph print pages and ask hosts for air-check recordings, then paste them into the report. Whatever they miss does not exist as far as the client is concerned. Side by side with a general-market agency's monitored clip count, the multicultural work looks smaller than it was.

## Why It's Still Broken
Monitoring contracts are bought for general-market sources, and adding community sources is nobody's job. Hosts and editors are happy to share air checks but nobody asks systematically.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A simple collection routine per campaign: a shared upload link sent to every outlet pitched, automated transcription and translation of what comes back, matching against the pitch log, and a report section that shows in-language coverage with the same fields as monitored coverage.

## Who Feels the Pain
Account staff at multicultural agencies doing collection by hand; clients undercounting their reach; outlets whose coverage goes unrecognised.

## Impact If Fixed
More complete reports at low cost, and fairer comparisons between multicultural and general-market work.

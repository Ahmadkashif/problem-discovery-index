# The Press Release on Its Fifth MLR Round

**Niche:** [[niches/public-relations-agencies/healthcare-communications/profile|Healthcare & Life Sciences Communications]]
**Industry:** [[industries/public-relations-agencies|Public Relations Agencies]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Reviewers' comments contradict each other across rounds because nobody reconciles them, so a release circles until the news window closes.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #compliance #workflow-orchestration #quick-win #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get trial readouts, disease-awareness campaigns and product news through medical-legal-regulatory review and into coverage without the material becoming unfiled or non-compliant promotion, and whoever gets compliant stories out fastest takes the account.

## The Problem
Medical, legal and regulatory reviewers comment separately. Medical wants the endpoint described precisely, legal wants a hedge, regulatory wants the safety language moved. Round three undoes a change round two required. The agency's account lead reconciles the comments by hand in a marked-up PDF, sometimes late at night before a congress embargo lifts.

## Why It's Still Broken
Comments arrive as annotations on a document, not as decisions. Nobody keeps a running list of which comments are resolved, which conflict, and which earlier version a reviewer approved, so each round restarts the argument.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Extract every comment into a decision log tied to the sentence it concerns, flag conflicts between reviewers before the next draft, carry forward approved language automatically, and send reviewers only the changed sentences with the reason each changed.

## Who Feels the Pain
Agency healthcare account leads, sponsor communications managers chasing reviewers, and reporters waiting on an embargoed release.

## Impact If Fixed
Fewer rounds and a release that clears inside the news window, with an audit trail of who approved which words.

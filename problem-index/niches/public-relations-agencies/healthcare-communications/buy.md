**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get trial readouts, disease-awareness campaigns and product news through medical-legal-regulatory review and into coverage without the material becoming unfiled or non-compliant promotion, and whoever gets compliant stories out fastest takes the account.

## The Problem
Earned media used to sit outside promotional review because the sponsor did not control the outlet. FDA's September 2025 letters treated some third-party programmes that interviewed company representatives as promotional communications that should have been submitted on Form 2253. Agencies arranging interviews, podcasts and sponsored segments now need to know before the booking whether the output will count as promotion.

## What Already Exists
Veeva PromoMats and similar platforms route promotional pieces through MLR, track approvals, and generate Form 2253 submissions. Law firms publish guidance on what FDA treats as promotion.

## The Customization Gap
Earned media needs a pre-booking screen (who controls the content, whether the company pays, whether the representative will discuss product claims), a spokesperson brief checked against approved claims, a post-publication check of what was actually said against the brief, and a route into the sponsor's 2253 process when the answer is "promotional". The review platforms assume a finished asset; earned media is a conversation that becomes an asset afterwards.

## Target Customer
Healthcare agency practice leads and sponsor heads of communications compliance.

## Impact If Solved
Interviews booked with a documented promotional determination, and earned media that no longer surprises the sponsor's regulatory team.

# Healthcare & Life Sciences Communications

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/public-relations-agencies|Public Relations Agencies]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get trial readouts, disease-awareness campaigns and product news through medical-legal-regulatory review and into coverage without the material becoming unfiled or non-compliant promotion, and whoever gets compliant stories out fastest takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$2.5B in US agency fees from pharmaceutical, biotech, medical device and health system clients (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~16% of US PR agency receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — review platforms such as Veeva PromoMats are standard at sponsors, but agency drafting, claim checking and media tracking are manual
**Target Buyer:** Healthcare practice lead at an agency; head of corporate or product communications at a sponsor
**Automation Potential:** Medium — claim-to-reference checking and fair-balance checks automate; scientific judgement and patient advocacy relationships do not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Every piece of health communications that touches a prescription product sits inside a regulatory frame. Promotional materials must be submitted to FDA on Form 2253 at first use; claims need substantiation and fair balance; off-label discussion is restricted; and in FDA's September 2025 batch of untitled and warning letters, even third-party programmes interviewing company representatives were treated as promotion. Every press release, media briefing, patient story and spokesperson script goes through a sponsor's medical-legal-regulatory (MLR) committee, often for several rounds. Speed through MLR, and staying out of an untitled letter, decides who keeps the account.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Sponsors run MLR in Veeva PromoMats or similar; agencies submit drafts as PDFs with annotated references. Monitoring and outreach tools are the same as elsewhere. The gaps: no agency-side tool checks a draft's claims against approved labelling and the reference pack before submission; MLR comments are not mined to predict which phrasings will be rejected; and earned media that could be read as promotion is not screened before an interview is arranged.

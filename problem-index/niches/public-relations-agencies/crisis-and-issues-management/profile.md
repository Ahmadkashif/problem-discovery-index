# Crisis & Issues Management

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/public-relations-agencies|Public Relations Agencies]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is that every serious competitor is fighting to say the right thing first when a client is under attack, but that splits into two contests with different winners: keeping statements consistent with legal filings and notices inside statutory clocks (cyber incidents), and calling whether an online flare-up will escalate at all (social flashpoints). Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.5B in US agency fees for crisis, issues and reputation-defence work (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~10% of US PR agency receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — monitoring is digital, but crisis response runs on phone trees, war-room calls, Word documents with tracked changes and spreadsheets of reporter calls
**Target Buyer:** Crisis practice lead at an agency; general counsel and chief communications officer at the client
**Automation Potential:** Medium — watching, logging and consistency checking automate; the call on what to say does not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Crisis work is retained in advance (crisis plans, simulations, on-call arrangements) and billed heavily when it fires. It is the most senior, most relationship-driven part of PR, and the least instrumented. The agency's value is judgement under pressure: what to say, when, through whom, and whether to say anything. That judgement differs sharply by crisis type, which is why this niche decomposes.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Monitoring and social listening dashboards (Meltwater, Cision, Brandwatch, Talkwalker), real-time alert services (Dataminr, Signal AI), crisis-plan documents and simulation exercises, and law-firm-led incident response for cyber events. The gaps: no record links past crises' early signals to how they unfolded, statement versions are reconciled by hand, and nothing tells the overnight watcher whether a spike is the start of a story.

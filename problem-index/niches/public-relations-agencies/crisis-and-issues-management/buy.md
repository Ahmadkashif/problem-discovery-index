## The Problem
During a crisis the communications team needs to know who owns which audience, which statement version is current, what has been said to which reporter, and what changed in the facts since the last call. That lives in a group chat, an inbox and a spreadsheet.

## What Already Exists
Incident management platforms such as PagerDuty, incident.io and FireHydrant give engineering teams declared incidents, roles, timelines, status updates and post-incident reviews. Critical-event platforms such as Everbridge handle mass notification.

## The Customization Gap
Communications incidents need: audience streams (media, employees, customers, regulators, investors) rather than service components; a single fact sheet with approval state that every draft is checked against; reporter interaction logging; statement versioning with legal sign-off; privilege handling when counsel directs the work; and a post-incident review that records what was said and how coverage moved. The engineering tools have the skeleton and none of this vocabulary.

## Target Customer
Crisis practices at agencies and in-house corporate communications teams that already use an incident platform elsewhere in the company.

## Impact If Solved
A war room with one timeline and one current statement, borrowed from a discipline that already solved the coordination problem.

## The Problem
A crisis practice's most valuable asset is its senior counsellors' memory of past cases: the recall that stayed regional, the activist campaign that went national when a celebrity shared it, the breach that faded because the company disclosed early. That memory is personal. The case files are email threads and decks with no common structure, and when a counsellor leaves, the pattern library goes too.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Each crisis is confidential, often privileged, and treated as unique. Monitoring vendors hold the signal history but not the response decisions or the outcome as the client experienced it.

## What to Build
A structured case record for every issue the agency handles: trigger, early signal series exported from monitoring, amplifier types, reporter inquiries, each response and its timing, statement versions, and outcome (tier-one coverage, duration, regulatory action, stock move for listed clients). On that archive, time-to-escalation models and similar-case retrieval ("the four past cases that started like this, and what happened") for the counsellor on the call.

## Target Customer
Crisis practice leads at mid-size and large agencies and at specialist crisis firms.

## Impact If Built
A practice that keeps its pattern library when people leave, and gives every counsellor the firm's full case history in the first hour of a new issue.

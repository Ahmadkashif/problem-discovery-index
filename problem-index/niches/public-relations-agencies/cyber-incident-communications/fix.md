# The Customer Letter That Contradicts the Filing

**Niche:** [[niches/public-relations-agencies/cyber-incident-communications/profile|Cyber Incident & Disclosure Communications]]
**Industry:** [[industries/public-relations-agencies|Public Relations Agencies]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A notice drafted on Tuesday's facts goes out on Thursday after the filing changed, and the inconsistency becomes an exhibit.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #bert #evaluation-metrics #compliance #quick-win #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep the media statement, customer and regulator notices and any Form 8-K saying the same thing inside the four-business-day and state-notification clocks, under counsel's privilege, and whoever keeps disclosure consistent and on time wins the insurer- and law-firm-panel slot.

## The Problem
Notification letters, FAQs and call-centre scripts are drafted early so mailing can start fast. When counsel revises the filing language (the scope, the data types, the phrase about misuse), the change does not always reach every downstream document before it ships.

## Why It's Still Broken
Each document has its own owner and approval path. The PR firm sees the media lines, the notification vendor sees the letter, and only counsel sees everything, under time pressure.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Before any document is released, compare its factual claims sentence by sentence against the latest approved filing and fact sheet, flag every mismatch with the source sentence, and require sign-off on the difference. Keep a log of each release with the fact-set version it used.

## Who Feels the Pain
Outside counsel defending later litigation; the PR firm whose statement is quoted against the filing; customers given contradictory information.

## Impact If Fixed
A cheap pre-release check removes a common, avoidable source of litigation and regulatory exposure.

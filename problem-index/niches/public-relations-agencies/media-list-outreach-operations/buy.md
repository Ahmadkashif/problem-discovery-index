# B2B Contact Data Hygiene Applied to Reporters

**Niche:** [[niches/public-relations-agencies/media-list-outreach-operations/profile|Media List Building & Outreach Operations]]
**Industry:** [[industries/public-relations-agencies|Public Relations Agencies]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Sales teams already run contact verification, job-change alerts and bounce suppression on their CRMs; PR lists get none of it.
**Tags:** #logistic-regression #k-nearest-neighbors #data-integration #automation #evaluation-metrics #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep every pitch list current to the week as reporters change beats, outlets and status, and whoever holds the freshest verified map of who covers what wins the database renewal.

## The Problem
An agency's saved lists in its media database and its own spreadsheets accumulate dead addresses, departed reporters and duplicates. Each campaign starts with an account executive checking names by hand.

## What Already Exists
B2B data hygiene tools for sales (email verification services, CRM enrichment and job-change alerting such as those built into sales intelligence platforms) verify addresses, detect job changes and suppress bounces automatically.

## The Customization Gap
Reporters differ from sales contacts: many have multiple outlets at once, beats matter more than titles, freelancers and newsletter writers have no corporate domain, and personal relationships mean a reporter who moved may still be the right contact at the new outlet. The adaptation needs byline-based verification rather than corporate directory data, beat fields rather than job titles, multi-outlet identities, and suppression rules that respect a reporter's stated pitch preferences.

## Target Customer
Agency operations leads and in-house media relations teams with large saved lists.

## Impact If Solved
Lists that clean themselves continuously, using a hygiene discipline sales operations already proved.

# The Form-Letter Flood Staffers Ignore

**Niche:** [[niches/public-relations-agencies/public-affairs-advocacy/profile|Public Affairs & Issue Advocacy]]
**Industry:** [[industries/public-relations-agencies|Public Relations Agencies]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Clients measure advocacy by message counts, so firms send thousands of near-identical letters that legislative staff discount on sight.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #bert #evaluation-metrics #compliance #quick-win #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to show legislators and agencies verifiable support from real constituents and credible third parties before a vote or comment deadline, and whoever produces support that survives scrutiny for astroturfing takes the account.

## The Problem
An advocacy platform makes it easy to generate ten thousand emails to Congress or comments on a proposed rule from a template. Staff sort them by template and count them as one. Agencies that review public comments group mass campaigns together. The client's report says "10,000 messages delivered"; the office registers one campaign.

## Why It's Still Broken
Volume is easy to report and easy to sell. Personalised, constituent-verified contacts are slower to produce and harder to count.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Report constituent verification rate, the share of messages with personal content, and contacts from validators each office has historically engaged with, alongside volume. Help supporters personalise with prompts drawn from their own situation rather than generating variants of the template, which staff and comment-review tools also detect.

## Who Feels the Pain
Corporate government affairs heads paying for campaigns that do not register; firms whose credibility with staff erodes; staffers sorting floods.

## Impact If Fixed
Fewer, more credible contacts that offices actually weigh, and campaign reports that measure what moves a vote.

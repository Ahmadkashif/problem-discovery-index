# The Source Request Answered by a Chatbot

**Niche:** [[niches/public-relations-agencies/the-beat-reporter/profile|The Beat Reporter]]
**Industry:** [[industries/public-relations-agencies|Public Relations Agencies]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Reporters post a request for an expert and receive dozens of fluent, generic answers written by language models on behalf of people who may not exist.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #bert #logistic-regression #evaluation-metrics #quick-win #compliance
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor serving the beat reporter is fighting to become the channel through which reporters choose to receive pitches and find sources, and whoever filters the inbox well enough to earn reporters' trust controls access to them for every PR firm.

## The Problem
Source-request services became a link-building channel, and generative tools made answering requests nearly free. A reporter asking for a tax accountant's view gets fluent quotes from "experts" whose credentials are unverifiable and whose answers read alike. Verifying them takes longer than finding a source directly.

## Why It's Still Broken
The services are paid by the sources, so volume is revenue. Identity and credential checks add friction the paying side resists.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Verify identity and credentials once per source (licence lookups for regulated professions, employer confirmation), flag responses likely to be machine-generated or templated, rank responses by verified expertise and specificity, and let reporters mark fabricated sources so they are removed from the network.

## Who Feels the Pain
Reporters on deadline; genuine experts drowned out; agencies whose real clients are lost among fake ones.

## Impact If Fixed
Source requests that return a handful of verified experts, which restores the channel's value to both sides.

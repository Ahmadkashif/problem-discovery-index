# Niche Analysis — Public Relations Agencies

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/public-relations-agencies|Public Relations Agencies]]

## Niche Selection

A PR agency is paid on retainer to earn coverage and defend reputation, and the judgements that do it (which reporter, which angle, whether an issue will escalate, whether coverage carried the message) are made by senior people and recorded nowhere. Around that core sit hard external clocks (SEC disclosure deadlines in cyber crises, FDA promotional rules in healthcare, legislative calendars and FARA filings in public affairs) and a tool stack of media databases, monitoring and newswires that each own one slice of the record. The eight niches below split the industry by what agencies and their vendors actually compete over. Market sizes are estimates of agency fees, tooling and staff time within each niche, derived from the ~$15.8B in US agency receipts the 2022 Economic Census reports; they are not separate industry revenues.

## Why These Niches

Technology and B2B media relations takes the largest share because tech clients announce constantly and judge agencies on tier-one coverage within a quarter; healthcare and life sciences is second because sponsors pay premium fees for agencies that can move stories through medical-legal-regulatory review without drifting into unfiled promotion. The two low-digitized niches are the industry's most senior, least instrumented work: crisis response run from phones and tracked-changes documents, and public affairs, which NAICS 541820 explicitly includes, run on relationships with legislative offices. The two underserved audiences are the in-language and community media that the big databases list poorly, and the beat reporter, the person every PR workflow targets and no PR tool serves. The two automatable niches are the most mechanical parts of agency life: turning monitoring exports into a coded monthly report, and keeping pitch lists current as newsrooms churn.

## Filter Notes

Seven of the eight level-1 niches are terminal. Crisis & Issues Management is not: writing its contested sentence produced two sentences with different winners. In a cyber incident at a listed company the contest is keeping the media statement, customer and regulator notices and the Form 8-K consistent inside a four-business-day clock, under counsel's privilege, and the winner is whichever firm sits on the insurer and breach-coach panels and runs disclosure discipline. In a social flashpoint there is no statute; the contest is calling, within hours, whether a flare-up will reach mainstream media and whether responding will feed it, and the winner is whoever forecasts escalation best. Different clocks, buyers and skills, so it decomposes into **Cyber Incident & Disclosure Communications** and **Social Media Flashpoint Response**. Coverage Monitoring & Measurement Reporting is the agency-side job of producing the monthly report; the vendor-side question of whether codes predict outcomes belongs to the Pass 2 measurement pocket, so the two are kept apart.

Two terminal calls were close. **Healthcare & Life Sciences Communications** could split between consumer disease-awareness work and scientific communications around trial readouts, but medical-legal-regulatory review binds both, and the same agencies win or lose on getting compliant material out fast, so one contest governs. **Media List Building & Outreach Operations** overlaps the industry's high-impact problem (which reporter fits which story); it is kept separate because its contest is record freshness, which database vendors win with research operations, while reporter–story fit is a judgement problem agencies own. **Multicultural & In-Language Media Relations** first drafted onto the same freshness contest (holding a verified map of who covers what); it was rewritten because what separates its winners is different: proving to public-health, utility and government clients that a message was picked up and heard in communities whose outlets have no audited audiences, so its build is a reach-evidence model rather than another contact map.

Three candidates were considered and rejected. **Solo and boutique practitioners** are three-quarters of establishments (74% had fewer than five employees in 2023), but the contest written honestly is "enterprise media intelligence at a solo price", a lower-price contest that fails the filter. **Consumer and lifestyle PR** was rejected because its distinct contest, creator partnerships and product seeding, belongs to [[industries/influencer-marketing-platforms|Influencer Marketing Platforms]], and what remains is the same media relations contest as niche 1. **Investor relations** was rejected because its contest (shareholder targeting and earnings-call preparation) sits with listed companies and their capital-markets advisers, and its research inputs run through MNPI controls; the financial communications firms that touch it are logged as a Pass 2 pocket below.

## Why These Pockets

The agencies themselves are not qualifiers, and that is the central finding. The typical establishment has fewer than five staff; the largest firms do have research units, but their corpus is mostly client-commissioned and their flagship studies are marketing (pocket 16), and the strategic communications firms with the hardest clocks in the industry work under MNPI (pocket 13). Three pockets that would otherwise be scored here are already profiled under SMB marketing agencies and are not re-scored: PR-specific financial benchmarking such as Gould+Partners' reports sits with [[niches/marketing-agencies-smb/agency-profitability-benchmarking/profile|Agency Profitability Benchmarking]], PR firm sales with [[niches/marketing-agencies-smb/agency-ma-brokerage/profile|Agency M&A Brokerage]], and holding-company acquisition analysis with [[niches/marketing-agencies-smb/agency-rollup-corporate-development/profile|Agency Rollup Corporate Development]].

No pocket qualifies; the closest sits beside the agencies. Measurement and media analysis firms sell coded coverage as their product, at labour scale (PRIME Research had more than 700 staff when Cision bought it in 2018; Onclusive runs a coding operations team), and hold the industry's only large archive of judged coverage. They share the shape of qualifiers elsewhere in the vault: an analytical product with a later, observable outcome that the producer holds and does not grade. AMEC's Barcelona Principles 4.0 asks for outcomes, the firms sell outputs, and none publishes whether its pull-through and tonality codes predict reputation, demand or AI citation. It stops at 49: the pocket is three large vendors plus a few small firms, the clock is the client's reporting calendar, not a regulator's, and the automation of coding is eroding the human-labour model.

Next comes corporate reputation tracking (45), which holds the outcome side of the same problem: long-running reputation scores that measurement firms could validate against, but sold from survey-and-model operations on rented panels. Media database research teams (44) are the purest labour-scaled verification work in the chain and hold cross-agency pitch-outcome data nobody grades, but no outside clock governs them. The kill-switch pockets repeat a pattern seen across the professional-services sweeps: social listening runs on platform data it does not own, cyber insurers' incident files are privileged, and the agencies' and strategic firms' research is either client-owned or wall-crossed. Three pockets with sizeable staffs fail the gate once only analysts are counted: newswire desks and broadcast monitors employ editors, transcribers and QA staff doing production work, and the FARA Unit's and congressional disclosure offices' filing staff are split across three offices with no analytical function of ten. Pollsters rent their panels from the operators indexed under [[niches/data-analytics-consultants/survey-panel-operators/profile|Data Analytics Consultants: Survey Research & Panel Operators]], and the bill-tracking vendors public affairs firms rely on are logged under [[niches/trade-associations/legislative-tracking-political-intelligence/profile|Trade Associations: Legislative Tracking & Political Intelligence]].

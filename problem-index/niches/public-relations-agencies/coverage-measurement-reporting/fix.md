# The AVE Line the Client Still Asks For

**Niche:** [[niches/public-relations-agencies/coverage-measurement-reporting/profile|Coverage Monitoring & Measurement Reporting]]
**Industry:** [[industries/public-relations-agencies|Public Relations Agencies]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Agencies know advertising value equivalents are invalid and keep reporting them because the client's finance team wants a dollar figure and nothing else is offered.
**Tags:** #causal-inference #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #quick-win #revenue-impact #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to produce a de-duplicated, message-coded coverage report that a chief communications officer will defend to the CFO without advertising value equivalents, and whoever does it with the least analyst time per client keeps the measurement line.

## The Problem
An AVE multiplies column inches or airtime by an ad rate, sometimes with an arbitrary multiplier for "credibility". AMEC's Barcelona Principles 4.0 call it invalid and name renamed variants such as Earned Media Value as the same metric. It survives because a client's chief financial officer asked for a dollar number years ago and dropping it looks like hiding the results.

## Why It's Still Broken
No replacement number is offered on the same page. Outcome measures need client data (site analytics, search, surveys) the agency often does not have access to, so the AVE stays as the only money figure.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Replace the AVE line with a short outcome panel built from data the client already holds: visits and branded searches in coverage windows against a baseline, with an interval; message pull-through in priority outlets; and, where tracked, citation share in AI answers. Ask for the data access in the contract, and show the AVE's removal with the reason.

## Who Feels the Pain
Agency leads defending reports; chief communications officers challenged by finance; the industry's credibility.

## Impact If Fixed
A defensible money-adjacent measure in place of an invalid one, at the cost of one data-access clause.

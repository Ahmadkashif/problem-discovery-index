# Interconnection Portals With Engineering Screens Built In

**Niche:** [[niches/electric-utilities/der-interconnection-hosting-capacity/profile|DER Interconnection & Hosting Capacity]]
**Industry:** [[industries/electric-utilities|Electric Distribution Utilities]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Application portals move DER paperwork online but leave the engineering screens to a person with a spreadsheet.
**Tags:** #decision-trees #feature-engineering #compliance #workflow-orchestration #automation #data-integration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to clear rooftop, storage and EV-charger applications against a hosting-capacity number that later studies confirm, and whoever sends the fewest applications to full study without a later violation takes the account.

## The Problem
Applicants file online, upload single-line diagrams and inverter specification sheets, and pay fees. Then an engineer applies the tariff's screens by hand: aggregate generation against peak or minimum load on the section, short-circuit contribution, transformer loading, export limits.

## What Already Exists
PowerClerk (Clean Power Research), GridUnity and utility-built portals; CRM and workflow platforms (Salesforce, ServiceNow) configured for interconnection; equipment databases such as the California Energy Commission's approved inverter list.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needed: each state's interconnection screens encoded as executable rules tied to live feeder data (section loading, existing DER queue position, transformer ratings), automatic validation of inverter settings against the utility's required profiles, export-limited and flexible interconnection options offered automatically when a full-export application fails, and an audit trail regulators can review when they ask why approvals slowed.

## Target Customer
Utilities already running a portal that want screen automation inside it rather than another system.

## Impact If Solved
~60%–80% of small applications decided by rule within days (estimate), with engineers reviewing only the ones that need judgement.

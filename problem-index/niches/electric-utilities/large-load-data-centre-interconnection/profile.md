# Large-Load & Data Centre Interconnection

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/electric-utilities|Electric Distribution Utilities]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to tell a data-centre or factory developer how many megawatts it can have, by when and for what upgrade, while screening out speculative requests, and whoever's answers still hold when the load energises takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.5B a year of large-load studies, load forecasting, tariff design and key-account engineering at US utilities (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~0.3% of retail electricity revenue, attached to a growing share of capital plans (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — requests arrive by email and meetings and are tracked in spreadsheets
**Target Buyer:** VP of Economic Development & Large Load / Director of System Planning
**Automation Potential:** Medium — screening, pipeline tracking and forecasting yes; agreements and studies remain human

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A handful of requests, each tens to hundreds of megawatts, can exceed a utility's whole historical peak growth. Developers often approach several utilities with the same project, so the request log overstates what will be built. Each real one needs a substation or sub-transmission study, often a new bank or line, equipment with long lead times (power transformers averaged 128 weeks in Q2 2025, per Wood Mackenzie), and a commercial agreement. That agreement increasingly comes under large-load tariffs with collateral, minimum bills and ramp schedules. Bulk-system studies sit with transmission planners, the queued Transmission & Grid Operators row. This niche is the utility as the load-serving entity deciding what to promise.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Planning software for the studies; Itron and in-house load forecasting; CRM for key accounts; consultants for large-load tariffs. Gaps: no stage-tracked pipeline with realisation probabilities; forecasts that add requests at face value or apply flat haircuts; no record of which requester attributes predicted withdrawal.

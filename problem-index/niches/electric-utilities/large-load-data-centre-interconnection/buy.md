# Sales CRM Turned Into a Load Pipeline of Record

**Niche:** [[niches/electric-utilities/large-load-data-centre-interconnection/profile|Large-Load & Data Centre Interconnection]]
**Industry:** [[industries/electric-utilities|Electric Distribution Utilities]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Every utility's key-account team already has a CRM; none of them tracks engineering stage, megawatt ramp and collateral the way planners need.
**Tags:** #logistic-regression #feature-engineering #data-integration #workflow-orchestration #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to tell a data-centre or factory developer how many megawatts it can have, by when and for what upgrade, while screening out speculative requests, and whoever's answers still hold when the load energises takes the account.

## The Problem
Key-account managers log large customers in Salesforce or Microsoft Dynamics. Planners keep their own spreadsheet of requests by substation. The two disagree on megawatts, dates and status, and the forecast team builds a third version.

## What Already Exists
Salesforce and Dynamics with utility templates; economic-development site-selection databases; utility large-load tariff documents defining stages and collateral.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation: opportunity stages mapped to the tariff's engineering and commercial gates; megawatt ramps by year rather than one deal value; linkage to the substation and feeder the load would use and to the study record; collateral and agreement milestones as hard fields; and a nightly feed to the load forecast and to capital planning, so one record drives all three.

## Target Customer
Economic development and large-load teams at utilities adopting large-load tariffs.

## Impact If Solved
One pipeline of record replaces the ~3 versions kept by key accounts, planning and forecasting (estimate), so the forecast in the rate case matches the planners' study queue and the account team's promises.

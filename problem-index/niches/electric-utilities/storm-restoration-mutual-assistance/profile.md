# Storm Restoration & Mutual Assistance

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/electric-utilities|Electric Distribution Utilities]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to restore the last customer sooner with fewer crew-days, and whoever stages the right number of crews in the right places before the storm arrives takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1B a year of storm logistics, damage assessment, outage prediction and storm cost-recovery software and services, excluding restoration labour itself (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~0.2% of retail electricity revenue; restoration costs in a major-storm year are many times larger (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — crew requests by phone and conference call through regional mutual-assistance groups, rosters and lodging in spreadsheets, damage assessment on paper or basic mobile forms
**Target Buyer:** Director of Emergency Preparedness / Storm Restoration Officer
**Automation Potential:** Medium — prediction, logistics and documentation yes; field restoration no

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Major events caused 80% of the 11 hours US customers spent without power in 2024 (EIA). Restoration is a logistics operation layered on the outage management system. The utility estimates damage before landfall, requests crews through its regional mutual-assistance group, then stages, lodges, feeds and fuels them. Field assessors confirm damage and crews are assigned and released, and every hour is documented for cost recovery. The decisions that matter most are made two to three days out with a weather forecast and a storm boss's experience. The contractor crews' own workflow is covered in [[niches/utility-contractors/emergency-repair-crews/profile|🟣 Emergency Repair Crews]].

## Current Tools & Gaps
Outage management systems (GE Vernova, Oracle, Schneider Electric); weather services and outage-prediction models (DTN, university models); crew and logistics tools, some built in-house; damage-assessment mobile apps; Neara digital twins for storm modelling; Buzz Solutions for inspection imagery. Gaps: crew requests sized by judgement; no single roster from request to release; damage assessments not joined to the restoration plan; cost documentation reconstructed afterwards.

# New Service Connection Design & Estimating

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/electric-utilities|Electric Distribution Utilities]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn a builder's or customer's new-service request into a designed, estimated and scheduled job in days, and whoever cuts request-to-energise time without redesigns in the field takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$900M a year of distribution design labour, estimating, staking and design software for customer-requested work at US utilities (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~0.2% of retail electricity revenue (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — design tools and compatible-unit libraries exist; intake, site information and load letters still arrive by email and PDF
**Target Buyer:** Manager of Distribution Design / Director of Customer Projects
**Automation Potential:** High for standard residential and small commercial jobs

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Every subdivision, apartment building, store, EV charger upgrade and heat-pump service upgrade needs a utility design. A distribution designer (sometimes a contract designer) collects the customer's load information and site plan, selects a transformer and service size, routes primary and secondary lines, picks compatible units from the utility's standards, estimates cost and customer contribution under the line-extension tariff, and stakes the job for construction. Electrification has raised volumes and service sizes. Builders complain about months-long waits, and designers retire faster than they are replaced. Large loads that need system studies belong to [[niches/electric-utilities/large-load-data-centre-interconnection/profile|🎯 Large-Load & Data Centre Interconnection]]. This niche is the high-volume standard work.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Design and staking tools in GIS (Esri with ArcFM Designer, Schneider Electric's ArcFM, Bentley, Trimble); compatible-unit estimating in work management (Maximo, SAP); customer portals for service requests. Gaps: intake missing load details, so designs are redone; transformer sizing by rule of thumb; no reuse of similar past designs; customer contribution calculations done by hand.

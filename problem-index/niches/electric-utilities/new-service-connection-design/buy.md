# Builder Portals That Collect What the Designer Needs

**Niche:** [[niches/electric-utilities/new-service-connection-design/profile|New Service Connection Design & Estimating]]
**Industry:** [[industries/electric-utilities|Electric Distribution Utilities]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Service request portals take an address and a contact; the designer still has to chase load letters, site plans and panel sizes by email.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #workflow-orchestration #automation #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn a builder's or customer's new-service request into a designed, estimated and scheduled job in days, and whoever cuts request-to-energise time without redesigns in the field takes the account.

## The Problem
Weeks of a new-service job's elapsed time go to waiting for information. Builders don't know the utility needs connected load by appliance, the main panel rating, meter locations or a CAD site plan in a particular format. Designers send back incomplete requests and start over.

## What Already Exists
Utility customer portals on CRM platforms (Salesforce, Microsoft Dynamics) and CIS self-service; general form builders; document capture.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation: request forms driven by the utility's service standards and job type; automatic extraction of loads, panel ratings and lot layouts from uploaded plans and load letters; completeness checks before the request reaches a designer; status tracking that shows builders where the job is; and a handoff that pre-populates the design tool.

## Target Customer
Customer projects directors at utilities with high new-construction volumes in growing metros.

## Impact If Solved
Complete requests cut ~1–3 weeks of back-and-forth from a typical job (estimate), designers stop chasing paperwork, and builders get a predictable date.

# Small Cooperatives & Municipal Utilities

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/electric-utilities|Electric Distribution Utilities]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to tell a small co-op or city utility with no engineer on staff where its feeders and transformers will run out of capacity, and whoever catches the most overloads before they cause a failure or stall a new connection takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$800M a year of capacity-planning, grid-analytics software and outsourced engineering spend by small co-ops and municipal electric systems (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** Public power and co-ops are 85% of utilities but 28% of customers (APPA); this segment's tooling spend is a small fraction of revenue (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — AMI is common, often bought through NISC or a G&T programme, but planning is done by a consulting engineer every few years and rates by spreadsheet
**Target Buyer:** General Manager / CEO of the co-op or city utility director, often through a G&T or joint action agency's member-services group
**Automation Potential:** High for packaged analytics; limited by data quality and staff time

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
APPA counts 1,998 public power utilities, 59% of all utilities, and cooperatives are another 26%. Most are small: a general manager, an operations superintendent, line crews, a billing office, and no engineers on staff. They face the same pressures as large utilities: new large loads, DER applications, storm recovery, rate increases from wholesale suppliers and cybersecurity rules. They depend on consulting engineers, their G&T or joint action agency, and statewide associations. Vendors design for large IOUs and price accordingly. The contest here is capacity: knowing which feeders and transformers are near their limits before one fails or a new request arrives. Rate studies for these utilities are a separate service sold by rate consultants and G&T rate staff, and they are left out of this niche. The G&T or association is how the work is sold, not what is contested. Their security needs are covered in [[niches/cybersecurity-mssp/rural-cooperative-security/profile|🟣 Rural Cooperative & Utility Security]].

## Current Tools & Gaps
NISC and other co-op billing and accounting suites; Milsoft WindMil for engineering analysis; consulting engineers' periodic construction work plans; G&T load forecasts; Camus Energy-style grid platforms aimed at co-ops (Camus listed no open roles on 2026-10-24). Gaps: no affordable continuous planning; AMI data barely used beyond billing; no staff to evaluate vendors.

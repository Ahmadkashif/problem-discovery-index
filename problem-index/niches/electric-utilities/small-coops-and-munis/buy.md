# Large-Utility Analytics Repackaged for a Board Budget

**Niche:** [[niches/electric-utilities/small-coops-and-munis/profile|Small Cooperatives & Municipal Utilities]]
**Industry:** [[industries/electric-utilities|Electric Distribution Utilities]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Grid analytics built for utilities with data science teams fail at a co-op whose IT department is one person.
**Tags:** #k-means-clustering #feature-engineering #descriptive-statistics #data-integration #automation #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to tell a small co-op or city utility with no engineer on staff where its feeders and transformers will run out of capacity, and whoever catches the most overloads before they cause a failure or stall a new connection takes the account.

## The Problem
A small utility buys AMI and then uses it to bill. The analytics that would find overloaded transformers, theft, voltage problems or line losses exist, but they are priced and staffed for large utilities and assume someone in-house will configure and act on them.

## What Already Exists
Meter data management and AMI analytics from Itron, Landis+Gyr and Bidgely; NISC's suite for co-ops; grid platforms from Camus and others; consulting engineers.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation: pre-built connectors to the systems small utilities actually run (NISC, Milsoft, common AMI head-ends); fixed-scope outputs a superintendent can act on (a weekly list of overloaded transformers, suspected theft and voltage complaints with likely causes); pricing per meter that fits a board-approved budget; and delivery through the statewide association or G&T so procurement is a member programme, not an RFP.

## Target Customer
General managers and operations superintendents at co-ops and municipal utilities of 5,000–50,000 meters.

## Impact If Solved
Each avoided distribution-transformer failure saves the unit, a truck roll and an outage. A 20,000-meter utility catching ~10–30 overloads a year before failure (estimate) pays for a per-meter service without hiring an analyst.

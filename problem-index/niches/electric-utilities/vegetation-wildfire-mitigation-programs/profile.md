# Vegetation Management & Wildfire Mitigation Programmes

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/electric-utilities|Electric Distribution Utilities]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to show regulators that each trimming and hardening dollar removed measurable ignition and outage risk, and whoever ties completed work to risk reduced, span by span, takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.5B a year of vegetation inspection, risk analytics, audit and programme-management spend at US utilities, excluding the trimming and hardening work itself (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~0.3% of retail electricity revenue; the work it directs is several times larger (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — utility foresters and contract inspectors record tree conditions on tablets or paper; work verification is sampled by hand
**Target Buyer:** Director of Vegetation Management / Chief Wildfire Mitigation Officer
**Automation Potential:** Medium — remote sensing and risk ranking yes; hazard-tree calls and landowner negotiation no

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Trees cause a large share of distribution outages, and in the West they and the lines near them can start fires. Utilities run trimming cycles, hazard-tree removal and line hardening, and in wildfire states file Wildfire Mitigation Plans with regulators. California's Office of Energy Infrastructure Safety approves or denies each utility's 2026–2028 base plan after public comment and denied Liberty Utilities' draft in February 2026. The utility forester's judgement of which tree will fail is the tacit core. The contractors who do the trimming, and the analytics vendors who survey for them, are covered in [[niches/utility-contractors/electric-transmission-distribution/profile|🔵 Electric Transmission & Distribution]] and [[niches/utility-contractors/utility-vegetation-management-analytics/profile|🔍 Utility Vegetation Management Analytics]]. This niche is the utility's programme: deciding where, when and how much, and proving it worked.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Work management systems (Clearion, Arbora, in-house builds); LiDAR and satellite analytics (AiDash, Overstory); wildfire-spread consequence models (Technosylva and others); GIS. Gaps: inspection findings, completed work, outages and ignitions sit in separate systems; risk-reduction claims in plans are modelled, not measured.

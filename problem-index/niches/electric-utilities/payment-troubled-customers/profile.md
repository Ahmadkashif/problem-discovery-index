# Payment-Troubled Residential Customers

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/electric-utilities|Electric Distribution Utilities]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep a payment-troubled household connected and paying, and whoever matches each household to the right arrangement or assistance programme before its disconnection notice takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$600M a year of credit and collections operations, assistance-programme administration and customer-engagement spend on residential arrears at US utilities (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~0.1% of retail electricity revenue (estimate), against residential revenue of $244.4B in 2024 (EIA Electric Power Annual Table 2.6)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — billing and collections are systematised; eligibility for arrangements and assistance is checked by hand and on paper
**Target Buyer:** Director of Credit & Collections / VP of Customer Operations
**Automation Potential:** Medium — explanation, eligibility and outreach yes; hardship judgement and commission complaints stay human

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
These customers are underserved by the tools, not just by the utility. Collections software is built to recover debt, engagement tools to sell programmes, and neither handles the rules that decide whether a household can be disconnected: winter moratoria, medical certificates, protections for older residents, minimum payment arrangement terms, and the timing of LIHEAP and utility discount programmes. These rules differ by state and often by month. Record rate requests in 2025 (nearly $31B for electric and gas, per PowerLines) are pushing more households into this group, and commissions now track disconnections closely.

## Current Tools & Gaps
CIS collections modules (Oracle, SAP, NISC); third-party collections agencies; engagement platforms (Bidgely, Uplight, Oracle Opower); state and community action agencies for LIHEAP. Gaps: no eligibility engine across arrangement types and programmes; no prediction of who will default on an arrangement; assistance enrolment not linked to the disconnection queue.

# Collections Software Taught the Disconnection Rules

**Niche:** [[niches/electric-utilities/payment-troubled-customers/profile|Payment-Troubled Residential Customers]]
**Industry:** [[industries/electric-utilities|Electric Distribution Utilities]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Debt collection platforms optimise recovery; a regulated utility must recover while obeying protections that vary by state, season and household.
**Tags:** #decision-trees #logistic-regression #compliance #workflow-orchestration #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep a payment-troubled household connected and paying, and whoever matches each household to the right arrangement or assistance programme before its disconnection notice takes the account.

## The Problem
Utilities run collections through their customer information system and, for closed accounts, outside agencies. The rules on what can be done to an active residential customer, and when, live in commission orders and tariffs, and representatives apply them from memory and job aids.

## What Already Exists
CIS collections modules; collections strategy platforms used in banking and telecom (for example FICO's and Experian's decisioning tools); contact-centre scripting; agencies for final-bill accounts.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation: each state's protections encoded as rules (weather and seasonal moratoria, medical and age protections, notice timing, minimum arrangement terms); assistance programmes and pledge timing as treatment options; commission reporting on disconnections and arrangements built in; and champion-challenger testing constrained so no treatment violates a protection.

## Target Customer
Utilities operating in several states, where rule variation makes manual compliance error-prone.

## Impact If Solved
Protection violations reaching customers fall toward zero by design, and commission complaints about disconnections drop by perhaps ~20%–30% (estimate). Collections strategy can then be tested without legal risk.

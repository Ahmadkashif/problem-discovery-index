# Inpatient & Post-Acute Concurrent Review

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/health-insurance-carriers|Health Insurance Carriers]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to set each inpatient and post-acute stay's length from the clinical record in daily review, and whoever's length-of-stay decisions hold through appeals and peer-to-peer reviews with no rise in readmissions takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$5B a year in concurrent review nursing, medical directors, post-acute management programmes and admission-review tools (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~4% of carrier administrative spend (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low-Medium — hospital records arrive through portals and EHR access, but skilled nursing and rehabilitation notes still come by fax
**Target Buyer:** VP of Care Management / Chief Medical Officer
**Automation Potential:** Medium — evidence assembly automates well; the end-of-stay decision is consequential and contested

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Concurrent review decides, day by day, whether a hospital stay should continue, whether an admission is inpatient or observation, and how long a member stays in a skilled nursing or rehabilitation facility. The clock is daily and the stakes are clinical: an early discharge can mean a readmission, and a late one costs thousands a day. Post-acute decisions in Medicare Advantage are among the most litigated UM decisions in the industry. Lawsuits since 2023 have targeted model-assisted stay predictions (recalled), and CMS's 2024 MA rule ties plans to traditional Medicare's inpatient admission criteria (recalled). The contest is not throughput, as in outpatient authorization. It is whether the length-of-stay call is right.

## Current Tools & Gaps
UM platforms with MCG and InterQual inpatient and post-acute criteria, post-acute management programmes from naviHealth (Optum) and other vendors, hospital-facing admission-review tools, and EHR viewing access at large hospitals. The gaps are that therapy and functional notes from facilities arrive late and unstructured, decisions are recorded without the functional evidence behind them, and readmission and appeal outcomes are never joined back to the stay decision.

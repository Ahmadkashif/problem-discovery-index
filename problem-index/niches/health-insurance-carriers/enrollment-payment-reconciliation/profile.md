# Enrollment & CMS Payment Reconciliation

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/health-insurance-carriers|Health Insurance Carriers]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to reconcile every member's enrollment, premium and subsidy record against CMS's, the exchanges' and the states' monthly files before the payment cycle closes, and whoever leaves the fewest unpaid or clawed-back member-months takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.5B a year in enrollment operations, reconciliation analysts, file processing vendors and BPO services (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~1% of carrier administrative spend (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — every input is an electronic file, but discrepancies are worked one by one in spreadsheets and queues
**Target Buyer:** Director of Enrollment & Billing / Medicare Finance Operations
**Automation Potential:** High — matching, discrepancy classification and corrective transactions follow codified rules

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A carrier is paid per member per month by CMS (MA and Part D), the states (Medicaid capitation) and the exchanges (advance premium tax credits plus member premium). Each payer sends its own monthly files: CMS membership and transaction reply reports for MA (recalled), exchange enrolment and reconciliation files, and state capitation and eligibility files. Each must match the plan's own enrolment. Every unmatched member-month is revenue not received, or a payment later recovered. The stakes rose in 2026. Medicaid redeterminations and the 2025 reconciliation law's eligibility checks (NAIC) churn membership, and the end of enhanced tax credits moved Marketplace members between plans and metal levels.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Core-platform enrolment modules, MA enrolment and reconciliation vendors and BPOs, exchange reconciliation tools, and finance reconciliation software. The gaps: discrepancies worked as undifferentiated queues, root causes not classified, and retroactive changes discovered after claims have been paid for months the plan was not paid for.

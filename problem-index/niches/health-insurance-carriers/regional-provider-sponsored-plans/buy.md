# National Payer Analytics Suites Cut Down for Small Plans

**Niche:** [[niches/health-insurance-carriers/regional-provider-sponsored-plans/profile|Regional & Provider-Sponsored Health Plans]]
**Industry:** [[industries/health-insurance-carriers|Health Insurance Carriers]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Payer analytics suites already calculate HEDIS, risk scores and cost trends; small plans need them pre-configured, run as a service, and interpreted for a team without data scientists.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #gradient-boosting #time-series-forecasting #data-integration #revenue-impact #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to give a 30,000-to-300,000-member plan the Stars, risk adjustment and interoperability capability a national carrier staffs internally, and whoever keeps a small plan's Star rating and risk scores level with its national competitors' without new analyst headcount takes the account.

## The Problem
Enterprise payer analytics platforms assume a data engineering team to load and maintain feeds and an analytics team to interpret outputs. A regional plan buys the licence and gets a dashboard nobody has time to read.

## What Already Exists
Payer analytics and quality platforms from Inovalon, Cotiviti, Optum and others; HEDIS engines; risk adjustment analytics; BI tools.

## The Customization Gap
A small-plan edition needs feeds pre-built for the plan's hosted core platform or BPO. It needs a fixed monthly set of decisions rather than open-ended dashboards: which care gaps to chase, which members to assess, which measures are at risk of losing a star. It needs small-denominator statistics that say when a change is noise, and pricing per member that scales down. The vendor's analysts act as the plan's analytics team.

## Target Customer
COOs and quality and finance leaders at plans under ~300,000 members.

## Impact If Solved
National-grade measurement without national-grade headcount, and decisions rather than reports.

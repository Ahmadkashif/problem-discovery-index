# Dual-Eligible & Special Needs Plan Members

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/health-insurance-carriers|Health Insurance Carriers]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to reach each new special needs plan member for a health risk assessment and care plan inside CMS's 90-day window, and whoever completes the most assessments with hard-to-reach dual-eligible members, and keeps them enrolled, takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$2B a year in SNP care coordination staff, member outreach, assessment vendors and model-of-care administration (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~1.5% of carrier administrative spend (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — members are reached by phone, mail and home visits, and many have no stable phone or address
**Target Buyer:** VP of Special Needs Plans / Medicaid–Medicare Integration Lead
**Automation Potential:** Medium — contactability, scheduling and documentation automate; the assessment and the trust it needs do not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Special needs plans served 8.2M people in 2026, 23% of MA enrollees, and accounted for 85% of the year's net MA enrollment growth (KFF). Most are dual-eligible SNPs for people with both Medicare and Medicaid: members with low incomes, high rates of disability, mental illness and housing instability, who move and change phone numbers often. Each plan must run a CMS-approved model of care, complete an initial health risk assessment within 90 days and build an individualised care plan with an interdisciplinary team (recalled). It must also coordinate with the state Medicaid programme under its contract with the state. Tools built for the average MA member, reached by email and app, fail here.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Care management platforms (HealthEdge's GuidingCare, ZeOmega's Jiva and others), outreach call centres and community health worker programmes, in-home assessment vendors, and state Medicaid data feeds. The gaps: no model of who can be reached, how and when; Medicare and Medicaid sides run on separate systems with separate notices; and assessments are counted rather than graded on whether they changed anything.

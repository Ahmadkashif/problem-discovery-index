# New-Client Health Underwriting

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/professional-employer-organizations|Professional Employer Organizations]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to decide, from a census, a few health questionnaires and a prescription-history score, which 25-person prospects to admit to the master plan and at what rate before the broker's quote deadline, and whoever's admitted groups run closest to their priced loss ratio in the first plan year takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$350M a year of new-business underwriting staff, morbidity scoring and pricing consulting at PEOs (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~3.5% of PEO industry gross profit (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low-Medium — census spreadsheets and scanned questionnaires, keyed into rating tools
**Target Buyer:** Head of New-Business Underwriting
**Automation Potential:** High for file assembly and comparables; low for the accept or decline call

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
This is the door. The underwriter sees a group once, with thin data, against a broker's clock, and fixes a price for a year. The PrestigePEO deck sets out the arithmetic: the average PEO client has 25-35 eligible employees, roughly eleven are needed to fund each $25,000 of forecast claims, and new-business models select against high morbidity scores, high claims and credible renewals. A plan of 3,000 subscribers retaining 88% of enrolment needs about 150 quotes a year just to grow 7.6%. The winner is whoever writes volume without adverse selection, a different contest from renewal pricing of clients the PEO already knows.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Carrier and in-house rating tools, morbidity scores built from prescription and claims history (the deck names SAIL and Curv), personal and group health questionnaires, and consultants such as OneDigital, whose PEO pricing role advises "when to walk away". Gaps: questionnaires rekeyed by hand; reasons for decisions and overrides unrecorded; no outcome feedback to the underwriter; declined groups never followed.

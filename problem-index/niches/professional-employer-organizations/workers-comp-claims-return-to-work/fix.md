# Injuries Reported a Week Late

**Niche:** [[niches/professional-employer-organizations/workers-comp-claims-return-to-work/profile|Workers' Comp Claims & Return-to-Work at Client Worksites]]
**Industry:** [[industries/professional-employer-organizations|Professional Employer Organizations]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Client owners report injuries days after they happen, usually when the worker's clinic bill or a lawyer's letter arrives, and every day of delay makes the claim more expensive.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #logistic-regression #evaluation-metrics #worker-facing #quick-win #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get an injury at a client site the PEO never controls reported within a day and the worker back on modified duty at an employer with fifteen staff, and whoever closes the most claims inside the PEO's deductible at the lowest incurred cost takes the account.

## The Problem
The PEO's service agreement tells clients to report injuries immediately. In practice a foreman sends a worker to urgent care, the owner hears about it at the end of the week, and the PEO learns of it when a bill or a call arrives. The late report delays medical management and the first contact with the worker. It also misses state filing windows, which in many states are a few days (estimate).

## Why It's Still Broken
Reporting means a form in a portal the owner rarely uses. Owners fear an injury report will raise their rates, and nobody at the worksite is responsible for reporting.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Text-message injury reporting that any supervisor can use in a minute. Payroll and timekeeping signals that suggest an unreported injury (a sudden unpaid absence after a shift, a timecard note) prompt a check-in with the supervisor. Clients see a simple monthly report showing how reporting speed affects their charges.

## Who Feels the Pain
Claims coordinators starting files late, injured workers waiting for treatment authorisation, and the PEO paying for the delay inside its deductible.

## Impact If Fixed
Reports within a day for most injuries, earlier medical management and fewer claims that turn into litigation.

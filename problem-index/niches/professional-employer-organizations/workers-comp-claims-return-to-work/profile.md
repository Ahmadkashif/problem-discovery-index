# Workers' Comp Claims & Return-to-Work at Client Worksites

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/professional-employer-organizations|Professional Employer Organizations]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get an injury at a client site the PEO never controls reported within a day and the worker back on modified duty at an employer with fifteen staff, and whoever closes the most claims inside the PEO's deductible at the lowest incurred cost takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$900M a year of PEO risk and claims staff, claims administration and nurse case management fees, and loss-control services, excluding claim payments (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~9% of PEO industry gross profit (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — injuries are reported by phone and email from client owners; return-to-work is negotiated by phone
**Target Buyer:** VP of Risk Management / Director of Claims at the PEO
**Automation Potential:** Medium — reporting, forms and follow-ups automate; persuading an owner to offer light duty does not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
For workers' compensation the PEO is the employer, under a master policy covering all clients or a multiple coordinated policy per client, and large PEOs carry the first layer of every claim themselves. Insperity is responsible for the first $1.5M per occurrence under its Chubb programme, up to a $6M aggregate per policy year. ADP cited higher workers' comp costs as one reason its PEO margin fell in fiscal 2026. Yet the PEO controls none of the worksites. Its claims staff must get injuries reported by owners who did not see them, and find modified duty at companies with no desk jobs. The money at stake is the PEO's own retained loss, so the winner is whoever keeps claims small, not whoever processes them fastest.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Carrier and TPA claims systems, nurse case management, state reporting portals, and the PEO's own risk team and loss-control consultants. The vault's TPA layer covers the adjusters ([[niches/insurance-tpa/workers-comp-claims/profile|Workers' Comp Claims TPA]]). Gaps: late first reports from clients; no library of transitional duties for small employers; claim status scattered between carrier, client and coordinator; no early-warning view of which claims will escalate.

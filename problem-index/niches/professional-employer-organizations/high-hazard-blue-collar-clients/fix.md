# Uninsured Subcontractors Found at Premium Audit

**Niche:** [[niches/professional-employer-organizations/high-hazard-blue-collar-clients/profile|High-Hazard & Blue-Collar Clients]]
**Industry:** [[industries/professional-employer-organizations|Professional Employer Organizations]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A contractor client pays subcontractors outside the PEO's payroll, and only at audit, or after an injured sub's claim, does the PEO learn it was covering workers it never priced.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #decision-trees #evaluation-metrics #compliance #quick-win #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to write roofing, trucking and light-manufacturing clients that mainstream PEOs decline, and whoever prices their workers' comp to each crew's actual exposure and enforces safety at the worksite well enough to keep retained losses under the comp premium charged takes the account.

## The Problem
Under most state comp laws an uninsured subcontractor's injured worker can claim against the hiring contractor's coverage. PEO construction clients often use 1099 subcontractors and casual labour paid outside payroll. Certificates of insurance are collected inconsistently or not at all. The exposure surfaces at the carrier's premium audit, measured by the firms logged as [[niches/independent-insurance-agents/premium-audit-services/profile|🔍 Premium Audit Services]], or when a claim arrives.

## Why It's Still Broken
The PEO runs the client's payroll but not its accounts payable, where subcontractor payments are recorded. Certificate tracking is a manual job nobody at a small contractor owns.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Quarterly collection of the client's vendor payment list. Each payee is classified as a subcontractor, supplier or casual labour, and matched to a current certificate of insurance and state coverage records where available. Gaps are flagged for the client with a deadline before the next audit, and the findings feed the client's comp pricing.

## Who Feels the Pain
PEO risk and audit staff, contractors hit with retroactive charges, and injured subcontractor workers whose claims become disputes.

## Impact If Fixed
Fewer audit surprises and uncovered claims, and comp priced on the full labour the client actually uses.

# High-Hazard & Blue-Collar Clients

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/professional-employer-organizations|Professional Employer Organizations]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to write roofing, trucking and light-manufacturing clients that mainstream PEOs decline, and whoever prices their workers' comp to each crew's actual exposure and enforces safety at the worksite well enough to keep retained losses under the comp premium charged takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$600M a year of workers' comp programme, safety and claims-management spend attributable to PEO clients in construction, transport and manufacturing (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~6% of PEO industry gross profit (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — job sites, paper safety logs, and payroll by crew sent in spreadsheets
**Target Buyer:** Owner of a specialty PEO, or VP of Risk at a regional PEO with a construction book
**Automation Potential:** Low-Medium — classification and reporting automate; safety on the site does not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The large PEOs' sweet spot is professional services, the largest client group at 19% (NAPEO, 2025). Insperity says it avoids certain higher-risk NAICS codes. Yet construction is 12% of PEO clients, and the IRS's CPEO list includes PEOs built around trucking. For a 20-person roofer or a small fleet, the PEO's value is mostly workers' comp: access to a programme the client could not buy alone, or at a mod it could not get. The risk is concentrated and physical, and the PEO prices it from payroll by class code it did not verify, at worksites it has never seen. The PEO wins these clients by underwriting crews and enforcing safety, not by HR software.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Comp programmes for mixed-class PEO books (Amwins' PEO programme quotes NCCI hazard groups A-F), loss-control consultants, contractor safety apps, and premium audit. The underwriters behind staffing firms face the same unseen-worksite problem ([[niches/staffing-agencies/staffing-workers-comp-underwriting/profile|🔍 Staffing Workers' Compensation Underwriting]]). Gaps: payroll by crew and task unverified until audit; uninsured subcontractors found late; safety requirements in the service agreement but rarely enforced.

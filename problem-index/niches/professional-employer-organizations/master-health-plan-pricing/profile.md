# Master Health Plan Pricing & Underwriting

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/professional-employer-organizations|Professional Employer Organizations]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is that every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep a PEO's master health plan at its target loss ratio while still undercutting what small employers would pay on their own. But that is two contests with different winners: selecting and pricing prospects from a census and a few morbidity flags before a broker's quote deadline, which new-business underwriters and scoring vendors win, and setting each in-force client's renewal so low-claim groups stay and the plan does not spiral, which renewal actuaries and account teams win. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$700M a year of underwriting, actuarial, consulting, morbidity-scoring and plan-management spend on PEO master health plans (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~7% of PEO industry gross profit (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — census files, rating tools and carrier reports are digital, but the decisions run on spreadsheets and underwriters' judgement
**Target Buyer:** VP of Benefits / Chief Underwriting Officer at the PEO
**Automation Potential:** Medium — file assembly and reporting automate; acceptance and rate decisions stay with accountable underwriters

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The master health plan is why most clients join a PEO and why most PEOs have bad years. NAPEO estimates over 1M people get medical coverage through PEOs. The PEO sponsors one large-group plan, usually fully insured or minimum-premium with a carrier such as UnitedHealthcare (about 85% of Insperity's participants), and must price each small client into it. The claims are lumpy: in a cross-PEO study, the 15% of groups running above 125% MLR carried half of all claims. A few points of loss ratio decide the year. Insperity's 2025 gross profit fell 14% on benefits costs, and TriNet's insurance cost ratio was 91%.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Carrier rating tools and experience reports; prescription-based morbidity scores; health questionnaires; PEO health-plan consultants such as OneDigital's PEO practice; in-house actuaries at the largest PEOs. The gap: no record links the acceptance decision and each renewal action to the client's subsequent cost and retention, so the PEO cannot tell whether a bad plan year came from new business, from renewals or from chance.

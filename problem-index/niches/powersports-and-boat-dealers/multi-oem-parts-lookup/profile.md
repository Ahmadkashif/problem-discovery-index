# Parts Lookup & Special Orders Across OEM Catalogs

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/powersports-and-boat-dealers|Powersports & Boat Dealers]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn a customer's description, photo or old part into the right current part number across several OEM and aftermarket catalogs, and whoever has the lowest wrong-part return rate takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$300M a year in parts-counter labour, wrong-part returns, restocking and obsolete stock tied to lookups, plus catalog software (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** Parts and accessories are a large share of the non-unit gross (OneWater's non-boat sales were 41.7% of gross profit in fiscal 2025)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — electronic catalogs are digital, but matching a worn part to a diagram is done by eye and memory
**Target Buyer:** Parts Manager
**Automation Potential:** High for identification, supersession and ordering

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A multi-line store keeps several OEM electronic parts catalogs — exploded diagrams, reference numbers, serial breaks, supersession chains — plus aftermarket distributor catalogs for cheaper equivalents. Customers bring a broken part in a bag or describe "the water pump thing". The associate's skill is matching it to the right diagram and number; a wrong match costs a week and a return. Car-repair aftermarket lookups run on a shared fitment standard maintained by catalog-data firms ([[niches/auto-repair-shops/parts-catalog-data-operations/profile|Aftermarket Parts Catalog Data Operations]]); powersports and marine OEM catalogs are diagram-driven and proprietary to each OEM.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Dealer Spike's PartSmart and the OEMs' own catalogs; distributor catalogs; DMS parts inventory; parts sections on dealer websites. Gaps: no cross-catalog search, no photo identification, no warning before ordering across a serial break, and no record of which lookups produced returns.

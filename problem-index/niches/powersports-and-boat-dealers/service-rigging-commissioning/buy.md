# Automotive Shop Scheduling Taught About Water Tests

**Niche:** [[niches/powersports-and-boat-dealers/service-rigging-commissioning/profile|Service, Rigging & Commissioning]]
**Industry:** [[industries/powersports-and-boat-dealers|Powersports & Boat Dealers]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Car-dealer service scheduling is mature; it assumes a vehicle that drives in and out the same day, not a boat on a trailer waiting for a water test.
**Tags:** #time-series-forecasting #workflow-orchestration #data-integration #worker-facing #automation
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is that every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn technician hours into billed, right-first-time work, but the spring queue of customers' boats and machines and the rigging bay that prepares new inventory for delivery are won on different measures (billed hours per technician across the year; comebacks in the first 30 days after delivery) by different vendors. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## The Problem
Marine and powersports service departments run multi-day jobs on units that arrive on trailers, sit outside, need a lift or a test tank, and often need pick-up and delivery from a storage yard or a slip.

## What Already Exists
Automotive service scheduling and service-lane platforms book appointments by labour time, manage loaner cars and send status texts, and are used by thousands of franchised stores.

## The Customization Gap
Needed: multi-day job blocks; yard, lift and water-test slots as scarce resources; pick-up and delivery runs with a truck and trailer; winterization and storage contracts as a pre-booked demand book; skills by OEM and engine type; and seasonal capacity planning rather than daily slot filling. The rigging queue needs its own view because it is internal work tied to sales delivery dates.

## Target Customer
Service managers at marine dealers with storage and at powersports stores with heavy spring volume.

## Impact If Solved
A shop that loses one technician-day a week to lift, trailer and water-test bottlenecks in season (estimate) recovers 60–80 billed hours a season by scheduling those resources explicitly.

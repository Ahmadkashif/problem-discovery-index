# The Rigging Bay That Steals Spring Technicians

**Niche:** [[niches/powersports-and-boat-dealers/service-rigging-commissioning/profile|Service, Rigging & Commissioning]]
**Industry:** [[industries/powersports-and-boat-dealers|Powersports & Boat Dealers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Every boat sold at the winter show needs rigging in the same weeks customers want their boats commissioned, and the sales manager wins the argument by shouting.
**Tags:** #workflow-orchestration #time-series-forecasting #quick-win #worker-facing #automation
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is that every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn technician hours into billed, right-first-time work, but the spring queue of customers' boats and machines and the rigging bay that prepares new inventory for delivery are won on different measures (billed hours per technician across the year; comebacks in the first 30 days after delivery) by different vendors. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## The Problem
Boat-show sales in January–March (recalled) produce a wave of deliveries promised for May. Rigging those boats pulls the best technicians off customer work exactly when the commissioning backlog is longest, and customers wait weeks.

## Why It's Still Broken
Rigging hours are booked to the sale, not to the service schedule, so they don't show on the service manager's board until the boat is pulled in. Delivery dates are promised by salespeople without seeing shop capacity.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Make every sold-not-delivered unit visible on the service board with rigging hours and promised date the moment the deal is written, and show the salesperson the earliest realistic delivery date before they promise one. Rig early-sold boats in February and March when the shop is quieter.

## Who Feels the Pain
Service managers and technicians caught between two queues; customers waiting for commissioning; buyers whose new boat isn't ready.

## Impact If Fixed
Moving even a third of spring rigging hours into the pre-season (estimate) frees roughly 10% of peak technician capacity for customer work at a store that rigs 150 boats a year.

# A Rigging Record for Every Hull

**Niche:** [[niches/powersports-and-boat-dealers/rigging-and-pre-delivery/profile|New-Unit Rigging & Pre-Delivery Inspection]]
**Industry:** [[industries/powersports-and-boat-dealers|Powersports & Boat Dealers]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** The store can't say which rigger set up the boat that came back with a steering fault, or which step they skipped, because rigging leaves no record beyond a signed checklist.
**Tags:** #cnns #object-detection #logistic-regression #workflow-orchestration #worker-facing #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to deliver every new boat and machine rigged and set up right the first time, and whoever has the fewest comebacks in the first 30 days after delivery takes the account.

## The Problem
Comebacks in the first month — a loose steering cable, a wrong propeller, engine height off, a battery switch wired wrong, a side-by-side accessory installed so it chafes a line — are costly. They cost a second trip, a warranty dispute and a customer's first impression.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Rigging is internal work with no customer invoice, so the DMS records hours, not steps. OEM checklists are per brand and on paper, and the hull and engine OEMs each have their own.

## What to Build
A configuration-driven rigging workflow on a tablet: from the deal's hull, engine, controls and options it generates the kit list and the ordered steps from both OEMs' guides, asks for photo evidence at critical steps (cable routing, engine mounting height, prop, wiring), uses a vision model to check a few of them, logs the water-test readings, and stores the record against the HIN. Comebacks within 30 days are linked to the record and the step that failed, and riggers see their own first-time-right rate.

## Target Customer
Service managers and rigging leads at marine dealers that rig 100 or more boats a year; set-up leads at high-volume powersports stores.

## Impact If Built
Halving 30-day comebacks at a store that rigs 150 boats a year with a 10% comeback rate (estimate) saves 75 repair visits and the goodwill and claim disputes behind them.

# New-Unit Rigging & Pre-Delivery Inspection

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/powersports-and-boat-dealers|Powersports & Boat Dealers]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to deliver every new boat and machine rigged and set up right the first time, and whoever has the fewest comebacks in the first 30 days after delivery takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1B a year in rigging, set-up and pre-delivery labour and the early-ownership warranty and goodwill it drives (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~1.5% of receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — OEM pre-delivery checklists on paper or PDF, rigging done from the rigger's experience
**Target Buyer:** Service Manager / Rigging Lead
**Automation Potential:** Low for the work, high for checklists, parts kitting and documentation

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A new boat leaves the factory without the dealer's choice of engine, or with it uninstalled; the dealer mates a hull from one OEM to an outboard from another (Mercury, Yamaha, Suzuki), installs controls, steering, gauges, batteries, electronics and propeller, sets engine height, matches the trailer, and water-tests it. Powersports units arrive crated and need assembly, fluids, accessory installation and an OEM pre-delivery inspection. Mistakes show up as comebacks in the first weeks of ownership, often as warranty claims the OEM later disputes. Brunswick's 10-K lists "rigging" among the Mercury propulsion segment's products, so rigging parts are themselves a product line. The contest is first-time quality, not hours.

## Current Tools & Gaps
OEM rigging guides and pre-delivery checklists; OEM installation certification for some engine lines; DMS internal repair orders. Gaps: no structured record of what was installed and checked on each unit, no parts kit generated from the configuration, and no link from 30-day comebacks back to the rigger and the step that failed.

# Utility Field-Service Scheduling for a Boat Shop

**Niche:** [[niches/powersports-and-boat-dealers/seasonal-service-capacity/profile|Spring Commissioning & Seasonal Service Capacity]]
**Industry:** [[industries/powersports-and-boat-dealers|Powersports & Boat Dealers]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** HVAC and pool-service contractors already plan seasonal demand from maintenance agreements; boat dealers hold the same kind of contracts and don't use them.
**Tags:** #time-series-forecasting #exponential-smoothing #workflow-orchestration #data-integration #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to fill the service department's technician hours through the spring commissioning peak and the winter trough, and whoever bills the most technician hours per technician across the year takes the account.

## The Problem
Seasonal service businesses with maintenance agreements smooth demand by pre-scheduling tune-ups before the peak. Marine and powersports dealers sell winterization and storage contracts that predict spring demand almost exactly, then wait for the phone to ring.

## What Already Exists
Field-service management software for HVAC, plumbing and pool contractors schedules maintenance-agreement visits ahead of the season, dispatches by skill and texts customers arrival windows.

## The Customization Gap
Needed: work done in the shop, not at the customer's home, with yard, lift and water-test slots as constraints; job types defined by engine and hull (outboard, sterndrive, PWC, side-by-side); OEM recall campaigns injected as demand; pick-up from storage yards and slips; and DMS integration for repair orders and parts.

## Target Customer
Service managers and fixed-operations directors at marine and powersports dealers.

## Impact If Solved
Turning storage contracts into pre-booked commissioning (estimate: half of stored units booked by February) gives a store a firm spring plan and 5–10% more billed hours in the peak.

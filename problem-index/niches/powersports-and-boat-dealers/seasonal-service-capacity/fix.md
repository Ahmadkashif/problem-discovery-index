# "Is My Boat Ready?" Forty Times a Day

**Niche:** [[niches/powersports-and-boat-dealers/seasonal-service-capacity/profile|Spring Commissioning & Seasonal Service Capacity]]
**Industry:** [[industries/powersports-and-boat-dealers|Powersports & Boat Dealers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** In May the service writer and the technicians spend hours answering status calls the repair order could answer itself.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #workflow-orchestration #quick-win #worker-facing #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to fill the service department's technician hours through the spring commissioning peak and the winter trough, and whoever bills the most technician hours per technician across the year takes the account.

## The Problem
Customers waiting for commissioning call, text and walk in to ask where their boat or machine is. The service writer asks the technician, the technician stops work, and the answer is usually "waiting on a part" or "next week".

## Why It's Still Broken
Status lives in the technician's head and on the whiteboard, not in repair-order fields a texting tool could read. Texting tools send what a person types.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Make repair-order status structured (checked in, diagnosed, waiting on parts with ETA, in progress, water test, ready) and updated by the technician with a tap or a voice note; then let an agent answer status questions by text and phone from that record, escalating only real exceptions.

## Who Feels the Pain
Service writers, flat-rate technicians interrupted mid-job, and customers who want their weekend.

## Impact If Fixed
Cutting status interruptions by two-thirds (estimate) gives each technician back 30–45 minutes a day in the peak, roughly 30–40 billed hours a season.

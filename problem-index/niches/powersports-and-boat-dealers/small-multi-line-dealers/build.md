# An F&I Office in a Tablet

**Niche:** [[niches/powersports-and-boat-dealers/small-multi-line-dealers/profile|Small Multi-Line Dealers Without an F&I Office]]
**Industry:** [[industries/powersports-and-boat-dealers|Powersports & Boat Dealers]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Half the volume at one large recreation lender already comes through outside providers doing F&I for small dealers, because those dealers can't staff it themselves.
**Tags:** #gradient-boosting #logistic-regression #large-language-models #compliance #workflow-orchestration #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to earn finance and protection-product income on deals at stores with no F&I manager, and whoever books the most back-end gross per retail unit at those stores takes the account.

## The Problem
At a ten-person store, the owner or a salesperson takes the credit application, sends it to the lender they know, prints the contract, maybe mentions a service contract, and moves on. Approvals that a different lender would have given are lost, rate reserve is left on the table, protection products go unoffered, and the paperwork is error-prone. Or the deal goes to an outside F&I provider who keeps a share.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
F&I software is sold to stores with F&I managers and priced per seat. Lender-built tools route to that lender. Small stores are hard to reach and slow to adopt.

## What to Build
A tablet workflow for the salesperson: credit application once; an approval model trained on the store's and the network's decisions routes it to the lenders most likely to approve this buyer for this unit; offers compared on payment and term; a compliant product menu with disclosures; e-contracting; and the title and registration packet generated from the same deal. Every step logged for lender and state audits.

## Target Customer
Owners of single-store powersports and marine dealers carrying several OEM lines.

## Impact If Built
Adding $300–600 of back-end gross per retail unit (estimate) at a store selling 250 units a year is $75,000–150,000 a year, often more than the owner's own salary.

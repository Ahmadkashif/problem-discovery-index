# The Failed Part Thrown Away Before the OEM Asked for It

**Niche:** [[niches/powersports-and-boat-dealers/oem-warranty-recall-claims/profile|OEM Warranty & Recall Claim Filing]]
**Industry:** [[industries/powersports-and-boat-dealers|Powersports & Boat Dealers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The OEM requests the failed part back weeks after the repair, the part is in the scrap bin, and the claim is charged back.
**Tags:** #workflow-orchestration #data-integration #quick-win #automation #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get warranty and recall claims paid across every OEM line a dealer carries, and whoever has the highest share of claim dollars paid on first submission and kept through audit takes the account.

## The Problem
Parts-retention and return rules differ by OEM and by part. Technicians tag some parts and toss others; when a return request arrives, nobody can find the part.

## Why It's Still Broken
Retention rules live in each OEM's policy and portal, not in the shop. Nothing tells the technician at the bench which parts to keep and for how long.

## What a Fix Looks Like
When a warranty line is opened, tell the technician whether to retain the part, print a tag with claim number and retention date, and track retained parts on a shelf map until the OEM releases or requests them.

## Who Feels the Pain
Warranty administrators facing chargebacks; technicians blamed for lost parts; service managers reconciling claims.

## Impact If Fixed
Ending part-return chargebacks (~2–4 a month at a busy multi-line store, estimate) saves ~$3,000–8,000 a year (estimate) and hours of shelf searching.

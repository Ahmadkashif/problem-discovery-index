# Variant Interpretation Knowledge Bases & Curation Vendors

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/clinical-laboratories|Clinical Laboratories]]
**Category:** Insight Layer
**Value-Chain Position:** Data & benchmark vendors

## What They Do
Vendors that read the genetics literature and curate what is known about each variant and gene, then sell that evidence to the labs that must classify variants in patient reports. Some also sell interpretation software in which labs classify their own cases, and disease landscapes to drug developers. QIAGEN Digital Insights (HGMD, QCI), Genomenon, Velsera, Fabric Genomics, Franklin by Genoox and Sophia Genetics (Switzerland) are examples (recalled). Labs pay subscriptions; pharma pays per project.

## Insight Function
**Size:** ~20-200 PhD curators, variant scientists and bioinformaticians per vendor (estimate)
**Output:** Curated variant and gene evidence, classification support aligned with ACMG criteria, literature landscapes for rare diseases
**Proprietary data:** A curated, structured layer over the published literature, built and maintained for years; the literature itself is public and ClinVar is a free competitor
**Clock:** The vendor's own release cadence; labs' report turnaround is the labs' clock
**Buyer:** Chief scientific officer or head of curation at the vendor

**Kill switches:** none for the literature-curated knowledge base, which is the vendor's own work on public sources; undetermined for the interpretation-platform half, where labs' patient variant data sits under customer agreements not read here. The switch, if any, is on the platform half.
**Verdict:** Logged — below threshold. Curation is labour-linear and the curated layer is genuinely the vendor's, but no outside party sets its deadlines and ClinVar and public resources cap the moat. Variant classification in the labs themselves is production work by variant scientists, so it is not a pocket.

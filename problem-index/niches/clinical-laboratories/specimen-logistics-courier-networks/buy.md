# Route Optimisation Software Given Specimen Clocks

**Niche:** [[niches/clinical-laboratories/specimen-logistics-courier-networks/profile|Specimen Logistics & Courier Networks]]
**Industry:** [[industries/clinical-laboratories|Clinical Laboratories]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Delivery routing software already plans fleets with time windows; it knows nothing about a specimen that becomes useless after a set number of hours unspun.
**Tags:** #optimization-fundamentals #time-series-forecasting #data-integration #automation #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get specimens from practices and patient service centres to the bench inside their stability windows, and whoever delivers the lowest rate of rejected or recollected specimens per thousand picked up takes the account.

## The Problem
Labs that adopt routing software use it like a parcel company: stops, time windows, minimised miles. Specimen integrity is managed by rules of thumb and driver experience.

## What Already Exists
Commercial route optimisation and dispatch platforms (Onfleet, OptimoRoute, Descartes and similar), plus courier-company dispatch systems.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs stop-level readiness forecasts instead of fixed windows, and specimen-type stability limits (ambient, refrigerated, frozen, time-critical) as constraints. It needs pickup scanning that records specimen count and temperature at the stop, an LIS link so rejections and stability comments flow back to the route, and lockbox temperature alerts in hot and cold seasons.

## Target Customer
Regional and outreach labs that already pay for routing software or contracted couriers.

## Impact If Solved
The lab keeps its existing fleet tools and adds the specimen logic, avoiding a custom build. A ~20% cut in stability rejections (estimate) comes from re-sequencing routes it already runs.

# Specimen Logistics & Courier Networks

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/clinical-laboratories|Clinical Laboratories]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get specimens from practices and patient service centres to the bench inside their stability windows, and whoever delivers the lowest rate of rejected or recollected specimens per thousand picked up takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$2B a year in lab-run and contracted courier fleets, drivers, routing and specimen transport supplies (estimate; BLS counts 15,740 couriers in labs and imaging centres, mean pay $39,330)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~4% of industry revenue (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — fixed routes, driver sheets and lockboxes; scanning at pickup is patchy outside the national labs
**Target Buyer:** Director of logistics or lab operations at a reference, regional or outreach lab
**Automation Potential:** Medium — forecasting, routing and exception alerts automate well; the driving and the lockbox do not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A lab's courier network is its supply chain, and the goods spoil. Unspun blood starts shifting potassium and glucose within hours; some tests need refrigeration, freezing, light protection or delivery within a set time. Routes are fixed loops past practices and lockboxes on schedules set years ago. Drivers often arrive before the afternoon's draws are ready, or after specimens have sat in a hot lockbox. The failure is counted at the bench as a rejected specimen or, worse, an altered result. Third-party couriers that serve many shippers are profiled as [[niches/last-mile-delivery/medical-courier-services/profile|🟠 Medical Courier Services]]; this niche is the lab's own network and its contest is specimen integrity, not delivery margin.

## Current Tools & Gaps
National labs run their own fleets, scanning and air networks; others use routing software, contracted couriers (MedSpeed and local firms) and paper manifests. Gaps: routes are not tied to practice draw schedules, specimens are not scanned at the stop, and nothing links a rejection back to the route and the minutes it spent in transit.

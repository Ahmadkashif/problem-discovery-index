# Prior-Authorization Automation Rebuilt for Lab-Initiated Requests

**Niche:** [[niches/clinical-laboratories/molecular-genetic-coverage-prior-auth/profile|Molecular & Genetic Test Coverage and Prior Authorization]]
**Industry:** [[industries/clinical-laboratories|Clinical Laboratories]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Prior-authorization automation exists for the ordering practice and the payer; the lab sits between them without the patient's chart and has to request authorization on someone else's behalf.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #workflow-orchestration #automation #compliance #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get each high-dollar molecular or genetic test paid, through authorization, Z-code, coverage evidence and appeal, and whoever collects on the highest share of tests performed takes the account.

## The Problem
Many payers and benefit managers let the performing lab request authorization, but the clinical evidence lives in the ordering office's EHR. Lab reimbursement staff phone and fax offices for notes, re-key them into portals, and track dozens of payer-specific forms.

## What Already Exists
Provider-side prior-authorization tools built into EHRs and revenue cycle suites, electronic prior-authorization networks, payer-side automation from vendors such as Cohere, and portal-automation agents.

## The Customization Gap
The lab version needs a request-for-records workflow to the ordering office, test-specific criteria libraries (personal and family history for hereditary panels, staging and prior lines of therapy for tumour profiling), and Z-code and MolDX article matching. It must tie each submission to the sample's run or hold status and track retroactive-authorization windows by payer. Appeals have to cite the specific policy clause and the evidence, drafted automatically.

## Target Customer
Reimbursement teams at genetic and oncology labs; lab RCM vendors adding a molecular module.

## Impact If Solved
Authorizations obtained before or soon after the run instead of after the denial cut prior-authorization denials, 27.6% of molecular denials in 2025. Halving them on a lab denying $40M a year recovers ~$5M (estimate).

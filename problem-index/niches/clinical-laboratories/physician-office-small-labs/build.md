# An Inspection-Readiness Ledger Fed by the Analysers

**Niche:** [[niches/clinical-laboratories/physician-office-small-labs/profile|Physician Office & Small Independent Labs]]
**Industry:** [[industries/clinical-laboratories|Clinical Laboratories]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A small lab learns whether it is compliant when the inspector arrives; its analysers already record most of what the inspector will ask for.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #descriptive-statistics #change-point-detection #compliance #automation #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep a small moderate-complexity lab inspection-ready on a part-time director's hours, and whoever gets it through its two-yearly inspection with no condition-level deficiency takes the account.

## The Problem
A physician office lab running chemistry, haematology and a few immunoassays must keep QC within rules, verify calibration every six months, enrol in proficiency testing, assess each tester's competency, maintain current procedures and document corrective actions. The director signs off monthly, the office manager keeps binders, and gaps are discovered at inspection.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Each requirement is served by a different tool or binder, and small labs are a fragmented market that enterprise quality vendors don't pursue. Analyser data stays in the analyser.

## What to Build
Pull QC, calibration and maintenance events from the analysers and the small-lab LIS. Check them continuously against CLIA and accreditor requirements, detect QC drift and rule violations the day they happen, and track proficiency results and competency due dates per person. Draft corrective-action records and procedure updates with a language model for the director's signature, and show one inspection-readiness view with every item green, amber or red.

## Target Customer
Practice administrators and CLIA directors of physician office labs and small independents; accreditors and PT providers as distribution partners.

## Impact If Built
Fewer deficiencies and no pre-inspection scramble. At ~40 staff hours saved per inspection cycle and ~2 hours a month of director review (estimates), the value per lab is modest, but across tens of thousands of non-waived labs it is a real market.

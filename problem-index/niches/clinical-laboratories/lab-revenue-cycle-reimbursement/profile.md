# Lab Revenue Cycle & Reimbursement

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/clinical-laboratories|Clinical Laboratories]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is that every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to collect what each requisition is owed. But routine tests and molecular or genetic tests are won on different things. Routine testing is a volume game of a few dollars a line, won by whoever recovers the most net dollars per thousand claims through the 2027 fee schedule reset. Molecular testing is a coverage game of hundreds or thousands of dollars a test, won by whoever gets paid on the highest share of tests performed through authorization, Z-codes and appeals. Staff, vendors and win conditions differ, so the niche is decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$5B a year in lab billing staff, revenue cycle software, outsourced billing and appeals work across US labs (estimate, about a tenth of the $48.1B Census revenue base)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~10% of industry revenue spent collecting it (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — claims and remittances are electronic, but the data behind them comes from incomplete requisitions, and appeals are still written by hand
**Target Buyer:** VP of Revenue Cycle or CFO at a reference, specialty or regional lab; outreach director at a health-system lab
**Automation Potential:** High for prediction, coding checks and variance detection; medium for appeals that need clinical records

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A lab bills for patients it never met, on information supplied by another office, at prices set by a federal fee schedule that is about to drop. CMS's preliminary CY2027 rates lower 1,171 of 1,947 codes, by about 16% on average, with cuts capped at 15% a year to 2029. Medicare paid $8.4B for lab tests in 2024, 43% of it for genetic tests. Denials split cleanly by test type: bundling edits dominate routine lab denials (59.4% in XiFin's 2025 data), prior authorization dominates molecular ones (27.6%).

## Current Tools & Gaps
XiFin (73% of the largest labs by its own count), Telcor and Quadax run lab billing; clearinghouses and Availity carry claims; Quest is rebuilding order-to-cash with Epic. Hospital and physician tools from [[industries/medical-billing|Medical Billing]] cover generic scrubbing. The gaps are prediction at accessioning, contract variance against a moving schedule, and coverage work for high-dollar tests.

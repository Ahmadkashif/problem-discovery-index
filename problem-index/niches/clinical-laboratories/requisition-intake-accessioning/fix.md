# The Problem-Specimen Shelf

**Niche:** [[niches/clinical-laboratories/requisition-intake-accessioning/profile|Requisition Intake & Accessioning]]
**Industry:** [[industries/clinical-laboratories|Clinical Laboratories]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Specimens with unmatched labels or incomplete requisitions sit on a shelf while client services phones offices, and some age past their stability limits before anyone calls back.
**Tags:** #workflow-orchestration #large-language-models #descriptive-statistics #automation #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to register each specimen and its requisition completely on first touch, and whoever accessions the highest share without a callback to the ordering office takes the account.

## The Problem
Every lab has a problem-specimen area: tubes with no label, a name that doesn't match the requisition, a test that can't be read, no order at all. Each needs a call to the ordering office, which may be closed, and the specimen ages while it waits. Many end as recollections.

## Why It's Still Broken
Problem specimens are tracked on whiteboards, paper logs or spreadsheets, and calls are made in no particular order. Nobody reports which offices generate the most problems, so the same mistakes recur every week.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Log every problem specimen with its issue and stability deadline, and contact the ordering office through its EHR inbox or portal with the exact question. Prioritise by time left before the specimen expires, and report problem rates by office so client services can retrain the worst ten.

## Who Feels the Pain
Accessioners and client-services staff making repeat calls, patients recalled for recollection, and ordering offices interrupted for preventable errors.

## Impact If Fixed
Resolving problem specimens before they expire avoids recollections. A lab with 200 problem specimens a day that saves a quarter of them from expiry avoids ~12,000 recollections a year (estimate).

# Requisition Intake & Accessioning

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/clinical-laboratories|Clinical Laboratories]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to register each specimen and its requisition completely on first touch, and whoever accessions the highest share without a callback to the ordering office takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.5B a year in accessioning, specimen processing and client-services labour plus document capture and LIS order entry tools (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~3% of industry revenue (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low-Medium — electronic orders cover much of the volume, but paper requisitions, handwritten labels and mismatches are keyed and resolved by hand
**Target Buyer:** Director of specimen processing or lab operations; client services manager
**Automation Potential:** High — document reading, order matching and label checking are mechanical; specimen handling is not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Accessioning is where a bag of tubes and a requisition become a patient record in the LIS. Accessioners match each tube to an order, read handwriting, find the patient, key tests and diagnosis codes, check labels against the requisition, and set aside the specimens that can't be matched. These "problem specimens" wait while client services phone the ordering office. Mislabelled and unlabelled specimens must be rejected or recollected under lab policy. The non-human sibling of this work, coolers and chain-of-custody forms in environmental and food labs, is [[niches/testing-laboratories/sample-receiving-chain-of-custody/profile|🟠 Sample Receiving, Login & Chain of Custody]]. Here every error can attach a result to the wrong patient.

## Current Tools & Gaps
LIS order entry and outreach modules, EHR order interfaces, requisition scanning and indexing, label printers at patient service centres, and front-end automation lines in the largest labs. Gaps: paper requisitions keyed by hand, no reading of handwriting or faxed orders, no check that billing-critical fields are complete, and problem specimens tracked on whiteboards.

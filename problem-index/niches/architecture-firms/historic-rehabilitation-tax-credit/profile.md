# Historic Rehabilitation & Tax-Credit Projects

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/architecture-firms|Architecture Firms]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get a rehabilitation through State Historic Preservation Office and National Park Service review without costly redesign while documenting existing conditions accurately enough to price, and whoever secures the tax-credit approval first time takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$3B in architecture fees on certified historic rehabilitations and other preservation projects (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~3% (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — existing conditions measured by hand or laser scan, archival drawings on paper or microfilm, approvals by narrative application
**Target Buyer:** Preservation architect or principal; developer using historic tax credits
**Automation Potential:** Medium — survey processing and application assembly automate; preservation judgement does not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Renovation, retrofit and preservation together account for about 45% of architecture billings, and the historic subset runs on its own approval track. A developer claiming the federal historic rehabilitation tax credit must have the building certified and the work approved in a three-part application reviewed by the State Historic Preservation Office and the National Park Service against the Secretary of the Interior's Standards for Rehabilitation (recalled structure). An unapproved change can cost the credit, which often makes the project financeable. Existing buildings rarely match their drawings, so documentation drives both approval and cost.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Laser scanning and scan-to-BIM services; Revit and AutoCAD; photo documentation; application forms prepared in Word. Gaps: point clouds converted to models by hand; reviewer comments from past applications unstructured; condition surveys not linked to scope and cost.

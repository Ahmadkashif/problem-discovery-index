# Scan-to-BIM Tuned for Historic Fabric

**Niche:** [[niches/architecture-firms/historic-rehabilitation-tax-credit/profile|Historic Rehabilitation & Tax-Credit Projects]]
**Industry:** [[industries/architecture-firms|Architecture Firms]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Point-cloud tools model rectangular offices well and historic façades badly.
**Tags:** #semantic-segmentation #cnns #transfer-learning #automation #data-integration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get a rehabilitation through State Historic Preservation Office and National Park Service review without costly redesign while documenting existing conditions accurately enough to price, and whoever secures the tax-credit approval first time takes the account.

## The Problem
Preservation firms buy scanning and point-cloud software built for new construction verification and modern renovation. Applied to a masonry warehouse or a theatre, the automated modelling fails on arches, irregular walls and ornament, and modellers fall back to manual tracing.

## What Already Exists
Point-cloud processing and scan-to-BIM tools from the laser scanner vendors and Autodesk ReCap, plus scan-to-BIM outsourcing services.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs: segmentation retrained on historic building types; tolerance settings that preserve real deviation rather than squaring it off; feature libraries for historic windows, cornices and mouldings; condition annotation (spalling, rot, cracking) carried as data; and outputs matched to preservation documentation standards.

## Target Customer
Preservation architects and the scanning firms that serve them.

## Impact If Solved
Existing-conditions documentation becomes a fraction of today's modelling effort using tools the firm already owns.

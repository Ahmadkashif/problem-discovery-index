# The Part 2 Application Written From a Blank Page

**Niche:** [[niches/architecture-firms/historic-rehabilitation-tax-credit/profile|Historic Rehabilitation & Tax-Credit Projects]]
**Industry:** [[industries/architecture-firms|Architecture Firms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Each tax-credit application re-argues treatments the same reviewers have accepted or rejected many times before.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #bert #k-nearest-neighbors #compliance #quick-win #workflow-orchestration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get a rehabilitation through State Historic Preservation Office and National Park Service review without costly redesign while documenting existing conditions accurately enough to price, and whoever secures the tax-credit approval first time takes the account.

## The Problem
The description of rehabilitation in a tax-credit application walks through each feature — windows, storefronts, interior finishes, new additions — with photos and the proposed treatment. Reviewers' conditions and denials follow recognisable patterns: window replacement, interior subdivision, rooftop additions. Firms write each application fresh.

## Why It's Still Broken
Past applications, reviewer conditions and outcomes sit in project folders; preservation practices are small and have no one assigned to build a precedent library.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A precedent register of every treatment the firm has proposed, the reviewer's response and the final condition, searchable by feature and building type, used to draft each new application section and to warn when a proposed treatment has been conditioned or denied before.

## Who Feels the Pain
Preservation architects; developers whose financing depends on the credit; owners facing redesign after review.

## Impact If Fixed
Fewer conditions and resubmissions, and faster applications built from the firm's own record.

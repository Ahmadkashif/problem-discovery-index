# The Escalation Assumption Nobody Revisits

**Niche:** [[niches/architecture-firms/k12-civic-bond-programs/profile|Public K-12 & Civic Bond Programmes]]
**Industry:** [[industries/architecture-firms|Architecture Firms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A bond budget assumed an escalation rate years ago, and the design team discovers at bid day that the market moved.
**Tags:** #time-series-forecasting #exponential-smoothing #evaluation-metrics #revenue-impact #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to deliver a voter-approved bond programme on its promised budget and schedule through state agency review (the Division of the State Architect in California), and whoever can evidence on-budget delivery wins the next qualifications-based selection.

## The Problem
Projects late in a multi-year bond programme are designed against budgets set with an escalation rate chosen when the bond was sold. When construction costs outrun it, the later projects carry the shortfall, and scope gets cut after design is largely done.

## Why It's Still Broken
The assumption sits in a bond document no one reopens, the cost consultant's phase estimates escalate to midpoint of construction using their own rate, and no one compares the two until the bids arrive.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A programme dashboard that re-escalates every remaining project's budget each quarter against current regional cost indices and recent local bid results, showing which projects are now short and by how much, early enough to re-sequence or rescope before design.

## Who Feels the Pain
District facilities directors facing their board; architects asked to redesign to budget unpaid; communities who were promised the project.

## Impact If Fixed
Shortfalls surfaced years earlier, when sequencing and scope can still absorb them.

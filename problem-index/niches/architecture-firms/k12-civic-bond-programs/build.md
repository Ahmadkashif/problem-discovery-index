**Industry:** [[industries/architecture-firms|Architecture Firms]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** The number voters approved, the architect's design estimates and the bids that came in live in three different documents, and no one can show how the gap opened.

**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to deliver a voter-approved bond programme on its promised budget and schedule through state agency review (the Division of the State Architect in California), and whoever can evidence on-budget delivery wins the next qualifications-based selection.

## The Problem
A bond programme sets a budget per project years before design starts, using square-foot costs and an escalation assumption. Design proceeds; cost estimates arrive at schematic design, design development and construction documents; bids come in. When bids exceed the budget, the district cuts scope or draws on reserves, and the architect's next selection suffers. Nobody holds a continuous record of how each project's cost moved and why.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The data spans the district's bond documents, the cost consultant's estimates, the architect's scope changes and the bid tabulations, each owned by a different party. Programme managers track budgets but not the design decisions behind cost movement.

## What to Build
A ledger per project and per programme that records the bond-era budget, each estimate with its scope basis, every scope change with its cost effect, and the bid result, plus a forecast of bid-day cost with an uncertainty band updated at each phase from the firm's and region's past bid results. It flags early when a project is trending past its bond allocation.

## Target Customer
K-12 and civic practice leaders, and district facilities directors and programme managers.

## Impact If Built
Budget overruns spotted at schematic design instead of bid day, and a delivery record that wins the next selection.

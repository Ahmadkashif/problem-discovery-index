# Public K-12 & Civic Bond Programmes

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/architecture-firms|Architecture Firms]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to deliver a voter-approved bond programme on its promised budget and schedule through state agency review (the Division of the State Architect in California), and whoever can evidence on-budget delivery wins the next qualifications-based selection.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$20B in architecture fees on public schools, community colleges, libraries, fire stations and civic buildings (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~19% (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — BIM for design; facilities master plans, bond-programme budgets and community engagement in PDFs and spreadsheets
**Target Buyer:** K-12 and civic practice leader; district facilities director or programme manager
**Automation Potential:** Medium — facility condition data, cost tracking and documentation automate; community and board engagement do not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A school district or city sells a bond to voters with a project list and a budget, then hires architects through qualifications-based selection to deliver it. The architect's work spans facility condition assessment and master planning before the bond, design within a fixed budget after it, and state agency review — in California, the Division of the State Architect reviews school structural, fire-life-safety and accessibility design. Missing the promised budget is a political event. The evidence that wins is a record of delivering what voters were told.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Revit; facility condition assessments in spreadsheets or owner software; cost estimates from cost consultants at each phase; agency review through state portals. Gaps: no link from bond-era cost assumptions to design-phase estimates to bid results; facility condition data not reused in design; programme-level reporting assembled by hand.

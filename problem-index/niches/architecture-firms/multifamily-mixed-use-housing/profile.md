# Multifamily & Mixed-Use Housing

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/architecture-firms|Architecture Firms]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to fit the most rentable units onto a site under zoning and code at a construction cost per unit that makes the developer's pro forma pencil, and whoever proves yield and cost fastest at feasibility takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$12B in architecture fees on apartments, condominiums, student and senior housing and mixed-use podium buildings (estimate, derived from the AIA's ~20% residential share less single-family's 8%)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~12% (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium-High — feasibility tools and Revit are widespread among housing specialists; unit mix, pro forma and code strategy still pass between developer and architect in spreadsheets
**Target Buyer:** Housing practice principal; developer's design manager or development director
**Automation Potential:** High at feasibility and unit layout; medium in documentation

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The client is a developer whose decision to buy land and proceed rests on a pro forma, and the architect's feasibility study is an input to it. The work is repeatable — unit types, corridor layouts, podium-over-garage or wood-frame-over-podium construction types chosen for cost — and judged in units, rentable square feet and cost per unit. Firms win repeat developers by being right quickly at the land-acquisition stage, often on speculative or reduced fees.

## Current Tools & Gaps
TestFit and Autodesk Forma for site yield; Revit and unit libraries for design; the developer's Excel pro forma; zoning research by hand or through zoning-data services. Gaps: yield studies disconnected from realistic cost per unit; code strategy (construction type, fire separations, egress) checked late; the firm's own history of yield versus final built units unused.

# Licensure-Path Emerging Professionals

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/architecture-firms|Architecture Firms]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to cut time to licensure by converting a candidate's daily project work into logged AXP hours and passed ARE divisions, and whoever ties exam preparation to the work the candidate is actually doing takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$150M a year in exam preparation, exam and retake fees, and firm-funded licensure support (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** <1% of billings, against a large share of architectural staff (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — NCARB's online record and exam scheduling, online prep courses; experience planning and supervision by conversation
**Target Buyer:** AXP supervisor and firm talent or HR lead; the candidate pays for most prep
**Automation Potential:** High for experience tracking and practice content; mentoring stays human

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
More than 39,000 people were working toward a US architecture licence in 2024, the average path took 12.9 years, and the exam pass rate fell to 55%. Candidates are employees whose experience is decided by their firm's staffing, whose study happens outside work, and whose progress is recorded in a system separate from their timesheets. Tools serve either the exam (prep providers) or the record (NCARB), never the work in between.

## Current Tools & Gaps
NCARB's online record for AXP hours and exam eligibility; ARE prep from providers such as Amber Book, Black Spectacles and PPI; firm mentoring programmes and study groups. Gaps: AXP hours logged from memory; no mapping from timesheet codes to experience areas; prep content generic rather than tied to the candidate's projects.

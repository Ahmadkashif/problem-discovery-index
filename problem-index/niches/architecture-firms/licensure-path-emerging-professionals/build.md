# Experience Maps From the Timesheet

**Niche:** [[niches/architecture-firms/licensure-path-emerging-professionals/profile|Licensure-Path Emerging Professionals]]
**Industry:** [[industries/architecture-firms|Architecture Firms]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A candidate's timesheet already records what they did all year; nobody converts it into the experience areas their licence depends on.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #bert #k-nearest-neighbors #feature-engineering #worker-facing #compliance
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to cut time to licensure by converting a candidate's daily project work into logged AXP hours and passed ARE divisions, and whoever ties exam preparation to the work the candidate is actually doing takes the account.

## The Problem
AXP requires ~3,740 hours across six practice areas (estimate). Candidates reconstruct their hours from memory every few months, and many discover years in that they have thousands of hours in project development and documentation and almost none in construction and evaluation or practice management. Firms staff by productivity, not by licensure need, and nobody sees the gap until it is late.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Timesheets in Deltek, BQE or Monograph are coded by project and phase, not by AXP task; NCARB's record is separate; and talent development at most firms is informal.

## What to Build
A service that reads timesheet entries, task descriptions and project-system activity (submittals reviewed, site visits, proposals drafted), classifies them into AXP areas and tasks, shows candidate and supervisor a running experience map against requirements, and recommends specific upcoming project tasks that would fill the gaps, so staffing decisions can account for licensure.

## Target Customer
Talent and HR leads and principals at firms of 30+ staff with several candidates; candidates directly at smaller firms.

## Impact If Built
Experience gaps visible in year one rather than year six, faster paths to licensure, and better retention of the staff firms train.

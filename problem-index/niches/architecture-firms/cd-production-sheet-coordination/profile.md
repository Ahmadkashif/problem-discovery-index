# Construction Document Production & Sheet Coordination

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/architecture-firms|Architecture Firms]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to cut the hours between a design-development model and an issued, internally consistent construction set, and whoever eliminates schedule, tag and cross-reference errors without adding a new authoring tool takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$15B in fee value spent on the production share of the construction documents phase (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~15% (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** High in authoring, manual in checking — the set is produced in Revit or Archicad, then checked by eye
**Target Buyer:** BIM manager; technical director
**Automation Potential:** High — sheet setup, schedules, annotation, cross-references and consultant-background comparison are mechanical

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The construction documents phase takes the largest share of the fee (~40%, estimate), and much of it is production: placing views, numbering details, building schedules, writing keynotes, overlaying consultant backgrounds and picking up redlines. It is the work junior staff spend their crunch weeks on and the source of the internal-inconsistency errors that become RFIs. Offshore drafting bureaus compete for it on price; the in-firm contest is hours and error rate.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Revit with add-ins (Ideate, pyRevit scripts, Dynamo routines), Archicad, Bluebeam for overlays and redlines, Newforma or ACC for consultant exchange. Gaps: no automated consistency check across sheets, schedules and specifications; redline pickup manual; consultant reissue comparison by visual overlay.

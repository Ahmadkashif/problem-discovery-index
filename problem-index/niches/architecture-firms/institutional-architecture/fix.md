# Post-Occupancy Lessons That Never Reach the Next Project

**Niche:** [[niches/architecture-firms/institutional-architecture/profile|Institutional Architecture]]
**Industry:** [[industries/architecture-firms|Architecture Firms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The firm designs its fifth library or fourth emergency department without a structured record of what the first ones got wrong once people moved in.

Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## The Problem
Institutional practices specialise by building type precisely so experience compounds. But post-occupancy feedback — the eleven-month warranty walk, the facilities manager's complaints, a user survey, the RFI and change order log — is filed per project and rarely reaches the team starting the next one. The same glare problem, the same undersized storage, the same detail that leaked appear again.

## Why It's Still Broken
Post-occupancy evaluation is rarely in the fee, so it gets done informally if at all. The records that exist are in different systems and formats, and nobody is assigned to turn them into design guidance. Liability caution discourages writing down what went wrong.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A lightweight lessons register per building type, populated automatically from the warranty walk notes, closeout RFI and change order logs and facilities tickets the owner will share, tagged by space type and building system, and surfaced to the next team at schematic design as "known issues on our last four projects of this type".

## Who Feels the Pain
Owners who pay twice for the same mistake; facilities managers; project architects who inherit problems they could have avoided; principals selling "experience" the firm doesn't systematically use.

## Impact If Fixed
Repeat defects fall on the firm's core building types, and the firm's experience claim in the next selection becomes demonstrable.

# Institutional Architecture

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/architecture-firms|Architecture Firms]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is that every serious competitor is fighting to win qualifications-based selections from institutional owners on the strength of past delivery — but health systems select on clinical planning and a record of passing state health-facility review, while school districts and civic owners select on delivering bond-funded programmes on budget, and the winners differ. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$55B in architecture fees on institutional buildings — education, healthcare, civic, justice, cultural (estimate, derived from the AIA's 53–55% institutional share of 2023 billings)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~53% (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — large institutional practices run Revit, Deltek and formal QA/QC programmes, but owner design standards, state reviews and selection submissions are handled as documents
**Target Buyer:** Principal-in-charge of a healthcare, education or civic studio
**Automation Potential:** Medium — documentation and review are automatable; programming and stakeholder judgement are not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Institutional owners buy design through qualifications-based selection, often required by statute for public work, and they return to firms that have delivered for them before. Projects are large, repeat by building type, and pass through reviewers beyond the local building department: state health departments, state school-facility agencies, accessibility reviewers. That is why the segment's share of billings far exceeds its share of construction spending. It is not one contest, though: clinical planning and health-facility review decide healthcare selections, and bond-programme delivery decides school and civic selections.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Revit, Bluebeam, Deltek Vantagepoint and proposal tools such as OpenAsset for project imagery; owner design standards delivered as PDF manuals; state review submissions through agency portals. Gaps: no structured record of how past projects fared in agency review or against the voter-approved budget; past-project data rebuilt for every proposal; owner standards checked by reading.

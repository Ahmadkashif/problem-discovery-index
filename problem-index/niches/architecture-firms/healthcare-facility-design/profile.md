# Healthcare Facility Design

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/architecture-firms|Architecture Firms]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to deliver clinical planning that passes FGI Guidelines-based state review (HCAI in California) on the first submission and matches the health system's operating model, and whoever holds that clinical and regulatory record wins the system's next project.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$15B in architecture fees on hospitals, ambulatory centres, behavioural health and senior care (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~14% (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — sophisticated BIM and simulation at specialist firms; room data, equipment planning and agency review tracked in spreadsheets and PDFs
**Target Buyer:** Healthcare practice leader; health system VP of design and construction
**Automation Potential:** Medium — room data and code checks are automatable; clinical workflow planning is judgement

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Healthcare buildings are designed around clinical operations and regulated beyond the building code: most states adopt the FGI Guidelines for Design and Construction of Hospitals and Outpatient Facilities, and state health departments — in California, the Department of Health Care Access and Information — review hospital drawings before construction. Room-by-room requirements (clearances, medical gases, infection control, pressure relationships) are dense. Health systems return to firms that plan clinical flow well and clear review without redesign; the evidence and clocks differ from school work.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Revit; room data sheets in dRofus or spreadsheets; medical equipment planning by specialist consultants; agency review through state portals. Gaps: FGI and state requirements checked by reading; review comments from past projects not structured; room data and model drift apart.

# The Scope That Grows Without an Additional-Services Letter

**Niche:** [[niches/architecture-firms/sole-practitioners-micro-firms/profile|Sole Practitioners & Micro-Firms]]
**Industry:** [[industries/architecture-firms|Architecture Firms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A homeowner asks for "one more option" five times, and the small-firm architect absorbs every hour.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #bert #change-point-detection #revenue-impact #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to give a one-person firm a second set of eyes on code and coordination before a set goes out, at a price a practice billing ~$150,000 per head can carry, and whoever does that inside the tools the architect already uses takes the account.

## The Problem
Residential and small commercial clients change their minds often and informally, by text and email. Each change is small; together they double the design phase. The sole practitioner, wanting to keep the client, rarely sends an additional-services request until it is too late to collect.

## Why It's Still Broken
There's no time to write formal change letters, and nothing compares the client's requests against the signed scope as they arrive.

## What a Fix Looks Like
An assistant that reads client emails and messages, recognises requests beyond the agreed scope, estimates the hours, and drafts a short additional-services note for the architect to send with one click.

## Who Feels the Pain
Sole practitioners and small-firm owners; clients surprised by late invoices.

## Impact If Fixed
Recovered fees on changed work and fewer strained client relationships at the end of a project.

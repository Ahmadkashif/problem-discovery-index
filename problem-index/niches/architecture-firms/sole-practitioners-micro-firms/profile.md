# Sole Practitioners & Micro-Firms

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/architecture-firms|Architecture Firms]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to give a one-person firm a second set of eyes on code and coordination before a set goes out, at a price a practice billing ~$150,000 per head can carry, and whoever does that inside the tools the architect already uses takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$8B in fees earned by firms of fewer than five people (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~8% of billings, against ~60% of firms (estimate; the AIA counts 28% sole practitioners and 32% with 2–4 employees)
**Digital Adoption:** Low-Medium — SketchUp, AutoCAD, Archicad or Revit for drawing; spreadsheets and QuickBooks for the business
**Target Buyer:** Owner-architect
**Automation Potential:** High for code checking, specifications and admin; the design relationship stays personal

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A sole practitioner is designer, drafter, code analyst, specification writer, construction administrator, bookkeeper and marketer, usually on residential additions, small commercial tenant improvements and renovations. The licence and the liability are personal. There is no QA/QC reviewer, no specification writer and no CA department — so the errors larger firms catch internally reach the building department and the contractor. Small firms' share of billings fell by half between 2015 and 2023.

## Current Tools & Gaps
SketchUp, AutoCAD LT, Archicad Solo, Vectorworks; UpCodes; QuickBooks; Monograph or BQE at the upper end. Gaps: no affordable independent review; no master specification sized to small jobs; professional liability premiums high relative to revenue.

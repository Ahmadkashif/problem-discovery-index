# Stopped-Out Students & Degree Completion

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/colleges-and-universities|Colleges & Universities]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to bring former students back to finish a degree, and whoever returns the most stop-outs to a completed credential per outreach dollar takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$400M a year in re-enrollment services, adult degree-completion marketing and coaching bought by four-year institutions (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~0.06% of institutional spending (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — former-student records sit in the SIS; outreach runs through vendors or ad hoc campaigns
**Target Buyer:** Dean of Continuing or Professional Studies, or the VP for Enrollment
**Automation Potential:** High for identification and outreach, medium for coaching

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The National Student Clearinghouse counted 43.1 million people who had stopped out without a credential at the start of 2023-24, 37.6 million of them of working age, and reported record re-enrollment. Re-enrollees earning a credential in their first year rose to only 4.7%. Admissions systems, aid rules and course schedules are built for 18-year-olds starting fresh. A working adult with 90 credits, an old balance and a job needs a different path: credits re-evaluated, holds cleared, evening or online courses, and a coach. Community colleges run their own re-enrollment work, which belongs to that queued industry; this niche is four-year institutions' former students.

## Current Tools & Gaps
EAB (which is recruiting sales staff for student re-enrollment), ReUp Education and InsideTrack provide outreach and coaching; degree-completion programmes run in-house. Gaps: former students never re-audited against current degree requirements; balances and transcript holds blocking return; and success measured as re-enrollment rather than completion.

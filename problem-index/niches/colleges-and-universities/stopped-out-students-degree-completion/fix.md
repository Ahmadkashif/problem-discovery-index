# Old Balances That Keep Students From Coming Back

**Niche:** [[niches/colleges-and-universities/stopped-out-students-degree-completion/profile|Stopped-Out Students & Degree Completion]]
**Industry:** [[industries/colleges-and-universities|Colleges & Universities]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A former student who owes a few hundred dollars cannot register again, so the university forgoes thousands in tuition to collect a small debt.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #logistic-regression #workflow-orchestration #quick-win #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to bring former students back to finish a degree, and whoever returns the most stop-outs to a completed credential per outreach dollar takes the account.

## The Problem
Students who leave with an unpaid balance carry a registration hold, and many institutions sent old debts to collections. Federal rules since July 2024 limit withholding transcripts for terms paid with federal aid (recalled, not re-read), but the registration hold remains. Former students who would return are blocked before they talk to anyone.

## Why It's Still Broken
The bursar's office owns the balance and is measured on collections; enrollment owns re-enrollment and has no say over holds.

## What a Fix Looks Like
For each former student within reach of a degree, compare the balance with the expected tuition from returning. Offer a structured forgiveness or payment plan tied to re-enrollment and completion, and lift the registration hold on agreement, with the bursar and enrollment office signing one policy.

## Who Feels the Pain
Former students, re-enrollment staff and bursars collecting little on old debts.

## Impact If Fixed
Balances under ~$1,000 are a common block (estimate), and forgiving them for returning students costs far less than the tuition they bring back.

# Finding the Stop-Outs Closest to a Degree

**Niche:** [[niches/colleges-and-universities/stopped-out-students-degree-completion/profile|Stopped-Out Students & Degree Completion]]
**Industry:** [[industries/colleges-and-universities|Colleges & Universities]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Every university holds thousands of former students who are a semester or two from a degree under today's requirements, and nobody has re-run their degree audits.
**Tags:** #gradient-boosting #survival-analysis #large-language-models #feature-engineering #evaluation-metrics #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to bring former students back to finish a degree, and whoever returns the most stop-outs to a completed credential per outreach dollar takes the account.

## The Problem
Re-enrollment campaigns email every former student, or buy lists of adults in the area. The students most likely to finish, those with most requirements met, no large balance, and a major still offered, are not separated from those who would need three more years. Requirements have changed since they left, so their old audit understates or overstates what remains.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Re-auditing old records against current catalogues needs the degree audit system, catalogue history and SIS records together, owned by the registrar, while re-enrollment is run by enrollment or continuing studies. Vendors work from contact lists, not audits.

## What to Build
A batch re-audit of former students since a chosen year against current and alternative degree paths, including degree-completion and general-studies programmes. It adds a model of return and completion likelihood from credits remaining, balance, time away and past performance, and outreach drafts telling each person exactly what they need to finish.

## Target Customer
Deans of continuing studies and registrars at public and private universities with large former-student populations.

## Impact If Built
Outreach goes to the people closest to a degree, measured in completions. Re-enrolling 1% of 20,000 former students for two terms is ~200 students and several million dollars in tuition (estimate).

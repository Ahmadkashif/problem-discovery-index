# Enrollment Management & Net Tuition Revenue

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/colleges-and-universities|Colleges & Universities]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is "every serious competitor is fighting to raise net tuition revenue from the next entering class", but it holds two contests with different winners: filling the funnel with students likely to enroll, won on enrolled students per recruitment dollar, and pricing each admit, won on the discount rate at target class size. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$2.5B a year in student search, enrollment marketing, admissions CRM and aid-optimisation services bought by four-year institutions (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~0.4% of institutional spending (estimate), steering tuition revenue that is most of a private college's budget
**Digital Adoption:** Medium-High — CRMs (Slate, Salesforce, Element451) and predictive models are standard; the aid offer and appeals still run in spreadsheets
**Target Buyer:** Vice President for Enrollment Management
**Automation Potential:** Medium — targeting and forecasting automate well; final admission and aid decisions stay human

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Enrollment management is where a tuition-dependent college's revenue is made. A projected 57.1% average first-year discount rate at private nonprofits in 2025-26 (NACUBO) and a 1.6% fall in their undergraduate enrollment in fall 2025 (National Student Clearinghouse) put it at the top of every president's list. Two distinct markets serve it. Recruitment marketing buys names, runs campaigns and builds the applicant pool. Aid leveraging prices each admitted student. They meet only in the enrollment VP's office, and each is judged on its own metric.

## Current Tools & Gaps
EAB (Enroll360, financial aid optimisation), Ruffalo Noel Levitz, Encoura, Carnegie, Maguire Associates and Human Capital Research Corporation sell strategy and services. Slate dominates admissions CRM at private colleges. College Board and ACT sell search names. The gap is the join: no vendor connects recruitment spend and aid spend to the same student's enrollment, retention and lifetime net tuition, so the two budgets are argued separately.

**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to set each admitted student's institutional aid so the class arrives at its target size and profile, and whoever brings in the target class at the lowest discount rate takes the account.

## The Problem
A college fills a fixed number of seats from an admitted pool with uncertain demand, offering a different net price to each admit. That is revenue management, done by grid and judgement.

## What Already Exists
Revenue management systems in airlines and hotels (PROS, IDeaS, in-house systems) that forecast demand by segment and set prices and inventory to maximise revenue at fixed capacity.

## The Customization Gap
The college version must: respect demonstrated need and federal and state aid rules; meet class-profile goals (academic quality, first-generation share, majors, residency) as constraints; treat the price as a grant offered once, with appeals, rather than a fare that changes daily; value students on four-year net revenue and retention, not one sale; and stay explainable to families and accreditors. Optimisation over uplift estimates with Monte Carlo simulation of class outcomes replaces fare-class nesting.

## Target Customer
Enrollment analytics leads at private colleges and at public flagships managing out-of-state discounting.

## Impact If Solved
Aid policy is set by an explicit optimisation the cabinet can inspect, and the class lands closer to target size, within a percentage point or two rather than five (estimate).

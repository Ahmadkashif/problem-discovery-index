# Student Search & Recruitment Marketing

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/colleges-and-universities|Colleges & Universities]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn purchased and owned prospect names into enrolled first-year students, and whoever delivers the most enrolled students per recruitment dollar takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.5B a year in search names, enrollment marketing services, digital campaigns and admissions CRM at four-year institutions (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~0.2% of institutional spending (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** High — names, campaigns and CRM journeys are fully digital; attribution stops at the application
**Target Buyer:** Dean or Director of Admissions, with the enrollment VP
**Automation Potential:** High for targeting and messaging, low for campus visits and counsellor relationships

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The funnel contest is about volume and fit before price enters. Colleges buy hundreds of thousands of student names from College Board Search, ACT and its Encoura data products and Niche, run multi-channel campaigns through agencies (EAB Enroll360, Ruffalo Noel Levitz, Carnegie) or in-house teams on Slate, and measure success in inquiries and applications. The winner here is not the firm that prices aid best but the one whose names and campaigns produce students who deposit and arrive. Test-optional admissions shrank the pool of names tied to test scores, and the demographic peak of 2025 shrinks every list from here.

## Current Tools & Gaps
CRMs (Slate, Salesforce Education Cloud, Element451) run journeys; agencies run search and digital campaigns and report funnel metrics; Encoura's Eduventures and EAB publish prospect research. Gaps: name lists judged on response, not enrollment; no cross-vendor attribution to deposit; and spend allocated by habit and by last year's high schools.

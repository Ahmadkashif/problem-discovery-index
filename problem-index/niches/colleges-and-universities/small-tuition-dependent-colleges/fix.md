# The Composite Score Nobody Modelled

**Niche:** [[niches/colleges-and-universities/small-tuition-dependent-colleges/profile|Small Tuition-Dependent Private Colleges]]
**Industry:** [[industries/colleges-and-universities|Colleges & Universities]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A college learns its federal financial responsibility score has failed when the audited statements are already final, and a letter of credit or heightened monitoring follows.
**Tags:** #linear-regression #monte-carlo-methods #descriptive-statistics #compliance #quick-win #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to run a small college's registrar, aid, business-office and reporting systems as a managed service, and whoever misses the fewest federal disbursement and reporting dates takes the account.

## The Problem
The Department of Education computes a composite score from each private institution's audited financial statements; a low score brings provisional certification, heightened cash monitoring or a letter of credit (thresholds recalled, not re-read). The score is a formula of ratios. Small colleges rarely model it during the year, so decisions about borrowing, endowment draws or a capital project are made without seeing their effect on it.

## Why It's Still Broken
The CFO is busy closing the books, the auditors compute the score after the fact, and nobody owns a forward projection.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A quarterly projection of the composite score from the general ledger and budget, with scenarios for enrollment, borrowing and transfers, and a warning when any plausible scenario crosses a threshold.

## Who Feels the Pain
CFOs, presidents and boards of small colleges; their lenders and accreditors.

## Impact If Fixed
Financing and spending decisions are made with the score in view. A letter of credit can tie up cash equal to a tenth or more of the college's annual Title IV funds (estimate), which a small college can rarely spare.

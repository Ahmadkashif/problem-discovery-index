# Financial Aid Verification, ISIR & Packaging Operations

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/colleges-and-universities|Colleges & Universities]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to take each student's ISIR to a final, disbursable aid offer, and whoever certifies the most files with no later correction or programme-review finding takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$800M a year in aid processing software, verification services, aid chat and agent tools and outsourced processing at four-year institutions (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~0.1% of institutional spending (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium-High — ISIRs and awards are electronic; verification documents, comment-code resolution and family questions are handled by hand
**Target Buyer:** Director of Financial Aid, with the CIO
**Automation Potential:** Very high for document reading, comment codes and status questions; certification and professional judgment stay with counsellors

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Every aided student's file passes through the same pipeline: ISIR in, verification if selected, comment codes resolved, institutional aid packaged, offer sent, disbursement. The rules are federal and change every year. The 2024-25 FAFSA rollout failure delayed offers nationally. The 2026-27 form opened early and completions hit a record (search summary), but the July 2026 loan caps, Pell changes and the new Repayment Assistance Plan put new rules on every file. It is the most repetitive, document-heavy work in the institution, and it is where agent vendors are already deployed. CollegeVine says its agents "package aid" and "resolve verification issues", and it still lists a forward deployed role.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Ellucian Banner and Colleague Financial Aid, PowerFAIDS (College Board), Workday Student and Regent run packaging and disbursement; CampusLogic and similar tools handle document collection; CollegeVine, Element451 and chat vendors add agents. Gaps: uploaded documents read by people; comment codes resolved from experience; status questions answered by phone; and appeals mixed into the same queue.

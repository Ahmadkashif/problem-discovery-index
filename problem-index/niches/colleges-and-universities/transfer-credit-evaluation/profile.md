# Transfer Credit Evaluation & Articulation

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/colleges-and-universities|Colleges & Universities]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn an incoming transcript into posted, degree-applicable course equivalencies, and whoever applies the largest share of incoming credit toward degree requirements takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$250M a year in transfer evaluation software, degree audit add-ons, transcript exchange and evaluator labour attributable to software replacement (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~0.04% of institutional spending (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — transcripts arrive as PDFs or paper, course descriptions are looked up in old catalogues, and faculty review happens by email
**Target Buyer:** University Registrar, with the Director of Transfer Admissions
**Automation Potential:** High for frequent sending institutions; faculty judgement remains for new or unusual courses

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
GAO estimated that students who transferred between 2004 and 2009 lost about 43% of their credits, and that about 35% of students transferred at least once. Receiving four-year institutions decide what counts. An evaluator reads each transcript, finds each course in the sending institution's catalogue for the year it was taken, checks the equivalency table, and routes unknown courses to faculty. Delays push students into registering without knowing what they still need, and lost credits add terms and debt. The sending side, articulation planning at two-year colleges, belongs to community colleges, which are queued separately; this niche is the receiving evaluation.

## Current Tools & Gaps
CollegeSource (TES, Transferology, uAchieve), Ellucian Degree Works, Stellic, Parchment and the National Student Clearinghouse for electronic transcripts. Gaps: equivalency tables that cover frequent senders only; catalogue-year descriptions not linked to transcripts; faculty review queues with no deadline; and evaluations finished after registration opens.

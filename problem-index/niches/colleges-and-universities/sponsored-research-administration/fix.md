# Effort Certified Without Being Read

**Niche:** [[niches/colleges-and-universities/sponsored-research-administration/profile|Sponsored Research Administration & Cost Recovery]]
**Industry:** [[industries/colleges-and-universities|Colleges & Universities]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Principal investigators certify the share of their salary charged to each grant by clicking through a form, and the mismatches surface only in audits.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #hypothesis-testing #compliance #workflow-orchestration #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to recover the full allowable cost of each federal research award as agency rules, caps and terminations change, and whoever leaves the least allowable cost unrecovered takes the account.

## The Problem
Salaries charged to federal awards must reflect the work actually performed. Universities collect effort or payroll certifications each term or year. Busy faculty sign without comparing charges to their real time, and departmental administrators make late cost transfers to fix overruns. Auditors test exactly these transfers and certifications, and disallowed salary is repaid from institutional funds.

## Why It's Still Broken
Certification is treated as a signature rather than a check, and nothing compares the certified split to other evidence of effort — commitments in proposals, other support, time on other awards, late transfers.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Before each certification, flag the lines that look wrong: charges exceeding committed effort, late cost transfers moving salary onto awards near their end date, totals over 100% across sponsored and institutional work. Show the PI only those lines, with a short reason each.

## Who Feels the Pain
Faculty asked to certify what they cannot check, departmental administrators, and the sponsored programs office facing audit.

## Impact If Fixed
Most certifications stay one click; the risky ones get reviewed. Cost transfers made more than 90 days after the charge are a standard audit target, and cutting them by half is plausible (estimate).

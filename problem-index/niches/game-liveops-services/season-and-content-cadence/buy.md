**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to set a season and content cadence that both the players and the team can sustain for years, when the only planning input is that each season must beat the last — and whoever models the fatigue takes the account.

## The Problem
Any business running continuous production learned to model sustainable throughput: utilisation limits beyond which quality and reliability degrade, queueing effects as load approaches capacity, staffing models with attrition built in, and explicit planning for recovery. Manufacturing, healthcare rostering, airline operations and software delivery all have versions of it. A live game team on a permanent seasonal treadmill has the identical structure and plans purely by revenue target.

## What Already Exists
Sustainable utilisation modelling; queueing and load analysis; staffing models incorporating attrition; recovery and slack planning; and demand smoothing.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a system where the consumers fatigue as well as the producers. It requires: (1) fatigue modelled on both sides — the players tiring is as consequential as the team tiring, and no operations framework models its customers' exhaustion, which is the substantive difference; (2) demand that is manufactured by the operator's own calendar rather than arriving externally, so smoothing is entirely within the operator's control; (3) output measured in player experience rather than in units, which resists the standard throughput definitions; (4) a ratchet created by quarterly targets rather than by market demand; and (5) attrition on the team that is itself caused by the cadence, making the staffing model reflexive.

## Target Customer
Live game operators, product and production leadership, live ops vendors, and operations consultancies.

## Impact If Solved
Operations management models sustainable throughput wherever production is continuous. No operations framework models its customers' exhaustion, and here the players fatigue alongside the team — that is what has to be built.

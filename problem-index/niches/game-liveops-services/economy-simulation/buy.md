## The Problem
Agent-based and microsimulation modelling is established practice wherever a policy affects a heterogeneous population and cannot be trialled at scale: tax and benefit reform, epidemic response, transport pricing, energy markets. The methods — calibrated behavioural agents, scenario comparison, sensitivity analysis, uncertainty propagation, and validation against observed outcomes — are mature and taught. Game economies are the same problem with complete observability and no ethical barrier to intervention, and the practice has not crossed over.

## What Already Exists
Agent-based population models; microsimulation of policy change; scenario comparison frameworks; sensitivity and uncertainty analysis; and validation against realised outcomes.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a population whose every action is observed and whose rules the modeller controls. It requires: (1) agent behaviour calibrated from complete telemetry rather than from survey data, which removes the single largest source of error in policy microsimulation and is the substantive difference; (2) intervention granularity at the level of individual parameters changed weekly, rather than policies changed yearly; (3) an objective combining retention, revenue and experience rather than a welfare function; (4) a population that churns rapidly, so cohort entry and exit dominate the dynamics; and (5) a designer as the user rather than an analyst, which changes the interface from a modelling environment into a design tool.

## Target Customer
Live game operators, economy designers, live ops platform vendors, and simulation consultancies.

## Impact If Solved
Policy microsimulation is mature wherever an intervention cannot be trialled at scale. Complete telemetry removes its largest error source here, and the user being a designer rather than an analyst changes it from a modelling environment into a design tool.

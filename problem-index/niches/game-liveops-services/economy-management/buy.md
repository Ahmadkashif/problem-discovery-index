## The Problem
Managing a currency economy is a solved measurement problem in the real world. Central banks and statistical agencies built price indices from representative baskets, monetary aggregates, velocity measures, distributional statistics and policy simulation models, precisely because an economy cannot be steered on revenue figures alone. Game economies are smaller, fully observable and completely controllable — and their operators have none of these instruments.

## What Already Exists
Price index construction from representative baskets; monetary aggregate definitions; velocity and circulation measures; distributional and inequality statistics; and policy simulation models.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to an economy where the central authority also sets every price and can change any rule instantly. It requires: (1) the operator being both the central bank and the entire supply side, so policy simulation must model design changes rather than interest rates — this is the substantive difference and it makes the instruments far more actionable than their originals; (2) a basket defined over game goods whose utility changes as the game is patched, unlike a stable consumption basket; (3) a population that enters and leaves continuously, with new players arriving into an economy older players have already inflated; (4) complete observability, which removes the sampling and estimation apparatus that dominates real statistical practice; and (5) an objective that is player experience and revenue rather than price stability and employment.

## Target Customer
Live game operators, live ops platform vendors, economy designers, and economic modelling consultancies.

## Impact If Solved
Central banks built indices and aggregates because an economy cannot be steered on revenue alone. Here the operator is also the entire supply side, which makes policy simulation model design changes and makes the instruments far more actionable than the originals.

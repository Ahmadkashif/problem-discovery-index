## The Problem
Price index construction is one of the oldest solved problems in applied statistics. Statistical agencies established how to choose a representative basket, weight it by expenditure, rebase it as consumption shifts, adjust for quality change and handle goods that appear and disappear. The methodology is public, documented and taught. A game economy needs precisely this instrument, has perfect transaction data to build it from, and nobody has built one.

## What Already Exists
Representative basket selection; expenditure weighting; index rebasing and chain-linking; quality adjustment methods; and handling of new and discontinued goods.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a catalogue the operator changes at will and a utility that is defined by game design. It requires: (1) quality adjustment where the operator itself patches an item's usefulness — an item whose power changes is not the same good, and this happens far more often than in any real economy, which is the substantive difference; (2) goods appearing and disappearing every season, so chain-linking is continuous rather than periodic; (3) basket weights that differ sharply by cohort age, since a new player and a veteran consume different economies; (4) multiple currencies including a premium one with an external exchange rate to real money; and (5) a weekly rather than monthly cadence, because the drift compounds faster than any national statistic.

## Target Customer
Live game operators, analytics and platform vendors, economy designers, and statistical consultancies.

## Impact If Solved
Basket selection, weighting and quality adjustment are a century-old solved problem with public methodology. An operator that patches an item's usefulness makes quality adjustment continuous rather than occasional, which is what has to be rebuilt.

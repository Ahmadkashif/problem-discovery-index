# The Designer Facing the Community

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/game-liveops-services|Game LiveOps Services]]
**Category:** 🟣 Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to let a designer make a change the data clearly supports without spending the following fortnight being told publicly that they have ruined the game — and whoever solves that takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$250M US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~8% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Low — no support
**Target Buyer:** Design and community leads
**Automation Potential:** Medium — communication, evidence and triage

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A designer changes a number to fix a problem the data clearly shows, and spends the following two weeks being told publicly, personally and at volume that they have ruined the game. The change was correct. The communication was absent or arrived after the reaction had set. There is no support structure, no standard for explaining a balance decision, and no protection for the individual — and the accumulated effect is that designers stop proposing correct changes.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Patch notes, a community manager, and a social feed nobody should be reading. The gaps: no evidence published alongside a change; no pre-announcement of intent; no separation of the designer from the decision publicly; no triage of feedback into signal and noise; and no support for the person absorbing it.

**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to let a designer make a change the data clearly supports without spending the following fortnight being told publicly that they have ruined the game — and whoever solves that takes the account.

## The Problem
Banks, insurers, utilities and telecoms routinely make changes their customers dislike, and are required to communicate them well. The practice that grew up — advance notice, a plain-language rationale, a stated effective date, a consultation or feedback route, and a named function rather than a named individual as the author — exists because the alternative produced complaints and regulatory attention. Game balance changes have the same structure, land on a far more vocal audience, and use none of it.

## What Already Exists
Advance notice periods; plain-language rationale statements; effective-date discipline; consultation and feedback channels; and institutional rather than individual attribution.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a community that responds in minutes and in public, about something they care about far more than a tariff. It requires: (1) a reaction window of hours rather than a notice period of weeks, so the explanation must ship with the change rather than before it — this is the substantive difference; (2) rationale drawn from behavioural data that is partly exploitable if published in full, requiring judgement about what to disclose; (3) an audience that will analyse the data itself and argue, which regulated notices never face; (4) changes made weekly rather than annually, so the process must be near-zero-cost per change; and (5) an individual designer who is identifiable to the community regardless of how the notice is attributed, requiring active protection rather than just institutional authorship.

## Target Customer
Live game operators, community and design leadership, live ops vendors, and communications tooling providers.

## Impact If Solved
Regulated industries built notice, rationale and institutional attribution because the alternative produced complaints. A reaction window of hours and an audience that will analyse the data themselves are what the borrowed practice has never had to handle.

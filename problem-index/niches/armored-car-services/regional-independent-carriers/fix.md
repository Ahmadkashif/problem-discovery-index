# The Insurance Renewal Without a Loss Record

**Niche:** [[niches/armored-car-services/regional-independent-carriers/profile|Regional Independent Armored Carriers]]
**Industry:** [[industries/armored-car-services|Armored Car Services]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A regional carrier renews its cash-in-transit cover with a few years of claims and a narrative, and pays for the underwriter's uncertainty.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #survival-analysis #compliance #quick-win #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep the bank and retail accounts that the national carriers bid with smart-safe credit and ATM bundles, and whoever can offer next-day provisional credit through the customer's own bank takes the account.

## The Problem
Cash-in-transit cover is written largely in the London specie market, and Brink's says even its premiums depend on its own loss experience and, to a limited extent, other carriers'. A small carrier has few losses in any year, so its record says little, and underwriters price the gap with deductibles, limits and premium.

## Why It's Still Broken
Independents keep incident files, not risk records. Near-misses, aborted stops, control changes (two-person rules, cameras, vault upgrades) and discrepancy rates are not recorded in a form an underwriter can read.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A structured loss-and-control log: every incident and near-miss by type, site and time; every discrepancy and its resolution; the controls in place by vehicle and vault, with dates; and annual exposure (value carried, stops, vault holdings). Exported as an underwriting submission, it gives the specie broker evidence rather than reassurance.

## Who Feels the Pain
Owners facing premium rises they cannot argue with; brokers placing thin submissions; underwriters pricing blind.

## Impact If Fixed
A credible record improves terms at renewal and identifies the sites and practices that drive losses — for a business where insurance is one of the largest controllable costs (estimate).

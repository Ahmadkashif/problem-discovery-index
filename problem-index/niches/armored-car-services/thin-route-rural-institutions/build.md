# A Shared Rural Route That Pools Demand

**Niche:** [[niches/armored-car-services/thin-route-rural-institutions/profile|Rural Banks & Credit Unions on Thin Routes]]
**Industry:** [[industries/armored-car-services|Armored Car Services]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Three small banks, a grocery chain and an ATM deployer in the same county each buy armored service separately, and each is told its stop is uneconomic.
**Tags:** #optimization-fundamentals #k-means-clustering #time-series-forecasting #data-integration #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep serving rural branches profitably as stops thin out, and whoever packs the most bank, retail and ATM stops onto one rural route takes the account.

## The Problem
Rural cash demand is scattered across customers who never see each other's needs. Each negotiates alone with a carrier that prices each stop at the cost of the whole drive. The result is high prices, infrequent service or no service.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Carriers sell account by account, and their sales teams have no tool for assembling a route from prospects. Customers have no reason to share schedules. Regional independents know the geography but lack planning software.

## What to Build
A planning service that maps cash demand in a rural region from existing and prospective customers — branches, retailers, ATMs, municipal offices — forecasts each site's needs, clusters them into feasible routes under the carrier's value limits and service windows, and prices each stop at its share of a viable route. Carriers use it to bid a route rather than a stop; customers see the price fall as others join.

## Target Customer
Regional carriers and the rural branch networks of national carriers; state bankers' associations or bankers' banks organising service for members.

## Impact If Built
A route with five more stops on it can cut each rural stop's price materially (estimate) and keep service in counties where carriers would otherwise leave.

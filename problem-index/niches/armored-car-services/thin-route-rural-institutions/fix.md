# The Branch That Moves Its Own Cash

**Niche:** [[niches/armored-car-services/thin-route-rural-institutions/profile|Rural Banks & Credit Unions on Thin Routes]]
**Industry:** [[industries/armored-car-services|Armored Car Services]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** When the carrier drops a rural stop, staff start driving cash between branches in their own cars, and nobody measures how often or how much.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #compliance #worker-facing #quick-win #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep serving rural branches profitably as stops thin out, and whoever packs the most bank, retail and ATM stops onto one rural route takes the account.

## The Problem
Self-transport starts as an exception — a branch manager carrying ~$20,000 (illustrative) to a sister branch on the way home — and becomes routine. The employee is exposed, the cash is often outside the terms the bank's financial institution bond expects, and the practice is invisible to the board.

## Why It's Still Broken
It saves an armored fee each time and nobody is assigned to count it. Policies say "use the carrier" while operations quietly depend on staff doing otherwise.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Record every self-transport as a transaction in the core: amount, people, route and time. Set thresholds above which the carrier must be used, report the totals to the risk committee monthly, and compare them with the carrier price the bank refused. The data also supports the bank's insurance renewal and any case for a pooled route.

## Who Feels the Pain
Branch staff carrying cash alone; risk officers who learn of it after an incident; bond underwriters pricing exposure they cannot see.

## Impact If Fixed
A simple log turns a hidden risk into a decision the bank makes on numbers, and protects the staff now carrying it.

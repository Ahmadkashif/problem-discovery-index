# Rural Banks & Credit Unions on Thin Routes

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/armored-car-services|Armored Car Services]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep serving rural branches profitably as stops thin out, and whoever packs the most bank, retail and ATM stops onto one rural route takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$200M of carrier revenue from rural and small-town bank and credit union branches (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~6% of receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — orders by phone or fax to a regional carrier, or staff driving cash themselves; little forecasting
**Target Buyer:** Chief operating officer or branch operations officer at a community bank; VP of operations at a rural credit union
**Automation Potential:** Low–Medium — order consolidation and route pooling help; the miles do not shrink

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
An armored route makes money on density. In a metro area a truck works dozens of stops a day within a few miles (estimate); in a rural county the same truck drives an hour between a branch, a grocery store and two ATMs. As small banks close branches, retailers move to smart safes and deployers pull ATMs, each remaining rural stop carries more of the route's cost. National carriers raise the price or drop the route, regional independents fill some gaps, and some small institutions move cash themselves in staff vehicles, a risk their financial institution bond underwriters care about. These buyers are [[industries/community-banks|Community Banks]] and [[industries/credit-unions|Credit Unions]] whose cash needs are small but not optional: rural and older customers use cash more (cash was 19% of payments for adults 55 and over in 2024).

## Current Tools & Gaps
Core-system teller cash modules (Fiserv, Jack Henry), the Fed's FedCash Services, regional carriers' phone and fax ordering, and correspondent or bankers' banks for cash services. Gaps: orders not consolidated across branches, no pooling of demand across institutions and retailers in an area, and no policy or measurement for self-transported cash.

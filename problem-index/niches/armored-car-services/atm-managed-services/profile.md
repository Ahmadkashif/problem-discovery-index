# ATM Managed Services

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/armored-car-services|Armored Car Services]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is that every serious competitor is fighting to keep each ATM in a bank's or deployer's fleet available to withdraw from, but writing it produces two contests with different winners: holding the least cash in machines at the contracted cash-out rate, which forecasting vendors, carriers' forecasting desks and banks' cash teams compete on; and restoring faulted machines fastest without sending technicians into staged jams, which carriers' service arms, ATM makers' service businesses and independent service organisations compete on. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.1B of US carrier revenue from ATM replenishment, first-line service and fully managed ATM programmes (estimate; Loomis's US ATM line alone was SEK 3.3B, ~$0.33B, in 2025)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~30% of receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium–High — remote monitoring, forecasting and dispatch systems are standard at the national carriers; visit-level coordination still leans on phone and email
**Target Buyer:** Head of self-service channels at a bank; operations head at an independent ATM deployer
**Automation Potential:** High for forecasting, monitoring and dispatch decisions; visits stay physical

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
ATM work is the most data-rich part of the industry. Every machine reports dispenses, deposits, faults and cassette levels, and the managed-service contract defines what the carrier does with them: Brink's lists cash forecasting, cash optimisation, remote monitoring, service-call dispatching, first- and second-line maintenance and funds settlement. The US had an estimated 520,000–540,000 active ATMs in 2023 (ATMIA), and deployers removed ~6,000 in 2025 (Datos Insights). Brink's agreement in February 2026 to buy NCR Atleos, which owns and operates ~78,000 ATMs, puts the largest carrier inside the largest ATM network. The niche splits because two different things decide renewal: whether machines had the right cash, and whether they were working.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Carriers' AMS platforms, forecasting software (Morphis, OptiCash, Hyosung), ATM makers' monitoring, and field-service systems. Gaps: cash visits and technical visits planned separately, forecasts made without the route, and fault dispatch blind to whether a fault was staged.

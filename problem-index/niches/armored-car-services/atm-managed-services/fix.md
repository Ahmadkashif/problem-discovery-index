## The Problem
Cash and service visits are dispatched by different desks, often by different companies. A first-line fault — a jammed card reader, a receipt printer out of paper, a dispenser reject bin full — opens a ticket that waits for a technician, even when an armored crew is scheduled at that machine the same day.

## Why It's Still Broken
Contracts separate the two services and price them separately. Crews are not always trained or permitted to do first-line fixes, and dispatch systems do not see each other's schedules.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Share each day's visit schedules between cash and service desks; when a first-line fault opens on a machine with a crew visit pending, offer the crew the fix with a short procedure, and close the ticket on their scan. Measure combined visits as a contract metric.

## Who Feels the Pain
ATM owners paying for two visits; technicians driving to five-minute fixes; customers facing a broken machine for hours.

## Impact If Fixed
A meaningful share of first-line tickets can ride on visits already planned (estimate), cutting cost and downtime with no new technology beyond shared schedules.

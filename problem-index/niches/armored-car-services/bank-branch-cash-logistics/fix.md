# The Emergency Order Taken by Phone

**Niche:** [[niches/armored-car-services/bank-branch-cash-logistics/profile|Bank & Credit Union Branch Cash Logistics]]
**Industry:** [[industries/armored-car-services|Armored Car Services]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A branch about to run out of a denomination phones the carrier, a truck is pulled off its route, and nobody records why it happened so it happens again.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #descriptive-statistics #workflow-orchestration #quick-win #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to fill a bank's branch and vault cash orders on the next scheduled stop across its whole footprint, and whoever has a vault close enough to every branch to make same-day emergency deliveries takes the account.

## The Problem
Emergency orders are expensive for both sides. The bank pays a premium fee; the carrier breaks a planned route, delays other customers and sometimes sends a truck out with less than a full load. The request arrives by phone or email to a dispatcher, is fulfilled, invoiced and forgotten.

## Why It's Still Broken
Emergency orders are treated as service events, not data. Neither the bank nor the carrier records the cause — an unforecast payroll, a recycler fault, a large commercial withdrawal, a missed regular order — so the same branches repeat the same emergencies. The person who takes the call is a dispatcher with a route to fix, not an analyst.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A structured emergency-order intake shared by bank and carrier: the branch states the denomination, amount and cause from a short list, and the system first proposes cheaper alternatives (moving cash from a nearby branch, bringing forward the next scheduled stop) before dispatching. Monthly, each bank sees its emergency orders by branch and cause, with the fee total.

## Who Feels the Pain
Branch managers who get blamed for running short; dispatchers rebuilding routes mid-morning; bank cash operations teams paying emergency fees nobody explains.

## Impact If Fixed
Recording the cause of each emergency order costs seconds and lets a bank remove the repeat offenders, typically a handful of branches (estimate), from its fee bill.

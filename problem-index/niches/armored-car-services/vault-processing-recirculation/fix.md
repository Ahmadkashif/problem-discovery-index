# The Fed Fee for Notes That Never Left Town

**Niche:** [[niches/armored-car-services/vault-processing-recirculation/profile|Vault Counting, Fitness Sorting & Local Recirculation]]
**Industry:** [[industries/armored-car-services|Armored Car Services]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A bank deposits fit $20s at the Fed on Tuesday, orders $20s on Thursday, and learns from the Fed's bill that it paid to send its own notes on a round trip.
**Tags:** #optimization-fundamentals #descriptive-statistics #quick-win #automation #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to sort deposited notes to Federal Reserve fitness standards and send them back out to the bank's own branches and ATMs instead of round-tripping them through the Fed, and whoever recirculates the most fit notes locally takes the account.

## The Problem
The Fed's Currency Recirculation Policy charges institutions that deposit fit $10 and $20 notes and order the same denomination within the same week above a de minimis level. Deposits and orders are often decided by different people on different days, so the overlap is only seen afterwards.

## Why It's Still Broken
Fed deposit decisions are made in the vault to clear excess inventory; orders are made by the bank's cash team for upcoming needs. Neither sees the weekly netting position until the fee appears.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A weekly netting view per bank and denomination showing planned Fed deposits against planned orders, with an alert when both are scheduled in the same week and a suggestion to hold the fit notes instead. A monthly report shows fees avoided and incurred.

## Who Feels the Pain
Bank cash operations teams paying avoidable fees; vault planners blamed for them; carriers whose vault service looks expensive.

## Impact If Fixed
A simple shared view removes most avoidable recirculation fees (estimate) with no new equipment.

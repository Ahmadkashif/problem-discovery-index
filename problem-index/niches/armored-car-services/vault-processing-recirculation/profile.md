# Vault Counting, Fitness Sorting & Local Recirculation

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/armored-car-services|Armored Car Services]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to sort deposited notes to Federal Reserve fitness standards and send them back out to the bank's own branches and ATMs instead of round-tripping them through the Fed, and whoever recirculates the most fit notes locally takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$500M of US carrier revenue from vault processing, currency inventory management and coin processing (estimate; Loomis's US cash management services line was SEK 2.2B, ~$0.22B, in 2025)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~15% of receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — high-speed sorters produce exact counts and fitness grades; inventory ownership, netting and exceptions are tracked in vault systems and spreadsheets
**Target Buyer:** Vault operations manager at a carrier; head of cash operations at a bank outsourcing its vault
**Automation Potential:** High — counting and sorting are machine work; planning and netting can be too

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Inside each carrier branch is a counting room. Deposits from retailers and branches are opened, counted, verified against declarations, sorted by denomination and fitness, and either held as inventory for the bank that owns them, issued to fill branch and ATM orders, or shipped to the Federal Reserve. Brink's describes vaulting as combining cash-in-transit, cash management and electronic reporting for banks, including currency inventory management and processing ATM replenishment orders. The Fed charges a recirculation fee when an institution deposits fit $10 and $20 notes and orders the same denomination back in the same week, so the economic contest is local recirculation: sorting deposits to the Fed's fitness standard and reusing them for the bank's own orders. The work is the most mechanical in the industry, done under dual control and cameras.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Currency sorters and counters from makers such as Glory and Crane Payment Innovations, vault inventory software, FedCash Services for Fed orders and deposits. Gaps: recirculation planned bank by bank rather than across a vault's inventory, fitness data not used in order planning, and recirculation fees discovered on the Fed's invoice.

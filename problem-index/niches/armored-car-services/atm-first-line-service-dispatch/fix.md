# The Technician Sent Alone at Dusk

**Niche:** [[niches/armored-car-services/atm-first-line-service-dispatch/profile|ATM First-Line Service & Fault Dispatch]]
**Industry:** [[industries/armored-car-services|Armored Car Services]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Dispatch rules weigh distance and service-level deadlines, not the site, the hour or the pattern of the fault.
**Tags:** #gradient-boosting #descriptive-statistics #worker-facing #quick-win #compliance #workflow-orchestration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to restore a faulted ATM fastest without sending a technician into a staged jam, and whoever clears the most faults remotely and sends people only to genuine, risk-checked calls takes the account.

## The Problem
A fault at a standalone drive-up ATM at closing time is dispatched exactly like one inside a busy supermarket at noon. The nearest technician gets it to meet the contract's response time. Technicians who feel uneasy can refuse or call for backup, but doing so costs them time on a schedule that judges them on response.

## Why It's Still Broken
Service-level agreements reward speed, not safety. Site risk is not in the dispatch system, and incidents are rare enough at any one branch that nobody builds a policy until after an attack.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Add a site risk tier and time-of-day rules to dispatch now, using simple inputs — site type, isolation, past incidents, local crime — and require two-person or escorted attendance for the highest tier after dark. Renegotiate response times for those sites with owners so technicians are not penalised for waiting for daylight or backup.

## Who Feels the Pain
First-line technicians, often working alone; their managers; ATM owners whose machines become the scene of an attack.

## Impact If Fixed
A few dispatch rules remove the riskiest solo calls immediately, before any model exists, and give technicians a sanctioned way to say no.

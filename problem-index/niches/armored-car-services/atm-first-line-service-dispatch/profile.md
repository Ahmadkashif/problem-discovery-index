# ATM First-Line Service & Fault Dispatch

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/armored-car-services|Armored Car Services]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to restore a faulted ATM fastest without sending a technician into a staged jam, and whoever clears the most faults remotely and sends people only to genuine, risk-checked calls takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$500M of US spend on first-line ATM service, remote monitoring and fault dispatch (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~14% of receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium–High — remote monitoring raises tickets automatically; risk assessment of each call is informal
**Target Buyer:** ATM channel operations manager at a bank; service delivery manager in a carrier's AMS business
**Automation Potential:** High for diagnosis, remote resets and dispatch decisions

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The contest here is uptime and safety, not inventory. Most first-line faults — card reader jams, printer and receipt issues, communication drops, reject bins full — can be diagnosed from device logs and many cleared remotely. The rest need a person on site, often alone. Since at least July 2022, according to court documents, robbers have jammed ATMs deliberately and ambushed the technicians sent to fix them, a scheme the FBI was still tracking in 2026. Competitors are carriers' AMS service arms, ATM makers' service businesses and independent service organisations. The winner restores machines fastest while keeping technicians out of set-ups, which requires reading the fault, not just routing the ticket.

## Current Tools & Gaps
ATM monitoring software and makers' remote-management tools; field-service dispatch; police escort arrangements for some sites. Gaps: fault codes routed without asking whether the pattern looks staged, no site risk in dispatch rules, and technician findings of tampering recorded as free text.

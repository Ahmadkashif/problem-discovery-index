# Retail Cash Pickup & Smart-Safe Programmes

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/armored-car-services|Armored Car Services]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to put a store's cash into its bank account the day it is taken without a daily truck stop, and whoever gets next-day provisional credit posted at the most of the retailer's banks with the fewest pickups takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.4B of US carrier revenue from retail cash pickups and smart-safe services (estimate; Loomis alone booked SEK 4.0B, ~$0.4B, of US Automated Solutions revenue in 2025)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~40% of receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium–High — safes validate and transmit totals and banks post credit automatically; exceptions, manual drops and pickup changes are handled by email and phone
**Target Buyer:** VP of treasury or cash office manager at a grocery, convenience or restaurant chain
**Automation Potential:** High for validation, credit and reconciliation; pickups stay physical

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
This is where the industry's growth is. A smart safe in the back office validates notes as they are dropped; the carrier transmits the validated total; the retailer's bank posts provisional credit, usually the next business day, before any truck arrives; and validated cash is guaranteed by the carrier. Pickups fall from daily to weekly or on demand. Loomis's US Automated Solutions line grew ~15% in reported kronor in 2025 (SEK 3.5B to 4.0B) while its US cash-in-transit line shrank ~7% on the same currency basis, and Brink's digital retail solutions and ATM services reached ~$1.5B, 28% of its revenue. The economics change from a fee per stop to a fee per device and month, and the carrier's real product becomes the bank integration that posts credit. Competition includes non-carrier safe providers that let the retailer choose any carrier, and GardaWorld's Sesami, which owns the safe maker Tidel.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Carrier programmes (Brink's Complete, Loomis SafePoint, GardaWorld/Sesami), safes from Tidel and FireKing, bank partnerships for provisional credit, carrier portals. Gaps: pickup schedules set by calendar rather than by cash at risk; manual drops outside the guarantee; credit rules that differ by bank; and no clear store-level view of whether a safe pays for itself.

# ATM Cash Forecasting & Replenishment

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/armored-car-services|Armored Car Services]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep each ATM between a cash-out and idle cash with the fewest unscheduled visits, and whoever holds the least cash in machines at the contracted cash-out rate takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$500M of US spend on ATM replenishment visits, cash forecasting services and software (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~14% of receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** High — machine data and forecasting tools are standard; overrides and route coupling are manual
**Target Buyer:** Self-service channel manager or cash manager at a bank; treasury lead at an independent deployer
**Automation Potential:** High

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The contest here is inventory. An owner pays for cash-outs and for idle cash, and the carrier pays for unscheduled visits, so every load is a bet on demand until the next route day. Vendors have cited a Deloitte study saying institutions hold up to 40% more cash than needed, and OptiCash's marketer claims up to 30% lower holdings and up to 90% fewer cash-outs. Competitors are forecasting software vendors (Morphis, OptiCash, Hyosung), carriers' own AMS forecasting desks and banks' in-house teams. The winner is whoever plans loads against the real route and denomination constraints and proves the cash-in-machine number at a contracted cash-out rate. Experienced cash managers still override forecasts before holidays and local events on instinct, and those overrides are rarely scored.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Forecasting products and carrier desks; owners' treasury spreadsheets; ATM monitoring feeds. Gaps: forecasts per machine without route days, value limits or cassette mix; overrides unrecorded; and cost of funds missing from the objective.

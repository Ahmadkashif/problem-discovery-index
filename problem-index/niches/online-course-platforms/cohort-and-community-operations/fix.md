# Forming Groups in a Spreadsheet

**Niche:** [[niches/online-course-platforms/cohort-and-community-operations/profile|Cohort & Community Operations]]
**Industry:** [[industries/online-course-platforms|Online Course Platforms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The facilitator spends a day sorting two hundred people into groups by time zone and experience, by hand, before every cohort.
**Tags:** #quick-win #optimization-fundamentals #automation #workflow-orchestration #worker-facing #evaluation-metrics #k-means-clustering #descriptive-statistics
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to run cohorts and communities that produce the accountability self-paced courses lack, without the operational load that makes them uneconomic — and whoever automates the running keeps the format that actually finishes.

## The Problem
Before each cohort someone exports the registration list and sorts participants into groups, balancing time zones so meetings are possible, experience so groups are not all beginners, goals so the work is relevant, and availability so people can attend. It takes a day, it is redone every cohort, and it is done badly because a person cannot optimise five constraints across two hundred people in a spreadsheet. Groups that are formed badly dissolve, and a dissolved group takes its members' completion with it.

## Why It's Still Broken
Group formation was a one-off task in the first cohort and became a ritual, so nobody framed it as an optimisation — a job done by hand once tends to be done by hand forever unless someone names it as a problem with a solution. The constraints live in the facilitator's head. The consequences of a bad grouping appear weeks later. And nobody measures group survival.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Solve the assignment automatically. Collect the constraints at registration — time zone, availability, experience, goal, language — which is the fix's precondition and is a short form. Optimise the assignment against those constraints rather than sorting, since it is a standard assignment problem and produces better groups in seconds. Report group composition so the facilitator can adjust rather than construct. Track group survival and participation, because a dissolving group is a leading indicator of several dropouts and is currently noticed late. Reassign members from a collapsed group rather than leaving them stranded, as that is a common and avoidable loss. Learn which compositions survive from past cohorts, which turns the constraints from guesses into evidence. Handle late registrations and dropouts without reshuffling everyone. Provide the group with a schedule and a structure rather than leaving them to organise, since unstructured groups dissolve fastest. Reuse the configuration across cohorts, as the same work is redone each time. And measure completion by group quality, which will demonstrate how much the formation matters.

## Who Feels the Pain
Facilitators losing a day per cohort; learners in groups that cannot meet or dissolve; instructors whose completion rates suffer for operational reasons; and the format's economics, which the manual load determines.

## Impact If Fixed
A job done by hand once tends to be done by hand forever unless someone names it as a problem with a solution. Group formation is a standard assignment optimisation, and better groups survive, which is what the completion rate actually depends on.

# A Certificate for Watching

**Niche:** [[niches/online-course-platforms/learning-measurement/profile|Learning Measurement]]
**Industry:** [[industries/online-course-platforms|Online Course Platforms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The certificate says the learner completed the course, which means the video reached the end.
**Tags:** #quick-win #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #confidence-intervals #compliance #worker-facing #automation #hypothesis-testing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to say whether a learner can now do the thing the course was about — and the contest splits cleanly enough that it is not terminal.

## The Problem
A certificate is issued on completion, completion is defined as watching the material, and watching is defined generously. Employers know this, which is why the credential carries little weight, which is why learners who did work hard get no benefit from it. The document says nothing about what was assessed, how, or to what standard, and a learner who genuinely mastered the material and one who left it playing receive the same thing.

## Why It's Still Broken
The certificate was designed as a completion artefact because completion was what the platform could observe, so the credential describes the platform's telemetry rather than the learner's ability — a document generated from the only available signal will always certify that signal. Stronger criteria reduce issuance, which looks like worse performance. Employers were never consulted. And no platform wants to be the one whose certificates are harder to get.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Say what the certificate actually attests. State on the credential what was assessed and how, which is the fix and costs nothing but makes the document honest. Distinguish attendance from assessed achievement with different credentials, since conflating them devalues the achievement. Report the assessment's nature plainly — multiple choice, reviewed project, timed — as that is what a reader needs to weigh it. Show the standard met rather than a pass, because a percentage with no context is uninterpretable. Include the date and the version of the course, since a credential in a fast-moving subject ages and nothing indicates it. Let a learner choose to take a harder assessed path, as many would and none are offered it. Ask employers what would make a credential useful, which nobody has done and which would reorder the whole design. Report issuance and assessment rates publicly, since transparency is what would differentiate a serious credential. Verify the credential externally so a reader can check it. And stop describing attendance as completion in the product language, because the word is doing work it has not earned.

## Who Feels the Pain
Learners who worked and gained nothing from it; employers who discount every credential equally; instructors whose good courses are indistinguishable from the rest; and a sector whose central artefact means nothing.

## Impact If Fixed
A document generated from the only available signal will always certify that signal, so the certificate attests to video playback. Stating what was assessed and separating attendance from achievement costs nothing and makes the credential readable.

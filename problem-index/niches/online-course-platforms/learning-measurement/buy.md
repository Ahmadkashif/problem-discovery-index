## The Problem
Educational measurement is a rigorous field: item response theory, adaptive testing, validity and reliability standards, knowledge tracing, spaced practice and retrieval effects, transfer measurement. Intelligent tutoring research has built and evaluated systems on these principles for decades. The commercial online course sector has essentially none of it, and the two communities barely interact despite one having the theory and the other having the data.

## What Already Exists
Item response theory and adaptive testing; knowledge tracing models; validity, reliability and fairness standards for assessment; spaced practice and retrieval research; and transfer and durability measurement.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to voluntary learners at consumer scale. It requires: (1) learners who can leave at any moment, so assessment that feels like a test drives them away and the measurement must be embedded in practice — this is the substantive constraint and is why the academic instruments do not transfer directly; (2) content authored by instructors rather than by psychometricians, so item quality is uncontrolled; (3) no proctoring or identity assurance, which limits what a credential can claim; (4) enormous sample sizes per item, which is a considerable advantage the academic setting never has; and (5) a commercial model that does not currently pay for any of it.

## Target Customer
Product and academic leadership, assessment and instructional design vendors, employers relying on credentials, and education researchers.

## Impact If Solved
The theory is a century old and the data is a decade old, and they have never met at commercial scale. Embedding measurement in practice rather than in tests is what makes it survivable for a learner who can leave at any moment.

# Measuring the Thing the Sector Claims

**Industry:** [[industries/online-course-platforms|Online Course Platforms]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** The most detailed record of human learning ever assembled has been used to optimise enrolment.

**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to say whether a learner can now do the thing the course was about — and the contest splits cleanly enough that it is not terminal.

## The Problem
Millions of learners move through the same material, and the platform records every pause, rewind, replay, quiz attempt, abandonment and return. That data supports a well-posed and long-studied problem: estimating what a learner currently knows and what they should do next. The sector has spent a decade optimising enrolment, recommendation and video delivery instead, because those are what the revenue model rewards, and the result is a product that cannot substantiate its own claim.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Revenue is recognised at enrolment, so nothing downstream of the purchase affects the number anyone is measured on — a business paid at the start of a process has no financial reason to measure its end. Measuring learning would expose how little of it occurs. Assessment is expensive and video is not. And the completion statistic has been public for a decade without changing anything, which demonstrates how little pressure exists.

## What to Build
Estimate mastery and prove it externally. Model what each learner knows from their behaviour and their attempts, which is one half and is a mature modelling area applied to an unusually rich record. Build assessment and credentialing that a third party believes, which is the other half and is where the commercial value lands. Use the behavioural record — time on a concept, rewinds, attempt patterns, spacing of returns — since these are established indicators of struggle and mastery and are being logged and discarded. Diagnose rather than score, because a learner who fails needs to know which prerequisite they lack and currently gets a percentage. Track the external outcome where it can be obtained, as the job, the interview and the applied project are the real measure and are entirely unobserved. Distinguish the learner who watched from the learner who can do, which is the distinction the certificate currently elides. Report course effectiveness rather than course rating, since the rating measures satisfaction and is what ranking runs on. Feed measurement back to instructors, who currently cannot see where their teaching fails. Accept that measuring will reveal uncomfortable results, because that is the cost of having a defensible product. And build it as the differentiator it is, since explanation is now abundant and evidence of capability is not.

## Target Customer
Product and academic leadership, employers consuming credentials, learners who want to know whether they learned, and instructional design and assessment vendors.

## Impact If Built
A business paid at the start of a process has no financial reason to measure its end, which is why a decade of behavioural data went unused. As explanation is commoditised, evidence of capability becomes the only thing a course platform has that a video library does not.

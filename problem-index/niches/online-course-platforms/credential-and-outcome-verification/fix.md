# Nobody Ever Asked What Happened Next

**Niche:** [[niches/online-course-platforms/credential-and-outcome-verification/profile|Credential & Outcome Verification]]
**Industry:** [[industries/online-course-platforms|Online Course Platforms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The learner finished the course and the platform's last interaction with them was a request for a star rating.
**Tags:** #quick-win #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #automation #worker-facing #revenue-impact #hypothesis-testing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to issue a credential an employer actually believes and to observe what happened to the learner afterwards — and whoever does both has the only evidence in the sector that any of this works.

## The Problem
A learner completes a course, rates it out of five within the hour, and is never contacted again. Whether they used what they learned, got the job, changed role, built the thing, or found the course useless in practice is unknown. The rating — collected at the moment of completion, before any application of the material — is the sector's primary quality signal and drives its ranking, which means the whole marketplace runs on satisfaction measured before the outcome could possibly be known.

## Why It's Still Broken
The rating is collected at the last moment the platform has the learner's attention, so it measures the experience rather than the result — a signal gathered for convenience becomes the quality metric regardless of what it can know. Following up costs something and yields nothing billable. Response rates would be low. And a follow-up asking whether it helped invites an answer nobody wants recorded.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Ask again, later. Survey learners three and six months after completion about whether they used the material, which is the fix and is the first outcome data the sector would have. Ask specific behavioural questions rather than satisfaction, since "did you build anything with this" is answerable and "were you satisfied" is not informative. Report the delayed response alongside the immediate rating, as the divergence will be instructive and will reorder courses. Track learners who return for another course, because repeat purchase after application is a strong implicit outcome signal already in the data. Ask about role changes and applied projects where learners will share them, which many will. Use the response to rank and recommend, so the marketplace rewards courses that work. Report response rates honestly, since a low rate is a real limitation and pretending otherwise invalidates the data. Give instructors the delayed feedback, which is more useful than a star rating and they currently never see it. Incentivise the response modestly, as the marginal cost is small and the data is the sector's missing evidence. And publish what is learned, because being the platform that measures outcomes is the position worth holding.

## Who Feels the Pain
Learners choosing courses on a signal collected before anything could be known; instructors judged on immediate satisfaction; employers with no basis to trust anything; and a sector that cannot demonstrate its own effect.

## Impact If Fixed
A signal gathered for convenience becomes the quality metric regardless of what it can know, so ranking runs on a rating collected before the material was used. A delayed follow-up is the sector's first outcome evidence and costs an email.

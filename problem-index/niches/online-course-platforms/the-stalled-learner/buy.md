## The Problem
Higher education and workforce training programmes take retention seriously because they are funded and measured on it. The practice includes early alert systems triggered by attendance and performance signals, advising outreach with trained staff, structured support for identified risks, and measurement of which interventions work. It is imperfect and it is a discipline. Online course platforms have the same failure at far greater scale and treat it as a market characteristic.

## What Already Exists
Early alert and risk flagging systems; advising and outreach protocols; targeted academic support; intervention effectiveness measurement; and retention as a governed institutional metric.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a learner with no relationship and no obligation. It requires: (1) intervention with no advisor and no institutional relationship, so the outreach must be automated and must not feel like marketing — this is the substantive difficulty; (2) a learner with no sunk commitment beyond a small purchase, who can leave costlessly; (3) enormous volume, which makes per-learner human outreach impossible and per-learner automation trivial; (4) causes that are mostly content difficulty rather than life circumstance, which is more tractable; and (5) no funding or regulatory pressure on the outcome, so it must be justified commercially.

## Target Customer
Learner success leadership, instructors, learners, and student success platform vendors.

## Impact If Solved
Education built early alert and advising because retention is funded and measured. Automating the advisor's function for a learner with no institutional relationship is the adaptation, and the causes here are more often fixable content problems than life circumstances.

# The Part That Actually Teaches

**Industry:** [[industries/online-course-platforms|Online Course Platforms]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Explanation became abundant and free, and the sector's product is still explanation.

**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to give a learner structured practice and real feedback on their work at a cost that scales — and whoever does it owns the part of learning that recorded video never provided and generative explanation does not replace.

## The Problem
Learning requires attempting, failing, being told why, and attempting again. The sector delivers explanation, a quiz that tests recall, and a project that is never reviewed. The practice component was always the expensive part and was therefore always thin, and it was tolerable while explanation was scarce. Explanation is now abundant, which leaves the category selling the commoditised half of the product and not the scarce half.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Practice with feedback required human time, so it was priced out of a product sold at consumer prices — a business model built on one-to-many delivery cannot afford one-to-one response, and that constraint shaped the whole category. Multiple choice was the automatable assessment available. Instructors are not instructional designers. And the completion statistic was never a commercial problem.

## What to Build
Build the practice layer and make the feedback real. Generate exercises at the right difficulty for each learner's current state, which is the core and connects directly to the knowledge model. Give substantive feedback on submitted work rather than a score, since the feedback is what teaches and is the component that has never existed at this price point. Support the practice forms that fit the subject — code that runs, writing that is critiqued, problems that are worked, artefacts that are compared — because a single quiz format serves almost nothing well. Space and schedule retrieval, as durability comes from spacing and no course does it. Diagnose the error rather than marking it wrong, which is the difference between practice and testing. Make peer review work by structuring it and measuring reviewer reliability, since unstructured peer review produces noise and is currently the main alternative. Keep the instructor in the loop for the work that needs judgement, and route only that. Use practice performance to drive what the learner sees next, closing the loop with the knowledge model. Measure whether practice improves later performance rather than whether it was completed. And price it as the product, because it is the part that is scarce.

## Target Customer
Product and instructional leadership, learners who watched and cannot do, instructors who cannot review at scale, and assessment and practice tooling vendors.

## Impact If Built
A business model built on one-to-many delivery cannot afford one-to-one response, and that constraint shaped the category. Substantive automated feedback on work is what lifts that constraint, and it arrives exactly as explanation stops being scarce.

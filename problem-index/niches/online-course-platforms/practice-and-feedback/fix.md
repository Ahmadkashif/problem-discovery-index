# The Project Nobody Ever Reads

**Niche:** [[niches/online-course-platforms/practice-and-feedback/profile|Practice & Feedback]]
**Industry:** [[industries/online-course-platforms|Online Course Platforms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The course ends with a project, the learner builds it, submits it, and nothing happens.
**Tags:** #quick-win #worker-facing #evaluation-metrics #automation #large-language-models #descriptive-statistics #confidence-intervals #workflow-orchestration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to give a learner structured practice and real feedback on their work at a cost that scales — and whoever does it owns the part of learning that recorded video never provided and generative explanation does not replace.

## The Problem
The capstone project is the one part of the course where the learner does the thing rather than watching it. They spend hours on it, submit it, and receive an automatic completion mark or nothing at all. Nobody tells them what was wrong, what was good, or whether what they built would be acceptable in practice. The most valuable learning opportunity in the course is the one with no response attached, and learners notice.

## Why It's Still Broken
Review requires someone to look, so the submission was made automatic to keep the course scalable — a step that costs human time in a product sold at volume gets replaced by an acknowledgement. Peer review was tried and produces inconsistent results. Instructors cannot review thousands of submissions. And nobody measures how many learners even submit.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Give the submission a response. Provide automated feedback against the project's criteria rather than a completion mark, which is the fix and is now feasible for most subject matter. Publish the criteria before the project, since learners currently guess at what is being asked. Show exemplar submissions at different quality levels, as comparison teaches quickly and costs nothing to produce once. Structure peer review with specific questions and measure reviewer reliability, which turns a noisy mechanism into a usable one. Route a sample to the instructor rather than all or none, so human judgement reaches the cases that need it. Let learners revise and resubmit, because the second attempt after feedback is where the learning happens and is currently not offered. Report submission rates, which nobody tracks and which will show how many learners skip the only practice in the course. Detect the common failure modes across submissions and address them in the course, as the same mistakes recur and are invisible to the instructor. Acknowledge the submission properly, since silence after hours of work is the specific injury here. And measure whether learners who submitted and received feedback performed better later, which validates the whole component.

## Who Feels the Pain
Learners who built something and heard nothing; instructors who cannot review at scale and know the project is hollow; employers reading a certificate backed by an unreviewed project; and a sector whose only practice component is unattended.

## Impact If Fixed
A step that costs human time in a volume product gets replaced by an acknowledgement, so the project became a submission with no response. Automated feedback against published criteria, with revision allowed, restores the one part of the course where learning actually happens.

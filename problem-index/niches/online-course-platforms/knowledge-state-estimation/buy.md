# Knowledge Tracing From Intelligent Tutoring

**Industry:** [[industries/online-course-platforms|Online Course Platforms]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Intelligent tutoring systems have modelled knowledge state for forty years, and consumer course platforms show a progress bar.
**Tags:** #hidden-markov-models #bayesian-inference #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #markov-chains #gradient-boosting #cross-validation #hypothesis-testing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to infer what a learner currently knows from pauses, attempts, abandonments and returns — and whoever estimates mastery accurately can tell each learner what to do next instead of what to watch next.

## The Problem
Knowledge tracing is an established research area with decades of work: Bayesian knowledge tracing, deep knowledge tracing, skill and prerequisite modelling, mastery learning, and evaluated tutoring systems with demonstrated learning gains. It has been deployed in school mathematics and in some corporate training. Consumer course platforms — which have orders of magnitude more learner data than any research deployment — use none of it.

## What Already Exists
Bayesian and neural knowledge tracing models; skill and prerequisite graph construction; mastery learning criteria; evaluated tutoring system designs; and learning gain measurement methodology.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to unstructured content and voluntary learners. It requires: (1) a concept and prerequisite structure that must be inferred from content authored without one, where tutoring systems are built on a curriculum — this is the substantive prerequisite for everything else; (2) evidence that is mostly viewing behaviour rather than problem attempts, since the content is video and the tutoring literature assumes exercises; (3) learners who will leave rather than persist, which changes what an adaptive system may demand; (4) thousands of independently authored courses rather than one curriculum, so the models must generalise; and (5) sample sizes far larger than any research setting, which supports approaches the literature could not evaluate.

## Target Customer
Product and data leadership, instructional designers, adaptive learning vendors, and education researchers.

## Impact If Solved
Forty years of knowledge tracing has run on school curricula with small samples, and the consumer platforms have the opposite. Inferring the concept structure from content authored without one is the prerequisite, and it is the step nobody has taken.

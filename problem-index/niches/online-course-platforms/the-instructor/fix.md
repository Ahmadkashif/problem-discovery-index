# The Algorithm Changed Again

**Niche:** [[niches/online-course-platforms/the-instructor/profile|The Instructor]]
**Industry:** [[industries/online-course-platforms|Online Course Platforms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Enrolments halved overnight and the instructor found out what happened from a forum thread three weeks later.
**Tags:** #worker-facing #quick-win #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #revenue-impact #automation #confidence-intervals #change-point-detection
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to let someone who knows a subject spend their week teaching rather than on funnels, pricing and platform changes — and whoever removes that overhead keeps the experts the whole catalogue depends on.

## The Problem
A ranking or promotion change is made. Instructor income moves sharply. No announcement is issued, no explanation is available, and the instructors work it out collectively on a forum over weeks, arriving at theories that are sometimes right. In the meantime they change prices, rewrite descriptions and produce new content in response to a mechanism they have misdiagnosed, wasting effort and deepening the sense that the platform is adversarial.

## Why It's Still Broken
Ranking changes are treated as internal product decisions, so there is no notification practice — a change made to a system the platform owns is not framed as a change to someone's income. Explaining the ranking invites gaming. Instructor communication is a support function. And nobody measures the effort instructors waste responding to changes they do not understand.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Tell them something before they find out. Announce changes that materially affect instructor income before making them, which is the fix and is what every comparable marketplace eventually had to do. Explain the direction of the change without publishing an exploitable specification, since directional guidance is useful and does not enable gaming. Show each instructor the effect on their own courses rather than describing a general change, as the individual impact is the question. Distinguish a platform change from a demand change in their analytics, because instructors currently cannot and misattribute constantly. Give notice proportionate to the impact, so a large change comes with time to adapt. Publish a changelog, which creates a record and reduces the forum's speculative authority. Respond in the forum where the conversation actually is. Tell instructors what they can do in response, since the absence of guidance is what produces wasted effort. Track instructor income volatility as a platform metric, as it is a direct measure of how much the platform is destabilising its own supply. And accept that some changes will be unpopular and explain them anyway, because silence costs more.

## Who Feels the Pain
Instructors whose income moves without explanation; learners whose instructors leave; support teams fielding speculation; and platforms whose supply relationship degrades with each unannounced change.

## Impact If Fixed
A change made to a system the platform owns is not framed as a change to someone's income, so no notification practice exists. Advance notice with per-instructor impact replaces weeks of collective speculation and the wasted effort that follows it.

# Niche Analysis — Online Course Platforms

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/online-course-platforms|Online Course Platforms]]

## Niche Selection

These platforms stream video, run quizzes, issue certificates and handle payments at scale, and none of them can say whether a learner learned anything. The business model monetises enrolment and the mission claims to produce capability, and nothing in the stack connects the two: completion rates have been publicly known and stable for over a decade, assessment is mostly multiple choice or an unreviewed project, and the outcome that matters happens outside the platform and is never observed. Meanwhile recorded video — the product — has been commoditised, and what remains scarce is structured practice, real assessment, feedback on work and a credential somebody believes. Technical courses decay within eighteen months while ranking signals lag by years. The eight niches below split the category by what competitors actually fight over.

## Why These Niches

Learning measurement takes the largest share because it is the gap between what the sector sells and what it claims, and because as recorded explanation is commoditised it becomes the only defensible product. Discovery is second because ranking determines what a learner buys and is built on signals that predict popularity rather than fit. The two low-digitized niches are the ones the category has invested in least and that now matter most: structured practice with real feedback on work, and keeping courses correct as the tools they teach change. The two underserved audiences are the expert spending their week on funnels instead of teaching and the learner who quietly stops in week two. The two automatable niches are a question queue that is mostly the same twenty questions and a cohort operation run by hand.

## Filter Notes

Seven of the eight level-1 niches are terminal. Learning Measurement is not: the label names an outcome rather than a contest, and writing the contested statement produces two sentences with different winners. Knowledge state estimation asks what a learner currently knows and what they should do next — an inference problem over the most detailed record of learning behaviour ever assembled, where the winner is whoever models mastery most accurately from pauses, attempts, abandonments and returns. Credential and outcome verification asks how to prove to a third party that this person can do the thing — an assessment design and external outcome problem, where the winner is whoever produces a credential an employer believes and can connect it to what happened after. One is inference inside the platform and the other is evidence for someone outside it; they are built by different people and sold to different buyers. It therefore decomposes into **Knowledge State Estimation** and **Credential & Outcome Verification**.

Two candidates were considered and rejected. **One-to-one tutoring and school-age instruction** shares the learning-outcome problem but the contest over it belongs to [[industries/online-tutoring-platforms|Online Tutoring Platforms]]. **Pre-hire skills assessment** is where a credential is consumed and is the contest of [[industries/talent-assessment-platforms|Talent Assessment Platforms]], where measuring capability for an employer is analysed on its own terms.

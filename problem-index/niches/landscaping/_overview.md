## Why These Niches

Landscaping fragments along service type (maintenance vs. install vs. design), customer type (residential vs. commercial), seasonality (year-round vs. snow overlay), and business scale (solo operator vs. multi-crew operation). These 8 niches cover the full span: the two largest revenue segments (residential maintenance and commercial grounds), the two most digitally neglected service specialties (landscape design-install and irrigation), the two most underserved operator profiles (solo mow-and-blow and seasonal snow removal), and the two highest-ROI automation targets that cut across all landscaping companies (crew routing and estimating). Excluded: tree care / arboriculture (distinct licensing, equipment, and insurance profile), hardscape-only contractors (closer to masonry/concrete trades), and lawn chemical application franchises (Scotts/TruGreen franchise model with different economics).

## Why These Pockets

**No pocket qualified**, and the reason is structural rather than a series of near misses. A $105 billion industry with essentially no analytical intermediary is a genuine finding, and it comes from the shape of the value chain: inputs flow from manufacturers through distributors to a fragmented contractor base, which sells labour directly to property owners. Nothing sits in between that gets paid to analyse anything.

The closest positions confirm it. Landscape architects produce a design that is unambiguously the invoice, and they specify plants across decades of projects without recording which specifications survived — an insight function that exists but has no accumulating asset. Consulting arborists assess whether a tree will fail, which is a real prediction with real liability attached, and almost never learn which assessed trees actually came down. Both are the right shape at the wrong scale, and both are missing the outcome loop that would give them a moat.

Where data does concentrate, it is attached to a distribution margin. The national landscape distributors hold contractor-level purchase histories across the industry — the only view of what landscape businesses actually install and when — and earn on the product. The field service platforms hold estimated-versus-actual crew hours across many contractors and properties, which is the entire margin question in recurring maintenance, and sell scheduling software.

Two of the industry's most relevant insight pockets belong elsewhere in the vault: plant diagnostics is the same pocket already logged under greenhouse horticulture, and pesticide registration science under crop farming. Landscaping's own layer is thinner than either.

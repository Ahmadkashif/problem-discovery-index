# Missile & Interceptor Production Ramps

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/defense-systems-manufacturers|Defense Systems Manufacturers]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is: every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to raise delivered rounds per month on a qualified missile line, but the shortage that caps the rate sits in two places with different winners — sub-tier motors and their energetic ingredients, where whoever delivers qualified motors at rate wins, and the prime's own final assembly and acceptance test, where whoever raises first-pass yield wins. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.5B a year of production planning, supply-chain, quality and test tooling and services spent on missile and interceptor ramps at primes and tier-1s (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~3% of the five codes' ~$52.9B receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — primes run ERP, MES and validated test stations, but ramp plans, shortage lists and constraint analyses live in spreadsheets and weekly slide decks
**Target Buyer:** Programme director or VP of production for a missile family
**Automation Potential:** Medium — planning and shortage prediction automate well; qualification and acceptance stay human

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
This is where the money is. Lockheed Martin's Missiles and Fire Control segment grew sales 14% to $14.45B in 2025 on JASSM, LRASM, GMLRS, PrSM and PAC-3 volume, and ended the year with a $46.65B backlog. CNAS counts $49.5B requested in FY2027 for ten munitions, more than eight times the FY2016–26 average, and finds production rates well below DoW objectives. Multi-year orders now outrun line capacity (THAAD's books cap output at 96 a year against an FY2027 order of 857, per CNAS), so the work is converting backlog into delivered rounds.

## Current Tools & Gaps
SAP and Oracle ERP with MRP, iBASEt Solumina or Siemens Opcenter for MES, Kinaxis- or o9-style supply planning at some primes, and supplier risk feeds from Exiger and Air. The gap is a single rate model that joins sub-tier motor and energetics supply, line capacity and acceptance-test yield, so the programme knows which constraint binds next quarter. The two sub-niches below hold the two halves of that constraint.

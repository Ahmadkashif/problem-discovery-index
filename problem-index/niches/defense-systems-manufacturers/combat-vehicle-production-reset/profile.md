# Combat Vehicle Production & Depot Reset

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/defense-systems-manufacturers|Defense Systems Manufacturers]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to return vehicles from production and reset on the contracted date, and whoever cuts the share of vehicles held for parts discovered only at teardown takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$350M a year of planning, technical data, inspection and parts-forecasting tooling and services at armoured vehicle plants and the depots and contractors that rebuild them (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~0.7% of the five codes' receipts (estimate); armoured vehicles (336992) took $7.1B of 2022 receipts across 57 firms (SUSB), and General Dynamics reported $4.97B of military vehicle revenue in 2025
**Digital Adoption:** Low — technical manuals, teardown findings and work orders are largely paper or PDF, and parts demand is forecast from history rather than condition
**Target Buyer:** Director of operations at a vehicle plant or reset line; Army depot production manager
**Automation Potential:** Medium — findings prediction and parts forecasting automate; teardown, welding and rebuild are hands-on

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Tanks, infantry fighting vehicles and armoured carriers are built in small numbers and then rebuilt, upgraded and reset for decades. Vehicles come back from units and from overseas with unknown wear and battle damage, are torn down to the hull, inspected, repaired and rebuilt to a new configuration. General Dynamics Land Systems, BAE Systems and the Army's own depots do this work (recalled). The schedule risk is the finding nobody planned for, a cracked hull or a worn component, and the parts it calls for. The contest is the reset date.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Army and contractor ERP, interactive electronic technical manuals, paper inspection checklists, and parts forecasting from replacement factors. Gaps: teardown findings recorded on paper and never analysed across vehicles; parts kits ordered on average replacement rates; and no link from a vehicle's history (hours, deployments, prior repairs) to what will be found. Aviation already runs the closest analogue, absorbing non-routine findings in [[niches/aircraft-mro-shops/airframe-heavy-checks/profile|Airframe Heavy Checks & Base Maintenance]].

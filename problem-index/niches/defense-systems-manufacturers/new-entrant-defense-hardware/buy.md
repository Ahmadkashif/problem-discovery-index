# Startup ERP Stacks Adapted to Cost Accounting Standards

**Niche:** [[niches/defense-systems-manufacturers/new-entrant-defense-hardware/profile|New-Entrant Defense Hardware Makers Crossing into Production]]
**Industry:** [[industries/defense-systems-manufacturers|Defense Systems Manufacturers]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Startup ERPs run a hardware company's books well; they don't segregate unallowable costs, pool indirect rates or produce incurred-cost submissions.
**Tags:** #feature-engineering #large-language-models #data-integration #compliance #workflow-orchestration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor serving these firms is fighting to get a prototype maker through its first production contract's business-system reviews, and whoever gets the most clients' accounting, estimating and earned value systems approved without a payment withhold takes the account.

## The Problem
New defence makers grow up on commercial ERPs and later face a painful migration to a government-contract system, or run both and reconcile by hand.

## What Already Exists
NetSuite, Rillet-style AI-native ledgers and SAP Business ByDesign serve startups; Deltek Costpoint and Unanet serve government contractors with project accounting, timekeeping, indirect-rate pools and incurred-cost schedules (recalled).

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs: daily timekeeping with charge-number discipline; unallowable cost segregation under FAR Part 31; indirect-rate pools and allocation bases consistent with a disclosed practice; contract-type-aware billing (cost-plus, fixed-price incentive, OTA milestones); incurred-cost submission schedules; and links from the ledger to work packages so earned value can be built on the same data.

## Target Customer
Finance leaders at defence hardware companies with commercial ERPs and a first cost-type award in sight.

## Impact If Solved
Running government-contract rules inside the existing ERP avoids a mid-ramp migration and the months of reconciliation that come with it.

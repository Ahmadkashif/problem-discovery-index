# The Prototype With No Usable Cost History

**Niche:** [[niches/defense-systems-manufacturers/new-entrant-defense-hardware/profile|New-Entrant Defense Hardware Makers Crossing into Production]]
**Industry:** [[industries/defense-systems-manufacturers|Defense Systems Manufacturers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A startup builds dozens of prototypes under milestone payments, then can't price its first production lot because its actual costs were never tracked by unit or task.
**Tags:** #bayesian-linear-regression #feature-engineering #evaluation-metrics #compliance #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor serving these firms is fighting to get a prototype maker through its first production contract's business-system reviews, and whoever gets the most clients' accounting, estimating and earned value systems approved without a payment withhold takes the account.

## The Problem
Prototype OTAs pay on milestones, so the company tracked spending by milestone, if at all. When the production proposal asks for bases of estimate traced to actual hours per unit and per operation, the history doesn't exist, and estimates rest on engineering judgement the government will question.

## Why It's Still Broken
Nobody required detailed charging during the prototype phase, and engineers in a fast-moving startup resist timekeeping by task.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Lightweight task-level time capture from day one of any build that might go to production, linked to work orders and serial numbers; reconstruction of prototype costs from purchase records, MES data and calendars where time wasn't kept; and a learning-curve model that turns prototype units into supportable production estimates.

## Who Feels the Pain
Pricing leads writing production proposals, CFOs negotiating with contracting officers, and founders who see a strong technical position eroded at negotiation.

## Impact If Fixed
A supportable cost history turns questioned hours into accepted ones, which on a first production lot can decide whether the contract is profitable: recovering even 5–10% of proposed labour hours from questioned to accepted on a lot worth tens of millions of dollars is worth a million dollars or more (estimate).

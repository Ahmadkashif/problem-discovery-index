# Artillery & Ordnance Metal Parts and Load-Assemble-Pack Plants

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/defense-systems-manufacturers|Defense Systems Manufacturers]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn new and modernised artillery lines into accepted lots, and whoever raises the share of projectile bodies and loaded lots accepted on first submission takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$300M a year of MES, process control, inspection, quality and engineering services at artillery, mortar and bomb metal-parts and load-assemble-pack plants (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~0.6% of the five codes' receipts (estimate); ammunition except small arms (332993) took $4.0B of 2022 receipts across 47 firms, ~94% at nine enterprises with 500+ employees (SUSB)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — many lines run in decades-old government-owned plants on paper travellers, chart recorders and manual inspection logs
**Target Buyer:** Plant manager or operations director at a contractor-operated ammunition plant; Army programme office for ammunition production
**Automation Potential:** Medium — inspection and process monitoring automate; hazardous handling and acceptance stay manual

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The 155mm programme is the industry's most public ramp. Output reached about 36,000 rounds a month by March 2026, against a 100,000 goal for October 2025; projectile metal parts are the limit; the $469M Mesquite line had produced none to specification; and the Army now aims to load, assemble and pack 140,000 a month by December 2027 with a modernised Iowa plant and new Kansas and Arkansas facilities (Defense News on the DoD IG, July 2026). The work is forging, machining and heat-treating shell bodies, then filling and assembling rounds in government-owned plants run by contractors such as General Dynamics OTS and American Ordnance (recalled). Small-arms ammunition belongs to the queued firearms-manufacturers industry.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Plant ERP, paper travellers, standalone gauging and X-ray stations, and statistical process control in spreadsheets. Gaps: no digital process record per shell, process knowledge held by long-tenured operators at older plants, and lot acceptance results not linked back to process conditions.

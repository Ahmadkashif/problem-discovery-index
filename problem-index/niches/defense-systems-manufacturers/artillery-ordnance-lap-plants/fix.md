# Paper Travellers in the Explosive-Fill Bay

**Niche:** [[niches/defense-systems-manufacturers/artillery-ordnance-lap-plants/profile|Artillery & Ordnance Metal Parts and Load-Assemble-Pack Plants]]
**Industry:** [[industries/defense-systems-manufacturers|Defense Systems Manufacturers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Fill-operation records are kept on paper in hazard-rated bays, so a radiographic reject can't be traced to its fill lot's record.
**Tags:** #cnns #object-detection #feature-engineering #compliance #worker-facing #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn new and modernised artillery lines into accepted lots, and whoever raises the share of projectile bodies and loaded lots accepted on first submission takes the account.

## The Problem
Projectile bodies are filled in hazard-rated bays where electronics are restricted, and operators keep the fill-operation record on paper sheets. Radiography later rejects some rounds, which are scrapped or downloaded, but the link back to the fill lot's record is a manual search through paper.

## Why It's Still Broken
Hazard rules have kept ordinary tablets and sensors out of the bays, and the plants are old and government-owned, so capital for instrumentation competes with capacity. Paper satisfies the minimum record requirement.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Intrinsically safe sensors on fill equipment feeding a lot record outside the bay, with operator checks confirmed on approved hardware at the bay exit. Radiography images are stored with the record, and a model pre-marks indications so the radiographer confirms. Weekly reports link reject rates to fill lots.

## Who Feels the Pain
Fill-line operators repeating paperwork, radiographers reviewing every round cold, and plant engineers who can't find the cause of a reject spike.

## Impact If Fixed
Tracing rejects to fill-lot records turns a recurring scrap cause into a fixable process setting, which matters at 140,000 rounds a month.

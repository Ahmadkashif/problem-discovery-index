# The Basis of Estimate That Cites a Proposal, Not an Actual

**Niche:** [[niches/defense-systems-manufacturers/cost-proposal-pricing-boe/profile|Cost-Type Proposal Pricing & Bases of Estimate]]
**Industry:** [[industries/defense-systems-manufacturers|Defense Systems Manufacturers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Bases of estimate are copied from earlier proposals, so they cite what the company once bid rather than what the work actually took.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #k-nearest-neighbors #evaluation-metrics #compliance #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to produce cost volumes whose bases of estimate survive government evaluation, and whoever's proposals draw the fewest questioned hours in DCAA and DCMA review takes the account.

## The Problem
Under a proposal deadline, the fastest basis of estimate is last proposal's, adjusted. Over several cycles the chain of copies drifts from the original actuals, and evaluators who ask for the underlying data find the trail ends in another proposal.

## Why It's Still Broken
Proposal archives are the easiest place to look, and nothing in the pricing workflow checks whether a cited figure traces to recorded actuals.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Before submission, check every basis of estimate's cited hours against the cost system: flag figures that trace only to prior proposals, figures that differ from recorded actuals by more than a set margin, and analogies whose jobs closed with large overruns. Give the estimator the matching actuals to cite instead.

## Who Feels the Pain
Estimators defending bases in fact-finding, pricing directors negotiating questioned hours, and companies exposed to defective-pricing claims on certified data.

## Impact If Fixed
A pre-submission trace check removes the most common reason hours are questioned and lowers defective-pricing exposure on certified proposals; cutting questioned hours by even 10–20% on a large production proposal is worth hundreds of thousands to millions of dollars at negotiation (estimate).

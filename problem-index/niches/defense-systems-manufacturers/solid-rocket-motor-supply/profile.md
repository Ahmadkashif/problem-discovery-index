# Solid Rocket Motor & Propellant Ingredient Supply

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/defense-systems-manufacturers|Defense Systems Manufacturers]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to put qualified motors on missile lines at the contracted rate, and whoever delivers the most flight-qualified motors per month takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$600M a year of process, quality, qualification and supply-chain tooling and services at motor makers and their energetic-ingredient suppliers (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~1% of the five codes' receipts (estimate); propulsion units (336415) themselves took $19.9B of 2022 receipts across 17 firms (SUSB)
**Digital Adoption:** Low-medium — mixers and CT systems are instrumented, but batch records, deviations and qualification evidence are paper and PDFs
**Target Buyer:** VP of operations or quality at a motor maker; propulsion supply-chain lead at a prime
**Automation Potential:** Medium — evidence assembly and drift detection automate; acceptance stays with qualified people

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
US solid rocket motor makers shrank from six to two between 1995 and 2017 (Northrop Grumman via Orbital ATK, L3Harris via Aerojet Rocketdyne). Anduril, Ursa Major, X-Bow and General Dynamics are now trying to become the third and fourth, backed by government awards: Anduril's Mississippi plant drew a $14M award, and Northrop says it has invested more than $1B to double output over four years (Breaking Defense, January 2026). The constraint runs down to ingredients: some have a single US source, smaller chemical firms are trying to qualify as second sources, and an ingredient supplier's closure forced Nammo toward requalification. The contest is qualified motors per month, and qualification evidence decides who gets there.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Process historians, CT and X-ray systems, LIMS for ingredient testing, MES travellers, and supplier quality packages in PDFs. The gaps: no joined batch-to-test record, qualification evidence assembled by hand for each motor and ingredient change, and the senior operators' process judgement uncaptured (the industry's [[problems/defense-systems-manufacturers/high-impact|high-impact problem]]).

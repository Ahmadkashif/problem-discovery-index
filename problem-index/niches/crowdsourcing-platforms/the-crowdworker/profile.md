# The Crowdworker

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/crowdsourcing-platforms|Crowdsourcing Platforms]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Whether the person doing the work has any instrument for understanding their own earnings, defending their record, or carrying their experience anywhere.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$390M — 13% of the US microtask and research-participant market
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~13%
**Digital Adoption:** Very low from the platform; substantial from the workers themselves
**Target Buyer:** Crowdworkers; worker organisations; researchers studying the market
**Automation Potential:** High — everything needed is derivable from records the worker can access

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche

Crowdworkers are globally distributed, classified as contractors everywhere, working across several platforms, with no employment relationship anywhere and no collective structure. They receive an approval rate and a payment history and nothing else.

They cannot say what their realised hourly rate is, because it requires timing their own unpaid search and instruction-reading alongside the paid work. They cannot compare platforms on anything except headline task rewards. They have no portable record of years of work. And when a rejection damages their standing they have no evidence, no appeal and no representation.

The niche is distinct because the workers have already built substantial infrastructure for themselves — forums, extensions, review sites, spreadsheets, mutual aid — and because that infrastructure is an unusually precise map of what the platforms decline to provide.

## Current Tools & Gaps

Platform dashboards showing earnings and approval rate. Community forums with extensive institutional knowledge. Browser extensions for task alerting, requester reputation and earnings tracking. Personal spreadsheets. Academic researchers who study this workforce and are, in practice, one of its few external advocates.

The gaps are measurement, portability and voice. No tool computes a true effective rate including unpaid time across platforms. No portable record of work exists. And there is no mechanism through which the workforce's experience reaches the people setting the rules — the platforms' product decisions, the requesters' pricing, or the ethics boards that have proven able to change behaviour.

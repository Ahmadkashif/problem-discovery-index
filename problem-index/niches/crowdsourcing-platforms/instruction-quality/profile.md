# Instruction & Task Design Quality

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/crowdsourcing-platforms|Crowdsourcing Platforms]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Whether a badly-specified task can be detected before it produces a batch of bad data and a set of unfair rejections.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$486M — 60% of the task design and quality niche
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~16% of the US microtask and research-participant market
**Digital Adoption:** Very low — the requester writes instructions and finds out afterwards
**Target Buyer:** Requesters; platform product and quality teams
**Automation Potential:** Very high — the signals are all recorded and unread

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche

Most bad crowd data is caused by unclear instructions, and the requester who wrote them is the person least able to notice. They know what they meant, so they cannot see the second reading, and the feedback that would reveal it — confusion, questions, split answers — either does not reach them or reaches them as a quality complaint about workers.

The distinguishing property is that everyone wants this. A requester with ambiguous instructions is getting bad data and paying for it; they would fix the wording in a moment if they knew. Workers want it because ambiguous instructions are what get them rejected. The platform wants it because bad batches produce disputes, refund requests and churn on both sides.

It is also detectable early and cheaply. Within the first fifty submissions, a defective instruction shows in the response distribution, the completion time variance, the clarification questions and the abandonment rate — long before the batch completes and the rejections start.

## Current Tools & Gaps

Preview and pilot facilities, which some platforms offer and few requesters use. Templates for common task types. Inter-annotator agreement reported after the fact. Worker-facing comment fields, where they exist, going to the requester's inbox.

The gaps are pre-launch review and early detection. Nothing reads a requester's instructions for ambiguity before they post, though that is now a routine capability. And nothing watches the first fifty submissions for the signature of a defective task, though every signal is recorded and the batch is still stoppable at that point.

# Requester Reputation & Trust

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/crowdsourcing-platforms|Crowdsourcing Platforms]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Whether a worker can see how a requester has behaved before agreeing to work for them.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$270M — 9% of the US microtask and research-participant market
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~9%
**Digital Adoption:** None on-platform — external sites and forums do it instead
**Target Buyer:** Crowdworkers; platform leadership; requesters who behave well
**Automation Potential:** Very high — every input is in the platform's database

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche

Workers are rated continuously — approval rate, qualification scores, task history — and requesters are not rated at all. A worker accepting a task knows nothing about whether this requester rejects indiscriminately, pays within hours or within weeks, answers questions, or posts tasks that turn out to be broken.

All of it is in the platform's database. Rejection rate, time to approval, question response rate, batch abandonment rate, realised hourly rate on previous batches — computable per requester, per batch, immediately.

The asymmetry is the point. One side of a two-sided market is comprehensively scored and the other is anonymous, and the side that is scored is the side without money. The informal infrastructure workers have built to approximate requester reputation — forums, review sites, browser extensions — is a direct measure of what the platforms have chosen not to provide.

## Current Tools & Gaps

Nothing on-platform on most services beyond the requester's name and sometimes their total task volume. Externally: worker-run requester review sites, forum threads, shared blocklists and browser extensions that overlay community ratings onto task listings. Some academic-focused platforms publish limited requester statistics.

The gaps are everything the platform could publish and does not: rejection rate, approval speed, payment reliability, responsiveness, and realised hourly rate on completed batches. Also missing is any consequence for a requester whose behaviour is far outside the norm.

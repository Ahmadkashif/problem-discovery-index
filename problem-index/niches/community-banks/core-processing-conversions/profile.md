# Core Processing & Conversions

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/community-banks|Community Banks]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is that every competitor here is fighting over the bank's core relationship, but at renewal the contest is pricing leverage against a three-vendor market, won by whoever holds the best benchmark, and in a conversion the contest is first-time-right data mapping on a fixed weekend, won by whoever maps most accurately. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$5B a year in core processing, item processing, digital banking and related contract spend by community banks, plus conversion and deconversion costs (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~50% of community bank technology, data and outside-services spend (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** High for processing itself; low for the contract and conversion work around it, which runs on spreadsheets and vendor PDFs
**Target Buyer:** CEO / COO / CFO, with the board approving the contract
**Automation Potential:** High for mapping and reconciliation, moderate for the negotiation

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A community bank does not run its own systems; it rents a core, and three vendors (Fiserv ~42%, Jack Henry ~21%, FIS ~9% of banks in 2022 survey data reported by the Kansas City Fed) serve more than 70% of banks. Contracts run for years, bundle dozens of services, and carry deconversion fees that make leaving expensive. Every bank merger forces a conversion of the acquired bank onto the acquirer's core; Jack Henry booked $33.9M of deconversion revenue in fiscal 2025, mostly from acquired clients. The two moments when the bank has any leverage or risk, renewal and conversion, are different problems with different winners.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Core vendors' own sales and conversion teams; independent advisors who benchmark and negotiate contracts (Cornerstone Advisors, Paladin fs and others); conversion consultants; spreadsheets. Gaps: no transparent pricing; contract terms buried in schedules; conversion mapping rebuilt from scratch at every deal.

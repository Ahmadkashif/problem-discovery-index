# Software Spend Management Adapted to Core Banking

**Niche:** [[niches/community-banks/core-processing-conversions/profile|Core Processing & Conversions]]
**Industry:** [[industries/community-banks|Community Banks]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** SaaS spend management tools already track contracts, renewals and usage for corporate software portfolios; a community bank's largest vendor relationship is billed in ways those tools do not understand.

**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is that every competitor here is fighting over the bank's core relationship, but at renewal the contest is pricing leverage against a three-vendor market, won by whoever holds the best benchmark, and in a conversion the contest is first-time-right data mapping on a fixed weekend, won by whoever maps most accurately. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## The Problem
Banks manage vendor contracts for regulatory vendor-management purposes (due diligence, risk ratings, SOC reports) but not for spend.

## What Already Exists
SaaS spend management and contract lifecycle tools extract renewal dates, track usage against licences and benchmark per-seat prices; bank vendor-management suites track third-party risk assessments.

## The Customization Gap
Core banking needs: (1) pricing units native to banking (per account, per item, per transaction type, per online user) rather than per seat; (2) invoice parsing for core vendors' formats; (3) usage derived from core volumes, not logins; (4) deconversion fee calculation from contract formulas; and (5) linkage to the regulatory vendor-management file so one record serves both purposes.

## Target Customer
CFOs and vendor managers at community banks with large core and digital banking spend.

## Impact If Solved
Spend visibility on the bank's largest vendor using an established category of tool, adapted to banking's billing units.

# Farm Management Accounting Adapted to Credit Review

**Niche:** [[niches/community-banks/agricultural-lending/profile|Agricultural Lending]]
**Industry:** [[industries/community-banks|Community Banks]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Farm financial analysis tools already produce accrual-adjusted farm statements and benchmarks; they are built for the farmer and the farm management educator, not for a bank's credit file.
**Tags:** #feature-engineering #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #data-integration #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn a farm borrower's year (acres, yields, contracts, input costs, crop insurance and government payments) into a projected carry-over before the operating line renews, and whoever updates that projection mid-season most reliably takes the account.

## The Problem
Farm income on a cash-basis tax return is a poor picture of a farm's year: inventory swings, prepaid inputs and deferred sales move income between years. Ag lenders adjust to accrual by hand.

## What Already Exists
FINPACK and its farm business management benchmark data, farm accounting software, and university farm business management programmes produce accrual-adjusted income statements, balance sheets and enterprise budgets in a standard format with peer benchmarks.

## The Customization Gap
The bank needs: (1) the accrual adjustment pulled directly into its spreading and credit memo, not re-keyed; (2) the bank's own credit policy ratios and FSA guarantee requirements applied; (3) collateral values tied to inspection records and market prices; (4) multi-entity farm families (parents, children, land LLCs) consolidated as the bank lends to them; and (5) a portfolio view across all ag borrowers rather than one farm at a time.

## Target Customer
Ag lending teams at rural community banks, and the correspondent banks that buy participations from them.

## Impact If Solved
Accrual-quality farm analysis in the credit file without hand adjustment, comparable across the bank's whole ag book.

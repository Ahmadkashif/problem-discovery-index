# The Collateral Inspection on a Clipboard

**Niche:** [[niches/community-banks/agricultural-lending/profile|Agricultural Lending]]
**Industry:** [[industries/community-banks|Community Banks]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Grain in the bin and cattle in the lot secure millions in operating lines, and the inspection that verifies them is a handwritten form scanned into the loan file.
**Tags:** #cnns #object-detection #large-language-models #quick-win #worker-facing #compliance
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn a farm borrower's year (acres, yields, contracts, input costs, crop insurance and government payments) into a projected carry-over before the operating line renews, and whoever updates that projection mid-season most reliably takes the account.

## The Problem
Operating lines are secured by stored grain, livestock and equipment. The borrowing base depends on how much is there. Lenders or contract inspectors visit, estimate bin volumes and head counts, and write them down; the numbers are typed into a borrowing base certificate weeks later.

## Why It's Still Broken
Inspections are infrequent and manual because they take a day of driving, the forms vary by bank, and nothing connects the inspection to the borrowing base or to the previous visit.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A mobile inspection app that captures photos, bin dimensions and fill levels, head counts and equipment serial numbers with location and time stamps, computes volumes and values against current prices, compares against the borrower's reported inventory and the last inspection, and writes the result to the borrowing base.

## Who Feels the Pain
Ag lenders driving between farms; credit administrators reconciling borrowing bases; examiners sampling ag files for collateral support.

## Impact If Fixed
Faster, more frequent and auditable inspections, and discrepancies between reported and actual collateral caught while there is still time to act.

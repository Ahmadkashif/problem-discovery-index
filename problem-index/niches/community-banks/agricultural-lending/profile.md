# Agricultural Lending

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/community-banks|Community Banks]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn a farm borrower's year (acres, yields, contracts, input costs, crop insurance and government payments) into a projected carry-over before the operating line renews, and whoever updates that projection mid-season most reliably takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$350M a year in ag lending software, farm financial analysis, collateral inspection and appraisal spend by community banks (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~3.5% of community bank technology, data and outside-services spend (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — farm balance sheets on paper or in Excel, cash flow projections built at the kitchen table, collateral inspections by clipboard
**Target Buyer:** Senior agricultural lender / Chief Credit Officer
**Automation Potential:** Medium — projection and monitoring automate; the farm visit and the read of the operator do not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Community banks held about 70% of bank agricultural loans in the FDIC's last community banking study, and for hundreds of rural banks farm lending is most of the book. The credit is seasonal and collateral is physical: an annual operating line funds seed, fertiliser and chemicals in spring and is repaid from harvest, livestock sales or a marketing contract in autumn. Whether a farmer can carry over a shortfall into next year depends on yields, prices, hedges, crop insurance guarantees and government programme payments that move all season. The competing lender is often the Farm Credit System, which has its own data and scale. The competitors are farm financial analysis tools such as FINPACK, the ag modules of lending platforms, and ag-specialist correspondent banks. The credit union counterpart is [[niches/credit-unions/agricultural-rural-cus/profile|🟠 Agricultural & Rural CUs]].

## Current Tools & Gaps
FINPACK (from the Center for Farm Financial Management) and farm management accounting packages, Excel projection templates, FSA guaranteed loan paperwork, crop insurance summaries from agents, USDA price and yield reports. Gaps: projections built once at renewal and not updated; inspections of grain inventory and livestock recorded on paper; the ag lender's read of which operators manage risk well held in memory.

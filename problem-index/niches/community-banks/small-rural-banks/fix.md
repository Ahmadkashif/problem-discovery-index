# The Compliance Officer Who Is Also the BSA Officer

**Niche:** [[niches/community-banks/small-rural-banks/profile|Sub-$500M Rural & Single-Market Banks]]
**Industry:** [[industries/community-banks|Community Banks]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** At a small bank one person often holds the compliance, BSA and security officer roles, and when they leave the bank loses its procedures, its calendar and its regulatory memory in a single resignation.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #compliance #worker-facing #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to be a small bank's outsourced second line (BSA, compliance, loan review and IT audit) under one evidence trail an examiner accepts as the bank's own function, and whoever the examiner stops writing up takes the account.

## The Problem
The officer knows when each disclosure review is due, which customers are on the high-risk list and why, what the examiner criticised last time, and where the procedures really live. Much of it is in their head and their inbox. Their departure is followed by months of reconstruction, often during an exam cycle.

## Why It's Still Broken
Small banks cannot afford a second person in the role, procedures are written once for the policy manual and not maintained, and nothing captures the officer's working knowledge as they work.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A working record built from the officer's own activity: the compliance calendar, decisions on high-risk customers with reasons, correspondence with examiners, procedure documents kept current from what is actually done. A successor, interim provider or examiner can find the current state of every obligation in one place.

## Who Feels the Pain
The officer carrying three roles; the CEO facing a vacancy; the successor starting from nothing.

## Impact If Fixed
Key-person risk falls from a crisis to a handover, and the bank keeps its compliance memory when people change.

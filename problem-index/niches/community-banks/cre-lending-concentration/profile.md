# CRE Lending & Concentration Risk

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/community-banks|Community Banks]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to show a CRE-concentrated bank's board and examiner, loan by loan, which properties will fail to refinance at today's rates before the maturity arrives, and whoever produces that answer from the bank's own rent rolls and maturity schedule fastest takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.2B a year in CRE data, stress testing, appraisal review and portfolio monitoring spend by community banks (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~12% of community bank technology, data and outside-services spend (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — loans and maturities sit in the core, but rent rolls, operating statements and appraisals arrive as PDFs and are re-keyed once a year
**Target Buyer:** Chief Credit Officer
**Automation Potential:** High for monitoring and stress testing, low for the workout decision

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Commercial real estate is the largest loan category on many community bank balance sheets; community banks held roughly 30% of all bank CRE loans in the FDIC's last community banking study. A bank whose non-owner-occupied CRE reaches 300% of total risk-based capital after 50% growth over 36 months, or whose construction and land loans reach 100%, is flagged for closer supervisory review under the 2006 interagency guidance and is expected to stress test its portfolio. Investor CRE loans made at 2019–2021 rates and five-year terms are now maturing into higher rates, so the question every examiner asks is which of them can refinance. That is a loan-by-loan cash flow question, not a portfolio average. The competitors are CRE data vendors such as Trepp and Moody's, the lending platforms that hold the loan records, and the consultants who run concentration stress tests.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Trepp, Moody's and CoStar sell CRE market data and stress tools; Abrigo and nCino hold the loan records; many banks still run concentration reports and stress tests in Excel or through a consultant once a year. The gaps: property cash flow updated only at annual review, stress tests applied to portfolio segments rather than each loan's own rent roll and lease expirations, and no running maturity-wall view joining rate, NOI and appraisal age.

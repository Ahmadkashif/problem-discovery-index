# The Exception Rate Nobody Wrote Down

**Niche:** [[niches/community-banks/core-deposit-pricing/profile|Core Deposit Franchise & Pricing]]
**Industry:** [[industries/community-banks|Community Banks]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Branch managers and lenders grant exception rates to keep deposits, and the bank has no record of who got what, why, or whether the money stayed.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #causal-inference #quick-win #workflow-orchestration #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to tell a bank's ALCO which deposit balances will stay, and at what rate, when the policy rate next moves, and whoever predicts beta and runoff by relationship rather than by product most accurately takes the account.

## The Problem
A customer with $400,000 in a money market account says a credit union down the road is paying more. The branch manager calls the CFO, or simply keys an exception rate in the core. Across a year, hundreds of these decisions set the bank's real deposit cost, and none of them is recorded as a decision.

## Why It's Still Broken
The core stores the rate, not the request, the competing offer, the approver or the reason. ALCO sees posted rates and blended cost, not the exception layer underneath, so it cannot tell whether exceptions retained balances or just raised cost.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A one-screen exception request that records the customer, balance, competing offer, requested rate, approver and reason, writes the rate to the core, and follows the balance for twelve months. Monthly reporting shows ALCO exception volume, cost and retention by approver and branch.

## Who Feels the Pain
CFOs explaining deposit cost to the board; branch managers improvising; ALCO setting posted rates blind to the exceptions.

## Impact If Fixed
The bank learns which exceptions retain money and which only cost it, and gets the labelled pricing decisions any future deposit model needs.

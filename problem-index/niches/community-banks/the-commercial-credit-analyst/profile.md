# The Commercial Credit Analyst

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/community-banks|Community Banks]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get an analyst from a borrower's raw returns to a credit memo the chief credit officer trusts in hours rather than days, and whoever makes every number traceable to its source and every senior edit reusable takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$500M a year in spreading, credit memo and annual review tooling and analyst training spend at community banks (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~5% of community bank technology, data and outside-services spend (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — spreading tools exist, but global cash flow, memo drafting and the annual review tickler run in Excel and Word
**Target Buyer:** Chief Credit Officer / credit administration manager
**Automation Potential:** High for spreading and drafting, low for the credit judgement

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The credit analyst is the person who turns a lender's relationship into a file: spreading three years of business and personal returns, building the global cash flow, testing covenants, writing the credit memo and doing it all again at annual review. BLS counts about 33,550 credit analysts across banks and credit unions (May 2023), a small occupation beside 167,130 loan officers, so a community bank may have ~two to six analysts supporting a dozen lenders (estimate). They are often junior, learn by having a senior credit officer red-line their memos, and leave for larger banks once trained. Tools are bought for lenders and for credit administration; the analyst's own day is the least supported part of the credit process. The competitors are spreading and memo vendors such as Abrigo, Moody's, nCino and Baker Hill, and AI-native credit memo start-ups.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Abrigo, Moody's, nCino and Baker Hill spreading and memo templates; Excel global cash flow workbooks; a spreadsheet tickler for missing financials and annual reviews. Gaps: entity structures rebuilt every year; senior officers' memo edits thrown away; no traceability from a memo figure to the return line it came from.

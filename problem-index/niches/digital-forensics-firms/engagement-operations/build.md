# Build: Activation in Minutes

**Niche:** Engagement & Retainer Operations
**Industry:** [[industries/digital-forensics-firms|Digital Forensics Firms]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** An activation path where authorisation, conflict checking, engagement terms and initial scope are pre-agreed and executed in minutes, so the first hours go to the incident.
**Tags:** #workflow-orchestration #evaluation-metrics #compliance #automation #data-integration #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Whether the commercial and administrative shell around an activation runs itself, or consumes hours of the first day.

## The Problem

A breach is discovered on a Friday evening. The organisation calls its broker, who contacts the insurer, who authorises a panel firm, who must run a conflict check, agree terms, establish the privilege arrangement, scope an initial engagement and get a cap approved.

Four parties, each with their own process, coordinated by phone and email, at a time when several of them are not at their desks. It can take hours and sometimes runs into the following day.

Those hours are the most valuable in the engagement. The attacker may still have access. Telemetry is ageing toward expiry. The statutory clock may already be running. And the work being done is administrative.

Where a retainer or panel relationship exists, much of it is pre-agreed and the activation is faster — which is the main practical benefit retainers deliver. Where none exists, which is common because most organisations have their first serious incident without one, the whole sequence happens from nothing, under crisis conditions, with an insured who has never done it.

Every element is knowable in advance. The terms, the conflict position, the privilege structure, the initial scope and the authorisation path are all decidable calmly, and are decided under pressure because nobody set them up.

## Why Nobody Has Built This

**It spans four organisations.** The insured, the broker, the insurer and the firm each own part of the process, and nobody owns the whole.

**Panel arrangements solve part of it and stop.** Pre-agreed rates and terms address the commercial layer and leave authorisation, conflict checking and scoping as live steps.

**Conflict checking is genuinely hard at speed.** A large firm must check the insured, the adversary if known, the affected parties and related entities against an extensive client base, and it is mostly manual.

**Privilege arrangements are jurisdiction-specific and lawyer-dependent.** The structure that establishes privilege over the investigation matters enormously and is set up by counsel, which adds a party and a step.

**The delay is not costed to anyone.** No party is measured on activation time, so nobody has optimised it.

**Most organisations activate once.** The insured has no reason to build a process they will use once, which is precisely the argument for the process living with the insurer or the firm.

## What to Build

**Pre-clear activation at the panel level.** Standing authorisation with a defined trigger, so a panel firm can begin on notification rather than after an approval chain. The insurer's exposure is bounded by an initial cap and their interest is unambiguously in a fast start.

**Run conflict checking continuously against the panel.** Pre-check the insured population and known adversary entities, maintained between incidents, so the check at activation is a lookup rather than an investigation.

**Pre-agree the engagement letter and privilege structure.** Templates agreed with the breach coach in advance, per jurisdiction, executed at activation rather than drafted.

**Set the initial scope from a standard package.** A defined first-phase scope with a standard cap that begins immediately, with the full scoping conversation happening in parallel rather than before. Starting the work should not wait for the negotiation of its eventual size.

**Provide a single activation channel.** One place where the insured, broker, insurer, coach and firm converge, with the state visible to all. Most of the delay is coordination between parties who cannot see each other's progress.

**Measure and report activation time.** From notification to work commencing. Firms and insurers that track it will improve it, and it would be a far more informative procurement metric than anything currently compared.

**Extend the retainer to include activation readiness.** A retainer that pre-agrees authorisation, conflicts, terms and initial scope delivers what clients believe they are already buying.

## Target Customer

Cyber insurers and their panel managers, who coordinate most of this work and whose claim costs fall directly with faster containment — they are the party with the leverage and the interest.

Forensics firms with panel and retainer practices, for whom faster activation is a genuine differentiator and frees the first hours for the work.

Brokers, who sit in the coordination path and whose clients experience the delay most acutely.

## Impact If Built

Hours recovered at the point in an engagement where an hour is worth the most — attacker access continues, evidence expires and the statutory clock runs during every one of them.

Continuous conflict pre-checking against the panel population converts the slowest administrative step into a lookup, using work done calmly between incidents.

And a standard first-phase scope that begins immediately would decouple starting the work from negotiating its size, which is the single structural reason activation waits.

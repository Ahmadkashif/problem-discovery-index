# Engagement & Retainer Operations

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/digital-forensics-firms|Digital Forensics Firms]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Whether the commercial and administrative shell around an activation runs itself, or consumes hours of the first day when hours matter most.

## Profile

**Market Size:** ~$150M
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~3%
**Digital Adoption:** Moderate — panels and retainers exist
**Target Buyer:** Firm operations, insurer panel managers
**Automation Potential:** Very high — it is repeatable process

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche

Around every engagement is a commercial shell: the insurer panel arrangement or retainer under which the firm is engaged, the scoping and authorisation, the engagement letter and privilege arrangements, conflict checking, rate agreement, the approval to exceed an initial cap, and eventually billing against a claim.

Most of it happens in the first hours, when the clock is running and the client is in crisis. A retainer or panel arrangement shortens it considerably, which is most of what retainers actually buy. Where no arrangement exists — which is common, since many organisations have their first incident without one — it happens from scratch under the worst conditions.

The contest is elapsed time. Hours spent on authorisation and conflict checking are hours the attacker still has access and evidence continues to expire. Every firm knows this and most improvement has gone into the panel relationship rather than into the process itself.

It is the smallest niche in the industry and sits alongside [[niches/digital-forensics-firms/reporting-production/profile|⚡ Reporting & Deliverable Production]] as the mechanical pair.

## Current Tools & Gaps

Insurer panel arrangements with pre-agreed rates and terms. Retainer agreements with pre-negotiated engagement letters. Professional services automation for time and billing. Conflict checking systems, usually manual for anything non-obvious. Claims portals on the insurer side.

The gaps are in elapsed time and in the joins between parties. Authorisation involves the insured, the broker, the insurer and the breach coach, each with their own process, coordinated by email at speed. Conflict checking is manual and slow for a firm with a large client base. Scope and cap approval is renegotiated as the incident grows, repeatedly, mid-engagement. Billing against a claim has its own substantiation requirements that are assembled afterwards from time records never kept with that purpose in mind. And retainers rarely pre-position anything beyond rates, which is the readiness gap described in [[niches/digital-forensics-firms/incident-response/profile|🔵 Incident Response Engagements]].

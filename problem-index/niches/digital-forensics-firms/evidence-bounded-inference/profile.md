# Evidence-Bounded Inference

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/digital-forensics-firms|Digital Forensics Firms]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Whether, mid-incident, a firm can state what the surviving evidence supports as a calibrated bound rather than as a narrative under a clock.

## Profile

**Market Size:** ~$600M
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~12%
**Digital Adoption:** Very low — held entirely in the practitioner's head
**Target Buyer:** General counsel, breach coaches, insurers, response leads
**Automation Potential:** High — the reasoning is structured and largely repeatable

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche

This is the inference problem during the incident. The evidence is whatever survived. The clock is statutory. The client's counsel needs a scope they can act on. And the responder must reason from an incomplete evidence set to a defensible statement about what happened.

The reasoning is more repeatable than the field's self-image allows. Which evidence sources answer which questions, what a given source's absence means, what can be inferred from credential scope and observed activity, what a timeline gap permits — these are structured inferences that experienced responders make consistently and hold entirely tacitly.

That is what separates it from its sibling. [[niches/digital-forensics-firms/evidence-readiness/profile|🎯 Pre-Incident Evidence Readiness]] is sold to a CISO with a budget cycle, months before anything happens, and returns nothing unless an incident occurs. Inference is sold into an engagement already underway, to a client already paying, improving a deliverable already due — which is why a firm would build it first and why it does not require anyone to buy anything new.

The contest is over whether the bounds can be calibrated. Stating a bound is an improvement on narrative; stating a bound whose reliability is known is a different product, and it requires outcomes that mostly never arrive.

## Current Tools & Gaps

Forensic examination suites, log analysis platforms, timeline tools and the practitioner's experience. Playbooks for common incident types. Internal peer review of significant conclusions. Data discovery to establish what an affected store contained.

The gaps are the whole niche. Nothing represents the relationship between evidence sources and answerable questions, so the map of what this engagement can establish is reconstructed by the responder each time. Bounds are stated in prose rather than computed. Nothing tracks how often a stated conclusion later proved wrong, so no bound is calibrated. Data-at-risk defaults to inventory rather than to what the observed access could reach. And the reasoning is tacit, so it neither transfers to junior responders nor survives a practitioner's departure.

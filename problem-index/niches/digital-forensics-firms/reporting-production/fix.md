# Fix: Written Tired, During the Next Incident

**Niche:** Reporting & Deliverable Production
**Industry:** [[industries/digital-forensics-firms|Digital Forensics Firms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The report that a notification decision and possibly a court case will rest on is written in the evenings by someone who is already three days into the next engagement.
**Tags:** #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #worker-facing #workflow-orchestration #compliance #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Whether the report is assembled from the investigation's own record, or written from memory during the next engagement.

## The Problem

The engagement ends. The responder is released and is on the next activation within days, sometimes within hours. The report is not written.

So it gets written in the gaps. Evenings. Weekends. Early mornings before the current incident's first bridge call. Weeks after the findings were established, by someone whose attention is on a different organisation's crisis.

The consequences are predictable and serious. The report is late, which delays a client's decisions and occasionally their notification. It is written from notes rather than from a fresh understanding, which loses detail. It is written tired, which is where inconsistencies and overstatements enter. And it is the artefact that may be tested in litigation years later, produced under the worst conditions any deliverable in this industry faces.

The cause is structural. Report writing is not scheduled because it is not billable in the way engagement days are, and the utilisation model treats a released responder as available. So the work happens outside the schedule, which means it happens outside working hours, which means it happens badly.

## Why It's Still Broken

**Utilisation treats writing time as idle time.** A responder scheduled for report writing shows as non-billable, which the utilisation metric penalises — the same structural problem as in [[industries/penetration-testing-firms|Penetration Testing Firms]] and for the same reason.

**Incident arrival is unpredictable.** A firm cannot promise a responder three quiet days after an engagement when the next activation may come tomorrow, which makes protected writing time genuinely hard rather than merely unprioritised.

**The client wants the report and has already moved on.** Pressure for the report is real and diffuse, so it does not force a schedule.

**Nobody measures the writing time.** It happens outside the schedule and is not recorded anywhere, so its cost is invisible.

**Practitioners absorb it.** Evening report writing is treated as part of the job, generates no escalation, and shows up instead as attrition.

**Delegation is hard.** A report can only be written by someone who understands the investigation, and the model of the investigation lives in the lead's head — so the writing cannot be handed over any more than the investigation could.

## What a Fix Looks Like

**Schedule the writing as engagement work.** Booked days after the engagement, protected the way engagement days are protected. This is the fix and it is blocked by a metric rather than by economics, since the work is already being done unpaid.

**Write during the engagement, not after.** Findings recorded as they are established, so the report is assembled from a record rather than reconstructed. This also improves the findings, because they are written while the understanding is fresh.

**Change the metric to delivery completion.** Engagements delivered complete and on time, including the report, rather than utilisation. This removes the structural pressure that erodes every attempt to protect writing time.

**Reduce the writing burden directly.** Structured findings, evidence binding and multi-audience rendering cut the work substantially, and the reduction helps regardless of whether the scheduling changes.

**Field a writer.** For larger engagements, a dedicated report writer working alongside the team from the start, capturing findings as they emerge. This is common in other investigative disciplines and rare here.

**Measure the hours once.** A quarter of honest recording of report-writing time, with an assurance it will not be used against anyone, produces the number that makes the case for everything above.

**Price the engagement to include it.** The report is part of the deliverable and should be inside the price rather than outside the schedule, which requires firms to quote a complete engagement rather than a count of response days.

## Who Feels the Pain

The responder, writing in the evenings during someone else's crisis, on work that will be read carefully by lawyers and possibly tested in court.

The client, receiving a late report that delays their decisions, containing findings described from a fortnight-old memory.

The firm, whose most exposed artefact is produced under its worst conditions, and which loses senior practitioners partly over this.

And the investigation itself, whose careful work is communicated by an exhausted person weeks later.

## Impact If Fixed

Scheduling the writing as engagement work is the whole fix and is prevented by a metric, not by cost — the work is already happening and is currently unpaid and unscheduled.

Recording findings during the engagement improves both the report and the investigation, because a finding written while the evidence is fresh is a better finding.

And measuring the hours once would establish what is almost certainly a large, invisible cost, which is the number that makes every other change in this section fundable.

# Reporting & Deliverable Production

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/digital-forensics-firms|Digital Forensics Firms]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Whether the report is assembled from the investigation's own record, or written from memory in the evenings during the next engagement.

## Profile

**Market Size:** ~$300M
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~6%
**Digital Adoption:** Low — a template and a week
**Target Buyer:** Firm operations, practice leadership, quality reviewers
**Automation Potential:** Very high — assembly, evidence binding and consistency are mechanical

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche

Every engagement ends in a report, and frequently several: a technical report, an executive summary, a version for the insurer, a version for counsel, and sometimes an expert report if litigation follows.

Producing them consumes days at the end of an engagement, from practitioners who are exhausted and frequently already mobilised on the next incident. The work is largely assembly — describing findings established weeks earlier, placing evidence exhibits, rebuilding the timeline into a narrative, cross-checking that the executive summary matches the technical body, and reformatting for each audience.

The contest is whether any of it is derived from the investigation record. Almost none is. The timeline was assembled in a tool and is retyped as narrative. The evidence exhibits are copied by hand. The inferences were made weeks ago and are reconstructed from notes. And because the reports are written late and tired, this is also where the errors are — the inconsistent figure, the finding that overstates what the evidence section supports, the caveat that appears in one document and not the others.

## Current Tools & Gaps

Report templates per firm and per audience. Timeline and forensic tooling whose output is exported and manually incorporated. Peer and quality review. Evidence management with exhibit numbering. Some firms have reporting platforms; most have a document.

The gaps are the standard ones for this kind of work. Findings are prose rather than structured objects, so nothing binds a finding to the evidence supporting it. The timeline is rebuilt by hand from a tool that already holds it. Multiple audience versions are produced by copying and diverge under later edits. Consistency between summary and body is checked by reading. And the reasoning recorded during the investigation, where it was recorded at all, is not carried into the report — so the basis for a finding must be reconstructed if it is challenged later.

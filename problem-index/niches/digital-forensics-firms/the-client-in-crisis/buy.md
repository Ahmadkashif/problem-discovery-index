# Buy: Crisis Decision Support From Other Emergencies

**Niche:** The Client During the Incident
**Industry:** [[industries/digital-forensics-firms|Digital Forensics Firms]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Product recall, aviation accident and public health emergencies all give decision-makers structured frameworks and rehearsed roles, and breach decisions are made from a bridge call.
**Tags:** #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #compliance #workflow-orchestration #worker-facing #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Whether the person making the notification decision receives what they need to make it, or a technical narrative they must interpret.

## The Problem

Deciding under time pressure, with incomplete information, about disclosure to the public and to regulators, is not unique to breaches. Several domains face it regularly and have built the apparatus.

Product recall has defined decision criteria, regulatory notification pathways, template communications, and a body of practice about scope and timing. Aviation accident response has pre-designated roles, a communication protocol and a defined relationship with investigators and regulators. Public health emergency response has decision frameworks for when to communicate uncertain risk, extensive practice in communicating what is not yet known, and rehearsed exercises involving the actual decision-makers.

Each has the same structure: a technical investigation producing incomplete findings, a decision-maker who must act before the findings are complete, regulatory obligations with deadlines, and a public communication with reputational consequences.

Breach response has assembled a professional service — the breach coach — around the same problem and has not built the apparatus.

## What Already Exists

Product recall: regulatory notification frameworks, decision criteria for scope and urgency, template communications, and established practice on staged recalls as information improves.

Aviation: the go-team model, pre-designated organisational roles, and a communication protocol governing what may be said and when during an ongoing investigation.

Public health: risk communication frameworks with explicit guidance on communicating uncertainty, and exercise programmes that rehearse the actual decision-makers rather than the technical teams.

Crisis management: the general discipline, with incident command, communication planning and stakeholder mapping.

Breach-specific: incident response plans focused on the technical response, breach coach counsel, and crisis communications firms.

## The Customization Gap

**Communicating uncertainty is the transferable skill and is unused.** Public health has developed guidance on saying what is not yet known without appearing evasive or alarming. Breach communication oscillates between saying nothing and overstating certainty, and this is exactly the gap that body of practice addresses.

**Staged disclosure has a direct precedent.** Product recall practice includes expanding scope as information improves, with established communication patterns for doing so. Breach notification treats the decision as single-shot, which drives either over-notification or delay.

**The exercises rehearse the wrong people.** Incident response exercises are technical. Public health and recall exercises rehearse the executives who will make the decisions, which is who needs it.

**Roles are not pre-designated.** Aviation and recall response have defined roles assigned in advance. Breach response assembles a decision group during the crisis and negotiates who decides what.

**Regulatory relationship is unmanaged.** Aviation has an established relationship with the investigating authority. Breach response frequently involves first contact with a regulator during the incident, which is a poor moment to establish it.

**Decision criteria are held as judgement.** Recall has criteria for when to act. Breach notification has thresholds in law and a great deal of judgement about how to apply them, held by breach coaches and never written into a framework the client can use.

## Target Customer

Breach coach law firms, who could package their tacit decision practice into a framework and serve more clients better — the same leverage argument that applies throughout this industry.

Cyber insurers, who could provide crisis decision preparation to insureds as a loss-mitigation service, which is directly in their interest.

Crisis management consultancies with recall or public health experience, who have the frameworks and lack the cyber specificity, and for whom this is an adjacent market.

## Impact If Solved

Mature crisis decision practice exists in several adjacent domains and has not been carried into breach response, which has assembled a service around the problem instead.

Risk communication practice for uncertainty is directly applicable to the field's hardest communication problem — saying what is not yet known about scope without either alarming or misleading.

And rehearsing the executives rather than the technical team would prepare the people who actually make the decisions, which is the group currently least prepared and most consequential.

# Incident Response Engagements

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/digital-forensics-firms|Digital Forensics Firms]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Whether the first week of an engagement is spent investigating or spent obtaining access, building an inventory and standing up collection.

## Profile

**Market Size:** ~$1.50B
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~30%
**Digital Adoption:** Moderate — good tooling on a manual process
**Target Buyer:** Cyber insurers, breach coach law firms, general counsel, CISOs
**Automation Potential:** High for mobilisation, low for the investigation

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche

This is the commercial core: a firm is called, mobilises, investigates, contains, and delivers a report, usually under an insurer panel arrangement or a retainer, with a breach coach directing.

The contest is mobilisation speed and it is the thing every competitor is fighting over. An engagement's first days are the most valuable and the most wasted. The responder arrives to an organisation in crisis, needs access to systems nobody can grant quickly, needs an inventory of an estate nobody has, needs collection agents deployed across endpoints that may be compromised, and needs log exports from platforms whose administrators are also handling the incident.

A week can go to this. It is a week of the statutory clock, a week of the attacker's remaining access, and a week of the most expensive people in the firm doing logistics. Every firm knows it and the standard response is more experienced people and longer hours, which is the pattern described in [[niches/digital-forensics-firms/the-responder/profile|🟣 The Responder]].

## Current Tools & Gaps

Remote collection agents deployed at engagement start. Forensic acquisition tooling. Retainer arrangements with pre-agreed terms and sometimes pre-deployed tooling. Insurer panel relationships that shorten the commercial step. Crisis management and bridge call structures.

The gaps concentrate in the first days. Access provisioning is negotiated per engagement with a client who has never done it, under crisis conditions, and is the largest single delay. Asset inventory is frequently absent or wrong, so collection targets are discovered rather than known. Collection tooling deployment into a compromised environment raises real questions nobody has pre-answered. Log export from cloud and SaaS platforms requires administrative access held by people who are busy. And pre-incident preparation, which would resolve most of it, is sold as an assessment rather than as a standing capability.

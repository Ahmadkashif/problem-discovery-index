# The Quote Lost to a First Article Test Nobody Priced

**Niche:** [[niches/aerospace-parts-suppliers/small-shops-government-spares/profile|Small Shops Bidding Government Spare Parts]]
**Industry:** [[industries/aerospace-parts-suppliers|Aerospace Parts Suppliers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A small shop wins a DLA award, then discovers the solicitation required a first article test it never priced or scheduled, and the job turns into a loss or a default.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #compliance #quick-win #automation #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to put a small AS9100 shop's quotes on the government solicitations it can actually win, and whoever lifts the shop's awards per quote submitted takes the account.

## The Problem
Solicitations bury requirements in referenced clauses and notes: first article testing with government approval before production, specialty-metals and domestic-melt rules, packaging and marking specs, and inspection at source. A shop that quotes the part and misses one of these finds out after award.

## Why It's Still Broken
The clauses are referenced rather than written out, the owner quotes in a hurry, and nobody keeps a checklist per solicitation type.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Before any quote is submitted, extract every referenced clause and requirement into a checklist with cost and schedule impact (first article test time and cost, testing lab, packaging), and require a tick or a price line for each.

## Who Feels the Pain
Owners who absorb the loss; DLA, which receives late or non-conforming parts; the shop's past-performance record, which affects future awards.

## Impact If Fixed
A pre-submission checklist costs minutes and avoids the occasional award that loses ~$10,000–$50,000 (estimate) or damages the shop's past-performance rating.

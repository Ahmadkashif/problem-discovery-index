# Engine Components & Rotating Parts

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/aerospace-parts-suppliers|Aerospace Parts Suppliers]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn more of the industry's scarce castings and forgings into accepted engine parts, and whoever proves the lowest scrap and re-process rate at final inspection with no rise in escapes takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.2B a year in inspection, quality, traceability and process-analytics spend at US engine-parts makers, including the inspection labour such tools assist (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~57% of the two codes' 2022 receipts (336412's $43.6B of ~$77.0B, Census SUSB)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — digital radiography, MES and serial traceability at the large houses; penetrant inspection judged by eye and recorded on paper travellers
**Target Buyer:** VP Quality or plant general manager at engine-component makers
**Automation Potential:** Medium for inspection assistance and traceability; low for final disposition, which stays with certified inspectors

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Engine parts are where the money and the bottleneck coincide. Airfoils, disks, shafts, rings and structural castings in nickel superalloys and titanium are made by a few large houses (Howmet's Engine Products sold $4.3B in 2025 at a 33.3% EBITDA margin; Precision Castparts is its principal competitor) and a tier of smaller machining and finishing specialists. Rotating parts are life-limited and need back-to-birth traceability from heat lot to serial. Every one passes penetrant, and many radiographic or ultrasonic, inspection before shipment, so final inspection decides both the escape risk and how many castings and forgings become saleable parts.

## Current Tools & Gaps
MES and quality suites (Siemens Opcenter, Plex, SAP QM), digital radiography from the NDT equipment makers, and SPC tools cover process data. The gaps: FPI indications are never imaged; dispositions are part-level ticks; re-processes on suspicion are not counted; and no system joins the inspector's call to what happened to the part later. The foundry and forge process itself is left to the queued investment-casting and forging industries.

# The Spec Revision That Changed Last Month

**Niche:** [[niches/aerospace-parts-suppliers/flowdown-spec-revision-control/profile|Customer Flowdown & Specification Revision Control]]
**Industry:** [[industries/aerospace-parts-suppliers|Aerospace Parts Suppliers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A process specification moves to a new revision, the customer's next PO cites it, and the shop keeps working to the old one until an auditor or a rejected FAI notices.
**Tags:** #data-integration #compliance #quick-win #workflow-orchestration #automation #descriptive-statistics
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn each purchase order's quality clauses and specification revisions into checked requirements on the router before a job is released, and whoever catches the most clause and revision mismatches before parts are made takes the account.

## The Problem
Specifications for heat treatment, plating, penetrant inspection and materials are revised every few years, sometimes with real technical changes. Shops subscribe to the standards but don't compare new revisions with what open POs and their own procedures cite.

## Why It's Still Broken
Nobody owns the comparison; the subscription service sends an email; the quality manager is busy.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A weekly list of every specification cited on open POs and routings with the revision cited, the current published revision and the revision the shop's procedure follows, with mismatches assigned to an owner and a due date.

## Who Feels the Pain
Quality managers facing audit findings; outside processors asked to work to a revision they don't hold; customers receiving parts made to superseded specs.

## Impact If Fixed
A weekly revision check costs an hour and removes a recurring source of findings and rework, worth ~1–2 avoided SCARs a year at a mid-size supplier (estimate).

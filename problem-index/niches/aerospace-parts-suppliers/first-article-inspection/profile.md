# First Article Inspection & AS9102 Packages

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/aerospace-parts-suppliers|Aerospace Parts Suppliers]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is: every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get an AS9102 package accepted by the customer's quality representative the first time. Two contests sit inside it, with different inputs, vendors and winners: extracting every requirement from the drawing or model onto Form 3, and proving every material and special process on Form 2 with genuine, conforming certificates from approved sources. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$400M a year in first article labour and software across US aerospace suppliers (estimate: ~5,000 AS9100 sites × ~15 full or delta FAIs a month × ~8 hours × ~$60 an hour loaded, plus software)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~0.5% of the two codes' receipts, spread across a supply base much wider than the two codes (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — ballooning by hand or in a PDF tool at most shops; forms in Excel; certificates in folders
**Target Buyer:** Quality manager
**Automation Potential:** High for assembly and checking; the signature stays human

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
AS9102 is the industry's universal gate: no first production part ships without a first article report, and every design change, source change or two-year lapse (recalled) brings a full or partial one. Revision C (released 23 June 2023, per vendor summaries of the standard) made the reason for a full or partial FAI mandatory on every report. The report is short in principle and long in practice, and customer quality representatives reject it for one missed characteristic or one certificate that doesn't trace. GroundControl's 70+ facilities generating almost 1,000 reports a month show both the volume and how recently software arrived.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Net-Inspect, DISCUS, High QA, InspectionXpert, 1factory and GroundControl generate forms and balloon vector drawings. The CMM-to-Form-3 transcription step is held in [[niches/contract-manufacturing/aerospace-precision-machining/profile|Aerospace Precision Machining]]. The gaps divide cleanly: requirement extraction on scanned and model-based drawings, and certificate verification against specs and approved sources. Each sub-niche below takes one.

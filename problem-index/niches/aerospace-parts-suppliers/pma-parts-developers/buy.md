# PLM Adapted to Equivalence, Not Design

**Niche:** [[niches/aerospace-parts-suppliers/pma-parts-developers/profile|PMA Parts Developers & Reverse-Engineering Teams]]
**Industry:** [[industries/aerospace-parts-suppliers|Aerospace Parts Suppliers]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** PLM manages a part's design history; a PMA project needs to manage the evidence that a copy equals an original.
**Tags:** #data-integration #feature-engineering #compliance #workflow-orchestration #automation #large-language-models
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get a high-demand part through FAA Parts Manufacturer Approval before the OEM's aftermarket price moves, and whoever turns a teardown into an accepted test-and-computation package in the fewest months takes the account.

## The Problem
PMA teams run PLM and document control because their quality systems require it, then manage the actual approval work (samples, measurements, tests, analyses, reviewer correspondence) in folders and spreadsheets beside it.

## What Already Exists
PLM suites (Siemens Teamcenter, PTC Windchill, Arena) and document control in QMS tools manage revisions, approvals and change control.

## The Customization Gap
An equivalence data model: OEM sample lots and their measurements as first-class objects, characteristic-by-characteristic comparison between OEM and PMA design, test and analysis evidence linked to each claim, and a review-item tracker for FAA or designee questions. Export control flags travel with every record, and the production inspection plan is generated from the critical characteristics.

## Target Customer
PMA makers already running a PLM who want approval projects in the same system.

## Impact If Solved
Keeping evidence and design in one place cuts package assembly and review-response time by ~20% (estimate) and preserves the reasoning when engineers move on.

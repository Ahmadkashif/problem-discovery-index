# A Substantiation Workbench for Teardown to Approval

**Niche:** [[niches/aerospace-parts-suppliers/pma-parts-developers/profile|PMA Parts Developers & Reverse-Engineering Teams]]
**Industry:** [[industries/aerospace-parts-suppliers|Aerospace Parts Suppliers]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** PMA engineers rebuild the same kinds of equivalence arguments for every part, and nothing captures the last hundred packages to start the next one.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #k-nearest-neighbors #feature-engineering #compliance #workflow-orchestration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get a high-demand part through FAA Parts Manufacturer Approval before the OEM's aftermarket price moves, and whoever turns a teardown into an accepted test-and-computation package in the fewest months takes the account.

## The Problem
A PMA package for a bearing, seal, blade or bracket repeats a pattern: dimensional and material characterisation of OEM samples, a design that matches, tests and analyses showing equivalence, a list of critical characteristics, and a production inspection plan. Engineers start from a blank document or a colleague's old one, and the FAA reviewer's past questions on similar parts live in email.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The market is a few hundred engineering teams; packages are confidential and sometimes export-controlled; and PLM vendors sell to design organisations, not to approval projects.

## What to Build
A workbench that structures each package (part, OEM samples, measurements, materials, tests, analyses, critical characteristics, reviewer questions and answers), retrieves similar past packages and the arguments that were accepted, drafts sections from the data, and tracks open review items. Measurement data flows in from CMM and CT, and test reports are linked rather than pasted.

## Target Customer
VP Engineering and PMA programme managers at independent replacement-part makers.

## Impact If Built
Cutting package preparation by a few months on a 12–24 month programme (estimate) brings revenue forward and lets the same engineering team approve ~20–30% more parts a year (estimate).

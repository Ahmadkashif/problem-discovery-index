# Aerostructures & Airframe Detail Parts

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/aerospace-parts-suppliers|Aerospace Parts Suppliers]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to show a prime that a supplier can hold the next rate break, and whoever builds a capacity plan from the shop's own routings and machine data that survives the prime's rate-readiness review takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$900M a year in planning, scheduling, capacity and supplier-management software and services at US airframe parts suppliers, including the planners' labour such tools assist (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~43% of the two codes' 2022 receipts (336413's $33.5B of ~$77.0B, Census SUSB), plus detail-part shops coded elsewhere
**Digital Adoption:** Medium-low — ERP scheduling at most shops, capacity plans in spreadsheets
**Target Buyer:** VP Operations or programme manager at tier-1 to tier-3 structures suppliers
**Automation Potential:** Medium-high for capacity modelling and scenario planning

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Airframe structures (fuselage sections, wing components, machined frames and fittings, sheet-metal and composite details, landing-gear and actuation parts) are paced by the airframer's rate. Boeing's 737 programme began moving from 42 to 47 a month in the first half of 2026 with the FAA's concurrence and is planning further increases on a new line (Boeing's Q2 2026 10-Q). Boeing completed its acquisition of Spirit AeroSystems on 8 December 2025 (10-Q), and Airbus took over Spirit's Airbus work packages at the same time (search summary), which reshuffled the tier-2 base. Before each rate break, primes assess whether suppliers can hold it. A shop that cannot show credible capacity loses share to one that can.

## Current Tools & Gaps
ERPs (Epicor Kinetic, Plex, ProShop, JobBOSS², SAP), finite schedulers and APS tools, and the primes' supplier portals. The gaps: capacity plans built by hand in spreadsheets from standard hours nobody trusts; no link between the prime's forecast and machine-level load; outside processing treated as fixed lead time. The lot-level expediting problem is its own niche.

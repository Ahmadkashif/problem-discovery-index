# The Rate-Readiness Spreadsheet Nobody Believes

**Niche:** [[niches/aerospace-parts-suppliers/aerostructures-detail-parts/profile|Aerostructures & Airframe Detail Parts]]
**Industry:** [[industries/aerospace-parts-suppliers|Aerospace Parts Suppliers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Before each rate break the supplier fills in the prime's capacity template from standard hours, the prime discounts it, and both sides find out the truth in delinquencies three months later.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #linear-regression #data-integration #quick-win #workflow-orchestration #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to show a prime that a supplier can hold the next rate break, and whoever builds a capacity plan from the shop's own routings and machine data that survives the prime's rate-readiness review takes the account.

## The Problem
The template asks for capacity by work centre at the new rate. The planner fills it from ERP standard hours, rounds optimistically because the business wants the work, and sends it. Nobody checks the standard hours against what the machines actually did last quarter.

## Why It's Still Broken
Machine-monitoring data, where it exists, is not joined to the ERP routing; planners have no time; and an honest "we can't" feels like losing the programme.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Before filling the template, compare standard hours with actual hours for the top 50 part numbers by load, using machine monitoring or job clock-ins. Correct the template with actuals, add inspection and FAI load, and send the prime the evidence alongside the numbers.

## Who Feels the Pain
Planners who are blamed for misses; buyers at the prime who must decide whether to dual-source; the floor, which works the weekends that follow.

## Impact If Fixed
An evidence-based submission takes ~2–3 days once (estimate), and either wins trust or surfaces a constraint early enough to fix before the rate break.

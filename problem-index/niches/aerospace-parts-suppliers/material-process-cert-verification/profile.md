# Material & Special-Process Certification Verification

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/aerospace-parts-suppliers|Aerospace Parts Suppliers]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this sub-niche is fighting to check every mill cert and special-process cert against the specification, the purchase order and the approved-processor list before material is cut, and whoever catches a non-conforming or falsified cert earliest in the chain takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$150M a year in receiving-inspection and certificate-review labour and tools at US aerospace suppliers (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~0.2% of the two codes' receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — certificates arrive as PDFs and paper, are read by eye and filed by job
**Target Buyer:** Quality manager or receiving-inspection lead
**Automation Potential:** High for extraction and cross-checks; authenticity questions go to people

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Form 2 of an AS9102 report, and every lot after it, rests on certificates: mill test reports for titanium, aluminium and nickel alloys, and certs from heat treaters, platers and NDT houses that must hold Nadcap accreditation and sit on the customer's approved list. In 2024 Spirit AeroSystems found titanium in Boeing and Airbus structures that had arrived with falsified records through a distributor, traced by AP to a Chinese source from about 2019 (search summary of AP and FAA statements). The parts tested as the right alloy, but the paper was not genuine. The contest here is document truth, not drawing reading: different inputs (certs, specs, approved lists), different vendors, and a different failure.

## Current Tools & Gaps
ERP receiving modules store cert PDFs; the primes' approved-process-source lists and PRI's eAuditNet show approvals; counterfeit programmes follow AS6174 and, for electronic parts only, DFARS 252.246-7008. The gaps: no extraction of cert values against spec limits, no check of processor approval at the cert's date, and no cross-supplier signal that a mill's cert format has changed.

# Product Catalogue Enrichment

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/retail-pos-platforms|Retail POS Platforms]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in retail catalogue is fighting to populate a specialty item's attributes without the merchant typing them — and whoever holds attribute completeness highest at zero merchant effort takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$310M US spend on retail product data, catalogue services and enrichment
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~3% of retail POS revenue, gating a much larger share of its value
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — UPC lookup is solved for packaged goods and irrelevant for specialty
**Target Buyer:** Catalogue and content leaders at POS vendors; merchants, who bear the cost in hours
**Automation Potential:** Very High — the same products are entered by thousands of merchants independently

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Catalogue quality gates almost everything else in retail software. Without attributes there is no meaningful category reporting, no size or colour curve analysis, no online listing, no comparable search, and no cross-merchant learning of the kind the merchandising niche depends on. For packaged goods with a UPC the problem is largely solved by lookup services. For the specialty categories where independents actually compete — apparel, footwear, outdoor, hardware, gift, home — the attributes that matter are not in any UPC database: fabric, fit, colourway, dimensions, material, compatibility, season, collection. Every merchant who buys a vendor's line types those attributes for the same products that the same vendor sold to two thousand other stores, and each of them types them differently. The waste is enormous, the inconsistency makes cross-merchant analysis impossible, and the merchant experiences it as the most tedious part of running the business.

## Current Tools & Gaps
UPC and barcode lookup services are mature and free or cheap. Some POS vendors maintain their own catalogues built from merchant entries. Vendor catalogues exist as spreadsheets and PDFs emailed to retailers, and wholesale platforms carry structured product data for the brands on them. Image-based product identification is available. The gaps: no shared catalogue for specialty goods spanning merchants, so the same item is entered thousands of times; vendor-supplied data arrives in inconsistent formats and is retyped rather than imported; attribute taxonomies differ per merchant, which is what prevents any cross-merchant analysis; and nothing extracts attributes from the photographs and descriptions the merchant already has.

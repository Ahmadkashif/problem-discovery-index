# Specialty Retail Merchandising

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/retail-pos-platforms|Retail POS Platforms]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in specialty retail software is fighting to tell an independent retailer what to mark down, when, and by how much — and whoever moves end-of-season sell-through and margin most takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$2.8B US specialty and independent retail operations software
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~28% of retail POS revenue
**Digital Adoption:** High for transactions, near zero for planning
**Target Buyer:** Owners and buyers at apparel, footwear, outdoor, home, gift and other specialty independents
**Automation Potential:** Very High — markdown and open-to-buy are well-posed planning problems on data the platform already holds

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Specialty retail lives and dies on two decisions and gets help with neither. The first is what to buy, months before a season, against a budget — the open-to-buy calculation, which is arithmetic over planned sales, planned markdowns, planned receipts and target inventory, and which the overwhelming majority of independents run on a fragile spreadsheet rebuilt every season by the owner. The second is when to mark down and by how much, which is where a season's margin is made or lost. Mark down too early and the margin is given away on units that would have sold; too late and the goods clear at a fraction or carry into the next season as dead stock. The decision is currently made by looking at the rack and feeling nervous. Enterprise retailers have had markdown optimisation for two decades, and the reason independents do not is that the capability required a planning analyst rather than an algorithm nobody had.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Lightspeed serves specialty verticals with the deepest inventory and vendor management in the small-merchant market; Shopify and Square increasingly reach the same merchants with payments-led offerings; retail ERPs serve the larger independents. Reporting is universal and descriptive: sales by item, by category, by period, with an inventory valuation. Markdown optimisation and demand planning exist as a mature enterprise category — the methods are published, the vendors are established — and have never been packaged for anyone below it. Open-to-buy appears as a report in some products and as a spreadsheet template in others, maintained manually. Nothing forecasts item-level demand, nothing recommends a markdown, and nothing learns sell-through curves across merchants, which is the one thing a platform can do that no individual retailer can.

# The Consignor Ledger Nobody Can Query

**Niche:** [[niches/retail-pos-platforms/consignment-resale-retail/profile|Consignment & Resale Retail]]
**Industry:** [[industries/retail-pos-platforms|Retail POS Platforms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A consignment store holds other people's property and owes them money when it sells, and the consignor's view of their own account is a phone call to the store during opening hours.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #compliance #workflow-orchestration #automation #worker-facing #quick-win #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in resale software is fighting to get a one-of-a-kind item identified, priced and listed in under a minute — and whoever lowers cost-per-item-intake most takes the account.

## The Problem
A consignor brings in twelve items. Over the following ninety days some sell, some are marked down, some expire back to them, and some cannot be found. To learn any of this they telephone the store, and a staff member looks it up between customers. Payouts are issued on a schedule the consignor half-remembers. Disputes about whether an item was received, what it sold for, or whether the expiry was applied correctly are common, unpleasant, and settled from the store's own records against the consignor's memory. The store's relationship with its consignors is its supply chain, and it is managed with the worst information flow of any supplier relationship in retail.

## Why It's Still Broken
The consignment software vendors built the ledger for the store's accounting rather than for the consignor's visibility, which was the correct first priority and has not been extended. Building a consignor-facing view means a second application for a population the store does not directly bill, so the business case is indirect — it improves supply and reduces staff interruption rather than generating revenue. And stores have operated this way for decades, so the friction is normal rather than notable.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Give the consignor their own account view. Every item they brought in, with a photograph taken at intake, its current status, its price and any markdown applied, what sold and for how much, the split, the balance owed and the next payout date. Notifications on sale and on approaching expiry, which is the moment a consignor most wants to know because they can choose to collect the item or let it go to the store's disposition. Intake receipts with photographs, which removes the most common category of dispute entirely. Payouts visible and, where the store offers it, initiated from the same screen. None of this is difficult and all of it removes work from a staff member currently answering the telephone between customers — while making the store a materially better place to consign, which is the actual competitive question for supply in this format.

## Who Feels the Pain
Consignors with no visibility into their own property; store staff fielding status calls during trading hours; and owners whose supply depends on a relationship managed through the least convenient channel available.

## Impact If Fixed
Consignor supply is the constraint on a resale business, and visibility is the cheapest available improvement to that relationship. Photographic intake receipts eliminate the dispute category that most damages consignor trust, and the reduction in status calls returns staff time during exactly the hours when the store is busiest.

# Consignment & Resale Retail

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/retail-pos-platforms|Retail POS Platforms]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in resale software is fighting to get a one-of-a-kind item identified, priced and listed in under a minute — and whoever lowers cost-per-item-intake most takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$420M US software spend across consignment, vintage, thrift and resale retail
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~4% of retail POS revenue, in one of retail's fastest-growing formats
**Digital Adoption:** Low — the systems that exist handle consignor accounting and little else
**Target Buyer:** Owners of consignment and resale stores, thrift operations and vintage dealers
**Automation Potential:** Very High — item identification, pricing and listing are the entire labour cost and are entirely manual

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Resale breaks every assumption retail software makes. There is no catalogue, because each item is unique. There is no reorder, because supply arrives from consignors and donors rather than from vendors. There is no demand forecast for an item that exists once. Pricing is a judgement per item, made by a person who must recognise a brand, assess condition, know the current secondhand market for that category, and decide in under a minute because there are two hundred more items in the bin. On top of that sits the consignor relationship: a ledger of who owns what, what sold, what is owed, and what has expired back to the consignor — which is an accounting obligation no other retail format carries. The economics of the entire business are set by cost per item processed, and that cost is almost entirely human judgement that nothing assists.

## Current Tools & Gaps
ConsignPro, Ricochet, SimpleConsign and a handful of others serve the format with consignor accounting, basic inventory and point of sale, and they handle the ledger competently. Online resale marketplaces have built sophisticated identification and pricing for their own operations and do not sell it to stores. Generic POS products are used by some stores and fit badly. The gaps are the labour: item identification from a photograph, condition assessment, comparable-price lookup against current secondhand market data, and listing generation for the store's own online channels are all manual. A store processing two hundred items a day spends most of its payroll on that sequence, and nothing in the available software touches it.

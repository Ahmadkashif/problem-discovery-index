# Sell-Through Sharing the Retailer Actually Wants to Give

**Niche:** [[niches/retail-pos-platforms/wholesale-brand-sell-through/profile|Wholesale Brand Sell-Through]]
**Industry:** [[industries/retail-pos-platforms|Retail POS Platforms]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A brand's sell-through data sits in three hundred retailers' point-of-sale systems, sharing it costs the retailer nothing, and no mechanism exists that makes the retailer want to.
**Tags:** #data-integration #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #workflow-orchestration #compliance #automation #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor serving independent brands is fighting to tell a wholesaler what actually sold at retail, store by store, in time to act on it — and whoever delivers sell-through from independent retail takes the brand.

## The Problem
A brand ships to three hundred stores. Some sell out in six weeks and never reorder because nobody followed up; some sit on the order for a year and reorder anyway because the rep is good; some sell one colour and none of the others. The brand's view is a shipping ledger. It plans its next production run — a substantial cash commitment with a long lead time — on sell-in, which is a measure of how persuasive its sales effort was rather than of what consumers wanted. Meanwhile each retailer's POS knows precisely what sold and when, and would give it up readily if asked in a way that cost them nothing and returned something.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Data sharing between retailers and vendors has been framed as something powerful brands extract from retailers, which makes it adversarial by default and unavailable to small brands with no leverage. Nobody has designed it as a mutual arrangement. Technically it requires the retailer's POS to scope sharing to a specific vendor's items, which is a straightforward permissioning feature that no product has built because no customer has asked. And the wholesale platforms, who are the natural intermediary, have focused on the order because the order is where their revenue is.

## What to Build
Scoped, retailer-controlled sharing with a return. A retailer grants a brand visibility of that brand's own items only — units sold, dates, prices, remaining stock — through a permission in their POS that takes one click and can be revoked. In return the retailer receives what they currently lack: how their sell-through on that brand compares to comparable stores, which items are performing elsewhere that they do not carry, restock recommendations timed to their own velocity, and early access or terms that the brand can offer to data-sharing partners. That exchange is what makes it voluntary rather than extracted. The brand receives store-level sell-through across its distribution, which transforms production planning, assortment decisions and reorder timing. The permission model is the product: scoped to the brand's items, revocable, visible to the retailer at all times, and never aggregated in ways that would let a brand infer a retailer's total business.

## Target Customer
Independent consumer brands and wholesalers, the wholesale ordering platforms positioned to broker the relationship, and the POS vendors who hold the data and could build the permission.

## Impact If Built
Sell-through transforms a brand's two hardest decisions — what to produce and where to place it — from guesses based on sell-in into measurements. For retailers, comparative benchmarking against similar stores is information they cannot get anywhere and would value, which is what makes the exchange work. This is a structural gap in independent retail's information flow and the mechanism to close it is a permission, not a technology.

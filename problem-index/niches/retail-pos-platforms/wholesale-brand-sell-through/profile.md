# Wholesale Brand Sell-Through

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/retail-pos-platforms|Retail POS Platforms]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor serving independent brands is fighting to tell a wholesaler what actually sold at retail, store by store, in time to act on it — and whoever delivers sell-through from independent retail takes the brand.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$380M US software spend across independent brands and wholesalers selling into independent retail
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~4% of retail POS revenue, serving a constituency the category does not count as customers
**Digital Adoption:** Low — orders are digitised through wholesale platforms and everything after the order is invisible
**Target Buyer:** Founders and sales leaders at independent consumer brands; wholesale platform operators
**Automation Potential:** High — the data exists in the retailers' POS systems and the mechanism to share it does not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A brand selling through three hundred independent retailers knows what it shipped and nothing about what sold. Sell-in is visible; sell-through is not. That single gap shapes the whole business: the brand cannot tell which stores are actually moving product and which are sitting on it, cannot know which colours and sizes performed, cannot time a reorder conversation, and cannot distinguish a store that reordered because it sold out from one that reordered because a sales rep was persuasive. Large brands solve this with chain partners who share point-of-sale data as a condition of doing business. Independent brands selling into independent retail have no leverage to demand it and no mechanism to receive it, so they fly blind through the most important part of their own distribution — and they are the constituency the POS industry does not treat as a customer at all.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Wholesale platforms — Faire, NuOrder, Brandboom and their competitors — handle ordering, catalogues and payments well and have improved the transaction substantially. Retail data aggregators sell point-of-sale data in some categories, priced and packaged for large brands. Some POS platforms offer retailers the ability to share data with vendors, and it is rarely used because the retailer has no reason to and is not asked in a way that makes it easy. The gaps: no standard mechanism for a retailer to share sell-through with a specific brand for their own items only, no incentive structure that makes a retailer want to, and consequently no reorder timing, no assortment feedback, and no way for a good retail partner to be recognised as one.

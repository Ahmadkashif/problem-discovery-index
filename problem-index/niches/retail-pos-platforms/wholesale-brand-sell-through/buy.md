# Retail Data Sharing Practice From Chain Partnerships

**Niche:** [[niches/retail-pos-platforms/wholesale-brand-sell-through/profile|Wholesale Brand Sell-Through]]
**Industry:** [[industries/retail-pos-platforms|Retail POS Platforms]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Large retailers and large brands have exchanged point-of-sale data for thirty years with established formats, governance and collaborative planning practice, and none of that apparatus has ever been made available at independent scale.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #time-series-forecasting #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #data-integration #compliance #workflow-orchestration #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor serving independent brands is fighting to tell a wholesaler what actually sold at retail, store by store, in time to act on it — and whoever delivers sell-through from independent retail takes the brand.

## The Problem
Collaborative planning between retailers and suppliers is a mature discipline: shared forecasts, shared inventory positions, jointly managed replenishment, with standardised data formats and decades of practice. It exists between large chains and large suppliers, supported by teams on both sides. An independent brand and an independent retailer have the same relationship, the same mutual interest and none of the apparatus, so they coordinate by a sales rep visiting twice a year.

## What Already Exists
Retail data exchange standards and formats are published and stable. Collaborative planning, forecasting and replenishment methodology is well documented. Vendor-managed inventory practice is established. Retail data aggregation and syndication is a functioning industry. Data clean room and scoped-sharing technology is now commodity and addresses the governance concerns that historically made sharing difficult. Every building block exists; all of it is sized and priced for the enterprise tier.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to relationships with no teams on either side. It requires: (1) zero-configuration onboarding, since neither party has an integration resource and a sharing arrangement that requires a project will never begin; (2) analysis delivered rather than data delivered, because an independent brand has no analyst and a spreadsheet of transactions is not usable — the product must produce the three conclusions the brand needs rather than the data behind them; (3) replenishment recommendations expressed as a reorder conversation rather than as an automated vendor-managed inventory programme, since independents will not cede ordering control and should not be asked to; (4) governance proportionate to the relationship, meaning a scoped permission rather than a negotiated data agreement, since a contract requirement kills the arrangement at this scale; and (5) benchmarking back to the retailer built in from the start, because that is the retailer's return and without it the exchange is one-sided and will not sustain.

## Target Customer
Wholesale platforms brokering brand-retailer relationships, independent brands, and the POS vendors holding the retail data.

## Impact If Solved
Bringing thirty years of collaborative planning practice down to independent scale addresses a coordination failure that costs both sides — brands overproduce and misallocate, retailers stock out on winners and sit on losers. The adaptation is almost entirely about packaging and governance rather than method, which is why it is achievable and why nobody has bothered.

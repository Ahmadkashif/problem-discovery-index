# Chargeback & Dispute Operations

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/retail-pos-platforms|Retail POS Platforms]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in dispute operations is fighting to win a representment against the card network's own evidence rules before the deadline — and whoever holds win rate highest per dollar of effort takes the merchant.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.5B US attributable to chargeback management, representment and dispute services
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~15% of retail POS revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — dispute notices arrive digitally and the response is assembled by a person
**Target Buyer:** Dispute operations leaders at POS platforms and acquirers; merchants who bear the losses
**Automation Potential:** Very High — the rules are published, the evidence is in the platform, and the assembly is manual

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A chargeback is a structured contest with published rules. The card networks specify the reason codes, the evidence that rebuts each one, the format of a representment and the deadline by which it must be filed. The merchant holds the evidence — the transaction record, the delivery confirmation, the customer communication, the device fingerprint, the prior order history — and frequently loses anyway, because assembling the right evidence for the specific reason code within the window is work that a small merchant has no capacity for and that a platform does generically. Meanwhile a substantial share of disputes are not fraud at all but friendly fraud or customer confusion, which is rebuttable with evidence that exists and is not assembled. The contest is therefore about the discipline of evidence rather than about detection, which makes it an entirely different problem from the underwriting sub-niche it shares a parent with.

## Current Tools & Gaps
The card networks publish their dispute rules and operate the systems through which representments are filed. Chargeback management vendors — Chargebacks911, Midigator, Verifi and Ethoca's network services — offer alerting, representment and prevention, with real capability. Platforms offer merchant-facing dispute interfaces of varying quality. The gaps: representment evidence is assembled manually or from a generic template rather than from the specific evidence that rebuts the specific reason code; deadlines are tracked inconsistently and a missed deadline is an automatic loss; win rate is rarely reported back by reason code so nobody knows which disputes are worth fighting; and prevention — resolving a customer's issue before it becomes a dispute — is handled by alert networks that many merchants do not use because nobody explained the economics to them.

## Why These Niches

The gap between what this category could compute and what it displays is the widest in vertical SaaS. Enterprise retail has had markdown optimisation and demand planning for two decades; the capability was never a technical secret, it required planning analysts that no independent retailer can employ. The POS platforms now hold, across hundreds of thousands of merchants, exactly the transaction and inventory history those systems consume, and they ship a sales summary. That makes specialty merchandising — markdown timing and open-to-buy — the contested capability of the category's largest block.

Payments is the other large block and is the category's actual business model, and it **failed the filter as one niche**. Approving a merchant in minutes without taking losses is a risk modelling problem at onboarding, against adversaries and with a severe cost asymmetry. Winning a chargeback is an evidence assembly and representment problem against card network rules and deadlines. Different teams, different data, different adversaries. Decomposed below.

Independent grocery and consignment are the two underdigitised retail models, each for a specific structural reason: grocery runs on direct store delivery invoices that nobody reconciles, and consignment sells one-of-a-kind items that no catalogue, no forecast and no reorder logic can describe. Store associates and wholesale brands are the two underserved constituencies — the associate is the person the customer actually asks and is given the least information, and the brand selling into independent retail cannot see whether its product sold.

The automation niches are the category's two permanent data burdens: catalogue attributes for specialty goods that no UPC database carries, and the gap between what the system says is in stock and what is on the shelf.

## Filter Notes

**Niche 2 failed the filter as stated and was decomposed.** "Payments and merchant services" is the category's revenue engine and contains two separate contests. Merchant underwriting is an adversarial risk problem at the moment of onboarding, where the competitive pressure is to approve in minutes and the cost of being wrong is a loss the platform absorbs — every competitor is fighting to approve faster at the same loss rate. Chargeback operations is an evidence assembly problem against network rules and deadlines, where the competitive pressure is win rate on representment and the adversary is a process rather than a fraudster. Both sub-niches are terminal.

**Niches 1, 3–8 are terminal as stated.** Each names something a merchant can test: sell-through at end of season against the markdown recommendation, direct-store-delivery invoice variance caught, time to list a unique item, whether an associate can answer a stock question from the floor, whether a brand can see its own sell-through, catalogue attribute completeness without merchant typing, and the gap between system stock and counted stock.

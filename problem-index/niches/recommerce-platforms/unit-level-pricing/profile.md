# Unit-Level Pricing

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/recommerce-platforms|Recommerce Platforms]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to price a one-of-a-kind unit correctly in seconds at a cost the item can bear — and whoever does that takes the economics, because the price decision determines whether a processed item makes money and it is made hundreds of times a shift.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$3.8B US attributable to the pricing decision
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~24% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Low — manual or crudely automated
**Target Buyer:** Every managed platform, and the resale-as-a-service providers
**Automation Potential:** Very High — the training data is the platform's own sales

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Every item is a single unit with its own condition, and pricing it correctly determines whether the platform makes money on an item that cost real labour to process and may sell for twenty dollars. The decision must be made in seconds, at intake, for hundreds of items a shift, by somebody who cannot research comparables. Price high and the item sits, consuming storage and eventually a markdown; price low and the margin that was meant to cover the processing cost is given away. The platform has the one asset that would solve it — a large history of what similar items in similar condition actually sold for, and how long they took — and mostly uses a rule table or a percentage of retail.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Percentage-of-retail rules, category price bands, occasional comparable lookups, and dynamic markdown after listing. The gaps: condition is not priced, though it is the largest driver of value in most categories; no time-to-sell estimate, so the price-versus-speed trade-off is implicit; brand and style demand are not modelled at the unit level; seasonality is ignored at intake; and the realised outcome never returns to improve the next price.

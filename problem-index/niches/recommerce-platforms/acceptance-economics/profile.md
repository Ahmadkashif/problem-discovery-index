# Acceptance Economics

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/recommerce-platforms|Recommerce Platforms]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to decide which items are worth accepting before the processing cost is spent — and whoever does that fixes the unit economics, because the cost is incurred at intake and the revenue is not.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.9B US in processing cost on items that should not have been accepted
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~12% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Very Low — fixed cost, uncertain revenue
**Target Buyer:** Operations and finance jointly
**Automation Potential:** Very High — the decision is a prediction the platform can make

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Managed platforms carry the whole cost of intake — receiving, inspecting, grading, photographing, listing, storing — and that cost is fixed per item while the revenue is not, which is why unit economics in this sector are persistently difficult. The decision that determines whether an item is profitable is therefore made before any of that cost is spent: should this item be accepted at all. Most platforms accept broadly, because acceptance is a seller acquisition tool and rejection is a difficult conversation, and then discover item by item that a large share of what they processed will never cover its cost. The prediction required — will this item sell, for how much, how soon — is the same one the pricing model makes, applied one step earlier where it is worth far more.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Category and brand acceptance rules, minimum value thresholds, and seller guidance. The gaps: acceptance is rule-based on category rather than predicted per item; the processing cost is not compared to the expected realised value at the decision point; rejected-item handling is not designed, so rejection is expensive; seller relationship consequences of rejection are unmanaged; and nobody reports what share of accepted items failed to cover their processing cost.

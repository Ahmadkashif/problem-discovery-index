**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)

**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to give the person making the platform's most economically consequential judgements the support and the feedback to make them well — and whoever does that takes the quality, because the whole model's economics are decided at that station.

## The Problem
How people become good at a judgement task is well established: repeated practice with timely, specific feedback on outcomes, in an environment with enough regularity to learn from. Domains with fast feedback produce genuine expertise and domains without it produce confident practitioners whose accuracy does not improve with experience. Intake grading has the repetition and none of the feedback, which the literature predicts produces exactly what it produces — long-tenured processors who are confident and uncalibrated.

## What Already Exists
The expertise and deliberate practice literature with conditions for skill acquisition; feedback design research distinguishing useful from harmful feedback; decision support system design from clinical and industrial settings; cognitive aids and checklists for high-volume judgement; and calibration training programmes from forecasting and assessment.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a warehouse role with a quota and a delayed outcome. It requires: (1) feedback delivered in aggregate and promptly rather than per item and late, since the individual outcome arrives weeks later and is noisy, while a weekly summary of calibration is both timely enough to learn from and reliable enough to trust — designing that is the specific work; (2) decision support that reduces the non-judgement work rather than making the judgement, since the processor's time is the constraint and automation should buy them seconds to think; (3) practice items seeded into the queue with known answers, which is how calibration training works elsewhere and is cheap to do here; (4) framing that survives a warehouse performance culture, since feedback that reads as monitoring will be resisted and the literature is clear that the framing determines the effect; and (5) an environment with enough regularity to learn from, which means the rubric and the reference standards have to be consistent before feedback can teach anything.

## Target Customer
Operations leadership and training functions, the processors, and the human factors practitioners for whom this is a textbook case of a learnable task with no feedback.

## Impact If Solved
The literature predicts exactly what this operation produces: long-tenured practitioners who are confident and uncalibrated. Aggregate weekly calibration feedback is both timely enough to learn from and reliable enough to trust, where per-item feedback is neither.

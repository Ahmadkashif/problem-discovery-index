# Catalogue & Attribute Automation

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/recommerce-platforms|Recommerce Platforms]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn a photograph into a complete, findable listing without a person typing — and whoever does that takes the cost out, because listing labour is a fixed cost on every item regardless of what it is worth.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.2B US in listing labour
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~8% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Moderate — recognition deployed, listing still manual
**Target Buyer:** Catalogue operations
**Automation Potential:** Very High — the capability is available and partially adopted

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Computer vision is deployed for category and attribute recognition at most serious platforms, and the listing is still substantially assembled by a person: choosing the category, entering the brand and size, writing a title and description, selecting attributes, and deciding what the photographs should show. That labour is a fixed cost on every item and is a large share of why the unit economics are difficult. The capability to do most of it automatically exists and is partially adopted — recognition tells the processor what the item is and the processor still types it into a form. The contest is closing the remaining gap between recognition and a complete, findable, accurate listing.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Category and brand recognition, attribute extraction of varying coverage, photography rigs and templated listings. The gaps: recognition assists rather than completes, so the labour remains; attribute coverage is partial and the gaps are exactly the fields buyers filter on; titles and descriptions are written or templated rather than generated for retrieval; the photograph set is not chosen per item; and listing completeness is not measured against what makes items findable.

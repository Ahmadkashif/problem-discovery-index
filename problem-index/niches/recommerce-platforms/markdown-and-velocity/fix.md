# The Item With No Views for Three Weeks

**Niche:** [[niches/recommerce-platforms/markdown-and-velocity/profile|Markdown & Velocity]]
**Industry:** [[industries/recommerce-platforms|Recommerce Platforms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** An item that has received almost no views in three weeks has a listing problem rather than a price problem, and the schedule's response is to discount it — which does not help, because nobody is seeing it.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #change-point-detection #confidence-intervals #revenue-impact #k-nearest-neighbors #quick-win #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to move each unique unit at the right moment for the right price rather than on a schedule — and whoever does that recovers the margin, because a markdown calendar applied to one-of-a-kind inventory is wrong for almost every item on it.

## The Problem
An item has been listed for three weeks and has eleven views. The markdown schedule reduces its price by fifteen percent. Its views the following week: nine. The problem was never the price. The item is miscategorised, the photographs are poor, the title omits the search terms buyers use, the attributes are incomplete so it fails every filter, or it is simply ranked below everything else and nobody has seen it to reject it. Discounting an item nobody is seeing gives away margin without addressing anything, and the schedule will do it twice more before disposing of it.

## Why It's Still Broken
The markdown rule takes time as its only input, so it cannot distinguish an item that was seen and rejected from one that was never seen. Views are in the storefront analytics and the markdown runs in merchandising. Nobody triages low-view items because there is no process for it. And the discount is a cheap action that looks like a response.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Diagnose before discounting. Split items into seen-and-not-bought and not-seen at the markdown decision point, which is a single threshold on impressions and is the fix — the two conditions have opposite remedies and the schedule treats them identically. For the not-seen items, fix the listing: check the category, the attributes, the title terms and the photographs against comparable items that are being seen, most of which is mechanically checkable and correctable. Re-promote rather than discount, since an unseen item needs exposure and the platform controls exposure. For the seen-and-not-bought items, discount, since that is the case where price is the plausible issue. Report the not-seen share of inventory, which is usually larger than anyone expects and is a ranking and catalogue problem rather than a pricing one. Compare each item's impressions against comparable items to detect suppression or miscategorisation. Fix the listing defects that recur, since a pattern across items is a catalogue process problem. Measure the recovery from re-promotion against the recovery from discounting, which settles the question empirically. And route persistent no-view items to a person before disposal, since some of them are valuable items with a fixable listing.

## Who Feels the Pain
Platforms discounting items nobody saw; sellers whose consignment sold for less than it should have; and merchandisers whose markdown budget goes to items with a catalogue problem.

## Impact If Fixed
A single impressions threshold at the markdown decision separates two conditions with opposite remedies that the schedule currently treats identically. The not-seen share is usually larger than anyone expects and is a ranking and catalogue problem that a discount cannot touch.

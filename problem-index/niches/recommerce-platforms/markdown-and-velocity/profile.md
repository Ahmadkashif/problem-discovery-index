# Markdown & Velocity

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/recommerce-platforms|Recommerce Platforms]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to move each unique unit at the right moment for the right price rather than on a schedule — and whoever does that recovers the margin, because a markdown calendar applied to one-of-a-kind inventory is wrong for almost every item on it.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.6B US in margin given away and storage consumed
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~10% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Low — rules-based markdown, not demand
**Target Buyer:** Merchandising and warehouse operations
**Automation Potential:** Very High — the signals are per-item and continuous

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Dynamic pricing exists in the more sophisticated operators and is generally rules-based markdown rather than demand modelling: reduce by fifteen percent at thirty days, thirty percent at sixty, remove at ninety. Applied to unique inventory that is the wrong policy for nearly every item — the piece with strong demand that simply had not been seen yet is discounted unnecessarily, the piece nobody will ever buy is held for ninety days of storage before anyone admits it, and the seasonal item is marked down in the month before its season. The per-item signals that would inform the decision — views, saves, similar items' behaviour, season — are all present and none are used, because the markdown is a calendar rule applied to a warehouse rather than a demand decision made per unit.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Time-based markdown schedules, occasional category-level adjustments, and manual intervention on high-value items. The gaps: engagement signals per item are not used; no prediction of whether an item will sell at its current price; markdown timing ignores seasonality; storage cost is not weighed against the discount; and the outcome of a markdown is not measured against what would have happened without it.

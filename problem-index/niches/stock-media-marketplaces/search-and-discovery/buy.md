# Relevance Engineering From Commerce Search

**Niche:** [[niches/stock-media-marketplaces/search-and-discovery/profile|Search & Discovery]]
**Industry:** [[industries/stock-media-marketplaces|Stock Media Marketplaces]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Commerce search built relevance engineering with judgement lists, offline evaluation and query understanding, and media search leans on embeddings and clicks.
**Tags:** #contrastive-learning #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #transformers #matrix-decompositions #gradient-boosting #hypothesis-testing #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to return the asset a buyer actually wanted from a library of hundreds of millions — and whoever ranks on what buyers licensed rather than on what they clicked decides which contributors earn anything.

## The Problem
Commerce search developed serious relevance practice: human judgement lists defining what a good result is, offline evaluation against them, query understanding and intent classification, faceted refinement, and continuous measurement of relevance separately from engagement. It exists because a retailer learned that optimising clicks produces a search that feels wrong. Media search has the same failure mode and much less of the apparatus.

## What Already Exists
Human relevance judgement lists and rating guidelines; offline relevance evaluation; query understanding and intent classification; faceted navigation design; and separation of relevance from engagement metrics.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a result whose correctness is aesthetic and contextual. It requires: (1) relevance judgements about images, where two raters disagree far more than on whether a product matches a query — this is the substantive difficulty and demands a different rating design; (2) a brief the buyer holds and does not state, unlike a product search where the intent is usually the query; (3) hundreds of millions of assets with heavy near-duplication, which changes what a good result set looks like; (4) licensing rights as a hard filter that interacts with relevance; and (5) a contributor economy on the other side, so relevance decisions are also income decisions in a way retail search is not.

## Target Customer
Product and search leadership, buyers, contributors, and search relevance vendors.

## Impact If Solved
Commerce learned that optimising clicks produces a search that feels wrong and built judgement lists to fix it. Designing those judgements for images, where raters genuinely disagree, is the adaptation and is what media search has skipped.

# Explaining Why Income Moved

**Niche:** [[niches/stock-media-marketplaces/the-contributor/profile|The Contributor]]
**Industry:** [[industries/stock-media-marketplaces|Stock Media Marketplaces]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** The people who supply everything the marketplace sells have the least information about how it works.
**Tags:** #worker-facing #descriptive-statistics #causal-inference #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #revenue-impact #automation #change-point-detection
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to tell a contributor why their income moved — search ranking, licensing terms, or a model trained on their own work — and whoever explains it keeps the supply the entire business rests on.

## The Problem
A contributor's earnings fall by a third. The cause could be a ranking change, a licensing term change, a shift in buyer demand, seasonal variation, a competitor's catalogue growth, an asset restriction, or the subscription mix changing what a download pays. The marketplace can decompose it. The contributor sees a smaller number and a forum full of speculation, concludes they are being treated badly, and in many cases is right about the outcome and wrong about the cause — which makes every subsequent conversation worse.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Explanation invites argument about decisions the marketplace considers its own, so opacity was the default — a party that does not want to negotiate its mechanisms does not explain them. Attribution of an income change requires joining ranking, pricing and demand data. Contributor relations is a support function rather than a data one. And the supply has historically been abundant enough that the relationship could be neglected.

## What to Build
Decompose the change and tell them. Attribute each contributor's income change to its causes — volume, rate, ranking, mix, restrictions, demand — which is the core and is computable from data the marketplace holds. Report the rate per download and how the subscription mix affects it, since that is invisible and is frequently the actual cause. Show ranking effects on their assets, as contributors currently cannot distinguish a ranking change from a demand change. Give advance notice of changes that will affect earnings, because discovering a rate change from a statement is what turns a commercial decision into a betrayal. Explain the licensing arrangements and what they mean for the contributor specifically. Show which of their assets are underperforming for fixable reasons, connecting to the metadata work. Provide a route to raise issues that produces an answer, since the current absence is why forums are the venue. Report aggregate contributor economics honestly, as the community will construct its own worse estimate otherwise. Support a contributor representative structure, because the alternative is an adversarial relationship with the only supply that exists. And measure contributor retention and submission rates as a business metric, since they are the leading indicator of the catalogue's future.

## Target Customer
Contributor relations and executive leadership, contributors themselves, marketplaces competing for supply, and contributor tooling vendors.

## Impact If Built
A party that does not want to negotiate its mechanisms does not explain them, so opacity became the default. Decomposing each contributor's income change is computable today and replaces a forum's speculation with an account.

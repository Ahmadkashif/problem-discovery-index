# Earnings Halved and No Announcement

**Niche:** [[niches/stock-media-marketplaces/the-contributor/profile|The Contributor]]
**Industry:** [[industries/stock-media-marketplaces|Stock Media Marketplaces]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The rate per download changed and contributors found out by comparing statements with each other on a forum.
**Tags:** #quick-win #worker-facing #descriptive-statistics #revenue-impact #evaluation-metrics #compliance #automation #confidence-intervals
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to tell a contributor why their income moved — search ranking, licensing terms, or a model trained on their own work — and whoever explains it keeps the supply the entire business rests on.

## The Problem
A change is made to how downloads are valued, or to the subscription mix, or to the ranking. Contributors notice their earnings move and discuss it publicly, comparing statements and constructing theories. The marketplace says nothing, or issues a statement after the speculation has hardened. By the time an explanation arrives, the community has decided what happened and the explanation is read as a defence rather than as information.

## Why It's Still Broken
Announcements about earnings changes invite backlash, so the default is silence until forced — a communication that can only generate complaints gets deferred until the complaints arrive anyway. There is no established notice practice. The changes are often consequences of buyer-side deals rather than deliberate contributor-side decisions, which makes them harder to explain. And nobody owns contributor communication as a discipline.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Announce before, explain during, report after. Give notice before changes that affect earnings, which is the fix and costs nothing that silence does not cost more of later. Explain the reason honestly, including when it is a consequence of a buyer-side commercial decision, since contributors can tell the difference between an explanation and a deflection. Show the effect on a typical contributor rather than describing the mechanism abstractly. Publish the aggregate effect after the change, because the community will estimate it anyway and a worse estimate will stand. Separate the deliberate change from the mix effect, as much of the variation is the latter and contributors attribute all of it to the former. Give contributors a regular earnings explanation, so a change lands in a context of routine transparency rather than into silence. Answer the forum, since it is where the conversation is and ignoring it concedes the narrative. Say when something is not going to change, as false hope is worse than a clear refusal. Track contributor sentiment and submission rates around changes, which is the business consequence and is measurable. And treat the communication as part of the change rather than as a response to it.

## Who Feels the Pain
Contributors learning about their own income from a forum; contributor relations teams arriving after the narrative has set; marketplaces whose supply relationship degrades with every change; and buyers, eventually, as the catalogue's supply weakens.

## Impact If Fixed
A communication that can only generate complaints gets deferred until the complaints arrive anyway. Notice before the change, with an honest reason and a worked example, costs less than the silence that currently precedes every announcement.

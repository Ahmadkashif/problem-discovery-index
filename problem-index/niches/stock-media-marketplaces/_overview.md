# Niche Analysis — Stock Media Marketplaces

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/stock-media-marketplaces|Stock Media Marketplaces]]

## Niche Selection

These marketplaces index hundreds of millions of assets with increasingly capable visual search, while the question of what a contributor's work is worth when it trains a model that substitutes for the library is being settled in courtrooms and licensing negotiations rather than in anything resembling a market. Generative models changed the demand side and the supply side at once: a meaningful share of routine commercial imagery can now be generated, and the libraries themselves are among the most valuable training corpora in existence. Either way the contributors receive compensation set by unilateral formula, and no participant has attempted to measure what an individual contribution is worth. Around it sit discovery that depends entirely on contributor-supplied metadata, rights clearance verified by a person, and a contributor who cannot see why their income moves. The eight niches below split the category by what competitors actually fight over.

## Why These Niches

Training corpus economics takes the largest share because it is the question the industry's future turns on and the one nobody has answered. Search is level with it because discovery determines which assets earn anything at all and is the category's strongest technical capability. The two low-digitized niches are the ones performed by people against enormous volume: keywording that determines whether excellent work is ever found, and rights clearance verified by looking at an image and its paperwork. The two underserved audiences are the reviewer rejecting someone's work in seconds and the contributor watching income move for reasons nobody will explain. The two automatable niches are a two-decade record of what buyers searched for and could not find, and a catalogue clogged with near-duplicates.

## Filter Notes

Seven of the eight level-1 niches are terminal. Training Corpus Economics is not: the label names a domain rather than a contest, and writing the contested statement produces two sentences whose winners are different companies. Contribution attribution asks what an individual asset contributed to a model's behaviour and output — a technical measurement problem over training data influence, where the winner is whoever can demonstrate a principled attribution that survives scrutiny. Licensing and compensation structure asks how the deal should be built and the money distributed — a commercial and governance problem about consent, opt-out, rate design and collective representation, where the winner is whoever constructs an arrangement that contributors accept and model developers will pay for. One is a measurement question answered by researchers and the other a design question answered by lawyers and executives; the first can exist without the second and the second is currently proceeding without the first. It therefore decomposes into **Contribution Attribution** and **Licensing & Compensation Structure**.

Two candidates were considered and rejected. **Production music and sound libraries** share the catalogue-licensing shape but the rights contest over recordings and compositions belongs to [[industries/music-distribution-platforms|Music Distribution Platforms]]. **The model developers licensing the corpora** are the counterparty here and their contest is analysed in [[industries/ai-inference-providers|AI Inference Providers]].

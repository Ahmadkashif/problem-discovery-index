# The Searches That Found Nothing

**Niche:** [[niches/stock-media-marketplaces/demand-signal-and-commissioning/profile|Demand Signal & Commissioning]]
**Industry:** [[industries/stock-media-marketplaces|Stock Media Marketplaces]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Twenty years of buyers searching for things the library did not contain is the most precise commissioning brief anyone could have.
**Tags:** #word-embeddings #k-means-clustering #time-series-forecasting #evaluation-metrics #revenue-impact #confidence-intervals #descriptive-statistics #bert
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn two decades of searches that returned nothing usable into a commissioning brief — and whoever does it fills the gaps a generative model cannot and competitors cannot see.

## The Problem
Buyers arrive with specific needs and frequently leave without licensing anything, because nothing in a library of hundreds of millions of assets matched. Each of those sessions is a precise statement of unmet demand, complete with the words used, the refinements tried and the results rejected. The record exists for twenty years. It is used to tune search relevance and is not used to decide what should be photographed.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Supply was contributed rather than commissioned, so demand analysis had no obvious consumer — a marketplace that does not direct its supply has no use for a commissioning brief. Zero-result searches were treated as a search quality problem rather than as a demand signal. Contributor guidance is generic. And the strategic question of what to commission only became urgent when generative models started absorbing the routine end of the library.

## What to Build
Mine the record and direct the supply. Identify unmet demand from zero-result and abandoned searches, which is the core and is a clean, precise and entirely unused signal. Quantify it in value rather than in volume, since a hundred searches for something a buyer would pay well for is worth more than ten thousand for a commodity image. Distinguish the demand a generative model will absorb from the demand it cannot — specific real people, real places, real events, documented reality — because that distinction determines where the library's future value is and nobody has drawn it. Direct contributors to the gaps with specific, actionable briefs rather than trending keyword lists. Commission deliberately for the highest-value gaps, as some will never be filled by speculative contribution. Detect emerging demand early from query growth, which is a leading indicator the marketplace uniquely holds. Distinguish a genuine gap from a findability failure, since a search returning nothing may mean the asset exists and is badly keyworded. Report the gap analysis to contributors as an opportunity, which is a service and a retention mechanism at once. Track whether filled gaps actually licensed, so the brief improves. And use it in the corpus valuation argument, because demonstrated unmet demand is evidence about what the library is for.

## Target Customer
Content strategy leadership, contributors seeking direction, buyers whose needs go unmet, and marketplaces competing on catalogue rather than volume.

## Impact If Built
A marketplace that does not direct its supply has no use for a commissioning brief, so the demand record went unread. Zero-result and abandoned searches are a precise, valued statement of what the library lacks and nobody has assembled it.

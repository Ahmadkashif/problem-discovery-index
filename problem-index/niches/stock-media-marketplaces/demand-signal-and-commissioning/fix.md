# Contributor Guidance From a Trend List

**Niche:** [[niches/stock-media-marketplaces/demand-signal-and-commissioning/profile|Demand Signal & Commissioning]]
**Industry:** [[industries/stock-media-marketplaces|Stock Media Marketplaces]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Contributors are told what is trending, which means they all photograph the same thing and none of them sells it.
**Tags:** #quick-win #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #revenue-impact #k-means-clustering #confidence-intervals #worker-facing #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn two decades of searches that returned nothing usable into a commissioning brief — and whoever does it fills the gaps a generative model cannot and competitors cannot see.

## The Problem
The guidance published to contributors is a list of popular search terms. Thousands of contributors read it, many photograph the same subjects, and the library gains ten thousand more images of an already-saturated concept while the actual gaps remain empty. The guidance is accurate about demand and says nothing about supply, so it directs effort precisely to where competition is highest and marginal value is lowest.

## Why It's Still Broken
Popular search terms are the easiest demand statistic to publish, so that is what gets published — guidance built from the most available number directs effort to where everyone else is already going. Supply-side saturation is not computed alongside demand. Contributors have no other source of direction. And nobody measures whether the guidance produced anything that licensed.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Publish demand relative to supply, not demand alone. Report the ratio of demand to existing supply per concept, which is the fix and inverts the guidance in exactly the way that matters. Name the specific gaps rather than the popular terms, since a gap is actionable and a trend is a crowd. Include the value dimension, as a low-volume high-value gap is a better use of a contributor's time than a high-volume commodity one. Describe the brief precisely — the subject, the treatment, the context — because a one-word concept produces forty interpretations and buyers want one of them. Say what already exists so contributors do not duplicate it. Flag the concepts a generative model now serves adequately, since directing contributors to photograph those is wasting their money. Track what was produced against each published gap, so the guidance can be evaluated. Report whether assets produced from guidance actually licensed, which is the honest measure and does not exist. Personalise the guidance by what a contributor can realistically shoot, as a global list is irrelevant to most of them. And update it continuously rather than quarterly, because a gap closes once it is published to thousands of people.

## Who Feels the Pain
Contributors investing in shoots that produce nothing; buyers whose genuine needs remain unserved; the marketplace accumulating redundancy instead of coverage; and a catalogue whose growth does not improve it.

## Impact If Fixed
Guidance built from the most available number directs effort to where everyone else is already going. Publishing demand relative to existing supply inverts the advice and points contributors at the gaps rather than at the crowd.

**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn two decades of searches that returned nothing usable into a commissioning brief — and whoever does it fills the gaps a generative model cannot and competitors cannot see.

## The Problem
Retail has a long-established practice of reading demand signals for assortment decisions: null search analysis, unmet demand quantification, category gap identification, and private label development targeted at demonstrated demand a supplier is not serving. The analysis directly drives buying decisions. Media marketplaces generate the same signals continuously and route them to a search team.

## What Already Exists
Null and low-result search analysis; unmet demand quantification; assortment gap identification; category planning from demand data; and private label development targeting demonstrated gaps.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to supply the marketplace does not buy. It requires: (1) inventory contributed speculatively rather than purchased, so the response to a gap is directing contributors rather than ordering stock — this is the substantive difference and makes the brief a communication problem as well as an analysis one; (2) a gap that may be a keywording failure rather than an absence, which retail's product catalogue does not suffer from; (3) an asset that once created serves demand indefinitely, so the gap closes permanently rather than needing replenishment; (4) a generative alternative absorbing part of the demand, which changes which gaps are worth filling; and (5) a query vocabulary that is descriptive rather than product-named, making gap identification a semantic problem.

## Target Customer
Content strategy leadership, contributors, buyers, and retail analytics vendors.

## Impact If Solved
Retail reads null searches as assortment gaps with a value attached and buys against them. Here the response is directing contributors rather than buying stock, and separating a real gap from a keywording failure is the new analytical step.

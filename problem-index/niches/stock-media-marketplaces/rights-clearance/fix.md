# Marked Editorial Just in Case

**Niche:** [[niches/stock-media-marketplaces/rights-clearance/profile|Rights Clearance]]
**Industry:** [[industries/stock-media-marketplaces|Stock Media Marketplaces]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The reviewer was not sure, so the asset was restricted to editorial use and lost most of its earning potential forever.
**Tags:** #quick-win #compliance #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #confidence-intervals #worker-facing #revenue-impact #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to determine whether an asset can be licensed commercially — releases, trademarks, property, editorial restrictions — without a person examining the image and the paperwork every time.

## The Problem
Under uncertainty the reviewer restricts. That is the rational choice for them: an over-restriction costs a contributor money nobody measures, and an under-restriction is a legal exposure with their name near it. The result is a catalogue containing large numbers of assets restricted on a maybe — a possibly visible logo, a person who might be recognisable, a building that may be protected — most of which could have been cleared with one question to the contributor that nobody asked.

## Why It's Still Broken
The two errors have wildly asymmetric visibility, so the reviewer's incentive is entirely one-directional — a decision where one mistake is attributable and the other is invisible will always tilt. The contributor is not consulted before the restriction. There is no route to review it later. And nobody measures how much licensing revenue the restricted catalogue would have generated.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Ask before restricting and allow a review after. Tell the contributor the specific reason and ask for the missing release or clarification, which is the fix and clears a large share of uncertain cases in one exchange. Provide a review route for existing restrictions, since years of over-restriction are sitting in the catalogue and are currently permanent. Report restriction rates by reviewer and by reason, which will show both the volume and the inconsistency. Estimate the lost licensing value of restricted assets, as that number is the counterweight the decision currently lacks. Grade the risk rather than applying one flag, because a barely visible background logo and a prominent brand are not the same case. Track whether any restriction category has ever produced a claim, since the policy is calibrated on fear rather than on evidence. Give reviewers a clear standard for the common ambiguous cases, as most inconsistency is guidance rather than judgement. Detect the removable element, since a croppable logo means an asset can be cleared with an edit rather than restricted. Record the reason in a fixed vocabulary, which makes all of the above analysable. And review the policy against the claim history annually, because it is currently never revisited.

## Who Feels the Pain
Contributors whose work earns a fraction of its potential; buyers who cannot license assets that were licensable; reviewers making one-sided decisions under pressure; and marketplaces whose catalogue is smaller than it appears.

## Impact If Fixed
A decision where one mistake is attributable and the other is invisible will always tilt, so uncertainty becomes restriction. Asking the contributor the specific question before restricting clears most uncertain cases in a single exchange.

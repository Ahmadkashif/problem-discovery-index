# Counting Assets as Coverage

**Niche:** [[niches/stock-media-marketplaces/duplicate-and-similarity-management/profile|Duplicate & Similarity Management]]
**Industry:** [[industries/stock-media-marketplaces|Stock Media Marketplaces]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The library added twelve million assets last year and got no better at serving anything.
**Tags:** #quick-win #descriptive-statistics #k-means-clustering #evaluation-metrics #revenue-impact #confidence-intervals #automation #contrastive-learning
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to stop a catalogue of hundreds of millions of assets being mostly the same few million pictures — and whoever measures redundancy honestly knows what their library is actually worth.

## The Problem
Growth is reported in assets added. Nobody asks whether the additions improved coverage. In practice a large share of new contribution lands on already-saturated concepts, because that is where contributors see demand, so the catalogue grows and the buyer's experience does not improve. Meanwhile the review cost, the storage cost and the search dilution all scale with the additions, and the marketplace reports the growth as a success.

## Why It's Still Broken
Assets added is the available growth metric, so it is the one reported — a number that can be computed daily becomes the measure of progress regardless of what it represents. Coverage requires clustering nobody runs. Contributors are told what is popular. And nobody has connected the growth number to any buyer outcome.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Report coverage growth alongside asset growth. Cluster new additions against the existing catalogue and report how much genuinely new coverage was added, which is the fix and turns a vanity number into an operating one. Report which concepts received the most contribution and how saturated they already were, since the mismatch will be stark. Report the review cost of redundant submissions, as it is substantial and is currently attributed to volume growth generally. Show the concepts where coverage did improve, which is the good news and is currently invisible alongside the noise. Track whether new assets in saturated concepts ever license, because the answer will inform contributor guidance immediately. Report effective catalogue size internally even if the marketing number stays, since the business should know. Batch near-identical submissions in review, which recovers cost immediately. Tell contributors when a submission is highly redundant with existing assets, as most would rather know before investing in a shoot. Connect the measure to the commissioning gaps, so growth can be directed. And set a coverage target rather than a volume target, because the target determines the behaviour.

## Who Feels the Pain
Buyers whose options do not improve as the catalogue grows; contributors investing in saturated concepts; reviewers processing redundancy; and marketplaces whose growth metric measures cost as much as value.

## Impact If Fixed
A number that can be computed daily becomes the measure of progress regardless of what it represents. Reporting coverage growth alongside asset growth turns a vanity metric into one that says whether the library got better.

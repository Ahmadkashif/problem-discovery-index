## The Problem
Library and archival science solved describing large heterogeneous collections for retrieval: controlled vocabularies, subject heading systems, authority control for names and entities, faceted classification, and professional cataloguers applying it consistently. The practice is rigorous and it assumes trained staff and a stable vocabulary. Stock marketplaces have far larger collections, described by the depositors themselves, in free text.

## What Already Exists
Controlled vocabularies and thesauri; subject heading and classification systems; authority control for names and places; faceted classification; and cataloguing quality standards.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to commercial retrieval by an untrained searcher. It requires: (1) a vocabulary derived from what buyers type rather than from a scholarly standard, since commercial search vocabulary moves with culture and marketing language — this is the substantive difference and is why library vocabularies underperform here; (2) description by depositors rather than cataloguers, so the practice must be embedded in tooling; (3) conceptual and emotional attributes that formal classification handles poorly and commercial demand depends on; (4) a vocabulary that must change continuously as usage shifts; and (5) description that determines income, which introduces gaming that libraries never face.

## Target Customer
Content operations leadership, contributors, buyers, and cataloguing and taxonomy vendors.

## Impact If Solved
Library practice is rigorous and assumes a stable scholarly vocabulary and trained cataloguers. A vocabulary derived from buyer queries and applied automatically is the adaptation, and the gaming incentive is the new problem.

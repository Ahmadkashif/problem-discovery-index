# Describing in the Buyer's Words

**Niche:** [[niches/stock-media-marketplaces/metadata-and-keywording/profile|Metadata & Keywording]]
**Industry:** [[industries/stock-media-marketplaces|Stock Media Marketplaces]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** The marketplace knows exactly what words buyers use and asks the photographer to guess them.
**Tags:** #transformers #cnns #word-embeddings #contrastive-learning #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #automation #bert
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to describe an asset well enough to be found without depending on the person who made it — and whoever does it stops excellent work earning nothing because its contributor is a poor keyworder.

## The Problem
Discovery runs on description, description is supplied by contributors, and contributors describe what they photographed rather than what a buyer would search for. A picture of a woman at a desk might be keyworded with the location, the camera settings and the subject's clothing, when the buyer is searching for "remote work burnout" or "female founder". The marketplace has two decades of queries showing exactly which words buyers use and does not give them back.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Keywording was pushed to contributors because it scaled without cost, so the quality became a property of the supply side rather than a product responsibility — a task delegated to unpaid labour is not measured or improved. Automatic description was historically poor and the caution stuck. Contributors are not told their keywording is why they earn little. And keyword quality has never been connected to income.

## What to Build
Describe automatically in demand vocabulary, and tell contributors the truth. Generate description from the asset itself covering subject, composition, mood, concept and use case, which is the core and no longer requires the contributor to be a good keyworder. Map descriptions onto the vocabulary buyers actually use, since the query log is the controlled vocabulary that matters and it is not being applied. Cover the conceptual and emotional terms buyers search for, as they are where contributor keywording is weakest and where commercial demand concentrates. Score every asset's metadata quality against its discovery outcomes, which is the measurement nobody performs. Tell contributors when poor keywording is limiting their income, because they currently attribute it to the market or to the ranking. Suggest specific improvements rather than a generic warning, as a list of missing terms is actionable. Retro-fit the back catalogue, since years of poorly described assets are sitting unfindable and the fix is bulk. Detect and remove keyword spam, as over-keywording pollutes search and is the rational response to the current rules. Keep the contributor's own terms alongside generated ones, because they know things the image does not show. And measure the income effect of improved description, which will demonstrate the value and motivate adoption.

## Target Customer
Content operations and product leadership, contributors earning below their work's value, buyers who cannot find what exists, and media cataloguing vendors.

## Impact If Built
A task delegated to unpaid labour is not measured or improved, so keyword quality became a property of the supply side. The query log is the vocabulary that matters and the marketplace has never given it back to the people describing the assets.

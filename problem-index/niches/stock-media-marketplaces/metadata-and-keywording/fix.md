# Fifty Keywords, None of Them the Query

**Niche:** [[niches/stock-media-marketplaces/metadata-and-keywording/profile|Metadata & Keywording]]
**Industry:** [[industries/stock-media-marketplaces|Stock Media Marketplaces]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The asset has the maximum number of keywords and none of them is what buyers type.
**Tags:** #quick-win #word-embeddings #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #automation #confidence-intervals #bert #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to describe an asset well enough to be found without depending on the person who made it — and whoever does it stops excellent work earning nothing because its contributor is a poor keyworder.

## The Problem
Contributors are told to use many keywords, so they fill the field — with synonyms, with camera details, with literal object names, with terms suggested by a tool that suggests what is common rather than what is searched. The asset is thoroughly described and undiscoverable, because the keywords and the queries do not overlap. The contributor sees low earnings and concludes the market is saturated, which it also is, but not for the reason they think.

## Why It's Still Broken
Keyword count was the quality guidance because it was measurable, so contributors optimised the count — a guideline expressed as a quantity produces quantity rather than the quality it was standing in for. Nobody compares an asset's keywords to the queries that should return it. Suggestion tools are trained on existing keywords rather than on queries. And contributors receive no feedback connecting description to income.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Compare the keywords to the queries. Report, per asset, which searched terms it should match and does not, which is the fix and is a comparison between two datasets the marketplace already holds. Suggest from the query log rather than from common keywords, since suggesting what other contributors used perpetuates the gap. Show contributors the queries that returned similar assets and not theirs, which is concrete, persuasive and immediately actionable. Drop the keyword count guidance in favour of a coverage measure, as the count is producing the behaviour. Detect and deprioritise irrelevant keywords, because spam pollutes results for everyone and is currently rewarded. Report a discoverability score per asset alongside its earnings, so the contributor can see the relationship. Offer bulk correction for a back catalogue, since fixing one asset at a time is why nobody does it. Tell contributors which of their assets are underperforming for metadata reasons specifically, distinguishing that from market saturation. Test the income effect of a corrected asset, which will prove the case. And update suggestions as query vocabulary shifts, because the language moves and the keywords do not.

## Who Feels the Pain
Contributors earning nothing from good work; buyers unable to find assets that exist; the marketplace losing licensing it could have had; and support teams answering "why doesn't my work sell".

## Impact If Fixed
A guideline expressed as a quantity produces quantity rather than the quality it stood in for. Comparing an asset's keywords against the queries that should return it is a join between two existing datasets and tells the contributor exactly what is wrong.

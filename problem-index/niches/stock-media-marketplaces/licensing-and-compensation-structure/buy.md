# Consent and Royalty Structures From Publishing

**Niche:** [[niches/stock-media-marketplaces/licensing-and-compensation-structure/profile|Licensing & Compensation Structure]]
**Industry:** [[industries/stock-media-marketplaces|Stock Media Marketplaces]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Book publishing built subsidiary rights, reversion and royalty structures over a century, and stock licensing is operating on a terms-of-service checkbox.

**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to build a corpus licensing arrangement that contributors accept and model developers will pay for — and whoever designs one that holds owns the terms on which visual training data is traded.

## The Problem
Publishing developed careful practice for rights it did not originally contemplate: subsidiary rights enumerated separately, new-use provisions, reversion when rights are unexploited, royalty rates that differ by use, statements that itemise, and agents representing authors against publishers. The structures exist because new uses kept arriving and the parties needed a way to handle them without renegotiating everything. Stock contributor agreements grant broad rights and enumerate almost nothing.

## What Already Exists
Enumerated subsidiary rights with separate royalty rates; new-use and future-technology provisions; reversion clauses; itemised royalty statements; and agent and collective representation structures.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a contributor base of hundreds of thousands of individuals. It requires: (1) terms that must be uniform because individual negotiation is impossible at this scale, which removes the agent-negotiated variation publishing relies on and is the substantive constraint; (2) a use that consumes the whole catalogue rather than one work, so the licence is collective in effect even when it is individual in form; (3) contributors who are not professionals with representation, so protections must be structural rather than negotiated; (4) a use whose value cannot be observed per work, which breaks the itemised statement model; and (5) an unsettled legal baseline, where publishing's rights are established.

## Target Customer
Executive and legal leadership, contributor representatives, model developers, and rights advisors.

## Impact If Solved
Publishing built enumerated rights and new-use provisions precisely because new uses keep arriving. Making those protections structural rather than negotiated, at a scale where nobody can negotiate, is the adaptation.

# Consent by Terms Update

**Niche:** [[niches/stock-media-marketplaces/licensing-and-compensation-structure/profile|Licensing & Compensation Structure]]
**Industry:** [[industries/stock-media-marketplaces|Stock Media Marketplaces]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The contributor consented to their life's work becoming training data by not reading an email.
**Tags:** #compliance #quick-win #worker-facing #workflow-orchestration #evaluation-metrics #automation #descriptive-statistics #confidence-intervals
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to build a corpus licensing arrangement that contributors accept and model developers will pay for — and whoever designs one that holds owns the terms on which visual training data is traded.

## The Problem
The mechanism was a terms-of-service update with a notice period. Contributors who did not read it, did not understand it, or did not act within the window are deemed to have agreed. Many found out later. Some discovered an opt-out existed after the deadline, or found that opting out also removed their work from other things they did want. The consent is legally arguable and practically worthless as a basis for a relationship with the people who supply everything the business sells.

## Why It's Still Broken
Terms updates are the standard mechanism for changing a platform agreement, so it was used for a change of an entirely different character — a process designed for routine amendments was applied to a fundamental one because it was the process that existed. Seeking affirmative consent risks a low response rate. The window was set by the deal timetable. And nobody measured how many contributors understood.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Ask properly and keep asking. Seek affirmative consent for training use specifically, separately from general terms, which is the fix and is the difference between a defensible position and an arguable one. Explain in plain language what the use is, since the current notices are legal text about a technology many contributors do not follow. Make opt-out reversible and independent of other settings, as bundling it with visibility or licensing is what makes it unusable. Keep the consent state per asset rather than per account, because contributors reasonably feel differently about different work. Re-ask for new deals rather than treating one consent as permanent, since each licence is a different use. Report the consent rate honestly, which is uncomfortable and is the only way to know where the corpus stands. Contact contributors directly rather than relying on a notice, as the relationship warrants it and the cost is small. Support the contributor who wants to negotiate rather than only accept or refuse, even if only through a representative body. Record the consent evidence properly, since a model developer buying a clean corpus needs it and nobody can currently supply it. And accept a smaller consenting corpus over a larger contested one, because that is what the buyer is actually paying for.

## Who Feels the Pain
Contributors who found out afterwards; contributor relations teams handling the fallout; model developers whose licensed corpus rests on contested consent; and marketplaces whose supply relationship is now adversarial.

## Impact If Fixed
A process designed for routine amendments was applied to a fundamental change because it was the process that existed. Affirmative, per-asset, re-askable consent is what converts an arguable legal position into a corpus a buyer can rely on.

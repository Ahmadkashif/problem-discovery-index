# An Arrangement That Holds

**Niche:** [[niches/stock-media-marketplaces/licensing-and-compensation-structure/profile|Licensing & Compensation Structure]]
**Industry:** [[industries/stock-media-marketplaces|Stock Media Marketplaces]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** The deals being signed now will define the terms for a decade, and they are being built without consent, representation or a rate basis.
**Tags:** #compliance #revenue-impact #workflow-orchestration #evaluation-metrics #data-integration #descriptive-statistics #automation #confidence-intervals
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to build a corpus licensing arrangement that contributors accept and model developers will pay for — and whoever designs one that holds owns the terms on which visual training data is traded.

## The Problem
Corpus licensing is being arranged bilaterally, under commercial pressure, on terms derived from existing contributor agreements that were written for a different purpose. Consent is a terms amendment. Opt-out is a toggle whose consequences are unstated. The rate is a negotiated lump sum divided by a formula. Nobody represents the contributors. The arrangements are therefore fragile: they depend on contractual rights that may be contested, on contributors who may leave, and on a legal position that may move.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The first deals were negotiated at speed because the demand appeared suddenly, so the structure was whatever could be signed — an arrangement built under time pressure uses the rights already in hand rather than the ones that would make it durable. Designing a better structure requires giving up negotiating position. Contributors are unorganised. And the legal uncertainty makes everyone prefer to move quickly rather than carefully.

## What to Build
Design the arrangement to survive. Obtain genuine, specific, revocable consent rather than relying on an amendment, which is the core and is what makes the whole structure defensible whatever the courts decide. Define the licence precisely — what use, what duration, what derivative outputs, what territories — since ambiguity is where the next dispute lives. Set the rate on a stated basis, which the attribution work would supply and which even a crude principled basis improves on. Make opt-out real and state its consequences honestly, as an opt-out whose effect is unclear is worse than none. Support contributor representation rather than resisting it, because a counterparty that can agree is worth more than one that can only object. Report distributions transparently, since the money reaching the right people is the arrangement's only visible proof. Handle the contributor who has left, who has died, or whose work was sold, as these cases are numerous and are currently unaddressed. Build the audit trail a model developer needs to demonstrate a clean corpus, which is what the buyer is actually purchasing. Design for the deals that follow rather than for this one, as the structure will be the template. And separate the marketplace's own generative products from the licensing arrangement, because the conflict is real and contributors can see it.

## Target Customer
Executive, commercial and legal leadership, contributors and emerging representative bodies, model developers, and policymakers shaping the regime.

## Impact If Built
An arrangement built under time pressure uses the rights already in hand rather than the ones that would make it durable. Genuine consent with a stated rate basis is what distinguishes an arrangement that survives from one that gets litigated.

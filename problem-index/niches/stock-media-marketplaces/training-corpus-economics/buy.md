# Collective Licensing From Music

**Industry:** [[industries/stock-media-marketplaces|Stock Media Marketplaces]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Music built collective licensing to handle many rights holders and many users, and visual media is negotiating one lump sum at a time.

**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to establish what a contributor's work is worth when it trains a model that substitutes for the library — and the contest splits cleanly enough that it is not terminal.

## The Problem
Music faced the problem of many rights holders, many users and transaction costs that made individual negotiation impossible, and answered it with collective licensing: societies that license on behalf of members, blanket licences, usage reporting, and distribution rules that are at least publicly argued about. The structure is imperfect and it exists. Visual media has no equivalent, so corpus licensing is a bilateral negotiation between a marketplace and a model developer with contributors as neither party.

## What Already Exists
Collective management organisations with member mandates; blanket licensing structures; usage reporting and distribution rules; governance with member representation; and the regulatory frameworks overseeing them.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a use that is training rather than performance. It requires: (1) usage that cannot be observed the way a broadcast can, so distribution cannot be based on reported plays and must rest on corpus composition or attribution — this is the substantive difference and is why the measurement half matters; (2) a one-time ingestion rather than recurring performances, which changes the licence's shape entirely; (3) rights holders who are individual contributors rather than publishers, with no existing collective structure; (4) marketplaces that hold contractual rights already and would have to cede negotiating position; and (5) an unsettled legal basis, where music's collective structures rest on established rights.

## Target Customer
Executive and rights leadership, contributor representatives and emerging collectives, model developers, and policymakers.

## Impact If Solved
Music built collective structures because individual negotiation was impossible, which is exactly the position contributors are in. The unsolved piece is that training is not an observable performance, which is what makes the attribution work load-bearing.

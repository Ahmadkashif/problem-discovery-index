# A Formula Nobody Negotiated

**Niche:** [[niches/stock-media-marketplaces/training-corpus-economics/profile|Training Corpus Economics]]
**Industry:** [[industries/stock-media-marketplaces|Stock Media Marketplaces]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The contributor receives a payment derived from a formula they did not agree to, calculated on terms they cannot see.
**Tags:** #compliance #quick-win #descriptive-statistics #revenue-impact #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #worker-facing #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to establish what a contributor's work is worth when it trains a model that substitutes for the library — and the contest splits cleanly enough that it is not terminal.

## The Problem
A licensing deal is announced. Contributors learn that their work was included, that a fund exists, and that payments will be made according to a formula involving download history and asset counts. They did not negotiate it, cannot verify it, and in many cases did not meaningfully consent to it. The amount is what it is. Whatever the eventual legal answer, the process has already damaged the relationship between the marketplaces and the people who supply everything they sell.

## Why It's Still Broken
The deal was negotiated bilaterally under commercial pressure, so contributor consent was handled through existing terms rather than sought — a party with contractual rights and a deal to close will use the rights it already has. Contributors have no collective body. Publishing the formula invites argument. And the alternative structure would take time the negotiation did not allow.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Explain, verify and consult, even within the existing deal. Publish the formula and the inputs, which is the fix and costs nothing that the announcement has not already cost. Show each contributor their own calculation, since an unverifiable payment is the core grievance and the arithmetic is per-contributor already. Report what was licensed — how many assets, to whom, for what use — as contributors are currently learning it from press coverage. Offer a genuine opt-out with a real mechanism, because an opt-out nobody can exercise is not one. Consult contributors before the next deal rather than after, as there will be a next deal and the pattern is now established. Explain what the licence does and does not permit, since contributors reasonably assume the worst in the absence of detail. Separate the corpus licence from the marketplace's own generative products, which contributors conflate and which the marketplace has not clarified. Report the total received and the total distributed, because the share is the question everyone is actually asking. Support a contributor representative body rather than resisting one, since the alternative is litigation and attrition. And commit to a principled basis for the next arrangement, which is the only thing that would restore the relationship.

## Who Feels the Pain
Contributors whose work was licensed without their agreement; contributor relations teams defending a formula they did not design; marketplaces whose supply depends on people who no longer trust them; and model developers whose licensed corpus is less defensible than it looks.

## Impact If Fixed
A party with contractual rights and a deal to close will use the rights it already has, so consent was assumed rather than sought. Publishing the formula and showing each contributor their own calculation costs nothing more than the announcement already did.

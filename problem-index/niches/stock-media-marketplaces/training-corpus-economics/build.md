# Pricing the Corpus Principally

**Niche:** [[niches/stock-media-marketplaces/training-corpus-economics/profile|Training Corpus Economics]]
**Industry:** [[industries/stock-media-marketplaces|Stock Media Marketplaces]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** The marketplaces with clean provenance are the only parties who could demonstrate a principled attribution, and none of them has tried.
**Tags:** #causal-inference #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #compliance #revenue-impact #monte-carlo-methods #hypothesis-testing #graph-theory
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to establish what a contributor's work is worth when it trains a model that substitutes for the library — and the contest splits cleanly enough that it is not terminal.

## The Problem
A licensed corpus and an unlicensed one are currently distinguished only by legal risk. The marketplaces hold something else: a large body of rights-cleared, human-described visual material with complete provenance and two decades of revealed demand attached. That is exactly what would be needed to say what an individual contribution is worth to a model's output. Instead compensation is a formula applied unilaterally, contributors have no basis to evaluate it, and the industry's strongest argument goes unmade.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Compensation was designed to be administrable rather than principled, so a formula was chosen and the measurement question was never opened — an arrangement that can be implemented immediately displaces one that would have to be built. Attribution in trained models is genuinely hard research. A measured attribution might imply higher payments. And the legal posture rewards ambiguity over precision.

## What to Build
Measure the contribution and design the arrangement around it. Develop an attribution method over the corpus that says what an asset contributed, which is one half and is the technically hard part. Design the licensing and compensation structure — consent, opt-out, rate basis, distribution, collective representation — which is the other half and is proceeding now without any measurement behind it. Use the provenance advantage as the argument, since a corpus whose every asset has a consenting, identified, compensated contributor is a fundamentally different product and nobody is selling it that way. Make consent genuine rather than a term of service change, because the durability of the whole arrangement depends on it. Price on something defensible — contribution, scarcity, revealed demand, exclusivity — rather than on a share of a negotiated lump sum. Report to contributors what was licensed and on what basis, as the current opacity is what makes the formula unacceptable regardless of its level. Support collective representation, since individual contributors have no negotiating position and the absence of one is why the formula stands. Distinguish the corpus licence from the output substitution question, as they are different harms and are currently conflated. Build the audit trail that a model developer would need to prove a clean corpus, which is a product in itself. And publish the methodology, because the argument only works if it can be examined.

## Target Customer
Executive and rights leadership, contributors and their representatives, model developers seeking defensible corpora, and regulators and courts assessing the question.

## Impact If Built
An arrangement that can be implemented immediately displaces one that would have to be built, so a formula replaced the measurement question. Clean provenance plus revealed demand is the only basis on which a licensed corpus could be distinguished from an unlicensed one on grounds other than risk.

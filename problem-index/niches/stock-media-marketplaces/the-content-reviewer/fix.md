# Rejected, Reason Code Four

**Niche:** [[niches/stock-media-marketplaces/the-content-reviewer/profile|The Content Reviewer]]
**Industry:** [[industries/stock-media-marketplaces|Stock Media Marketplaces]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The contributor is told their work failed on quality and cannot tell which of the forty images, or why, or what to do.
**Tags:** #quick-win #worker-facing #evaluation-metrics #automation #descriptive-statistics #workflow-orchestration #confidence-intervals #compliance
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to let a reviewer assess technical quality, rights, similarity and policy in seconds without every rejection being an unexplained loss to someone — and whoever supports that decision improves both the catalogue and the relationship it depends on.

## The Problem
A batch is submitted, some of it is rejected, and the notification gives a reason code and a policy link. The contributor does not know which specific images failed, what the defect was, whether it was a technical issue they could correct, a similarity issue they could avoid, or a commercial judgement they cannot argue with. They resubmit the same work with random changes, it is rejected again, and both sides waste the effort.

## Why It's Still Broken
The rejection message was designed to close the transaction rather than to inform the supplier, so it carries a code — a notification built to record an outcome does not teach the person receiving it. Reviewers are paced by the queue and cannot write explanations. Detailed reasons invite argument. And nobody measures resubmission of the same material.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Say which asset and what was wrong. Identify the specific assets rejected rather than the batch, which is the fix and is the single most common complaint. Give the specific defect with the region of the image where it occurs, since automated technical checks already know this and it is discarded. Distinguish a correctable defect from an unappealable judgement, because the contributor's next action is completely different. Show an example of an accepted asset that is similar, as comparison teaches faster than description. Report each contributor's rejection reasons over time, so a systematic problem is visible to them. Detect resubmission of rejected material and say so, which stops a loop that wastes both sides' effort. Let the contributor ask one question about a rejection, as a single exchange resolves most confusion. Publish the acceptance criteria with real examples, since the current policy text is abstract. Measure resubmission and repeat rejection rates, which will show the scale of the waste. And track contributors who stop submitting after a rejection, because losing a good contributor over an unexplained code is the expensive outcome.

## Who Feels the Pain
Contributors resubmitting in the dark; reviewers processing the same material twice; support teams handling rejection disputes; and marketplaces losing suppliers over a message they never read carefully.

## Impact If Fixed
A notification built to record an outcome does not teach the person receiving it, so the rejection is a code. Naming the specific asset and the specific defect uses information the automated checks already produce and discard.

# Paying Twice for the Same Verification

**Niche:** [[niches/mortgage-lenders/income-asset-verification/profile|Income, Asset & Employment Verification]]
**Industry:** [[industries/mortgage-lenders|Mortgage Lenders]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A lender pays for an employment verification at application and again shortly before closing, and sometimes a third time when a closing slips past the report's validity.
**Tags:** #workflow-orchestration #automation #descriptive-statistics #quick-win #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to verify income, employment and assets from third-party data so the agencies grant rep-and-warrant relief on that component, and whoever validates the largest share of files at the lowest cost per loan takes the account.

## The Problem
Agency rules require employment to be confirmed close to the note date, and validation reports carry close-by dates. When closings slip, verifications expire and are re-ordered at full price; when they are re-ordered automatically at a fixed milestone, some are bought for loans that will never close.

## Why It's Still Broken
Verification orders are triggered by origination-system milestones set once, without regard to each loan's real closing probability, and per-transaction pricing from the dominant payroll-data provider makes every duplicate expensive. Nobody owns the verification budget across the pipeline.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Time re-verifications to each loan's scheduled closing and current pull-through probability, use cheaper verbal or connector re-verification where the agency permits it, and report verification cost per funded loan, including orders on loans that fell out.

## Who Feels the Pain
Operations and finance leaders watching per-loan costs at a time when production profit averaged $785 per loan (MBA, 2025).

## Impact If Fixed
A scheduling rule and one report cut duplicate verification spend without touching the underwriting standard.

# Document Comparison Tools for Returned Conditions

**Niche:** [[niches/mortgage-lenders/the-mortgage-underwriter/profile|The Mortgage Underwriter]]
**Industry:** [[industries/mortgage-lenders|Mortgage Lenders]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Lawyers compare contract versions with one click; underwriters re-read whole documents to find what changed when a condition comes back.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #automation #worker-facing #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to let an underwriter clear a file in one full review with every income and asset figure traced to its source, and whoever cuts condition re-reviews without moving the lender's QC defect rate takes the account.

## The Problem
A returned condition might be an updated bank statement, a corrected letter of explanation or a new paystub. The underwriter must check that it resolves the condition and that nothing else in the file changed in a way that matters, which in practice means re-reading.

## What Already Exists
Document comparison and review tools from legal and contract management (Litera, Workshare-style redlining, and contract-review AI) that show differences between document versions and flag changed clauses.

## The Customization Gap
The mortgage layer: comparison across document types (a new statement against the old one, a revised paystub against the prior period); mapping each change to the conditions and the income or asset figures it affects; recalculating ratios when a figure moves; checking the document against the AUS data and flagging when a resubmission is required; and a record of what the underwriter accepted.

## Target Customer
Underwriting managers measured on turn time and touches per file.

## Impact If Solved
Condition re-reviews drop from re-reading to confirming a short list of changes, which is where much of an underwriter's day goes in a busy month.

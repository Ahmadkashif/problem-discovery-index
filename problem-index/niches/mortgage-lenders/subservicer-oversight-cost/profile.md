# Subservicer Oversight & Cost to Service

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/mortgage-lenders|Mortgage Lenders]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to service the retained book below the cost and default assumptions priced into the MSR while passing agency, investor and CFPB servicing audits, and whoever proves the lowest cost per loan with clean audit results takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$3B a year in subservicing fees, servicing platform licences and oversight services paid by lenders that own servicing rights (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~25% of mortgage lenders' technology, data and outsourced-service spend (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** High in the servicer's systems; low in the owner's oversight, which runs on monthly decks, sample reviews and SOC reports
**Target Buyer:** Head of Servicing Oversight or Chief Risk Officer at the MSR owner
**Automation Potential:** High for monitoring; medium for loss-mitigation judgement

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Most independent lenders that retain servicing rights do not service the loans themselves; they pay a subservicer per loan per month and remain responsible to the agencies and regulators for how borrowers are treated. Cost to service, especially for delinquent loans, is an explicit assumption in the MSR value, and servicing failures under Regulation X timelines or agency loss-mitigation rules land on the owner. The contest is between subservicers and servicing platforms on cost and audit quality, judged by owners who see only what the subservicer reports.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Subservicers (Cenlar, Dovenmuehle, LoanCare and others) run ICE MSP or Sagent and deliver SLA scorecards, complaint logs and SOC 1 reports. Owners oversee them with vendor-management platforms and periodic file sampling. The gap: the owner cannot see loan-level servicing decisions (a loss-mitigation denial, a missed escrow analysis, a mishandled complaint) until a sample review or a regulator finds them.

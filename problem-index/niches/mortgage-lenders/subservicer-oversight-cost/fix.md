# The Monthly SLA Deck That Hides the Borrower

**Niche:** [[niches/mortgage-lenders/subservicer-oversight-cost/profile|Subservicer Oversight & Cost to Service]]
**Industry:** [[industries/mortgage-lenders|Mortgage Lenders]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The subservicer reports that nearly every call was answered on time, and says nothing about whether the delinquent borrowers got the right loss-mitigation answer.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #compliance #quick-win #data-integration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to service the retained book below the cost and default assumptions priced into the MSR while passing agency, investor and CFPB servicing audits, and whoever proves the lowest cost per loan with clean audit results takes the account.

## The Problem
SLA scorecards measure what is easy to count (call answer times, payment posting, investor reporting deadlines). The owner's risk sits in outcomes the deck does not show: loss-mitigation decisions, complaint resolution and the cost of delinquent loans.

## Why It's Still Broken
SLAs were negotiated when the contract was signed, by procurement, around what the subservicer's systems already reported. The owner lacks the loan-level data to propose better measures.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Add outcome measures to the monthly review, computed from the loan-level files the owner already receives: share of complete loss-mitigation applications decided within the regulatory timeline, decisions consistent with the investor waterfall on a re-check, repeat complaints by topic, and cost per loan by delinquency status against the MSR assumption.

## Who Feels the Pain
Oversight officers; CFOs whose cost-to-service assumption is untested; borrowers in default.

## Impact If Fixed
A modest reporting change gives the owner a view of borrower outcomes and cost without a new system, and evidence to renegotiate the contract.

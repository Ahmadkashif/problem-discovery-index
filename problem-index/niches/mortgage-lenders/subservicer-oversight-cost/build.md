# Loan-Level Oversight of the Subservicer

**Niche:** [[niches/mortgage-lenders/subservicer-oversight-cost/profile|Subservicer Oversight & Cost to Service]]
**Industry:** [[industries/mortgage-lenders|Mortgage Lenders]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** The servicing-rights owner answers for every borrower decision its subservicer makes and reviews a few dozen of them a quarter.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #bert #gradient-boosting #evaluation-metrics #compliance #workflow-orchestration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to service the retained book below the cost and default assumptions priced into the MSR while passing agency, investor and CFPB servicing audits, and whoever proves the lowest cost per loan with clean audit results takes the account.

## The Problem
Oversight is sample-based: the owner's team pulls a few dozen files a quarter, checks loss-mitigation timelines, escrow handling and complaint responses, and reviews the subservicer's SLA report. A pattern of wrong decisions across hundreds of loans can run for months unseen.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Subservicers control the data and the systems, and share loan-level activity in fixed monthly files. Owners have small oversight teams and buy oversight as a compliance cost, not as a lever on MSR value.

## What to Build
Continuous monitoring of the subservicer's loan-level activity files, letters and call transcripts: each loss-mitigation application checked against the investor's waterfall and Regulation X timelines, complaint text classified and trended, escrow and fee anomalies flagged, and cost per loan by status compared with the MSR assumption. Exceptions go to the owner's oversight team with the evidence attached.

## Target Customer
Heads of servicing oversight at lenders retaining $10B+ of servicing through subservicers.

## Impact If Built
Problems found in weeks rather than at the next exam or agency audit, and cost-to-service evidence the owner can bring to the subservicer's contract renewal and the MSR valuation.

# Tour Costing, Pricing & Currency Risk

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/tour-operators|Tour Operators]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to publish a tour price a year or more ahead that still earns its target margin after the hotels, coaches and guides are paid in other currencies, and whoever lands closest to costed margin after currency moves takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$150M of costing software, FX services and product and finance staff time spent on pricing outbound programmes (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~2% of the ~$9.0B employer receipts (estimate); the margin it governs is far larger
**Digital Adoption:** Low. Cost sheets are multi-tab workbooks rebuilt each season; hedging is a phone call or a bank portal
**Target Buyer:** CFO or head of product pricing at an outbound operator
**Automation Potential:** High for exposure calculation and repricing triggers, medium for the pricing decision

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Outbound operators sell abroad in dollars and pay suppliers in euros, pounds, yen, rand or soles. The costing workbook is where the season's margin is decided: line by line, with an assumed load, an assumed exchange rate and a markup. Prices are often fixed for a season published 12-18 months ahead (estimate). Lindblad's land brands take deposits about nine months before travel and final payment 60-120 days out, and the business hedges only a portion of tour-contract currency risk with forwards and options. Smaller operators often do not hedge at all. The contest is not FX forecasting. It is knowing the real, dated exposure that the cost sheet and the booking curve create, and pricing and hedging against it.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Costing in Excel, Tourplan or Tourwriter; FX through banks or specialists (Kantox, Convera, Corpay, Ebury) offering forwards, layered hedging and pricing at forward rates; currency surcharge clauses in booking terms. Gaps: exposure is estimated at season level, not derived from cost lines and bookings; hedges are not tied to the itineraries they protect; and margin by departure after currency is known only at season end.

# EDI-Style Document Exchange for Hotels and Ground Suppliers

**Niche:** [[niches/tour-operators/rooming-lists-manifests/profile|Rooming Lists, Manifests & Supplier Reconfirmation]]
**Industry:** [[industries/tour-operators|Tour Operators]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Supply chains exchange orders and acknowledgements as structured messages; tour operators exchange rooming lists with suppliers as email attachments.
**Tags:** #data-integration #large-language-models #automation #workflow-orchestration #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get every hotel, coach company, restaurant and guide the final names, rooms, numbers and needs without a discrepancy on arrival, and whoever delivers the most departures with zero arrival-day manifest errors takes the account.

## The Problem
Large hotel groups accept group bookings through portals and APIs; most independent hotels, coach companies and restaurants work by email. Operators cannot impose a standard on thousands of small suppliers.

## What Already Exists
Supply-chain document exchange (EDI networks, B2B integration platforms such as SPS Commerce and TrueCommerce) converts between trading partners' formats and tracks acknowledgements. Hotel group-booking APIs exist at the chains.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs a hybrid:
- structured exchange where the supplier can take it (chain APIs, portals);
- email with a supplier-friendly confirmation link for the rest;
- language-model reading of free-form replies for suppliers who answer by email anyway;
- version control of every list sent and confirmed;
- hospitality-specific objects (room types, bedding, accessibility, dietary codes);
- deadline schedules per supplier contract.

## Target Customer
Operations teams at larger operators with thousands of supplier documents a season.

## Impact If Solved
Each departure's supplier documents tracked like purchase orders, from sent to acknowledged to confirmed, with exceptions visible.

# Package Travel Insolvency Protection Schemes

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/tour-operators|Tour Operators]]
**Category:** Insight Layer
**Value-Chain Position:** Regulatory & enforcement bodies

## What They Do
Where package travel is regulated, a public scheme licenses tour operators and protects travellers' money if one fails. The UK Civil Aviation Authority's ATOL scheme is the clearest case: licence holders pay a protection contribution per passenger into the Air Travel Trust, which has had no insurance backing since the Thomas Cook payout of 2019. EU member states run guarantee funds under the Package Travel Directive. The CAA's Aviation & Travel Finance function regulates about 1,600 ATOL holders and closely supervises the 100 largest tour operators. Its analysts assess licence holders' finances at grant and renewal and monitor them through the year. The United States has no federal equivalent. Its coverage is the state seller-of-travel registrations, logged under Travel Agencies as [[niches/travel-agencies/seller-of-travel-regulators/profile|🔍 State Seller of Travel Regulators]], plus USTOA's private $1M Travelers Assistance Program.

## Insight Function
**Size:** ~10-25 financial-monitoring analysts in the CAA's Aviation & Travel Finance function, which also covers airlines (estimate; no headcount published, licensing administration excluded)
**Output:** Financial assessments behind licensing decisions, financial conditions and bonding requirements; failure handling
**Proprietary data:** Audited accounts, passenger forecasts, cash-flow returns and protection contributions for every licence holder over decades, with the record of which ones failed
**Clock:** Statutory licence renewal dates and accounts-filing deadlines
**Buyer:** Head of consumer protection or ATOL licensing at the regulator

**Kill switches:** statutory confidentiality. Section 23 of the Civil Aviation Act 1982 bars the CAA and its staff from disclosing information about a particular person furnished under the Act without that person's consent or a CAA determination, and breach is a criminal offence (checked). ATOL licence holders' returns fall within it because the ATOL Regulations are made under the same Act (recalled), so the failure-prediction record cannot leave the regulator.
**Verdict:** Logged — kill switch

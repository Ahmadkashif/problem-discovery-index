# Receptive Operators & Destination Management Companies

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/tour-operators|Tour Operators]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn an overseas operator's or agency's request into an accurate, costed proposal within hours, and whoever quotes fastest without missing a cost line takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.2B US receipts of inbound receptive operators and destination management companies (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~13% of the ~$9.0B employer receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low. Requests arrive by email, supplier rates live in contracts and spreadsheets, and quotes are assembled by hand or in Tourplan
**Target Buyer:** DMC owner or head of contracting and operations
**Automation Potential:** High for quoting and supplier booking, medium for itinerary design

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A receptive operator sells the United States to foreign tour operators and agencies: a German operator's national parks circuit, a Japanese group's West Coast itinerary, an incentive trip for a Brazilian sales team. It contracts US hotels, coaches, guides and attractions at net rates, packages them, and is paid in dollars by a foreign buyer who compares several receptives on price and speed. DMCs do the same for meetings and incentive groups. The requests are many and the win rate is low (~20-40%, estimate). The first accurate quote often wins. A missed cost line, such as a resort fee, a driver's overnight room or a national park entrance charge, comes out of margin when the trip runs. Buyers' sales calendars and fair-season contracting deadlines set the pace.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Tourplan (an enterprise system for inbound operators and DMCs, listed from ~$900 per user per month), Tourwriter for FIT itineraries, bedbanks for fill-in hotel rates, and Excel everywhere else. Gaps: requests are read and re-keyed by hand; supplier rates and seasonality are loaded manually from contracts; quotes miss conditional costs; and win/loss on quotes is not analysed.

# Teacher-Led School Tours

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/tour-operators|Tour Operators]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn a teacher's interest into a departure that reaches its enrolment minimum, with parents paying in instalments over a year, and whoever converts the most teacher sign-ups into groups that actually travel takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.4B US operator receipts from middle- and high-school group tours, domestic and international (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~15% of the ~$9.0B employer receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium. Parent enrolment and payment plans are online; teacher recruitment, district approval and parent meetings are in person and on paper
**Target Buyer:** VP of Sales or Head of Teacher Recruitment at a school tour operator
**Automation Potential:** Medium. Forecasting, nudges and paperwork automate well; the parent meeting and the teacher relationship do not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The teacher is the sales channel. Operators recruit teachers, who travel free with one leader place per several paying students (estimate), and the teacher then recruits students through a parent information night. Parents pay through instalment plans lasting a year or more. The trip needs school or district approval, which can be withdrawn after a news event abroad. The clocks are the school calendar, the district board's approval date and the operator's enrolment deadline, all ahead of air-block name deadlines. Winning means more of those teacher sign-ups becoming groups that travel. Losing means a cancelled group and a teacher who does not sign up again.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Operator enrolment platforms and parent portals, teacher-leader dashboards, CRM for teacher recruitment, and district field-trip forms. Gaps: no forecast of which groups will reach minimum; parent-night conversion is not measured; district approval status is tracked by phone; and instalment defaults show up too late to act on.

# The Programme Fee Set Before Enrolment Is Known

**Niche:** [[niches/tour-operators/faculty-led-university-programs/profile|Faculty-Led University Programmes]]
**Industry:** [[industries/tour-operators|Tour Operators]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The fee is published for twenty students, fourteen apply, and the provider, the department and the students argue over who pays the difference.
**Tags:** #probability-distributions #monte-carlo-methods #revenue-impact #quick-win #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get a faculty-led programme through the university's risk, academic and procurement review and deliver it at the per-student price students were quoted, and whoever clears approval fastest with a price that holds to departure takes the account.

## The Problem
Universities publish the programme fee before applications open, because students need it to apply and to arrange aid. The provider's quote assumed a number. When fewer students enrol, the department subsidises, the provider cuts margin or content, or the programme is cancelled close to departure.

## Why It's Still Broken
Quotes are written as a single per-student number, so the dependence on enrolment is hidden. Faculty members are optimistic about numbers. Nobody shows the price at twelve, sixteen and twenty students side by side when the fee is set.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Every quote shows a price ladder by enrolment band and the currency assumption behind it. A minimum-enrolment date and a clear fallback (price step, content change or cancellation) are agreed with the education-abroad office in advance. The ladder updates automatically from the cost sheet.

## Who Feels the Pain
Faculty directors who promised a price, education-abroad offices handling complaints, providers absorbing losses, and students facing late cancellations.

## Impact If Fixed
Fewer late cancellations and disputes, at almost no extra effort per quote.

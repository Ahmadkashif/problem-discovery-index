# Faculty-Led University Programmes

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/tour-operators|Tour Operators]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get a faculty-led programme through the university's risk, academic and procurement review and deliver it at the per-student price students were quoted, and whoever clears approval fastest with a price that holds to departure takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$600M US operator receipts from customised short-term programmes for colleges and universities (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~7% of the ~$9.0B employer receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium-Low. Proposals, budgets and approvals move as Word documents, spreadsheets and email through the education-abroad office
**Target Buyer:** Director of education abroad or the faculty programme director; the operator's university-programmes lead on the sell side
**Automation Potential:** Medium. Budgets and approval packets automate well; academic design and risk judgement stay human

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A professor wants to take twenty students to Costa Rica or Berlin for three weeks over the summer or spring break, for credit. The university's education-abroad office runs the process: academic approval, a risk review against the institution's travel policy and State Department advisories, insurance, procurement rules for the provider, and a per-student programme fee that must be set before students apply. Providers range from large educational operators to specialist custom-programme firms. Unlike school tours, the buyer is an institution with policy and procurement, the clock is the academic calendar and application deadline, and the operator is often paid through the university. Winning means clearing that review first and keeping the price students were quoted, despite currency moves and a final enrolment that is often smaller than planned.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Education-abroad offices use study-abroad management systems (Terra Dotta, Via TRM) for applications and risk registration. Operators send proposals and budgets as documents. Travel risk intelligence covers duty of care. Gaps: budgets are rebuilt by hand at each enrolment change; approval packets vary by university; and price-per-student sensitivity to final numbers and currency is invisible to the faculty member who promised it.

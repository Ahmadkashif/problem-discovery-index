# The Season Offer Sent After the Best Guides Are Booked

**Niche:** [[niches/tour-operators/the-tour-leader-and-guide/profile|The Tour Leader & Local Guide]]
**Industry:** [[industries/tour-operators|Tour Operators]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Operators wait to see which departures will run before offering guide days, and by then the guides travellers love have filled their calendars elsewhere.
**Tags:** #survival-analysis #probability-distributions #worker-facing #quick-win #workflow-orchestration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to lock in the tour leaders and guides travellers rate highest before rival operators book their season, and whoever confirms those guides' seasons first takes the account.

## The Problem
Offering a guide a departure that is later cancelled means paying a cancellation fee or breaking trust. So operators delay offers until departures look safe. Guides, who need income certainty, accept the first firm offers they get.

## Why It's Still Broken
The operator does not know which departures will run until late, so it cannot commit early without risk. Nobody connects departure forecasts to guide offers.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Use departure-run probabilities from the booking forecast to make tiered offers: firm days on departures very likely to run, offered early to the best guides; provisional days with a stated cancellation fee on the rest. Guides see a season they can plan around, and the operator prices the risk instead of avoiding it.

## Who Feels the Pain
Top guides juggling uncertain offers, staffing teams scrambling in spring, and travellers who get whoever was left.

## Impact If Fixed
Better guides on more departures, at the cost of a modest, known cancellation-fee budget (estimate).

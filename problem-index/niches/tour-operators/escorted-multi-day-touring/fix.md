# The Consolidation Worked Out on a Whiteboard

**Niche:** [[niches/tour-operators/escorted-multi-day-touring/profile|Escorted Multi-Day Touring]]
**Industry:** [[industries/tour-operators|Tour Operators]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** When two thin departures should become one, operations staff work out by hand which travellers can move, what it costs, and which hotels must be told.
**Tags:** #optimization-fundamentals #large-language-models #workflow-orchestration #automation #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to guarantee departures early enough for advisors to sell them without running them half empty, and whoever guarantees the most departures while holding load above break-even takes the account.

## The Problem
Consolidating a weak departure into a neighbouring date saves a coach, a tour director and a block of rooms. Doing it means checking for each booked traveller:
- whether their flights, pre- and post-tour nights and connecting arrangements still work on the new date;
- what compensation or refund the terms require;
- which advisor needs to be told.

The hotels on both dates then need new numbers. Staff do this on a whiteboard or in a spreadsheet, often late in the window, which is why many weak dates simply run.

## Why It's Still Broken
The information sits in different places: bookings in the reservation system, air in the advisor's or the operator's air desk, extensions in separate records, allotments in contracting spreadsheets. No screen shows the cost of a consolidation. The effort means it is considered too late or not at all.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A consolidation planner that, for any pair of departures, lists each affected traveller and what changes for them. It flags air and extension conflicts, totals refund and compensation cost against the saving, and shows the revised numbers for each hotel. Once approved, it drafts the advisor, traveller and supplier messages. It can run on every weak departure at each checkpoint, so consolidation becomes a routine option rather than a crisis.

## Who Feels the Pain
Operations managers and coordinators at escorted brands, the advisors who must call their clients, and travellers moved late without a clear explanation.

## Impact If Fixed
Seeing consolidation cost and feasibility in minutes lets an operator act at 60 days instead of 30. Each early consolidation saves a departure's fixed cost and gives travellers more notice (estimate).

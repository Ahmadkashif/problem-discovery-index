# The Specification That Lives in an Email

**Niche:** [[niches/ecommerce-aggregators/catalogue-normalisation/profile|Catalogue Normalisation]]
**Industry:** [[industries/ecommerce-aggregators|Ecommerce Aggregators]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The actual specification of an acquired product — the material grade, the tolerance, the packaging, the print file — exists in an email thread with a factory, and the acquisition transferred a spreadsheet.
**Tags:** #data-integration #compliance #large-language-models #automation #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #quick-win #graph-theory
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to describe forty acquired brands' products in one vocabulary — and whoever does that unlocks the synergy, because every portfolio-level decision requires comparability the catalogues do not have.

## The Problem
A product needs reordering from a different factory. Nobody can say what it is made of precisely, what tolerance the mating part requires, which print file was used, what the packaging specification is, or which certification testing was done. The seller knew, or rather the seller's email history with the factory knew. The purchase agreement listed the products and transferred the supplier contact. The replacement order comes back slightly different, buyers notice, reviews mention it, and the ranking that was the acquisition's whole value degrades because of a specification nobody wrote down.

## Why It's Still Broken
Small sellers never needed written specifications because they dealt with one factory that knew. Diligence checks financials and listings rather than product documentation. The absence is invisible until a reorder from a new source. And by the time it matters the seller has gone and the emails were on a personal account.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Capture the specification while the seller is still available. Make specification handover a condition of completion — the material, the tolerances, the print and artwork files, the packaging spec, the certification records and the supplier correspondence — which costs nothing to require before completion and is impossible afterwards, and is the fix. Extract specifications from the transferred correspondence automatically, since the emails contain them in prose and reading eleven hundred products' worth of threads manually is why it does not happen. Reverse-engineer what is missing from physical samples, which is a cost but is far cheaper than discovering the gap on a reorder. Record a specification completeness score per product, so the portfolio knows which products it can safely resource and which it cannot. Retain the seller briefly for specification questions, which is cheap and is the highest-value part of any transition arrangement. Store it in a system rather than a folder, so it survives the next staff change. Verify samples against the specification on every supplier change, which is standard practice in any manufacturing business and is skipped here. And treat specification completeness as a diligence finding that affects price, since a product that cannot be resourced is worth less than one that can.

## Who Feels the Pain
Sourcing teams unable to resource a product without changing it; buyers who notice a product has changed and say so in reviews; and acquirers whose consolidation synergy is blocked by missing documentation.

## Impact If Fixed
The specification exists in a seller's email history and is impossible to recover after completion. Requiring the handover as a closing condition costs nothing, and automatic extraction from the transferred correspondence is what makes eleven hundred products tractable.

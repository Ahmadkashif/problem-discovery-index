# Catalogue Normalisation

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/ecommerce-aggregators|Ecommerce Aggregators]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to describe forty acquired brands' products in one vocabulary — and whoever does that unlocks the synergy, because every portfolio-level decision requires comparability the catalogues do not have.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$150M US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~9% of category operating spend
**Digital Adoption:** Very Low — forty inconsistent catalogues
**Target Buyer:** Sourcing and category teams
**Automation Potential:** Very High — extraction and reconciliation are mechanical

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Every portfolio-level question — which products to consolidate, which categories are over-exposed, which suppliers serve which products, where inventory can be pooled, what the portfolio actually contains — requires the products to be described in one vocabulary. They are described in forty, inherited from forty sellers who each invented their own, with specifications in supplier emails, dimensions in mixed units, materials named informally and no consistent identifier scheme. The normalisation is unglamorous, was never budgeted because ownership was assumed to confer comparability, and is the precondition for every synergy the model was sold on.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Spreadsheets per brand, marketplace listing data, and supplier documents. The gaps: no common product schema; specifications live in correspondence rather than in a system; no identifier reconciliation across brands; units and measures are inconsistent; and nobody can produce an accurate statement of what the portfolio contains.

**Automation Potential:** High

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
This is the operating half of the model: running dozens of acquired brands profitably enough to justify what was paid for them. It is the largest cost line after acquisition and the surface on which the thesis is proved or disproved.

It is **not terminal**, because the operating work divides into two contests with nothing in common. The demand side fights for organic ranking and advertising efficiency inside a marketplace whose algorithm is opaque and whose competitors include single-brand sellers who react within hours; the levers are listing content, price, advertising and review flow. The supply side fights for landed cost, lead time and availability across products that share no supplier, specification or minimum order quantity; the levers are negotiation, consolidation, freight and inventory. The claimed acquisition synergies are almost entirely on the supply side, and the value that erodes after acquisition is almost entirely on the demand side — which is precisely why treating them as one function has let the model's failure hide. Filter Notes in the overview records the two rejected alternatives; the sub-niches below are the split.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Marketplace seller tooling, third-party advertising and keyword management, listing optimisation tools, and spreadsheets for supply. The gaps are specific to each side and are stated in the sub-niches.

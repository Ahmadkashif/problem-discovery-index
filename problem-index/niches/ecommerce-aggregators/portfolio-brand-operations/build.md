# Synergies on One Side, Erosion on the Other

**Niche:** [[niches/ecommerce-aggregators/portfolio-brand-operations/profile|Portfolio Brand Operations]]
**Industry:** [[industries/ecommerce-aggregators|Ecommerce Aggregators]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** The model's claimed value comes from supply-side consolidation and the value that actually disappears after acquisition is demand-side ranking and responsiveness, and reporting them as one operating result hides both.
**Tags:** #revenue-impact #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #confidence-intervals #data-integration #causal-inference #hypothesis-testing #convex-optimization
**Contested on:** Not terminal — the contest differs by whether the lever is demand or supply, and the decomposition is recorded in the profile.

## The Problem
An aggregator reports portfolio margin. Inside it, supply consolidation genuinely reduced landed cost across a subset of products — a real synergy, achieved. Simultaneously, ranking eroded across most brands as single-brand competitors out-responded a portfolio operator and review velocity fell after the original sellers stopped soliciting. Margin improved and revenue fell, and the reported figure moved little. Nobody can say which half of the operating model is working, because the operating result reports the sum and the two halves are managed by the same people against the same target.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The firms were built around a single playbook applied brand by brand, so reporting followed the brand rather than the function. Supply savings are easy to claim and demand erosion is easy to blame on the market. Separating them requires attributing revenue change to ranking, competition, price and advertising rather than to a general decline, which is analysis nobody staffed for. And a combined figure that looks stable is more comfortable than two figures that do not.

## What to Build
Report the two halves separately and manage them separately. Decompose every brand's performance change into demand-side and supply-side components — ranking position, conversion, traffic, price and advertising on one side; landed cost, availability and stockouts on the other — which is an attribution exercise on data the firm holds and is the reporting change everything else depends on. Report realised supply synergies per product against what was underwritten, since the synergy claim is the model's justification and is almost never checked against outcomes. Report demand erosion per brand against its pre-acquisition trajectory, which is the natural experiment the corpus niche exploits and which tells an operator whether their playbook is working or merely being applied. Staff and target the two functions separately, because a brand manager optimising a combined margin will take the supply saving and accept the revenue loss. Model the interaction, since a stockout is a supply failure that destroys demand-side ranking and the two are coupled in exactly one important direction. Share the demand-side learning across brands, which is the one demand-side synergy available and is not consolidation. Report portfolio concentration risk, since a portfolio of brands on one marketplace in adjacent categories is far less diversified than a count of brands suggests. And be explicit with capital about which half of the thesis is delivering.

## Target Customer
Aggregator operating leadership, the investors assessing the thesis, and the two operating functions currently sharing a target.

## Impact If Built
The claimed synergy and the actual erosion sit on opposite sides of a combined number that moves little. Decomposing performance change into demand and supply components is the reporting change that tells an operator which half of their model works.

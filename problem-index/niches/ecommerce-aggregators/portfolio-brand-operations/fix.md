# A Playbook Applied to Brands That Differ

**Niche:** [[niches/ecommerce-aggregators/portfolio-brand-operations/profile|Portfolio Brand Operations]]
**Industry:** [[industries/ecommerce-aggregators|Ecommerce Aggregators]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The same operating playbook is applied to every acquired brand regardless of category, price point, competitive structure or why it was working, because the playbook is the only thing that scales.

**Contested on:** Not terminal — the contest differs by whether the lever is demand or supply, and the decomposition is recorded in the profile.

## The Problem
The playbook says: rewrite the listing to the house style, raise the price to portfolio margin targets, switch advertising to the standard structure, move to the consolidated supplier, and consolidate the variants. Applied to a brand whose advantage was a niche listing that spoke to a specific buyer, whose price was competitive by design, whose advertising was built around a long tail of specific terms, and whose supplier made a spec no generic factory matches, every step destroys something. The playbook works on average across the portfolio and destroys a subset of brands completely, and nobody measures which subset because the results are reported in aggregate.

## Why It's Still Broken
A playbook is the only thing that scales across dozens of brands with a small operating team, and that is a real constraint rather than laziness. The brands that the playbook destroys are individually small and the aggregate looks acceptable. Judgement about which brands to exempt requires category knowledge that the portfolio structure removed. And a manager who deviates is overriding a process, which requires standing they do not have.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Segment the playbook rather than abandoning it. Classify each brand on the dimensions that determine whether the playbook helps — category competitiveness, price positioning, listing specificity, supplier uniqueness, review profile — and apply the version that fits, which is a small number of playbooks rather than one and is the practical middle between a process and bespoke management. Measure playbook steps individually against outcomes, since some steps reliably help and some reliably harm and the combined application makes them indistinguishable — running them as separable interventions with staggered timing makes each one measurable. Hold a control group, applying the playbook to some brands and not others, which the portfolio structure makes unusually easy and which nobody does. Require an explicit exemption process with evidence, so a manager can protect a brand without overriding anything informally. Feed outcomes back into the classification, so the playbooks improve. Report per-brand performance against its pre-acquisition trajectory, so the destroyed subset becomes visible against an aggregate that looks fine. Start every brand with a period of observation before intervention, which is cheap and prevents the most avoidable destruction. And accept that some brands should be left alone, which is a legitimate operating decision the current model has no way to express.

## Who Feels the Pain
Brands destroyed by a standard treatment; brand managers who could see it coming and had no mechanism; and investors whose returns include a subset of write-offs caused by the operating model rather than by the market.

## Impact If Fixed
A single playbook is a real scaling necessity and a few segmented playbooks cost little more. Running steps as separable interventions with staggered timing makes each one measurable, and a control group is unusually easy in a portfolio and is run by nobody.

# A Permanent Risk Nothing Watches

**Niche:** [[niches/ecommerce-aggregators/marketplace-policy-risk/profile|Marketplace Policy Risk]]
**Industry:** [[industries/ecommerce-aggregators|Ecommerce Aggregators]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Policy changes and account actions are the category's permanent operating risk, the inputs are all published or visible, and no tooling puts them together.
**Tags:** #compliance #large-language-models #change-point-detection #evaluation-metrics #automation #time-series-forecasting #confidence-intervals #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to know before the marketplace does that a listing or an account is about to be actioned — and whoever does that protects the portfolio, because a suspension on a concentrated estate is an existential event and nothing watches for it.

## The Problem
A policy update adds a restriction on a claim type. Forty listings across six brands in the portfolio use that claim, written years ago by sellers who are gone. Nobody reads the policy update, because it is one of many and nobody's job. Three weeks later a listing is suppressed, then another, then the account receives a warning. The operations team responds to each individually, finds the pattern on the fourth, and spends a week auditing eleven hundred listings by hand. The policy was published, the listings were inspectable, and the check that would have found all forty on day one takes a machine minutes.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Policy monitoring is not anybody's role and the announcements arrive in a channel nobody owns. Checking listings against policy language was tedious before text analysis was cheap and the assumption of difficulty persists. The marketplace provides account health metrics but not a listing-level compliance view. And the risk is treated as an unavoidable fact of operating on the platform rather than as something to be managed.

## What to Build
Monitor the policies against the listings continuously. Ingest policy documents and changes automatically and map them to the listing attributes and content they affect, which is now straightforward and turns an unread announcement into a specific work list — this is the fix and the compliance check itself is the easy part. Scan the full listing estate against current policy continuously, reporting violations by revenue at stake so remediation is prioritised. Alert on policy changes with the affected listings already identified, so the response begins before any suppression. Forecast account metric drift — defect rate, late shipment, cancellation — so an approaching threshold is addressed rather than breached. Monitor category requirement changes such as new certifications, which arrive with notice and are missed. Track competitor complaints and intellectual property claims as leading indicators, since these frequently precede an action. Maintain a rehearsed response for a suspension, because the response time determines the loss and improvising it during the event is what makes a recoverable action a catastrophic one. And report the portfolio's exposure — how much revenue sits behind each account and each policy area — which the fix note develops.

## Target Customer
Aggregator operations and risk functions, large marketplace sellers, and the seller tooling vendors whose products stop at account health.

## Impact If Built
The policy is published, the listings are inspectable, and nothing joins them, so a machine-minute check becomes a week of manual audit after the fact. Mapping a policy change to the affected listings before any suppression turns an announcement into a work list.

# Acquisition Outcome Corpus

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/ecommerce-aggregators|Ecommerce Aggregators]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn dozens of brands observed before and after acquisition into an empirical account of what drives marketplace performance — and whoever does that underwrites and operates better than anybody, because nothing else in the sector produces that evidence.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$110M US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~6% of category operating spend
**Digital Adoption:** None — a natural experiment run dozens of times, unanalysed
**Target Buyer:** The aggregators themselves, and the capital behind them
**Automation Potential:** Very High — the data is structured and the design is given

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
An aggregator that has bought dozens of brands holds something no individual seller and no marketplace analyst has: the same businesses observed before and after acquisition, with the operational changes made and the revenue consequences measured. That is a natural experiment on what actually drives marketplace performance, repeated dozens of times, with a treatment applied at a known moment — the closest thing to a controlled study that exists outside the marketplace's own research. It answers what the playbook should be, what migration costs, which underwriting features predict persistence, and what the algorithm responds to. The sector contracted while sitting on it.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Per-brand operating reports, acquisition memos, and an operating playbook derived from experience. The gaps: no before-and-after dataset assembled across acquisitions; the operational changes are not recorded in a form that supports analysis; outcomes are not attributed to interventions; underwriting is not updated from realised outcomes; and nothing from the corpus reaches the playbook.

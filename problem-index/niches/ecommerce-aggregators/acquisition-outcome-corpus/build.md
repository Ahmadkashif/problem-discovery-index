# A Natural Experiment Run Dozens of Times

**Niche:** [[niches/ecommerce-aggregators/acquisition-outcome-corpus/profile|Acquisition Outcome Corpus]]
**Industry:** [[industries/ecommerce-aggregators|Ecommerce Aggregators]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** An aggregator holds dozens of brands observed before and after acquisition with the operational changes and their consequences attached, which is a natural experiment on what drives marketplace performance, and the sector contracted while sitting on it.
**Tags:** #causal-inference #gradient-boosting #confidence-intervals #hypothesis-testing #evaluation-metrics #revenue-impact #time-series-forecasting #cross-validation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn dozens of brands observed before and after acquisition into an empirical account of what drives marketplace performance — and whoever does that underwrites and operates better than anybody, because nothing else in the sector produces that evidence.

## The Problem
Forty brands were bought, migrated, restyled, repriced, resourced and readvertised, each at a known date, with the revenue and ranking consequences observable throughout. That is forty treatment events with pre-periods and post-periods, applied by one operator with variation in timing and sequence. It answers which playbook steps help, what migration actually costs, what predicts persistence, and how the marketplace responds to a listing change — questions the whole sector guesses at. The data sits in operating reports and nobody has assembled it, because analysis was not staffed and the playbook was assumed to be correct.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The firms staffed for acquisition and operations rather than for analysis. The interventions were not recorded in a structured form at the time, which makes retrospective assembly harder than it needed to be. The answers would show that parts of the playbook destroy value, which is uncomfortable for the people who wrote it. And the sector's growth phase left no time for looking backwards.

## What to Build
Assemble the corpus and analyse it. Record every intervention in structured form at the time it happens — what changed, on which listings, on what date — which costs a form and is the difference between a corpus and an archive, and is the piece to start with even in a firm that has not done it retrospectively. Assemble the before-and-after series per listing from marketplace data, which exists and only needs collecting. Estimate the effect of each intervention using the variation in timing across acquisitions, since brands migrated in different orders provide the comparison a single migration cannot. Feed the results into the playbook, so the steps that destroy value are removed and the ones that help are done deliberately — this is the loop the sector never closed. Feed realised outcomes into the underwriting model, so each acquisition improves the pricing of the next. Publish nothing, since this is the one genuine competitive asset an operator can build in this sector and the analysis is more valuable held. Measure the corpus's predictive accuracy over time, so the underwriting's improvement is demonstrable to capital. And treat the analysis capability as a core function rather than a support one, since the evidence it produces is the only structural advantage available.

## Target Customer
Aggregator leadership and their capital, the operating and acquisition functions, and any operator intending to continue in the sector.

## Impact If Built
Forty treatment events with pre-periods and post-periods is the closest thing to a controlled study outside the marketplace's own research, and the sector contracted without assembling it. Recording interventions in structured form at the time is the difference between a corpus and an archive.

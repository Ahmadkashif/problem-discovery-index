# The Playbook That Never Learned

**Niche:** [[niches/ecommerce-aggregators/acquisition-outcome-corpus/profile|Acquisition Outcome Corpus]]
**Industry:** [[industries/ecommerce-aggregators|Ecommerce Aggregators]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The operating playbook was written early from a handful of experiences and applied to dozens of subsequent acquisitions without ever being tested against their outcomes.
**Tags:** #evaluation-metrics #causal-inference #confidence-intervals #hypothesis-testing #descriptive-statistics #revenue-impact #quick-win #compliance
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn dozens of brands observed before and after acquisition into an empirical account of what drives marketplace performance — and whoever does that underwrites and operates better than anybody, because nothing else in the sector produces that evidence.

## The Problem
The playbook was written after the first four acquisitions by people who had run marketplace brands and knew what generally worked. It was then applied to thirty-six more, unchanged, through a period in which the marketplace's algorithm changed, the competitive landscape changed, and the portfolio's brand mix changed. At no point was any step tested against outcomes. Steps that helped in the first four and harm now are still being applied. The playbook is the firm's accumulated knowledge and it stopped accumulating at acquisition four.

## Why It's Still Broken
The playbook is a management artefact owned by people whose credibility rests on it, which makes testing it politically awkward. Testing requires the corpus work and a control discipline nobody staffed. The steps are applied together, which makes individual attribution impossible without deliberate variation. And the aggregate result is attributed to market conditions, which absorbs any evidence that the playbook is part of the problem.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Test the playbook against outcomes. Stagger the steps across acquisitions deliberately so their effects are separable, which costs nothing, is invisible to anybody outside the firm, and is the precondition for learning anything — this is the fix and it can start on the next acquisition. Hold some brands out from specific steps, which the portfolio makes easy and which no operator does. Review each step annually against the evidence and remove the ones that do not survive, treating the playbook as a living document with a version history rather than as doctrine. Record the reasoning for each step, so a future reviewer knows what it was meant to achieve and can test that. Track playbook adherence separately from outcomes, since a step applied inconsistently and a step that does not work are different findings. Allow and record deviations, so the variation that occurs naturally becomes evidence rather than noise. Assign ownership of the playbook to whoever runs the analysis rather than to whoever wrote it. And state plainly when a step is retained on judgement rather than on evidence, because a playbook that distinguishes the two is more trustworthy than one that does not.

## Who Feels the Pain
Brands treated with steps that stopped working; operators whose accumulated experience stopped accumulating; and investors whose thesis rested on an operating capability that was never tested.

## Impact If Fixed
The playbook stopped accumulating at acquisition four and was applied thirty-six more times. Staggering steps across acquisitions costs nothing, is invisible externally, and can begin on the next deal — and it is the precondition for the firm learning anything from its own scale.

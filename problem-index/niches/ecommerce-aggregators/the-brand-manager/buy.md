# Exception Management and Work Prioritisation

**Niche:** [[niches/ecommerce-aggregators/the-brand-manager/profile|The Brand Manager]]
**Industry:** [[industries/ecommerce-aggregators|Ecommerce Aggregators]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Operations management built exception-based working and value-ranked work queues decades ago, and portfolio brand management runs on escalation.
**Tags:** #worker-facing #evaluation-metrics #revenue-impact #confidence-intervals #workflow-orchestration #descriptive-statistics #automation #gradient-boosting
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to direct a brand manager's attention to where it is worth most across a dozen brands — and whoever does that keeps the portfolio, because attention is the scarce input and it is currently allocated by whatever is on fire.

## The Problem
Managing many things with few people is what exception-based management was built for: define the normal state, detect departures from it automatically, rank them by consequence, and spend human attention only on the exceptions that warrant it. Manufacturing, logistics and financial operations all run this way. Portfolio brand management, which has the same structure, runs on whatever gets escalated and on a weekly review that samples rather than covers.

## What Already Exists
Exception-based management with defined normal ranges and automated detection; work queue prioritisation by value and urgency; alarm rationalisation practice from process industries; span-of-control research on how many units one person can manage; and workload forecasting for operational teams.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to exceptions defined against a commercial trajectory rather than a process tolerance. It requires: (1) normal defined per listing from its own history and its category's behaviour, since there is no specification limit and the reference is the listing's own trajectory — establishing that baseline is the technical precondition; (2) consequence estimated in revenue rather than in severity classes, which the portfolio can compute and which makes the ranking meaningful; (3) alarm rationalisation applied from the start, since hundreds of listings monitored on six metrics will generate a flood and the process industries learned exactly this lesson painfully; (4) span of control revisited with evidence, since the sector set portfolio sizes by cost arithmetic rather than by measuring what one manager can actually attend to; and (5) forecasting attention demand, so integration and seasonality do not land on the same person at once.

## Target Customer
Portfolio operations leadership, brand managers, and the operations management discipline whose practice transfers with a commercial baseline.

## Impact If Solved
Exception-based management solved attending to many things with few people and this sector uses escalation. Defining normal from each listing's own trajectory is the precondition, and alarm rationalisation from the start avoids the flood that would otherwise make the system inert.

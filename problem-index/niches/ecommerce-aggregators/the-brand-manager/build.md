# Attending to Whatever Is on Fire

**Niche:** [[niches/ecommerce-aggregators/the-brand-manager/profile|The Brand Manager]]
**Industry:** [[industries/ecommerce-aggregators|Ecommerce Aggregators]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A brand manager responsible for a dozen acquired brands across hundreds of listings can only attend to whatever is currently on fire, and everything else drifts.
**Tags:** #worker-facing #gradient-boosting #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #revenue-impact #change-point-detection #automation #descriptive-statistics
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to direct a brand manager's attention to where it is worth most across a dozen brands — and whoever does that keeps the portfolio, because attention is the scarce input and it is currently allocated by whatever is on fire.

## The Problem
A manager's Monday: a stock-out on a mid-size brand, an account health warning, a supplier delay, and a request from finance. Their week goes to those four. Meanwhile the largest brand in their portfolio lost two ranking positions on its best listing three weeks ago and has been declining quietly since, a competitor has entered a second brand's category, and a third's review rating has slipped below a threshold that affects conversion. All three are worth more than the four things they worked on. None of them escalated, because nothing broke. The manager is doing their job correctly and the portfolio is eroding through the gaps.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The escalation model came with the operating structure, where problems arrive because somebody raises them and nobody raises a slow decline. Detecting drift requires monitoring at listing level across a portfolio, which the marketplace tooling does not do. Prioritising by value requires knowing what each problem is worth, which requires the per-brand economics the sector lacks. And the manager's output is unmeasured, so nobody knows their time is going to the wrong things.

## What to Build
Rank the attention. Detect drift across every listing continuously — ranking, conversion, review rating, price position, availability, buy box — and surface the changes that matter rather than waiting for an escalation, which is the substitute for noticing and is the core of the build. Estimate the revenue at stake for each detected issue, so the queue is ordered by value rather than by volume or by who complained. Predict which issues will resolve themselves and which will compound, since a manager's attention should go to the compounding ones and the two look identical on the day they appear. Separate routine from judgement: automate or delegate the mechanical responses so the manager's week goes to the decisions only they can make. Forecast attention demand, so a manager with three brands entering a difficult season is not also given a new acquisition to integrate. Measure where attention actually went against where the value was, which is the diagnostic nobody has and which would justify the whole build. Cap portfolio size by measured attention demand rather than by headcount arithmetic, since the current allocation assumes brands are interchangeable units of work. And report the cost of drift, because it is the portfolio's largest quiet loss and is currently invisible against the fires.

## Target Customer
Portfolio operations leadership, the brand managers, and the investors whose portfolio erosion has an operational cause.

## Impact If Built
Escalation surfaces the loud problems and drift is silent, so attention goes to the wrong things every week. Ranking detected issues by revenue at stake, and predicting which compound, is what redirects the scarce input to where it is worth most.

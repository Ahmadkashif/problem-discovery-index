# The Brand Manager

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/ecommerce-aggregators|Ecommerce Aggregators]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to direct a brand manager's attention to where it is worth most across a dozen brands — and whoever does that keeps the portfolio, because attention is the scarce input and it is currently allocated by whatever is on fire.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$130M US in loaded management cost
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~7% of category operating spend
**Digital Adoption:** None — attending to whatever is on fire
**Target Buyer:** Portfolio operations leadership
**Automation Potential:** Very High — prioritisation is a computable ranking

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A brand manager responsible for a dozen acquired brands across hundreds of listings can only attend to whatever is currently on fire, and everything else drifts. The job assumes that managing a brand is a set of tasks that scales, and it is not — it is a continuous act of noticing, which does not divide by twelve. The manager's week is consumed by the loudest problem, which is rarely the most valuable one, and the drift across the other eleven brands is the erosion that shows up in the portfolio result. This is the operational expression of the model's central assumption, and the constraint is attention allocation rather than capability.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Marketplace dashboards per brand, spreadsheets, and escalations. The gaps: no ranking of where attention is worth most; problems surface by escalation rather than by detection; no forecast of which brands will need attention; routine work is not separated from judgement work; and nobody measures where the manager's time actually went.

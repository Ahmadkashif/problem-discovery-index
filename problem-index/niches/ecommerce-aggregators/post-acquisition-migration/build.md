# Routine Work That Destroys the Asset

**Niche:** [[niches/ecommerce-aggregators/post-acquisition-migration/profile|Post-Acquisition Migration]]
**Industry:** [[industries/ecommerce-aggregators|Ecommerce Aggregators]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Migrating a brand onto the acquirer's seller account, logistics and payment setup is well-trodden operational work that reliably disturbs the marketplace ranking the brand was bought for.
**Tags:** #causal-inference #change-point-detection #time-series-forecasting #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #revenue-impact #workflow-orchestration #hypothesis-testing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to move a brand onto the acquirer's infrastructure without disturbing the ranking it was bought for — and whoever does that keeps the asset, because the migration is a predictable, self-inflicted loss the sector treats as routine.

## The Problem
A brand is acquired in March and migrated over six weeks. Listings are rewritten to house style in week one, fulfilment moves in week three with a four-day stock gap, the seller identity changes in week four, and the advertising is restructured in week five. By week eight the brand's organic position has fallen across its main listings, conversion is down, and advertising costs more to hold a worse position. The integration checklist was completed correctly. Nobody knows which of the four changes caused most of the damage, because they were done at once, on every acquisition, and the result is reported as a post-acquisition dip that is expected.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The dip is expected, which is the whole problem — an expected loss is not investigated. Integration is a project management function measured on completing the migration rather than on preserving performance. The steps are done together because that is efficient for the project. And the marketplace does not explain its algorithm, so the damage is treated as unknowable rather than as measurable.

## What to Build
Sequence the migration against measured damage. Stagger the steps across acquisitions and measure each one's effect on ranking and conversion, which the portfolio structure makes possible — dozens of migrations is enough to attribute the damage to the steps, and this is the one place the sector's scale is an analytical advantage it has not used. Sequence to protect the asset: make the changes that carry no ranking risk first, keep listings untouched through the fulfilment transition, and separate the changes that do carry risk so their effects are attributable and recoverable. Guarantee stock continuity through the fulfilment change, which the fix note develops and which is frequently the largest single cause. Delay listing edits until the brand has stabilised, and make them one at a time. Forecast the dip and the recovery period per brand, so it is budgeted in the acquisition case rather than absorbed as a surprise. Monitor ranking daily through the migration with a defined rollback for changes that cause a drop, since some steps are reversible if caught quickly. Preserve review flow, since the mechanisms the previous seller used frequently stop at completion and the velocity change is a ranking input. And report migration damage per acquisition as a standing metric, because it is the most controllable value loss in the model and is currently nobody's number.

## Target Customer
Integration and operations teams, aggregator leadership, and the investors whose acquisition cases do not include this cost.

## Impact If Built
The dip is expected, which is why nobody investigates it, and it is the most controllable loss in the model. Dozens of migrations is enough to attribute damage to individual steps, which makes the sector's scale an analytical advantage for the first time.

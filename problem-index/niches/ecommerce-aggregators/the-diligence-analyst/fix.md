# Numbers the Seller Chose to Show

**Niche:** [[niches/ecommerce-aggregators/the-diligence-analyst/profile|The Diligence Analyst]]
**Industry:** [[industries/ecommerce-aggregators|Ecommerce Aggregators]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Every figure in diligence comes from an interface the seller controls, in exports the seller prepared, for a period the seller selected, and the analyst has no independent source for any of it.
**Tags:** #compliance #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #confidence-intervals #hypothesis-testing #data-integration #quick-win #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to give an analyst a real view of a target instead of seller-interface screenshots under an auction clock — and whoever does that takes the deal quality, because the analyst's output is the only thing standing between capital and a bad acquisition.

## The Problem
The trailing twelve months shown starts conveniently after a poor quarter. The advertising figures exclude a period of heavy spend. A product line that was discontinued because of a quality problem is presented as a range rationalisation. The return rate is given as a blended average that hides one product at four times the rest. None of this is fabrication; every number is real and selectively presented, which is what a seller in an auction does. The analyst has no independent view of any of it and no way to ask a question whose answer they can verify.

## Why It's Still Broken
The marketplace will not give a prospective buyer access to a seller's data, reasonably, so the seller is the only source. External reconstruction is possible for some of it and nobody has built the capability. The auction timeline prevents the back-and-forth that would surface the selection. And the analyst flagging that a period was selected sounds like an accusation rather than an observation.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Build an independent view and ask for the rest by right. Collect public marketplace data on target categories continuously, so when a brand comes to market the buyer already holds an independent ranking, price and review history that is not the seller's to select — this is the fix and it requires collecting in advance rather than on demand. Request full-period exports as a standard condition rather than accepting selected ranges, which is a negotiating position the buyer can take and frequently does not. Cross-check what can be cross-checked: reviews against claimed volumes, price history against claimed margins, ranking against claimed traffic. Detect the common presentation choices — period selection, blended rates hiding a bad product, advertising under-investment, discontinued lines — as a standard checklist, since they recur and are recognisable. Ask for the data rather than the report, since a prepared spreadsheet is an argument and a raw export is evidence. Require seller warranties on the specific figures the price depends on, with a price adjustment mechanism, so selection carries a cost. Report the verification status of every number the valuation rests on. And walk from processes that will not permit verification, which is the discipline that makes the rest work.

## Who Feels the Pain
Analysts held responsible for numbers they could not verify; investment committees approving on selected presentations; and the capital that funded the sector's contraction.

## Impact If Fixed
Every number comes from a source the seller controls, and an independent ranking and review history can be collected in advance by anyone who bothers. Asking for raw exports rather than prepared reports turns an argument into evidence.

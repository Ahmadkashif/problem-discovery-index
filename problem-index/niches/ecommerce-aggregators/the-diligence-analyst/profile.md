# The Diligence Analyst

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/ecommerce-aggregators|Ecommerce Aggregators]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to give an analyst a real view of a target instead of seller-interface screenshots under an auction clock — and whoever does that takes the deal quality, because the analyst's output is the only thing standing between capital and a bad acquisition.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$90M US in analyst cost and mispriced deals
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~5% of category operating spend
**Digital Adoption:** None — screenshots under a deadline
**Target Buyer:** Acquisition teams and investment committees
**Automation Potential:** Very High — the analysis is mechanical given the data

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A diligence analyst evaluates an acquisition target through screenshots and exports from a marketplace reporting interface built for sellers, under a deadline set by a competitive auction. The interface is designed to help a seller run their own business, not to help a buyer assess it: the history is limited, the exports are awkward, the metrics that matter for persistence are absent, and the seller controls what is shared and when. The analyst assembles the best picture they can, flags what they could not verify, and the investment committee decides. They are the last control before capital is deployed and they are working with a screenshot.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Seller interface exports, screen-sharing sessions with the seller, third-party keyword and competitor tools, and financial statements. The gaps: no standard data request that gets what diligence actually needs; no independent verification of seller-provided figures; ranking and review history are not reconstructed; competitive entry is not assessed systematically; and no template of what to look for by category.

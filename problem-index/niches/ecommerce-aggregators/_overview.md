## Why These Niches

The category's defining failure is underwriting. Aggregators paid multiples on trailing earnings for businesses whose performance rested on a seller's supplier relationship, their ranking momentum, their review velocity and their responsiveness — none of which transferred with the sale — and discovered it afterwards. The sector's contraction was a failure of prediction rather than of operations, which makes the question of whether an acquired brand's revenue survives the change of ownership the largest contested surface in it, and the one the remaining capital depends on.

Portfolio brand operations **failed the filter as one niche**. The demand side is fought over organic ranking and advertising efficiency inside a marketplace whose algorithm nobody outside it understands, run by advertising specialists, competing against an independent seller who watches one brand full-time and reacts within hours. The supply side is fought over landed cost and availability across a heterogeneous portfolio of products with different suppliers, specifications and minimum order quantities, run by sourcing staff, competing against the original owner's personal supplier relationship. Different teams, different levers, different failure modes, and the claimed synergies live entirely on one of the two sides. Decomposed below.

The two underdigitised areas are both where the model's value leaks. Migrating a brand onto the acquirer's account, logistics and payment setup is well-trodden operational work that reliably disturbs the marketplace ranking the brand was bought for. And consolidated purchasing, the headline synergy, requires knowing that two acquired brands' products are similar enough to buy together, which nobody can determine across forty inconsistent catalogues.

The two underserved constituencies are the brand manager responsible for a dozen brands across hundreds of listings who can only attend to what is on fire, and the diligence analyst evaluating a target through screenshots from a seller reporting interface under a deadline set by a competitive auction.

The automation niches are marketplace policy risk, which is permanent and addressed by no tooling, and the record of dozens of brands observed before and after acquisition — a natural experiment on what actually drives marketplace performance, which the sector contracted while sitting on.

## Filter Notes

Seven of the eight level-1 niches are terminal. **Portfolio Brand Operations** is not: it names the running of acquired brands rather than a contest, and the two halves are unrelated. Marketplace ranking and advertising is a demand-side contest against an opaque algorithm and against sellers who react faster, won on organic position and advertising efficiency. Supply and sourcing is a physical contest over landed cost, lead times and minimum order quantities across products that have nothing in common, won against the relationship the previous owner had with a factory. The claimed acquisition synergies are almost entirely on the supply side and the value that erodes after acquisition is almost entirely on the demand side, which is precisely why treating them as one function conceals the model's failure. Decomposed into two contested sub-niches.

Two candidates were rejected. *Selling tools for independent marketplace sellers* was rejected because its buyer is a single-brand seller rather than a portfolio operator and it belongs to the ecommerce sellers industry covered separately in this vault. *Third-party logistics and fulfilment* was rejected because it belongs to the logistics industries covered separately, and the aggregator's role is procurement rather than competition.

# Acquisition Underwriting

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/ecommerce-aggregators|Ecommerce Aggregators]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to predict whether an acquired brand's revenue survives the change of ownership — and whoever does that takes the market, because the sector's contraction was the consequence of that prediction being made badly at scale.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$420M US attributable to the underwriting decision
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~24% of category operating spend
**Digital Adoption:** None — multiples applied to trailing earnings
**Target Buyer:** Acquirers, and the debt and equity behind them
**Automation Potential:** High — the predictors are observable and the outcomes are known

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
An aggregator's entire return depends on one prediction: will this brand still earn what it earns today once somebody else owns it. The sector answered it with a multiple applied to trailing earnings, which implicitly assumes everything transfers. What did not transfer was frequently the whole business: the seller's supplier relationship and the pricing it commanded, their ranking momentum, their review velocity, their ability to respond to a competitor within hours, their undocumented knowledge of a category. The prediction is the product, it is the one thing the aggregator does that the market pays for, and the sector contracted because it was made from an accounting figure rather than from an analysis of what was actually producing the earnings.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Financial diligence on seller-provided statements, marketplace reporting exports, keyword and competitor tooling, and a multiple negotiated against comparable transactions. The gaps: no model of revenue persistence; no distinction between earnings driven by transferable assets and by the seller personally; no assessment of ranking durability or review velocity; competitive vulnerability is not assessed; and the realised outcomes of past acquisitions are not fed back into the next underwriting.

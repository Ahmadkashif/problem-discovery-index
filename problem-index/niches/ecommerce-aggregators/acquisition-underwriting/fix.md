# Diligence at the Speed of an Auction

**Niche:** [[niches/ecommerce-aggregators/acquisition-underwriting/profile|Acquisition Underwriting]]
**Industry:** [[industries/ecommerce-aggregators|Ecommerce Aggregators]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The analysis that would predict whether a brand's revenue survives takes weeks, the auction gives days, and the deal goes to whoever underwrites fastest rather than best.
**Tags:** #evaluation-metrics #automation #confidence-intervals #descriptive-statistics #revenue-impact #workflow-orchestration #quick-win #hypothesis-testing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to predict whether an acquired brand's revenue survives the change of ownership — and whoever does that takes the market, because the sector's contraction was the consequence of that prediction being made badly at scale.

## The Problem
A broker runs a competitive process with a one-week diligence window. The analysis that would establish whether the brand's ranking is durable, whether its reviews were solicited in ways that stopped, whether its supplier pricing is transferable, and whether three competitors entered the category last quarter takes a skilled person three weeks. The acquirer does what fits in the week — verify the financials, check the listings, ask the seller some questions — and bids. The competitive structure of the market selects for speed, which selects against accuracy, and every participant knows it and bids anyway.

## Why It's Still Broken
Brokers design processes for speed because speed serves the seller. Declining to bid on an unanalysable deal means declining most deals, which is unacceptable to a firm deploying a fund on a schedule. The analysis is manual, which is why it takes three weeks. And the consequences arrive after the fund has been deployed.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Make the analysis fast enough to fit the window. Automate the marketplace analysis — ranking history, review velocity and pattern, competitive entry, price and promotion dependence, listing health — so what took three weeks takes a day, which is the fix and it is an engineering exercise rather than a research one. Pre-screen the market continuously rather than starting when a deal appears, since the categories and the competitive landscape can be analysed before any specific target is known. Maintain a standing category view, so when a brand appears the acquirer already knows the category's dynamics and only needs the brand-specific work. Produce a preliminary underwriting within hours of a teaser, which changes which deals get the scarce analyst time. Score the deal's analysability and decline the ones where the window does not permit the work, which is a discipline rather than a tool and is what stops the fast-not-accurate selection. Structure the offer to carry the uncertainty — earn-outs, seller transition periods, supplier introductions as conditions — since the diligence gap can be priced into terms as well as into the multiple. Track which deals were won and lost against the model's price, so the firm learns whether its discipline is costing it good deals or saving it bad ones. And publish nothing, since this is the one capability worth keeping quiet about.

## Who Feels the Pain
Acquirers bidding on analysis they know is incomplete; the capital behind them; and the sellers whose good businesses are valued by the same shallow process as the bad ones.

## Impact If Fixed
The competitive structure selects for speed, which selects against accuracy, and every participant knows it. Automating the marketplace analysis turns three weeks into a day, and maintaining a standing category view means only the brand-specific work happens inside the auction window.

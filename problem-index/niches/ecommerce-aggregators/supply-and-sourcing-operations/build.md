# The Synergy Nobody Can Locate

**Niche:** [[niches/ecommerce-aggregators/supply-and-sourcing-operations/profile|Supply & Sourcing Operations]]
**Industry:** [[industries/ecommerce-aggregators|Ecommerce Aggregators]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Consolidated purchasing is the model's headline synergy and delivering it requires knowing which of forty brands' products can be bought together, which is a question the catalogues cannot answer.
**Tags:** #k-nearest-neighbors #k-means-clustering #convex-optimization #revenue-impact #evaluation-metrics #data-integration #confidence-intervals #descriptive-statistics
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this sub-niche is fighting to deliver a lower landed cost across products that share nothing — and whoever does that proves the thesis, because supply consolidation is the synergy the whole model was underwritten on.

## The Problem
An aggregator owns forty brands with eleven hundred products. Somewhere in there are groups of products made from similar materials by similar processes at similar volumes, which one supplier could produce for all of them at a better price. Nobody knows which groups, because the products are described in forty different vocabularies inherited from forty different sellers, with specifications in supplier emails, dimensions in inconsistent units, and materials named informally. The synergy that justified the acquisition prices is real and cannot be located, so what gets consolidated is whatever a sourcing manager happens to notice.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The catalogue normalisation this requires is unglamorous and was not budgeted, since the synergy was assumed to follow from ownership. Product specifications live in emails and supplier documents rather than in a system. The sourcing function was staffed for relationship management rather than for analysis. And the synergy is claimed in reporting whether or not it is delivered, which removes the pressure to locate it.

## What to Build
Find the consolidation opportunities and price them. Normalise the product catalogue into comparable attributes — material, process, dimensions, volume, certifications — which the catalogue normalisation niche develops and which is the precondition for everything here. Cluster products by manufacturability rather than by category, since two products in unrelated categories made by the same process at the same factory are the consolidation opportunity and category taxonomy actively hides it. Estimate the savings per cluster from volume-price curves, so the opportunity is ranked by value rather than by how obvious it is. Model landed cost per product properly — unit cost, freight, duty, inspection, financing — since savings claimed on unit cost alone frequently disappear once freight and minimum order quantities are counted. Plan inventory across the portfolio rather than per brand, which reduces working capital and smooths the order quantities that make consolidation viable. Report realised savings against claimed synergies, which is the accountability the sector conspicuously lacks. Measure supplier concentration, since consolidation increases it and the resulting single-supplier dependency is the risk the strategy creates. And sequence consolidation to protect the demand side, because a supplier change that alters a product enough for buyers to notice destroys reviews and ranking, which is a larger loss than the saving.

## Target Customer
Aggregator sourcing functions, operating leadership, and the investors who underwrote the synergy.

## Impact If Built
The synergy is real, was paid for, and cannot be located because forty catalogues describe products in forty vocabularies. Clustering by manufacturability rather than by category is what finds it, and landed cost modelling is what stops a claimed saving disappearing into freight.

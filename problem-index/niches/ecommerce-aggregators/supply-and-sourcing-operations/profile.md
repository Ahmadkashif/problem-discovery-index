# Supply & Sourcing Operations

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/ecommerce-aggregators|Ecommerce Aggregators]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this sub-niche is fighting to deliver a lower landed cost across products that share nothing — and whoever does that proves the thesis, because supply consolidation is the synergy the whole model was underwritten on.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$220M US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~13% of category operating spend
**Digital Adoption:** Very Low — relationships and spreadsheets
**Target Buyer:** Sourcing and supply chain staff
**Automation Potential:** High — consolidation analysis and planning are both mechanisable

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Supply consolidation is the synergy the aggregator model was sold on: buy forty brands, combine their purchasing, negotiate better terms, reduce landed cost. Delivering it means finding where two brands' products are similar enough that one supplier can make both, which is hard across forty catalogues that describe products inconsistently, and then negotiating against a supplier who previously had a personal relationship with a seller who is now gone. The contest is real, the payoff is the model's justification, and most operators run it on spreadsheets and relationships while claiming it as a completed synergy in their reporting.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Spreadsheets, supplier relationships inherited or rebuilt, freight forwarders, and enterprise systems at the largest operators only. The gaps: no comparable product view across brands, so consolidation opportunities are invisible; no landed cost model per product, so savings are claimed rather than measured; inventory planning is per brand rather than across the portfolio; supplier concentration risk is unmeasured; and the supplier relationship the previous owner had is treated as transferred when it frequently was not.

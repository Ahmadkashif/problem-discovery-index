# Two Deals in the Same Category

**Niche:** [[niches/creator-talent-agencies/contract-and-obligation-administration/profile|Contract & Obligation Administration]]
**Industry:** [[industries/creator-talent-agencies|Creator Talent Agencies]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The creator signed a six-month category exclusivity in March and a competing brand deal in July, and nobody checked.
**Tags:** #quick-win #compliance #automation #data-integration #evaluation-metrics #workflow-orchestration #confidence-intervals #descriptive-statistics
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to track hundreds of live obligations written in prose across a roster — and whoever turns bespoke deal terms into monitored commitments stops the breaches that are currently the ordinary state.

## The Problem
Exclusivity terms are common, are category-defined, run for months, and are checked by a manager remembering what they signed. Across a roster of forty creators, several managers and hundreds of deals, the remembering fails. The breach is discovered by the brand, who are entitled to be upset, and is resolved by apology, a discount or a cancelled deal — and the agency's reputation with a brand it wanted to keep is damaged over an administrative failure.

## Why It's Still Broken
Exclusivity lives in a clause in a PDF, so the check depends on whether someone recalls reading it — a constraint that exists only in prose cannot be enforced by a system. Category definitions vary by contract and are ambiguous. No deal pipeline checks against live obligations. And the failures are settled quietly, so the frequency is never visible.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Check before signing, automatically. Extract exclusivity terms into a structured register with creator, category and dates, which is the fix and makes the check possible at all. Run the check at the point a new deal is proposed rather than at signing, since discovering it earlier saves the negotiation. Normalise category definitions into a usable taxonomy, because vague categories are where the disputes come from. Show the manager the creator's live exclusivity commitments when they open a new deal, as that single view prevents most of it. Alert before an exclusivity expires, since the expiry is a selling opportunity nobody tracks. Flag ambiguous category language at signing, so it is negotiated rather than discovered. Maintain the register across the whole roster, as a manager sees their creators and the conflicts arise firm-wide. Record breaches and near misses, which will show the frequency and justify the work. Give the creator visibility of their own commitments, because they are usually the one who accidentally breaches. And make the check a required step, since a voluntary one will be skipped under deadline.

## Who Feels the Pain
Creators in breach of terms they did not remember; brands whose exclusivity was sold twice; managers apologising for an administrative failure; and agencies losing relationships over something a register would have prevented.

## Impact If Fixed
A constraint that exists only in prose cannot be enforced by a system, so exclusivity depends on recall across hundreds of deals. A structured register checked when a new deal is proposed prevents the breach before the negotiation starts.

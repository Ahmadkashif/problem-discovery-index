# The Manager Left and Took the Prices

**Niche:** [[niches/creator-talent-agencies/deal-pricing-and-market-data/profile|Deal Pricing & Market Data]]
**Industry:** [[industries/creator-talent-agencies|Creator Talent Agencies]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Nobody at the agency knows what that brand paid last year because the person who negotiated it works somewhere else now.
**Tags:** #quick-win #data-integration #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #revenue-impact #automation #worker-facing #large-language-models
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn a year of signed deals into a pricing model rather than a folder of PDFs — and whoever structures it negotiates from evidence while everyone else negotiates from memory.

## The Problem
A brand comes back for a second campaign. The manager who handled the first has left. The contract is in a shared drive, the fee is in the CRM, and everything else — what was negotiated away, what the brand pushed on, what they actually used, whether they paid on time, what the relationship was like — is gone. The new manager starts from nothing against a brand that has complete records, and the agency's second deal with the same brand is priced worse than its first.

## Why It's Still Broken
Deal knowledge accumulates in the person who did the deal, so the firm's memory is its staff — a business that never wrote anything down loses its knowledge at the rate its people leave. The CRM records a value and a date. Handover is a conversation. And nobody has noticed the pattern because each instance looks like an individual gap.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Record the deal, not just the number. Capture the key terms in structured fields at signing, which is the fix and takes minutes at a moment when everything is fresh. Record what was conceded and what was pushed on, since that is the negotiation knowledge and is lost first. Note the brand's behaviour — approval rounds, payment timeliness, scope changes — as it is the most useful thing to know before the next negotiation. Extract terms retrospectively from past contracts, because years of them exist and the extraction is now feasible. Attach a short post-deal note, which is the cheapest possible knowledge capture and is currently nobody's habit. Make handover a structured transfer rather than a conversation, so a departure is survivable. Show the brand's full history when a new negotiation opens, which is what makes all of this pay. Report pricing variance across managers, since it will reveal who is underpricing and by how much. Keep the record even when a relationship ends, as brands return. And make it a condition of closing a deal, because voluntary documentation does not happen.

## Who Feels the Pain
Managers negotiating blind against a brand with records; agencies pricing their second deal worse than their first; creators represented by someone with no history; and firms whose institutional knowledge is a headcount.

## Impact If Fixed
A business that never wrote anything down loses its knowledge at the rate its people leave. Capturing structured terms and a post-deal note at signing takes minutes and is the difference between a firm with a memory and one with staff.

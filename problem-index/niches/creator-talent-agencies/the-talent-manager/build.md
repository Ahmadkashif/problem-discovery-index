# A Role With Boundaries

**Niche:** [[niches/creator-talent-agencies/the-talent-manager/profile|The Talent Manager]]
**Industry:** [[industries/creator-talent-agencies|Creator Talent Agencies]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** The relationship is the product, so the product is someone's availability, and nothing in the business acknowledges that.
**Tags:** #worker-facing #workflow-orchestration #automation #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #large-language-models #confidence-intervals #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to make a role survivable when the relationship is the product and being unavailable is a professional failure — and whoever does it keeps the people the agency's revenue is attached to.

## The Problem
The manager handles negotiations, contracts, brand relationships, creative direction, career strategy, payment chasing, crises and the daily anxieties of people whose income is volatile and public. There is no distinction between what requires them and what merely arrives at them. They carry a small number of creators because the role cannot scale, the agency's revenue is tied to relationships they personally hold, and the role's characteristic ending is a manager who leaves the industry taking the relationships with them.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The manager's availability is the service, so anything that reduces it looks like reducing the service — a business that sells a person's attention does not build the thing that rations it. The administrative load is invisible because it is mixed with the relationship work. Agencies compete on service level. And nobody measures where a manager's week goes.

## What to Build
Separate the relationship from the administration. Measure where the manager's time actually goes, which is the core and is the first honest picture anyone will have of the role. Take the administration — contracts, chasing, scheduling, reporting, invoicing — off them entirely, since it is most of the volume and none of the value. Distinguish what requires the manager from what requires the agency, so a creator's question about a payment does not have to reach the person negotiating their next deal. Provide an escalation path for the genuine crises, as those arrive rarely and are handled alone. Support a second point of contact per creator, which is both continuity insurance and a boundary. Set response expectations explicitly rather than leaving availability unbounded, because an unstated standard defaults to immediate. Capture the relationship knowledge so a departure is survivable, which serves the agency and lets the manager take leave. Share the load across the team for the routine, keeping the relationship personal. Track manager caseload against revenue and attention, so overload is visible before it is a resignation. And measure manager retention as a business metric, since it is the asset most directly tied to revenue.

## Target Customer
Talent leadership and managers, agency owners whose revenue is relationship-held, creators, and talent management platform vendors.

## Impact If Built
A business that sells a person's attention does not build the thing that rations it, so the role has no boundaries. Measuring where the week goes and removing the administration is what separates the relationship the creator is paying for from the volume that is destroying the manager.

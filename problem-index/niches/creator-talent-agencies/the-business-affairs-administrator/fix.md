# Reconciling From a PDF

**Niche:** [[niches/creator-talent-agencies/the-business-affairs-administrator/profile|The Business Affairs Administrator]]
**Industry:** [[industries/creator-talent-agencies|Creator Talent Agencies]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A payment arrives for an amount that does not match anything, and finding out which deal it is takes an hour.
**Tags:** #quick-win #data-integration #automation #evaluation-metrics #revenue-impact #descriptive-statistics #worker-facing #workflow-orchestration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to stop one person reconciling hundreds of deals whose terms live in PDFs — and whoever gives them structured data turns an impossible job into a manageable one.

## The Problem
A brand pays. The amount is net of something, or covers two deals, or is a partial payment against a deliverable that was rescheduled. Matching it means opening contracts to check the fee, the payment terms and any deductions, cross-referencing the delivery record, and often emailing the brand. This happens continuously, each instance takes real time, and the creator waiting for their share is told the payment is still being processed.

## Why It's Still Broken
Payments arrive into accounting and deals live in documents, so the match has to be made by a person who can read both — a reconciliation between two systems that share no key will always be manual. Remittance information is poor. Fees are not structured anywhere machine-readable. And the time cost is absorbed by one person.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Give the payment something to match against. Record the expected amount and date for every deal in a structured field, which is the fix and is what makes automatic matching possible. Match incoming payments automatically on amount, reference and timing, since most will match cleanly once the expectation exists. Flag the unmatched for investigation rather than reviewing everything, which is where the time should go. Ask brands for a usable reference on payment, as many will and nobody asks. Handle the partial and combined payment explicitly, because those are the cases that consume the hours. Record deductions and their reasons, since unexplained shortfalls are currently chased case by case. Trigger the creator's share automatically once a payment is matched, as that is the delay they experience. Show the creator their own payment status, which removes a large share of the questions. Report the unmatched backlog, so the problem is visible rather than absorbed. And reconcile continuously rather than at month end, since the backlog is what makes each item harder.

## Who Feels the Pain
Administrators spending hours on matching; creators waiting for money that has arrived; managers fielding payment questions; and agencies whose cash position is unclear because receipts are unallocated.

## Impact If Fixed
A reconciliation between two systems that share no key will always be manual. Recording the expected amount and date per deal gives the payment something to match against and turns an hour of investigation into an exception.

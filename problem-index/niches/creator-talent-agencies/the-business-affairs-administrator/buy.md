## The Problem
Order-to-cash is a solved operational pattern: an order record carries the agreed terms, delivery is recorded against it, invoicing triggers from delivery, payment reconciles to the invoice, and any question about the state of an order is answered from one place. Every business that sells and delivers has some version of it. Agencies sell and deliver hundreds of bespoke engagements a year and have no order record at all.

## What Already Exists
Order-to-cash process design; order records carrying terms; delivery confirmation and milestone billing; invoice-to-payment reconciliation; and exception and dispute handling.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a bespoke creative engagement with a three-party structure. It requires: (1) terms that differ on every deal rather than line items from a catalogue, so the order record must be flexible enough for prose-derived obligations — this is the substantive difference; (2) three parties — brand, agency, creator — with obligations flowing in several directions, where order-to-cash assumes two; (3) delivery that is creative work subject to approval rounds rather than a shipment; (4) commission and creator payment splitting every receipt, which adds a distribution step; and (5) a firm too small for an enterprise system and too complex for a simple one.

## Target Customer
Operations and finance leadership, administrators, creators awaiting payment, and agency management software vendors.

## Impact If Solved
Order-to-cash is a solved pattern and this business has no order record. Making it flexible enough for prose-derived obligations, across three parties with a split receipt, is what an agency version requires.

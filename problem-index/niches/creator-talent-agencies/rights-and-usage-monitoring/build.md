# Watching What Is Still Running

**Niche:** [[niches/creator-talent-agencies/rights-and-usage-monitoring/profile|Rights & Usage Monitoring]]
**Industry:** [[industries/creator-talent-agencies|Creator Talent Agencies]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** The licence expired in March and the advertisement is still running, and the only way anyone finds out is by seeing it.
**Tags:** #contrastive-learning #object-detection #compliance #automation #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #revenue-impact #data-integration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to know whether a brand is still running a creator's content after the licence expired — and whoever monitors it turns an unbilled overrun into either a renewal or a fee.

## The Problem
A deal grants a brand the right to run a creator's content as paid media for ninety days in two territories. After ninety days the right ends. Whether it actually stops depends on whether someone at the brand remembers to turn off a campaign, and whether anyone at the agency checks. Nobody checks, because checking means monitoring paid media placements across several platforms and territories against a date buried in a contract. The overrun is worth money and is usually discovered by a creator spotting their own face.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Enforcement requires monitoring the counterparty's activity, so the agency would have to look at something outside its own systems — a party that cannot see the breach has no reason to build a register of the rights that were breached. Rights are prose in contracts. The overruns are individually modest. And raising one risks the relationship, so even known cases are sometimes let go.

## What to Build
Build the register and watch for the overrun. Maintain a structured rights register with dates, platforms and territories from every deal, which is the core and is the precondition for any monitoring at all. Monitor where a creator's content is appearing as paid media using the platforms' own ad transparency surfaces, which are public and are not being used for this. Match detected placements against the register and flag expiries, since the comparison is mechanical once both sides exist. Alert before expiry so the conversation is a renewal rather than a breach, which is both more valuable and better for the relationship. Track whitelisting spend against agreed caps, as that is another commonly exceeded term nobody watches. Quantify the unbilled usage, which will surprise everyone and is the business case. Handle the organic reuse as well as the paid, since content reposted beyond its term is common. Give the manager a commercial framing rather than a legal one, because a renewal offer lands better than an accusation. Record outcomes so the pattern by brand is visible, as repeat overruns are concentrated. And price usage properly in future deals once the real duration is known, since brands are effectively buying longer terms than they pay for.

## Target Customer
Business affairs leadership, managers and creators, brands who would rather renew than breach, and rights monitoring vendors.

## Impact If Built
A party that cannot see the breach has no reason to build a register of the rights being breached. Public ad transparency surfaces make detection possible, and an alert before expiry converts an unbilled overrun into a renewal conversation.

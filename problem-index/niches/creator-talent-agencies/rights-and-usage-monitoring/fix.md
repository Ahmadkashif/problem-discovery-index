# The Advert That Never Stopped

**Niche:** [[niches/creator-talent-agencies/rights-and-usage-monitoring/profile|Rights & Usage Monitoring]]
**Industry:** [[industries/creator-talent-agencies|Creator Talent Agencies]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The creator sees their own face in an advertisement eight months after the licence ended and tells their manager.
**Tags:** #quick-win #compliance #automation #evaluation-metrics #revenue-impact #descriptive-statistics #worker-facing #data-integration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to know whether a brand is still running a creator's content after the licence expired — and whoever monitors it turns an unbilled overrun into either a renewal or a fee.

## The Problem
The discovery mechanism is the creator noticing. They see the advertisement, or a friend does, and message their manager. The manager checks the contract, finds the licence expired months ago, and is now in an awkward conversation with a brand they want more work from, about money that should have been billed at the time. The creator, meanwhile, has learned that nobody was watching on their behalf.

## Why It's Still Broken
Nobody owns expiry, so the date passes and no process wakes up — a term with an end date and no alarm is a term that ends only if someone remembers. The rights are in a contract. Raising it late is awkward, which discourages raising it at all. And the aggregate value is never computed, so the effort is never justified.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Diarise the expiry and ask before it passes. Extract every usage expiry date into a calendar with an owner, which is the fix and is the minimum viable version of the whole problem. Contact the brand two weeks before expiry to ask whether they want to extend, since that converts an awkward breach into a renewal and is a revenue opportunity currently ignored. Confirm in writing that usage has ceased, as the confirmation is cheap and is the record if it has not. Ask the creator to flag sightings through a structured route rather than a message, so the reports accumulate. Check the platforms' ad transparency surfaces for the largest deals at expiry, which is a few minutes per deal and catches the material cases. Track overruns by brand, because they will be concentrated and the pattern changes how those deals are priced. Quantify the unbilled value, which is the case for doing any of this. Handle it commercially with a renewal offer rather than a claim, since the relationship is worth more than the fee. Include an overrun clause with an agreed rate in future contracts, so the conversation is pre-negotiated. And tell the creator what was found, because knowing someone is watching is a substantial part of what they are paying the agency for.

## Who Feels the Pain
Creators whose likeness runs unpaid; managers in an avoidable awkward conversation; agencies leaving fees uncollected; and brands who would have renewed if anyone had asked.

## Impact If Fixed
A term with an end date and no alarm ends only if someone remembers. Diarising every expiry and asking two weeks before turns a late awkward claim into a renewal conversation with revenue attached.

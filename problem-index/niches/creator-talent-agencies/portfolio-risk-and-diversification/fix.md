# The Whole Roster Is on One Platform

**Niche:** [[niches/creator-talent-agencies/portfolio-risk-and-diversification/profile|Portfolio Risk & Diversification]]
**Industry:** [[industries/creator-talent-agencies|Creator Talent Agencies]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A ranking change lands and every revenue forecast in the business moves at once, and nobody had written down that this could happen.
**Tags:** #quick-win #descriptive-statistics #revenue-impact #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #compliance #worker-facing #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to stop a roster being one undiversified bet on a distribution algorithm — and whoever measures and manages the correlated exposure runs an agency that survives a platform change rather than one that discovers it.

## The Problem
The agency knows, in a general way, that its creators are mostly on one platform. Nobody has written down what proportion of revenue that represents, what a twenty percent reach reduction across those creators would do to the year, or which creators would be affected worst. So when it happens — and it periodically does — the response is improvised, the forecast is revised downward repeatedly, and the lesson is absorbed as bad luck rather than as an exposure that was always there.

## Why It's Still Broken
Revenue is reported by creator and by deal, so the platform dimension never appears in any view — a reporting structure organised by client cannot show a risk that runs across clients. The exposure is obvious enough to feel known and is never quantified. Nobody owns risk. And the event is infrequent enough to be forgotten between occurrences.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Write the number down. Report revenue by platform, format and category across the roster, which is the fix and is a straightforward regrouping of data the agency already has. Estimate the effect of a material reach reduction across the dominant platform, since a single scenario on a page changes the conversation. Identify the creators who would be worst affected, as they are the ones who need diversification support now. Track the number over time, because it drifts upward silently as the agency signs into its strengths. Report it to leadership monthly and to investors periodically, which is what makes it managed rather than known. Include creator revenue concentration alongside platform concentration, as the two compound. Record what happened in past platform changes, since the agency has the evidence and has never measured the effect. Set a target rather than a limit, as a hard limit is impractical and a stated ambition changes signing conversations. Discuss it with creators, because their own diversification is the mitigation and they benefit from it too. And review after every platform change, which is when the lesson is available and is currently when it is forgotten.

## Who Feels the Pain
Agencies whose forecasts collapse together; creators whose manager is suddenly distracted by a firm-wide problem; investors surprised by correlated revenue moves; and firms that experience the same event repeatedly without learning from it.

## Impact If Fixed
A reporting structure organised by client cannot show a risk that runs across clients, so the exposure is felt and never quantified. Regrouping revenue by platform is a straightforward query and produces the number the business has never written down.

# A Portfolio That Is Modelled

**Niche:** [[niches/creator-talent-agencies/roster-strategy/profile|Roster Strategy]]
**Industry:** [[industries/creator-talent-agencies|Creator Talent Agencies]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** The agency holds concentrated exposure to a set of careers with one shared risk factor and selects new positions on a lagging popularity metric.
**Tags:** #survival-analysis #time-series-forecasting #gradient-boosting #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #revenue-impact #optimization-fundamentals #causal-inference
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to build a roster that survives the platform changes that will hit all of it at once — and the contest splits cleanly enough that it is not terminal.

## The Problem
An agency's economics depend on representing creators whose income persists for years. Its signing criterion is current reach, which is the most lagging available indicator and the one every competitor also uses. Its portfolio is concentrated in a handful of careers, all of which depend on the same small number of distribution algorithms, so a single platform change moves the entire book at once. Neither the individual forecast nor the portfolio exposure is modelled, in a business that is structurally a portfolio.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Representation is a relationship business, so selection is framed as judgement about a person rather than as a position in a portfolio — a firm that thinks of itself as relationships does not build the apparatus of an investor. Follower counts are visible and immediate. Careers fail for reasons that feel idiosyncratic even when they are correlated. And signing outcomes are never reviewed as a set.

## What to Build
Forecast the individual and manage the portfolio. Forecast a creator's trajectory from early behavioural and audience signals rather than from reach, which is one half and is what improves the signing decision. Model the roster's exposure — platform concentration, format concentration, category concentration, correlated risk — which is the other half and is what leadership should be managing. Use the agency's own history of signings and outcomes, since that is a real if small dataset and nobody has assembled it. Identify the signals that distinguish a durable creator from a momentary one, as the difference is the entire business and is currently intuition. Measure the correlated exposure explicitly, because a roster of forty creators on one platform is one position and is treated as forty. Diversify deliberately across platform, format and category, which is the only available mitigation and is currently accidental. Review signing decisions against outcomes, so the firm accumulates knowledge rather than anecdotes. Model the value of a representation agreement properly, since terms are set without any valuation. Support the decision to decline, as the discipline is in what is not signed. And report roster risk to leadership as a standing measure, which does not exist in any agency.

## Target Customer
Agency leadership and signing managers, investors in agencies, creators being represented, and talent management platform vendors.

## Impact If Built
A firm that thinks of itself as relationships does not build the apparatus of an investor, which is why a portfolio business selects on a lagging metric. Forecasting durability and measuring correlated platform exposure are both available from data the agency already generates.

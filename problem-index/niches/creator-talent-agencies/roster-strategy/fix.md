# Signed on a Follower Count

**Niche:** [[niches/creator-talent-agencies/roster-strategy/profile|Roster Strategy]]
**Industry:** [[industries/creator-talent-agencies|Creator Talent Agencies]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The agency signed a creator at the peak of a format that stopped working two quarters later.
**Tags:** #quick-win #descriptive-statistics #time-series-forecasting #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #revenue-impact #worker-facing #gradient-boosting
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to build a roster that survives the platform changes that will hit all of it at once — and the contest splits cleanly enough that it is not terminal.

## The Problem
Signing happens when a creator is visible, which is when they are at or near a peak and when every competing agency is also calling. The criterion is current reach, which says nothing about whether the growth is a format that is working now, an algorithmic tailwind, a single viral moment, or a durable audience relationship. The agency commits years of representation and sometimes an advance, and discovers which it was afterwards.

## Why It's Still Broken
Reach is the number everyone quotes because it is public and comparable, so it became the criterion — a metric that is visible to all parties becomes the shared basis of a negotiation regardless of what it predicts. Competition compresses the decision timeline. Signing outcomes are not reviewed as a set. And the alternative signals require assembling data nobody assembles.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Look at the shape rather than the level. Examine the growth curve rather than the current number, which is the fix and distinguishes a steady build from a spike at a glance. Report the share of reach from a single format or a single piece of content, since concentration in one thing is the clearest fragility signal available. Check audience retention across releases rather than follower totals, because a creator whose audience returns is a different asset from one whose reach is distributed. Look at the creator's behaviour off-platform — owned audience, direct relationships, other surfaces — as that is their diversification and therefore the agency's. Review past signings against outcomes, which nobody does and which will be instructive within an afternoon. Record the reasoning at signing, so the review is possible in future. Check how many competing agencies are calling, since a contested signing at a peak is the classic overpay. Look at the creator's own trajectory expectations, as their plans matter and are rarely discussed analytically. Slow the decision where the terms allow, because speed is the adversary of every one of these checks. And treat the follower count as one input rather than the input.

## Who Feels the Pain
Agencies carrying representation costs on careers that ended; creators signed at a peak and unable to sustain it; managers holding rosters they cannot service; and firms whose selection process never improves.

## Impact If Fixed
A metric visible to all parties becomes the shared basis of a negotiation regardless of what it predicts. Looking at the growth shape, the format concentration and the audience retention distinguishes a durable creator from a moment, using public data.

# Portfolio Construction From Investment

**Industry:** [[industries/creator-talent-agencies|Creator Talent Agencies]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Investment management builds portfolios against measured risk factors, and an agency holds forty correlated positions and calls it a roster.

**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to build a roster that survives the platform changes that will hit all of it at once — and the contest splits cleanly enough that it is not terminal.

## The Problem
Constructing a portfolio of risky positions is a solved discipline: identify the risk factors, measure exposure to each, diversify across uncorrelated sources, size positions against conviction and risk, and review the selection process against outcomes. Venture and private investment do it with assets as illiquid and idiosyncratic as a creator career. Agencies hold exactly such a portfolio and manage it as a list of relationships.

## What Already Exists
Factor exposure measurement; diversification and correlation analysis; position sizing frameworks; selection process review and attribution; and portfolio-level risk reporting.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to positions that are people with agency of their own. It requires: (1) positions that cannot be sold or hedged, so diversification must happen at selection and the only other lever is helping the creator diversify their own risk — this is the substantive difference; (2) a dominant risk factor that is a small number of private distribution algorithms, which is more concentrated than any market factor; (3) returns that depend on the agency's own work, so the position's performance is partly endogenous; (4) a relationship obligation that makes a purely portfolio-driven decision inappropriate; and (5) small numbers, where a roster of forty makes statistical reasoning delicate.

## Target Customer
Agency leadership, investors in agencies, creators, and portfolio analytics vendors.

## Impact If Solved
The discipline handles illiquid idiosyncratic portfolios routinely and agencies do not use it. The genuinely new constraint is that positions cannot be sold, which makes selection and helping the creator diversify the only available levers.

# Ninety Days Is Normal Here

**Niche:** [[niches/creator-talent-agencies/collections-and-payment-operations/profile|Collections & Payment Operations]]
**Industry:** [[industries/creator-talent-agencies|Creator Talent Agencies]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The creator asks where their money is and the honest answer is that nobody has chased it for three weeks.
**Tags:** #quick-win #workflow-orchestration #automation #revenue-impact #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #worker-facing #confidence-intervals
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to collect money already earned without a person spending their month on it — and whoever automates the chase pays creators on time, which is the thing they most notice.

## The Problem
Chasing is manual, so it happens when someone has time, which is not weekly. An invoice sits for three weeks without a reminder, then gets one, then sits again. The creator asks, is told it is being chased, and asks again a fortnight later. The agency's response to the creator is not dishonest and is not accurate either, and the cumulative effect is a creator who believes their agency is not looking after them.

## Why It's Still Broken
Chasing has no schedule, so it competes with everything else and loses — a task with no due date is done when nothing with a due date is pending. Nobody tracks the last contact per invoice. The creator's question is answered from memory. And the agency is embarrassed to escalate.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Put the chase on a schedule and tell the creator the truth. Set an automatic reminder schedule per invoice, which is the fix and is the difference between chasing and remembering to chase. Record the last contact and the response on every invoice, since that is what makes the creator's question answerable. Show the creator the actual status — invoiced, chased, queried, promised — rather than a reassurance, because specificity is what restores trust. Report the ageing list weekly, as it is short and nobody looks at it. Escalate on a defined schedule rather than on frustration, so escalation is a process step and not a confrontation. Identify the invoices with a query blocking them, since those are not late so much as stuck and need a different action. Ask the brand's accounts payable contact directly rather than the marketing contact, as the marketing contact cannot pay anyone. Pay the creator on a schedule where cash allows, which removes the question entirely. Track days to payment by brand, so the pattern is visible. And tell creators the expected timing at deal signing, because an expectation set in advance is not a grievance.

## Who Feels the Pain
Creators who believe their agency is not chasing; administrators answering the same question; agencies whose relationships degrade over a cash timing issue; and finance teams with an unpredictable cash position.

## Impact If Fixed
A task with no due date is done when nothing with a due date is pending, so chasing happens in gaps. An automatic reminder schedule with the contact history recorded turns an unanswerable creator question into a status.

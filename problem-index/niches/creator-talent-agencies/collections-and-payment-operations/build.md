# Getting Paid Without a Person Chasing

**Niche:** [[niches/creator-talent-agencies/collections-and-payment-operations/profile|Collections & Payment Operations]]
**Industry:** [[industries/creator-talent-agencies|Creator Talent Agencies]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A person spends their month asking large companies for money those companies agreed to pay.
**Tags:** #workflow-orchestration #automation #gradient-boosting #evaluation-metrics #revenue-impact #confidence-intervals #descriptive-statistics #data-integration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to collect money already earned without a person spending their month on it — and whoever automates the chase pays creators on time, which is the thing they most notice.

## The Problem
The work is delivered, the invoice is raised, and then nothing happens for sixty days. The agency chases by email. The brand's accounts payable process has its own logic, invoices are queried for reasons nobody anticipated, and the payment eventually arrives. Meanwhile the creator, whose income this is, has been waiting and has been told repeatedly that it is coming. The agency's relationship with its own client is damaged by a process failure at a third party.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Collections in a small firm is a task rather than a function, so it is done by whoever has capacity in whatever way they know — a job assigned to a person rather than to a process inherits none of the discipline of a receivables operation. The agency has little leverage over large brands. Late payment is normalised industry-wide. And nobody has quantified what it costs in working capital and in creator relationships.

## What to Build
Run it as a receivables operation. Automate the dunning sequence with escalating contact, which is the core and is a solved pattern the industry simply does not apply. Track payment terms and behaviour per brand, since late payment is concentrated in identifiable counterparties and is currently rediscovered each time. Predict which invoices will be late from brand history, so effort is directed rather than uniform. Raise invoices correctly and promptly against the deal terms, because a meaningful share of delay is the agency's own invoicing error or lateness. Understand each brand's payables process — portal, purchase order requirement, approval chain — as failing to satisfy it is a common cause of an invoice sitting unpaid. Escalate to the commercial relationship rather than staying in accounts, since a manager mentioning it moves things that email does not. Pay the creator on a predictable schedule rather than on receipt where the agency can bear it, as that single change transforms the creator's experience. Report days sales outstanding by brand, which will name the problem counterparties. Price late payment into future deals with repeat offenders, because it is a real cost currently absorbed. And quantify the working capital cost, which is what justifies the whole effort.

## Target Customer
Finance and operations leadership, administrators doing the chasing, creators waiting to be paid, and receivables automation vendors.

## Impact If Built
A job assigned to a person rather than to a process inherits none of the discipline of a receivables operation. Automated dunning with brand-level behaviour tracking is a solved pattern, and paying creators predictably is the part they actually notice.

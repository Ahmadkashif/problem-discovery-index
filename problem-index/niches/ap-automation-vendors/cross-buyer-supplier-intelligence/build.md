# The Supplier Seen From Every Side

**Niche:** [[niches/ap-automation-vendors/cross-buyer-supplier-intelligence/profile|Cross-Buyer Supplier Intelligence]]
**Industry:** [[industries/ap-automation-vendors|AP Automation Vendors]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Hundreds of buyers' experience of the same supplier sits in one database and produces nothing but invoice processing.
**Tags:** #graph-theory #gradient-boosting #time-series-forecasting #change-point-detection #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #revenue-impact #k-means-clustering
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn the sight of one supplier invoicing hundreds of buyers into an assessment of that supplier nobody else can produce — and whoever builds it holds the most complete record of business-to-business commerce outside a bank.

## The Problem
A single buyer knows what they pay a supplier, when they pay, and whether anything has gone wrong. The platform knows that for hundreds of buyers of the same supplier, continuously. From that it could say whether a price is competitive, whether a supplier's invoicing behaviour has changed in ways that precede distress, whether a bank detail change is genuine, and whether a buyer's dependency on a supplier is riskier than they realise. None of it is assembled.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The data was collected to process invoices, so its aggregate value was never the point of holding it — nobody asked what the network could see that no tenant could. Cross-customer use requires governance nobody wrote. The commercial model is per-buyer software and payments, where intelligence is a new line of business. And the supplier-side view was never anyone's remit.

## What to Build
Aggregate the network's view of each supplier. Build a supplier profile from cross-buyer invoice and payment data, which is the core and is the asset that makes several other problems in this category solvable. Compute price dispersion for comparable goods and services, since buyers consistently want to know whether they are paying market and nobody can tell them. Detect supplier distress from invoicing and payment behaviour changes, because a supplier invoicing earlier, changing terms or chasing harder is signalling something visible only in aggregate. Verify bank details against what other buyers pay, which is the payment verification problem and is solved by this asset. Assess buyer dependency and supplier concentration, as a supplier who is a single point of failure is identifiable and rarely identified. Flag the supplier whose behaviour changes across many buyers at once, since that is a strong signal of a real event. Establish governance and consent explicitly, because this is the binding constraint and building without it is how the asset gets destroyed. Aggregate and anonymise so no buyer's specific terms are exposed, which is what makes participation acceptable. Deliver value back to contributors first, since that is the durable arrangement. And validate the signals against known outcomes, so the intelligence is calibrated rather than plausible.

## Target Customer
Data and commercial leadership, procurement and finance functions, credit insurers and trade credit providers, and business data vendors with no invoice-level view.

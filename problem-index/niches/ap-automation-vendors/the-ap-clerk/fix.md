# Nobody Replies to the Third Email

**Niche:** [[niches/ap-automation-vendors/the-ap-clerk/profile|The AP Clerk]]
**Industry:** [[industries/ap-automation-vendors|AP Automation Vendors]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The clerk has sent three emails about the same invoice and the approver has ignored all of them with no consequence.
**Tags:** #worker-facing #quick-win #workflow-orchestration #automation #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #compliance
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to stop the clerk's job being email to people who do not reply — and whoever gets the answer without the chase changes what the role is.

## The Problem
An invoice waits on an approval. The clerk emails, waits, emails again, waits, emails a third time. The approver is busy, the request is one of many, and nothing happens if they ignore it. The supplier is paid late, the clerk is blamed, and the approver experiences no consequence and often no visibility. The clerk has no authority to escalate and no report showing their manager where the delays actually originate.

## Why It's Still Broken
The approval request arrives as an email, so it competes with everything else in an inbox and has no standing — a request with no deadline and no visibility is rationally deprioritised by its recipient. Escalation requires organisational backing the clerk does not have. Late payment is attributed to AP. And nobody reports approval latency by approver.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Make the delay visible and give escalation teeth. Report approval latency by approver and by department, which is the fix and moves the accountability to where the delay is. Show each approver their own pending queue and ageing, since most are simply unaware. Escalate automatically to a manager after a defined period, with the policy agreed in advance so the clerk is not the one escalating. Make approving possible in one action from email or chat, because response rates are driven by effort more than by intent. Show the consequence in the request — this supplier will be paid late, this discount will be lost — as a specific consequence gets a response and a generic reminder does not. Set and publish an approval service level, so there is a standard to be measured against. Route to a delegate when the approver is away, which is a common and entirely avoidable cause of delay. Batch requests to each approver rather than sending them individually, since one message with six items outperforms six messages. Report late payments by root cause, as that reframes the conversation from AP performance to approval performance. And give the clerk a report they can show their manager, because they currently carry the blame without evidence.

## Who Feels the Pain
Clerks chasing without authority; suppliers paid late for internal reasons; approvers unaware of what waits on them; and finance leaders attributing delay to the wrong function.

## Impact If Fixed
An approval request arriving as an email competes with everything else and has no standing, so ignoring it is rational. Reporting latency by approver and escalating on an agreed policy moves the accountability to where the delay actually is.

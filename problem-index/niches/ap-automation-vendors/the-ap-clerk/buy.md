# Case Management and Chase Automation

**Niche:** [[niches/ap-automation-vendors/the-ap-clerk/profile|The AP Clerk]]
**Industry:** [[industries/ap-automation-vendors|AP Automation Vendors]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Collections and service management built systematic chase operations with cadences, escalation and measured response, and AP chases by writing individual emails.
**Tags:** #workflow-orchestration #automation #worker-facing #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #large-language-models #confidence-intervals #data-integration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to stop the clerk's job being email to people who do not reply — and whoever gets the answer without the chase changes what the role is.

## The Problem
Getting a response from someone who owes you something is a solved operational problem. Collections runs contact strategies with cadence, channel mix, escalation paths and measured response rates. Service management runs ticket queues with service levels, ownership and escalation. AP chasing has the same structure — a pending item, a person who must act, a consequence for delay — and is executed as individual emails written from scratch.

## What Already Exists
Contact strategy and cadence management; multichannel outreach with response tracking; escalation matrices and service levels; ticketing with ownership and ageing; and response rate measurement and optimisation.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to chasing colleagues rather than debtors or customers. It requires: (1) no leverage over the recipient, since the approver is a colleague who outranks the clerk and has no obligation — which is the substantive difference and is why systematic cadence alone is insufficient; (2) the answer often derivable, so the best outreach is one that does not happen; (3) escalation that must be organisationally sanctioned to mean anything, which is a configuration and a policy question rather than a feature; (4) a consequence that lands on the supplier and the company rather than on the person being chased, which removes the natural incentive; and (5) requests that are questions rather than demands, so the framing and channel differ entirely.

## Target Customer
Product and customer leadership, AP managers, finance leadership setting approval expectations, and service management vendors with no AP presence.

## Impact If Solved
The chase discipline is mature and assumes leverage over the recipient. Adapting it to colleagues — with derivation replacing most outreach and sanctioned escalation behind the rest — is what the AP version needs.

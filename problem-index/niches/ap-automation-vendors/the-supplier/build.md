# The Side of the Transaction Nobody Designed

**Niche:** [[niches/ap-automation-vendors/the-supplier/profile|The Supplier]]
**Industry:** [[industries/ap-automation-vendors|AP Automation Vendors]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** The supplier controls the invoice quality, the bank details and the contact, and the product treats them as a data source.
**Tags:** #workflow-orchestration #data-integration #automation #worker-facing #evaluation-metrics #large-language-models #confidence-intervals #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to make the supplier's side of the transaction worth their cooperation — and whoever does it gets accurate invoices, current details and reachable contacts, which is most of the buyer's problem solved.

## The Problem
Almost everything that goes wrong on the buyer's side originates on the supplier's: an invoice missing a purchase order reference, a name that does not match the master file, a bank detail the supplier changed and did not communicate, a contact who left. The supplier would fix all of it if doing so were easy and if they got something in return. Instead they are handed a portal that serves the buyer's requirements, offers them nothing, and is one of hundreds they are asked to use.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The buyer pays, so the product was designed for the buyer — and a user who is not the customer never gets a design budget. Portals were built to collect rather than to serve. The supplier's cooperation was assumed rather than earned. And nobody measured what supplier-side data quality costs the buyer, so the business case for serving them was never made.

## What to Build
Make the supplier's side genuinely useful. Give the supplier reliable payment status and expected dates, which is the core because it is the only thing they actually want and it is the reason they call. Validate invoices at submission and tell them what is wrong before it becomes a rejection, since submission errors are the largest source of exceptions and are entirely preventable at entry. Let one supplier profile serve many buyers on the platform, as two hundred logins is the reason portals are ignored. Let them maintain their own details, with the verification the payment problem requires, because a supplier-initiated change through an authenticated channel is far stronger evidence than an email. Prefill from the purchase order so the invoice matches by construction rather than by luck. Tell them who to contact and what is blocking payment, since the information exists and withholding it generates the support calls. Offer early payment where the buyer supports it, which is a real benefit and the natural currency for cooperation. Measure supplier satisfaction and portal usage, as both are currently unmeasured and both predict data quality. Design for the small supplier without an accounts receivable team, because that is most of them. And quantify what supplier-side quality is worth to the buyer, which is the argument that funds all of it.

## Target Customer
Network and product leadership, suppliers of every size, buyers whose exception rate is supplier-caused, and accounts receivable vendors on the other side of the same transaction.

## Impact If Built
A user who is not the customer never gets a design budget, so the portal collects rather than serves. Giving suppliers reliable status and submission validation fixes the buyer's exceptions at their source.

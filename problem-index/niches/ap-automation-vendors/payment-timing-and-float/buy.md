# Working Capital Optimisation From Treasury

**Niche:** [[niches/ap-automation-vendors/payment-timing-and-float/profile|Payment Timing & Float]]
**Industry:** [[industries/ap-automation-vendors|AP Automation Vendors]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Corporate treasury optimises working capital as a discipline with cash forecasting and payment timing policy, and it sits above a payables system that pays on a fixed schedule.
**Tags:** #optimization-fundamentals #time-series-forecasting #evaluation-metrics #revenue-impact #confidence-intervals #data-integration #compliance #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to decide when each invoice should be paid by which method, balancing discount capture, working capital and supplier relationship — and whoever optimises it honestly, while float pays for the product, holds the category's central conflict.

## The Problem
Treasury has a developed practice around this: cash forecasting, working capital management, days payable outstanding targets, payment timing policy, and supply chain finance programmes. It operates at the policy level and hands the payables function a target. The payables system then pays on a schedule, and the gap between the policy and the individual payment decision is where the value leaks.

## What Already Exists
Cash forecasting and liquidity management; working capital and days payable outstanding optimisation; supply chain finance and dynamic discounting programmes; payment method cost analysis; and treasury management systems.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is from policy to per-invoice execution. It requires: (1) optimisation at the individual invoice rather than at the aggregate target, which is where discount capture actually happens and is the substantive difference; (2) forecasting from the payables pipeline rather than from historical patterns, which is more accurate and available only in this system; (3) mid-market customers with no treasury function at all, where the practice must be embedded rather than advised; (4) the platform's own float and method economics as a conflict treasury practice never contemplated, requiring disclosure rather than assumption; and (5) supplier relationship as an explicit term, which treasury targets treat as an externality.

## Target Customer
Treasury and payment operations leadership, CFOs of companies without a treasury function, and treasury and working capital vendors.

## Impact If Solved
Treasury sets a target and the payables system pays on a schedule, and the value leaks in between. Optimising per invoice with a pipeline-based forecast is the adaptation, and disclosure of the platform's own economics is what makes it trustworthy.

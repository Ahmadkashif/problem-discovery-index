**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to predict what an unmade title will be worth from a script, a cast, a comparable set and a gap in the catalogue — and whoever forecasts better than instinct changes which nine-figure commitments get made.

## The Problem
Forecasting demand for a product with no sales history is a standard problem in consumer goods, fashion, publishing and games: attribute-based models, analogue selection, pre-launch testing, and portfolio planning that accepts a known hit rate. None of those industries believes their forecasts are precise, and all of them find that a structured forecast outperforms unaided judgement. Content commissioning has the same structure and none of the practice.

## What Already Exists
New product demand forecasting from attributes; analogue and comparable selection methodologies; pre-launch concept and audience testing; portfolio planning under a known hit rate; and forecast accuracy tracking.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a product whose value is subscriber behaviour rather than units sold. It requires: (1) an outcome that is retention and acquisition rather than sales, which depends on the causal valuation and is the binding dependency; (2) creative attributes that are hard to structure, where a consumer good's attributes are physical — this is the substantive difficulty and is where language models genuinely help; (3) a hit-rate portfolio logic the industry already understands intuitively and does not manage explicitly; (4) pre-launch testing that is feasible for concepts and artwork and is barely used; and (5) a commissioning culture that treats forecasts as an affront, which is the adoption problem the other industries did not face as acutely.

## Target Customer
Content and finance leadership, commissioning executives, producers, and forecasting vendors serving consumer goods.

## Impact If Solved
Several industries forecast unmade products from attributes and find it beats unaided judgement even when imprecise. Structuring creative attributes is the hard part and the political reception is the harder one.

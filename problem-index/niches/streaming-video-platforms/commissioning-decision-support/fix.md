# The Comparables Chosen to Support the Pitch

**Niche:** [[niches/streaming-video-platforms/commissioning-decision-support/profile|Commissioning Decision Support]]
**Industry:** [[industries/streaming-video-platforms|Streaming Video Platforms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The comparable titles in the greenlight deck were selected by the person who wants the greenlight.
**Tags:** #quick-win #k-nearest-neighbors #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #revenue-impact #hypothesis-testing #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to predict what an unmade title will be worth from a script, a cast, a comparable set and a gap in the catalogue — and whoever forecasts better than instinct changes which nine-figure commitments get made.

## The Problem
Every commissioning case rests on comparables: this is like those three successful titles. The three were chosen by whoever is advocating, from memory, with the failures omitted. There is no rule for what counts as comparable, no requirement to include the unsuccessful analogues, and no record of whether previous comparable-based arguments turned out to be right. It is the central analytical device in a business spending billions and it has no methodology.

## Why It's Still Broken
The comparable is a rhetorical device in a pitch rather than an analytical one, so selection bias is structural rather than accidental — an argument's supporting evidence chosen by the advocate will always favour the argument. Nobody defined similarity. The failures are not top of mind and are unflattering to mention. And no record connects past comparable arguments to outcomes.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Make the comparable set systematic. Define similarity from structured attributes and generate the comparable set automatically, which is the fix and removes advocate selection entirely. Include the unsuccessful analogues by construction, since their omission is the whole bias and their inclusion changes the conversation immediately. Show the full distribution of outcomes for the comparable set rather than the best three, as the range is the decision-relevant information. Report how many comparable titles existed and how many succeeded, which is a base rate and is never stated. Keep a record of every commissioning case and its comparables, so the device itself can be evaluated over time. Check retrospectively whether comparable-based arguments predicted outcomes, because nobody knows and the answer matters. Require the advocate to state why any excluded close comparable does not apply, which is a light process change with a large effect. Use the platform's own catalogue rather than industry-wide press reports, since internal outcome data is better and is available. Present it alongside rather than instead of the creative case, as the case is not only quantitative. And publish the base rates internally, because a room that knows the hit rate reasons differently.

## Who Feels the Pain
Executives approving on a curated evidence set; commissioners who lose out to better pitches rather than better projects; finance teams with no base rate; and a business whose central analytical device is selected by the advocate.

## Impact If Fixed
An argument's supporting evidence chosen by the advocate will always favour the argument, so comparable selection bias is structural. Generating the comparable set from measured similarity, failures included, changes the greenlight conversation with no modelling at all.

# Niche Analysis — Streaming Video Platforms

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/streaming-video-platforms|Streaming Video Platforms]]

## Niche Selection

These platforms deliver adaptive video globally and personalise catalogues with genuinely sophisticated systems, then spend billions a year on content decided by executives using comparables, relationships and instinct, because nobody can attribute a subscriber to a title. The industry's substitute is hours viewed, which measures consumption rather than causation and systematically overvalues titles watched by loyal subscribers who were never going to leave. That single gap propagates into renewals, licensing and marketing allocation. Around it sit a metadata and localisation operation producing artwork, subtitles and dubs by hand at enormous volume, an advertising business on the ad tiers whose measurement is weaker than the subscription side, and a reliability function whose failures are public and simultaneous. The eight niches below split the category by what competitors actually fight over.

## Why These Niches

Content economics takes the largest share because it is the cost base and the question underneath every decision in the business. Discovery is second because it determines what the catalogue is worth once acquired and is the category's strongest existing capability. The two low-digitized niches are the ones done by hand at volume: artwork, tags, subtitles and dubs across dozens of languages, and an advertising operation whose measurement lags the subscription side. The two underserved audiences are the reviewer checking every asset against a specification by watching it, and the executive committing nine figures on comparables. The two automatable niches are a reliability function whose failures are simultaneous and public, and a subscription lifecycle managed reactively.

## Filter Notes

Seven of the eight level-1 niches are terminal. Content Economics is not: the label names a domain rather than a contest, and writing the contested statement produces two sentences with different winners. Title causal valuation asks what an existing title was worth in subscribers acquired and retained — a causal inference problem over an exceptionally complete viewing record with poor experimental design, where the winner is whoever can defend a number. Commissioning decision support asks what to greenlight before anything exists — a forecasting and portfolio problem with no viewing data at all, worked from scripts, talent, comparables, market gaps and the platform's own audience segments, where the winner is whoever predicts best from the little that exists before production. One is measurement after the fact with abundant data and the other is prediction before the fact with almost none; they need different methods and are used by different people at opposite ends of the content cycle. It therefore decomposes into **Title Causal Valuation** and **Commissioning Decision Support**.

Two candidates were considered and rejected. **Music and spoken audio streaming** shares the subscription and recommendation shape but the contest over it belongs to [[industries/digital-audio-platforms|Digital Audio Platforms]]. **User-generated video** operates on entirely different content economics and is the contest of [[industries/ugc-video-platforms|UGC Video Platforms]].

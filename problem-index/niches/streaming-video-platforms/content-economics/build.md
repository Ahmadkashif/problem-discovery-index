# The Number the Cost Base Depends On

**Niche:** [[niches/streaming-video-platforms/content-economics/profile|Content Economics]]
**Industry:** [[industries/streaming-video-platforms|Streaming Video Platforms]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** The most complete record of media consumption ever assembled has not been used to answer what any title is worth.
**Tags:** #causal-inference #survival-analysis #hypothesis-testing #confidence-intervals #evaluation-metrics #gradient-boosting #revenue-impact #monte-carlo-methods
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to know what a title is worth before and after it exists — and the contest splits cleanly enough that it is not terminal.

## The Problem
Every play, pause, abandonment and rewatch, by individual, over years, sits alongside each subscriber's signup and cancellation decision. From that it should be possible to say which titles caused signups, which prevented cancellations, and which were simply watched by people who were staying anyway. The platform reports hours viewed instead, which credits the popular title watched by committed subscribers and discredits the narrow title holding a segment nobody else serves — and commissioning, renewal and licensing all run on that.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The gap is not primarily technical; it is that the people who commission content have strong reasons to prefer the number remain unknown — a measurement that would constrain a discretionary decision faces resistance from the people who hold that discretion. Experimental design is poor because withholding titles from subscribers is commercially awkward. The causal problem is genuinely hard with observational data. And hours viewed is available and defensible internally.

## What to Build
Answer both halves and change what the money follows. Estimate the causal contribution of existing titles to acquisition and retention, which is one half and is where the data advantage lies. Build a forecasting apparatus for titles that do not yet exist, which is the other half and is a different problem with almost no data. Exploit the natural experiments that already occur — staggered regional releases, licensing windows opening and closing, catalogue removals, marketing variation — since each is an experiment nobody analysed. Design deliberate experiments where possible, as randomised promotion and staggered availability are feasible and would settle far more than any observational method. Model retention contribution over the subscriber's whole tenure rather than at the moment of viewing, because the effect of a title persists and is not captured by a completion rate. Value the narrow title that holds a segment, since the current metric guarantees it is undervalued and its loss is discovered later. Express uncertainty honestly, because a confident wrong valuation applied to a nine-figure decision is worse than the current ignorance. Report valuation by title, genre and segment so allocation can change. Handle the political reality deliberately, as the measurement's adoption is the binding constraint rather than its construction. And validate prospectively, since credibility is what determines whether anyone acts on it.

## Target Customer
Content and data leadership, chief financial officers funding content, studios and licensors negotiating without a valuation, and investors assessing streaming economics.

## Impact If Built
A measurement that would constrain a discretionary decision faces resistance from the people who hold the discretion, which is why the number does not exist. The natural experiments already occurring — staggered releases, catalogue removals, window changes — are unanalysed evidence sitting in the record.

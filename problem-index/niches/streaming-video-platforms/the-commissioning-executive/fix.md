# Nobody Knows Their Own Hit Rate

**Niche:** [[niches/streaming-video-platforms/the-commissioning-executive/profile|The Commissioning Executive]]
**Industry:** [[industries/streaming-video-platforms|Streaming Video Platforms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** An executive has greenlit forty projects over six years and could not tell you how many worked.
**Tags:** #quick-win #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #worker-facing #revenue-impact #hypothesis-testing #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to give the person committing nine figures something better than comparables and instinct — and whoever supports that decision without insulting the judgement it depends on changes how the money is spent.

## The Problem
Decisions are made, projects are produced, titles are released, and the record dissolves. The executive remembers the successes vividly and the failures vaguely, as everyone does. Nobody maintains a list of what each person greenlit, what they said would happen, and what actually happened. The result is that a profession built entirely on judgement has no mechanism by which that judgement can improve, and no basis for knowing whose is better.

## Why It's Still Broken
Recording a decision with its rationale creates accountability nobody asked for, so the record was never kept — a profession whose practitioners are evaluated on relationships and taste does not volunteer for a scoreboard. The outcome measure is contested. People change roles and projects change hands. And the feedback delay is years, which makes the connection feel tenuous even when it is not.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Keep the record, privately first. Log every greenlight with its decision-maker, rationale and expected outcome, which is the fix and costs minutes at the moment of decision. Reconstruct the historical record where possible, since several years of decisions and outcomes exist and can be assembled. Report each executive's record to them privately, because that is how it becomes a development tool rather than a threat and is the only route to adoption. Report base rates across the organisation without attribution, as the aggregate is safe to share and is genuinely useful to everyone. Compare the stated expectation to the outcome rather than only the outcome, since a project that performed as predicted is a different signal from one that got lucky. Separate the decision from the execution, because a well-chosen project can be badly made and the two should not be conflated. Track the decisions that were declined where they were made elsewhere, which is the most informative and most uncomfortable data available. Use it for calibration rather than for evaluation initially, since trust must precede consequence. Hold post-mortems on a sample, as the discussion is more valuable than the number. And connect it to the outcome measurement work, since the record is only as meaningful as the outcome it is scored against.

## Who Feels the Pain
Executives with no way to improve; organisations that cannot tell good judgement from good luck; creators whose projects depend on an unexamined process; and a business allocating billions through people whose accuracy is unknown.

## Impact If Fixed
A profession evaluated on relationships and taste does not volunteer for a scoreboard, so no record is kept. Logging the decision and rationale and reporting it back privately is what turns years of decisions into the only feedback the role could ever have.

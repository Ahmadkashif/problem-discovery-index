# Evidence for the Room

**Niche:** [[niches/streaming-video-platforms/the-commissioning-executive/profile|The Commissioning Executive]]
**Industry:** [[industries/streaming-video-platforms|Streaming Video Platforms]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** The largest financial decisions in the business are made in the room with the least evidence in the building.
**Tags:** #worker-facing #gradient-boosting #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #k-nearest-neighbors #revenue-impact #descriptive-statistics #large-language-models
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to give the person committing nine figures something better than comparables and instinct — and whoever supports that decision without insulting the judgement it depends on changes how the money is spent.

## The Problem
The executive is deciding whether to commit a very large sum to a project that does not exist. What they have is a pitch, a comparable set assembled by the party who wants the money, their own sense of the market, and a relationship. What the platform has and does not give them is: which segments are underserved, what similar titles actually did, how the talent's previous work performed on this platform specifically, what the slate already contains, and what the base rate of success looks like for this kind of project.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The role is understood as creative judgement, so supporting it with data reads as an attempt to constrain it — support that looks like supervision is refused by the people it is meant to help, which is why nothing gets built even where the data is available. The outcome measure does not exist, so any evidence pack rests on an unbuilt foundation. Track records are not kept. And the executives with the strongest relationships have the least reason to want a system.

## What to Build
Give the room evidence and leave the judgement alone. Assemble an evidence pack per decision — comparable performance, talent history on this platform, segment demand, catalogue gaps, slate composition — which is the core and is assembly rather than modelling. Generate the comparable set systematically rather than accepting the advocate's, since that single change removes a structural bias from every meeting. Show the platform-specific talent record, because a creator's general reputation and their performance with this audience are different things and only one is known. Show what the slate already has too much and too little of, as the portfolio view is absent and the individual decisions are made without it. Report the base rate for this kind of commitment, which changes how a room reasons about a single case. Track every decision with its rationale and its outcome, so a track record exists at all. Show the executive their own record privately first, since that is how it becomes acceptable rather than threatening. Support the argument for a narrow commission with segment evidence, which is the case that most needs data and currently has the weakest defence. Frame everything as input to judgement, because the alternative is refusal. And build the outcome measurement alongside, since the pack is only as good as the outcome it is calibrated on.

## Target Customer
Content leadership and commissioning executives, finance leadership funding slates, producers and studios pitching, and analytics teams inside platforms.

## Impact If Built
Support that looks like supervision is refused by the people it is meant to help, which is why the evidence pack does not exist. Systematic comparables and platform-specific talent records are assembly rather than modelling and would change every greenlight meeting.

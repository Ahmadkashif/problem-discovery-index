## The Problem
Manufacturing quality control established that hundred-percent inspection is expensive, error-prone and often unnecessary, and replaced it with acceptance sampling, statistical process control, supplier quality management and the principle that quality is built rather than inspected in. The discipline is old and rigorous. Content operations inspects everything, by hand, forever.

## What Already Exists
Acceptance sampling plans with defined risk levels; statistical process control; supplier quality management and audits; defect classification by severity; and the build-quality-in principle with in-process control.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a defect that reaches millions of viewers at once. It requires: (1) an escaped defect that is instantly public and reputationally costly, which changes the acceptable risk level and is why hundred-percent inspection persists — the answer is stratified sampling by severity rather than uniform sampling; (2) defects that are perceptual and linguistic rather than dimensional, so automated detection must cover judgement-like properties; (3) suppliers who are creative partners rather than component vendors, which changes how quality management can be applied; (4) a unit that is hours of content rather than a part, so inspection cost scales with runtime; and (5) post-release viewer signals that manufacturing never has and that work as a detection layer.

## Target Customer
Content operations leadership, suppliers, quality control vendors serving media, and manufacturing quality vendors entering the sector.

## Impact If Solved
Manufacturing abandoned complete inspection and built the statistics to justify it. Stratifying by defect severity, with automated detection covering the perceptual properties, is what makes the same move possible here.

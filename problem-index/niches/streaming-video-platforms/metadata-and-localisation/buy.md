**Industry:** [[industries/streaming-video-platforms|Streaming Video Platforms]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Software localisation industrialised translation with memory, terminology and quality estimation, and media localisation is still largely priced per minute of output.

**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to produce descriptions, tags, artwork, subtitles, dubs and audio description for every title in dozens of languages without the cost scaling with the catalogue — and whoever breaks that relationship can carry a catalogue everyone else cannot afford.

## The Problem
Software and documentation localisation solved industrial-scale translation: translation memory that reuses prior work, terminology management for consistency, machine translation with post-editing, and quality estimation that routes segments by predicted difficulty. The economics were transformed. Media localisation retains much more of the per-unit manual model, partly for good reasons about creative quality and partly by inertia.

## What Already Exists
Translation memory and reuse; terminology and glossary management; machine translation with measured post-editing effort; quality estimation for routing; and localisation project management platforms.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to timed, performed, culturally loaded content. It requires: (1) timing and reading-speed constraints on subtitles, which have no equivalent in document translation and materially change what a correct translation is; (2) dubbing with performance, lip sync and casting, which is production rather than translation; (3) creative and cultural adaptation where a literal translation is wrong, so quality estimation must detect cultural failure rather than linguistic error — this is the substantive gap; (4) reuse that is limited because dialogue rarely repeats, weakening translation memory's core mechanism; and (5) a viewer feedback signal that document localisation never has and that media has in abundance.

## Target Customer
Content operations and localisation leadership, localisation vendors, and translation technology vendors entering media.

## Impact If Solved
Software localisation transformed its economics with memory, terminology and quality-routed post-editing. Media's constraints are real, but quality estimation that detects cultural failure and a viewer feedback loop are both available and unused.

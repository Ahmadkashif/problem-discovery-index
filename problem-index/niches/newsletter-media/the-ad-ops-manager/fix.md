# Every Sponsor Wants a Different Report

**Niche:** [[niches/newsletter-media/the-ad-ops-manager/profile|The Ad Operations Manager]]
**Industry:** [[industries/newsletter-media|Newsletter Media]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The same four numbers are retyped into eleven different templates every month.
**Tags:** #quick-win #automation #workflow-orchestration #worker-facing #evaluation-metrics #data-integration #descriptive-statistics #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to stop one person assembling every sponsor's report by hand in whatever format that sponsor asked for — and whoever automates it removes the bottleneck the whole revenue line runs through.

## The Problem
Each sponsor wants their report differently: one wants a spreadsheet, one a slide, one an email with the numbers in the body, one a specific set of metrics the publisher does not otherwise track. The underlying data is the same four or five numbers per placement. The operations manager spends a day a month reformatting the same information eleven ways, and any error is discovered by the sponsor.

## Why It's Still Broken
Each sponsor's format was agreed individually during a sale, so the requirements accumulated one deal at a time and nobody rationalised them — a concession made to close a deal becomes a permanent monthly cost that the person making the concession never pays. No system holds the requirements. The work is invisible. And nobody has proposed a standard.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Hold the data once and render it many ways. Define the underlying report dataset once, which is the fix and makes every format a rendering rather than a retyping. Record each sponsor's required format and metrics in a system rather than in someone's memory, since the memory is also the continuity risk. Generate each format automatically from the dataset, as reformatting is deterministic. Propose a standard format to new sponsors, because most will accept the default if it is offered first and the accumulation is caused by never offering one. Include the metrics the publisher can defend rather than whatever was requested, which connects to the measurement problem. Deliver on a schedule automatically, since chasing and lateness are both avoidable. Keep a copy of every report sent, as disputes arise and reconstruction is currently impossible. Let the sponsor self-serve where the relationship allows, which removes the request entirely. Flag placements whose numbers look anomalous before the report goes out, because the sponsor should not be the one to notice. And count the hours this takes, since a day a month is a meaningful share of the role.

## Who Feels the Pain
Operations managers losing a day a month to retyping; sponsors receiving reports late or wrong; publishers whose renewal conversations start with a correction; and a revenue line bottlenecked on formatting.

## Impact If Fixed
A concession made to close a deal becomes a permanent monthly cost paid by someone else, and the formats accumulate one deal at a time. Defining the dataset once makes every sponsor's format a rendering rather than a retyping.

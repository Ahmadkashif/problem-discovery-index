# The Calendar in a Spreadsheet

**Niche:** [[niches/newsletter-media/advertising-operations/profile|Advertising Operations]]
**Industry:** [[industries/newsletter-media|Newsletter Media]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Two sponsors were sold the same slot because the calendar lives in a spreadsheet that two people edit.
**Tags:** #workflow-orchestration #quick-win #automation #revenue-impact #evaluation-metrics #worker-facing #data-integration #descriptive-statistics
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to sell, deliver and report newsletter inventory in numbers both sides actually believe — and whoever produces a credible measurement changes what this inventory is worth.

## The Problem
Inventory is tracked in a spreadsheet. Sales promises a date, someone updates a row, an operations person reads it to build the send. Dates move, creative arrives late, a slot is double-sold, a sponsor's copy is inserted in the wrong issue, a make-good is agreed verbally. None of this is exotic and all of it happens regularly, because the system of record for a business's entire revenue is a shared file with no constraints.

## Why It's Still Broken
The spreadsheet worked when there were four sponsors a month and scaled by adding rows — a tool that never visibly fails is never replaced, and the failures here are individually small and absorbed as apologies. Proper inventory systems are built for web publishing and do not fit. The operations person holds the real state in their head. And nobody counts the errors.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Give inventory a system of record with constraints. Hold the calendar in a system that prevents double-selling, which is the fix and eliminates the most damaging error outright. Model slots explicitly by issue, position and format, since "a sponsorship" is not a unit and the ambiguity causes the disputes. Track creative deadlines and chase automatically, as late creative is the most common cause of scrambling. Show sales what is genuinely available, because they currently ask a person and that person is a bottleneck. Record the agreed terms with the booking, so make-goods and disputes have a reference. Generate the insertion from the booking rather than by copying text, which removes the wrong-issue error class. Proof placements automatically against the booking before send, since a final check is currently a person reading carefully at speed. Report sold and unsold inventory ahead, which enables both yield decisions and honest forecasting. Keep the sponsor's reporting requirements with the booking, as they differ and are remembered rather than recorded. And count the errors, because a business that has normalised occasional double-sells has not priced the cost.

## Who Feels the Pain
Operations managers holding the real calendar in their head; sponsors placed in the wrong issue; sales promising inventory that is gone; and publishers giving away make-goods for avoidable errors.

## Impact If Fixed
A tool that never visibly fails is never replaced, and these failures are individually small and absorbed as apologies. A calendar with constraints eliminates double-selling and generating insertions from the booking removes the wrong-issue error entirely.

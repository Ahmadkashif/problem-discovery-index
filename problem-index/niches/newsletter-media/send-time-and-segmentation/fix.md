# Six in the Morning for Everyone

**Niche:** [[niches/newsletter-media/send-time-and-segmentation/profile|Send Time & Segmentation]]
**Industry:** [[industries/newsletter-media|Newsletter Media]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The send time was chosen years ago on a hunch and has never been tested.
**Tags:** #quick-win #hypothesis-testing #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #confidence-intervals #automation #revenue-impact #time-series-forecasting
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to decide who receives what and when, per subscriber rather than per list — and whoever does it lifts engagement without writing a single additional word.

## The Problem
The newsletter goes at six in the morning eastern time because that is when the founder decided it should, several years ago, based on convention. The audience has since grown across time zones and habits. Nobody has tested an alternative, nobody has looked at when subscribers actually open, and the one setting that could lift engagement across the entire list with no editorial effort has never been examined.

## Why It's Still Broken
The send time was set once during setup, so it is treated as a property of the publication rather than as a variable — a configuration chosen at founding acquires the status of an identity. Testing it feels risky because the audience has a habit. Open data became noisy, which made measurement look harder. And nobody owns the question.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Look at the data and run the test. Plot when subscribers actually open and click, which is the fix and takes minutes — the distribution is frequently nothing like the send time assumes. Break it down by time zone, since a list that grew geographically is being served one region's morning. Test a small number of alternatives properly, as a randomised split is easy here and nobody runs one. Measure on clicks rather than opens, because the open signal is contaminated and the click is not. Test per-subscriber send time optimisation against the fixed time, since the platform may support it and nobody has checked. Give the change time to settle, as a habit disruption looks like a loss for a week. Consider the day as well as the hour, which is tested even less often. Coordinate with the advertising, since sponsors may care about delivery timing. Record the result, because this test will otherwise be re-run from scratch in two years. And revisit as the audience changes, since the right answer moves with the list.

## Who Feels the Pain
Subscribers receiving mail while asleep; publishers leaving engagement on the table; advertisers whose placements are read less than they could be; and teams who assume the setting is fixed.

## Impact If Fixed
A configuration chosen at founding acquires the status of an identity, so the send time has never been questioned. Plotting when subscribers actually open takes minutes and a randomised test of two alternatives is the cheapest lift in the business.

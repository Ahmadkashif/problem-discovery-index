**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to buy subscribers who read rather than subscribers who count — and whoever prices acquisition on downstream engagement rather than on cost per signup stops paying to damage their own delivery.

## The Problem
Subscription commerce went through exactly this. Early growth was bought on cost per acquisition, cheap channels looked best, and the cohorts churned. The industry moved to cohort-based lifetime value, payback period analysis, channel-level retention curves and marginal rather than average economics. The methods are standard and well documented. Newsletters buy on cost per subscriber and quote list size.

## What Already Exists
Cohort retention analysis; lifetime value modelling with payback periods; channel-level quality measurement; marginal versus average acquisition economics; and blended versus paid attribution practice.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a free subscriber whose value is indirect. It requires: (1) value realised through advertising impressions rather than through a subscription payment, so lifetime value must be built from engagement and inventory rather than from revenue per user — this is the substantive translation; (2) a negative externality where a bad subscriber harms delivery for the good ones, which subscription commerce has no analogue for and is the most important term; (3) no churn event, since a disengaged subscriber remains on the list indefinitely and must be identified rather than observed leaving; (4) list size as a sales asset, which creates a direct incentive against quality; and (5) small publishers with no analytics function, so the practice must be a product.

## Target Customer
Growth and publisher leadership, newsletter platforms, advertisers, and growth analytics vendors.

## Impact If Solved
Subscription commerce made this exact transition and documented it. The new term is the delivery externality — a bad subscriber harming everyone else's placement — which no lifetime value model has ever needed to include.

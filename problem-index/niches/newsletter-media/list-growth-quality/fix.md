# Cost Per Subscriber Is the Only Number

**Niche:** [[niches/newsletter-media/list-growth-quality/profile|List Growth & Acquisition Quality]]
**Industry:** [[industries/newsletter-media|Newsletter Media]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The growth report ranks channels by cost per signup, and the cheapest channel is the one destroying the list.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #quick-win #evaluation-metrics #revenue-impact #confidence-intervals #automation #data-integration #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to buy subscribers who read rather than subscribers who count — and whoever prices acquisition on downstream engagement rather than on cost per signup stops paying to damage their own delivery.

## The Problem
The weekly growth report shows subscribers added and cost per subscriber by channel. Budget follows the cheapest. Six months later the list has doubled, engagement has halved, the complaint rate is rising, delivery is degrading, and nobody has connected those facts to the channel mix — because the report that drives the spending decision contains no information about what happened after the signup.

## Why It's Still Broken
The report was built from what the acquisition platforms return, so it stops at the conversion — a dashboard assembled from ad platform data cannot see what the subscriber did next. The two datasets sit in different systems. Growth targets are set in raw count. And the harm appears as a delivery problem that gets blamed on something else.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Put quality in the same report. Add thirty and ninety day engagement rate by acquisition source, which is the fix and is a join the publisher can run today. Rank channels by cost per engaged subscriber, since that single reordering will change the budget allocation immediately. Show complaint rate by source, as it is the direct link to the delivery problem and is never attributed. Show the cohort curves rather than a point, because decay rate differs by source and the average conceals it. Flag sources below an engagement floor for review, which is a short list and a straightforward decision. Report the list's overall engagement rate over time, so dilution is visible as it happens. Separate paid, organic and referral in every report, since blending them hides the paid quality problem entirely. Set the growth target in engaged subscribers, as the target determines the behaviour. Review the channel mix monthly rather than annually. And tell the sales team the engaged list size, because that is what they should be selling and it is a better product than the raw number.

## Who Feels the Pain
Growth teams rewarded for acquiring subscribers who harm the business; publishers whose delivery degrades for unexplained reasons; advertisers buying against a diluted list; and the engaged core, whose mail lands worse.

## Impact If Fixed
A dashboard assembled from ad platform data cannot see what the subscriber did next, so the report stops at the conversion. Adding engagement and complaint rate by source reorders the channel ranking and connects the delivery problem to its cause.

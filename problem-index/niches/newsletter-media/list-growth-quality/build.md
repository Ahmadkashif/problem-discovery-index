# Pricing Acquisition on What Happens Next

**Niche:** [[niches/newsletter-media/list-growth-quality/profile|List Growth & Acquisition Quality]]
**Industry:** [[industries/newsletter-media|Newsletter Media]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A subscriber who never opens costs money twice — once to acquire and again in the delivery they degrade for everyone else.
**Tags:** #survival-analysis #gradient-boosting #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #revenue-impact #descriptive-statistics #causal-inference #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to buy subscribers who read rather than subscribers who count — and whoever prices acquisition on downstream engagement rather than on cost per signup stops paying to damage their own delivery.

## The Problem
Growth is bought and measured at the signup. What happens afterwards — whether the subscriber opens, clicks, stays, complains or silently rots on the list — differs enormously by source and is not attributed back. A channel producing subscribers at a low cost per signup and a near-zero engagement rate looks like the best-performing channel in every report, and it is actively harming the business by degrading the sender reputation that governs delivery to the engaged core.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Cost per subscriber is available immediately and quality is not, so the fast metric governs — a growth team optimising weekly steers by the number available on Monday. The delivery harm is diffuse and lands on everyone rather than on the channel that caused it. Subscriber count is what media kits quote. And nobody joined the acquisition record to the engagement record.

## What to Build
Attribute quality back to source. Track engagement, complaint and retention behaviour by acquisition source and cohort, which is the core and is a join between two systems the publisher already runs. Compute lifetime value per source including the advertising revenue a subscriber actually generates, since that is what the channel should be priced against. Price in the delivery externality, because a source producing complaints imposes a cost on every other subscriber and it is currently free to the channel. Report cost per engaged subscriber alongside cost per subscriber, which will reorder the channel ranking immediately. Predict a new subscriber's likely engagement at signup from source and behaviour, so weak cohorts can be handled differently rather than sent everything. Cut or renegotiate the sources that fail, as the analysis is worthless without the decision. Handle co-registration and giveaway sources carefully, since they are the cheapest and usually the worst. Set growth targets in engaged subscribers, which is the structural fix — a target in raw count guarantees the current behaviour. Segment onboarding by source, because a weak cohort can sometimes be rescued with a different first sequence. And report the list's engagement distribution over time, since dilution is gradual and invisible in aggregate numbers.

## Target Customer
Growth and publisher leadership, advertisers buying against diluted lists, acquisition channels whose quality is unmeasured, and sending platforms whose shared reputation absorbs the harm.

## Impact If Built
A growth team optimising weekly steers by the number available on Monday, and quality arrives months later. Joining acquisition source to engagement and complaint behaviour reorders the channel ranking and prices the delivery harm that is currently free.

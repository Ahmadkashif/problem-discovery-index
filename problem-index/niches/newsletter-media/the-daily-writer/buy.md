## The Problem
Newsrooms solved daily production with an apparatus: wire services that pre-filter, a budget meeting that allocates, a research desk, editorial workflow systems with assignment and status, a copy desk, and an archive that is searchable and used. It works and it assumes a staff. A daily newsletter reaching a hundred thousand people is produced by one or two people with a feed reader and a shared document.

## What Already Exists
Wire services and pre-filtered feeds; editorial workflow and assignment systems; budget and planning practice; archive and research desks; and copy and fact-checking processes.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a newsroom of one. It requires: (1) every function collapsed into one person, so the tooling must perform the desk's role rather than coordinate between desks — this is the fundamental difference; (2) a narrow audience-specific relevance filter, where wire services filter for general newsworthiness and the value here is knowing this audience; (3) voice as the product, so copy support must assist rather than standardise; (4) an archive small enough to be fully used and currently not searchable at all; and (5) no budget for a research function, which is what the automation replaces.

## Target Customer
Editorial leadership and writers, newsletter platforms, media companies with newsletter portfolios, and editorial systems vendors.

## Impact If Solved
The newsroom apparatus assumes desks and staff, and this is one person doing all of it. Tooling that performs the research desk's role — audience-specific relevance over the publication's own archive — is what a newsroom of one actually needs.

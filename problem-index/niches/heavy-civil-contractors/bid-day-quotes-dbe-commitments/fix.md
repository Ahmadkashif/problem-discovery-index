# Good-Faith-Effort Files Built After the Bid

**Niche:** [[niches/heavy-civil-contractors/bid-day-quotes-dbe-commitments/profile|Bid-Day Subcontractor Quotes & DBE Commitments]]
**Industry:** [[industries/heavy-civil-contractors|Heavy Civil Contractors]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** When a bid misses its participation goal, the good-faith-effort file is reconstructed from emails after the fact, and owners reject thin ones.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #feature-engineering #compliance #automation #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to close a complete set of conforming subcontractor and supplier quotes, with the owner's DBE or small-business participation documented, before the bid deadline, and whoever has the lowest conforming quote plugged on every scope when the deadline hits takes the account.

## The Problem
Proving good faith means showing solicitations sent, follow-ups made and quotes received and why they were not used. Estimators send the emails but don't keep the log.

## Why It's Still Broken
Solicitations go out from personal inboxes and bid platforms, and the record of who quoted what lives in the estimator's spreadsheet.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Every solicitation, reply and quote is logged automatically against the bid, with the reason a quote wasn't used recorded at plug time. The good-faith package is generated on demand.

## Who Feels the Pain
Estimators rebuilding files after bid day and compliance officers defending low bids under protest.

## Impact If Fixed
If the file is ready at submission (estimate: saving ~1–2 days per contested bid), the low bid survives review.

# Post-Letting Debriefs Nobody Does

**Niche:** [[niches/heavy-civil-contractors/public-bid-tab-unit-price-intelligence/profile|Public Bid Tab & Unit-Price Intelligence]]
**Industry:** [[industries/heavy-civil-contractors|Heavy Civil Contractors]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** When a bid loses, the tab shows exactly which items the winner priced lower, and the estimating team rarely looks before starting the next bid.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #hypothesis-testing #large-language-models #revenue-impact #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn the bid results of Corps districts, port authorities, transit agencies and railroad owners, plus DOT marine, structures and rail items, into a clean, item-mapped unit-price history the estimator trusts on bid day, and whoever holds the largest correctly mapped item history across those owners takes the account.

## The Problem
The estimator learns the firm was 6% high (illustrative) and moves on. Whether that came from one item (a production rate) or everywhere (overhead and margin) is never established.

## Why It's Still Broken
Pulling the tab and lining it up against the firm's own estimate takes hours, and the next bid is due.

## What a Fix Looks Like
An automatic debrief the morning after each letting: the firm's items versus the winner's and the field's, the gap decomposed by item family, and a running record of where the firm is consistently high.

## Who Feels the Pain
Estimators who keep losing on the same items and chief estimators who can't tell skill from bad luck.

## Impact If Fixed
If a firm finds one item family where it is consistently ~10% high (estimate) and fixes its rate, its hit rate on that work rises without cutting margin elsewhere.

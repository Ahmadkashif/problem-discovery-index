# Public Bid Tab & Unit-Price Intelligence

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/heavy-civil-contractors|Heavy Civil Contractors]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn the bid results of Corps districts, port authorities, transit agencies and railroad owners, plus DOT marine, structures and rail items, into a clean, item-mapped unit-price history the estimator trusts on bid day, and whoever holds the largest correctly mapped item history across those owners takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$30M a year of bid-data and estimating-analytics spend by heavy civil contractors on non-highway owners' bid results (estimate: ~734 firms with 20+ employees in 237990, Census SUSB 2022, at ~$20K–$60K a year each); highway contractors' spend on DOT road-and-bridge data is excluded
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~0.1% in spend (estimate); informs the price of most public bids in the code
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — results are posted online, but as PDFs and spreadsheets with owner-specific item codes, and many estimators keep their own spreadsheets of past lettings
**Target Buyer:** Chief estimator or VP of estimating at a marine, dredging, rail or heavy civil contractor
**Automation Potential:** Very high — collection, parsing, item mapping, outlier detection and competitor tracking are all mechanical

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Public work publishes its own price history, unevenly. The Corps' Navigation and Civil Works Decision Support Center posts current-year dredging bid abstracts and past contracts awarded (search summary; its page returned 403), which give at least each bidder's total against the government estimate. Line-item abstracts appear where districts post them on SAM.gov or district sites: a 2006 New Orleans District abstract lists bidders and amounts, and a FY26 South Atlantic Division maintenance-dredging abstract reportedly lists unit prices by line and the government estimate (both via search summary). Port authorities, transit agencies and some railroad owners post bid results, and DOTs publish full tabulations that include the marine, structures and rail items heavy civil firms bid. The data is cheap or free and almost unused, because every owner codes items differently, quantities vary, and the files are PDFs. It is the most automatable piece of the estimate and the owner's MVP: unit-price outliers across these owners. DOT road-and-bridge unit-price intelligence (paving, grading, bridge decks) belongs to the queued highway contractors build, which should link this niche for non-highway owners and items rather than profile them again.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Oman Systems' Bid Tabs Professional (Nashville, since 1992), built on DOT bid data and used by WSDOT, INDOT and MoDOT (search summary); DOT-side estimating systems; Edgevanta's "Bid Tabs Agent", pitched over 20+ states (startup directory, search summary); estimators' own spreadsheets. The gaps: Corps districts, ports, transit agencies and railroads barely covered, weak item mapping across owners, no link to the contractor's own estimate, and averages instead of modelled uncertainty.

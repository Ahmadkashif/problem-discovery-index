# Standby Days Nobody Can Prove

**Niche:** [[niches/heavy-civil-contractors/marine-waterway-construction/profile|Marine, Port & Waterway Construction]]
**Industry:** [[industries/heavy-civil-contractors|Heavy Civil Contractors]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** When a floating spread sits idle for the owner, every day costs thousands, and the contractor usually can't prove which hours were the owner's fault.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #change-point-detection #feature-engineering #compliance #quick-win
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is "every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to deliver work in and over water inside the owner's navigation and environmental windows," but that fight splits three ways. Dredging is won by having a certified dredge near the work and pricing under the Corps' 125%-of-estimate ceiling. Port and marine structures are won by crane-barge and pile-driving spreads that keep berths working. Locks, dams and levees are won by pricing in-the-wet cofferdam and river-stage risk on federal megaprojects. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## The Problem
A dredge waits for a ship to clear the channel, a crane barge for an inspector, a pile crew for a design answer. The diary says "standby", without start and end times or a cause tied to the contract.

## Why It's Still Broken
Standby is recorded by the foreman or the dredge's log in free text. The dredge's DQM sensors already show when it stopped pumping, but nobody links that to the diary or the clause.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Pumping and engine-state data marks every stoppage automatically. The crew taps a cause (owner, ship traffic, weather, breakdown), and the stoppage log rolls up weekly by cause and party, ready to attach to a notice.

## Who Feels the Pain
Project managers who lose standby claims, superintendents blamed for low production, and estimators who pad the next bid because they can't separate idle time from slow work.

## Impact If Fixed
If a dredge spread's daily cost runs ~$40,000–$100,000 (estimate) and ~3–5% of days carry owner-caused standby (estimate), proving those hours is worth more than any other paperwork the crew does.

# Marine, Port & Waterway Construction

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/heavy-civil-contractors|Heavy Civil Contractors]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is "every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to deliver work in and over water inside the owner's navigation and environmental windows," but that fight splits three ways. Dredging is won by having a certified dredge near the work and pricing under the Corps' 125%-of-estimate ceiling. Port and marine structures are won by crane-barge and pile-driving spreads that keep berths working. Locks, dams and levees are won by pricing in-the-wet cofferdam and river-stage risk on federal megaprojects. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$12B a year of marine, port, dredging, lock, dam and levee construction by 237990 contractors (estimate: ~30–35% of the code's ~$37B 2022 receipts, consistent with a ~$2.3B federal dredging bid market and ~$545M of 2025 marine revenue at Orion alone, the latter via search summary)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~30–35% (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — dredges stream sensor data to the Corps and surveys are digital, but estimating, scheduling and change records follow the industry's manual pattern
**Target Buyer:** President, chief estimator or VP of operations at a marine or dredging contractor
**Automation Potential:** Medium for estimating, survey reconciliation and dredge production advice; low for the work itself

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Work in water has its own physics, fleet and law. Equipment floats: dredges, crane barges, work tugs, scows. Production depends on tides, waves, river stages and seasonal environmental windows such as dredging restrictions for turtles and fish. The Foreign Dredge Act of 1906 and the Jones Act keep foreign fleets out of U.S. dredging, and Great Lakes Dredge & Dock says it and two other companies held ~56% of the federal bid market in 2023–2025. The owners are the Corps of Engineers, port authorities, state coastal agencies and private terminals. But "marine" is three different contests, which is why the niche is decomposed.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Estimating in HeavyBid, B2W or spreadsheets; hydrographic survey software; the Corps' DQM monitoring on dredges; P6 for schedules. The gaps: production rates that live with chief estimators, pay quantities reconciled by hand, and the industry's change and delay records assembled late.

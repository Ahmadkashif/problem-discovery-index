# Lost Windows Nobody Bills For

**Niche:** [[niches/heavy-civil-contractors/rail-transit-track-construction/profile|Railroad & Transit Track Construction]]
**Industry:** [[industries/heavy-civil-contractors|Heavy Civil Contractors]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** When the railroad cancels a window, sends no flagger or hands back track late, the crew is paid to wait and the contractor rarely recovers it.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #feature-engineering #compliance #revenue-impact #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to finish new track, turnouts and yard work inside the outage windows the host railroad or transit agency grants, and whoever returns track to service on time in the largest share of granted windows takes the account.

## The Problem
Cancelled or shortened windows are logged in a foreman's diary, if at all, without the time lost or the contract clause that makes it compensable.

## Why It's Still Broken
The railroad controls the window record; the contractor's evidence is scattered across texts and diaries.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A window log recording requested, granted and actual hours, with the cause and the crew and equipment on standby. It is summarised monthly against the contract's delay and standby provisions, ready for the change request.

## Who Feels the Pain
Rail superintendents, project managers and estimators who pad the next bid for lost windows.

## Impact If Fixed
If a third of lost-window standby is recovered (estimate), a contractor losing ~20 windows a year (estimate) recovers crew and equipment costs worth more than the logging effort.

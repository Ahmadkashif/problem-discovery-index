# Railroad & Transit Track Construction

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/heavy-civil-contractors|Heavy Civil Contractors]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to finish new track, turnouts and yard work inside the outage windows the host railroad or transit agency grants, and whoever returns track to service on time in the largest share of granted windows takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$8B a year of new railroad, yard, siding, industrial spur and transit trackwork construction by 237990 contractors (estimate: ~20–25% of the code's ~$37B 2022 receipts; FRA describes $66B of IIJA advance appropriations for rail, including $36B for the Federal-State Partnership for Intercity Passenger Rail, via search summary)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~20–25% (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low-Medium — railroads run digital track charts and work-window systems, but the contractor's window plans, flagging requests and production tracking are spreadsheets and phone calls
**Target Buyer:** Division manager or VP of rail at a heavy civil or railroad contractor
**Automation Potential:** Medium for window planning, production tracking and roadway-worker compliance records; low for the trackwork

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Rail construction happens on or beside a live railroad. Work is allowed only in track outages the host railroad grants (hours on a busy main line), under roadway worker protection with railroad flaggers (49 CFR Part 214, recalled), using contractors the railroad has qualified and insured. Owners range from Class I railroads building sidings and intermodal yards, to short lines rebuilding with CRISI grants, to transit agencies building light-rail and commuter lines with FTA money and DBE goals. The contest is getting the work done inside the granted windows; a blown window delays trains and often costs the next one. Railroad maintenance-of-way services (tie and rail replacement programmes, surfacing) belong to the queued rail services contractors build, and grade-separation bridges to the queued highway contractors build. This niche is new track and its civil works.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Railroads' work-window and track-authority systems, P6, estimating systems, field apps. The gaps: window plans built by hand against the railroad's operating plan, production per window not measured, and flagging cost and delay not tracked against the railroad's own failures to provide protection.

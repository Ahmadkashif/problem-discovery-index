# Change, Delay & Differing-Site-Condition Records

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/heavy-civil-contractors|Heavy Civil Contractors]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn each field event into a notice inside the contract's clock and a time impact analysis the owner accepts, and whoever gets the most time and money granted at the change-order stage, before it becomes a claim, takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.2B a year of project-controls, scheduling and change-management labour and tools inside 237990 contractors (estimate: ~3% of ~$37B receipts), governing change orders that commonly run ~5–15% of contract value (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~3% in cost (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — schedules are in P6, but the evidence (diaries, photos, RFIs, survey notes) is assembled by hand after the event
**Target Buyer:** Project manager or VP of operations; the chief scheduler at larger firms
**Automation Potential:** High for event detection, notice drafting and fragnet assembly; low for negotiation

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Heavy civil contracts are full of unknowns priced on borings, surveys and river records, so change is normal and entitlement depends on paperwork done on time. On Corps work the schedule specification wants a justification of delay within 10 days and an AACE 52R-06 time impact analysis. FAR 52.236-2 wants differing-site-condition notice before the condition is disturbed. Transit, port and railroad contracts set their own clocks. The work sits between the field (foremen and superintendents who see the event) and the office (project managers and schedulers who write the notice), which is why it stays manual. It is the pre-claim stage. Once a dispute goes to counsel it becomes the privileged work at [[niches/general-contractors/construction-claims-forensics/profile|Construction Claims & Schedule Forensics]].

## Current Tools & Gaps
P6, project-management platforms with change-event modules, field apps, Trunk Tools' TrunkChange, and scheduling consultants. The gaps: no detection of notice-worthy events in the field record, notice clocks tracked in calendars, and fragnets built from memory.

# Change Orders Priced Without Their Cost Codes

**Niche:** [[niches/heavy-civil-contractors/change-delay-site-condition-records/profile|Change, Delay & Differing-Site-Condition Records]]
**Industry:** [[industries/heavy-civil-contractors|Heavy Civil Contractors]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Changed work gets charged to base-contract cost codes, so when the change order is priced the contractor can't show what the extra work actually cost.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #feature-engineering #data-integration #revenue-impact #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn each field event into a notice inside the contract's clock and a time impact analysis the owner accepts, and whoever gets the most time and money granted at the change-order stage, before it becomes a claim, takes the account.

## The Problem
The foreman keeps charging the crew to "drive piles" while it drives the extra piles the owner added. Weeks later the project manager prices the change order from estimates, and the owner's auditor asks for actuals.

## Why It's Still Broken
New change cost codes are opened late, and foremen aren't told. Equipment hours follow the same pattern.

## What a Fix Looks Like
The moment a change event is logged, a change code goes to the foremen's time-card app and their drafts suggest it when the diary mentions the changed work. Force-account hours, equipment and materials roll up daily for the pricing package.

## Who Feels the Pain
Project managers negotiating change orders, foremen asked to recall hours from weeks ago, and accountants reconciling.

## Impact If Fixed
If change orders are priced on actuals rather than estimates (estimate: recovering ~10–20% more of the real cost of changed work), margin on change-heavy jobs rises by points.

# Quotes That Expire While the Permit Waits

**Niche:** [[niches/heavy-civil-contractors/small-marine-waterfront-contractors/profile|Small Marine & Waterfront Contractors]]
**Industry:** [[industries/heavy-civil-contractors|Heavy Civil Contractors]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A seawall price given in spring is built in autumn after permits clear; steel, timber and fuel moved in between, and the contractor eats the difference.
**Tags:** #time-series-forecasting #linear-regression #feature-engineering #revenue-impact #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get a waterfront owner's dock, seawall or bulkhead through Corps and state permitting and onto the barge schedule, and whoever gets the permits approved in the fewest weeks after the site visit takes the account.

## The Problem
Quotes are fixed when the owner signs, but the job waits months for permits. Sheet pile, treated timber and fuel prices drift.

## Why It's Still Broken
Small contractors don't track material indices or write escalation clauses; owners expect a firm price.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A quote tool that shows expected permit time for the district, prices material at the expected build date, and offers a simple escalation clause tied to a public index, with a re-price prompt when permits clear.

## Who Feels the Pain
Owner-operators whose margin is a few points and homeowners surprised by change requests.

## Impact If Fixed
If material escalation on delayed jobs is passed through rather than absorbed (estimate: ~2–5% of job value), a small firm keeps margin it currently loses on its slowest permits.

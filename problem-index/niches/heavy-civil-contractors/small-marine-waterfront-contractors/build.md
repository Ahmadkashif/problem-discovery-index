# Permit Packages Drafted From the Site Visit

**Niche:** [[niches/heavy-civil-contractors/small-marine-waterfront-contractors/profile|Small Marine & Waterfront Contractors]]
**Industry:** [[industries/heavy-civil-contractors|Heavy Civil Contractors]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A seawall or dock permit needs plan and section drawings, water depths, mean high water lines and a project description in each agency's format; small contractors assemble them by hand for every job.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #cnns #semantic-segmentation #workflow-orchestration #worker-facing #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get a waterfront owner's dock, seawall or bulkhead through Corps and state permitting and onto the barge schedule, and whoever gets the permits approved in the fewest weeks after the site visit takes the account.

## The Problem
The owner-operator measures the shoreline, takes photos and soundings, then spends evenings drawing plan views and cross-sections and filling out joint permit applications. Rejections for missing details add weeks.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The buyers are tiny and the requirements differ by Corps district and state. Permit software targets agencies, and engineering firms charge per job.

## What to Build
A phone workflow that captures the site (photos, a simple depth survey, property lines from parcel data) and drafts plan and section sheets from templates for the chosen structure. It fills the district's and state's application forms and checks them against each agency's completeness list. A demo needs one state's joint application and public parcel data.

## Target Customer
Owner-operators of marine contractors doing ~20–100 waterfront jobs a year (estimate).

## Impact If Built
If permit packages take ~2 hours instead of ~2 days (estimate) and fewer bounce back incomplete, a small firm quotes more jobs and starts them weeks sooner.

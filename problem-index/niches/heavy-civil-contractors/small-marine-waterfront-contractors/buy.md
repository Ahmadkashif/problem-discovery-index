# Field-Service Software Taught the Barge Schedule

**Niche:** [[niches/heavy-civil-contractors/small-marine-waterfront-contractors/profile|Small Marine & Waterfront Contractors]]
**Industry:** [[industries/heavy-civil-contractors|Heavy Civil Contractors]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Home-service scheduling tools book technicians to addresses; a marine contractor schedules a barge and crane that move by water, by tide and by permit window.
**Tags:** #optimization-fundamentals #dynamic-programming #time-series-forecasting #workflow-orchestration #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get a waterfront owner's dock, seawall or bulkhead through Corps and state permitting and onto the barge schedule, and whoever gets the permits approved in the fewest weeks after the site visit takes the account.

## The Problem
A small marine contractor's capacity is one or two floating rigs. Sequencing jobs along a shoreline, around tides, permit approvals and environmental windows, is done in the owner's head.

## What Already Exists
Field-service management software (quotes, scheduling, invoicing), tide tables and marine weather.

## The Customization Gap
Rig-based scheduling instead of technician-based; transit time by water between sites; tide and weather operability; permit status gating each job; and material lead times for piles and sheet.

## Target Customer
Owner-operators with one to three barges.

## Impact If Solved
If each rig gains a few productive days a month (estimate) through better sequencing, a small firm adds ~5–10% revenue without buying equipment.

# Small Marine & Waterfront Contractors

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/heavy-civil-contractors|Heavy Civil Contractors]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get a waterfront owner's dock, seawall or bulkhead through Corps and state permitting and onto the barge schedule, and whoever gets the permits approved in the fewest weeks after the site visit takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$2.5B a year of docks, piers, seawalls, bulkheads, boat lifts and shoreline stabilisation for homeowners, HOAs, marinas and small commercial owners (estimate: ~40% of the ~$5.9B taken by 237990 firms under 20 employees in 2022, Census SUSB; counted apart from the ~$12B marine, port and waterway pool, which is mostly public work by larger firms)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~7% (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — quotes are drawn by hand or in basic CAD, permits prepared by the owner or a permit expediter, scheduling on a whiteboard
**Target Buyer:** Owner-operator of a 3–20 person marine contractor
**Automation Potential:** Medium for permit packages, quoting and scheduling; low for the work

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Most of the code's 3,799 firms under 20 employees are not bidding the Corps. Many are small marine contractors with a barge or two, a crane and a pile-driving crew, building residential and marina docks, seawalls and bulkheads on coasts, lakes and rivers (estimate). Their bottleneck is not production rates but permits: work in navigable waters needs Corps authorisation under Section 10 or 404, usually under a nationwide or regional general permit, plus state submerged-lands, coastal-zone and local approvals, typically months in total (recalled). The owner-operator draws the sketch, assembles the permit package and quotes, often for jobs that won't be buildable for months. Estimating and project tools built for heavy civil firms or for home-service trades fit neither the permit work nor the barge.

## Current Tools & Gaps
General field-service software (quotes, invoices), basic CAD, permit expediters and engineers hired per job, state online permit portals. The gaps: no tool that turns a site visit into a permit-ready drawing package, no tracking of permit status across agencies, and quotes that go stale while permits wait.

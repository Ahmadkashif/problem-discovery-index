# The Opening-Date Risk Board

**Niche:** [[niches/heavy-civil-contractors/sports-field-golf-park-builders/profile|Sports Field, Golf Course & Park Builders]]
**Industry:** [[industries/heavy-civil-contractors|Heavy Civil Contractors]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Every field job has one date that matters, and weather, material deliveries and test results decide whether it holds; nobody puts them on one board the athletic director can read.
**Tags:** #monte-carlo-methods #time-series-forecasting #gradient-boosting #workflow-orchestration #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to hand over a laser-graded, drained field or course that passes the owner's grade, drainage and playability tests before a season opener the owner won't move, and whoever opens on the promised date takes the account.

## The Problem
A turf field build in summer has a few weeks of slack. Rain on the subgrade, a late stone delivery or a failed planarity test eats it, and the owner hears about it when it's too late.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The builders are small, the owners are schools, and generic scheduling tools don't know grading weather limits or test sequences.

## What to Build
A schedule-risk board that simulates remaining work against local weather history and forecasts, material lead times and test outcomes. It shows the probability of opening on time and the decision that protects it (add a crew, change the sequence). A demo can run on NOAA history and a template field schedule.

## Target Customer
Owners of regional field and course builders doing ~10–40 projects a year (estimate).

## Impact If Built
If late openings fall by half (estimate), builders avoid liquidated damages and keep the school-district relationships that drive repeat work.

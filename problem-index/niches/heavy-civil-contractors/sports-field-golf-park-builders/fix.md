# Planarity Failures Found at Final Inspection

**Niche:** [[niches/heavy-civil-contractors/sports-field-golf-park-builders/profile|Sports Field, Golf Course & Park Builders]]
**Industry:** [[industries/heavy-civil-contractors|Heavy Civil Contractors]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A synthetic field base that fails the owner's planarity or grade test at the end has to be reworked under the opening-date clock, though the machine data could have shown it weeks earlier.
**Tags:** #gaussian-processes #numerical-methods #change-point-detection #quick-win #data-integration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to hand over a laser-graded, drained field or course that passes the owner's grade, drainage and playability tests before a season opener the owner won't move, and whoever opens on the promised date takes the account.

## The Problem
Base grade is checked by survey or a straightedge test when the crew thinks it's done. Low spots found then mean re-grading stone and re-testing.

## Why It's Still Broken
Machine-control logs and drone surveys exist but aren't compared with the tolerance surface during the work.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Daily comparison of as-built surfaces from machine logs or drone flights against the design with the spec tolerance, so out-of-tolerance zones show as a heat map before the next layer goes down.

## Who Feels the Pain
Grading foremen, project managers facing an immovable opening, and owners' inspectors.

## Impact If Fixed
If rework at final inspection falls by ~70% (estimate), a builder saves several crew-days per field and protects the opening date.

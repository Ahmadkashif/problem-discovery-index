# Locks, Dams & Levee Construction

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/heavy-civil-contractors|Heavy Civil Contractors]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to price and control in-the-wet risk on federal and utility water projects (cofferdams, river stages, foundation conditions, navigation kept open), and whoever bids the lowest contingency that the river and the ground don't overrun takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$4B a year of lock, dam, spillway, levee and floodwall construction and rehabilitation by 237990 contractors (estimate; the Corps' FY2026 civil works budget was a record ~$10.4B per the Great Lakes Dredge & Dock 10-K, and hydropower dam owners fund rehabilitation found by FERC Part 12D inspections)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~11% (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low-Medium — instrumentation (piezometers, inclinometers) is digital, but risk pricing, river-stage planning and change records are manual
**Target Buyer:** Project executive or chief estimator at a large heavy civil contractor or joint venture
**Automation Potential:** Low for the work; medium for risk modelling, instrumentation monitoring and schedule-weather planning

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
These are the industry's megaprojects: new locks and lock rehabilitations on the inland waterways, spillway and embankment rehabilitations, levee and floodwall systems. They are built in the river, behind cofferdams, while barges keep moving and the river keeps rising. Contracts often run several years and several hundred million dollars, are let by Corps districts or utility dam owners, and are bid by joint ventures of the largest heavy civil firms (estimate). The binding risk is the river: a stage above the cofferdam's design floods the work, and an unexpected foundation condition becomes a differing-site-condition fight. That risk contest has different winners from dredging or port work.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Geotechnical instrumentation platforms, hydrologic forecasts from USGS gauges and NOAA river forecast centres, P6 and schedule risk tools, estimating systems. The gaps: river-stage risk priced from a few historical hydrographs by feel, and instrumentation data watched by engineers but not tied to the schedule or the claim record.

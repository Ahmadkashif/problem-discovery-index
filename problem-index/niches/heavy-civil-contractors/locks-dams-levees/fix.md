# Foundation Surprises Documented Too Late

**Niche:** [[niches/heavy-civil-contractors/locks-dams-levees/profile|Locks, Dams & Levee Construction]]
**Industry:** [[industries/heavy-civil-contractors|Heavy Civil Contractors]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** When rock, voids or soft zones differ from the borings, the claim depends on notice given "before the conditions are disturbed", and crews keep excavating.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #cnns #object-detection #compliance #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to price and control in-the-wet risk on federal and utility water projects (cofferdams, river stages, foundation conditions, navigation kept open), and whoever bids the lowest contingency that the river and the ground don't overrun takes the account.

## The Problem
FAR 52.236-2 bars an equitable adjustment for a differing site condition unless the contractor gave written notice promptly and before disturbing it. In a dewatered pit with a schedule to hold, the photos and notice come after the excavator has moved on.

## Why It's Still Broken
The condition is recognised by a superintendent or geologist on site, but the paperwork belongs to the project manager in the office. Photos sit on phones without location or elevation.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A field capture flow that compares the exposed condition with the nearest boring log and the geotechnical baseline, tags photos with position and elevation, and drafts the notice the same day for the project manager to send.

## Who Feels the Pain
Superintendents and geologists who know the ground changed, and project managers who lose the claim on notice grounds.

## Impact If Fixed
If notices go out the same day instead of after the condition is gone (estimate), contractors keep entitlement on differing-site-condition claims that run ~$1M–$10M on a large lock or dam job (estimate).

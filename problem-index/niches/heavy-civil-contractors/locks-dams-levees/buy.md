# Instrumentation Monitoring Tied to the Work Plan

**Niche:** [[niches/heavy-civil-contractors/locks-dams-levees/profile|Locks, Dams & Levee Construction]]
**Industry:** [[industries/heavy-civil-contractors|Heavy Civil Contractors]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Geotechnical monitoring platforms plot piezometers and inclinometers well; a contractor needs the readings tied to the dewatering, excavation and river-stage plan so a trend changes the next shift.
**Tags:** #change-point-detection #time-series-forecasting #gaussian-processes #data-integration #compliance
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to price and control in-the-wet risk on federal and utility water projects (cofferdams, river stages, foundation conditions, navigation kept open), and whoever bids the lowest contingency that the river and the ground don't overrun takes the account.

## The Problem
Instrumentation alarms are set at fixed thresholds by the designer. A slow trend inside a cofferdam is noticed only when someone looks at the chart.

## What Already Exists
Instrumentation data loggers and cloud monitoring platforms, the owner's threshold criteria, and USGS real-time gauges.

## The Customization Gap
Trend detection against the excavation stage and pumping rate, river-stage forecasts joined in, and alerts written to the work plan ("hold excavation below elevation X until piezometer P3 stabilises"). Every reading is logged for the owner's dam-safety and quality records.

## Target Customer
Project engineers and geotechnical leads on in-the-wet projects.

## Impact If Solved
Catching a cofferdam seepage trend a few days earlier (estimate) can avoid a flooding event that costs ~4–12 weeks and ~$2M–$20M to recover on a large lock or dam job (estimate).

# Fleet Scheduling Taught the Corps' Advertising Calendar

**Niche:** [[niches/heavy-civil-contractors/dredging-navigation-contracts/profile|Dredging & Navigation Channel Contracts]]
**Industry:** [[industries/heavy-civil-contractors|Heavy Civil Contractors]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Generic fleet and resource planners move assets between jobs; a dredger needs one that knows which Corps lettings are coming, which dredge fits each, and what mobilisation costs.
**Tags:** #optimization-fundamentals #dynamic-programming #monte-carlo-methods #probability-distributions #workflow-orchestration #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to have a DQM-certified dredge of the right type mobilised near the work when the Corps advertises, at a price under 125% of the government estimate, and whoever books the most working dredge-days a year at awardable prices takes the account.

## The Problem
A dredging fleet's profit is utilisation. Which lettings to chase, in what order, with which dredge, given environmental windows and transit times, is decided by a few executives with a whiteboard.

## What Already Exists
Enterprise resource scheduling and fleet planning tools, and the Corps' published advertising and five-year contracted dredging schedules.

## The Customization Gap
Dredge-type suitability by material and pumping distance, mobilisation cost and time between ports, environmental windows by region, the win probability of each letting given likely competitors, and the 125% ceiling, all combined into a plan that is re-run when an award or loss lands.

## Target Customer
COOs and fleet managers at dredging contractors running ~3–20 dredges (estimate).

## Impact If Solved
If idle dredge-days fall by ~10 a year per dredge (estimate) at ~$40,000–$100,000 of daily cost (estimate), each dredge recovers ~$0.4M–$1M a year.

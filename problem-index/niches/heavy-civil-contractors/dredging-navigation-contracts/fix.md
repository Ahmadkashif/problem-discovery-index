# Unpaid Overdepth Found After the Final Survey

**Niche:** [[niches/heavy-civil-contractors/dredging-navigation-contracts/profile|Dredging & Navigation Channel Contracts]]
**Industry:** [[industries/heavy-civil-contractors|Heavy Civil Contractors]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Dredges cut deeper than the paid prism and nobody sees how much until the government's after-survey, when the material is already moved and unpaid.
**Tags:** #numerical-methods #gaussian-processes #change-point-detection #data-integration #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to have a DQM-certified dredge of the right type mobilised near the work when the Corps advertises, at a price under 125% of the government estimate, and whoever books the most working dredge-days a year at awardable prices takes the account.

## The Problem
Under UFGS-35 20 23 only material down to the allowable overdepth is paid. In soft material a cutter runs deep, and the cost of every extra yard is the contractor's.

## Why It's Still Broken
Progress surveys are processed days later, the cut depth on the leverman's display isn't compared with the pay template by reach, and the surveyor's volumes come as totals.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Daily pay-template volumes by reach from the contractor's own surveys and the dredge's GPS cut log. Paid, unpaid and side-slope material are mapped, and the reaches running deep are flagged to the superintendent the next morning.

## Who Feels the Pain
Superintendents and levermen blamed for low pay volume, project managers who argue the final quantity, and surveyors working nights.

## Impact If Fixed
If unpaid overdepth falls from ~10% to ~5% of material moved (estimate), a $20M contract (illustrative) keeps ~$1M of production cost from going unpaid.

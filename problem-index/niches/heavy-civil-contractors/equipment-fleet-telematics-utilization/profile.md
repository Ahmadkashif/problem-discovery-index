# Equipment Fleet Telematics & Utilisation

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/heavy-civil-contractors|Heavy Civil Contractors]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn mixed-OEM telematics into equipment hours posted to job cost codes and hourly rates estimators bid with, and the vendor whose posted hours and rates match audited job cost most closely takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$6B a year of owned-equipment ownership and operating cost at 237990 contractors (estimate: ~15–20% of receipts), and ~$80M a year of fleet management and telematics software spend (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~15–20% of receipts in cost (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — most newer machines report through OEM portals and AEMP 2.0 (ISO 15143-3) feeds, but hours still reach job cost through foremen's entries and equipment rates are updated yearly in a spreadsheet
**Target Buyer:** Equipment manager or VP of equipment; the CFO for rate and fleet-mix decisions
**Automation Potential:** Very high for hour posting, idle detection and rate calculation; medium for buy, rent and sell decisions

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Equipment is the heavy civil contractor's largest owned asset and the second input to every unit price after labour. Fleets mix Caterpillar, Komatsu, Deere, Volvo and others plus floating plant, older units without telematics, and rentals. The same machine-hour has to land in four places: the job's cost code, the equipment's maintenance record, the internal rate calculation, and the estimate's crew cost. Telematics standards now let one system pull location, hours and fuel from every OEM, so the work is mechanical, but most firms still re-key it. The renter's version of the problem, time and dollar utilisation of a rental fleet, belongs to the queued equipment rental build.

## Current Tools & Gaps
OEM portals (Cat VisionLink and peers), mixed-fleet platforms such as Tenna and Trimble's B2W Maintain, HCSS's equipment and telematics modules, aftermarket trackers. The gaps: hours posted to jobs by hand, idle time hidden in hour meters, and internal rates that don't reflect real utilisation.

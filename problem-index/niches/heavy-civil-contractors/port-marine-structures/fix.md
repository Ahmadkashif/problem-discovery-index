# Pile Acceptance Held Up by Paper

**Niche:** [[niches/heavy-civil-contractors/port-marine-structures/profile|Port, Wharf & Marine Structures]]
**Industry:** [[industries/heavy-civil-contractors|Heavy Civil Contractors]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Pile caps can't be poured until each pile is accepted, and acceptance waits on handwritten logs travelling from barge to engineer.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #data-integration #workflow-orchestration #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to drive, cap and deck wharves, piers and bulkheads while the port keeps ships working at the neighbouring berths, and whoever hands back each berth with the fewest days of closure takes the account.

## The Problem
Driving logs are written on the barge, scanned or retyped, checked by the engineer of record, then accepted. A missing log holds the next pour.

## Why It's Still Broken
Pile-driving analysers record digitally, but the field log, cutoff elevation and as-built position are still paper or spreadsheets, and the engineer works from email.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A tablet log that captures blow counts, elevations and position at the hammer, merges the analyser record, and routes each pile for acceptance with exceptions flagged. Accepted piles release their cap pour in the schedule.

## Who Feels the Pain
Pile foremen retyping logs, field engineers chasing signatures, and concrete crews waiting.

## Impact If Fixed
If acceptance turnaround falls from ~2–3 days to same day (estimate), cap pours follow driving without gaps, saving about a week on a typical wharf phase (estimate).

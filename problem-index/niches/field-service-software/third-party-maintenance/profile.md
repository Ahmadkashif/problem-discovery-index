# Third-Party Maintenance — Parts Without the Manufacturer

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/field-service-software|Field Service Software]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in third-party maintenance software is fighting to source, verify and certify a part for equipment its customer's manufacturer would rather it did not service — and whoever makes parts provenance reliable takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$550M US software spend across independent service organisations and third-party maintainers
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~11% of field service software revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — strong in dispatch and contracts, weak in parts sourcing and technical documentation
**Target Buyer:** Owners and operations directors at independent service organisations across IT hardware, medical imaging, industrial and laboratory equipment
**Automation Potential:** High — parts identification, cross-referencing and provenance tracking are matching and record-keeping problems done by phone

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Third-party maintainers service equipment they did not build, for customers who choose them because they are cheaper or more responsive than the manufacturer, in a relationship where the manufacturer is a competitor rather than a supplier. That single fact determines every operational problem the segment has. Technical documentation is restricted. Diagnostic software may be locked. Parts must be sourced from brokers, from harvested machines, from independent manufacturers, or from the OEM at a price set by a competitor — and each of those sources carries a different question about whether the part is genuine, functional and appropriate for the exact configuration. A wrong or counterfactual part in medical imaging or laboratory equipment is a safety and regulatory matter, not an inconvenience. The segment's contested capability is therefore parts provenance, and it is a genuinely different business from the OEM's prediction problem.

## Current Tools & Gaps
General field service platforms serve dispatch and contracts adequately. Parts sourcing runs on broker relationships, marketplaces of varying quality, phone calls, and an inventory of harvested components in a warehouse whose contents are known to whoever manages it. Cross-referencing a part across OEM numbers, aftermarket equivalents and superseded revisions is done by experienced people from memory and binders. Documentation is assembled informally. Right-to-repair developments have improved access in some categories and not others. The gaps are consistent: no system tracks a part's provenance and test history as a record that travels with it, no system cross-references part numbers reliably across sources, and nothing assembles the configuration-specific compatibility knowledge that determines whether a part will actually work in this machine at this revision.

# Residential Trades Platforms

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/field-service-software|Field Service Software]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in residential trades software is fighting to predict what the job actually is before the truck leaves, and to match the technician and the parts to it — and whoever moves first-time fix rate most takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$2.1B US residential field service management software
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~42% of field service software revenue
**Digital Adoption:** High — scheduling, dispatch, invoicing and payments are on platform at most established residential contractors
**Target Buyer:** Owners and general managers of residential HVAC, plumbing and electrical businesses; product leads at ServiceTitan, Jobber, Housecall Pro and FieldEdge
**Automation Potential:** Very High — diagnosis prediction, technician matching and parts pre-loading are all supervised problems on data the vendor already holds

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A residential service business's economics reduce to one ratio: how often a technician resolves the problem on the first visit. A return trip consumes a slot in a day that has six of them, a drive across a territory, parts handling, and the customer's willingness to recommend the company. First-time fix is decided at dispatch, from a customer's description — "the air conditioning is making a noise" — matched against a dispatcher's mental model of which technician handles which equipment well and what is on which truck. The platform vendor, meanwhile, holds millions of visits across every make and model, with the reported symptom, the diagnosis reached, the parts consumed and whether anyone had to come back. It is the only party with the full service life of the installed base across brands, and it prints invoices with it.

## Current Tools & Gaps
ServiceTitan dominates the larger residential contractors with deep vertical functionality; Jobber and Housecall Pro serve the smaller end well; FieldEdge and others compete in the middle. All of them offer routing and scheduling optimisation and all of them see low adoption of it, because the constraints that decide a real dispatch — who is good with this brand of furnace, who the customer liked last time, which truck has the part — are informal and unmodelled. Symptom-to-diagnosis prediction does not exist anywhere in the category. Parts inventory on trucks is tracked poorly and is almost never used as a dispatch constraint. The result is that the industry's decisive decision is made by a person with a card board and the industry's decisive dataset is used for reporting.

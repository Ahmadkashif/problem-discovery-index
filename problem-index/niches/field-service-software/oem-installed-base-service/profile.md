# OEM Service on Its Own Installed Base

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/field-service-software|Field Service Software]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in OEM service software is fighting to convert telemetry and engineering knowledge from the manufacturer's own installed base into a dispatched action before the customer notices a fault — and whoever converts most reliably takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$850M US software spend across equipment manufacturers' service organisations
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~17% of field service software revenue
**Digital Adoption:** High — OEM service organisations are among the most systematised in the industry
**Target Buyer:** VPs of Service and aftermarket directors at equipment manufacturers; connected-product and service transformation leads
**Automation Potential:** Very High — the data is proprietary, continuous, and mostly unused for service decisions

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A manufacturer servicing its own machines holds an information position no other service provider can approach. It designed the equipment, so it knows the failure modes. It holds warranty and field failure history across the entire installed base. In a growing share of categories it receives telemetry continuously from machines in the field. And it controls parts. The contested capability follows directly: with that position, the manufacturer should know a machine is failing before the customer does and should arrive with the right part without being called. The ones that do this well convert their service business from a cost centre into the majority of their profit, and the ones that do not are outcompeted on service by third parties who have none of the advantages. What makes this terminal under the filter is that the capability is measurable — the share of service events that were predicted rather than reported.

## Current Tools & Gaps
ServiceMax, Salesforce Field Service, IFS and PTC's service lifecycle products serve this market, and connected-product platforms — the OEM IoT stacks — sit alongside them. The gap is almost always the join. Telemetry lands in a connected-product platform built by engineering; service dispatch happens in a service platform built by operations; and the two are connected, if at all, by a threshold alert that fires on a sensor value and generates a case. Nothing models a machine's condition against its own failure history to predict a specific component's failure with enough lead time and enough precision for a service action. Engineering's reliability knowledge and field service's outcome data are in separate organisations and separate systems, which is the structural reason the manufacturer's advantage goes unused.

# Flat-Rate Price Book Content

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/field-service-software|Field Service Software]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in price book content is fighting to make a task's price right for this trade in this market with this contractor's cost structure — and whoever needs the least customisation on arrival takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$190M US spend on flat-rate price book content, licensed and maintained
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~4% of field service software revenue, and the most-cited reason contractors switch platforms
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — delivered in software, maintained by content teams and then rebuilt by every contractor
**Target Buyer:** Content directors at price book vendors and platforms; contractors who have spent weeks customising one
**Automation Potential:** Very High — the pricing signal is in the contractor's own won-and-lost job history and is never used

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Flat-rate pricing means a technician quotes a fixed price for a task from a book rather than charging time and materials, which customers prefer and which requires the book to be right. A book has thousands of tasks, each needing a labour time, a materials cost, and a price that reflects the local market. National average pricing is wrong in every specific market: labour rates, competitive intensity, customer willingness to pay and even typical equipment vary enormously between a dense metropolitan area and a small town two hundred miles away. So every contractor licenses a book and then spends weeks customising it, usually once, at implementation, after which it ages. It is the single most-cited reason contractors switch platforms, which makes it commercially central out of proportion to its apparent triviality.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Profit Rhino, Callahan and a handful of others license price book content; ServiceTitan and the larger platforms bundle or integrate one; many contractors maintain their own in a spreadsheet lineage going back years. Content is maintained by people who price tasks from labour standards and supplier costs, updated periodically. Regional adjustment, where offered, is a multiplier applied to a national base, which is a crude instrument for a genuinely local question. The decisive gap is that nobody uses the outcome data: every platform records which quoted jobs were accepted and which were declined, with the price, the task, the market and the competitor context implied — a complete record of price sensitivity that would let a book be fitted to a market, and it is used for a close-rate report.

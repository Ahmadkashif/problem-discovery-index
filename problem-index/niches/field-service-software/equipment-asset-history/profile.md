# Equipment Asset History

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/field-service-software|Field Service Software]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in field service is fighting to populate an equipment record completely without costing the technician a minute — and whoever gets record completeness per visit highest takes the capability everything else in the category depends on.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$340M attributable to asset and equipment management within field service platforms
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~7% of field service software revenue, and a precondition for most of the rest
**Digital Adoption:** Low in substance — every platform has an equipment record and almost none are populated usefully
**Target Buyer:** Product leads at every field service platform; contractors who have tried to run a maintenance agreement programme
**Automation Potential:** Very High — nameplates are photographable, parts are scannable, and the record can be built from the visit

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The equipment record is the field service industry's unbuilt foundation. Every platform has one. Almost none contain enough to be useful: a make, sometimes a model, rarely a serial, seldom an install date, and a service history that consists of invoice descriptions. The reason is simple and is the whole niche — populating one properly costs a technician ten minutes they are not paid for, at the end of a job, for a benefit that accrues to somebody else on a future visit. The consequence propagates everywhere. Diagnosis prediction needs equipment identity. Maintenance agreement programmes, which are the most valuable recurring revenue in the trades, need to know what equipment is under agreement and what its condition is. Replacement recommendations need age and service history. Warranty recovery needs serial numbers. Every one of those is worth real money and every one is blocked by a data collection problem that nobody has made free.

## Current Tools & Gaps
All the major platforms have equipment and asset modules with fields for make, model, serial, install date, warranty and location, plus service history linkage. The fields are optional because making them mandatory causes technicians to enter placeholders, which is worse. Some vendors have added nameplate photo capture, which stores an image rather than extracting the data. Manufacturer model databases exist and are not integrated, so a model number, once captured, is not enriched into specifications, common failure modes, parts lists or warranty terms. Condition assessment is entirely absent — the platform records that a unit was serviced, not what state it is in — which is the single most valuable field for the replacement and maintenance-agreement conversations the contractor actually wants to have.

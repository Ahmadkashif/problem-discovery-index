# Commercial & Enterprise Field Service

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/field-service-software|Field Service Software]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is that every competitor is fighting to keep expensive equipment running with fewer visits — but a manufacturer servicing its own installed base and a third-party maintainer servicing someone else's face opposite problems with opposite data. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.4B US commercial and enterprise field service management software
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~28% of field service software revenue, with the largest individual contracts
**Digital Adoption:** High — enterprise service organisations run mature systems with contract, entitlement and parts management
**Target Buyer:** Service operations directors at equipment manufacturers, commercial service providers and third-party maintainers
**Automation Potential:** High but split — predictive maintenance where telemetry exists, parts and provenance where it does not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Commercial field service is about equipment whose downtime is expensive: medical imaging, industrial refrigeration, commercial HVAC, semiconductor and packaging equipment, elevators, laboratory instruments. Service is sold as a contract with uptime commitments rather than as a visit, which changes everything about the software — entitlement, contract profitability, parts logistics and response time guarantees are all first-class concerns that residential platforms do not have. What the category label conceals is that the buyers divide into two populations with opposite situations. The manufacturer servicing its own machines has design knowledge, telemetry from the installed base, failure history and parts supply; its whole contest is converting that into an action before the customer calls. The third-party maintainer servicing the same machines has none of those things and is frequently obstructed from getting them; its contest is parts and provenance. One product cannot serve both.

## Current Tools & Gaps
ServiceMax, Salesforce Field Service, IFS and SAP's service modules anchor the enterprise market with contract, entitlement, parts and workforce management, and they do that well. Remote monitoring and condition-based maintenance are real in commercial refrigeration, elevators and imaging and are largely absent elsewhere. The consistent gap across both populations is that service contract profitability is computed retrospectively at the account level, so nobody knows which contracts are losing money until they have lost it, and nobody prices a renewal from anything except last year's price plus an increase. The specific gaps diverge from there, which is what the decomposition below is about.

# Brokerage Trust Accounting Adapted to Auction Escrow

**Niche:** [[niches/auctioneers/post-sale-settlement-and-pickup/profile|Post-Sale Settlement & Pickup]]
**Industry:** [[industries/auctioneers|Auctioneers]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Real estate brokerages already run regulated trust accounts with software built for audit; auctioneers' escrow accounts run on general ledgers and spreadsheets.
**Tags:** #compliance #data-integration #automation #workflow-orchestration #descriptive-statistics
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to own the money flow after the hammer, and whoever collects from bidders, settles every consignor and reconciles the escrow account fastest without a licensing-board complaint takes the account.

## The Problem
Licensing states regulate how auctioneers hold other people's money: deposit deadlines, separate ledgers, reconciliation, record retention and interest. Few auction firms use software designed for a regulated trust account.

## What Already Exists
Trust accounting modules in real estate brokerage back-office software, with per-transaction ledgers, three-way reconciliation and audit reports for state regulators.

## The Customization Gap
The auction version needs: many consignors per sale with tiered commissions and charges; buyer's premium and sales tax separated from consignor proceeds; unpaid-lot reversals and resales; platform payouts net of fees reconciled to gross sales; consignor advances; and each licensing state's deadlines and reports, such as Florida's two-working-day deposit and monthly reconciliation.

## Target Customer
Auction firms in licensing states, especially those holding large consignor balances between sale and settlement.

## Impact If Solved
Monthly escrow reconciliation would drop from ~4–8 hours to under an hour of review (estimate), with every deposit traceable to its sale and consignor when the licensing board examines the account.

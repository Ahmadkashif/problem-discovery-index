# Bidder Default Risk Scored at Registration

**Niche:** [[niches/auctioneers/post-sale-settlement-and-pickup/profile|Post-Sale Settlement & Pickup]]
**Industry:** [[industries/auctioneers|Auctioneers]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Auction firms learn a bidder will not pay after they have won, when the lot has already missed its best buyer.
**Tags:** #gradient-boosting #logistic-regression #graph-theory #evaluation-metrics #feature-engineering #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to own the money flow after the hammer, and whoever collects from bidders, settles every consignor and reconciles the escrow account fastest without a licensing-board complaint takes the account.

## The Problem
Every online timed sale produces some winners who never pay or never collect (~2–8% of winning bids by practitioner accounts, estimate). Each one means a second-chance offer or a relisting, a delayed settlement and a frustrated consignor. Firms approve registrations with a card on file and little else.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Each firm sees few defaults and platforms keep cross-firm histories to themselves; Auction Flex lets firms grade bidders for non-payment, but grades are manual and local.

## What to Build
A registration-time score from the bidder's history across the firm's sales, platform signals, payment instrument checks and linked identities, used to require a deposit or a card pre-authorisation for risky registrants, plus a post-close model that predicts non-collection so pickups are confirmed early.

## Target Customer
Auction firms running weekly online timed sales, and the platforms whose payment products they use.

## Impact If Built
Fewer unpaid lots and relistings, faster settlements, and better prices from second-chance offers made while under-bidders are still interested.

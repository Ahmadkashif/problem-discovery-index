## The Problem
Between consignment and settlement the executor, and the siblings watching the executor, cannot see which items were lotted together, what sold for what, what passed and why, or what was discarded. Questions arrive by phone and email, often from several heirs, and the auctioneer answers them one by one.

## Why It's Still Broken
Clerking software is built for the auction firm, not for the consignor. Statements are produced once, at settlement, as a document rather than a live view.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A consignor portal populated from the catalogue and clerking data: lot photos and descriptions as listed, live bids during the sale, hammer and fees after, unsold items and their disposition, and a plain-language settlement, with routine questions answered from the record and the rest routed to staff.

## Who Feels the Pain
Executors answering to heirs, auction office staff fielding repeated calls, and owners who lose referrals over a sale that went well but looked opaque.

## Impact If Fixed
Fewer calls per estate, faster acceptance of the settlement, and a consignor experience attorneys will recommend.

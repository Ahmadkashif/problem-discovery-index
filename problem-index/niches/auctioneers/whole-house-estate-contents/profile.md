# Whole-House Estate Contents

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/auctioneers|Auctioneers]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to clear a whole house by the executor's closing date while netting more than an estate-sale company or a cleanout would, and whoever can lot, photograph and close a full house in the fewest crew-days without leaving value in the box lots takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$700M–1B a year of U.S. commission and buyer's premium on household contents sold at estate auctions (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~15% of auctioneer commission and fee revenue (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — online timed sales are the norm; lotting, photography and crew scheduling run on experience
**Target Buyer:** Owner-auctioneer or operations manager of a local estate auction firm
**Automation Potential:** High — lot drafting, photography workflow and scheduling repeat in every house

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Most of a house is worth little item by item, and the contest is throughput against a deadline. The executor wants the house empty and broom-clean before closing; the alternatives are a tag sale run by an estate-sale company (often on a percentage), a cleanout firm that charges to haul, or donation. The auction firm wins when it can turn a full house into a few hundred online lots, close the sale and run pickup within the window, and still return more than the tag sale. The deciding variables are crew-days per house, lotting judgement (when to group and when to split) and the box lots, where both value leakage and wasted labour hide.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Clerking and cataloguing apps (Wavebid, AuctionMethod, Auction Flex), HiBid and Proxibid for bidding, field-service scheduling borrowed from other trades or none. Gaps: workload forecasts from a walkthrough, scheduling across overlapping houses against closing dates, and a check on box lots before they leave.

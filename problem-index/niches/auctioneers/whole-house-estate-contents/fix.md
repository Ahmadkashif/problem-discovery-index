# The Box Lot That Hid the Best Piece

**Niche:** [[niches/auctioneers/whole-house-estate-contents/profile|Whole-House Estate Contents]]
**Industry:** [[industries/auctioneers|Auctioneers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Dealers buy estate box lots hoping for the signed piece or the first edition the crew missed, and too often they find it.
**Tags:** #cnns #object-detection #k-nearest-neighbors #transfer-learning #tacit-knowledge-ml #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to clear a whole house by the executor's closing date while netting more than an estate-sale company or a cleanout would, and whoever can lot, photograph and close a full house in the fewest crew-days without leaving value in the box lots takes the account.

## The Problem
Under deadline, crews group low-value items into box lots: a shelf of books, a drawer of jewellery, a crate of tools. Box lots are where an estate leaks value. A pin with a maker's mark, a scarce book, a collectible tool brand goes for a few dollars to a dealer who knows, and the executor never finds out.

## Why It's Still Broken
Checking every item in every box takes the time the crew does not have, and only experienced staff recognise what to pull. The box is photographed as a group, so nothing downstream checks it either.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A quick scan: the box contents are spread on a table and photographed once; a model flags items that resemble higher-value sold comparables (marks, bindings, brands) for the cataloguer to pull and lot separately. The flags and outcomes are logged so the scan improves.

## Who Feels the Pain
Executors and heirs who are underpaid without knowing it, and auctioneers whose reputation suffers when a dealer's find becomes a local story.

## Impact If Fixed
A few pulled pieces per house can add materially to an estate's net (estimate), for minutes of crew time.

**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to persuade a consignor that it will put a valuable piece in front of the bidders who will pay most for it, and whoever's attribution and estimate are confirmed by the hammer, rather than undercut by a passed lot or a post-sale re-attribution, takes the account.

## The Problem
Preparing a consignment proposal for a collection means researching each piece: past results for the artist or maker, size, medium, subject, condition, date and venue. Specialists do this in several databases and then write a memo by hand.

## What Already Exists
The Artnet Price Database, Artprice, MutualArt, Invaluable's price archive, LiveAuctioneers' free price results and WorthPoint for decorative arts and collectibles, each searchable by keyword and filters.

## The Customization Gap
A specialist's tool needs image-based retrieval rather than keyword search alone; adjustment for size, condition, date and venue; the house's own unpublished results and buyer notes alongside public results; deduplication of the same piece re-offered across sales; and output as a consignor proposal with a range and the comparables behind it.

## Target Customer
Specialists and business-getters at regional houses preparing proposals against competing houses.

## Impact If Solved
Proposals in hours rather than days, with evidence the consignor can check.

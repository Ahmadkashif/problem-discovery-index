# Liquidation Estimates a Trustee Can File

**Industry:** [[industries/auctioneers|Auctioneers]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Trustees choose liquidators on an estimate of net proceeds that is usually a single number in a letter, which neither the court nor creditors can test.

**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to be the liquidator a trustee, receiver or secured lender retains, and whoever's up-front liquidation estimate is met at the sale, so the court-approved fee and any guarantee hold, takes the account.

## The Problem
A Chapter 7 trustee with a closed fabrication shop asks two auctioneers what the equipment will net. Each walks the floor and replies with a figure and a fee proposal. The trustee needs a number to decide whether to sell piecemeal, accept a bulk offer, or abandon assets, and creditors may object. A high estimate wins the retention and later disappoints; a low one loses the case.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Regional liquidation auctioneers have years of results but no analyst, and the estimate is a letter, not a model. Value guides price individual machines for lenders, not net proceeds after removal, rigging and fees in a forced sale.

## What to Build
An estimate tool that inventories assets from a walkthrough, prices each from the firm's own forced-sale results and public comparables with intervals, subtracts removal, rigging and selling costs, compares piecemeal and bulk scenarios, and produces an exhibit for the retention or sale motion. Results are joined back to estimates after each sale.

## Target Customer
Regional industrial and commercial liquidation auctioneers serving trustees, receivers and lenders.

## Impact If Built
Retentions won on evidence, fewer disappointed trustees, and estimates that can survive an objection.

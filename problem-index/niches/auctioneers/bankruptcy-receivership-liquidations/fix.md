# The Report of Sale Typed From a Clerking Printout

**Niche:** [[niches/auctioneers/bankruptcy-receivership-liquidations/profile|Bankruptcy, Receivership & Court-Ordered Liquidations]]
**Industry:** [[industries/auctioneers|Auctioneers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** After a court-authorised sale the auctioneer owes an itemised report, and someone retypes hundreds of lots, buyers and prices into a court filing.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #seq2seq #compliance #automation #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to be the liquidator a trustee, receiver or secured lender retains, and whoever's up-front liquidation estimate is met at the sale, so the court-approved fee and any guarantee hold, takes the account.

## The Problem
Bankruptcy practice requires an itemised statement of property sold, with prices and buyers, after a sale outside the ordinary course (Rule 6004(f), recalled), and the auctioneer's fee and expenses must be supported for approval. The data is in the clerking system, but the filing is assembled by hand in a word processor, late and with errors.

## Why It's Still Broken
Small liquidation firms do a handful of court sales a year each, not enough to justify building templates, and clerking software is not designed for court formats.

## What a Fix Looks Like
An export that turns sale results into the report of sale, an expense schedule and a draft fee application in the district's format, with lot-level reconciliation to receipts, for the auctioneer and the trustee's counsel to review.

## Who Feels the Pain
Auctioneers' office staff, trustees waiting to close cases, and counsel correcting filings.

## Impact If Fixed
Hours saved per case and fewer amended filings.

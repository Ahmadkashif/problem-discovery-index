# Restricted Items Found After the Listing Went Live

**Niche:** [[niches/auctioneers/lot-cataloguing-and-listing/profile|Lot Cataloguing & Online Listing]]
**Industry:** [[industries/auctioneers|Auctioneers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A firearm listed without dealer-transfer terms, an ivory carving, a can of old pesticide: restricted items slip into catalogues and are caught by a bidder or a platform, not the firm.
**Tags:** #cnns #object-detection #bert #compliance #evaluation-metrics #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn a photo table into a published, searchable catalogue, and whoever gets a sale listed in the fewest cataloguer-hours with the fewest "not as described" disputes takes the account.

## The Problem
Estate and consignment sales contain items with legal restrictions: firearms and ammunition, ivory, tortoiseshell and some feathers under wildlife law, hazardous chemicals, prescription drugs, titled vehicles and trailers needing paperwork. Cataloguers under time pressure miss them, and the consequences range from a platform takedown to a legal problem.

## Why It's Still Broken
Restricted-item knowledge sits with experienced staff, rules vary by state and platform, and nothing in the cataloguing flow checks lots before publication.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A pre-publication check that scans every lot's photos and text for restricted categories, applies the platform's and the state's rules, and either adds required terms (dealer transfer, pickup only, title to follow) or holds the lot for review.

## Who Feels the Pain
Owners exposed to legal and platform risk, cataloguers blamed for misses, and bidders who win items they cannot legally receive.

## Impact If Fixed
Restricted items caught before listing and fewer takedowns and cancellations.

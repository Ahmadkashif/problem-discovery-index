# International Auction House Specialist & Valuation Departments

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/auctioneers|Auctioneers]]
**Category:** Insight Layer
**Value-Chain Position:** Specialist advisory & valuation

## What They Do
The specialist departments at Sotheby's ($5.7B of auction sales in 2025), Christie's ($4.7B), Heritage ($2.16B total sales in 2025), Phillips and Bonhams research, attribute and estimate every lot, pitch for consignments with valuation proposals, and sell insurance, estate and collection valuations as a separate service. Consignors choose a house partly on the specialist's estimate and market knowledge; the house is paid by seller's commission and buyer's premium.

## Insight Function
**Size:** 100-600 specialists and valuers per major house (estimate)
**Output:** Lot attributions and estimates, consignment proposals, paid valuations for insurance and probate
**Proprietary data:** Bidder and consignor records, condition files and archive photographs are the house's own; the estimates themselves are printed in public catalogues and the results are published (Heritage keeps public archives), which is why academic studies such as Mei and Moses could grade them from public data
**Clock:** The house's own sale calendar, which it sets itself; estate tax filing dates apply only to the minority of probate valuations
**Buyer:** Chairman of a specialist division / Head of Valuations

**Kill switches:** none — the archive is the house's own; consignor identities are confidential but not needed to grade estimates.
**Verdict:** Logged — below threshold. Large specialist teams, but the estimate wins commission rather than being invoiced, the estimate-to-hammer record is largely public, the sale calendar is the house's own, and the market is a handful of houses. Academic work using that public record found these houses' estimates for expensive paintings biased upward (Mei and Moses, 2005).

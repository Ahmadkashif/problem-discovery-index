# Disposal Timing for the Public Fleet

**Niche:** [[niches/auctioneers/public-surplus-sellers/profile|Municipal & Public Surplus Sellers]]
**Industry:** [[industries/auctioneers|Auctioneers]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Small public fleets sell retired trucks and mowers whenever paperwork allows, not when resale value peaks, and nobody measures what the delay costs.
**Tags:** #gradient-boosting #time-series-forecasting #survival-analysis #evaluation-metrics #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to become a public body's standing surplus-disposal contractor, and whoever returns the highest net proceeds with a disposal record that satisfies the auditor and the public-records request takes the account.

## The Problem
A county replaces a dump truck. The old one sits in the yard through budget season, a council meeting and a holiday, loses value, and finally sells in a month when buyers are scarce. Multiplied across a fleet and years, delayed and badly timed disposals cost real money that never appears in a budget line.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Fleet software tracks maintenance and replacement, marketplaces sell what is listed, and nobody owns the decision of when to list.

## What to Build
A disposal planner that forecasts each retiring asset's resale value by month from auction results by class, age, hours and region, flags the cost of holding it, recommends a listing window, and drafts the surplus declaration in time for the right meeting.

## Target Customer
Fleet managers and purchasing agents at small and mid-size public bodies, sold by the auctioneers and marketplaces that want their contracts.

## Impact If Built
Listing each retired vehicle or machine in its best month, rather than whenever the paperwork clears, could lift proceeds ~5–10% per asset (estimate) and avoid months of holding depreciation, with a documented timing rationale an auditor can follow.

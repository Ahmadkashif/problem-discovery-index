# Municipal & Public Surplus Sellers

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/auctioneers|Auctioneers]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to become a public body's standing surplus-disposal contractor, and whoever returns the highest net proceeds with a disposal record that satisfies the auditor and the public-records request takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$150–300M a year of U.S. fees and buyer's premiums on surplus vehicles, equipment and property sold for cities, counties, school districts and utilities (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~5% of auctioneer commission and fee revenue (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — online sales are common, but surplus declarations, approvals and asset records run on paper and council agendas
**Target Buyer:** Fleet manager, purchasing agent or finance director of a small public body
**Automation Potential:** Medium-High — disposal paperwork and listings repeat; timing decisions need judgement

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Public bodies must dispose of surplus property under statute or local ordinance: a governing body declares it surplus, the sale is publicly noticed or run through an approved method, proceeds go to the right fund, and the record is open to audit and public-records requests. Small towns and school districts have no disposal staff. Their options are online government marketplaces such as GovDeals and AllSurplus (Liquidity Services reported ~$1.57B of GMV across its marketplaces in FY2025), equipment auctioneers on cooperative purchasing contracts such as Purple Wave, and local auctioneers running a consignment sale. Tools and contracts are designed for large fleets and commercial consignors.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Government auction marketplaces, cooperative purchasing agreements, fleet management software, spreadsheets of retired assets. Gaps: timing disposals against resale values, linking the asset register to the sale, and an auditable record from declaration to deposit.

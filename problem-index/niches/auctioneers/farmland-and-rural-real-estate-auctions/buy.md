# Farmland Comparables Adjusted for Auction Format

**Niche:** [[niches/auctioneers/farmland-and-rural-real-estate-auctions/profile|Farmland & Rural Real Estate Auctions]]
**Industry:** [[industries/auctioneers|Auctioneers]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Farmland data platforms already sell parcel data and comparable sales; they treat an auction sale and a private sale as the same kind of comparable.
**Tags:** #linear-regression #feature-engineering #causal-inference #data-integration #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to convince heirs or a retiring landowner that a public auction on a set date will beat a private listing, and whoever's price-per-acre call against soil productivity and recent county sales proves closest to the hammer takes the account.

## The Problem
An auctioneer arguing for auction over private listing needs to show what auction achieves. Comparable-sale tools mix the two channels and rarely record format, tract structure or the number of registered bidders.

## What Already Exists
Farmland valuation data providers with parcel, soil and sale records, county assessor data, and land value surveys by universities and associations. See [[niches/crop-farming/farmland-valuation-data/profile|Farmland Valuation Data Providers]].

## The Customization Gap
The auction firm needs: channel labels on every comparable (auction, private, family transfer); auction mechanics as features (tracts, buyer's choice, absolute or reserve, online share); the firm's own registered-bidder counts and underbids; adjustments for sale-day rates and commodity prices; and a presentation for families comparing an auction with a listing.

## Target Customer
Broker-auctioneers who already subscribe to a farmland data platform.

## Impact If Solved
A defensible case for auction where it wins, and an honest one for listing where it does not.

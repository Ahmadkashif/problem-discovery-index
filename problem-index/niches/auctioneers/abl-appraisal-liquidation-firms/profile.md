# Asset-Based Lending Appraisal & Liquidation Firms

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/auctioneers|Auctioneers]]
**Category:** Insight Layer
**Value-Chain Position:** Specialist advisory & valuation

## What They Do
Hilco (Hilco Valuation Services; ORIX agreed in July 2025 to buy 70% at a ~$1B valuation), Gordon Brothers, Tiger Group and Great American Group (majority sold by B. Riley to Oaktree in November 2024 at a $386M enterprise value) appraise inventory, machinery and equipment for asset-based lenders and then, when borrowers fail, run the auctions and liquidations that realise the collateral. Lenders pay for the net orderly liquidation value (NOLV) appraisals that set borrowing bases; credit agreements often name one of these firms as the approved appraiser. These are the equipment appraisal and liquidation firms [[industries/repossession-services|Repossession Agencies]] hands recovered collateral to.

## Insight Function
**Size:** ~10–25 central valuation-analytics and recovery-data staff at the largest firm (estimate). Hilco says it runs a proprietary machine-learning inventory valuation platform with "a team of data scientists and analysts" but gives no headcount. The field appraisers who write one report per borrower are production staff and are not counted.
**Output:** NOLV and orderly liquidation value appraisals of inventory and machinery, recurring re-appraisals, liquidation analyses for restructurings, expert testimony
**Proprietary data:** Decades of appraisals joined to the firms' own liquidation and auction outcomes, the only record of what an appraised liquidation value actually recovered; none of the four firms publishes an NOLV-to-recovery index
**Clock:** Appraisal frequencies and borrowing-base cycles written into credit agreements (contract dates between lender and borrower, not regulator deadlines); bankruptcy court sale and hearing dates on the minority of engagements that end in liquidation
**Buyer:** Head of Valuation Services / Chief Appraisal Officer

**Kill switches:** live: the data isn't theirs to use. Each appraisal is built on the borrower's perpetual inventory, cost and sales records, supplied through the lender-client under engagement confidentiality. The report is addressed to the lender with reliance limits, and for public borrowers the data can be material non-public information. No checked evidence shows that engagement terms permit pooling across clients. Scored consistently with [[niches/hedge-funds/credit-fund-valuation-advisory/profile|Portfolio Valuation Advisory for Credit Funds]] (53, kill switch). A version free of the kill switch, built only on NOLVs and recoveries disclosed in public bankruptcy filings, would cap Q3 at 3 and score ~44.
**Verdict:** Logged — kill switch. On the rubric this is the strongest pocket in the chain: the valuation is invoiced, the recurring appraisals repeat, and only these firms can join an appraisal to its recovery. All of it rests on borrower data held in confidence.

# Personal Training & Coaching

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/fitness-wellness-software|Fitness & Wellness Software]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is that every competitor is fighting to help a coach keep clients doing the programme — but an in-person trainer's constraint is hours and a remote coach's is observation, which are different products. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$540M US personal training and coaching software
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~22% of fitness software revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Medium-High — programme delivery and billing are systematised; the coaching relationship is not
**Target Buyer:** Independent trainers, small training studios and online coaching businesses
**Automation Potential:** High but split — scheduling and roster economics on one side, adherence and engagement on the other

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Coaching businesses are the part of the fitness industry where the product is a relationship and the revenue is per client rather than per membership. That makes their unit economics unusually legible — a coach knows exactly what each client is worth and roughly what each costs in time — and it makes client loss immediately painful in a way a studio's monthly churn is not. What the category label obscures is that the business has bifurcated into two models with opposite constraints. The in-person trainer sells hours and is bounded by them, so density and roster stability are everything. The remote coach sells programmes and is bounded by nothing except how many clients they can meaningfully attend to, so the whole question is whether clients actually do the work when nobody is watching. Software built for one fits the other badly, which is visible in the market's own segmentation.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Trainerize, TrueCoach, Everfit and a competitive field of others provide programme building, exercise libraries, client messaging, progress tracking and billing, and they do it well. The common gaps across both models: programme adherence is displayed as completion checkboxes rather than analysed; client progress is charted without being compared to anything, so neither coach nor client can tell whether the trajectory is good; pricing and packaging are entirely the coach's guess; and the coach's own business — which clients are profitable, which are at risk, what the capacity looks like next month — is not modelled at all. Where the two models diverge is in what the binding constraint is, which is the subject of the sub-niches below.

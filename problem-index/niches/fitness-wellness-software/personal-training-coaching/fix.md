# Pricing Set by What the Coach Down the Road Charges

**Niche:** [[niches/fitness-wellness-software/personal-training-coaching/profile|Personal Training & Coaching]]
**Industry:** [[industries/fitness-wellness-software|Fitness & Wellness Software]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Coaches set their rates by looking at what other coaches charge, never compute what a client actually costs them in time, and discover their cheapest package is their least profitable only if they happen to think about it.

## The Problem
A coach charges $75 an hour for in-person sessions and $200 a month for online coaching. The online client messages daily, needs a programme rebuilt every four weeks, and consumes three or four hours a month once everything is counted — an effective rate well below the in-person one, on the package the coach promotes as their scalable offering. The coach has never counted the hours, so the belief that online coaching is more profitable survives indefinitely, and the business grows in the direction that makes it worse.

## Why It's Still Broken
Time spent on a client outside sessions is invisible: message answering, programme building, check-in review and administration happen in gaps and are never logged. Coaching platforms record sessions and not the surrounding work, so there is no data to compute against even if someone wanted to. And pricing in this profession is emotionally loaded — many coaches undercharge from a discomfort with the transaction and set rates by looking sideways, which is a habit no amount of data addresses on its own but which a number does at least make visible.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Count the time the platform can already see. Messages sent and their timing, programme edits, check-in reviews, session duration — most of the invisible work happens inside the coaching app and is trivially measurable without any logging by the coach. From that, effective hourly rate per client and per package falls out, which is the number nobody has. Report it plainly, with the comparison between packages, because the finding is usually that the scalable package is not scalable at current service levels. Pair it with what the coach controls: message response expectations, programme rebuild cadence, check-in frequency — since the fix is usually to define the service rather than to raise the price. Anonymous rate ranges by market, experience and modality, drawn from the platform's own base, give the coach the external reference they are currently getting from a competitor's website.

## Who Feels the Pain
Coaches working far more hours than they charge for and burning out; clients on cheap packages receiving service the coach cannot sustain; and the profession, whose attrition is driven substantially by this arithmetic never being done.

## Impact If Fixed
Effective hourly rate per package is computable from in-app activity the platform already records and consistently surprises the coach. It typically produces two changes at once — a service definition and a price correction — and both are available immediately, without any modelling, from data that is currently displayed as message counts.

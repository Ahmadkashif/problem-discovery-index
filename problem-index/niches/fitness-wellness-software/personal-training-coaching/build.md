## The Problem
A coach has thirty-four clients, a calendar, and a bank balance. They cannot say which clients are at risk of leaving in the next month, what their revenue will be in three months given typical attrition and their current enquiry rate, how much of their week is unpaid administration, which client segments are actually profitable once preparation and messaging time is included, or whether they have capacity to take two more clients without degrading the service. These are the questions of any small business and there is no product in this category that answers any of them, because every product was designed around the client's experience rather than the coach's.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The platforms were built as delivery tools and their product roadmaps follow the client-facing experience, which is what a coach evaluates in a trial. Business modelling is invisible in a demo and requires the vendor to hold an opinion about how a coaching business should be run. And coaches themselves, who are overwhelmingly practitioners rather than businesspeople, do not articulate the need — they experience it as a general anxiety about whether things are going well rather than as a request for a forecast.

## What to Build
A business layer over the coaching relationship. Client retention risk from engagement, communication frequency and adherence, which the platform already records. Revenue forecast from the current roster, historical attrition and enquiry-to-conversion rate, with an interval — because a coach's real question is whether they can afford to turn down a client, and that requires a forecast rather than a balance. Time accounting that includes what is currently invisible: programme building, message answering, check-in review, which for many coaches exceeds the paid session hours and is the reason their effective hourly rate is far below their stated one. Capacity modelling that says what taking another client actually costs. And profitability per client and per package, which almost always shows that the cheapest package consumes disproportionate attention — a finding that changes how coaches price and which they cannot currently see.

## Target Customer
Independent coaches and small coaching businesses, and the coaching platform vendors whose products stop at the client relationship.

## Impact If Built
Coaches routinely work far more hours than they bill and cannot see why, and the unbilled time is the difference between a viable business and burnout in a profession with heavy attrition. Making the business legible — retention risk, forecast, true hourly rate, capacity — is the first product any of these platforms could offer the coach rather than the client.

# Class Schedule Optimisation

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/fitness-wellness-software|Fitness & Wellness Software]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in studio scheduling is fighting to build a grid from measured demand rather than from instructor availability and habit — and whoever raises revenue per class hour most takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$170M US spend attributable to scheduling capability within studio platforms
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~7% of fitness software revenue, gating a much larger share of studio economics
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — schedules are built and published in software and designed by habit
**Target Buyer:** Studio owners and multi-location programming leads; product leads at every studio platform
**Automation Potential:** Very High — this is constrained scheduling against a demand model, and the demand data is fully recorded

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A studio's class grid is its product and its cost structure simultaneously. Every class on it commits an instructor's pay and a room for an hour, and earns whatever it fills. The grid at most studios was set when the studio opened, adjusted occasionally when an instructor left, and is otherwise inherited. It reflects instructor availability, the owner's beliefs about when people want to train, and habit. What it does not reflect is demand, which the platform has measured continuously since opening: every booking, every waitlist entry, every cancellation, every class that ran at four and every class that turned people away. Two classes on the same grid can differ by a factor of several in revenue per hour, and the studio frequently cannot say which is which because the reporting is by class type rather than by time slot.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Every studio platform builds and publishes schedules competently, handles instructor assignment, and manages booking and waitlists. Reporting shows attendance by class. The gaps: no product models demand by time slot and format, so the grid cannot be evaluated against anything; waitlist data, which is the clearest available signal of unmet demand, is treated as a booking overflow mechanism rather than as a measurement; the effect of a schedule change is never evaluated, so studios that do change the grid learn nothing from it; and instructor availability is treated as a hard constraint rather than as one input to a decision, which means the grid ends up optimised for the instructors' convenience by default rather than by intent.

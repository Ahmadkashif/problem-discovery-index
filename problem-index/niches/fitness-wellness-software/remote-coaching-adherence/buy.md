**One-liner:** Behaviour change techniques for adherence are a well-developed evidence base, packaged and deployed in digital health and medication adherence products, and remote fitness coaching relies on the coach's personal style.

**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in remote coaching software is fighting to keep a client doing the programme when the coach cannot see them and everything is self-reported — and whoever raises sustained adherence takes the account.

## The Problem
Two coaches with identical programmes get very different adherence, and the difference is how they handle the relationship — when they check in, how they respond to a missed week, whether they adjust the programme downward temporarily or hold the line, how they frame a setback. These are behaviour change techniques with a substantial research literature and documented effect sizes, and they are transmitted in this industry as personal style, learned by imitation, inconsistently applied.

## What Already Exists
The behaviour change technique taxonomy is published and widely used in health research, with evidence on which techniques work for which behaviours. Medication adherence and chronic disease management products have operationalised much of it at scale. Digital health companies run continuous experiments on engagement messaging. Habit formation tooling exists in consumer apps. The evidence base and the operational patterns are both available and neither originates in fitness.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a coached relationship rather than an app-delivered programme. It requires: (1) techniques delivered through the coach rather than by the system, since the relationship is the active ingredient and an automated nudge that bypasses the coach undermines the thing the client is paying for — the product should be prompting and equipping the coach, not messaging the client; (2) matching technique to the client's actual barrier, distinguishing a client short on time from one short on confidence from one whose programme is simply too hard, because the right response differs completely and coaches routinely apply the same one; (3) programme adjustment as a first-class intervention, since the most effective response to a struggling client is usually to make the next two weeks easier and the industry's instinct is to exhort; (4) measurement per technique per client segment, which is achievable at the scale these platforms operate and would produce the first real evidence about what works in this specific context; and (5) honesty about the limits, since adherence is influenced by far more than coaching and a product that implies otherwise sets coaches up to blame themselves and their clients.

## Target Customer
Online coaching businesses, coaching platform vendors, and the digital health companies with adjacent adherence capability.

## Impact If Solved
Adherence technique is the difference between coaches and is currently untaught and unmeasured, which makes it the largest available quality improvement in remote coaching. Importing an evidence base rather than inventing one is faster and better grounded, and measuring at platform scale would produce evidence the field does not have.

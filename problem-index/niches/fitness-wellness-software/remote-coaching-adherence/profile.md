# Remote Coaching — Adherence at a Distance

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/fitness-wellness-software|Fitness & Wellness Software]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in remote coaching software is fighting to keep a client doing the programme when the coach cannot see them and everything is self-reported — and whoever raises sustained adherence takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$220M US software spend across online and hybrid coaching businesses
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~9% of fitness software revenue, and the segment's fastest-growing part
**Digital Adoption:** High — the entire relationship happens through software
**Target Buyer:** Online coaching businesses and hybrid coaches, from individuals to teams of coaches with thousands of clients
**Automation Potential:** Very High — adherence prediction and engagement intervention are exactly what the logged data supports

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Remote coaching removed the capacity ceiling and replaced it with an observation problem. A coach with three hundred online clients has no idea whether any given one trained this week except by what they logged, and clients who are struggling are precisely the ones who stop logging. Adherence collapses quietly: a client misses two sessions, feels behind, avoids opening the app because opening it means confronting the missed sessions, and three weeks later cancels having not spoken to the coach since. This is the defining failure of the model and it is different in kind from anything an in-person trainer faces, because an in-person client who does not show up is visibly absent at a specific hour. The signals available are all digital — app opens, logged sessions, message responses, check-in submissions, wearable data where connected — and they are abundant, timely and almost entirely unused for anything except a completion percentage.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Trainerize, Everfit, TrueCoach and a competitive field provide programme delivery, logging, messaging, habit tracking and check-in workflows. Wearable integration is common. Client-facing experience is genuinely good. The gaps: adherence is displayed as a completion figure rather than modelled as a trajectory, so a client sliding is visible only to a coach who looks; disengagement — which shows up as reduced app opening before it shows up as missed sessions — is not surfaced at all; check-in review is manual and consumes the coach's week, which caps how many clients can be served well; and nothing distinguishes a client who is fine and busy from one who is quietly disengaging, which is the distinction the whole business depends on.

# Progression the Parent Can See

**Niche:** [[niches/fitness-wellness-software/martial-arts-youth-programs/profile|Martial Arts & Youth Programmes]]
**Industry:** [[industries/fitness-wellness-software|Fitness & Wellness Software]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A parent pays monthly for their child's progression and can observe none of it, so retention in these schools depends on a perception formed at pickup from a thirty-second conversation.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #workflow-orchestration #automation #worker-facing #revenue-impact #survival-analysis
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor selling to martial arts and youth programmes is fighting to let a parent see progression, manage enrolment and pay without telephoning the school — and whoever makes the parent's side self-serve takes the account.

## The Problem
A parent pays $140 a month for their eight-year-old's classes. They drop off and collect. They do not know whether the child is doing well, what they are working on, whether they are close to the next belt, or whether the instructor thinks they are progressing. When the family's schedule gets tight, or the child says they do not want to go one week, the parent has no counterweight to the decision to stop — because nothing has ever demonstrated that progress is being made. Attrition in these schools is heavily concentrated in exactly this dynamic, and the school experiences it as children who simply drift away.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The products in this category were built for the school's operations — billing, attendance, class lists — and the parent is modelled as a payer rather than as the customer whose perception determines retention. Progression tracking is also genuinely tacit: an instructor knows a child's readiness from watching them, and turning that into a record means asking instructors to assess and record in a structured way, which they experience as bureaucracy and will resist unless it takes seconds. And the schools that do communicate progression well do it through the instructor's personal relationship with the family, which works and does not scale past a certain size.

## What to Build
A progression record that is cheap to maintain and visible to the parent. Curriculum is modelled explicitly — the techniques, skills or levels a student works through — and instructors mark progress with a tap during or after class, on the few students they worked with closely that day, rather than by completing an assessment for everyone. Attendance, which the system already has, contributes automatically. The parent sees an app view: what their child is working on, what they have achieved recently, attendance, what is coming, and a short instructor note when there is one. Grading readiness is derived and shown as a path rather than as an announcement, which converts a mystery into a goal the family can participate in. Photographs and short clips from class, captured occasionally, are the single most effective element and cost almost nothing.

## Target Customer
Martial arts schools, dance studios, gymnastics and youth activity programmes, and the specialist software vendors serving them.

## Impact If Built
Visible progression is the retention mechanism in this format and it is currently left to chance. Schools that communicate progress well retain substantially better, and the difference is not the teaching but the visibility — which means it is available to any school with the tooling, and today almost none have it.

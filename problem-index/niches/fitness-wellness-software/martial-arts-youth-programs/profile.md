# Martial Arts & Youth Programmes

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/fitness-wellness-software|Fitness & Wellness Software]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor selling to martial arts and youth programmes is fighting to let a parent see progression, manage enrolment and pay without telephoning the school — and whoever makes the parent's side self-serve takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$310M US software spend across martial arts schools, dance studios, gymnastics and youth activity programmes
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~12% of fitness software revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Low — enrolment, progression and communication run on paper, text messages and a front desk
**Target Buyer:** School owners, who are usually also the head instructor
**Automation Potential:** High — enrolment, progression tracking and parent communication are structured and are handled individually

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
These businesses have a structure no other fitness format shares: the person paying is not the person attending, and the thing being sold is progression rather than access. A parent enrols a child, pays monthly, and wants to know whether the child is progressing, when the next grading or recital is, what it costs, what equipment is needed and whether the child is enjoying it — none of which they observe directly, since most of them are not in the room. Retention is driven by the parent's perception of progression and by the logistics of the family's week, which are entirely different levers from a fitness member's own motivation. Meanwhile the school runs a belt or level system, seasonal enrolment cycles, gradings, tournaments and recitals, all of which are operationally significant and none of which generic fitness software models at all.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Zen Planner, Kicksite, Martialytics and a handful of specialist products serve martial arts; dance and gymnastics have their own small vendors; many schools use generic fitness software badly or a spreadsheet. Billing is generally handled. The gaps are the format's own structure: progression is tracked on a wall chart or in the instructor's head rather than as a record the parent can see; grading eligibility, which drives both revenue and retention, is determined by the instructor's recollection of attendance and readiness; sibling and family billing is handled awkwardly by products designed for individual memberships; and parent communication is a group text message, so the parent's experience of the school is whatever they can glean at pickup.

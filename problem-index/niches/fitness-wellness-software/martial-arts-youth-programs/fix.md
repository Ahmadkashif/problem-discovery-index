# Grading Eligibility Held in the Instructor's Head

**Niche:** [[niches/fitness-wellness-software/martial-arts-youth-programs/profile|Martial Arts & Youth Programmes]]
**Industry:** [[industries/fitness-wellness-software|Fitness & Wellness Software]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Who is ready to grade is decided by the head instructor from memory a fortnight before the event, which drives most of the school's episodic revenue and leaves the students who were not selected with no explanation.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #hypothesis-testing #workflow-orchestration #automation #worker-facing #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor selling to martial arts and youth programmes is fighting to let a parent see progression, manage enrolment and pay without telephoning the school — and whoever makes the parent's side self-serve takes the account.

## The Problem
Grading is in three weeks. The head instructor sits down and works out who is ready, from memory of who has been attending and who has the material. The list goes up. Some families are delighted and pay the grading fee; others are confused and slightly hurt, and a proportion of those quietly stop coming — because a child who was not selected, with no explanation, concludes they are not good at it. The instructor's judgement is sound and the process around it is opaque, and the opacity costs the school students in a format where each one is a multi-year relationship.

## Why It's Still Broken
Readiness is a judgement, and instructors are rightly protective of it — a grading is a standard, not an entitlement, and a system that automated eligibility would damage exactly what the school is selling. That correct concern has prevented the separable part from being addressed: the attendance and curriculum-coverage requirements, which are objective, and the communication of where a student stands, which is what the family needs. Both could be handled without touching the instructor's judgement, and neither has been.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Separate the objective requirements from the judgement and make the path visible. Attendance thresholds and curriculum coverage are computed from records the school already has, so a student and their family can see in advance what remains before they are eligible to be considered — which turns a mystery into a goal and lets a family act on it. The instructor's assessment of readiness stays entirely with the instructor and is recorded as their decision, with a short reason where the answer is not yet, communicated to the family rather than left to inference. Forecast the grading roster earlier, since the instructor's fortnight-before list is also a revenue forecast the school makes too late to plan around. And track what happens after a not-yet: attrition following a non-selection is a measurable and, on most schools' experience, substantial effect that nobody has quantified and that the communication change directly addresses.

## Who Feels the Pain
Children who were not selected and were told nothing; parents who cannot tell whether their child is progressing or being overlooked; and instructors who make a defensible judgement and are experienced as arbitrary.

## Impact If Fixed
Making the objective portion of the path visible costs nothing and converts a recurring source of quiet attrition into a motivating goal. The measurement of post-non-selection attrition is the finding most likely to persuade an owner that the communication matters, and it is computable from the school's own history today.

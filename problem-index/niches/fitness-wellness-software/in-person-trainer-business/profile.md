# In-Person Training — Schedule Density and Roster Retention

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/fitness-wellness-software|Fitness & Wellness Software]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor selling to in-person trainers is fighting to fill the hours a trainer can physically work and keep a roster where every departure hurts — and whoever raises paid hours per available hour takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$320M US software spend across in-person personal trainers and small training studios
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~13% of fitness software revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — scheduling and payment are digitised, the economics are not
**Target Buyer:** Independent trainers, small-studio owners and gym-based training departments
**Automation Potential:** High — scheduling density, cancellation handling and roster risk are all structured problems

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
An in-person trainer has a hard ceiling: the hours they can physically deliver, minus the ones nobody books. Everything about the business follows from that. A gap between a 7am and a 9am client is unrecoverable revenue, so schedule density is the single largest determinant of income. A late cancellation is an hour lost that cannot be refilled. A client who leaves takes a recurring slot with them, and finding a replacement who wants that specific hour is genuinely hard — the 6am slots fill instantly and the 1pm slots do not. A roster of thirty means each departure is three percent of revenue, which makes retention a per-relationship concern rather than a statistical one. None of this resembles the remote coaching business the same software is sold to.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Trainerize, TrueCoach and the studio platforms all handle booking, programme delivery and payment for this segment competently, and gym-based trainers often use whatever the gym runs. The gaps are the economics of the calendar: nothing optimises the schedule for density, nothing manages the cancellation and fill process — which is where most recoverable revenue sits — and nothing models which clients are likely to leave and what their slot is worth. Waitlists, where they exist, are passive lists rather than active fill mechanisms. And the trainer's most valuable scarce resource, the premium early-morning and evening slots, is allocated on a first-come basis rather than deliberately.

# Network-Funded Fitness

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/fitness-wellness-software|Fitness & Wellness Software]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor serving network-participating studios is fighting to let a small studio see, verify and reconcile what a fitness network actually owes it — and whoever makes network revenue legible takes those studios.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$260M US software spend attributable to studios participating in employer, insurer and aggregator fitness networks
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~10% of fitness software revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Low-Medium — check-ins are digital and the settlement behind them is opaque
**Target Buyer:** Independent studios and small operators inside these networks; the networks themselves as an alternative buyer
**Automation Potential:** High — reconciliation, attribution and member-value analysis are all computable and none is provided

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A growing share of studio visits are paid for by somebody other than the person attending: employer wellness benefits, insurer fitness programmes, and consumer aggregators that sell access across many studios. For the studio this is a distinct commercial relationship with distinct problems. The rate per visit is set by the network and is typically well below the studio's own price. The volume is unpredictable. The member belongs to the network rather than to the studio, so the studio frequently cannot contact them, cannot see their attendance history, and cannot convert them without violating terms. Settlement arrives as a monthly statement the studio cannot verify against its own records without manual work. And the strategic question — whether network visits are filling empty capacity profitably or displacing full-price members — is the one that determines whether participation is good for the business, and no studio can answer it.

## Current Tools & Gaps
The networks operate their own platforms and integrate with studio software for check-in and reporting to varying depths. Studio platforms record network check-ins as a membership type. The gaps are all on the studio's side of an asymmetric relationship: statements are summaries rather than itemised records that reconcile to the studio's own check-ins; the studio cannot see which of its classes network members are filling, at what hours, or whether those are the hours it could have sold; conversion of a network member to a direct member is restricted and unmeasured; and the displacement question — network visits occupying spots that direct members wanted — is invisible because no product joins capacity, network visits and turned-away demand.

# Wellness & Recovery Services

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/fitness-wellness-software|Fitness & Wellness Software]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in recovery and wellness software is fighting to keep practitioners and rooms utilised when service durations vary and no-shows are costly — and whoever raises utilisation per available practitioner hour takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$280M US software spend across massage, recovery, float, sauna, cryotherapy and IV wellness businesses
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~11% of fitness software revenue, in a fast-growing category
**Digital Adoption:** Low — booking is online and everything about capacity is manual
**Target Buyer:** Owners of independent recovery studios, massage practices and multi-modality wellness businesses
**Automation Potential:** High — utilisation scheduling under variable durations and resources is a solved class of problem

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Recovery and wellness businesses look like studios and behave like clinics. The unit of sale is an appointment with a duration that varies by service, delivered by a practitioner or in a room, and the economics are utilisation of those two constrained resources. Unlike a fitness class, which fills or does not without changing cost, an unfilled practitioner hour is a direct loss and a room sitting empty is rent. The services also combine — a client books a massage and a sauna, which must be sequenced, which occupies two different resources for different durations with a buffer between them. Memberships have appeared as a model across the category and introduce a second problem: a member with unlimited access consumes capacity that could have been sold, and nobody models the relationship between membership pricing and utilisation. Fitness software, built around classes with fixed capacity, models none of this.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Mindbody and the spa-oriented products handle appointment booking and payment adequately; some clinic and salon platforms serve parts of the category better than the fitness ones do. The gaps: resource scheduling treats a practitioner and a room as the same kind of constraint or ignores rooms entirely, so double-booked resources are discovered on the day; service durations are configured as fixed when they vary substantially by client and practitioner; no-show and late cancellation handling is weak in a business where an unfilled hour is unrecoverable; multi-service sequencing is manual; and membership utilisation — how much capacity a membership base actually consumes and what that implies for pricing and for how many members can be sold — is not modelled anywhere, which is how several businesses in this category have oversold memberships into congestion.

# Clinic Scheduling Practice Instead of Class Scheduling

**Niche:** [[niches/fitness-wellness-software/wellness-recovery-services/profile|Wellness & Recovery Services]]
**Industry:** [[industries/fitness-wellness-software|Fitness & Wellness Software]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Clinics, salons and medical practices have scheduled practitioners against rooms with variable-duration appointments for decades with mature software, and recovery studios bought fitness products because they thought of themselves as fitness.
**Tags:** #optimization-fundamentals #dynamic-programming #time-series-forecasting #logistic-regression #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #workflow-orchestration #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in recovery and wellness software is fighting to keep practitioners and rooms utilised when service durations vary and no-shows are costly — and whoever raises utilisation per available practitioner hour takes the account.

## The Problem
Everything a recovery studio needs — multi-resource scheduling, variable durations, practitioner qualifications, room turnover, no-show prediction, waitlist fill, deposit policies — is standard in clinic and salon practice management software and absent from the fitness products these businesses actually run. The mismatch happened because the category emerged out of fitness culture and bought accordingly, and it has persisted because switching a live booking system is disruptive.

## What Already Exists
Salon and spa management platforms, clinic practice management systems and healthcare scheduling software all handle multi-resource appointment scheduling well, with mature no-show prediction, waitlist automation, deposit handling and practitioner commission structures. Constraint-based scheduling engines are commodity. The methods and the products are both available, and several are priced within reach of a small studio.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to the recovery category's own service model. It requires: (1) multi-modality visits as a normal case rather than an exception, since combining services is the format's distinguishing product and clinic software generally assumes one appointment per visit; (2) membership entitlement checked against booking, because unlike a clinic these businesses sell unlimited or credit-based memberships and the booking engine must know what a member is entitled to and what it costs the business to serve them; (3) equipment as a resource alongside rooms, since a cold plunge or a compression unit is a constrained asset in a way a treatment room in a clinic is not; (4) practitioner preference and client relationship modelled as a soft constraint, because clients in this category attach to individual practitioners strongly and a scheduler that ignores it will be overridden; and (5) commission and tip handling, which salon software does well and fitness software does badly, and which is central to how practitioners in this category are paid.

## Target Customer
Recovery and wellness studios, massage practices and multi-modality businesses, and the salon and clinic software vendors who could reach this segment with a light adaptation.

## Impact If Solved
This is the clearest case in the industry of a segment running the wrong software because of category identity rather than fit. The capabilities exist, are mature, and are priced for small businesses; the adaptation is mostly membership entitlement and equipment resources, which is a modest amount of work against a substantial improvement.

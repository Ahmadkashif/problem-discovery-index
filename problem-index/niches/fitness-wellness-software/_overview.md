## Why These Niches

A fitness business is a subscription business whose customers stop attending weeks before they stop paying and then cancel. Retention is the whole economic model, attendance decay predicts cancellation with unusual reliability, and the platforms record every check-in and use it to print a class roster. That makes churn prediction and intervention the contested capability of the category's largest block.

Personal training and coaching is the other large block and **failed the filter as one niche**. A trainer working in person with thirty clients contests on schedule density and the retention of a small roster where every loss is significant. A remote coaching business with three hundred clients contests on adherence at a distance, where the coach never sees the client and the only signal is what the client chooses to report. Different products, different competitors, different failure modes. Decomposed below.

Martial arts and youth programmes and wellness recovery services are the two underdigitised formats, each with its own structure — a payer who is not the attendee and a progression system in the first, variable-duration services and practitioner utilisation in the second. Instructors and network-funded studios are the two underserved constituencies: the instructor delivers the service and is nobody's customer, and a small studio inside an employer or insurer fitness network is paid on terms it cannot see or verify.

The two automation niches are the category's recurring operational leaks: a class grid set by habit rather than demand, and a fitness-specific card decline pattern that generic dunning handles badly.

## Filter Notes

**Niche 2 failed the filter as stated and was decomposed.** "Personal training and coaching" covers two businesses with opposite constraints. An in-person trainer has a capacity ceiling set by hours in the day, so their contest is filling those hours densely and keeping a roster small enough that every departure matters — a scheduling and relationship problem. A remote coach has effectively unlimited capacity and no observation, so their contest is client adherence when they cannot see the client and the only evidence is self-reported — a behavioural and engagement problem. Both sub-niches are terminal.

**Niches 1, 3–8 are terminal as stated.** Each names a measurable capability: lead time on a churn prediction that was acted on successfully, whether a parent can manage enrolment and see progression without calling, practitioner utilisation against variable service durations, whether an instructor's week assembles in one place, whether a studio can reconcile what a network owes it, revenue per class hour against the grid it replaced, and involuntary churn recovered.

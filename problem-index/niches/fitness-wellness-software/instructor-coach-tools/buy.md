# Shift Marketplace Mechanics for Sub Coverage

**Niche:** [[niches/fitness-wellness-software/instructor-coach-tools/profile|Instructor & Coach Tools]]
**Industry:** [[industries/fitness-wellness-software|Fitness & Wellness Software]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Shift swapping and open-shift marketplaces are standard features in healthcare and hospitality workforce software, and fitness studios cover classes by posting in a group chat and hoping.
**Tags:** #optimization-fundamentals #logistic-regression #gradient-boosting #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #workflow-orchestration #automation #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor building for instructors is fighting to assemble a week that spans several studios — schedule, sub requests, availability and pay — into one place, and whoever the instructors actually carry takes the layer above the studios.

## The Problem
An instructor cannot teach Thursday's 6:30. She posts in the studio's instructor chat. The message is seen by whoever is looking, competes with unrelated conversation, and is answered or not. If nobody answers by Wednesday the studio owner starts telephoning people. If nobody covers, the class is cancelled, members who booked are disappointed, and some of them do not rebook. The whole sequence runs on a medium with no state, no matching and no confirmation.

## What Already Exists
Healthcare and hospitality workforce management platforms have mature open-shift and swap marketplaces with qualification matching, approval workflows, notification targeting and confirmation tracking, refined against far more complex staffing rules than a fitness studio has. Messaging and notification infrastructure is commodity. The mechanics are entirely solved in adjacent industries.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a workforce that is largely contract, cross-employer and qualification-specific in an unusual way. It requires: (1) qualification matching on what the class actually needs — format, certification, equipment familiarity, and whether the instructor knows this studio's room and members — which is finer-grained than a generic skill tag and is what determines whether a sub is acceptable; (2) targeting by genuine availability rather than broadcasting, which requires the cross-studio availability the build note describes and is the reason the two capabilities belong together; (3) cross-studio coverage where instructors are willing, since the sub who is free on Thursday may teach at a different studio and neither studio currently has a way to find them; (4) confirmation and accountability, so a claimed sub is a commitment with a record rather than a message, which is the most common failure in the chat-based process; and (5) a fair notification design, since a marketplace that repeatedly offers the least desirable classes to the newest instructors will be resented, and the allocation deserves the same deliberateness that healthcare scheduling gives it.

## Target Customer
Studios and multi-location operators bearing the cost of uncovered classes, and instructors bearing the coordination burden.

## Impact If Solved
A cancelled class is a direct member-experience failure in a business whose retention depends on habit, and the coverage process is currently the weakest link in studio operations. The mechanics are bought; the adaptation is qualification matching and cross-studio reach, which is where the coverage rate actually improves.

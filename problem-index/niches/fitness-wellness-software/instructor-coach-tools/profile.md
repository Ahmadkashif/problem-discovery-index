# Instructor & Coach Tools

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/fitness-wellness-software|Fitness & Wellness Software]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor building for instructors is fighting to assemble a week that spans several studios — schedule, sub requests, availability and pay — into one place, and whoever the instructors actually carry takes the layer above the studios.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$190M US spend attributable to instructor-facing tooling within fitness software
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~8% of fitness software revenue, against the workforce that delivers every class
**Digital Adoption:** Low — the instructor's tools are a group chat and a personal calendar
**Target Buyer:** Nobody, currently — instructors are not customers of the platforms whose software they use daily
**Automation Potential:** High — schedule aggregation, sub matching and pay reconciliation are mechanical

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The fitness instructor is the person who delivers the product and is nobody's customer. A typical boutique instructor teaches at three or four studios, each running different software, each with its own schedule, its own sub-request process — usually a group chat — its own pay rate and its own pay cycle. Their week is assembled manually from four sources into a personal calendar, their availability is communicated separately to each studio, sub requests arrive as messages they may miss while teaching, and reconciling what they were paid against what they taught requires checking four statements against their own notes. This is a real job with real logistics and the industry's software addresses none of it, because the software is sold to studios and instructors are modelled as a resource on a schedule.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Every studio platform has an instructor view, scoped to that studio, with that studio's schedule and sometimes a sub-request feature. None of them spans studios, because none of them has a reason to. Group messaging apps are the de facto sub-request infrastructure across the industry. Pay is handled through each studio's payroll or contractor payment separately. The gaps are the entire multi-studio reality: no aggregated schedule, no single availability that all studios can see, no sub matching beyond broadcasting to a chat, no consolidated record of classes taught, and no way for an instructor to know whether they were paid correctly — which, given the number of employers and the variety of rate structures, is a genuine and recurring problem.

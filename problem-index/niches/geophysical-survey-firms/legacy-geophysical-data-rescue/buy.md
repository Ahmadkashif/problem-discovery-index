**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn decades of paper sections, field tapes and observer logs into correctly navigated, loadable digital data with clean licence title, and whoever does it at the lowest cost per line-kilometre without navigation errors wins the archive.

## The Problem
Every box in a seismic warehouse holds documents that must be catalogued before anything is scanned in detail: section side labels with line name, contractor, date and processing sequence; observer reports with instrument settings and geometry; tape labels and transmittal sheets. Cataloguing is done by reading and typing.

## What Already Exists
ABBYY, Kofax and cloud document-AI services classify documents and extract fields from forms; library catalogues and spreadsheets hold the results.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs: document classes for section labels, observer reports, tape labels and processing sheets; field schemas for line name, shot-point range, contractor, acquisition date, recording parameters and processing sequence; normalisation of line names that changed across vintages; linking every document to the survey, line and owner; and flags where the same line appears with conflicting metadata.

## Target Customer
Data rescue firms and data managers at libraries and state geological surveys.

## Impact If Solved
A warehouse catalogued in weeks rather than months, so owners know what they have before they decide what to rescue.

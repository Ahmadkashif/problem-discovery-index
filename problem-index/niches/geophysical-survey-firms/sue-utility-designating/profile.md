# Subsurface Utility Designating (QL-B)

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/geophysical-survey-firms|Geophysical Survey Firms]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to deliver QL-B utility designations that hold up when the QL-A test holes are dug, on the DOT's design schedule, and whoever's lines survive excavation most often keeps the on-call contract.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$600M US QL-B geophysical designating and associated QL-A test-hole work (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~20% of US geophysical survey spend (estimate; much of it sits inside engineering firms' SUE practices)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — GPR and EM data are digital, utility records arrive as scanned PDFs and as-builts, deliverables go out as CAD
**Target Buyer:** SUE Practice Leader at an engineering firm; Utility Coordinator at a state DOT
**Automation Potential:** Medium — tracing and drafting automate; deciding which line is which, in congested corridors, does not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Under ASCE 38-22, QL-B "designating" uses surface geophysics — electromagnetic line tracing, GPR, sometimes acoustic and magnetic methods — to map the horizontal position of essentially every utility in a project corridor before design, and QL-A vacuum excavation exposes selected points to verify position and depth. The buyer is a highway, transit or rail designer under a DOT or authority schedule, not an excavator under a 48-hour locate ticket: that is the [[niches/utility-contractors/underground-locating-damage-prevention/profile|Underground Utility Locating & Damage Prevention]] world. The engineering-firm SUE practice is logged in Pass 2 under [[niches/utility-contractors/subsurface-utility-engineering/profile|Subsurface Utility Engineering & Mapping]]; this niche is the geophysical designating work those practices perform or subcontract.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Radiodetection and Vivax-type EM locators, GSSI and Sensors & Software GPR carts, Civil 3D and ArcGIS for deliverables, and AI entrants such as Exodigo selling multi-sensor maps directly to DOTs. Gaps: no record of how QL-B lines compare with QL-A exposures across projects; non-metallic and congested utilities quietly downgraded; and quality-level attributes handled inconsistently in CAD.

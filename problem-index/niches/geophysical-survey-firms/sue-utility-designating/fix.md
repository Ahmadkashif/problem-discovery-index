# The Segment Quietly Downgraded to QL-C

**Niche:** [[niches/geophysical-survey-firms/sue-utility-designating/profile|Subsurface Utility Designating (QL-B)]]
**Industry:** [[industries/geophysical-survey-firms|Geophysical Survey Firms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Where geophysics cannot trace a line — plastic pipe, deep clay, a congested intersection — the segment falls back to records-based QL-C or QL-D, and the plans give the designer little sign of it.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #compliance #workflow-orchestration #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to deliver QL-B utility designations that hold up when the QL-A test holes are dug, on the DOT's design schedule, and whoever's lines survive excavation most often keeps the on-call contract.

## The Problem
State directives such as MassDOT's allow a provider to fall back to a lower quality level where a segment cannot be shown at QL-B. That is honest practice. But the downgrade often appears only as a line style and a note in the report, and the gaps cluster exactly where risk is highest — non-metallic gas services, abandoned lines, crowded intersections. Designers and contractors then treat the drawn line as reliable.

## Why It's Still Broken
Nothing measures or reports downgrade rates; the report's limitations section is boilerplate; and DOT reviewers rarely have time to check segment by segment.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A downgrade register produced automatically from the segment attributes: every non-QL-B segment listed with the reason (method failure, access, material), mapped as a risk overlay, and paired with a recommended QL-A test-hole or alternative method where it crosses proposed work. A one-page summary goes to the designer with the plans.

## Who Feels the Pain
Designers who discover conflicts at construction, contractors who strike lines drawn from records, and SUE providers blamed for gaps they did report.

## Impact If Fixed
Test holes placed where the uncertainty is, fewer construction-phase surprises, and a clear record of what the provider did and did not claim.

# Job Costing Built for Channel-Days and Standby

**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is "every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to deliver a usable seismic image over the client's acreage at the lowest cost per square mile", but it hides two contests with different winners: field crews compete on clean source and receiver points recorded per crew-day across permit gaps and weather, and processing houses compete on depth images that tie the wells before the lease or drilling decision. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## The Problem
A seismic program is priced per square mile or per point, with day-rate standby when permits, weather or the client hold the crew, and pass-through reimbursables for permits, damages, camps and helicopters — Dawson Geophysical reports fee and reimbursable revenue separately. Margins are decided by crew productivity against the bid, and the contractor often learns a program lost money after it ends.

## What Already Exists
NetSuite, Sage Intacct and similar ERPs with project accounting; spreadsheets for daily production; recorder and node QC systems that count points but don't price them.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs: units of production (points recorded, channel-days, vibrator hours) as cost drivers; standby and weather days coded by cause and contract clause; reimbursable categories matched to the client's approval rules; daily production imported from the recorder rather than typed; and a burn-versus-bid forecast that projects final margin from the first two weeks of production.

## Target Customer
CFOs and operations managers at onshore seismic contractors and their Canadian peers, typically running a handful of crews.

## Impact If Solved
A contractor that sees margin erosion in week two rather than month three can renegotiate, redeploy equipment or bill standby properly — on crews whose daily cost runs to tens of thousands of dollars (estimate).

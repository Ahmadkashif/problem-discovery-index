## The Problem
Seismic contracts pay standby for delays the client causes and not for those the contractor causes. A morning lost because a permit agent hadn't cleared a tract, a day lost to lightning, a week waiting on a client's decision about a no-permit zone — each is recorded as a line in a daily report, often hours later, in the party chief's words. At invoice, the client's representative disputes the causes, and the contractor writes off what it cannot prove.

## Why It's Still Broken
The evidence exists but is scattered: recorder logs show when shooting stopped, weather services show the storm, the permit tracker shows the tract status, emails show the client's instruction. Nobody assembles them at the time, because the party chief is running a crew of up to a hundred people.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Detect production stoppages automatically from the recorder and node logs, attach the contemporaneous evidence (weather record, permit status, client correspondence), draft a cause code and a one-line explanation for the party chief to confirm, and share it with the client's representative the same day so disputes are settled while memory is fresh.

## Who Feels the Pain
Party chiefs writing reports at night, contractor CFOs writing off standby, and client representatives asked to approve charges weeks later.

## Impact If Fixed
Standby billed with same-day evidence is standby collected; a few disputed days per program recovered is a material share of a contractor's thin margin.

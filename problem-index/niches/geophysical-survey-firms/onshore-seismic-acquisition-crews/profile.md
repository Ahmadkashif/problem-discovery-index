# Onshore Seismic Acquisition Crews

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/geophysical-survey-firms|Geophysical Survey Firms]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this sub-niche is fighting to record the most clean source and receiver points per crew-day across permit gaps, weather and terrain, and whoever keeps a large-channel crew productive at the lowest cost per square mile wins the library and operator programs.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$500M North American onshore seismic acquisition (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~20% of US geophysical survey spend (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — recorders, nodes and vibrator guidance are digital; crew logistics, daily reports and repairs run on radio, paper and spreadsheets
**Target Buyer:** VP Operations or Party Chief at a seismic contractor
**Automation Potential:** Medium — production planning and QC reporting automate, field work does not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The field half of energy seismic is a logistics business. A crew of 40–100 technicians lays out and retrieves receivers, runs a fleet of vibrators or drills shot holes, and records continuously; Dawson Geophysical operates about 130 vibrators and 280,000 channels and names SAExploration, Echo Seismic and Paragon as competitors. Its customers are mostly multi-client libraries and a few operators, and the winner is whoever records the program fastest at the bid price — image quality is the processor's contest.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Nodal systems and cabled recorders with QC displays, vibrator guidance, survey design packages, ERP for costing. The gaps: production forecasting from the crew's own history, maintenance of vibrators and nodes in the field, and observer and production reporting assembled by hand every night.

# Maintenance Software for Vibrators and Nodes

**Niche:** [[niches/geophysical-survey-firms/onshore-seismic-acquisition-crews/profile|Onshore Seismic Acquisition Crews]]
**Industry:** [[industries/geophysical-survey-firms|Geophysical Survey Firms]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Generic maintenance systems track trucks by hours and miles; a seismic crew needs to track vibrator baseplates, hydraulics and sweep quality, and hundreds of thousands of nodes by battery cycles and failure codes.
**Tags:** #survival-analysis #change-point-detection #feature-engineering #data-integration #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this sub-niche is fighting to record the most clean source and receiver points per crew-day across permit gaps, weather and terrain, and whoever keeps a large-channel crew productive at the lowest cost per square mile wins the library and operator programs.

## The Problem
A vibrator that drops out mid-sweep or a batch of nodes that records noise costs the crew production immediately and the processor image quality later. Maintenance is done by field mechanics working from experience and paper logs, and node failures are discovered on retrieval.

## What Already Exists
CMMS products such as Fiix, UpKeep and IBM Maximo schedule preventive maintenance and track work orders; vibrator electronics and nodal systems log their own diagnostics.

## The Customization Gap
The seismic adaptation needs: ingestion of vibrator sweep-quality and force-control logs as condition signals; node-level records of deployment cycles, battery health and QC failures across hundreds of thousands of units; failure prediction that tells the crew which nodes to pull before the next layout; field-parts inventory across remote camps; and maintenance records tied to production loss so the cost of a failure is visible.

## Target Customer
Equipment managers and party chiefs at seismic contractors owning their own vibrator fleets and node pools.

## Impact If Solved
Fewer mid-program equipment failures and fewer dead channels translate directly into points recorded per day, the crew's competitive number.

# Concrete Scanning Before Cutting & Coring

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/geophysical-survey-firms|Geophysical Survey Firms]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to mark every tendon, rebar mat and conduit in a slab correctly before the core drill arrives, the same day it is called, and whoever's marks never lead to a cut tendon becomes the contractor's default scanner.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$500M US concrete scanning before cutting, coring and anchoring (estimate; much of it billed by private locating firms coded outside 541360)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~20% of US near-surface geophysics spend (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — handheld GPR is digital; the deliverable is paint or tape on the slab and a few photos
**Target Buyer:** Project Manager or Superintendent at a general contractor or MEP subcontractor; facility manager in a hospital, data centre or campus
**Automation Potential:** High — the same task on near-identical slabs thousands of times a year

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Before an electrician cores through an elevated slab, a plumber saw-cuts a trench or an installer sets an anchor, someone scans the concrete with high-frequency GPR to find rebar, post-tensioned tendons and embedded conduit. Cutting a tendon in a post-tensioned slab is a structural and safety event; cutting a live conduit is an electrical one. The work is high volume, same-day, priced per visit, and dominated by national platforms — GPRS reported a network of more than 250 project managers in 2020 — and regional firms. Each core drilled is a verification of the marks.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Handheld GPR units (GSSI StructureScan-class and peers) with on-screen hyperbola display, X-ray for critical cores, field service software for dispatch. Gaps: tendon-versus-rebar calls made by eye on a small screen; marks that disappear before coring; and no record of which marks were right.

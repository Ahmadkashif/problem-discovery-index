# The Layout Mark Gone Before the Core Drill Arrives

**Niche:** [[niches/geophysical-survey-firms/concrete-scanning-before-coring/profile|Concrete Scanning Before Cutting & Coring]]
**Industry:** [[industries/geophysical-survey-firms|Geophysical Survey Firms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The scanner marks the slab in paint or tape, other trades walk, sweep and mop over it, and the driller cores days later from a faded mark or a guess.
**Tags:** #object-detection #cnns #workflow-orchestration #worker-facing #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to mark every tendon, rebar mat and conduit in a slab correctly before the core drill arrives, the same day it is called, and whoever's marks never lead to a cut tendon becomes the contractor's default scanner.

## The Problem
On an active job site, the scan and the core rarely happen together. Marks are scuffed, painted over or confused with another trade's layout. Drillers either call the scanner back, paying for a second visit, or drill near what they think was marked.

## Why It's Still Broken
The physical mark is the only deliverable most clients receive; photos, if taken, sit on the technician's phone; and nobody owns the gap between scan and core.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A digital layout tied to fixed references — gridlines, columns, existing penetrations — captured as a scaled photo overlay at the time of scanning and shared with the driller by link, so the clear zone can be re-found from the photo even if the paint is gone; plus a prompt to the driller to photograph the finished core for the record.

## Who Feels the Pain
Drillers and electricians waiting for a return visit, superintendents paying for it, and scanners blamed for cores drilled off their marks.

## Impact If Fixed
Fewer return visits and fewer cores drilled in the wrong place, with a record of where the safe zone actually was.

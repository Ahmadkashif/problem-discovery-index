# Seismic Permitting & Landowner Relations

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/geophysical-survey-firms|Geophysical Survey Firms]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to secure access to the most source and receiver locations on a survey's footprint before the crew arrives, with the fewest damage disputes afterwards, and whoever's permit rate keeps the crew from standing by gets the next program.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$120M North American permit-agent, land and damage-settlement services for onshore seismic (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~5% of US geophysical survey spend (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — door-knocking, phone calls, paper permits signed at kitchen tables, spreadsheets and exported shapefiles
**Target Buyer:** Permit Manager at a seismic contractor or permit-agent firm; Land Manager at the client
**Automation Potential:** Medium for tracking, risk ranking and paperwork; low for the negotiation itself

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Every onshore seismic program needs permission for each tract its lines cross. A seismic operator cannot enter private land without the owner's or occupant's consent, agricultural tenants may also need to agree, and state and university lands issue their own permits with surface damage rates set at issue. Permit agents — often independent contractors or small land firms — negotiate access, fees and conditions, and settle damage claims after the crew leaves. Their output decides where the crew can record and therefore what the image looks like.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Lease-oriented land management systems, GIS layers of tracts and survey design, CRMs or spreadsheets for contacts, paper or PDF permit forms. Gaps: no per-point permit status, no risk ranking before outreach, conditions that never reach the crew in usable form, and damage claims settled without contemporaneous evidence.

# Refusal Risk Mapped Before the Survey Is Laid Out

**Niche:** [[niches/geophysical-survey-firms/seismic-permitting-landowner-relations/profile|Seismic Permitting & Landowner Relations]]
**Industry:** [[industries/geophysical-survey-firms|Geophysical Survey Firms]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A permit team discovers which landowners will refuse by knocking on doors in order, when parcel data and past permit outcomes could tell the survey designer where the gaps will be before a single visit.
**Tags:** #gradient-boosting #logistic-regression #feature-engineering #evaluation-metrics #data-integration #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to secure access to the most source and receiver locations on a survey's footprint before the crew arrives, with the fewest damage disputes afterwards, and whoever's permit rate keeps the crew from standing by gets the next program.

## The Problem
Refusals and restrictive conditions create no-permit zones, which force source skids and receiver gaps that reduce fold and image quality, and late refusals force redesign after the crew is mobilised. Experienced agents know which kinds of owners refuse — absentee owners, small acreages near towns, owners burned by a previous crew — but the knowledge is personal and the outreach order is set by geography.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Permit outcomes are kept in spreadsheets per program and discarded; permit-agent firms are small; and seismic contractors see each program as a one-off in a cyclical market.

## What to Build
A tract-level risk model trained on digitised permit outcomes from past programs and joined to county parcel attributes (ownership type, absentee status, acreage, improvements, land use), prior seismic history and offered fees. It ranks tracts for outreach, estimates the likely no-permit footprint for the survey designer before layout, and suggests where a higher fee or a senior agent is worth it.

## Target Customer
Permit managers at seismic contractors and permit-agent firms; survey designers at libraries commissioning programs.

## Impact If Built
Gaps designed around rather than discovered, fewer crew standby days, and agents' time spent on the tracts that decide the program.

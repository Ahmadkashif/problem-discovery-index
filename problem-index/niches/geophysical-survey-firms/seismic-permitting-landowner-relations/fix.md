# The Damage Claim With No Before Photo

**Niche:** [[niches/geophysical-survey-firms/seismic-permitting-landowner-relations/profile|Seismic Permitting & Landowner Relations]]
**Industry:** [[industries/geophysical-survey-firms|Geophysical Survey Firms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** After the crew leaves, a landowner claims rutted fields, a broken fence or cracked plaster from vibrators, and the contractor has no record of the tract's condition before the crew arrived.
**Tags:** #cnns #object-detection #data-integration #compliance #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to secure access to the most source and receiver locations on a survey's footprint before the crew arrives, with the fewest damage disputes afterwards, and whoever's permit rate keeps the crew from standing by gets the next program.

## The Problem
Damage settlement is a standing source of friction and litigation — a New Mexico case turned on vibration damage to plaster and truck tracks on ranch land. Claims are negotiated by permit agents from memory, a few photos and the permit's damage clause. Without before-and-after evidence the contractor overpays to keep peace or fights claims it cannot disprove.

## Why It's Still Broken
Nobody is assigned to document condition before entry; crews' GPS tracks and vehicle logs exist but are never joined to tracts; and photos taken on phones are not geotagged to a permit.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A pre-entry condition record captured by the agent at signing (geotagged photos of gates, fences, structures near lines, crop state) and a post-recording pass, both attached to the tract; vehicle and vibrator GPS tracks overlaid on the tract to show where equipment actually went; and an image-comparison step that flags visible changes for the agent to review before settlement talks.

## Who Feels the Pain
Permit agents negotiating claims, contractor CFOs paying them, and landowners who feel ignored.

## Impact If Fixed
Faster, fairer settlements, fewer disputes escalating to lawyers, and better relations for the next program in the same county.

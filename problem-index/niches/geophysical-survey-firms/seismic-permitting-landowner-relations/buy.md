# E-Signature for Multi-Party Tract Permits

**Niche:** [[niches/geophysical-survey-firms/seismic-permitting-landowner-relations/profile|Seismic Permitting & Landowner Relations]]
**Industry:** [[industries/geophysical-survey-firms|Geophysical Survey Firms]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** DocuSign and its peers can collect signatures; a seismic permit needs owner and tenant consent on one tract, a map of where equipment may go, encoded conditions, a fee schedule and a damage clause that travel with it to the crew.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #data-integration #workflow-orchestration #compliance #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to secure access to the most source and receiver locations on a survey's footprint before the crew arrives, with the fewest damage disputes afterwards, and whoever's permit rate keeps the crew from standing by gets the next program.

## The Problem
Permits are still largely signed on paper at a kitchen table, photographed or scanned, and keyed into a spreadsheet. Conditions are written in the margin. When the owner and the grazing tenant are different people, the agent drives twice.

## What Already Exists
DocuSign, Adobe Acrobat Sign and Dropbox Sign handle signatures, templates and audit trails; GIS tools draw maps.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs: templates for each permit type (private owner, tenant, state or university lands) with fee schedules and damage clauses; a tract map with the planned lines embedded and conditions drawn on it; multi-party routing for owner, occupant and lessee; conditions captured as structured fields (no vibrators within a distance of buildings, access dates, gate rules) rather than margin notes; offline signing in areas without coverage; and automatic update of point-level permit status when a permit is signed or revoked.

## Target Customer
Permit-agent firms and contractors' permit departments.

## Impact If Solved
Permits signed in one visit, conditions that reach the crew intact, and a record that stands up when a damage claim arrives.

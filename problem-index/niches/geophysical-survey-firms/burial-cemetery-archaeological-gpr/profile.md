# Burial, Cemetery & Archaeological GPR

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/geophysical-survey-firms|Geophysical Survey Firms]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to produce a map of probable burials that families, tribes and regulators accept as honest — neither overclaiming anomalies as graves nor missing them — and whoever's confidence language holds up when ground-truthing happens is hired for the next search.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$40M US and Canadian cemetery, archaeological and unmarked-grave geophysics (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~2% of US geophysical survey spend (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — GPR and magnetometry are digital; cemetery records are paper plat books and burial registers
**Target Buyer:** Tribal Historic Preservation Officer, cemetery board or church, cultural resource management firm principal investigator, developer's archaeologist
**Automation Potential:** Low to medium — grid processing automates; the interpretation is contested, consequential and must not be overstated

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
GPR searches for unmarked graves — historic cemeteries before development, church and municipal cemeteries with lost records, archaeological sites, and the searches at former residential and boarding schools — are a small market with outsized consequence. GPR detects disturbances in soil, not bodies; reports describe "reflections of interest" consistent with burials. The Star Blanket search reported more than 2,000 anomalies, and archaeologists have warned both against anomalies becoming "children" in public narrative and against using ambiguity to discredit the method. The buyer is usually a community, not an engineer, and the tools assume an engineer.

## Current Tools & Gaps
GSSI and Sensors & Software GPR, GPR-Slice and similar processing, magnetometry and resistivity as complements, and narrative reports. Gaps: no calibrated way to express burial likelihood, no link to cemetery records and plat maps, and community communication handled ad hoc.

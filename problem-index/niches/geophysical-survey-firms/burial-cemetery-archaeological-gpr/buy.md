# GPR Processing Adapted to Grave-Shaft Signatures and Records

**Niche:** [[niches/geophysical-survey-firms/burial-cemetery-archaeological-gpr/profile|Burial, Cemetery & Archaeological GPR]]
**Industry:** [[industries/geophysical-survey-firms|Geophysical Survey Firms]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** GPR processing packages produce excellent depth slices; none of them knows what a grave row looks like or reads the cemetery's plat book and burial register alongside the data.
**Tags:** #cnns #large-language-models #feature-engineering #data-integration #workflow-orchestration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to produce a map of probable burials that families, tribes and regulators accept as honest — neither overclaiming anomalies as graves nor missing them — and whoever's confidence language holds up when ground-truthing happens is hired for the next search.

## The Problem
A cemetery survey's interpretation depends on context: the original plat, burial registers, headstone surveys, aerial photos showing old rows, and oral history. Interpreters assemble this by hand alongside GPR depth slices.

## What Already Exists
GPR-Slice, GSSI RADAN, Sensors & Software EKKO_Project, and GIS for overlays.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs: feature extraction tuned to grave-shaft dimensions and orientation; row and alignment detection across depth slices; georeferenced plats and registers parsed into expected burial positions for comparison; a classification scheme with the community's preferred language and documented criteria; and outputs designed for community meetings as well as technical review.

## Target Customer
Cemetery and archaeological geophysics practitioners, including small firms and university groups.

## Impact If Solved
Interpretation that weighs data and records consistently, and deliverables communities can understand without a geophysicist in the room.

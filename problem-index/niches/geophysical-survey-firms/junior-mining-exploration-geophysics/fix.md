# The Survey Delivered After the Drill Season Closed

**Niche:** [[niches/geophysical-survey-firms/junior-mining-exploration-geophysics/profile|Junior Mining Exploration Geophysics]]
**Industry:** [[industries/geophysical-survey-firms|Geophysical Survey Firms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A junior funds a survey in spring to drill in summer, and the final interpretation arrives after the drill rig has left or the snow has come.
**Tags:** #time-series-forecasting #workflow-orchestration #automation #worker-facing #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn a ground or airborne survey into a ranked drill-target list that a junior explorer's board will fund and its qualified person will sign, and whoever's targets hit mineralisation most often keeps the claim-holder's next season.

## The Problem
Exploration seasons in Canada, Alaska and the mountain West are short. Survey crews, inversion and interpretation all queue behind each other at a busy contractor, and the junior's drill contractor is booked months ahead. A late interpretation means drilling on preliminary maps or losing a season, and a junior's financing depends on news flow.

## Why It's Still Broken
Survey firms schedule interpretation behind fieldwork; inversion runs are set up by hand; and the junior has no visibility into where its project sits in the queue.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Preliminary targets produced in the field from daily data with automated processing and quick inversions, clearly labelled as preliminary; a shared timeline tying survey completion, inversion and final interpretation to the drill contract dates; and alerts when slippage threatens the drill window.

## Who Feels the Pain
Junior CEOs and VPs of exploration, drill contractors left idle, and survey firm staff working nights in August.

## Impact If Fixed
Drill programs that start on time with defensible targets, and fewer lost seasons for companies that may only get one.

# Junior Mining Exploration Geophysics

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/geophysical-survey-firms|Geophysical Survey Firms]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn a ground or airborne survey into a ranked drill-target list that a junior explorer's board will fund and its qualified person will sign, and whoever's targets hit mineralisation most often keeps the claim-holder's next season.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$250M North American ground and airborne geophysics bought by junior and mid-tier explorers (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~10% of US-serving geophysical survey spend (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — magnetic, gravity, IP and EM data are digital; interpretation reaches the client as PDF maps and a slide deck
**Target Buyer:** VP Exploration or CEO of a junior explorer
**Automation Potential:** Medium — inversion and anomaly picking automate; targeting judgement and geological context do not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The junior explorer is a company of a few people with a claim, a share price and a drill budget raised for one season. It buys geophysics — induced polarisation and resistivity, ground and airborne magnetics, EM, gravity — to decide where to drill, and must disclose results through a qualified person under NI 43-101 in Canada or S-K 1300 in the US. It has no in-house geophysicist, so it depends on the survey firm or a consultant to turn data into targets. Tools are built for majors with geoscience departments; the junior gets maps and must decide alone. Federal airborne programs such as USGS Earth MRI now release regional data the juniors can build on.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Seequent Oasis montaj and Leapfrog, inversion codes, Geoscience ANALYST-style viewers, and consultants' reports. Gaps: no record of which targets were drilled and what they hit; uncertainty absent from target maps; and deliverables that arrive too late for the drill season.

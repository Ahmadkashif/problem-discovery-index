# The Research Nobody Commissioned

**Niche:** [[niches/creator-businesses/comment-and-community-signal/profile|Comment & Community Signal]]
**Industry:** [[industries/creator-businesses|Creator Businesses]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** The audience writes thousands of words of unsolicited feedback under every upload and the creator reads the first twenty.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #bert #k-means-clustering #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #automation #word-embeddings
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn the audience's own written feedback into the research the creator never commissions — and whoever reads it as data holds the only direct account of what the audience actually wants.

## The Problem
A channel with three hundred uploads has an enormous body of audience writing attached to it: explicit requests for topics, questions that reveal what was unclear, disagreement that shows where the creator lost people, references to what else the audience watches, and expressions of what they are trying to do with the information. It is timestamped, attached to specific content, and correlated with measured outcomes. It is read sporadically by one exhausted person and analysed by nobody.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The comment section was built as a social feature, so it is presented as a feed to scroll rather than as a corpus to analyse — an interface designed for conversation makes aggregate reading impossible. Volume grows past the point of manual reading exactly when the channel becomes valuable. Moderation tooling addresses harm rather than insight. And nobody framed the audience's writing as research.

## What to Build
Read the whole corpus and report what it says. Extract explicit requests and questions across the catalogue, which is the core and is the most directly actionable content — creators are told what to make next and cannot hear it. Cluster themes over time so recurring interests and emerging ones are distinguishable. Attach sentiment and reaction to the specific decisions made in each video, since the audience frequently names exactly what worked and what did not. Identify the confusion points, because a cluster of questions at the same moment in a video is a direct instruction about the edit. Track how the audience describes the channel, as their language is better marketing copy than anything the creator will write. Detect audience change over time, since drift is slow and invisible and shows up first in the comments. Surface the audience's other interests, which informs both content and sponsorship fit. Separate the loud minority from the representative body, as the most active commenters are not the audience and creators over-index on them. Feed the requests into the topic prediction work, so the ideation stage has evidence. Include community platforms and other surfaces, since the same audience writes in several places. And keep the creator reading some of it directly, because the relationship matters and analysis is not a substitute.

## Target Customer
Creators and audience managers, management companies, brands assessing audience fit, and community and moderation tool vendors.

## Impact If Built
An interface designed for conversation makes aggregate reading impossible, so the corpus grows unread. Extracting requests and questions across the catalogue gives the creator the audience research they never commissioned and already own.

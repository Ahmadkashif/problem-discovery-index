## The Problem
Advertising and production agencies formalised creative direction: the written brief with objective, audience, message and mandatories; the review process with defined rounds; consolidated feedback from a single voice; and sign-off gates. The discipline exists because unclear direction and scattered feedback destroy schedules and morale. Creator production has the same structure — a brief, an execution, rounds of feedback — and none of the practice.

## What Already Exists
Creative brief templates and disciplines; structured review with defined rounds; consolidated feedback protocols; approval gates; and creative team management practice.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a brief the creator cannot easily articulate. It requires: (1) capturing direction from someone whose vision is intuitive and who is also the on-screen talent, so the brief must be extracted rather than written — this is the substantive difficulty; (2) a weekly cadence rather than a campaign timeline, which rules out heavyweight process; (3) a team of one to three, often contractors, where agency process assumes roles; (4) an objective that is algorithmic performance rather than a campaign message, which changes what the brief must specify; and (5) measurable feedback available after publication, which agencies rarely have and which can close the loop here.

## Target Customer
Production leadership, creators, editors and contractors, and production management tool vendors.

## Impact If Solved
Agencies formalised the brief because bad direction destroys schedules, and creator production has none of it. Extracting a brief from an intuitive creator, at weekly cadence, is the adaptation — and publication metrics can close a loop agencies never had.

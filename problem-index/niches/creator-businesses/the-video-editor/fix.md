# Timestamps in a Message Thread

**Niche:** [[niches/creator-businesses/the-video-editor/profile|The Video Editor]]
**Industry:** [[industries/creator-businesses|Creator Businesses]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Feedback arrives as forty timestamps across three messages sent at midnight, and the editor has to reconstruct what the creator actually wants.

**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to give the editor a brief they can act on and feedback that means something — and whoever does it retains the people the whole production depends on.

## The Problem
The creator watches the cut and sends notes as they go: a timestamp and a few words, forty times, in a message thread, often late at night, sometimes contradicting an earlier note. The editor works through them, guesses at the intent behind the ambiguous ones, misses two in the scroll, and returns a version that prompts another round. Nobody is being unreasonable and the process is exhausting for both of them.

## Why It's Still Broken
Feedback goes wherever the two people already talk, so it inherits the properties of a chat thread — unstructured, unordered, unresolvable and easy to lose. Review tools exist and adoption requires both parties to change habit. The creator is giving feedback in the only spare moment they have. And nobody counts the revision rounds or the hours they cost.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Put the feedback in one place and give it structure. Move notes to a timestamped review tool rather than a chat thread, which is the fix and makes every note locatable and resolvable. Require a sentence of intent alongside the correction, since the intent is what lets an editor fix it properly rather than literally. Consolidate into one pass rather than a trickle, as notes arriving over six hours cause rework. Mark each note resolved, so nothing is missed in a scroll. Ask the creator for the three things that matter most, because a ranked list changes the edit and a flat list of forty does not. Bound the rounds explicitly, since the third round is where goodwill goes. Keep a record of recurring notes, as the same feedback given fifteen times is a brief nobody wrote down. Separate taste from error, because the two are mixed together and treated identically. Let the editor respond to a note rather than only comply, which prevents a wrong literal fix. And measure rounds per video, since it is the clearest indicator of whether the brief is working.

## Who Feels the Pain
Editors reconstructing intent from fragments; creators giving feedback in exhausted moments; teams losing days to avoidable rounds; and production schedules that never have slack.

## Impact If Fixed
Feedback inherits the properties of wherever the two people already talk, and a chat thread is unstructured and unresolvable. A timestamped review pass with intent attached to each note removes most rounds without changing anyone's standards.

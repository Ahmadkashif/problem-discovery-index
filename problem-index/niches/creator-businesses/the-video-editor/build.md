# A Brief and a Feedback Loop

**Niche:** [[niches/creator-businesses/the-video-editor/profile|The Video Editor]]
**Industry:** [[industries/creator-businesses|Creator Businesses]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** The editor makes the video and never finds out which of their decisions the audience responded to.
**Tags:** #worker-facing #workflow-orchestration #evaluation-metrics #automation #large-language-models #descriptive-statistics #confidence-intervals #tacit-knowledge-ml
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to give the editor a brief they can act on and feedback that means something — and whoever does it retains the people the whole production depends on.

## The Problem
The editor receives footage and a rough direction, makes several hundred decisions about structure, pacing, cuts and emphasis, and delivers. They get back a list of timestamps with corrections. The video publishes and its retention curve — which is a direct measurement of the pacing decisions they made — is never shown to them. Two years in, an editor has produced hundreds of videos and has no evidence about which of their own instincts are good.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The analytics belong to the creator's account and the editor is often a contractor, so the feedback loop crosses an access boundary nobody thought to bridge — a permissions model built for account security incidentally severed the learning loop. Briefs are verbal because the creator is the brief. Revision is handled in whatever tool is at hand. And editor development is nobody's responsibility.

## What to Build
Give the role direction and evidence. Structure the brief — what this video is for, who it is for, what the pacing should feel like, what must land — which is the core and replaces correction-after-the-fact with direction-before. Show the editor the retention curve mapped to their own cuts, since that is a direct measurement of their work and they have never seen it. Aggregate across videos so the editor learns their own patterns rather than reacting to one result. Structure feedback as intent rather than as a correction list, because "this section drags" teaches and "cut 4:12 to 4:20" does not. Bound revision rounds explicitly, as unbounded revision is the main source of schedule pressure and resentment. Retain the decisions made, so a returning editor or a replacement has continuity. Show the editor which videos performed and what they contributed, which is the missing connection to the business. Give feedback on the edit rather than only on the output, since much of what matters is craft the metrics do not capture. Let the editor propose rather than only execute, because they see more footage than anyone. Track revision volume and turnaround, which reveals where the process is failing. And measure editor retention, since it is the real cost and is currently unexamined.

## Target Customer
Production leadership and creators, editors themselves, editing and review tool vendors, and management companies employing production staff.

## Impact If Built
A permissions model built for account security incidentally severed the learning loop, so the editor never sees the measurement of their own work. The retention curve mapped to their cuts is direct evidence about craft and it already exists.

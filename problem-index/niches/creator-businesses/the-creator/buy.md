# Key Person Risk From Professional Practice

**Niche:** [[niches/creator-businesses/the-creator/profile|The Creator]]
**Industry:** [[industries/creator-businesses|Creator Businesses]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Professional practices have managed key person dependency for a century with partnership, delegation and succession, and creator businesses have none of it.
**Tags:** #workflow-orchestration #revenue-impact #evaluation-metrics #worker-facing #survival-analysis #confidence-intervals #automation #compliance
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to make a business that depends on one person's continuous output survivable for that person — and whoever does it keeps the asset the entire industry is built on from burning out.

## The Problem
Law firms, medical practices, consultancies and agencies all faced the problem of a business whose value sits in specific people. They developed answers: leverage models that push work down, associate development, client relationships held by the firm rather than the individual, partnership structures, succession planning and key person insurance. None of it is exotic and all of it is relevant to a business built on one person's output.

## What Already Exists
Leverage and delegation models; associate and staff development paths; institutional client relationships; succession and continuity planning; key person insurance; and partnership and equity structures.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a business where the person is the product rather than the practitioner. It requires: (1) an audience relationship that is with the individual and cannot be transferred to a firm, which is the fundamental constraint — a law firm's client can be reassigned and an audience cannot; (2) output that must appear to be the creator's even when produced by a team, so leverage is possible but must be invisible; (3) revenue that depends on an algorithm rather than on client relationships, removing the stability professional practice relies on; (4) no partnership structure or equity market, so there is nothing to sell into; and (5) a career that may be a decade rather than forty years, which changes what succession even means.

## Target Customer
Creators and their business managers, management companies, accountants and advisors serving creators, and insurers considering the risk.

## Impact If Solved
Professional practice solved key person dependency with transferable client relationships, and an audience is not transferable. Building leverage that stays invisible and continuity that does not require succession is the genuinely new problem.

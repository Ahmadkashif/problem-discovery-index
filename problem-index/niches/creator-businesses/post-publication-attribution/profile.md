# Post-Publication Attribution

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/creator-businesses|Creator Businesses]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to separate what the creator did from what the recommendation system decided — and whoever can say what a video was actually responsible for replaces a decade of anecdote with evidence.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$3.2B US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~13% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Low — outcomes reported, causes guessed
**Target Buyer:** Creator and channel leadership
**Automation Potential:** High — causal inference under a confound

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
This contest is explanation. After publication, the creator has a result that mixes their own choices with the platform's distribution decision — how many people it was shown to, which people, and how quickly it was given up on. Separating those is a causal problem under a confound that cannot be randomised away, and it is what turns a result into a lesson. It is used at the back of the process, by someone trying to learn, and shares little with choosing between candidate thumbnails.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Impression and traffic source breakdowns, retention graphs and subscriber attribution. The gaps: distribution effect not separated from content effect; early-impression allocation not modelled; results not compared against a matched baseline; variance not expressed; and no way to tell an unlucky video from a bad one.

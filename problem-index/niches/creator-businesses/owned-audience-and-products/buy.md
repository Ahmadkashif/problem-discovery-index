## The Problem
Direct marketing and subscription commerce solved this shape thoroughly: acquisition funnels with measured conversion at every step, offer testing, onboarding sequences, cohort retention analysis, churn prediction, lifetime value modelling and win-back programmes. The practice is old, documented and effective. Creators build subscription businesses with none of it, and their churn curves look exactly like an untended subscription business.

## What Already Exists
Conversion funnel design and measurement; offer and pricing testing; onboarding and activation sequences; cohort retention and churn modelling; lifetime value calculation; and win-back practice.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a product that is a person's ongoing attention. It requires: (1) the creator's own output as the retention mechanism, so churn is driven by publishing cadence and content fit rather than by a feature set — this is the substantive difference; (2) acquisition from a borrowed platform the creator cannot target, unlike paid acquisition with a controllable funnel; (3) a relationship that is parasocial, which makes aggressive direct marketing tactics actively damaging to the underlying business; (4) a one-person operation with no marketing team, so the practice must be embedded rather than staffed; and (5) an audience who can consume the free version indefinitely, which changes the value proposition from access to support.

## Target Customer
Creators and business managers, membership and newsletter platform vendors, management companies, and subscription tooling vendors.

## Impact If Solved
The subscription playbook is mature and assumes a controllable funnel and a product independent of a person. Adapting it where the product is the creator's attention, and aggressive tactics damage the asset, is the work.

# What Should I Charge

**Niche:** [[niches/creator-businesses/sponsorship-operations/profile|Sponsorship Operations]]
**Industry:** [[industries/creator-businesses|Creator Businesses]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The creator has no idea what their integration is worth and the brand knows exactly what it pays everyone else.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #quick-win #revenue-impact #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #worker-facing #automation #workflow-orchestration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn the highest-margin revenue in the business from an inbox and a guess into a priced, tracked, repeatable operation — and whoever does it captures the money creators are currently leaving on the table.

## The Problem
A brand offers a number. The creator has no benchmark, no sense of whether it is generous or insulting, and no comparison to what similar channels receive. The brand has bought a hundred integrations this year and knows the market precisely. The information asymmetry is total and it runs one way, which is why creators routinely accept offers well below what the same brand paid a comparable channel the previous month.

## Why It's Still Broken
Deal terms are private and creators are reluctant to discuss them, so no benchmark exists — and an asymmetry that benefits the buyer is not one the buyer will fix. Creators without agencies have no advisor. Anchoring on the brand's first offer is the default behaviour. And nobody has assembled the data because the people who could are the ones being underpaid.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Build the benchmark and give the creator a number. Collect anonymised deal data — audience size, category, deliverable, price — and report the distribution, which is the fix and is the single highest-value thing anyone could do for this profession. Compute a suggested range from the creator's own metrics rather than from follower count alone, since engagement and audience quality vary enormously at the same size. Show what the creator's last deals achieved on a per-view basis, as that is the internal benchmark they already have and never compute. Encourage a counter rather than acceptance, because the anchor is almost always low and creators rarely counter. Provide the language for a negotiation, since many creators find this the hardest part and avoid it entirely. Price deliverables separately — dedicated, integration, mention, usage rights, exclusivity — as brands bundle them and creators charge one number. Flag usage rights and exclusivity as priced items, because they are given away free constantly and are worth a great deal. Track what each brand has paid across deals, which reveals patterns. Report the creator's revenue per thousand views by brand, so the weak deals are identifiable. And keep contributions anonymous, since that is the only way the benchmark gets built.

## Who Feels the Pain
Creators accepting offers far below market; smaller creators with no agency advice; the profession as a whole, whose pricing power is suppressed by an asymmetry; and brands whose inconsistent pricing eventually becomes public.

## Impact If Fixed
An asymmetry that benefits the buyer is not one the buyer will fix, and creators will not discuss terms individually. An anonymised benchmark plus a per-view internal comparison gives the creator the only number they have never had.

# Pricing and Running the Partnership Business

**Niche:** [[niches/creator-businesses/sponsorship-operations/profile|Sponsorship Operations]]
**Industry:** [[industries/creator-businesses|Creator Businesses]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A million-dollar revenue line is priced by asking a friend what they charge and delivered against terms nobody tracked.
**Tags:** #gradient-boosting #revenue-impact #workflow-orchestration #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #automation #descriptive-statistics #data-integration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn the highest-margin revenue in the business from an inbox and a guess into a priced, tracked, repeatable operation — and whoever does it captures the money creators are currently leaving on the table.

## The Problem
A brand emails. The creator invents a number, or repeats the last one, or asks another creator what they would charge. The deal is agreed in the thread, deliverables are noted somewhere, usage rights are whatever the brand's contract says because nobody read it closely, and delivery is tracked by memory. When the brand asks how it performed, the creator screenshots a dashboard. Every part of this is beneath the value of the revenue, and creators know it and have no alternative.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The revenue arrived before the business function did, so it was absorbed by whoever was already answering email — a revenue line that grows inside an inbox never acquires a system. Pricing has no public benchmark, since deal terms are private and creators are reluctant to share. Agencies solve it for the largest creators only. And the tooling market has focused on the brand side, which has the budget.

## What to Build
Price it, track it, and prove it. Build a pricing model from audience size, engagement, category, deliverable type and historical deal data, which is the core and replaces a guess with a defensible number. Collect anonymised deal benchmarks so creators can see the market, since the information asymmetry is the single largest reason they are underpaid. Manage the pipeline properly — inbound, qualification, proposal, negotiation, contract — because an inbox is not a pipeline and deals are lost by simply not being followed up. Track deliverables, deadlines and usage rights against the contract, as rights overruns and missed deliverables are common and expensive. Report performance to the brand automatically in the terms the brand measures, which improves renewal rates and is currently a screenshot. Record every deal's terms and outcome, so the next negotiation has evidence. Detect undersold inventory — the integration that could have been a dedicated video, the category never pitched — since creators price reactively and rarely propose. Support outbound rather than only inbound, because the best-fit brands are frequently not the ones emailing. Handle exclusivity and category conflicts, which creators track in their heads and occasionally breach. And report revenue per thousand views by partner type, which is the metric that would reshape how creators sell.

## Target Customer
Creators and business managers, management companies and agencies, brands buying from disorganised counterparties, and influencer platform vendors serving the brand side.

## Impact If Built
A revenue line that grows inside an inbox never acquires a system, so the highest-margin money is the worst-run. Pricing from deal data and anonymised benchmarks removes the information asymmetry that keeps creators underpaid.

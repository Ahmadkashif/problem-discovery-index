# Collaborative Editing Adapted to CAT Files

**Niche:** [[niches/court-reporting-firms/steno-scoping-proofreading/profile|Steno Scoping & Proofreading Networks]]
**Industry:** [[industries/court-reporting-firms|Court Reporting Firms]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Cloud document editors let several people edit one file with tracked changes and comments; a reporter and her scopist still pass a CAT file back and forth.
**Tags:** #workflow-orchestration #data-integration #worker-facing #automation #evaluation-metrics
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to edit a reporter's raw CAT file into a certifiable transcript overnight in that reporter's own dictionary and style, and whoever needs the fewest audio checks per page in that reporter's writing takes the account.

## The Problem
On an expedited job, the reporter scopes the first half while the scopist does the second, and they merge files and dictionaries at midnight. Questions ("is it Smith or Smyth?") go by text.

## What Already Exists
Google Docs and Microsoft 365 co-authoring, with tracked changes, comments and version history; CAT vendors' file-sharing features.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs stroke-linked text, so every word keeps its underlying steno and audio timestamp, and live dictionary synchronisation between the editors. It needs reporter-only certification locks, comment threads tied to audio positions, and a change log that separates scopist edits from reporter edits for quality review.

## Target Customer
Reporters and scopists on expedited and daily-copy work; agencies with in-house scoping teams.

## Impact If Solved
Expedites finished by two people in parallel without a midnight merge, and a clean record of who changed what.

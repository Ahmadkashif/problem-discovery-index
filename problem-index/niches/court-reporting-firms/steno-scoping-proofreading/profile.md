# Steno Scoping & Proofreading Networks

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/court-reporting-firms|Court Reporting Firms]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to edit a reporter's raw CAT file into a certifiable transcript overnight in that reporter's own dictionary and style, and whoever needs the fewest audio checks per page in that reporter's writing takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$250M US spend on freelance scoping and proofreading of stenographic transcripts (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~8% (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — work happens in CAT software, files move by email and cloud folders
**Target Buyer:** The freelance reporter who hires a scopist; production managers at agencies that run scoping pools
**Automation Potential:** High — Stenograph's Check It and similar tools already suggest edits; the reporter-specific layer is the gap

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A scopist edits inside the reporter's own CAT file, using the reporter's dictionary, audio and job dictionary. A good one learns how a particular reporter writes: her conflicts, briefs and habitual misstrokes. That relationship is personal and sticky. Reporters keep the same scopist for years because a stranger's edit needs more checking. The competitors are individual scopists, scoping networks, agency pools and now CAT vendors' own AI editing tools, and they all win the same way: by needing fewer audio checks per page on that reporter's writing.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Case CATalyst and Eclipse with scopist licences, Check It and other built-in suggestion tools, shared audio files, and dictionaries emailed back and forth. The gaps: dictionary updates drift out of sync, nothing learns the reporter's patterns across jobs, and the edit history that would train such a model is discarded.

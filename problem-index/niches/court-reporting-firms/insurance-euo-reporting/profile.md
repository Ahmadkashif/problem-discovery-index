# Insurance EUOs & Recorded Examinations

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/court-reporting-firms|Court Reporting Firms]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get a claimant examined under oath, and the signed transcript back, inside the insurer's claim-decision window, and whoever turns no-shows and unsigned transcripts into a documented file inside that window takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$150M US spend on reporting examinations under oath and related sworn statements for insurers (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~4% (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — demand letters by mail, scheduling by phone with claimants' lawyers, interpreters booked separately, transcripts mailed for signature
**Target Buyer:** Special investigations unit manager or coverage counsel at a property, auto or no-fault insurer; account manager at a reporting firm serving carriers
**Automation Potential:** Medium — scheduling, reminders and signature tracking automate; the examination does not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
An examination under oath is a policy condition, not litigation. The insurer's counsel questions the insured under oath before deciding a claim, most often in property losses, suspected fraud and no-fault auto claims. The reporter's transcript, or a certificate of non-appearance when the claimant does not show, becomes the evidence on which coverage is granted or denied. The clock is the insurer's claim-handling deadline under state unfair-claims-practices rules, not a court's. Claimants often need interpreters, which links the work to [[industries/localization-services|Localization Services]]. Adjusters' phone-recorded statements are transcribed under [[niches/document-preparation-services/evidentiary-transcription/profile|Evidentiary & Investigative Transcription]]; EUOs taken before a reporter belong here.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Carriers' claims systems (Guidewire ClaimCenter, Duck Creek), counsel's calendars, reporting-firm scheduling and CAT software. The gaps: no-shows rebooked by phone, signature windows tracked in spreadsheets, interpreter bookings separate, and the evidence a denial rests on scattered across firms.

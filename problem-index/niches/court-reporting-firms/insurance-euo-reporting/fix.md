# The Certificate of Non-Appearance Nobody Can Find

**Niche:** [[niches/court-reporting-firms/insurance-euo-reporting/profile|Insurance EUOs & Recorded Examinations]]
**Industry:** [[industries/court-reporting-firms|Court Reporting Firms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** When a claimant skips two EUOs, the denial rests on two certificates of non-appearance, and months later counsel cannot find them.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #bert #workflow-orchestration #compliance #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get a claimant examined under oath, and the signed transcript back, inside the insurer's claim-decision window, and whoever turns no-shows and unsigned transcripts into a documented file inside that window takes the account.

## The Problem
A reporter waits the customary time, goes on the record, and certifies that the claimant did not appear. The certificate is emailed to counsel as a PDF. If the claimant later sues for the benefits, counsel needs every certificate, every scheduling letter and proof each was sent. Those documents sit in different inboxes and different firms' repositories.

## Why It's Still Broken
Reporting firms bill per appearance and treat the certificate as a minor deliverable. Counsel's document systems file it under the claim number if someone remembers. Nobody owns the chain.

## What a Fix Looks Like
The reporting firm issues each certificate as a structured record linked to the claim number, the scheduling letter it answers and any earlier appearances. A running non-appearance history per claim goes to counsel and the carrier's claims system. Documents arriving by email are classified and matched to claims automatically.

## Who Feels the Pain
Coverage counsel defending denials; SIU managers; reporters asked to testify about a non-appearance two years later.

## Impact If Fixed
Denials supported by a complete, findable record, and fewer coverage suits lost because one certificate went missing.

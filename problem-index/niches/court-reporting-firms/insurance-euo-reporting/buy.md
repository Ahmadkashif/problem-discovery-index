# Claims Diary Systems Adapted to EUO Conditions

**Niche:** [[niches/court-reporting-firms/insurance-euo-reporting/profile|Insurance EUOs & Recorded Examinations]]
**Industry:** [[industries/court-reporting-firms|Court Reporting Firms]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Carriers' claims systems already diary every claim deadline; the EUO's letters, appearances, transcript and signature live outside them.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #workflow-orchestration #compliance #data-integration #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get a claimant examined under oath, and the signed transcript back, inside the insurer's claim-decision window, and whoever turns no-shows and unsigned transcripts into a documented file inside that window takes the account.

## The Problem
An adjuster sees the claim's deadlines in the claims system but must ask counsel, who asks the reporting firm, whether the EUO happened, whether the transcript is out for signature and when the signature window closes.

## What Already Exists
Guidewire ClaimCenter, Duck Creek Claims and similar platforms with diaries, activity tracking and integrations for vendors such as appraisers and independent adjusters.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs an EUO activity type with the policy-condition letter dates, scheduled and rescheduled appearances, interpreter bookings and a certificate of non-appearance as a structured outcome. It needs transcript delivery and signature status pushed by the reporting firm, the signature window and its consequence under the policy and state rules, and a single evidence pack for a denial or a coverage suit.

## Target Customer
Claims operations and SIU leaders at carriers already running a modern claims platform; reporting firms seeking preferred-vendor integration.

## Impact If Solved
Adjusters see EUO status without phone calls, claim decisions stay inside regulatory windows, and the denial file is complete the first time.

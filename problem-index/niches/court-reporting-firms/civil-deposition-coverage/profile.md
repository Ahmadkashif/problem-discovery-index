# Civil Litigation Deposition Coverage

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/court-reporting-firms|Court Reporting Firms]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to put a licensed reporter, or a capture method the venue and parties accept, into every booked deposition, including the ones noticed for tomorrow morning, and whoever fills the most short-notice bookings without a cancelled examination takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.8B US spend on civil deposition reporting, including realtime, rough drafts, copies and video add-ons (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~50% (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — national agencies run booking portals, remote platforms and repositories; independent firms book by phone, email and text
**Target Buyer:** VP of operations or director of scheduling at a national or regional agency; litigation support manager at a law firm
**Automation Potential:** Medium — matching and confirmation are automatable, the reporter in the chair is not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Depositions are where the industry's money is: Ducker put freelancers at 72% of stenographic reporters, and the code's receipts concentrate in litigation-heavy states (California, Florida, Texas, New Jersey, New York). Law firms and insurers buy coverage first and transcript quality second, because a deposition with no reporter wastes a witness's day, an expert's fee and sometimes a discovery deadline. The national agencies (Veritext, Esquire, U.S. Legal Support, Lexitas, Magna Legal Services) grew by buying local firms largely for their reporter rosters. Independents compete on knowing the local bar and keeping their own reporters. The legal-support rollups that assemble these agencies are profiled as a Pass 2 pocket under [[niches/legal-support-services/legal-support-rollups/profile|Legal Support Services Rollups]].

## Current Tools & Gaps
Agency client portals, Zoom and agency remote-deposition platforms, CAT software, and spreadsheets and texting at smaller firms. The gaps: no forecast of which bookings will cancel or go unfilled, licensing and certification matched by memory, and no shared view of which reporters will accept short-notice work at what rate.

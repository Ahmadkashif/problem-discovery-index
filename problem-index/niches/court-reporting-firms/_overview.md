# Niche Analysis — Court Reporting Firms

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/court-reporting-firms|Court Reporting Firms]]

## Niche Selection

NAICS 561492 is the trade that makes and certifies the verbatim record of depositions, hearings and other proceedings, and sells live captions to deaf and hard-of-hearing users. It took ~$3.07B in 2022 at 2,989 employer firms, and 89% of establishments have fewer than five employees. Two forces shape every niche. The stenographers who make the record are ageing faster than they are replaced, while voice writers, digital reporters and speech recognition compete for the work under licensing rules that differ state by state. And the record is never the firm's own: it belongs to the proceeding and is sold to every party on equal terms. The eight niches below split the industry by what operators and their suppliers actually compete over. Market sizes are estimates of what US buyers spend on the service and its tooling within each niche, including nonemployer freelancers. They overlap and are not shares of Census receipts.

## Why These Niches

Civil deposition coverage takes the first market-share slot because freelance deposition work is most of the industry. Ducker put freelancers at 72% of stenographic reporters, and the national agencies were assembled to sell coverage. Court and appeal transcripts take the second because every appeal needs one, prices are set by statute and court rule, and the courts' turn to digital recording has created a transcription market from court audio. The two low-digitized niches still run on letters, paper exhibits and per-agency templates. Insurers' examinations under oath answer to claim-handling deadlines rather than courts, and administrative and arbitration hearings answer to each forum's own format and contract. The two underserved audiences are people the tools ignore. Low-income and self-represented litigants increasingly go through family and civil hearings that no court reporter covers. Deaf and hard-of-hearing users need live CART text accurate enough to follow technical content, and institutions are tempted to replace it with automatic captions. The two automatable niches are the most mechanical work in the trade: editing a raw capture into a certifiable transcript, and the rule-bound tail of signature, errata, delivery and retention after certification.

## Filter Notes

Seven of the eight level-1 niches are terminal. Transcript Production is not: its contested sentence produced two contests with different winners. In stenographic production, scopists and proofreaders compete to edit one reporter's raw CAT file overnight in her own dictionary. The winner is whoever knows that reporter's writing well enough to need the fewest audio checks, a sticky personal relationship that CAT vendors' AI editing tools now try to replicate. In digital reporting, transcription pools compete to turn a multi-channel recording and a monitor's annotations into a transcript that courts in that state will accept as certified. There the winner is whoever can certify in the most jurisdictions with evidence of accuracy, a legal and political contest, not an editing one. The inputs (stroke files versus audio), the workforce, the legal exposure and the buyer differ. It decomposes into **Steno Scoping & Proofreading Networks** and **Digital Reporting Transcription Pools**.

Three terminal calls were close. **Civil Litigation Deposition Coverage** could have split into the national agencies and the independent CSR-owned firms. It was kept whole because both win or lose on the same thing, a licensed officer in the chair on short notice, and independents mostly lose accounts by failing to cover a booking, not on price. The agencies' corporate development is profiled under Legal Support Services as [[niches/legal-support-services/legal-support-rollups/profile|Legal Support Services Rollups]]. **Court and Appeal Transcript Production** overlaps the digital-reporting sub-niche, since both transcribe audio. They are separate because court transcripts are priced by statute, ordered by courts and appellants, and judged against appellate deadlines, while digital deposition transcripts are a private agency product whose acceptance is the contest. The court systems themselves belong to the queued courts industry. **Insurance EUOs** and **Administrative, Arbitration & Public Hearings** are both non-litigation reporting. They are separate because one answers to a carrier's claim clock and the other to a forum's master contract and briefing schedule.

Five candidates were considered and rejected. **Legal videography and transcript-video synchronisation** is already a niche under Video Production SMB as [[niches/video-production-smb/legal-video-specialists/profile|Legal Video Specialists]], and its contest, admissible capture and sync, is the videographer's. **Interpreted and multilingual depositions** were rejected because the scarce input and the contest belong to interpreters, analysed under [[industries/localization-services|Localization Services]]. **Deposition summaries and litigation support** are paralegal work, covered under [[niches/legal-support-services/freelance-contract-paralegals/profile|Freelance & Contract Paralegal Services]] and the e-discovery providers under Small Law Firms. California's board has ruled only that AI summaries do not breach its rule against reporters writing them. **Broadcast realtime captioning** for television stations was rejected as its own niche: Ducker counted fewer than 1,000 reporters working primarily as captioners, station contracts are won on hourly rate at the FCC quality standard (a generic price contest), and the regulator is logged under Postproduction Studios. **Transcription of police, insurance and investigative recordings** made without a reporter in the room is [[niches/document-preparation-services/evidentiary-transcription/profile|Evidentiary & Investigative Transcription]] under Document Preparation Services, which left court proceedings, depositions and EUOs to this industry.

## Why These Pockets

No pocket qualifies, and the operators fail the gate first. A reporting firm sells certified text. Its largest staffs are reporters, scopists, proofreaders, schedulers and repository clerks, all production. The richest corpora in the industry are never the firm's own. The agencies' transcript archives, probably the largest bodies of sworn testimony anywhere, are commissioned through counsel and sold within each case. The reporters' stroke files and dictionaries sit on contractors' laptops, and court recordings are court records. That puts a live data-ownership switch on the CAT vendors (19) and the transcript-AI vendors (30), and would put one on the agency repositories if their summary desks were anything but production. Several pockets that touch this chain are already profiled elsewhere and get no second profile: [[niches/legal-support-services/legal-support-rollups/profile|🔍 Legal Support Services Rollups]] (fails gate, the agencies' corporate development); [[niches/personal-injury-law/litigation-analytics-court-data/profile|🔍 Court Data & Litigation Analytics Platforms]] (53, indexed under Personal Injury Law, which sells the docket-level view of the same proceedings); [[niches/small-law-firms/legal-bill-review-spend-management/profile|🔍 Legal Bill Review & Spend Management]] (49, which audits the reporting lines on defence invoices); [[niches/personal-injury-law/jury-verdict-settlement-reporters/profile|🔍 Jury Verdict & Settlement Reporters]] (45); [[niches/personal-injury-law/litigation-funding-underwriting/profile|🔍 Litigation Funding Underwriting]] (47); [[niches/document-preparation-services/speech-to-text-api-vendors/profile|🔍 Speech-to-Text API Vendors]] (22, kill switch); [[niches/recording-studios/forensic-audio-labs/profile|🔍 Forensic Audio Authentication Labs]] (fails gate, which examine disputed recordings); and [[niches/postproduction-studios/caption-and-loudness-regulators/profile|🔍 Caption Quality & Loudness Regulators]] (fails gate, the FCC's captioning oversight).

The strongest near-miss is the expert-testimony databanks (42). They hold what the vault looks for: member-contributed deposition transcripts of experts that appear in no public record, research that is itself the invoice (challenge studies bought before a Daubert motion), and a court-set clock. They fall short on the market around them, a handful of providers split by bar affiliation, and on labour, because most access is self-serve and research teams stay small. Their scheduling-order deadlines are extendable, which holds Q4 at 4. The "no kill switch" call rests on an estimate that transcripts under protective orders or client restrictions are kept out of the contributed corpus; if they are not, the switch is live. Court workforce research institutes (37) are where the stenographic-versus-digital fight is now being decided on evidence, in studies commissioned against statutory report dates, but each study is bespoke and the market is effectively one standing institute. Insurers' litigation management teams (28) have analysts and data but use the insight only to cut their own costs.

The rest fail the gate honestly. The associations on both sides of the licensing fight commission advocacy research rather than employing researchers, and both NCRA's Ducker study and AAERT's 2025 report came from outside consultancies. Licensing boards and the federal court reporting program set rules and administer exams, which is production. Recording-system vendors, deferred-payment financiers and M&A advisers are too small, or sell products rather than insight. The vault's favourite shape, a dated judgement whose outcome the producer holds and never grades, sits inside the operators. Every certified page is a claim of accuracy that errata, readbacks and disputed passages later test, and every realtime decision to interrupt or write through is a bet on what the audio will hold. That is a product opportunity for the agencies and CAT vendors, set out in [[niches/court-reporting-firms/digital-reporting-transcription/build|Per-Passage Accuracy Evidence for Digital Transcripts]] and [[niches/court-reporting-firms/read-sign-errata-delivery/build|Errata as a Quality Signal]]. It is not an insight business the operators could sell.

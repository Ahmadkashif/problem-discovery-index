# Transcription Platforms Adapted to Legal Certification

**Niche:** [[niches/court-reporting-firms/digital-reporting-transcription/profile|Digital Reporting Transcription Pools]]
**Industry:** [[industries/court-reporting-firms|Court Reporting Firms]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Hybrid transcription platforms already route machine drafts to human editors at scale; a certified legal transcript needs a chain from the officer in the room to the signature on the certificate.
**Tags:** #transformers #large-language-models #workflow-orchestration #compliance #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn a digital reporter's multi-channel recording and annotations into a transcript that courts in that state will accept as certified, and whoever can certify digital transcripts in the most jurisdictions with evidence of their accuracy takes the account.

## The Problem
Agencies send digital reporters' audio to transcription pools built for general business content, then rebuild legal formatting, speaker identification and certification by hand.

## What Already Exists
Hybrid human-and-machine transcription platforms (Rev, Verbit and others) and speech-to-text APIs with diarisation and custom vocabulary.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs the monitor's annotation log (speaker identities, spellings, exhibit marks, off-record times) imported as constraints, and legal page-line formatting with colloquy and parentheticals. It needs a custody record from capture to delivery, a certification workflow that follows each state's rule on who may certify, and confidentiality controls for protective-order material.

## Target Customer
Agencies scaling digital reporting; transcription companies wanting legal-grade work.

## Impact If Solved
Digital transcripts produced at platform speed without losing the legal chain that makes them usable.

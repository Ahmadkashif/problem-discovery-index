# Digital Reporting Transcription Pools

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/court-reporting-firms|Court Reporting Firms]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn a digital reporter's multi-channel recording and annotations into a transcript that courts in that state will accept as certified, and whoever can certify digital transcripts in the most jurisdictions with evidence of their accuracy takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$200M US spend on transcribing depositions and proceedings captured by digital reporters (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~6% (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** High — audio, annotations and transcripts are all digital; acceptance is the bottleneck
**Target Buyer:** VP of digital reporting at a national agency; production manager at a transcription company serving agencies
**Automation Potential:** High — speech recognition drafts most of the text; certification and acceptance are the human and legal steps

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Digital reporting splits the stenographer's job in two. A digital reporter in the room runs a multi-channel recording, swears the witness, logs speakers and keywords, and marks problem passages. A transcriptionist, often remote and often working from a machine draft, later produces the text. The national agencies use it to cover the stenographer shortage, and AAERT certifies the reporters. Whether the result counts depends on state law. Several states require a certified shorthand reporter, California's 2025 bill to license digital reporters failed, and Texas is studying the question. The contest is acceptance, not speed. General transcription platforms are analysed under [[niches/document-preparation-services/transcription-platforms/profile|General Transcription Platforms (Per-Minute, Hybrid)]].

## Current Tools & Gaps
Multi-channel recording and annotation software, speech-to-text engines, transcription platforms, and agency certification workflows. The gaps: monitor annotations rarely reach the transcriptionist in usable form, there is no per-passage evidence of accuracy, and certification practice varies by state and is tracked by hand.

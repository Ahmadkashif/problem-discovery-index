# Pro Bono Matching Platforms Adapted to Reporter Coverage

**Niche:** [[niches/court-reporting-firms/litigants-without-a-record/profile|Litigants Without a Record]]
**Industry:** [[industries/court-reporting-firms|Court Reporting Firms]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Pro bono platforms already match volunteer lawyers to legal aid cases; nothing matches reporters, reduced-rate or reimbursed, to the hearings those lawyers attend.
**Tags:** #k-nearest-neighbors #large-language-models #workflow-orchestration #automation #compliance
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to give a litigant a record an appellate court will accept for a hearing the court does not staff, at a price a fee-waiver litigant or legal-aid budget can pay, and whoever gets low-income litigants a reviewable record before the appeal deadline takes the account.

## The Problem
Many reporters will cover a pro bono hearing at a reduced rate or bill a reimbursement fund, but legal aid staff find them through personal contacts, and the reimbursement paperwork is left to the reporter.

## What Already Exists
Pro bono management platforms used by legal aid organisations and law firms post matters, match volunteers by practice area and track hours.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs reporters as a participant type, with licence and county coverage, and hearing-level requests with date, courtroom and expected length. It needs eligibility checks for fee waivers and transcript reimbursement funds, application packets generated for the reporter, and transcript delivery and reimbursement status tracked to the appellate deadline.

## Target Customer
Statewide legal aid networks and bar foundations running pro bono platforms; court reporter associations organising reduced-rate panels.

## Impact If Solved
Reduced-rate coverage that reaches beyond personal networks, and reimbursement money that actually flows to the hearings it was meant for.

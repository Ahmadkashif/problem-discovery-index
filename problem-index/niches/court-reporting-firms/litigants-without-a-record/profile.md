# Litigants Without a Record

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/court-reporting-firms|Court Reporting Firms]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to give a litigant a record an appellate court will accept for a hearing the court does not staff, at a price a fee-waiver litigant or legal-aid budget can pay, and whoever gets low-income litigants a reviewable record before the appeal deadline takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$120M US spend by parties, legal aid programmes and courts on private reporters and transcripts for family, civil and housing hearings that courts do not report (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~3% (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — requests by paper form, reporters booked by phone, and many hearings never recorded at all
**Target Buyer:** Managing attorney at a legal aid organisation; family law attorney; court self-help centre manager; the self-represented litigant
**Automation Potential:** Medium — triage and booking automate; a record the appellate court accepts still needs a certified officer or an approved recording

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Where courts are short of official reporters, they stop staffing civil, family and housing calendars, and parties who want a record must hire a private reporter. Wealthier litigants do; most self-represented parties do not know to ask. Without a transcript an appeal falls back on a settled statement, the parties' reconstruction of what was said, which is slow and often loses. California requires a reporter at no cost for fee-waiver litigants who ask (recalled). Its Transcript Reimbursement Fund pays transcript costs for indigent civil litigants, but in 2024/25 it had paid out only about $87,000 for pro bono cases and $48,000 for self-represented applicants by May. AAERT's 2025 report describes an unprecedented number of proceedings with no verbatim record. These litigants are invisible to tools built for law firms.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Court request forms, legal aid case management systems, pro bono matching platforms and the reporting firms' own scheduling. The gaps: nobody predicts which hearings will matter on appeal, fee-waiver rights go unused, and reimbursement funds are underused because applying is hard.

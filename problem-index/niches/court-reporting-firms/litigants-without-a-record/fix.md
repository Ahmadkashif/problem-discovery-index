# The Fee Waiver That Does Not Bring a Reporter

**Niche:** [[niches/court-reporting-firms/litigants-without-a-record/profile|Litigants Without a Record]]
**Industry:** [[industries/court-reporting-firms|Court Reporting Firms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A litigant with a fee waiver may be entitled to a free reporter, but only if she asks in time, on the right form, and the court can find one.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #workflow-orchestration #compliance #worker-facing #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to give a litigant a record an appellate court will accept for a hearing the court does not staff, at a price a fee-waiver litigant or legal-aid budget can pay, and whoever gets low-income litigants a reviewable record before the appeal deadline takes the account.

## The Problem
Self-represented litigants learn the court will not provide a reporter only when they arrive, or when they try to appeal and discover there is no transcript. The fee-waiver right, where it exists, depends on a request made in advance. Courts short of officials struggle to honour requests even when they are made.

## Why It's Still Broken
The right is explained in dense court notices, the request is a separate form, and nobody owns the moment between filing the fee waiver and the hearing date. Courts have no incentive to advertise a service they cannot staff.

## What a Fix Looks Like
When a fee waiver is granted, the litigant gets a plain-language prompt (text or email) explaining the record and offering a one-click request. The court sees requests against its reporter roster early enough to assign an official, a pro tem reporter or an approved recording.

## Who Feels the Pain
Self-represented parents and tenants; self-help centre staff; appellate courts deciding cases on settled statements.

## Impact If Fixed
More hearings with a record for the litigants with the most at stake, at the cost of a notification and a form.

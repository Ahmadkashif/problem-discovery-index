# Notes and Audio Kept on a Personal Drive

**Niche:** [[niches/court-reporting-firms/read-sign-errata-delivery/profile|Read-and-Sign, Errata & Certified Delivery]]
**Industry:** [[industries/court-reporting-firms|Court Reporting Firms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** State rules require reporters to keep their notes for years, so the record of a deposition taken in 2019 sits on a contractor's external hard drive, if it still exists.
**Tags:** #data-integration #compliance #workflow-orchestration #quick-win #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to close every transcript's review window (signature, errata, certification and delivery to every party) on the jurisdiction's clock without a staff member chasing it, and whoever closes the most transcripts on time with a complete certification record takes the account.

## The Problem
Reporters must retain stenographic notes, and increasingly audio, for periods set by state rules that differ by jurisdiction. Freelancers keep them on their own laptops and drives. When a transcript is challenged or a copy ordered years later, the firm asks a reporter who may have retired, moved agencies or lost a drive.

## Why It's Still Broken
The notes legally belong with the reporter, not the agency. Contractors have no shared storage, and retention periods are tracked by memory.

## What a Fix Looks Like
An encrypted archive owned by the reporter, with retention dates computed from each job's jurisdiction, automatic upload at certification, and agency access only on the reporter's authorisation or a court order.

## Who Feels the Pain
Reporters answering subpoenas years later; agencies unable to produce notes; litigants whose challenged record cannot be checked.

## Impact If Fixed
Retention obligations met and records recoverable, at storage cost measured in dollars per reporter per year.

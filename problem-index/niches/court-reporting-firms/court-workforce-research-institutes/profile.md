# Court Workforce & Technology Research Institutes

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/court-reporting-firms|Court Reporting Firms]]
**Category:** Insight Layer
**Value-Chain Position:** Specialist advisory & valuation

## What They Do
Research and consult for state court systems on staffing, workload and technology, including whether to replace stenographic reporters with digital recording. The National Center for State Courts is the standing provider, a non-profit whose research and court consulting divisions take paid studies for state courts. University policy institutes take occasional legislatively commissioned studies: Texas A&M's Public Policy Research Institute evaluated digital court recording for the Texas Office of Court Administration, with a final report dated 1 October 2026. Court administrators and legislatures pay for the studies.

## Insight Function
**Size:** ~10-30 workforce, workload and court-technology researchers and consultants at NCSC (estimate; NCSC's total staff was 167 in 2025 per OnThinkTanks). University institutes staff individual contracts and are not counted toward the gate
**Output:** Workload and staffing studies, cost and accuracy evaluations of recording methods, surveys of practice across states, policy recommendations
**Proprietary data:** Court statistics compiled from many states, survey and interview data gathered for each study, and accumulated comparisons of court practices; much of the underlying record is public
**Clock:** Report dates set in statute or legislative direction, such as Texas's October 2026 deadline; extendable in practice
**Buyer:** NCSC's vice president of research or head of court consulting

**Kill switches:** none. The studies run on public court statistics and data gathered for the commission.
**Verdict:** Logged — below threshold. The research is the invoice and the clocks are real, but each study is bespoke, much of the underlying data is public, and the market is effectively one standing institute plus occasional university contracts. The gate rests on NCSC alone and is marginal: if its court-workforce and technology staff number fewer than ten, this pocket fails the gate.

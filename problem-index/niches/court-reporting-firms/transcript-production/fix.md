# Per-Page Pay for Unequal Pages

**Niche:** [[niches/court-reporting-firms/transcript-production/profile|Transcript Production (Scoping, Proofreading & Digital Transcription)]]
**Industry:** [[industries/court-reporting-firms|Court Reporting Firms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A page of a medical expert talking over three lawyers pays the editor the same as a page of a cooperative witness answering yes and no.
**Tags:** #gradient-boosting #feature-engineering #worker-facing #quick-win #evaluation-metrics
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is that every competitor is fighting to turn a raw capture into a certifiable transcript by the delivery date at the lowest edit cost per page. But a stenographer's raw CAT file and a digital reporter's multi-channel recording are different inputs, worked by different people under different legal exposure, and the contests have different winners. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## The Problem
Scopists, proofreaders and transcriptionists are paid per page, so hard jobs are declined, passed around and finished late. The best editors drift away from the hardest work, which most needs them.

## Why It's Still Broken
Per-page pricing mirrors how transcripts are sold to clients, and nobody measures difficulty well enough to price it.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Attach a simple difficulty band to every job from the raw file's measurable features and pay a published premium for the top bands, funded by the expedite and technical surcharges clients already pay.

## Who Feels the Pain
Freelance editors; reporters whose hard jobs sit unassigned; clients waiting on expedited technical transcripts.

## Impact If Fixed
Hard jobs taken promptly by experienced editors, and pay that tracks effort, at little cost to the agency.

# Transcript Production (Scoping, Proofreading & Digital Transcription)

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/court-reporting-firms|Court Reporting Firms]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is that every competitor is fighting to turn a raw capture into a certifiable transcript by the delivery date at the lowest edit cost per page. But a stenographer's raw CAT file and a digital reporter's multi-channel recording are different inputs, worked by different people under different legal exposure, and the contests have different winners. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$500M US spend on the labour between capture and certification: scoping, proofreading and transcription of digitally recorded proceedings (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~15% (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — entirely screen work, but jobs move by email and file share and are paid per page
**Target Buyer:** Production manager at an agency; the reporter who hires her own scopist
**Automation Potential:** High — the most mechanical work in the industry, and where speech recognition and language models land first

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Every page an agency sells passes through an edit. A stenographer's raw translation goes to a scopist who works in the reporter's own CAT software and dictionary, then to a proofreader, then back to the reporter to certify. A digital reporter's recording goes to a transcriptionist who types or corrects a machine draft from audio, guided by the monitor's annotations, and the result must then be certified under whatever rule the jurisdiction allows. Both are paid by the page, and both are where Stenograph's Check It, speech-to-text vendors and AI editing tools are aimed. They differ on the decisive point. The steno edit is won by knowing one reporter's writing. The digital transcript is won by getting a court to accept it.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Case CATalyst and Eclipse for scoping, Word and PDF markup for proofreading, transcription platforms and speech-to-text engines for digital work, and file shares between them. The gaps: no estimate of edit effort before a job is assigned, per-page pay that ignores how hard a page was, and no record of what each pass changed.

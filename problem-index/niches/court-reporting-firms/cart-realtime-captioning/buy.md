# Meeting-Platform Captions Adapted to Human CART

**Niche:** [[niches/court-reporting-firms/cart-realtime-captioning/profile|CART & Realtime Captioning]]
**Industry:** [[industries/court-reporting-firms|Court Reporting Firms]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Zoom and Teams already accept third-party captions through an API; the human captioner's preparation, handoffs and transcript rules sit outside it.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #word-embeddings #workflow-orchestration #worker-facing #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to give a deaf or hard-of-hearing user live text accurate enough on technical and multi-speaker content to count as effective communication under the ADA, and whoever proves that accuracy on the user's own courses and meetings, against cheaper automatic captions, takes the account.

## The Problem
A remote CART session means a captioner connected to the meeting, streaming text through a caption token or a separate viewer, often juggling links for back-to-back sessions. When the platform's automatic captions are switched on as well, users see two competing streams.

## What Already Exists
Zoom and Microsoft Teams caption APIs, caption viewers from CAT vendors and captioning agencies, and the platforms' built-in automatic captions.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs per-session preparation packs attached to the calendar event and delivered to the assigned captioner, plus clean handoff between two captioners on long sessions. It needs a priority rule so human CART supersedes automatic captions for the user who requested it, transcript distribution honouring instructors' intellectual-property restrictions, and session logs for the disability office.

## Target Customer
Captioning agencies and in-house accessibility teams at universities and large employers standardised on one meeting platform.

## Impact If Solved
Less time lost to links and handoffs, a cleaner experience for the user, and records the institution can show when its ADA compliance is questioned.

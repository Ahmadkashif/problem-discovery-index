# CART & Realtime Captioning

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/court-reporting-firms|Court Reporting Firms]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to give a deaf or hard-of-hearing user live text accurate enough on technical and multi-speaker content to count as effective communication under the ADA, and whoever proves that accuracy on the user's own courses and meetings, against cheaper automatic captions, takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$350M US spend on CART and live captioning for education, employment, government meetings and events, excluding offline film and TV captions (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~10% (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** High — mostly remote, streamed into Zoom, Teams and browser viewers; preparation and accuracy evidence are not digitised at all
**Target Buyer:** Director of disability or accessibility services at a university; ADA coordinator at an employer or public body; owner of a captioning agency
**Automation Potential:** Medium — automatic captions handle clear single-speaker speech; technical and multi-speaker content is where human CART earns its rate

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
CART providers write realtime text for a person, not for a record. The user is a deaf or hard-of-hearing student, employee or citizen, and nobody corrects the text afterwards. Census codes real-time captioning to this industry, and the providers are mostly trained stenographers and voice writers; Ducker counted fewer than 1,000 reporters working primarily as captioners in 2013. BLS expects CART demand to grow as the population ages, while institutions weigh automatic captions as a cheaper substitute. Offline captions for film and television belong to [[niches/postproduction-studios/captions-subtitles-and-accessibility/profile|Captions, Subtitles & Accessibility Files]], and accessible document and media remediation to [[niches/digital-accessibility-firms/document-and-media-accessibility/profile|Document & Media Accessibility]].

## Current Tools & Gaps
CAT software with realtime output, caption streaming viewers, the meeting platforms' caption APIs, and agency scheduling. The gaps: preparation materials rarely arrive, accuracy on the user's actual content is never measured, and the decision to replace CART with automatic captions is made without evidence.

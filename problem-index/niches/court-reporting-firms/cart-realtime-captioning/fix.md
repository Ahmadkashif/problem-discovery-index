# Preparation Materials That Never Arrive

**Niche:** [[niches/court-reporting-firms/cart-realtime-captioning/profile|CART & Realtime Captioning]]
**Industry:** [[industries/court-reporting-firms|Court Reporting Firms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Every captioning contract asks instructors for slides and vocabulary in advance, and most sessions start without them.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #word-embeddings #k-nearest-neighbors #worker-facing #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to give a deaf or hard-of-hearing user live text accurate enough on technical and multi-speaker content to count as effective communication under the ADA, and whoever proves that accuracy on the user's own courses and meetings, against cheaper automatic captions, takes the account.

## The Problem
The disability office emails instructors asking them to share materials with the captioner. Few reply, and those who do send a syllabus at the start of term. The captioner meets each week's vocabulary live.

## Why It's Still Broken
Instructors see the request as extra work with no visible benefit. The captioner has no access to the course's learning-management site, and the agency has no process for gathering materials.

## What a Fix Looks Like
With the user's and institution's consent, give the captioner read-only access to the course site, or have a tool pull new slides and readings weekly. Generate a vocabulary pack automatically, ranked by the terms this captioner has struggled with before, so the instructor needs to do nothing.

## Who Feels the Pain
CART captioners writing technical content cold; deaf and hard-of-hearing students reading the errors; disability offices fielding complaints.

## Impact If Fixed
Better captions on the hardest content within weeks, at near-zero cost to instructors.

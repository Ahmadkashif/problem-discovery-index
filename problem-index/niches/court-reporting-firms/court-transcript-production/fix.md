# The Extension Request Nobody Tracks

**Niche:** [[niches/court-reporting-firms/court-transcript-production/profile|Court and Appeal Transcript Production]]
**Industry:** [[industries/court-reporting-firms|Court Reporting Firms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** An official reporter in court all day produces appeal transcripts at night, asks the appellate clerk for one extension after another, and no one can see her backlog until an appeal stalls.
**Tags:** #time-series-forecasting #linear-regression #workflow-orchestration #compliance #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to deliver certified transcripts of court proceedings, from an official reporter's notes or the court's own audio, inside the appellate record deadline at the statutory page rate, and whoever clears appeal transcripts without extension requests takes the account.

## The Problem
Official reporters write proceedings during the day and produce ordered transcripts in their own time. A long trial can generate thousands of pages of appeal transcript for one reporter. Extension requests go to the appellate court case by case, and the trial court administrator, who assigns courtrooms, rarely sees how many pages each reporter owes.

## Why It's Still Broken
The appellate court tracks extensions per appeal, the trial court tracks courtroom coverage per day, and the reporter tracks her own backlog. The shortage that left Bexar County offering more than $10,000 a month makes officials hard to relieve, so backlogs grow quietly.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A shared backlog board per reporter: pages owed, due dates and extensions granted, with a simple forecast of completion from the reporter's own historical pages per week. The administrator can then reassign courtroom days or send overflow to a contract reporter or transcription company before the clerk sees a third extension.

## Who Feels the Pain
Official reporters working nights and weekends; appellants, including criminal defendants, waiting on the record; appellate clerks processing extension motions.

## Impact If Fixed
Appeal records filed on time more often, fewer extension motions, and a measurable case for staffing that administrators currently make from anecdote.

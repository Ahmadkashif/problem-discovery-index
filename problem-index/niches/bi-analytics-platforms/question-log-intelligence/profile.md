# Question Log Intelligence

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/bi-analytics-platforms|BI & Analytics Platforms]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor that gets here is fighting to turn the record of how an organisation questions itself into a product — and whoever does that takes a position no competitor can copy, because the query log is the one asset each platform holds exclusively.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$680M US, almost entirely latent
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~4% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** None — no platform analyses the asking
**Target Buyer:** Data leadership, and the platform vendors themselves
**Automation Potential:** Very High — the log is complete, structured and already stored

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Every BI platform records, in full, how an organisation asks questions about itself: every query, every dashboard opened, every filter set, every drill-down, by whom and how often. That is a map of what the business genuinely cares about, where its attention has shifted, which questions recur and which are abandoned halfway. It is the single dataset each vendor holds exclusively — the warehouse vendors have the data but not the asking, and the collaboration tools have the discussion but not the queries. The category has spent two decades rendering charts and has never analysed the asking, which its own industry Analysis note identifies as the unexploited asset. The distinct capability is treating the question log as a first-class dataset with its own products: demand-driven roadmapping for the data team, early detection of shifting business attention, and identification of the questions the estate cannot answer.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Usage and audit logs in every platform, query history in every warehouse, and basic adoption reporting. The gaps: logs are retained for audit and billing rather than analysed for meaning; queries are not mapped to the business questions behind them, so the log is a stream of SQL rather than a record of concerns; drill-downs and abandoned explorations — the richest signal about what someone was trying to find out — are discarded; no product tells a data team what the organisation is asking about that the estate does not cover; and nothing detects shifts in what is being asked, which is an early indicator of what the business is worrying about.

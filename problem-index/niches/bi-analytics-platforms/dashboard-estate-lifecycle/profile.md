# Dashboard Estate Lifecycle

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/bi-analytics-platforms|BI & Analytics Platforms]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in analytics governance is fighting to make retiring an asset safe enough that an organisation will actually do it — and whoever does that takes the governance account, because every estate grows monotonically and every governance programme has failed to stop it.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$940M US analytics governance, cataloguing and estate management
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~6% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — governance features ship in every platform and go unused
**Target Buyer:** Data governance leads and platform owners
**Automation Potential:** Very High — usage, lineage and duplication are all computable

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Every analytics estate grows and none shrinks. Eleven thousand dashboards, of which four hundred are opened in a month; the rest are not deleted because somebody might need one, and that somebody is never identified because nobody looks. This is the same shape as macro sprawl in support and template sprawl in signature workflow, and it has the same cause: creation is one click, deletion requires certainty nobody can obtain, and the asymmetry compounds every quarter. The consequences are concrete rather than aesthetic. Search becomes useless, which drives people to build new assets rather than find existing ones, which grows the estate further. Users cannot tell which of six similarly-named dashboards is authoritative, so they ask an analyst, which is the previous niche's queue. And every asset is a maintenance liability against schema changes. The contested capability is making retirement safe rather than exhorting people to tidy up.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Certification badges, folders and workspaces, usage reporting of varying depth, catalogues with ownership fields, and periodic manual clean-up campaigns. The gaps: certification marks what is good and does nothing about the uncertified mass, which is where the confusion lives; usage reports exist and stop short of a recommendation, so nobody acts; duplicate and near-duplicate assets are never identified, though they are the easiest win; ownership is a field that goes stale the moment somebody changes team; and there is no reversible retirement path, so the only options are keep and delete, and the rational choice is always keep.

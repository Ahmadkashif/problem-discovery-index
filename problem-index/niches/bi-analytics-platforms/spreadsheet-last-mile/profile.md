# The Spreadsheet Last Mile

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/bi-analytics-platforms|BI & Analytics Platforms]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor here is fighting to make the spreadsheet a governed, refreshable surface on the warehouse rather than a dead export — and whoever does that takes the finance account, because finance will not stop using spreadsheets and every vendor has spent fifteen years pretending otherwise.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$2.4B US attributable to the analytical work that finishes outside the BI platform
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~15% of category revenue, and a larger share of the work
**Digital Adoption:** Low — the work leaves the platform and never returns
**Target Buyer:** Finance, FP&A and operations analysts
**Automation Potential:** High — the export, the transformation and the recombination are all observable

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The export button is the most used feature in business intelligence and the least discussed. A very large share of analytical work in every organisation ends outside the platform: the dashboard is opened, the data is exported, and the actual work — the reconciliation, the allocation, the scenario, the commentary, the version that goes to the board — happens in a spreadsheet. This is not user failure. Spreadsheets do things BI tools do not: arbitrary calculation, what-if modelling, cell-level annotation, and a mental model every finance professional already has. The category's response for fifteen years has been to treat this as an adoption problem and to build features intended to make it unnecessary, which has not worked anywhere. The contested capability is the opposite posture — making the spreadsheet a first-class, governed, live surface on the warehouse, so the work that will happen there anyway happens on trustworthy refreshable data with lineage intact.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Spreadsheet connectors from the major platforms and warehouses, live-query add-ins of varying quality, cloud spreadsheet integrations, and enterprise performance management products aimed at replacing the spreadsheet outright. The gaps: exports are dead the moment they are taken, so every figure in circulation is a snapshot of unknown age; the transformations applied after export are invisible, which means the numbers in the board pack cannot be traced to the warehouse; nobody knows which spreadsheets exist or what they depend on, so a schema change breaks work nobody can enumerate; and versions proliferate by email with no authority among them.

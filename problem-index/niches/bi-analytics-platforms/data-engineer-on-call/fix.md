# Paged Without Knowing What Breaks Downstream

**Niche:** [[niches/bi-analytics-platforms/data-engineer-on-call/profile|Data Engineer On-Call]]
**Industry:** [[industries/bi-analytics-platforms|BI & Analytics Platforms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** An alert says a table did not refresh and does not say whether anything downstream of it matters, so every page is treated as urgent and the engineer cannot tell the difference at two in the morning.
**Tags:** #graph-theory #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #hypothesis-testing #worker-facing #quick-win #workflow-orchestration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor here is fighting to turn a pipeline alert into a diagnosis and a remedy rather than a page — and whoever does that takes the data platform account, because detection is a solved and crowded market and remediation is nobody's product.

## The Problem
The page says a staging table is stale. The engineer does not know whether that table feeds the executive dashboard reviewed at eight in the morning, the finance close running tomorrow, or an experiment nobody has opened since March. They get up and fix it, because the cost of being wrong about that is high and the information to be right is not in the alert. Over a year this is a large number of unnecessary nights, and the lineage that would have answered it in one line is sitting in the orchestrator and the catalogue.

## Why It's Still Broken
Alerts are generated by the component that detected the problem and carry that component's view, which stops at the asset. Lineage exists in a different tool with a different owner and has not been joined to alerting, despite both being present in most organisations. Asset importance is also undefined — nothing records that this dashboard is reviewed by the executive team and that one is abandoned — so even with lineage the criticality question needs a second missing input. And the cost falls on the on-call engineer rather than on a budget, which is why it has stayed unfixed.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Put the blast radius in the alert. Join lineage to alerting so every page lists what is downstream: which dashboards, which reports, which extracts, which downstream pipelines. Derive importance from usage rather than from a declaration nobody maintains — views in the last month, audience seniority, whether it feeds a scheduled delivery or a regulatory report — which is computable from platform logs and is far more accurate than a criticality field somebody set in 2022. Combine the two into a severity the alert carries, with the reasoning shown, so an engineer can act on it at two in the morning without opening three tools. Route by that severity: a page for the executive dashboard's upstream, a morning ticket for the abandoned experiment's, which is the entire point and is currently impossible. Include the next scheduled consumption time, since a table that feeds a report due in nine hours is a different problem from one feeding a report due in twenty minutes. And report what the routing change saved in pages, because that number is what protects it from being reverted the first time something important is missed.

## Who Feels the Pain
On-call data engineers treating every page as urgent because they cannot tell; data leaders losing people to on-call burden; and organisations whose genuinely critical pipelines get the same response as their abandoned ones.

## Impact If Fixed
Lineage and usage logs both exist in nearly every organisation and joining them to alert routing is integration rather than invention. Usage-derived importance avoids the declared-criticality field that has failed everywhere, and severity-based routing removes a large share of night pages outright.

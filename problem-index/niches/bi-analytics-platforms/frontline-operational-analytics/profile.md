# Frontline Operational Analytics

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/bi-analytics-platforms|BI & Analytics Platforms]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor here is fighting to put a decision-shaped answer in front of someone standing on a shop floor in the moment they can act on it — and whoever does that takes the operations account, because a dashboard nobody on shift opens is worth nothing.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.8B US operational and frontline analytics
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~11% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Low — the decision-makers are not at desks and were not designed for
**Target Buyer:** Operations leadership in retail, manufacturing, logistics, healthcare and hospitality
**Automation Potential:** High — the decisions are recurring, bounded and well specified

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Business intelligence was built for people with a laptop, a query editor and an hour. The majority of operational decisions in the economy are made by people with none of those: a shift supervisor deciding whether to open another line, a store manager deciding what to mark down, a charge nurse deciding how to staff the next four hours, a dispatcher deciding which job to move. These people have the highest-frequency, most immediately consequential decisions in their organisations and the least analytical support, and the reason is not that nobody built them a dashboard — most have one — but that a dashboard is the wrong artefact. It answers what happened, on a screen they would have to go and find, in a form that requires interpretation, at a moment when they are doing three things. The contested capability is delivering a specific recommended action, at the moment of decision, on whatever device is to hand, with the reasoning attached.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Mobile BI apps from the major vendors, operational dashboards on wall displays, alerting on thresholds, and in some verticals purpose-built operational tools. The gaps: mobile BI is a shrunken desktop experience and is used accordingly; wall displays are ambient and non-actionable, so they become wallpaper within a fortnight; alerts are threshold-based and either constant or absent; nothing is framed as a decision, so the frontline worker must translate a number into an action, which is the analytical step they have least time for; and the outcome of decisions is not captured, so nothing improves.

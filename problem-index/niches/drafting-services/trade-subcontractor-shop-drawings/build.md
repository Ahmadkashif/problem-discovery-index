## The Problem
A millwork specification section names the quality grade under the AWI standards, materials, hardware, finishes and what the submittal must include. A storefront section names the system, finish, performance requirements and anchorage. The architect reviews shop drawings against these, and the most common comments are omissions: hardware not identified, grade not stated, a finish missing, a detail not shown. The drafter, working from the elevations, often never read the specification section.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Specification sections are long, inconsistent documents, and the trades are too small and fragmented for software vendors to target.

## What to Build
A checker that reads the relevant specification sections and the architect's drawings, produces a submittal requirements list (what must appear on the shop drawings and product data), and checks the draft shop drawings against it before submittal, citing the paragraph for each gap.

## Target Customer
Millwork shops, glazing contractors and miscellaneous metals fabricators, and the bureaus that draw for them.

## Impact If Built
Fewer rejections for omissions, and weeks saved per submittal cycle on the general contractor's schedule.

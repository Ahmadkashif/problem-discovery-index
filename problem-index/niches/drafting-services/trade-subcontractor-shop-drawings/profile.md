# Trade Subcontractor Shop Drawings

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/drafting-services|Drafting Services]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get a small trade subcontractor's shop drawings approved on the first submittal inside the general contractor's submittal schedule, and whoever turns architect comments into a clean resubmittal fastest takes the subcontractor's repeat work.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$600M a year in outsourced shop drawings for millwork and casework, storefront and curtain wall, miscellaneous and ornamental metals, railings and similar architectural trades (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~12% (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium-Low — drawings are digital, but inputs are architect elevations, specification sections and field dimensions that arrive late
**Target Buyer:** Owner or project manager at a millwork shop, glazing contractor or miscellaneous metals fabricator
**Automation Potential:** Medium — layout and detailing automate; reading the specification and the architect's intent does not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Under AIA A201 the subcontractor, not the architect, is responsible for the dimensions and quantities on its shop drawings, and nothing can be built until they are approved. A ten-person millwork shop or glazing contractor rarely employs a full-time drafter, so it buys shop drawings from a bureau. The tools ignore this buyer: construction software serves general contractors, design software serves architects, and cabinet and glazing software serves production rather than submittals. The contest is first-submittal approval, because each "revise and resubmit" costs the trade weeks in the general contractor's schedule.

## Current Tools & Gaps
AutoCAD; Cabinet Vision and Microvellum for casework; glazing system manufacturers' tools; Procore or Autodesk Build for the general contractor's submittal register. The gaps: no cross-check of the shop drawings against the specification section, architect comments retyped by hand, and field dimensions arriving after the drawings were due.

# The Revision Round That Was Never Priced

**Niche:** [[niches/drafting-services/per-sheet-overflow-drafting/profile|Per-Sheet Overflow Drafting]]
**Industry:** [[industries/drafting-services|Drafting Services]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The quote included two revision rounds, the client sent five, and the bureau did the last three free because nobody kept count until the invoice.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #descriptive-statistics #workflow-orchestration #revenue-impact #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn a one-off marked-up set into correct, standards-conforming sheets by the client's deadline at a fixed per-sheet price, and whoever needs the fewest clarification rounds takes the overflow work.

## The Problem
Per-sheet quotes include one or two rounds of client comments. In practice comments arrive in fragments, by email and phone, and whether a set of comments is "a round", a correction of the bureau's error or new scope is decided at invoice time, when the bureau has the least leverage.

## Why It's Still Broken
Nothing classifies incoming comments as they arrive. Small bureaus do not want to argue with a client who may send more work, so they absorb the cost.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Log every incoming comment set against the job's revision allowance as it arrives, classified as bureau error, client clarification or new scope, with a running count visible to both sides. When a round exceeds the allowance, a change request is drafted before work starts.

## Who Feels the Pain
Owners of two- and three-person bureaus; drafters doing unpaid rework.

## Impact If Fixed
Revision rounds become priced work rather than margin leakage, and clients see the cost of piecemeal comments.

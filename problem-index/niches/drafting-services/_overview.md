# Niche Analysis — Drafting Services

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/drafting-services|Drafting Services]]

## Niche Selection

A drafting bureau turns someone else's design intent into drawings at a per-sheet, per-ton or hourly price and carries no design liability, only accountability for its own errors. The US industry is small and fragmented — 2,956 firms and ~$1.68B of receipts in 2022, 85% of firms with fewer than five people — while the volume work has moved offshore and to the buyers' own detailing departments. The eight niches below split the industry by what the buyer is purchasing and what competitors actually fight over. Market sizes are estimates of US-serving outsourced drafting and detailing spend in each niche, onshore and offshore, against an estimated ~$5B total; they are not construction or design-fee figures.

## Why These Niches

Steel detailing takes the first high-share slot because it is the largest, most formalised specialty, with its own standard of practice, certification body and per-ton price, and a buyer who sees every error at erection. Outsourced construction documents take the second because design firms' production hours are the largest pool of drafting work in the economy, and the pool most exposed to AI documentation tools. The two low-digitized niches are where tape measures and paper still start the job: residential plans drawn from site visits and local plan-check habits, and drawing archives that exist only as blueprints and faded scans. The two underserved audiences are buyers the software ignores: small trade subcontractors who must produce shop drawings but employ no drafter, and patent practitioners whose figures must satisfy a regulator's rulebook that neither CAD nor legal software checks. The two automatable niches are the most rule-bound work in the industry: MEP fabrication models cut into spools by fitting and hanger rules, and rebar placing drawings and bar lists governed by ACI and CRSI practice.

## Filter Notes

Seven of the eight level-1 niches are terminal. Outsourced Construction Document Production is not: writing its contested sentence produced two contests with different winners. One-off overflow sets are bought per sheet, under a deadline, from whichever bureau can read unfamiliar markups right the first time; the winner is the one needing the fewest clarification rounds, and small US bureaus and freelancers compete with offshore firms on equal terms. Dedicated offshore teams are bought as multi-year contracts by larger firms; the winner is whoever keeps a trained team stable inside one firm's standards, which is a retention and knowledge-continuity contest that only firms of some scale can enter. Different buyers, contracts and failure modes, so the niche decomposes into **Per-Sheet Overflow Drafting** and **Dedicated Offshore Production Teams**.

Two terminal calls were close and are recorded honestly. **Structural Steel Detailing** could be split into building and bridge detailing, which NISD certifies separately, but both are bought by fabricators on the same evidence, fewest errors reaching the shop inside the approval schedule, so one contest governs. **Trade Subcontractor Shop Drawings** groups millwork, glazing and miscellaneous metals, which use different tools; it is kept whole because the buyer, the clock (the general contractor's submittal register) and the failure (a rejected first submittal) are the same across trades.

Three candidates were considered and rejected. **Mechanical part drawings for manufacturers** employ a fifth of US drafters, but most of that work sits in-house at manufacturers and is being absorbed by model-based definition in SolidWorks and Inventor; the bureau share is small and its contest belongs to the manufacturing industries. **Fire sprinkler layout** looks like drafting but carries design responsibility under NICET or engineer oversight, which crosses this industry's no-design-liability boundary; it belongs with fire protection contractors. **Survey plat and civil drafting** was rejected because the sealed deliverable belongs to [[industries/land-surveyors|Land Surveyors]] and [[industries/engineering-consultants|Engineering Consultants]], and the drafting inside it competes on nothing distinct from overflow drafting. Precast concrete detailing was folded into the rebar niche as a close sibling rather than given its own slot.

## Pass 2 — Insight-Layer Discovery

Second, orthogonal sweep. Pass 1 searched across the operator layer, where the typical US bureau has about three people and even the largest offshore enterprises sell production rather than research. This pass sweeps seven positions in the value chain — aggregator/rollup, payer & intermediary, data & benchmark vendors, association research arms, specialist advisory & valuation, regulatory bodies, and suppliers selling into the industry — looking for a dedicated insight function of 10+ people whose analytical output is the billable deliverable. Full method in `_direction.md`.

Construction project lead and plan data, which detailers and trade subcontractors buy to find work, was indexed under `electrical-contractors` at 53; engineering standards publishers (AISC, ACI) and BIM object content libraries were logged under `engineering-consultants`; design professional liability insurers under `engineering-consultants` and third-party plan review under `architecture-firms`. ## Why These Pockets

No pocket qualifies, and that is the finding. Drafting is a production industry from end to end: the operators sell sheets, the aggregators sell sheets at scale, and the suppliers sell the software that makes sheets. Nobody in the chain is paid for analysis of drafting itself. The operators fail the gate as expected — the US bureau averages three people, and the offshore enterprises with thousands of staff have no team whose output is insight. The positions that usually yield qualifiers in other industries are empty here: there is no data vendor selling drafting benchmarks, the associations run on volunteers, and the analysts who rate outsourcing providers give AEC drafting a handful of people.

What the sweep does find is where the outcome data sits. The fabricators and their detailing coordinators (pockets 10 and 15), the joist and metal-building manufacturers (14) and the general contractors' VDC teams (11) hold exactly the record the industry lacks: which drawings, from which detailer, failed at review, in the shop or in the field. All of them use it internally and none invoices it, so they score low on the first question, and the fabricators' and manufacturers' in-house detailing departments fail the gate outright: their headcount is production, not analysis, the same test that fails the offshore enterprises. That matters for the Pass 1 opportunities more than for Pass 2: a learned checker or a vendor scorecard has to be sold to the buyer of detailing, not to the bureau.

The strongest near-miss is delegated steel connection design engineering (43). The sealed calculation package is what the fabricator pays for, it repeats at volume, and the AISC approval window sets the clock. It falls short because each connection depends on forces supplied by the engineer of record, so the archive is less reusable than it looks, and because connection automation inside the detailing packages is absorbing the routine share. Scan-to-BIM providers have real modelling volume but no analytical function, so they fail the gate, and the point clouds and models belong to the building owner in any case.

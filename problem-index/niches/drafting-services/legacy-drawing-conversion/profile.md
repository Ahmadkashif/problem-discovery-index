# Legacy Drawing Conversion & As-Built Documentation

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/drafting-services|Drafting Services]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn paper archives, scanned blueprints and field measurements into accurate, layered CAD that the owner's facilities team can maintain, and whoever proves dimensional fidelity without a return site visit takes the facility portfolio.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$300M a year in paper-to-CAD conversion, raster-to-vector work and field-verified as-built drawings for building, campus and utility owners (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~6% (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — the inputs are paper blueprints, aperture cards, faded scans and contractors' marked-up record sets
**Target Buyer:** Facilities director or campus space planner; utility or plant records manager
**Automation Potential:** High for vectorising and text recognition; low for reconciling drawings to what was built

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Owners of hospitals, universities, government buildings, plants and utilities hold decades of drawings on paper and as raster scans. They need maintainable CAD for space management, renovation planning and emergency plans, and they buy conversion by the sheet. Accuracy is the contest: a vectorised drawing that faithfully reproduces a 1970s blueprint can still be wrong, because the building was changed and the contractor's record set never updated. Some work is pure conversion; some adds field verification, which shades into laser scanning (see [[industries/land-surveyors|Land Surveyors]] for survey-grade capture). Bureaus win on knowing which dimensions to trust.

Laser-scan capture of existing conditions is the adjacent business, logged as the [[niches/drafting-services/scan-to-bim-existing-conditions/profile|Scan-to-BIM & Existing-Conditions Modelling Providers]] pocket; this niche starts from the drawings the owner already has.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Scan2CAD, WinTopo and similar raster-to-vector converters; AutoCAD tracing over scans; OCR for title blocks; laser measures and point clouds for verification. The gaps: converters vectorise lines without understanding walls, doors or dimensions; no confidence scoring tells the owner which parts need field verification; and record drawings are taken at face value.

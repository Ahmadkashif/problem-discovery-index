# Fabrication Content Adapted to the Contractor's Shop

**Niche:** [[niches/drafting-services/mep-fabrication-modelling/profile|MEP Fabrication & Spool Modelling]]
**Industry:** [[industries/drafting-services|Drafting Services]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Fabrication modelling only pays off if the model's fittings are the shop's real fittings, and every contractor's content library differs.
**Tags:** #k-nearest-neighbors #decision-trees #data-integration #automation #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn the coordinated MEP model into spool, duct and hanger packages that the prefabrication shop can build and the installer can hang without field modification, and whoever cuts model-to-spool hours while holding first-time fit takes the mechanical contractor.

## The Problem
A bureau modelling for several mechanical contractors must use each contractor's fabrication database: their fittings, connectors, gauges, seams and costs. Setting up and maintaining that content per client is slow, and modelling with generic content produces spools the shop cannot build as drawn.

## What Already Exists
Autodesk Fabrication databases and Revit fabrication parts; manufacturer content; vendor-supplied starter libraries.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs: building a client's content profile from its past jobs and shop standards; mapping generic parts to the client's equivalents automatically; flagging parts in a model that the client's shop does not make; and keeping per-client libraries in step as the shop changes.

## Target Customer
MEP modelling bureaus serving multiple contractors; contractors onboarding a new bureau.

## Impact If Solved
Faster client onboarding and models that build as drawn in each contractor's shop.

# Outsourced Construction Document Production

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/drafting-services|Drafting Services]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is "every serious competitor is fighting to produce an architect's or engineer's construction documents for less than the firm's own staff cost", but it yields two contests with different winners: one-off per-sheet overflow sets, won on turnaround and clarification rounds, and dedicated offshore production teams, won on retaining a trained team inside one firm's standards. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.4B a year in outsourced architectural and engineering drawing production for US design firms, onshore and offshore (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~28% (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** High — the work arrives as Revit models, DWGs and marked-up PDFs and leaves the same way
**Target Buyer:** Principal or operations director at an architecture or engineering firm; BIM manager
**Automation Potential:** High — sheet setup, annotation, schedules and redline pickup are the most mechanical part of the design fee

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Architecture and engineering firms spend the largest share of their fee on construction documents, and much of that is production: setting up sheets, annotating, building schedules and picking up the principal's redlines (see [[niches/architecture-firms/cd-production-sheet-coordination/profile|Construction Document Production & Sheet Coordination]] for the in-firm side). Bureaus sell that production to the firm. The design intent and the stamp stay with the architect or engineer; the bureau delivers sheets. It is the niche most directly exposed to AI documentation tools such as SWAPP, which target the same hours.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Revit, AutoCAD and Archicad on both sides; Bluebeam for markups; shared drives, Autodesk Construction Cloud or email for exchange; project accounting in Deltek, BQE CORE or Monograph on the firm side and spreadsheets on the bureau side. The gaps: no way for the firm to compare outsourced and in-house cost per sheet including its own review time; markups transmitted as PDFs with no task structure; and no measured first-pass acceptance rate.

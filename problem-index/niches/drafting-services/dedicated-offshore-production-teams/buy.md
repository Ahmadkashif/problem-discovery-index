# Software Defect Tracking Adapted to Drawing Review

**Niche:** [[niches/drafting-services/dedicated-offshore-production-teams/profile|Dedicated Offshore Production Teams]]
**Industry:** [[industries/drafting-services|Drafting Services]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Offshore software teams and their clients share a defect tracker and agreed quality metrics; offshore drafting teams and their clients share a folder of marked-up PDFs.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #workflow-orchestration #data-integration #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep a stable, trained offshore team working inside one firm's standards and templates as if it sat in the office, and whoever holds drafter retention and first-pass acceptance highest takes the multi-year contract.

## The Problem
In a dedicated-team contract, quality is argued from impressions. The client feels the team is slipping; the vendor points to volume. There is no shared record of defects by type, sheet, drafter or project, so neither side can show a trend.

## What Already Exists
Jira, Azure DevOps and Linear give software teams defect tracking with types, severity, ownership and cycle-time metrics; offshore IT contracts commonly include defect-rate service levels.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs: a defect as a markup on a sheet, linked to the sheet and model element; a drawing-specific defect taxonomy (standards, coordination, missing information, misread markup, drafting error); automatic capture from the client's Bluebeam markups; and service-level reports on first-pass acceptance and defects per sheet type.

## Target Customer
Offshore enterprises running dedicated teams, and the design firms that contract them.

## Impact If Solved
A shared, objective quality record turns contract renewals from impressions into data and shows the vendor where to train.

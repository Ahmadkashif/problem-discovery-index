# Client Memory That Survives Drafter Turnover

**Niche:** [[niches/drafting-services/dedicated-offshore-production-teams/profile|Dedicated Offshore Production Teams]]
**Industry:** [[industries/drafting-services|Drafting Services]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** What a dedicated team learns about one client — its standards, its project architects' habits, its recurring corrections — lives in the drafters' heads and leaves when they do.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #word-embeddings #k-nearest-neighbors #random-forests #tacit-knowledge-ml #workflow-orchestration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep a stable, trained offshore team working inside one firm's standards and templates as if it sat in the office, and whoever holds drafter retention and first-pass acceptance highest takes the multi-year contract.

## The Problem
After a year on one client, a good offshore drafter knows that this project architect wants door tags in a certain style, that this engineer always forgets to update the section when the plan changes, and that "typical" on this firm's details means something specific. None of it is written down. When the drafter leaves, which happens often in competitive offshore labour markets, the replacement relearns it through rejected sheets.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The knowledge is accumulated in markups, emails and the corrections clients make, all unstructured. Vendors treat retention as an HR problem rather than a knowledge problem.

## What to Build
A per-client memory built automatically from every markup, correction and rejection the team receives: recurring correction types, standard conventions learned from accepted sheets, and reviewer-specific preferences. It surfaces relevant precedents to whoever is drawing ("this reviewer has corrected this three times") and checks outgoing sheets against the client's learned habits.

## Target Customer
Delivery heads at offshore drafting and BIM enterprises; BIM directors at client firms who want the know-how to survive vendor changes too.

## Impact If Built
New team members reach the client's first-pass acceptance rate in weeks instead of months, and the vendor's value stops depending on keeping particular people.

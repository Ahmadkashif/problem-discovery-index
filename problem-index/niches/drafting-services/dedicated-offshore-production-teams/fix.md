# Attrition That Resets the Learning Curve

**Niche:** [[niches/drafting-services/dedicated-offshore-production-teams/profile|Dedicated Offshore Production Teams]]
**Industry:** [[industries/drafting-services|Drafting Services]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Every time a trained drafter on a dedicated team leaves, the client's rejection rate jumps for months, and nobody plans for it.
**Tags:** #survival-analysis #logistic-regression #descriptive-statistics #worker-facing #workflow-orchestration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep a stable, trained offshore team working inside one firm's standards and templates as if it sat in the office, and whoever holds drafter retention and first-pass acceptance highest takes the multi-year contract.

## The Problem
Dedicated-team contracts sell continuity, but offshore drafting labour turns over constantly, to competitors, to in-house roles at multinationals, or abroad. Each departure costs weeks of overlap that is rarely billed and months of reduced first-pass acceptance while the replacement learns the client.

## Why It's Still Broken
Departures are handled one at a time as staffing events. Nobody measures the acceptance dip after a change or predicts who is likely to leave, and handover is a few days of shadowing.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Track first-pass acceptance per team member and per client before and after every change; flag retention risk from workload, night-shift tenure and pay progression; and require structured handover from the client memory and defect record rather than shadowing alone.

## Who Feels the Pain
Delivery managers explaining a quality drop; client BIM managers reviewing more; the replacement drafter learning through rejections.

## Impact If Fixed
Shorter, planned learning curves after each departure, and data that shows clients what continuity is worth.

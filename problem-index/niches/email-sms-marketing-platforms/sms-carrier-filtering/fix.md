# The Registration Filled In by Whoever Was Available

**Niche:** [[niches/email-sms-marketing-platforms/sms-carrier-filtering/profile|SMS Carrier Filtering]]
**Industry:** [[industries/email-sms-marketing-platforms|Email & SMS Marketing Platforms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The campaign registration was completed as onboarding paperwork by someone who guessed the use case, and it silently determines the brand's throughput and filtering for years.
**Tags:** #compliance #workflow-orchestration #evaluation-metrics #automation #quick-win #descriptive-statistics #confidence-intervals #data-integration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to decode why carriers filter what they filter and navigate the registration regime that gates every brand — and whoever does that turns an opaque gatekeeper relationship into something a brand can manage.

## The Problem
Before a brand can send, it registers: the business, the campaign, the use case, sample content, the opt-in flow. This is presented as onboarding paperwork and is usually completed by whoever was setting up the account, with a use case chosen from a dropdown and sample messages written in a hurry. That registration then determines the brand's throughput allocation and how aggressively carriers filter its traffic, indefinitely. Eighteen months later a brand with persistent filtering problems discovers that their classification was wrong from the start and nobody has looked at it since.

## Why It's Still Broken
Registration is framed as a compliance formality rather than as a performance setting, so it receives the attention a form receives — the framing determines the care, and the framing is wrong. The consequences are delayed and opaque, so the causal link back to the form is never made. Nobody reviews registrations after onboarding. And platforms treat it as a step to get through rather than as something to optimise.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Treat registration as a performance configuration. Explain what each choice determines at the point it is made, which is the fix and costs one sentence per field — most people filling these in have no idea the dropdown sets their throughput. Recommend the classification from what the brand actually does, using their catalogue, their site and their message history, rather than asking someone to self-select. Review registrations against observed sending behaviour, since a brand whose real traffic does not match its declared use case will be filtered and currently never finds out why. Flag mismatches proactively and support re-registration, which is the remedy and is rarely initiated. Provide sample content that fits the classification, since poorly written samples are a common and entirely avoidable cause of restrictive treatment. Verify the opt-in flow rather than accepting an assertion, which also serves the consent work and is what carriers are actually trying to establish. Monitor throughput allocation against need, because a brand hitting an allocation ceiling sees it as filtering and it is a provisioning problem with a straightforward fix. Keep registration current as a brand's programme evolves, since use cases change and the registration does not. Report the registration's effect on delivery explicitly, which makes the link visible for the first time. And escalate to carriers with evidence when a classification is wrong, which a platform can do and a brand cannot.

## Who Feels the Pain
Brands filtered for years because of a dropdown; platforms fielding deliverability complaints caused by their own onboarding; and carriers receiving traffic that does not match its declared purpose.

## Impact If Fixed
Registration is framed as compliance paperwork and is actually a performance setting, so it gets the care a form gets. Explaining what each field determines and recommending the classification from observed behaviour removes a cause of filtering that persists for years.

# Placement & Deliverability

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/email-sms-marketing-platforms|Email & SMS Marketing Platforms]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** This niche is not terminal — inferring where an email landed and decoding why a carrier dropped a text are different contests with different winners, and they are stated separately in the sub-niches below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$2.2B US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~24% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Low — delivered is not placed
**Target Buyer:** Platform engineering and deliverability leadership
**Automation Potential:** Very High — both halves are inference problems

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Delivery is not placement. A message accepted by a mailbox provider may land in the inbox, in promotions, in spam, or be silently dropped; the provider does not say which, and the platform reports delivered for all of them. Carriers filter text messages on opaque criteria and return an error code that means several different things. Whether the channel works at all is decided here, and it is the one thing the platforms cannot observe, which makes it the largest concentration of contested value in the category.

It is also **not terminal**. The label spans two channels whose contests share an outcome and nothing else. Email placement is an inference problem against mailbox providers who publish partial signals — authentication results, complaint feedback, engagement patterns — where the fight is reconstructing where a message landed, and where a platform's cross-brand corpus makes tractable what is impossible for one sender. Carrier filtering is a different problem: a handful of carriers, undisclosed criteria, error codes that conflate outcomes, and a registration regime with no email equivalent. The techniques, the counterparties and the expertise barely overlap. The two contests are stated in the sub-niches.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Delivery and bounce reporting, authentication configuration, seed list testing, reputation monitoring services, and carrier error codes. The gaps: delivered reported for every outcome; no placement inference; seed panels unrepresentative of the real audience; carrier codes conflating distinct failures; and no cross-brand view of provider or carrier behaviour.

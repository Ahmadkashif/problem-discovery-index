# The Ninety-Nine Percent Delivery Rate

**Niche:** [[niches/email-sms-marketing-platforms/placement-and-deliverability/profile|Placement & Deliverability]]
**Industry:** [[industries/email-sms-marketing-platforms|Email & SMS Marketing Platforms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Every dashboard in the category reports a delivery rate near a hundred percent, which counts messages the provider accepted and then put somewhere nobody will look.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #quick-win #revenue-impact #hypothesis-testing #compliance #change-point-detection

## The Problem
The delivery rate is the reassuring number at the top of every campaign report and it is almost always above ninety-eight percent. It measures acceptance by the receiving system, which is a technical handshake, and says nothing about whether a human will ever see the message. A brand in serious placement trouble — landing in spam for a large share of its audience — sees the same ninety-nine percent as a brand performing perfectly. The metric is not false; it answers a question about transport that the industry has allowed to stand in for a question about outcome, and the substitution is invisible to almost every user of these platforms.

## Why It's Still Broken
Acceptance is measurable and placement is not, so the measurable thing became the metric and then became the expectation — the same substitution that produced the open rate problem next door. A high number reassures customers and reflects well on the platform. Reporting anything lower without a competitor doing the same is commercially punishing. And customers have learned to read it as a health indicator.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Stop letting transport stand in for outcome. Rename the metric to what it measures — accepted rather than delivered — which is the fix, is a one-word change, and immediately stops it being read as a health indicator. Report an estimated placement figure alongside, with its uncertainty, so the honest question has a number even while it is imprecise. Show engagement distribution by provider, since a provider whose engagement has collapsed relative to others is the clearest available placement signal and is computable today from data every platform holds. Alert on divergence between providers, because a placement problem is usually provider-specific and an aggregate rate conceals it entirely. Surface complaint rates against the thresholds that now gate delivery, which are enforceable conditions rather than guidance. Separate the outcomes currently collapsed into delivered — accepted, deferred, dropped, filtered — as far as the responses permit. Educate customers on what the number means, since the misreading is the mechanism and most users have never been told. Make placement rather than acceptance the default headline, because whichever number is at the top is the one that gets managed. Adopt consistent definitions across the industry, as incomparable metrics are what allowed this to persist. And report the estimated revenue effect of placement loss, which is what makes a brand act rather than nod.

## Who Feels the Pain
Brands reassured by a number that cannot detect their worst failure; deliverability specialists whose warnings contradict the dashboard; and the channel, whose health metric measures a handshake.

## Impact If Fixed
Acceptance is measurable and placement is not, so transport stands in for outcome and a brand in serious trouble sees the same ninety-nine percent. Renaming the metric and showing engagement divergence by provider uses data already held and stops the misreading.

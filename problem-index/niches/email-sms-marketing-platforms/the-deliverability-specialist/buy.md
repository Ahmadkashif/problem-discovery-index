## The Problem
Turning scarce expert judgement into something a non-expert can apply is a solved organisational problem in several fields. Clinical decision support, aviation checklists, diagnostic protocols and structured troubleshooting trees all exist because expertise held individually is unavailable when and where it is needed, and because experts themselves make fewer errors with a checklist. The methods for eliciting, structuring and validating expert knowledge are documented. Deliverability expertise is transmitted through conference talks and forum threads.

## What Already Exists
Knowledge elicitation methodology; clinical decision support and diagnostic protocols; checklist design and validation practice; case-based reasoning systems; and structured troubleshooting with escalation.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a domain whose rules are set by adversarial counterparties and change without notice. It requires: (1) a knowledge base that decays, since a provider's behaviour change invalidates guidance and a static protocol is wrong within months — continuous revalidation against outcomes is the central requirement and is what clinical protocols achieve through review cycles far too slow for this; (2) ground truth that is unavailable, so diagnosis is validated by whether remediation worked rather than by a confirmed cause; (3) evidence drawn from a cross-sender corpus rather than from published literature, which is a different and richer basis than the field usually has; (4) a user who is a marketer under commercial pressure rather than a trained professional, changing the interface and the tolerance for ambiguity; and (5) experts whose commercial position depends partly on the scarcity, which makes elicitation a political exercise as well as a methodological one.

## Target Customer
Messaging platforms, deliverability service providers, and knowledge system vendors for whom fast-decaying adversarial domains are unserved.

## Impact If Solved
Every field with scarce expert judgement codified it, and experts themselves make fewer errors with a checklist. A knowledge base that decays within months needs continuous revalidation against outcomes, which is the requirement clinical review cycles are far too slow to meet.

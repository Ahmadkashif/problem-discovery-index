# Static Posting & Structure Maintenance

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/outdoor-advertising-companies|Outdoor Advertising Companies]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to post every contracted face by its start date with proof the buyer accepts, because each late or wrong posting becomes a makegood that wipes out that face's margin for the period, and whoever posts on time with zero rejected proofs keeps the renewal.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$700M a year in crew labour, vinyl and paper handling, lighting power and structure repair (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~8% of industry receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — printed or emailed route sheets, phone photos uploaded at day's end, maintenance logged when someone remembers
**Target Buyer:** Director of Operations / market operations manager
**Automation Potential:** Medium — routing and proof verification automate fully; climbing and installing never will

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Most faces are still static: Lamar's ~5,500 digital billboards sit among ~159,300. Every static contract needs a vinyl or paper installed by its start date and photographed as proof, and start dates bunch at the beginning of each sales period. The same crews climb the structures, so they are the only people who regularly see catwalks, ladders, lamps and steel up close. A late posting is a makegood. A missed structural defect is a fall, like the 30-foot catwalk fall NIOSH investigated in Oklahoma, or a structure lost in a storm along with its nonconforming permit.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Posting instructions and proof modules in Apparatix and BillboardPlanet; internal work-order systems at the large operators; generic field service apps; spreadsheets. Gaps: routes that ignore weather and vinyl arrival; proof photos checked by eye days later; structure condition never captured systematically; crew hazard knowledge not passed on.

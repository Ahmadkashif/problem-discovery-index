## The Problem
Widening and interchange projects appear in state and metropolitan transportation improvement programmes years before acquisition. When a structure is taken, its compensation (lease, structure and permit together under the valuation approaches the OAAA's condemnation brief describes) and any relocation right depend on preparation: comparable sales, income evidence, a relocation site identified in advance. Operators that find out late negotiate from a weak position. Right-of-way acquisition firms are profiled at [[niches/land-surveyors/right-of-way-acquisition-services/profile|Right-of-Way & Land Acquisition Services]].

## Why It's Still Broken
Project lists are published as PDFs and spreadsheets by many agencies; nobody matches them to structure locations as a routine.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Automatic matching of published project limits to structure coordinates, with alerts when a structure falls within a project corridor, followed by a standard preparation pack: valuation evidence, relocation options under the local code, and the state's relocation rules.

## Who Feels the Pain
Real estate managers and counsel handling condemnation; operators who lose high-value structures for less than they were worth.

## Impact If Fixed
Years of warning instead of weeks, turning condemnation from a loss into a negotiated relocation where codes allow it.

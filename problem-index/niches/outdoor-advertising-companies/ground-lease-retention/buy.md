# The Cell Tower Ground Lease Playbook Applied to Billboards

**Niche:** [[niches/outdoor-advertising-companies/ground-lease-retention/profile|Ground Lease Retention]]
**Industry:** [[industries/outdoor-advertising-companies|Outdoor Advertising Companies]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Tower companies built ground-purchase programmes and analytics when aggregators started buying the land under their towers; billboard operators face the same buyers with smaller, more numerous leases.
**Tags:** #survival-analysis #gradient-boosting #data-integration #workflow-orchestration #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep each ground lease at a rent the face can carry before a lease aggregator or a rival operator reaches the landowner, and whoever knows each site's true value and renewal risk first keeps the location.

## The Problem
Aggregators that buy ground leases under cell towers also buy them under billboards, and many offer landowners the same lump-sum pitch. Billboard operators respond case by case.

## What Already Exists
Tower operators run ground-lease purchase and extension programmes with portfolio analytics that prioritise sites by lease cost, term remaining and strategic value, and track aggregator activity by market.

## The Customization Gap
Billboard leases are an order of magnitude more numerous and individually smaller, often rural and month-to-month; rent is frequently a percentage of face revenue; site value depends on the permit and the face's sales, not on carrier tenancy; and the purchase alternative is often a permanent easement rather than fee land. The prioritisation model and the purchase economics must be rebuilt around face revenue and permit status, at a cost per site that a ~$3,000-a-year poster lease (estimate) can bear.

## Target Customer
Real estate and corporate development teams at large operators.

## Impact If Solved
A tested defensive programme adapted rather than invented, with capital going first to the leases that carry the most face revenue.

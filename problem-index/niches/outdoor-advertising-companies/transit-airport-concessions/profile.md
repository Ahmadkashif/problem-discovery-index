# Transit, Airport & Street Furniture Concessions

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/outdoor-advertising-companies|Outdoor Advertising Companies]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to bid a minimum annual guarantee and revenue share that the concession's advertising will actually cover over a seven-to-ten-year term, and whoever forecasts concession revenue most honestly wins contracts without the write-downs that follow an overbid.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.2B a year in concession fees and revenue shares paid to transit agencies, airports and cities (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~13% of US out-of-home revenue paid onward to concession grantors (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium-High — stations and terminals are full of screens, but bids are modelled in spreadsheets
**Target Buyer:** SVP of Transit and Airports / CFO
**Automation Potential:** Medium — revenue scenario modelling and deployment tracking automate; the bid strategy stays a judgement

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Here the operator doesn't own land or permits. It rents the right to sell advertising on a transit system, in an airport or on a city's bus shelters, typically for the greater of a minimum annual guarantee or a percentage of gross. Phoenix Sky Harbor's 2026 solicitation sets at least 65% of gross or a $5M guarantee; Anchorage's new term runs seven years to 2033. The risk is one-sided: the guarantee is owed whether the ads sell or not. Outfront took $534.7M of impairment charges in 2023 on its MTA asset group and transit unit, and still owed $1,396.7M in guaranteed payments to the MTA at the end of 2024. Transit was the fastest-growing format in 2025 (up 9.2%), which keeps these contracts attractive to bidders despite that history.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Bid models are spreadsheets built on the authority's ridership or enplanement forecast and the operator's sales history in comparable systems; Geopath place-based measurement and the authority's own counts feed the audience case. The gaps: no scenario model joining ridership recovery, advertiser demand, screen deployment cost and the guarantee into a probability of shortfall, and no learning across concessions about how bids performed.

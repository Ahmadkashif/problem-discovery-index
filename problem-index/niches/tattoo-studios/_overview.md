## Why These Niches

Custom design and walk-in flash represent the two dominant revenue models in tattooing — together they account for roughly 70% of studio revenue — making them essential high-market-share segments. Cover-up work and cosmetic/medical tattooing are growing specializations that remain almost entirely paper-based or Instagram-DM-based, with zero purpose-built digital tools. Culturally-specific artists and rural studios are systematically underserved by platforms designed around urban, Western tattoo culture. Appointment management and consent/aftercare compliance were selected as automation targets because no-show rates run 15-25% and health department paperwork is still largely paper forms, both problems with clear rule-based solutions.

## Why These Pockets

No qualifiers, and five of twelve pockets fail the gate outright — the highest gate-failure rate of any industry in this sweep. This is the clearest case in the whole map of an industry too small to grow an insight layer. At roughly a billion and a half dollars, spread across studios of one to three artists, there is not enough money above the operator layer to fund anyone whose job is analysis.

The only pocket clearing 50 is borrowed: tattoo ink is regulated as a cosmetic, so the cosmetic safety substantiation consultancies already indexed under hair salons cover it. The industry-specific version of that work — reformulating inks around pigment restrictions — is real, carries a hard statutory clock that removed products from the market on a fixed day, and serves a global supplier base of a few dozen manufacturers.

Two absences are worth recording because they are questions the industry genuinely cannot answer about itself. Nobody knows what happens to a tattoo over decades: how pigments migrate, fade and degrade in skin is the central technical unknown, and no manufacturer, regulator or clinic holds a dataset on it. And nobody knows what actually clears in removal — which inks, in how many sessions, at what cost — despite the removal clinic chains holding session-by-session outcome records, because those chains run analytics functions of a handful of people and the data sits inside a HIPAA perimeter.

The safety picture has the same shape. Infection complaints and inspection findings are investigated county by county and never pooled, so the sector has no national record of its own safety performance, and the liability underwriters who hold the only cross-studio claims data run programmes too small to fund looking at it.

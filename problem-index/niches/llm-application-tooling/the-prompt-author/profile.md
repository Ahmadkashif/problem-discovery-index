# The Prompt Author

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/llm-application-tooling|LLM Application Tooling]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to let the person who knows what the application should say change what it says, safely, without an engineer — and whoever does that takes the account, because that person is the bottleneck on every content change.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$130M US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~7% of category revenue equivalent
**Digital Adoption:** None — owns the content, cannot ship it
**Target Buyer:** Domain specialists who own what the application says
**Automation Potential:** Medium — the tooling is buildable, the judgement is theirs

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The person who knows what a support assistant should say about a refund policy is a support lead, not an engineer. The person who knows how a clinical assistant should hedge is a clinician. The person who knows what a legal assistant must never assert is a lawyer. They own the content of the prompt and can neither see it in a form they can read nor change it without an engineer's sprint capacity. The result is that the domain knowledge reaches the application through a game of telephone, changes take weeks, and the specialist reviews behaviour by using the application and guessing at what to ask for. They are the source of the application's actual quality and are served by nothing.

## Current Tools & Gaps
An engineer, a ticket, and occasionally a prompt registry the specialist has no access to. The gaps: no interface that presents the prompt as editable content rather than as code; no way for a specialist to test a change before requesting it; no review workflow in terms they understand; no visibility into what the application currently does in their domain; and no record connecting a behaviour to the instruction that produced it.

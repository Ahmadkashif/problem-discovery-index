## The Problem
The problem of letting a non-engineer change product-facing content safely is the founding problem of content management, and the answers are thoroughly worked out: structured content types, drafts, preview, editorial review workflow, scheduled publishing, versioning, rollback and field-level permissions. Marketing teams have edited production websites for twenty-five years without engineering involvement. Prompt content is edited by filing a ticket.

## What Already Exists
Headless content management with structured content types and field-level permissions; draft, preview and publish workflows; editorial review and approval chains; scheduled publishing and rollback; localisation and variant management; and audit trails of who changed what.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to content whose effect is probabilistic and indirect. It requires: (1) preview that shows behaviour rather than rendering, since previewing a prompt means running it against representative inputs and comparing — this substitution is the central adaptation and the one that makes the whole content management model fit; (2) review that includes an automated quality check, because a reviewer reading a prompt change cannot tell what it will do and a regression report can; (3) structured prompt content types — policy, tone, example, constraint — so permissions and review can be scoped to the part that matters; (4) variant management for different segments and locales, which content management handles well and prompt registries handle by duplication; and (5) rollback measured in behaviour rather than in content, since reverting the text restores the previous behaviour only approximately when the model underneath has moved.

## Target Customer
Domain specialists, the platform teams who would grant them access, prompt tooling vendors, and the content management vendors for whom this is an adjacent content type.

## Impact If Solved
Content management solved safe non-engineer editing decades ago and this category files tickets. Preview that runs the change against representative inputs — behaviour rather than rendering — is the substitution that makes the entire model transfer.

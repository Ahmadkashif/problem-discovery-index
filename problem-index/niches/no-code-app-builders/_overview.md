## Why These Niches

The category's defining failure is the crossing from convenience to dependency, which happens to every successful app and is visible to nobody until the builder leaves. Detecting it is the largest contested surface and nothing in the market attempts it. Integration is the other large block and **failed the filter as one niche**: connecting to a bespoke internal API is a discovery-and-generation problem bought by a builder in the moment, while keeping thousands of existing connectors working as upstream APIs change is a reliability problem bought by a platform team. Decomposed below.

The underdigitised areas are where the category's promise has not landed. IT cannot inventory what exists, because the whole premise was that people build without asking — which makes discovery a network-and-billing problem rather than a console feature. And an enormous volume of business process still runs on spreadsheets and email that no-code was supposed to absorb and has not, because migration means a rebuild and nobody has time for a rebuild.

The two underserved constituencies sit on either side of the handover. The person who built the useful thing has acquired an unpaid second job supporting software, which nobody acknowledges and no product addresses. The IT administrator inherits applications they did not build, cannot read, and cannot safely change or switch off.

The automation niches are the newest problem and the oldest asset. AI-assisted building has accelerated creation without touching ownership, which sharpens every problem above. And the platforms hold a structured, executable description of thousands of real business processes — the corpus the industry's own Analysis note identifies — and use it to render interfaces.

## Filter Notes

Seven of the eight level-1 niches are terminal. **Integration & Connectors** is not: it names a component of the product rather than a contest, and the two contests inside it have different buyers and different definitions of winning. Bespoke API connectivity is won by whoever can get a builder connected to an internal system nobody has written a connector for, in the moment, without them hand-rolling authentication — a generation and discovery problem, bought by the builder. Connector reliability is won by whoever keeps thousands of existing connectors working as the upstream APIs change underneath them — a drift-detection and maintenance problem, bought by a platform team after the third silent breakage. Decomposed into two contested sub-niches.

Two candidates were rejected. *Template and component marketplaces* was rejected because its contest is the same one already documented in work collaboration and e-signature — templates authored rather than derived — and adding a third instance would repeat rather than extend it. *Pricing and consumption governance* was folded into Shadow App Inventory, since the billing signal is the discovery mechanism rather than a contest of its own.

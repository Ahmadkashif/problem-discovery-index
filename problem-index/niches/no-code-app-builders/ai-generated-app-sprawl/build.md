**Contested on:** Every serious competitor here is fighting to make a generated application ownable — attributable, reviewable and maintainable — at the rate generation now produces them, and whoever does that takes the enterprise account, because generation without ownership is a governance problem arriving faster than any governance process.

## The Problem
A team lead describes a vendor onboarding process and receives a working app: six tables, a form, three automations and a public submission link. It is good enough to use. They use it. They could not tell you what the third automation does, what the sharing setting on the form is, or which fields collect personal data, because they did not make those decisions — they described an outcome and accepted a result. Eleven more people in the company do the same thing that month. The organisation's application estate grew by twelve, its understanding of that estate grew by nothing, and its governance capacity is unchanged.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Generation is the competitive feature of the moment and every vendor is shipping it at speed, where ownership infrastructure is invisible in a demonstration and slows the flow the feature is selling. Provenance is not retained because nobody designed for the question being asked later. And the governance problem is genuinely new: existing controls assume a human made each decision deliberately, so a review process that asks whether the builder considered the sharing setting is meaningless when the builder did not set it.

## What to Build
Ownership infrastructure that keeps pace with generation. Retain provenance completely — the prompt, the model, the version, what was generated and what the person subsequently changed — as first-class metadata, which costs nothing at generation time and is unrecoverable afterwards. Review generated output against policy before it runs, not after: sharing defaults, external exposure, data field classification, outbound automations, and any credential usage, checked automatically and either corrected or surfaced to the prompter in plain language. Establish comprehension deliberately, by explaining back what was built — in particular the parts the person did not ask for and would not expect — since the shallow understanding is the root of the downstream inheritance problem and a thirty-second explanation at creation is the cheapest possible intervention. Track the abandoned majority, since generation encourages producing several and keeping one, and the rest are indistinguishable from live apps without usage data. And report generation volume against governance capacity, which is the number that tells an organisation whether it is falling behind and which nobody currently produces.

## Target Customer
Platform governance and IT leadership at organisations adopting generation, security and compliance functions, and the platform vendors whose enterprise sale now requires an answer to this.

## Impact If Built
Generation multiplies every existing problem in the category while introducing a new one — a builder who does not know what they built. Provenance and pre-run policy review both cost almost nothing at the moment of generation and are impossible to reconstruct later.

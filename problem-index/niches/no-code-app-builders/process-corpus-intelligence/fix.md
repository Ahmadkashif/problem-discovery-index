# Templates Written From Imagination

**Niche:** [[niches/no-code-app-builders/process-corpus-intelligence/profile|Process Corpus Intelligence]]
**Industry:** [[industries/no-code-app-builders|No-Code App Builders]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Template galleries are authored by content teams describing how they imagine a process works, while thousands of observed implementations of that exact process sit in the same product.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #k-means-clustering #survival-analysis #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #hypothesis-testing #quick-win #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor that gets here is fighting to turn a corpus of thousands of executable business processes into a product, and whoever does it holds a position no competitor can replicate, because the corpus was created by customers and exists nowhere else.

## The Problem
A content team publishes a supplier onboarding template with nine stages and twelve fields, derived from research and judgement. Customers adopt it, delete four stages, add two, and ignore half the fields. The platform observes every one of those modifications and learns nothing from any of them. The next template is authored the same way, and the abandonment rate is unmeasured because the gallery reports adoptions.

## Why It's Still Broken
Templates are a content function whose craft is writing, and deriving them from observation is an analytical activity nobody in that function is staffed for. Survival and modification are not measured, so the current templates appear to be working. And the cross-customer analysis needed to do better has not been governance-cleared, because nobody has asked — the question is avoided rather than declined.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Measure and then derive, in that order, since the measurement is cheap and makes the case for the rest. Template survival: what proportion of adopters still run a recognisably similar structure after one, three and twelve months, which is computable from structural snapshots the platform already stores. Modification aggregate: what adopters add and remove, which is simultaneously the quality signal and the specific instruction for the next version — a field ninety percent of adopters delete does not belong in the template. Rank the gallery by survival rather than adoption, which changes what gets promoted and therefore what gets adopted. Start templates minimal, since the consistent observed pattern is that people strip them down. Then derive new templates from the clusters in the corpus rather than authoring them, specialised to the adopting company's size and industry, which is the endpoint and is justified by the measurement that precedes it.

## Who Feels the Pain
Teams who adopted a structure that did not fit and spent a fortnight discovering it; content teams optimising against an adoption number that measures a thumbnail; and vendors whose main onboarding mechanism has an unmeasured failure rate.

## Impact If Fixed
Survival and modification are queries over structural history and would immediately reorder the gallery by something meaningful. The modification aggregate converts near-universal template abandonment into a specific instruction about what each template should have contained.

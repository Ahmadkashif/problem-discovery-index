# Thousands of Business Processes, Never Read

**Industry:** [[industries/no-code-app-builders|No-Code App Builders]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** These platforms hold a structured, executable description of how thousands of real companies actually run their processes, built by the people who do the work, and use it to render interfaces.
**Tags:** #k-means-clustering #graph-theory #dbscan #bert #dimensionality-reduction #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #transfer-learning
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor that gets here is fighting to turn a corpus of thousands of executable business processes into a product, and whoever does it holds a position no competitor can replicate, because the corpus was created by customers and exists nowhere else.

## The Problem
A platform vendor wants to know how companies handle supplier onboarding so it can improve its template. It commissions research: a dozen customer interviews and a review of published material, producing a generalised model. Inside the platform are eleven thousand supplier onboarding apps in active use, each a precise executable specification of how one company does it, with their fields, their approval sequences, their exception paths and their integrations, plus usage data showing which ones are still running two years later. Nobody has looked at any of them, at any vendor.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Customer data is treated as untouchable in the absence of a considered position on what analysis is acceptable, and no vendor has done the work of articulating one — so the question is avoided rather than answered, even though structural analysis that never touches content discloses nothing about any customer. The analysis also requires a structural representation and clustering across heterogeneous apps, which is a genuine data science project rather than a feature. And nobody has framed the corpus as an asset, because it accumulated as a side effect of the product rather than as a deliberate collection.

## What to Build
The corpus as a dataset with products on top. Represent every app structurally — entities, fields and types, relationships, state sequences, automation topology, external connections — with no content, which is what makes the analysis both privacy-preserving and legitimate, and a position a vendor should be able to state publicly. Classify apps into process categories, then cluster within each, which reveals the genuinely distinct variants of a process rather than the one idealised version a content team imagines. Attach an outcome proxy — still in use after a year, work items completing rather than accumulating, low error rates — so the clusters carry evidence about which designs work. From that: derived templates specialised to the adopting company's size and industry, which is the direct commercial product; benchmarking, telling a customer what comparable organisations' processes include that theirs does not, which is a genuinely valuable and genuinely novel service; and product discovery, since a process implemented independently by four hundred companies in the same way is a software category that does not exist yet, and this corpus is the only place that signal appears. Governance is a first-class part of the design: structure only, minimum cohort sizes before any pattern is published, and an explicit customer-facing statement of what is and is not analysed.

## Target Customer
The platform vendors themselves, primarily; secondarily the process consultancies and industry analysts who currently produce this understanding from interviews.

## Impact If Built
The corpus is unique, large, structured, and untouched — a combination that appears rarely in this vault and never with a cleaner privacy story, since structure alone answers the question. Benchmarking and product discovery are outputs nobody else in the economy is positioned to produce.

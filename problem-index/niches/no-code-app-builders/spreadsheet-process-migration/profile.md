# Spreadsheet Process Migration

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/no-code-app-builders|No-Code App Builders]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor here is fighting to turn an existing spreadsheet-and-email process into a working application without the owner rebuilding it from scratch — and whoever does that takes the department, because the rebuild is the only reason the process is still a spreadsheet.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.1B US addressable in departmental process migration
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~18% of category revenue, and the largest untapped pool
**Digital Adoption:** Very Low — the processes are still in spreadsheets and inboxes
**Target Buyer:** Operations managers and departmental leadership
**Automation Potential:** Very High — the spreadsheet is a structural description of the process

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The category's addressable market is not people who want to build an app. It is the vastly larger population running a real business process on a spreadsheet, an email thread and a shared folder — the intake tracker with a tab per month, the approval process that is a chain of forwarded messages, the schedule maintained by one person and distributed as a PDF. These people are not resistant to better tooling; they are unable to justify the rebuild. Starting from a blank app means recreating columns, rules, statuses and validations that already exist, then migrating the data, then retraining everyone, for a process that currently works. The contest is removing the rebuild: taking what exists and producing something that runs, so the cost of moving is an afternoon rather than a project. Nobody competes on this because every vendor's onboarding assumes a blank canvas.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Spreadsheet import that produces a table of rows, templates for common process types, implementation partners for larger customers, and AI-assisted app generation from a text prompt. The gaps: import moves the data and discards the process — the formulas, the conditional formatting that encodes status, the validation lists, the pivot that is the report, the comment that is the audit trail; the email half of the process is ignored entirely, though it contains the approvals and the handoffs; templates start from a generic model rather than from this team's actual process; and nothing verifies that the resulting app does what the spreadsheet did, which is the check that would make the move safe.

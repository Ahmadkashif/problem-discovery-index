# Load-Bearing App Detection

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/no-code-app-builders|No-Code App Builders]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor here is fighting to detect the moment an app crosses from a personal convenience into a business process the company cannot lose — before the person who built it leaves — and whoever does that takes the platform governance account, because that crossing is the category's only genuine crisis.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.4B US attributable to application governance, criticality management and continuity within the category
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~23% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** None — no platform attempts the detection
**Target Buyer:** IT leadership, operations leadership and platform governance functions
**Automation Potential:** Very High — usage, dependency and process evidence are all in the platform

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Every successful no-code app follows the same trajectory. Someone builds it for themselves. Two colleagues start using it. A team adopts it. Within a year it is how the company handles returns, or approvals, or shift swaps, and it has no owner, no documentation, no tests, no error handling and no backup. Nothing marks the crossing. The application does not change on the day it becomes load-bearing; the organisation's dependence on it does, quietly, and the first visible sign is usually a resignation letter. What makes this a distinct contest is that the crossing is measurable — user growth beyond the builder's team, dependency from other systems, integration into a process with a deadline, data volume, the appearance of external parties — and no vendor measures it, because the category was built to celebrate creation and has never modelled consequence.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Admin consoles listing apps in a tenant, usage statistics per app, licensing and consumption reporting, and in the enterprise platforms an environment and approval model that applies only to apps built inside it. The gaps: criticality is a field somebody sets, if anyone does, rather than something observed; no signal fires when an app crosses a threshold, so there is no moment at which governance can intervene cheaply; the builder's continued presence is not tracked against the apps they own, though the directory knows when they leave; and there is no graduated response — the only available actions are ignore and take over, when what is needed is documentation, a second owner and a backup.

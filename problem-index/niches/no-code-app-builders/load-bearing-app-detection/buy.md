**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** IT service management has classified application criticality and business impact for decades with established frameworks, and no-code apps sit entirely outside every one of them.

**Contested on:** Every serious competitor here is fighting to detect the moment an app crosses from a personal convenience into a business process the company cannot lose — before the person who built it leaves — and whoever does that takes the platform governance account, because that crossing is the category's only genuine crisis.

## The Problem
Determining which applications a business cannot operate without, what depends on what, and what the recovery objectives should be is core IT service management, with mature frameworks, configuration databases and business impact analysis methodology behind it. All of it assumes applications that IT knows about, provisioned through a process. The no-code estate is invisible to that machinery by construction, so an app running a revenue process has no criticality classification while a decommissioned server still has one.

## What Already Exists
Business impact analysis frameworks, configuration management databases with dependency modelling, application portfolio management products, service mapping and discovery tooling, and recovery objective methodology. Graph analysis over dependency structures is standard. Several vendors sell application rationalisation on exactly this basis. The conceptual work is entirely done.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to applications nobody registered. It requires: (1) observed rather than declared classification, since the whole premise is that nobody filed anything — the existing frameworks assume a human assessment per application and here the assessment must come from behaviour; (2) signals specific to this estate, where the strongest indicators are social and structural rather than infrastructural: who uses it outside the builder's team, whether an external party touches it, whether an automation acts in the world; (3) continuous re-scoring rather than periodic review, because the crossing is a transition and an annual assessment cycle will miss every one of them; (4) a single-person dependency measure, which conventional frameworks barely model because enterprise applications have teams — here it is the dominant risk and belongs at the centre of the score; and (5) graduated, low-friction responses, because applying enterprise change control to a departmental tracker will simply push the next one onto a personal account, which is the failure mode every governance attempt in this category has produced.

## Target Customer
IT service management and application portfolio vendors, no-code platform vendors building enterprise governance, and IT organisations with a significant and unmeasured no-code estate.

## Impact If Solved
A mature discipline exists and structurally cannot see this estate, which is why the assets running real processes are the ones with no classification. Observed scoring and single-person dependency are the two adaptations, and the graduated response is what keeps governance from driving the behaviour underground.

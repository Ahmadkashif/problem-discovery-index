# Shadow App Inventory

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/no-code-app-builders|No-Code App Builders]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor here is fighting to produce a complete list of the applications a company's employees have built, including the ones built on accounts IT does not know exist — and whoever can produce that list takes the security and compliance account, because nobody can produce it today.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$780M US attributable to discovery, governance and compliance for citizen-built applications
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~13% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Low — the premise of the category defeats console-based inventory
**Target Buyer:** IT, security and compliance functions
**Automation Potential:** Very High — the evidence is in network, billing and identity data

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Admin consoles list every application in the tenant, which is a complete answer to the wrong question. The premise of no-code is that a person with a business problem signs up and builds something without asking anyone, which means the applications that most need governing are the ones in tenants IT has never heard of — created on a personal email, expensed on a card, or started on a free tier that never triggered a procurement conversation. The gap is structural rather than a product deficiency: no console can enumerate what is outside its own tenant. Discovery therefore has to come from elsewhere — network traffic, expense and card data, identity provider sign-in logs, browser telemetry, and the connection records of the systems these apps talk to — which is a different discipline from tenant administration and is where the contest actually is.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Admin consoles per platform, cloud access security brokers with application discovery, SaaS management platforms tracking spend and sign-ins, and identity providers logging authentication. The gaps: SaaS management tooling identifies that a company uses a platform and not what has been built on it, which is the part that matters; free tiers leave no financial trace, which is where a large share of the estate lives; personal accounts are invisible to identity-based discovery by definition; and even where an app is found, nothing assesses what data it touches or what process depends on it, which is the question compliance is actually asking.

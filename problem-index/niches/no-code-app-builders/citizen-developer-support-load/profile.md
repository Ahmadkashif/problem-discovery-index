# Citizen Developer Support Load

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/no-code-app-builders|No-Code App Builders]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor that takes this seriously is fighting to stop the person who built a useful app becoming its unpaid support desk — and whoever does that takes the builder's loyalty, which is what determines whether the platform spreads inside a company.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$420M US attributable to support, documentation and operability tooling for citizen-built apps
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~7% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** None — the load is unpaid, unrecorded and unowned
**Target Buyer:** Nominally IT and platform governance; the beneficiary is the builder
**Automation Potential:** Very High — the app's own structure and error history supply most of what is needed

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Someone builds a useful thing. It spreads. Their reward is a second job: fielding questions about how to use it, diagnosing why an automation did not fire, restoring a record somebody deleted, explaining a field's meaning for the fortieth time, and being messaged on holiday when it breaks. None of this is in their job description, none of it is measured, and none of it is compensated. The predictable outcomes follow — builders stop sharing what they build, or build deliberately fragile things that nobody else will adopt, or leave, taking the app's only operational knowledge with them. This is a distinct contested surface because the remedies are specific and none of them exists: an app that explains itself, that handles its own failures legibly, that tells its users what went wrong instead of telling the builder, and that can be handed to somebody else. Every vendor optimises for the moment of creation and nothing for the two years after it.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Error logs visible to the app's editor, version history, sharing and permission controls, and community forums. The gaps: errors are reported to the builder rather than explained to the user, so every failure becomes a message; nothing generates documentation from the app, though the app is a complete specification of itself; there is no support path for users other than the builder personally; the load is invisible to management because it lands in direct messages; and there is no concept of a second owner, so the builder is a single point of failure by default.

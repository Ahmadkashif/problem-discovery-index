# Integration & Connectors

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/no-code-app-builders|No-Code App Builders]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor here is fighting to make the connection to whatever system the customer actually has work and keep working — and that contest splits between getting connected and staying connected, which is why this niche is not terminal and is decomposed below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$2.1B US integration and automation attributable to this category
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~35% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** High in breadth, low in depth
**Target Buyer:** Builders directly for connection; platform teams for reliability
**Automation Potential:** Very High — API specifications and traffic both describe what a connector needs

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Connector count is the category's headline marketing number and its least informative one. A marketplace with three thousand integrations sounds comprehensive and is, for the three thousand systems everyone has heard of; the system that matters to this customer is the internal one with a bespoke API, the industry-specific product with four hundred users nationally, or the well-known product whose connector exists and supports six of its forty endpoints. The builder falls back to a generic HTTP block and hand-rolls authentication, pagination and error handling — which is precisely the engineering the platform existed to remove — and what they produce is fragile in ways they will discover later.

The filter fails here. "Integration and connectors" names a component of the product, not what the competition is about, and the contest splits cleanly in two. Getting connected to something nobody has written a connector for is a generation and discovery problem, bought by a builder in the moment of frustration. Keeping thousands of existing connectors working as upstream APIs change underneath them is a drift and maintenance problem, bought by a platform team after the third silent breakage. The two contested sub-niches below carry the terminal statements.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Large connector marketplaces, generic HTTP and webhook blocks, community-contributed connectors of uneven quality, and integration platforms as a separate category. The gaps: the long tail is unserved by construction, since a connector is a per-system engineering investment and the tail does not repay it; connector depth is invisible before use, so a builder discovers the missing endpoint after committing; and maintenance is reactive everywhere, with breakage detected by the customer.

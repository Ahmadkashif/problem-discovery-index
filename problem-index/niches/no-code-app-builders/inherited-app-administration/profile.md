# Inherited App Administration

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/no-code-app-builders|No-Code App Builders]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor here is fighting to make an application readable and safely changeable by somebody who did not build it — and whoever does that takes the IT account, because the alternative is an administrator who can neither modify nor retire what a department depends on.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$540M US attributable to comprehension, change safety and takeover of citizen-built applications
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~9% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Low — inheritance is handled by reading the app and hoping
**Target Buyer:** IT administrators and platform operations teams
**Automation Potential:** High — the application definition is complete and machine-readable

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The builder leaves and the app stays. An IT administrator is handed something a department depends on, built by somebody they never met, with no documentation, no tests, no comments and no statement of intent. They can see every field, formula, view and automation — the definition is complete — and they cannot tell what any of it is for, which parts are load-bearing, or what will break if they change something. Their position is materially worse than inheriting undocumented code, because code at least has a history of commits and a language with conventions, whereas an app has a builder's private logic expressed through a drag-and-drop interface with no record of why anything is the way it is. The contest here is comprehension and change safety for a reader who was not the author, and it is distinct in buyer and in product shape from everything else in the category.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Version history of varying depth, environment separation in the enterprise platforms, export and backup features, and permission models. The gaps: there is no way to understand an app without clicking through every element, and nothing summarises intent; change impact is unknowable, so an administrator cannot tell what a field rename breaks; there is no test capability, so the only verification is production; the reason for anything is unrecoverable, since the builder's intent was never recorded anywhere; and retirement is as unsafe as modification, so inherited apps accumulate permanently.

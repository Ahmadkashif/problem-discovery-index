# Usage Pricing & Packaging

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/api-infrastructure-providers|API Infrastructure Providers]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor here is fighting to tell an API business what to charge for, at what tier, with what overage behaviour — and whoever answers that takes the commercial side of the category, because metering is solved and the pricing decision is guessed at by everybody.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$310M US attributable to usage metering, pricing and monetisation for APIs
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~8% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — metering is mature, the commercial decision is not supported at all
**Target Buyer:** API business owners, product and commercial leadership
**Automation Potential:** Very High — usage, cost and churn are all recorded

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Metering usage and billing for it is solved plumbing, available from several vendors, and nobody competes on it any more. What remains entirely unsupported is the commercial decision it serves: what unit to charge for, where to set the tiers, what happens at the boundary, whether to meter calls or records or compute or outcomes. Every API business makes those choices by analogy to a competitor and revisits them painfully, because changing pricing on existing consumers is expensive and slow. The choices have large consequences — the wrong unit makes the product's cost and its price diverge, badly placed tiers leave a large share of consumers just below a boundary they will never cross, and harsh overage behaviour produces a customer relationship that ends at the first surprise invoice. The provider holds complete usage data, complete cost data and a complete record of who churned, and uses it to produce invoices.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Metering and usage-based billing platforms, rate limiting in every gateway, revenue reporting, and pricing pages. The gaps: nothing relates a pricing structure to the outcomes it produced, so pricing decisions are never evaluated; the distribution of consumer usage against tier boundaries is never plotted, though it shows immediately where the tiers are wrong; cost to serve per consumer is rarely computed, so margin by customer is unknown; overage behaviour is chosen once and its effect on churn is unmeasured; and changing pricing is treated as a one-off project rather than as an ongoing practice with evidence.

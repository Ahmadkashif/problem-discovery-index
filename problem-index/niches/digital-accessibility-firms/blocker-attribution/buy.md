## The Problem
Safety investigation in aviation, healthcare and industry developed a rigorous practice for exactly this: many things were wrong, one sequence produced the outcome, and the report must distinguish the causal factor from the contributing ones, rate severity by consequence and likelihood, and produce findings that withstand legal scrutiny. The methods are formal, taught and defensible. Accessibility reporting lists everything found and rates it against a standard's own categories.

## What Already Exists
Causal versus contributing factor analysis; severity rating by consequence and likelihood; evidence standards for investigation findings; recommendations tied to specific causal factors; and reporting formats built for legal scrutiny.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a barrier rather than an incident, and to a population rather than an event. It requires: (1) the outcome being a person unable to proceed rather than an event that occurred, so the investigation is of a condition rather than of a sequence — this is the substantive difference; (2) severity that depends on how many users and which technologies are affected rather than on harm magnitude; (3) findings that must also map to a standard for compliance purposes; (4) a remediation audience that is a product backlog competing with commercial work; and (5) a legal environment where the report may be used by either side.

## Target Customer
Accessibility firms, client leadership and counsel, compliance and procurement functions, and assurance providers.

## Impact If Solved
Safety investigation distinguishes causal from contributing factors and rates severity by consequence, to a standard that withstands scrutiny. A barrier affecting a population rather than an incident affecting a person is what has to be adapted.

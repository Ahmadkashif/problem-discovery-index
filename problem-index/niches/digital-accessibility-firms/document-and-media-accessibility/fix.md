# The Form Nobody Can Complete

**Niche:** [[niches/digital-accessibility-firms/document-and-media-accessibility/profile|Document & Media Accessibility]]
**Industry:** [[industries/digital-accessibility-firms|Digital Accessibility Firms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The only way to apply is a scanned form, and a screen reader user cannot fill it in at all.
**Tags:** #quick-win #compliance #automation #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #worker-facing #data-integration #workflow-orchestration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to make tens of thousands of documents, videos and images usable without remediating each one by hand, and whoever automates the bulk takes the account.

## The Problem
Forms are where document accessibility failures bite hardest. A scanned or untagged form that a screen reader user cannot complete is not an inconvenience — it is a complete barrier to whatever the form is for, which is frequently something consequential like an application, a claim or a registration. These forms sit in estates of thousands of documents, indistinguishable in any inventory from a brochure, and are remediated in the same undifferentiated queue if at all.

## Why It's Still Broken
Forms are not distinguished from documents — an inventory that counts files equally cannot show that one of them is the only route to a service, so the remediation queue treats them the same. Scanned forms need rebuilding rather than tagging. Nobody has ranked the estate by consequence. And the affected users frequently have no alternative route.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Find the forms and fix those first. Identify every form in the estate and treat it as a separate, higher-priority class, which is the fix and is a filter rather than a project. Rank forms by what they gate — a benefit application and a feedback form are not comparable. Rebuild scanned forms as accessible electronic forms rather than attempting to remediate the scan, since tagging a scan rarely produces something usable. Provide an accessible alternative route immediately where remediation will take time, which is the interim obligation. Test the forms with a screen reader end to end rather than checking their tags, as the difference is the whole point. Prevent at source by requiring accessible form creation tools, which stops the estate growing. Check that the submission path works, not only the form, because a form that completes and cannot be submitted is the same barrier. Publish which forms are accessible, so users are not discovering it by attempting. Give users a route to request an alternative format and meet it promptly. And treat a form that gates a statutory service as the highest priority in the entire estate, which it is.

## Who Feels the Pain
People unable to apply for something they are entitled to; organisations with a statutory duty they are not meeting; support staff handling requests for alternative formats; and the users who simply do not apply.

## Impact If Fixed
An inventory that counts files equally cannot show that one of them is the only route to a service, so the queue treats them the same. Filtering forms out as a separate class puts the absolute barriers first.

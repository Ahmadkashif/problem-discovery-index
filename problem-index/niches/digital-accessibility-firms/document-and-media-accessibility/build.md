# The Back Catalogue at Volume

**Niche:** [[niches/digital-accessibility-firms/document-and-media-accessibility/profile|Document & Media Accessibility]]
**Industry:** [[industries/digital-accessibility-firms|Digital Accessibility Firms]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Forty thousand documents remediated by hand at a price per document is a programme nobody will fund.
**Tags:** #automation #large-language-models #cnns #evaluation-metrics #object-detection #data-integration #workflow-orchestration #compliance
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to make tens of thousands of documents, videos and images usable without remediating each one by hand, and whoever automates the bulk takes the account.

## The Problem
An organisation's document and media estate is large, old and mostly inaccessible. Remediation is priced per document, so the full estate is unaffordable and the programme is scoped down to a fraction, chosen by guesswork. The rest stays inaccessible indefinitely. Meanwhile new documents are produced daily by people with no accessibility training, so the estate grows faster than any remediation programme can work through it.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Remediation is a services business priced per unit, which is the opposite of an automation incentive. Generated output quality has been variable enough to distrust. Prevention at authoring requires changing how a large number of people work. And nobody has prioritised by what is actually accessed.

## What to Build
Prioritise by access, automate the bulk, and verify. Rank the estate by what is actually accessed rather than remediating chronologically or alphabetically, which is the core — a small fraction of documents accounts for nearly all access and nobody has looked. Generate structure, alternative text, captions and transcripts automatically at volume, which is now tractable for the majority of cases. Verify generated output rather than trusting it, since a wrong alternative text is worse than a missing one and this is the legitimate objection to automation here. Route the cases that automation handles poorly — complex tables, charts, scanned forms — to human remediation, which is a far smaller queue. Prevent at authoring with checks in the tools people write in, so the estate stops growing. Handle forms specifically, as those are where the real barriers concentrate and generic processing does least well. Report coverage by access rather than by document count, which is the honest measure. Retire documents nobody accesses rather than remediating them, which is frequently the correct answer. Re-run the pipeline as tooling improves, since the back catalogue is a fixed input. And make the process auditable, because the output is a compliance artefact as well as a user-facing one.

## Target Customer
Content operations and communications functions, accessibility firms and remediation vendors, document and media platform vendors, and public sector bodies with statutory duties.

## Impact If Built
A small fraction of documents accounts for nearly all access and nobody has looked, so remediation is scoped by guesswork. Access-ranked prioritisation with verified bulk generation makes the estate tractable.

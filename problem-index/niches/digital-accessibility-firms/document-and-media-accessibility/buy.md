**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to make tens of thousands of documents, videos and images usable without remediating each one by hand, and whoever automates the bulk takes the account.

## The Problem
Content operations at scale is an automated pipeline problem and has been solved as one: assets are ingested, transformed, enriched with metadata, quality-checked and published, with human review reserved for exceptions. Media companies process millions of assets this way. Accessibility remediation of the same kinds of asset is sold as a per-document manual service, which is why estates stay inaccessible.

## What Already Exists
Bulk ingestion and transformation pipelines; automated metadata enrichment; quality checking with exception routing; human review only on exceptions; and reprocessing as tooling improves.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to output whose correctness is a judgement about usefulness to a person. It requires: (1) quality defined as whether a description is useful to someone who cannot see the image, which no automated check can fully assess and which makes verification the central problem — this is the substantive difference; (2) documents that are compliance artefacts, so the process must be auditable; (3) forms and complex tables that need structural remediation rather than enrichment; (4) an estate that is the organisation's own back catalogue rather than an incoming feed, so the prioritisation question is which parts matter; and (5) a legal context where a wrong description may be worse than an absent one.

## Target Customer
Content operations functions, accessibility and remediation vendors, document and media platform vendors, and public sector bodies.

## Impact If Solved
Content operations solved bulk processing with exception-routed human review and reprocesses as tooling improves. Quality defined as usefulness to a person is what makes verification the central problem here.

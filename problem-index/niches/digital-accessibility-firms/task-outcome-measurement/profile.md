# Task Outcome Measurement

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/digital-accessibility-firms|Digital Accessibility Firms]]
**Category:** 🔵 High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to state whether a disabled person can complete the task they came for, rather than how many standards criteria a page fails — and whoever measures that takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$400M US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~27% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Very low — rarely attempted
**Target Buyer:** Accessibility and product leadership
**Automation Potential:** Medium — testing coverage plus attribution

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The industry delivers a list of standards violations when the question is whether a disabled person can finish what they came to do, and those two things come apart in both directions. A site can pass every automated check and remain unusable with a screen reader; it can carry technical violations nobody ever encounters. Measuring the outcome reframes the deliverable from a list into a statement about people, which is both the honest version of the service and the one that survives a legal environment increasingly sceptical of conformance claims.

### Contested sub-niches
This niche is not terminal. It names an objective, and delivering it needs two different kinds of work:
- [[niches/digital-accessibility-firms/at-testing-at-scale/profile|🎯 Assistive Technology Testing at Scale]] — establishing whether the flow completes
- [[niches/digital-accessibility-firms/blocker-attribution/profile|🎯 Blocker Attribution & Evidence]] — saying what blocked it and what that means

## Current Tools & Gaps
An automated scanner, a manual audit against success criteria and a conformance report. The gaps: no definition of the flows that matter; no completion testing; no measurement across assistive technology combinations; no link from a failure to a blocked task; and no statement of outcome anywhere in the deliverable.

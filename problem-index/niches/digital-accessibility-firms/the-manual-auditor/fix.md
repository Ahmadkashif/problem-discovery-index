# Writing Up Instead of Looking

**Niche:** [[niches/digital-accessibility-firms/the-manual-auditor/profile|The Manual Auditor]]
**Industry:** [[industries/digital-accessibility-firms|Digital Accessibility Firms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The auditor spends the afternoon transcribing what they found in the morning.
**Tags:** #worker-facing #quick-win #automation #workflow-orchestration #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #data-integration #compliance
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to support an auditor working a site keyboard-only and then twice with screen readers for weeks, documenting each failure against a criterion — and whoever supports that work takes the account.

## The Problem
Auditors examine and then write up, in separate blocks. Findings noted roughly during examination are expanded afterwards into descriptions, classified against criteria, located, and evidenced. Detail fades between the two, so the write-up is partly reconstruction, and the reconstruction is slower and less accurate than capture at the moment would have been. Roughly half the audit is spent describing what the other half found.

## Why It's Still Broken
Capture and documentation are separate steps — a finding noted roughly and expanded hours later must be partly remembered, which costs time and accuracy that capture at the moment would not. The spreadsheet does not support in-flow capture. Examination feels like the real work. And the write-up time is billed.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Capture properly at the moment, in whatever tool is available. Record the full finding at the point of observation rather than a note to expand later, which is the fix and needs only a better-structured capture form. Capture the element selector and the page state at the time, since reconstructing them is the slowest part. Record the screen reader output verbatim rather than paraphrasing later. Use a structured template so classification happens at capture rather than in a second pass. Use voice capture where hands are occupied, which suits the working posture. Group findings as they are captured rather than sorting afterwards. Write the description in the moment while the context is present, which is both faster and better. Review rather than compose during the write-up block, which is what the block should be. Measure the ratio of examination time to write-up time, which is the number that shows the problem. And carry the same capture structure across engagements so the habit and the tooling improve together.

## Who Feels the Pain
Auditors spending half an engagement transcribing; clients paying full rate for reconstruction; findings that lost detail between observation and write-up; and the audit's coverage, limited by the time the documentation consumed.

## Impact If Fixed
A finding noted roughly and expanded hours later must be partly remembered, which costs time and accuracy that capture at the moment would not. Structured capture in the moment converts write-up into review.

# Niche Analysis — Digital Accessibility Firms

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/digital-accessibility-firms|Digital Accessibility Firms]]

## Niche Selection

This is a field where the measurable proxy and the actual goal have drifted apart under legal pressure, and everyone in it knows. Conformance is cheap, documentable and defensible in a demand letter; whether a disabled person can complete the task they came for is expensive to establish and is the thing that matters. The eight niches below follow that gap — what is measured, what is claimed, what reaches a backlog, and who does the work that only people can do.

## Why These Niches

Task outcome measurement and conformance auditing are the two ways the field can describe its own work, and only one of them is practised at scale. Remediation prioritisation and overlay claims are where the incentive distortion shows most clearly — four thousand unranked findings landing in a backlog, and a market of products promising conformance through a script that disability advocacy organisations have publicly objected to. The two underserved audiences are the disabled assistive technology specialists who can actually say whether a site works and are frequently engaged as research participants rather than as experts, and the auditor working through a site keyboard-only for weeks. The last two are mechanical: catching new failures in the build, and handling documents and media at volume.

## Filter Notes

Seven of the eight are terminal — each names one contest that every serious competitor is fighting over, and decomposing further would produce features rather than markets.

**Task outcome measurement** is not. It names an objective, and delivering it requires two things that are different work sold to different buyers. Assistive technology testing at scale asks how to establish whether a flow can actually be completed, with which screen reader, browser and operating system combination, at a cost that permits doing it repeatedly rather than once a year — a labour, tooling and coverage problem sold into the firm's own delivery. Blocker attribution and evidence asks what to do with the result: which specific defect blocked which task, how severe that is relative to everything else, and how to state it in a form a product team can act on and a legal process will respect — an attribution, prioritisation and documentation problem sold to the client and increasingly to their counsel. A firm can run better assistive technology testing and still deliver a list; a firm can state outcomes well and have no affordable way to establish them. Both halves are required and each fails alone.

Two adjacent candidates were rejected as belonging elsewhere: **designing the interface so the barriers are not created** belongs to [[industries/product-design-studios|Product Design Studios]], and **running research studies with disabled participants as a study** sits with [[industries/ux-research-agencies|UX Research Agencies]].

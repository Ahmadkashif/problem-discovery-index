# The Assistive Technology Specialist

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/digital-accessibility-firms|Digital Accessibility Firms]]
**Category:** 🟣 Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to engage the disabled experts who can actually say whether a site works as specialists rather than as research participants — and whoever builds that practice takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$150M US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~10% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Low — engaged as participants
**Target Buyer:** Practice leadership
**Automation Potential:** Medium — tooling, credentialing and workflow

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The people who can actually say whether a site works are disabled users with years of assistive technology expertise, and the industry frequently engages them as research participants rather than as specialists. The distinction is substantial: a participant is recruited, compensated at a participant rate, and asked to perform tasks; a specialist is contracted, paid professionally, and asked for an expert assessment. The expertise is the same and the engagement model determines both the fee and the standing of the finding.

## Current Tools & Gaps
A participant recruitment process, an incentive rate and a session protocol. The gaps: no professional engagement route; no credentialing or specialism recognition; no tooling built for an expert assessor rather than a study participant; no career path within accessibility firms; and no rate benchmark for the expertise.

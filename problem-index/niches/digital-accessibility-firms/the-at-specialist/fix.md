# Paid as a Participant, Asked as an Expert

**Niche:** [[niches/digital-accessibility-firms/the-at-specialist/profile|The Assistive Technology Specialist]]
**Industry:** [[industries/digital-accessibility-firms|Digital Accessibility Firms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The session asks for a professional assessment and the engagement pays a participant incentive.
**Tags:** #worker-facing #quick-win #compliance #revenue-impact #evaluation-metrics #workflow-orchestration #descriptive-statistics #data-integration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to engage the disabled experts who can actually say whether a site works as specialists rather than as research participants — and whoever builds that practice takes the account.

## The Problem
A person with a decade of screen reader expertise is booked for a session, compensated at a research participant rate, and asked questions that amount to a professional assessment: does this work, what is wrong with it, how would you fix it. The mismatch between what is asked and what is paid is obvious to everyone in the room. The expertise is used, the finding carries the firm's name rather than the specialist's, and the person is recruited again next quarter on the same terms.

## Why It's Still Broken
The engagement route determines the rate — a person booked through participant recruitment is paid a participant rate regardless of what they are actually being asked to do, and nothing in the process reconsiders that. There is no alternative route. The firm's costing assumes it. And the specialist has little leverage.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Match the engagement to what is being asked. Engage on a professional contract when asking for an assessment rather than an observation, which is the fix and is a procurement decision. Pay a professional rate for professional judgement, benchmarked against comparable specialist consultancy. Attribute the assessment to the specialist, since authorship is part of what professional engagement means. Distinguish the two kinds of session explicitly in the firm's own methodology, which is the clarity that makes the rest follow. Offer ongoing engagement rather than repeated one-off bookings, which most specialists would prefer and which serves the firm better. Include specialists in the design of the assessment method, not only its execution. Provide the same working tooling and support given to any other assessor. Recognise the specialism in the firm's own role structure. Ask specialists what engagement terms would work, which is rarely done. And say publicly how the firm engages this expertise, because the client is buying it and should know.

## Who Feels the Pain
Specialists paid participant rates for professional work; firms whose method depends on expertise they have not properly engaged; clients buying assessments whose authorship is obscured; and the field's ability to attract and retain this expertise.

## Impact If Fixed
A person booked through participant recruitment is paid a participant rate regardless of what they are actually asked to do, and nothing reconsiders it. Matching the engagement route to the work is a procurement decision.

# The Badge That Says Accessible

**Niche:** [[niches/digital-accessibility-firms/overlay-claims/profile|Overlay & Quick-Fix Claims]]
**Industry:** [[industries/digital-accessibility-firms|Digital Accessibility Firms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The site displays an accessibility badge and a screen reader user still cannot complete the form.
**Tags:** #quick-win #compliance #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #worker-facing #confidence-intervals #automation #data-integration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting over what a conformance claim is worth when it rests on a script, and whoever can establish what a remediation actually achieved takes the account.

## The Problem
Public accessibility claims are made without a defined basis. A badge, a statement, a certification mark — each asserts something to users and to procurement, and none states what was tested, by whom, when, or against what. A disabled user who cannot complete a task on a site carrying such a claim has no route to contest it, and an organisation relying on the claim during procurement has no way to assess it.

## Why It's Still Broken
The claim has no required content — a statement that asserts accessibility without saying what was assessed cannot be evaluated or challenged by anyone, which is precisely why it is stated that way. There is no standard format. Procurement accepts the badge. And users have no channel.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Require the claim to say what it rests on. Publish what was tested, when, by whom and with what, alongside any accessibility claim, which is the fix and is a paragraph. State the version tested and the date, since a claim about a continuously changing product ages immediately. Describe the known limitations rather than omitting them, which several organisations already do well and is more credible than a clean assertion. Provide a route for users to report that the claim does not match their experience, and respond to it. Ask for the substantiation during procurement rather than accepting a badge, which is the single change that would alter vendor behaviour fastest. Distinguish claims about a remediation product from claims about the site itself, since those are frequently conflated. Do not display a claim the organisation cannot support if asked. Re-test and re-date the statement on a defined cadence. Say which flows were assessed rather than making a site-wide assertion. And treat the statement as a representation to users, which is what it is.

## Who Feels the Pain
Disabled users blocked on sites that claim accessibility; procurement functions relying on unverifiable badges; honest vendors competing against unsubstantiated claims; and organisations carrying a legal exposure they created.

## Impact If Fixed
A statement that asserts accessibility without saying what was assessed cannot be evaluated or challenged by anyone. Requiring the claim to state its scope, date and method is a paragraph that makes it answerable.

# Substantiating What a Remediation Achieved

**Niche:** [[niches/digital-accessibility-firms/overlay-claims/profile|Overlay & Quick-Fix Claims]]
**Industry:** [[industries/digital-accessibility-firms|Digital Accessibility Firms]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Buyers choose between competing assertions about accessibility because nobody measures what a remediation changed.
**Tags:** #evaluation-metrics #compliance #causal-inference #confidence-intervals #hypothesis-testing #descriptive-statistics #data-integration #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting over what a conformance claim is worth when it rests on a script, and whoever can establish what a remediation actually achieved takes the account.

## The Problem
The overlay dispute is conducted entirely in assertions. Vendors claim conformance; advocacy organisations and disabled users report that the products do not deliver it and sometimes interfere with assistive technology; litigation continues against sites using them. The question underneath — what measurably changed for a user attempting a task before and after a remediation — is answerable and nobody answers it, so procurement decisions are made on marketing material and legal opinion.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Independent verification has no obvious buyer: the vendor does not want it, the site owner wants the claim rather than the truth, and users have no budget. Measuring requires assistive technology testing before and after. The legal environment rewards a defensible document over a demonstrated outcome. And the dispute is polarised enough that measurement is read as taking a side.

## What to Build
Measure before and after with assistive technology and publish what is found. Test task completion before and after any remediation, with real assistive technology, which is the core and is the only thing that settles what a remediation achieved. Report what changed by flow and by technology rather than by a conformance score, since the score is what the dispute is about. Check for interference explicitly — whether the remediation disrupted assistive technology that was working — as that is a documented complaint and is directly testable. Establish a substantiation standard for what evidence supports what claim, which is the durable contribution and would serve procurement, litigants and honest vendors alike. Make the testing independent of both the vendor and the site owner, since a verification either party pays for carries the obvious problem. Publish the methodology so results can be challenged on method rather than on motive. Include disabled expert testers as the assessors, because their assessment is the one that matters. Give users a route to report that a claim does not match their experience. Cover all remediation approaches on the same standard rather than singling out one. And state plainly what the evidence does and does not support, which is the whole point.

## Target Customer
Procurement functions requiring assurance, legal and compliance teams, disability advocacy organisations, and accessibility firms offering verification.

## Impact If Built
The dispute is conducted in assertions because nobody measures what a remediation changed for a user attempting a task. Before-and-after task testing with assistive technology is what turns a claim into evidence.

## The Problem
Advertising regulation established a workable principle: a claim must be substantiated before it is made, the evidence must be held, the standard of evidence scales with the strength of the claim, and complaints are adjudicated by a body applying that standard. It works across sectors and is why product claims are not simply whatever a vendor wishes to say. Accessibility claims — displayed publicly, relied on by procurement and litigated over — have no equivalent apparatus.

## What Already Exists
Substantiation-before-claim requirements; evidence standards scaled to claim strength; independent complaint adjudication; published rulings that set precedent; and consequences for unsubstantiated claims.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a technical claim about software behaviour with disabled users as the affected party. It requires: (1) substantiation that is an assistive technology test result rather than a study or a laboratory report, so the evidence standard must be defined for this medium — this is the substantive difference and does not currently exist; (2) a claim about a product that changes continuously, so substantiation has a shelf life; (3) an affected population who experience the failure directly and have standing to complain; (4) an existing litigation channel that operates without any substantiation standard; and (5) no adjudicating body, so the standard must be established commercially or by procurement demand.

## Target Customer
Procurement functions, legal and compliance teams, advocacy organisations, and standards and certification bodies.

## Impact If Solved
Advertising established that a claim must be substantiated before it is made, with adjudication and consequences. An evidence standard defined for assistive technology results, with a shelf life, is what accessibility claims lack.

**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get four thousand findings into a backlog in an order somebody can work through, and whoever makes that list actionable takes the account.

## The Problem
Security faced exactly this: scanners producing thousands of findings, engineering teams unable to act, and programmes stalling. The response was triage as a discipline — deduplicate to root cause, rank by exploitability and asset criticality rather than by raw severity, route to the owning team automatically, set service levels by rank, and track mean time to remediate. It turned an unusable volume into a managed queue. Accessibility remediation is where security was before that.

## What Already Exists
Deduplication to root cause; risk-based ranking beyond raw severity; automatic routing to owning teams; remediation service levels by rank; and mean-time-to-remediate as a programme metric.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to findings whose severity depends on human consequence rather than on exploitability. It requires: (1) ranking by whether a person is blocked rather than by whether an attacker could act, so the ranking input is task impact and affected population rather than a vulnerability score — this is the substantive difference; (2) root causes that are shared components and design patterns rather than library versions, making deduplication a codebase question; (3) a regulatory and litigation context that rewards documented effort, which can distort prioritisation toward what is countable; (4) no equivalent of a patch, since most fixes are bespoke; and (5) verification requiring assistive technology rather than a rescan.

## Target Customer
Accessibility firms, client engineering leadership, issue tracking and programme vendors, and security programme tooling vendors.

## Impact If Solved
Security turned an unusable finding volume into a managed queue through deduplication, risk ranking and routing. Ranking by whether a person is blocked, rather than by exploitability, is what the accessibility version has to substitute.

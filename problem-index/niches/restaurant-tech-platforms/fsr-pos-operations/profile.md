# Full-Service Restaurant POS & Operations

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/restaurant-tech-platforms|Restaurant Tech Platforms]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is that every competitor is fighting to turn transaction history into the two forecasts that decide a restaurant's survival — but an independent and a forty-unit chain have different versions of that problem and buy from different vendors. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$4.2B US full-service restaurant point of sale and operations software
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~35% of restaurant technology revenue
**Digital Adoption:** High — point of sale penetration is effectively total and the surrounding suites are broadly adopted
**Target Buyer:** Product leads at Toast, Square, Lightspeed, SpotOn and their competitors; operators as the purchasing side
**Automation Potential:** Very High — forecasting, prep planning and labour deployment are all well-posed on data the platform already captures

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
This is where the industry's data lives. Every transaction, every item, every modifier, every timestamp, every void and comp, at minute resolution, across hundreds of thousands of locations, joined implicitly to weather, day of week, local events and price changes. It is among the densest consumer demand datasets in the economy and nobody analyses it. Meanwhile the two decisions that determine whether a restaurant survives — how many people to schedule and how much food to prep — are both forecasts, and both are made by a manager from last week's numbers and a feeling about the weather. The gap between what the data supports and what the product delivers is the defining fact of this niche, and it is the same gap for every vendor, which is what makes the capability contested rather than differentiating.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Toast is the default for independent full-service operators with an increasingly broad suite; Square owns the counter-service and small-format end; Lightspeed and SpotOn compete in the middle. Every one of them ships reporting — sales by daypart, item mix, labour percentage — and every one of them ships it as a description of last week. Forecasting appears in labour scheduling products as a claimed feature and is widely distrusted, largely because it is built on a single location's history, which is genuinely too thin for the question being asked. That thinness is exactly the thing the platform vendor is positioned to solve and the independent operator is not, and it is also the reason the niche splits: the chain operator has the data volume and a different problem entirely.

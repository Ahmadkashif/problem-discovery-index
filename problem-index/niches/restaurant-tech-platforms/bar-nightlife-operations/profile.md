# Bar & Nightlife Operations

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/restaurant-tech-platforms|Restaurant Tech Platforms]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in beverage operations software is fighting to reconcile what was poured against what was sold, at a granularity and a cost that a bar will actually sustain — and whoever closes that variance takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$700M US software spend across bars, nightclubs, taprooms and beverage-led restaurants
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~6% of restaurant technology revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Low-Medium — point of sale is universal; beverage inventory and cost control are largely manual
**Target Buyer:** Bar owners and beverage directors; corporate beverage programme managers at restaurant groups
**Automation Potential:** High — the measurement problem is physical and the reconciliation problem is arithmetic, and the industry does both with a clipboard

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Beverage is the highest-margin category in hospitality and the one with the largest and least-understood shrinkage. A bottle of spirits yields a number of pours that depends on the pour size actually delivered, which varies by bartender, by hour, and by how busy the bar is; beer yields a number of pints that depends on foam, line cleaning and temperature; wine by the glass yields a number that depends on who is pouring. Between what a bar buys and what it rings up sits a variance that is routinely double digits and is attributed vaguely to over-pouring, comps, spillage and theft without anyone knowing the split. Counting inventory means weighing or eyeballing partial bottles, weekly, after close, by a person who has been working since six. The measurement problem is genuinely physical, which is why this segment stayed manual while the rest of hospitality digitised.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Point of sale is universal and captures sales perfectly. Inventory products for beverage exist — BevSpot, Partender, WISK, Backbar — and mostly work by making the manual count faster: photograph or tap a bottle's level, and the app computes depletion. Flow meters and smart pour spouts exist and have had limited adoption because of cost, maintenance and the disruption of fitting hardware to every bottle. Draft monitoring is more mature than spirits. The consistent gap is that the count, however captured, is compared to sales at the level of a total, producing a variance percentage with no attribution — the operator learns that 14% is missing and nothing about where, when, or on which product, which is the only form of the answer that permits a response.

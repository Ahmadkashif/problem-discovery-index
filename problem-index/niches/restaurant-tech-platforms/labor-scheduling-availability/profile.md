# Labour Scheduling & Availability

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/restaurant-tech-platforms|Restaurant Tech Platforms]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in restaurant scheduling is fighting to produce a schedule that respects who can genuinely work, so the manager stops spending the week on the phone finding cover — and whoever gets the share of shifts that survive the week highest takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$800M US restaurant labour scheduling and workforce management software
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~7% of restaurant technology revenue, with near-universal awareness and inconsistent adoption
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — scheduling apps are widespread and the schedule is still built by a manager in an afternoon
**Target Buyer:** Product leads at 7shifts, HotSchedules, Sling and their competitors; general managers as the users who live with the result
**Automation Potential:** Very High — this is constrained scheduling with a demand forecast, both of which are well-posed

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Building a restaurant schedule is a puzzle with more constraints than anyone writes down. Forecast demand by daypart determines how many people are needed. Each employee has stated availability, actual availability that differs from it, skill and station qualifications, minimum and maximum hours they want, overtime thresholds, minor labour restrictions, predictive scheduling laws in a growing number of jurisdictions, and preferences about who they work with. The manager holds most of this in their head and spends an afternoon a week assembling a schedule, which then requires a further several hours across the week of phone calls when it does not survive contact with reality. The scheduling products in the market solve the publishing and swap problem well and the construction problem barely, because construction requires both a demand forecast the category does not produce and a model of availability that is more honest than what employees enter in a form.

## Current Tools & Gaps
7shifts, HotSchedules, Sling, Homebase and the scheduling modules inside the major platforms all handle publishing, shift swaps, time clocks and communication competently, and that is genuinely useful. Auto-scheduling exists in several and is used by a minority, because the schedules it produces need heavy editing — the demand forecast underneath is thin, the constraint model is incomplete, and the soft preferences that make a schedule workable are not represented at all. Stated availability is treated as ground truth when every manager knows it is aspirational in both directions. Compliance features for predictive scheduling laws are present where required. Nothing measures the thing that matters to the manager: how much of the published schedule actually happened as published.

# Non-Commercial Foodservice

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/restaurant-tech-platforms|Restaurant Tech Platforms]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in institutional foodservice software is fighting to produce a cycle menu that satisfies the nutrition regulation, the allergen safety requirement and the per-meal budget simultaneously — and whoever generates a compliant menu people will actually eat takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$900M US software spend across school districts, hospitals, senior living, correctional and campus foodservice
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~7% of restaurant technology revenue, against a very large share of meals served
**Digital Adoption:** Low-Medium — the large contract caterers have systems; independent districts and single facilities run on spreadsheets
**Target Buyer:** Foodservice directors at school districts, hospital nutrition services directors, senior living dining directors
**Automation Potential:** High — menu planning under multiple hard constraints is an optimisation problem currently solved by a dietitian with a spreadsheet

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Institutional foodservice serves an enormous number of meals under constraints no restaurant faces. A school district's menu must meet federal nutrition standards across a weekly average, fit a per-meal reimbursement rate that is fixed by statute, use commodity entitlement allocations, satisfy allergen and religious requirements for individual students, and be eaten by children who can decline it — and unconsumed food is both a cost and a nutrition failure. A hospital adds therapeutic diets per patient, changing daily with clinical orders. Senior living adds texture-modified diets and a resident population whose intake must be monitored. These are multi-constraint planning problems with regulatory teeth, and the software sold into them is largely a menu database plus a nutrient calculator, with the planning done by a dietitian in a spreadsheet.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Nutrislice, PrimeroEdge, Titan and Meals Plus serve K-12; CBORD, Computrition and Vision Software serve healthcare and senior living; the contract caterers run internal systems. All of them do nutrient analysis and production records well, because those are the compliance artefacts. None of them plans: the dietitian assembles the cycle menu by hand and the software checks it, which is backwards for a problem with this many hard constraints. Nothing forecasts participation, so production quantities are set from historical averages and the resulting waste — which in school foodservice is both a budget item and a documented nutrition problem — is accepted. Individual dietary restrictions are managed as flags and warnings rather than as constraints on what the student or patient is actually offered.

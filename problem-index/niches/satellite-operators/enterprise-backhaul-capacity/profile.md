# Enterprise, Backhaul & Government Data Capacity

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/satellite-operators|Satellite Operators]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this sub-niche is fighting to sell guaranteed throughput across GEO, MEO and partner LEO capacity at a price per megabit that survives comparison with Starlink, and whoever can design, quote and prove a multi-orbit service level fastest takes the carrier or enterprise contract.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$12B a year in global managed data, cellular backhaul and government capacity revenue for GEO and MEO operators (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~11% of global satellite services revenue (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium-High — networks are software-defined; solution design and SLA reporting are still engineer-built per deal
**Target Buyer:** VP Networks / Head of Solutions Engineering
**Automation Potential:** High for design and reporting

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
This is the growth half of the legacy operators: SES's Networks segment reached 62% of its 2025 revenue. Customers are mobile operators backhauling remote cell sites, energy and mining companies, enterprises with remote branches, and governments buying assured capacity. Since Starlink and OneWeb began selling business and backhaul plans, buyers compare offers per megabit and per site, and the GEO/MEO operator's answer is a multi-orbit managed service with an SLA that pure LEO can't yet guarantee everywhere. Selling that means designing the network across orbits for each customer and then proving the SLA.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Ground platforms from ST Engineering iDirect, Hughes and Gilat; SD-WAN overlays; link-budget spreadsheets; network management dashboards; SLA reports assembled monthly by hand. The gaps: designing a multi-orbit network for a customer's sites is a week of engineering per bid, and SLA performance is argued over rather than shown.

# The Fleet Performance Report Built by Hand Each Month

**Niche:** [[niches/satellite-operators/aviation-maritime-connectivity/profile|Aviation & Maritime Connectivity]]
**Industry:** [[industries/satellite-operators|Satellite Operators]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Every month an account team pulls usage, availability and speed per tail or vessel from three systems into a slide deck, and the customer's first question is why the numbers don't match its own.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #large-language-models #data-integration #automation #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep every aircraft and vessel connected at a guaranteed speed as it crosses beams, orbits and borders, and whoever can show the airline or ship operator per-flight and per-voyage performance it trusts takes the fleet contract.

## The Problem
Fleet contracts require monthly reporting: sessions, data used, availability per platform, speed tests, outages and their causes. The reports are compiled by hand from network management, billing and ticketing, and differ in definitions from the airline's or ship manager's own measurements.

## Why It's Still Broken
Definitions of a flight's or voyage's "availability" vary by contract and by what the customer can measure; data sits in separate systems; and the report has always been a monthly document rather than a shared live view.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A shared portal with contract-specific metric definitions computed automatically per flight and voyage, reconciled against the customer's own data feeds where available, with a generated monthly narrative and drill-down to every exception.

## Who Feels the Pain
Account and service managers spending days a month on reports; airline connectivity managers defending the supplier to their own executives.

## Impact If Fixed
Days of reporting work removed each month, and trust in the numbers that decide fleet renewals.

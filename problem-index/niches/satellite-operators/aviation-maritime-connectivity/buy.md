# Airline IT Service Management Pointed at Connectivity Per Tail

**Niche:** [[niches/satellite-operators/aviation-maritime-connectivity/profile|Aviation & Maritime Connectivity]]
**Industry:** [[industries/satellite-operators|Satellite Operators]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Airlines and ship managers already run IT service management for every system they own; connectivity faults per aircraft or vessel still travel by email between the operator's NOC and the customer's maintenance team.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #workflow-orchestration #data-integration #automation #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep every aircraft and vessel connected at a guaranteed speed as it crosses beams, orbits and borders, and whoever can show the airline or ship operator per-flight and per-voyage performance it trusts takes the fleet contract.

## The Problem
An aircraft's terminal fault needs a maintenance action at a station where the aircraft overnights; a vessel's antenna fault needs a technician at the next port. The satellite NOC detects the fault, emails the airline or ship manager, and waits. Faults are deferred, forgotten or fixed twice.

## What Already Exists
IT service management platforms (ServiceNow, Jira Service Management) used by airlines and ship managers, and aviation maintenance systems (AMOS, TRAX) that schedule work by tail.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs: incidents keyed by tail number or IMO number rather than user; automatic creation from terminal telemetry; routing to the station or port where the platform will next be, using flight schedules or voyage plans; parts and technician coordination for line maintenance; and SLA clocks that pause while the platform is unreachable.

## Target Customer
Mobility service managers at satellite operators and resellers, and the connectivity leads at their airline and shipping customers.

## Impact If Solved
Faults fixed at the next stop instead of the next month, fewer flights flown with a dead terminal, and a shared record of what was wrong.

# Legal Docketing Rules Engines Taught the Radio Regulations

**Niche:** [[niches/satellite-operators/spectrum-orbital-filings/profile|Spectrum & Orbital Filings]]
**Industry:** [[industries/satellite-operators|Satellite Operators]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Law firms compute every court deadline automatically from a rules engine; satellite regulatory teams compute ITU and FCC deadlines in spreadsheets.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #compliance #workflow-orchestration #automation #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep an operator's orbital and spectrum priority intact through every ITU and FCC deadline — the four-month comment windows, coordination meetings, bringing-into-use dates and deployment milestones — and whoever never lets a window pass unanswered takes the regulatory account.

## The Problem
A regulatory team tracks deadlines from the Radio Regulations and its Rules of Procedure (comment windows, due-diligence submissions, bringing-into-use and suspension periods, Resolution 35 milestones) and from FCC rules (pleading cycles, milestones, bond and reporting dates). Each is computed by hand from a publication date or a grant date.

## What Already Exists
Court-rules docketing engines (CompuLaw within Aderant, LawToolBox) that compute deadlines from triggering events, used by every large law firm.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs: triggering events from BR IFIC publications and FCC grants rather than court filings; rule sets for the Radio Regulations, ITU Rules of Procedure, Resolution 35 and the FCC's Part 100; per-network and per-filing calendars rather than per-matter; and links from each deadline to the satellite or launch it depends on.

## Target Customer
Regulatory affairs teams at operators and the space practices of regulatory law firms.

## Impact If Solved
No deadline depends on someone remembering to compute it, and counsel and operator share one calendar.

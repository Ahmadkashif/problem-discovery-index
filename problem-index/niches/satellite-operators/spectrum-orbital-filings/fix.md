# Milestones Tracked Apart From the Launch Schedule

**Niche:** [[niches/satellite-operators/spectrum-orbital-filings/profile|Spectrum & Orbital Filings]]
**Industry:** [[industries/satellite-operators|Satellite Operators]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The regulatory team's milestone spreadsheet and the programme office's launch schedule are maintained by different people, so a slipped launch becomes a regulatory problem only when the milestone is weeks away.
**Tags:** #monte-carlo-methods #probability-distributions #compliance #workflow-orchestration #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep an operator's orbital and spectrum priority intact through every ITU and FCC deadline — the four-month comment windows, coordination meetings, bringing-into-use dates and deployment milestones — and whoever never lets a window pass unanswered takes the regulatory account.

## The Problem
FCC deployment milestones and ITU bringing-into-use and Resolution 35 milestones depend on satellites actually reaching orbit. Launch dates slip with rocket availability and manufacturing delays. Amazon's request in January 2026 for two more years on its half-constellation milestone, blamed on launch capacity, shows how a programme's schedule becomes a regulatory filing.

## Why It's Still Broken
The two calendars belong to different departments, the milestone rules are complex, and nobody owns the probability that a milestone will be missed — only the date.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Milestones linked directly to the launch manifest and manufacturing schedule, with a probability of meeting each one from historical slip rates of the chosen launcher and supplier, and an early warning months ahead so extension or waiver requests are filed with evidence rather than in a rush.

## Who Feels the Pain
Regulatory affairs heads; programme managers; executives who learn of a milestone risk from a regulatory filing deadline.

## Impact If Fixed
Milestone risks are seen a year early, extension requests are prepared calmly, and the operator's priority is never lost to a calendar nobody joined.

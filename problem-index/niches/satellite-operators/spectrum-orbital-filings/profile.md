# Spectrum & Orbital Filings

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/satellite-operators|Satellite Operators]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep an operator's orbital and spectrum priority intact through every ITU and FCC deadline — the four-month comment windows, coordination meetings, bringing-into-use dates and deployment milestones — and whoever never lets a window pass unanswered takes the regulatory account.

**Automation Potential:** High for watching and deadline tracking, low for negotiation

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Orbital slots and frequencies are not owned; they are held through a place in the ITU's coordination queue and through national licences. The ITU publishes its space circular every two weeks and gives four months to comment on new filings. A filed network must be brought into use within seven years, and constellations must then deploy on the milestone schedule of Resolution 35, which the FCC's new Part 100 rules (adopted 22 July 2026) mirror for non-processing-round systems. Amazon's half-constellation milestone, due 30 July 2026, needed FCC relief in June 2026. A missed date can cost an operator a slot or a constellation's priority.

## Current Tools & Gaps
The ITU's SpaceCap, SpaceQry, SNS, GIBC and SpaceCom tools; the FCC's licensing systems; Transfinite Visualyse and in-house scripts for EPFD and ΔT/T analyses; spreadsheets of open coordinations; regulatory counsel's docketing. The gap is a record that joins each filing, its deadlines, its coordination correspondence and the launch schedule, and tells a small team what to do this fortnight.

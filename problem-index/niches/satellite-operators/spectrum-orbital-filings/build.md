**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep an operator's orbital and spectrum priority intact through every ITU and FCC deadline — the four-month comment windows, coordination meetings, bringing-into-use dates and deployment milestones — and whoever never lets a window pass unanswered takes the regulatory account.

## The Problem
An operator may be in coordination with dozens of administrations and operators at once, each thread running through letters, technical exchanges, agreed parameter limits, draft agreements and bilateral meetings, sometimes for a decade. When the engineer who held a thread leaves, the next one starts from a folder of PDFs and an ITU database entry.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Coordination records are confidential, few in number per operator, and formatted differently by every administration. Regulatory software vendors serve terrestrial spectrum managers; legal tools handle documents but not orbital parameters.

## What to Build
A dossier per counterpart that assembles every filing on both sides, every letter and meeting minute, every agreed limit (power flux-density, off-axis EIRP, orbital separation), and the open issues, extracted from correspondence by language models and linked to the ITU database. Before a meeting it produces a brief: what was agreed, what was refused, what changed in either side's filings since, and comparable agreements with other counterparts.

## Target Customer
Heads of spectrum and regulatory affairs at GEO operators and constellation operators with many concurrent coordinations, and the administrations that file for them.

## Impact If Built
Negotiations start from the full record, agreements are consistent across counterparts, and a departure no longer resets a decade of coordination.

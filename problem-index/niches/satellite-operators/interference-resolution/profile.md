# Interference Detection & Resolution

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/satellite-operators|Satellite Operators]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to find, identify and stop an interferer before the customer's service level is breached, and whoever closes cases fastest across operator boundaries — where the source is usually someone else's customer — takes the operator's interference-management budget.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$300M a year in carrier monitoring, geolocation systems and services, and interference staff time across operators and teleports (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~0.3% of global satellite services revenue (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — detection is digital; identification, contact and resolution run on phone calls, email and personal networks between NOCs
**Target Buyer:** Head of Network Operations / Director of Carrier Management
**Automation Potential:** Medium-High for classification and case handling; the fix is still a person re-pointing a dish

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Interference is chronic in satellite communications. In a 2013 IRG/Newtec survey of ~500 respondents, 93% had suffered it at least once a year and 17% continuously; Eutelsat put accidental or human error at ~58% of cases and deliberate signals at ~10% in 2012. No more recent industry-wide count has been published. The source is often another operator's customer or a terrestrial system, so resolution depends on cooperation that no one is obliged to give quickly. Carrier ID, which embeds an identifier in each uplink, has been an industry goal for over a decade and is still unevenly deployed. C-band terrestrial 5G leakage and LEO-GEO sharing add new sources.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Carrier monitoring and geolocation from Kratos (Monics, satID) and Integrasys, operators' own NOC case tools, the IRG's guidelines and contact networks, and regulators for deliberate cases. The gaps: no structured case exchange between operators, Carrier ID decodes that can't be matched to an owner, and case history that leaves with the engineer.

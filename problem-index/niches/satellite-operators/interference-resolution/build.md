# A Shared Interference Case Exchange Between Operators

**Niche:** [[niches/satellite-operators/interference-resolution/profile|Interference Detection & Resolution]]
**Industry:** [[industries/satellite-operators|Satellite Operators]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** When one operator's customer interferes with another operator's satellite, the two NOCs find each other by phone and email; there is no shared, structured case that both can work.
**Tags:** #cnns #k-nearest-neighbors #large-language-models #graph-theory #workflow-orchestration #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to find, identify and stop an interferer before the customer's service level is breached, and whoever closes cases fastest across operator boundaries — where the source is usually someone else's customer — takes the operator's interference-management budget.

## The Problem
A victim operator detects interference, classifies it and, with luck, geolocates it to a region. It then has to determine which operator's network the interferer uses, find a contact at that operator's NOC, send evidence, and wait. The receiving NOC must find the terminal among its customers and get it fixed. Every case rebuilds this chain from scratch.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Operators are competitors and reluctant to share customer or network data; the industry groups that coordinate interference are small and volunteer-driven; and the case volume per operator seems too low to justify a product.

## What to Build
A neutral case exchange where a victim operator posts a structured case — trace, classification, Carrier ID decode, geolocation ellipse, affected frequencies — that is routed automatically to the operators whose networks could carry the interferer, with a response clock, status updates and resolution recorded. Each operator sees only what concerns it; the exchange builds anonymised statistics on repeat sources, terminal types and installers.

## Target Customer
Heads of network operations at GEO operators and large teleports, possibly through an industry body as the neutral host.

## Impact If Built
Cross-operator cases resolved in hours rather than days, and repeat offenders identified across the industry instead of rediscovered by each victim.

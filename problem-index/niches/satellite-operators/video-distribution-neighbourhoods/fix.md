# Occasional-Use Feeds Booked by Phone

**Niche:** [[niches/satellite-operators/video-distribution-neighbourhoods/profile|Video Distribution & Contribution]]
**Industry:** [[industries/satellite-operators|Satellite Operators]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Live sport and news feeds still book transponder time through a desk that takes calls and emails, checks availability by hand and confirms with a PDF, while the customer has already moved to apps for every other link.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #workflow-orchestration #automation #quick-win #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this sub-niche is fighting to keep broadcasters and pay-TV platforms on its established video neighbourhoods as channels move to IP delivery, and whoever proves, headend by headend and channel by channel, that satellite is still the cheaper and more reliable path keeps the capacity renewal.

## The Problem
A broadcaster needs 90 minutes of capacity for a match feed tomorrow. The booking desk checks free segments on suitable satellites, confirms the uplink and downlink parameters, issues a booking confirmation and coordinates with the teleport. Changes, overruns and cancellations follow the same manual path, and the desk is busiest when live events cluster.

## Why It's Still Broken
Occasional use is a small share of revenue, so systems investment went elsewhere, and each booking needs a link-budget and conflict check that generic booking tools can't do. Bonded cellular and IP contribution, which book in an app, keep taking the easier events.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Self-service booking that checks capacity, power and link budget against the customer's registered uplink and downlink sites, confirms instantly within policy, routes edge cases to the desk, and handles overruns automatically from the carrier monitor.

## Who Feels the Pain
Booking desk staff on event weekends; broadcast operations teams; the operator's media business losing contribution volume.

## Impact If Fixed
Bookings in minutes rather than hours, fewer booking errors on live events, and a satellite contribution offer that competes on convenience as well as reliability.

# Video Distribution & Contribution

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/satellite-operators|Satellite Operators]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this sub-niche is fighting to keep broadcasters and pay-TV platforms on its established video neighbourhoods as channels move to IP delivery, and whoever proves, headend by headend and channel by channel, that satellite is still the cheaper and more reliable path keeps the capacity renewal.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$8B a year in global video distribution, direct-to-home capacity and contribution revenue for satellite operators (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~8% of global satellite services revenue (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — playout and monitoring are digital; renewals, headend inventories and occasional-use bookings are manual
**Target Buyer:** Head of Media / VP Video Services
**Automation Potential:** Medium

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Video is the satellite operator's legacy cash engine and its most visibly shrinking line: SES's Media revenue fell 12.1% in the first half of 2025. Value comes from orbital "neighbourhoods" — slots such as 19.2°E and 13°E in Europe, or the North American cable neighbourhoods, that millions of dishes and thousands of cable headends already point at — so the contest is retention, not acquisition. Broadcasters weigh satellite against IP distribution over fibre and the public internet for each channel and each region. Contribution (live sport and news feeds back to studios) is a separate, event-driven occasional-use market, increasingly contested by bonded cellular and IP. The networks and stations whose signals ride these neighbourhoods, and their retransmission and affiliation economics, are analysed under [[industries/broadcast-stations|Broadcast TV & Radio Stations]].

## Current Tools & Gaps
Playout and multiplexing from broadcast vendors, carrier monitoring from Kratos, headend registries kept by the operator's media team, and booking desks for occasional use. The gaps: no model of which channels and distributors are likely to leave at renewal, no per-channel cost comparison against IP that a broadcaster would accept, and occasional-use booked by phone and email.

# Satellite Maritime Domain Awareness Analytics

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/satellite-operators|Satellite Operators]]
**Category:** Insight Layer
**Value-Chain Position:** Data & benchmark vendors

## What They Do
Firms that turn satellite-collected vessel signals — AIS position reports picked up from orbit, radio-frequency emissions geolocated by satellite clusters, and radar imagery of ships that switch their transponders off — into vessel risk scores, dark-activity alerts, ship-to-ship transfer detection and deceptive-shipping investigations. Windward, Pole Star, HawkEye 360 and Unseenlabs are representative, alongside the satellite AIS feed Spire Maritime sells (now owned by Kpler). Banks, commodity traders, insurers, ship charterers and coast guards pay for the analysis rather than the raw signals. Kpler's main product, commodity-flow and freight intelligence built on AIS, is a different business and is left out of this pocket; it belongs to the commodity-brokers build.

## Insight Function
**Size:** ~20-60 maritime risk analysts and data scientists per firm, excluding Kpler's commodity and freight teams (estimate)
**Output:** Vessel and fleet risk scores, behavioural alerts, investigation reports
**Proprietary data:** Years of vessel behaviour histories and the labelled deceptive-shipping cases behind the risk models; RF collection owned by HawkEye 360 and Unseenlabs, while most firms license satellite AIS that anyone can buy
**Clock:** Sanctions designations, price-cap enforcement and port-state actions change the inputs, but no third party sets a deadline on the behavioural analysis itself
**Buyer:** Head of maritime research / Chief Product Officer

**Kill switches:** none for the commercial risk products scoped here; HawkEye 360's and Unseenlabs' defence and intelligence contracts carry classification and export controls and are left out of the pocket.
**Verdict:** Logged — below threshold. Q4 is 3 rather than the 5 given to [[niches/customs-brokers/sanctions-screening-data-providers/profile|Sanctions & Restricted Party Screening Data]] (55, indexed) because the designation clock binds the list itself: a published designation must be in the screening list at once, and that is the list providers' product, which these firms license and ingest. Their own product, a behavioural score on a vessel, has no deadline set by anyone; the screening before each charter or loan is the client's timetable. With AIS mostly licensed, much of the scoring automated and a field of a handful of firms, the pocket sits below threshold. The shipping companies being screened belong to the queued ocean-shipping-carriers industry.

# Direct-to-Device: Users Beyond the Last Tower

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/satellite-operators|Satellite Operators]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to deliver texts, then voice and data, to ordinary phones outside terrestrial coverage within the FCC's emission limits and its carrier partner's spectrum, and whoever lights up coverage the carrier can promise to its customers first, without degrading the terrestrial network next door, takes the carrier partnership.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1B a year in direct-to-device and satellite IoT service revenue today (estimate); the capital at stake is far larger — SpaceX agreed in September 2025 to pay ~$17B for EchoStar's AWS-4 and H-block spectrum for its next direct-to-cell constellation
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~1% of global satellite services revenue today (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** High in the network, low in how the service is explained to users and care agents
**Target Buyer:** Head of satellite partnerships at a mobile carrier; product lead at the satellite operator
**Automation Potential:** High for coverage prediction and capacity steering

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
For decades the person with a phone and no signal — hikers, rural drivers, fishing crews, disaster victims — was served only by dedicated satellite handsets from Iridium, Globalstar and Inmarsat. Direct-to-device (the FCC calls it supplemental coverage from space, framed in March 2024) reaches unmodified phones through a carrier's spectrum: Starlink with T-Mobile, AST SpaceMobile with AT&T and Verizon, Lynk, Skylo for NB-NTN messaging, Apple's emergency service on Globalstar. Capacity per beam is tiny next to a tower, and out-of-band emissions must stay under limits that carriers fight over: the FCC kept −120 dBW/m²/MHz and then gave SpaceX a conditional waiver to −110.6. The carriers' side of these partnerships — spectrum, towers and their own subscribers — is analysed under [[industries/wireless-carriers|Wireless Carriers]], which leaves direct-to-cell service from orbit to this industry.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Satellite RAN and beam-steering software built by each operator, carrier RF planning tools, and handset chipsets. The gaps: telling a customer when and where satellite service will actually work, sharing scarce beam capacity during disasters, and helping care agents explain delayed messages.

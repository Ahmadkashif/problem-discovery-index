# Terrestrial Interference Management Extended to Moving Satellite Beams

**Niche:** [[niches/satellite-operators/direct-to-device-services/profile|Direct-to-Device: Users Beyond the Last Tower]]
**Industry:** [[industries/satellite-operators|Satellite Operators]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Mobile carriers already manage interference between their own cells with planning and self-organising network tools; a satellite beam sweeping across neighbouring carriers' cells is outside anything those tools model.
**Tags:** #convex-optimization #time-series-forecasting #compliance #automation #data-integration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to deliver texts, then voice and data, to ordinary phones outside terrestrial coverage within the FCC's emission limits and its carrier partner's spectrum, and whoever lights up coverage the carrier can promise to its customers first, without degrading the terrestrial network next door, takes the carrier partnership.

## The Problem
The FCC's waiver for SpaceX's higher emission level is conditioned on resolving harmful interference to adjacent-band terrestrial networks. Neighbouring carriers measure their own network for degradation and complain; the satellite operator and its partner must show compliance and respond.

## What Already Exists
Carrier RF planning and optimisation tools (Forsk Atoll, Infovista Planet) and self-organising network software from Ericsson and Nokia that detect and manage interference between terrestrial cells.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs: satellite beams modelled as moving, time-varying sources with known ephemerides; aggregate power-flux-density computation at the ground against the FCC limit and the waiver; correlation of neighbouring carriers' performance counters with beam passes; and a compliance record the satellite operator can file.

## Target Customer
Spectrum engineering teams at direct-to-device operators and their carrier partners.

## Impact If Solved
Interference complaints answered with evidence in days, and the operator keeps the emission headroom that sets its service quality.

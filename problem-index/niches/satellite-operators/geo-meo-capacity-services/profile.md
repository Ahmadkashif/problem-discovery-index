# GEO & MEO Capacity and Managed Services

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/satellite-operators|Satellite Operators]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is that every serious competitor is fighting to keep a fleet of fifteen-year satellites full while capacity prices fall, but writing it out produced two contests with different winners. In video, operators fight to keep broadcasters and pay-TV platforms on established orbital neighbourhoods as channels move to IP, and the incumbent with the most dishes pointed at its slot wins. In enterprise, backhaul and government data, they fight to sell guaranteed throughput across orbits at a price that survives comparison with LEO, and whoever can quote and prove a multi-orbit SLA wins. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$20B a year in global GEO and MEO capacity and managed-service revenue (estimate, against SIA's $105.0B for all satellite services in 2025)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~20% of global satellite services revenue (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — networks and monitoring are digital; contracts, renewals and fleet-fill planning run on spreadsheets
**Target Buyer:** Chief Commercial Officer; VP Fleet and Capacity Planning
**Automation Potential:** Medium — inventory and planning automate well, renewal negotiation does not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
This is the traditional satellite operator business: SES (with Intelsat since July 2025, ~90 GEO and nearly 30 MEO satellites), Eutelsat, Telesat, Viasat's GEO fleet, Hispasat, Arabsat, and regional national operators. They lease transponders and sell managed bandwidth to broadcasters, telcos, enterprises and governments on multi-year contracts. Each satellite is a fixed inventory of beams and transponders with a known end of life, so the commercial problem is filling it and deciding when to replace it. SES's 2025 results show the shape: Networks grew to 62% of revenue while Media revenue fell 12.1% in the first half.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Capacity inventory in operator-built systems and spreadsheets, carrier monitoring from Kratos, billing on telecom BSS platforms, fleet plans in slide decks. The gap is a single view joining each satellite's remaining life, contracted backlog, renewal dates and the demand pipeline, so fill and replacement decisions are made on evidence.

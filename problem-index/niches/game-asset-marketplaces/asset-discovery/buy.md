**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to let a buyer find the right asset among hundreds of thousands without guessing the word the creator happened to type — and whoever solves that takes the account.

## The Problem
Fashion, furniture and interiors retail confronted the identical problem: shoppers know what they want visually and cannot describe it in words that match a product title. The industry responded with visual search, search by photo, style-based recommendation, "more like this" browsing and attribute extraction from product images. It is standard in major retail and measurably improves conversion. Asset marketplaces sell visually-selected goods through a keyword box.

## What Already Exists
Visual similarity search; search by uploaded image; style and attribute extraction from imagery; "more like this" recommendation; and multimodal query understanding.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a 3D asset whose appearance depends on how it is rendered and used. It requires: (1) appearance that varies with lighting, material setup and engine, so a single thumbnail is not the product — this is the substantive difference and means representations must combine geometry with rendered views; (2) technical constraints as first-class search dimensions, since a beautiful asset outside the budget is a failed result; (3) art style consistency across a set of purchases rather than a single item's appeal; (4) audio and code listings alongside visual ones in the same catalogue; and (5) a success measure that is integration and use rather than purchase, because returns and abandonment are common.

## Target Customer
Asset marketplaces, studios buying at volume, visual search vendors, and 3D retrieval providers.

## Impact If Solved
Retail built visual and style search because keywords failed shoppers, and it measurably improved conversion. Appearance that depends on rendering, with technical budgets as search dimensions, is what makes this a different index.

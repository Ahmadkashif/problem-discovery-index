# Support and Lifecycle From Software Vendors

**Niche:** [[niches/game-asset-marketplaces/the-supported-creator/profile|The Supported Creator]]
**Industry:** [[industries/game-asset-marketplaces|Game Asset Marketplaces]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Software vendors formalised supported versions, end-of-life policies and paid support tiers, and asset creators support everything forever for free.
**Tags:** #workflow-orchestration #compliance #automation #worker-facing #revenue-impact #evaluation-metrics #large-language-models #data-integration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to bound the support obligation a creator takes on when they sell an asset once and owe help across every engine version for years — and whoever bounds it takes the account.

## The Problem
Commercial software solved the perpetual support problem with policy rather than technology. Supported version matrices, defined end-of-life dates, long-term support releases, maintenance contracts and paid support tiers exist because vendors learned that a one-time sale cannot fund indefinite support. The norms are universal and buyers accept them. Asset creators sell perpetual licences with implicit perpetual support and no policy at all.

## What Already Exists
Supported version matrices; end-of-life and deprecation policy; long-term support release lines; maintenance and support contracts; and tiered support offerings.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to individual creators selling low-value items to buyers who expect nothing formal. It requires: (1) a seller who is one person with no support function, so any policy must be enforced by the platform rather than by the vendor — this is the substantive difference and it makes the marketplace the necessary actor; (2) a sale price too low to fund even a single support interaction, inverting the software economics; (3) breakage driven by engine publishers on their own schedule rather than by the vendor's release cycle; (4) ratings that penalise non-response, giving the obligation teeth no software vendor faces; and (5) buyers who are individuals or small teams rather than organisations used to support contracts.

## Target Customer
Asset creators, asset marketplaces, engine vendors, and support and lifecycle tooling providers.

## Impact If Solved
Software formalised supported versions and end-of-life because one-time sales cannot fund indefinite support. A seller who is one person means the platform must enforce the policy, which is the change that makes it possible.

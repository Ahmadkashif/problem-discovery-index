# The Engine Updated and the Inbox Filled

**Niche:** [[niches/game-asset-marketplaces/the-supported-creator/profile|The Supported Creator]]
**Industry:** [[industries/game-asset-marketplaces|Game Asset Marketplaces]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A major engine version shipped on Tuesday and by Thursday the creator had sixty messages about assets they sold four years ago.
**Tags:** #worker-facing #quick-win #automation #workflow-orchestration #evaluation-metrics #large-language-models #descriptive-statistics #compliance
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to bound the support obligation a creator takes on when they sell an asset once and owe help across every engine version for years — and whoever bounds it takes the account.

## The Problem
Engine releases are the recurring crisis of this business. A new version ships, changes something in the import pipeline or the rendering path, and every affected asset in every buyer's project breaks simultaneously. The creator finds out from the volume of messages, has no idea which of their assets are affected until they check each one, and spends weeks on unpaid work for sales made years earlier.

## Why It's Still Broken
Nobody tests the catalogue against the new version — a breakage that could be detected the day a release candidate ships is instead discovered by every buyer individually, and the creator absorbs the entire cost of that discovery. Creators have no test infrastructure. The marketplace is not involved. And the engine release schedule is not theirs to manage.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Test before the buyers do and answer once instead of sixty times. Test every listed asset against engine release candidates automatically and tell creators what broke, which is the fix and converts an ambush into a fortnight of warning. Notify affected buyers proactively with a known status rather than leaving them to report it. Publish a per-asset compatibility matrix so buyers can check before contacting anyone. Generate migration guidance from the detected differences, since the diagnosis is the expensive part. Provide a canned response creators can send that actually answers the question. Let creators mark an asset as not yet updated with an expected date, which defuses most of the frustration. Pause review penalties during a known engine-wide breakage window, because punishing creators for a publisher's release is indefensible. Share findings across creators hit by the same change, as the fix is usually identical. Keep older asset versions downloadable for buyers who have not upgraded. And give creators advance notice of engine changes that will affect them, which the platform can obtain and they cannot.

## Who Feels the Pain
Creators doing weeks of unpaid work; buyers with broken projects and no information; marketplaces whose catalogue quality drops after every release; and the creators who stop selling because of it.

## Impact If Fixed
A breakage that could be detected the day a release candidate ships is instead discovered by every buyer individually, and the creator absorbs the entire cost of that discovery. Automatic testing against release candidates turns an ambush into a fortnight of warning.

# Making a Claim Worth Something

**Niche:** [[niches/game-asset-marketplaces/originality-attestation/profile|Originality Attestation]]
**Industry:** [[industries/game-asset-marketplaces|Game Asset Marketplaces]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** The file cannot say how it was made, so the only available evidence is what the creator asserts and nothing makes that assertion cost anything.
**Tags:** #compliance #workflow-orchestration #data-integration #evaluation-metrics #automation #confidence-intervals #sets-and-logic #descriptive-statistics
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to make a creator's claim about how an asset was made worth something, when the file cannot answer the question — and whoever builds that regime takes the account.

## The Problem
A commercial studio buying assets carries real legal exposure over their origin. It needs to know whether the work was original, what it was derived from, whether any generative tooling was involved and on what basis. None of that is recoverable from the asset. The only source is the creator's statement, and the current statement is a checkbox with no evidence behind it, no consequence attached, and no reliance available to the buyer.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Requiring evidence reduces listings and pushes creators to less strict platforms. The legal position on generative derivation is unsettled, so platforms prefer not to establish facts. Creators mostly cannot produce documentation even when honest. And no buyer has yet forced the issue hard enough.

## What to Build
Design the regime so that a claim carries consequence and a buyer can rely on it. Define graduated attestation tiers with escalating evidence requirements rather than one checkbox, which is the core — a single undifferentiated claim serves neither the hobbyist nor the studio, and tiers let both exist. Capture provenance at creation time through tool integrations where possible, since retrospective documentation is the weakest form and forward capture is the strongest. Attach real consequences to a false attestation — delisting, account termination, clawback — because a claim with no downside is not evidence. Record a chain of custody for derived and generatively assisted work, naming sources and basis. Surface the attestation tier to buyers so the market can price it, which is what creates the incentive to attest. Offer an indemnity backed by the tier, as that is what a commercial studio's legal team is actually asking for. Verify a sample rather than everything, which makes the regime affordable and still credible. Keep the evidence retained and retrievable for dispute. Publish enforcement statistics, since an unenforced regime is quickly discounted. And design it so honest creators find it easy and dishonest ones find it expensive, which is the whole test of a regime like this.

## Target Customer
Asset marketplaces, commercial studios with legal exposure, creators seeking a premium position, and compliance and assurance vendors.

## Impact If Built
A claim with no downside attached is not evidence, and it is the entire regime today. Graduated attestation tiers with real consequences and a buyer-facing statement is what lets the market price provenance at all.

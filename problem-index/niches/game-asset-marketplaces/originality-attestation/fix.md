# The Studio's Legal Team Said No

**Niche:** [[niches/game-asset-marketplaces/originality-attestation/profile|Originality Attestation]]
**Industry:** [[industries/game-asset-marketplaces|Game Asset Marketplaces]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The studio wanted to buy, legal asked what the provenance position was, and the honest answer ended the purchase.
**Tags:** #quick-win #compliance #workflow-orchestration #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #data-integration #automation #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to make a creator's claim about how an asset was made worth something, when the file cannot answer the question — and whoever builds that regime takes the account.

## The Problem
Commercial studios increasingly cannot buy marketplace assets. Their legal teams ask what assurance exists about origin, what happens if a rights claim arises, and whether any generative tooling was involved and on what basis. The marketplace's answer is a checkbox and a terms-of-service clause that places the risk entirely on the buyer. The purchase does not happen. This is a growing and entirely commercial loss for the platforms, separate from any legal outcome.

## Why It's Still Broken
The terms allocate risk rather than reduce it — a contract that moves liability to the buyer solves the platform's problem and creates the buyer's, which is why the sale stops rather than the risk. No tier exists for buyers who need more. Nobody has quantified the lost commercial sales. And the hobbyist segment is unaffected, so the pressure is muted.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Create a tier for buyers who need one, rather than changing everything. Offer a verified tier with stronger attestation for creators who want commercial buyers, which is the fix and requires no change to the rest of the catalogue. Publish a plain provenance statement per listing describing what is and is not known, since honesty at a known level beats silence. Provide an indemnity on the verified tier, as that is the specific thing legal teams ask for. Record creator identity properly in that tier, which most platforms do not do at all. Ask for and retain source files and working history from verified creators, which is strong evidence and costs an honest creator nothing. Track how many commercial purchases are lost to this question, which is the number that will fund the programme. Let buyers filter to the verified tier, which is what makes creators want it. Price the tier so creators gain from it rather than pay for it. State clearly what the standard tier does and does not assure, which reduces disputes across the whole catalogue. And start with the highest-value categories rather than the whole catalogue at once.

## Who Feels the Pain
Marketplaces losing commercial sales; studios that want to buy and cannot; creators shut out of the professional market; and legal teams reading terms that assign them everything.

## Impact If Fixed
A contract that moves liability to the buyer solves the platform's problem and creates the buyer's, which is why the sale stops rather than the risk. A verified tier with real attestation and an indemnity is what reopens commercial purchasing.

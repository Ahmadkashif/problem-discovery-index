# Fifteen Conventions Into One

**Niche:** [[niches/game-asset-marketplaces/multi-source-coherence/profile|Multi-Source Asset Coherence]]
**Industry:** [[industries/game-asset-marketplaces|Game Asset Marketplaces]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** The real cost of buying assets is making them belong to the same game, and it falls entirely on one person.
**Tags:** #automation #workflow-orchestration #data-integration #evaluation-metrics #contrastive-learning #descriptive-statistics #worker-facing #feature-engineering
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to make assets from fifteen creators with fifteen conventions look like one game, and whoever automates that work takes the account.

## The Problem
A project assembled from marketplace assets inherits every creator's conventions. Models arrive at different scales with pivots in different places and axes oriented differently. Textures vary in density so surfaces read inconsistently. Materials are authored against different assumptions. Naming and folder structures conflict. Art styles differ in ways that are obvious in a scene and invisible in a thumbnail. One technical artist reconciles all of it by hand, on every asset, on every project.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The marketplace's involvement ends at download and studios have not asked their vendors to solve it. Engine import tools handle format conversion rather than convention reconciliation. The work is skilled and looks inherently manual. And the person doing it is not the person who buys tooling.

## What to Build
Normalise the mechanical conventions, then measure the stylistic ones. Detect and correct scale, pivot and axis conventions automatically against a project standard, which is the core and is the largest block of purely mechanical time. Audit texture density across all sources and flag assets that will read inconsistently, since that mismatch is subtle in isolation and glaring in a scene. Convert materials to the project's pipeline and shading conventions rather than leaving each as authored. Enforce naming and structure conventions on import rather than after. Assess style consistency across a set of assets and flag the outliers, which is the judgement work and is the part that most benefits from a second opinion. Preview assets together in the project's lighting before purchase, as that is when the clash becomes visible and the decision is still reversible. Maintain a project profile that new purchases are checked against automatically. Report the integration time saved, which is how this gets funded in a studio. Handle animation, rig and audio conventions with the same treatment. And keep the artist in control of every stylistic decision while removing every mechanical one.

## Target Customer
Game studios buying assets, technical artists, asset marketplaces wanting post-purchase value, and pipeline tooling vendors.

## Impact If Built
The real cost of buying is reconciling fifteen conventions, and it falls on one person in every project. Automating scale, pivot, material and naming normalisation removes the mechanical half and leaves the judgement.

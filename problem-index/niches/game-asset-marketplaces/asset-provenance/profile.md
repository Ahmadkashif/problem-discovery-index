# Asset Provenance

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/game-asset-marketplaces|Game Asset Marketplaces]]
**Category:** 🔵 High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to establish that a listing is what the seller says it is, when verification currently rests on the creator ticking a box — and whoever can establish it takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$300M US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~20% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Low — self-declared
**Target Buyer:** Trust and legal leadership
**Automation Potential:** High — detection plus a governance regime

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Marketplaces have always dealt with stolen and resold assets. Generative tooling added a harder question — what a model was trained on, and whether a listing is original work, a derivative, or a repackaged scrape. Platforms have adopted disclosure policies of varying strictness and have limited ability to verify any of them. The exposure is legal, commercial and reputational simultaneously, and the whole apparatus currently rests on a self-declaration.

### Contested sub-niches
This niche is not terminal. It names a domain, and two problems with entirely different evidence sit inside it:
- [[niches/game-asset-marketplaces/similarity-detection/profile|🎯 Similarity & Derivation Detection]] — does this match something already in the corpus
- [[niches/game-asset-marketplaces/originality-attestation/profile|🎯 Originality Attestation]] — what a claim is worth when the file cannot answer

## Current Tools & Gaps
A disclosure checkbox, a takedown process and a reporting form. The gaps: no similarity search across the catalogue; no detection of repackaged or rescaled uploads; no verifiable attestation; no training data disclosure with any substance behind it; and no chain of custody for a derived asset.

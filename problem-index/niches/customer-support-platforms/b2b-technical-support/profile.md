# B2B & Technical Support

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/customer-support-platforms|Customer Support Platforms]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in technical support software is fighting to make an escalation carry enough for the next person to diagnose without going back to the customer — and whoever makes escalations self-sufficient takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.9B US technical and business-to-business support software
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~14% of support software revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Low-Medium — the ticketing is modern and the diagnostic process is email and screenshots
**Target Buyer:** Support leaders at software, hardware and technical product companies
**Automation Potential:** High — diagnostic data collection and escalation packaging are mechanical and are done by hand

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Technical support for business customers is a different job from consumer support. The issues are genuinely complex, frequently novel, and require reproduction and diagnosis rather than an answer. The customer is technical and has already tried the obvious things, which means a knowledge base article is the least likely resolution rather than the most. Entitlements and service levels are contractual and differ per customer. And the defining feature is escalation: a case moves from a front-line engineer to a specialist to a product engineering team, and each transition is a point at which the case either carries enough information to be worked or sends someone back to the customer for logs, configuration, versions or a reproduction — which adds days and, in a contractual service level context, jeopardises a commitment. Deflection, which dominates the rest of the category, is largely irrelevant here; escalation quality is everything.

## Current Tools & Gaps
The support platforms serve this market with entitlement management and escalation workflow; developer-focused vendors and specialist technical support tools serve parts of it; observability and log management products hold the diagnostic data. The gaps: diagnostic data collection is a human asking a customer for logs, which is slow, incomplete and repeated at every escalation; the escalation package is assembled manually, so what reaches engineering is a ticket thread rather than a reproduction; engineering's view of support cases is an escalation queue with no aggregate — the same product-signal gap this industry's last niche describes, felt acutely here; and prior cases with the same signature are found by searching, which works if the engineer knows what to search for.

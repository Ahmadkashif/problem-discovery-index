## Why These Niches

Generative answering made the knowledge base load-bearing overnight, and the knowledge base was already stale. That is the category's defining moment: deflection built on a decaying corpus produces confident wrong answers at scale, which is worse than a queue. Detecting which articles have quietly become wrong — before a thousand customers are answered from them — is the contested capability on which the whole repricing of the category rests.

Channels is the larger block by spend and **failed the filter as one niche**. Voice is a synchronous, real-time problem with its own stack, its own vendors and a contest about containment and live assistance under the constraint that the customer is waiting. Messaging and asynchronous support is a resolution-without-a-session problem where the contest is whether an issue closes without the customer having to return. Different technology, different economics, different failure modes. Decomposed below.

B2B technical support and regulated support are the two underdigitised areas, each because deflection is hard for a specific reason: a technical escalation must carry enough to be diagnosable, and a regulated answer must be right in a way that makes automated answering a compliance question. Agents and customers who cannot self-serve are the two underserved constituencies — the agent is measured by two metrics that are respectively gameable and statistically meaningless, and the customer who cannot use the deflection path is routed in circles by a system optimised for the ones who can.

The automation niches are the category's two unclaimed capabilities: a content library where most of the content is dead, and the most direct record of what is wrong with a company's product, used to route tickets.

## Filter Notes

**Niche 2 failed the filter as stated and was decomposed.** "Support channels" groups two businesses with different technology and opposite constraints. Voice is synchronous: the customer is on the line, the contest is containment and real-time agent assistance, the stack is telephony and speech, and the vendors are largely different from the digital ones. Messaging and asynchronous support is the opposite: there is no session, the customer may return hours later, and the contest is whether an issue resolves without them having to come back — a continuity and context problem rather than a real-time one. Both sub-niches are terminal and are written in full.

**Niches 1, 3–8 are terminal as stated.** Each names something testable: detecting a stale article before it is served, whether an escalation carries enough to diagnose, whether a regulated answer can be given with verified accuracy, whether an agent is measured on resolution rather than handle time, whether a customer who cannot self-serve reaches a person quickly, the share of content that is dead, and whether a product organisation acts on a support signal.

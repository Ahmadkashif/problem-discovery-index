**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in regulated support is fighting to let an organisation answer with verified accuracy in a domain where being wrong is a compliance event — and whoever makes an automated answer defensible takes the account.

## The Problem
A regulated support function must be able to demonstrate what was communicated to customers, that required disclosures were given, that advice boundaries were respected, and that the organisation was supervising all of it. It does so through immutable retention and a quality assurance sample of a small percentage of conversations. Financial services solved the surveillance half of this problem a long time ago — lexicon and model-based monitoring of communications at full coverage, with alerting, escalation and case management — and the practice has not crossed into customer support in the adjacent regulated sectors.

## What Already Exists
Communications surveillance and archiving is a mature commercial category serving financial services, with immutable retention, full-coverage monitoring, risk lexicons, model-based detection, alert triage and audit trails. Quality management products for contact centres exist with regulatory alignment. Speech and text analytics are commodity. Every component is available and is deployed at scale one sector over.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to support conversations and to the specific obligations of each sector. It requires: (1) detection tuned to the actual risks in support rather than to trading misconduct — advice given where only information was permitted, a required disclosure omitted, a guarantee implied, a vulnerable customer signal missed, a complaint not recognised as one, each of which is a defined obligation in one or more of these sectors; (2) full coverage rather than sampling, which is now computationally trivial and is the substantive upgrade over current quality assurance practice; (3) complaint identification as a first-class detector, since regulated sectors have complaint handling obligations with clocks and a substantial share of complaints are not recognised as complaints by the agent who received them — this is a well-documented and consequential failure; (4) alert precision managed deliberately, because full-coverage detection with poor precision produces a queue nobody works, which is the failure mode financial surveillance itself has learned from; and (5) the automated channel monitored on the same basis as the human one, which is the connection to the build note and is currently absent everywhere.

## Target Customer
Support functions in financial services, insurance, healthcare and utilities, support platform vendors serving them, and the compliance surveillance vendors for whom this is an adjacent market.

## Impact If Solved
Full-coverage monitoring replaces a small sample with complete oversight at a cost that is now negligible, which is a genuine improvement in supervision rather than a reporting change. Complaint identification is the single highest-value detector, since unrecognised complaints are a common regulatory finding and the obligation clock starts whether or not anyone noticed.

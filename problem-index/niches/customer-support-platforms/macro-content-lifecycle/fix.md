# Nothing Is Ever Retired

**Niche:** [[niches/customer-support-platforms/macro-content-lifecycle/profile|Macro & Content Lifecycle]]
**Industry:** [[industries/customer-support-platforms|Customer Support Platforms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Support content is created continuously and retired never, because removing something might break a workflow nobody can identify, so every library grows until agents trust only the few items they know.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #hypothesis-testing #confidence-intervals #workflow-orchestration #automation #quick-win #compliance
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in support content is fighting to keep a library alive rather than merely large — and whoever can retire dead content with evidence takes the knowledge function.

## The Problem
A knowledge manager inherits eight hundred macros. Around forty are in regular use. The rest were created by people who have left, for products that have changed, in response to situations that no longer arise. Deleting any of them carries a small risk that somebody depended on it and a certain amount of work to establish who; keeping them costs nothing visible. So the library grows, agents develop personal shortlists, new agents are overwhelmed, search becomes useless, and the generative layer retrieves from all of it. The same monotonic accumulation appears throughout this vault, and here it has become actively harmful rather than merely untidy.

## Why It's Still Broken
The asymmetry is complete: the cost of a wrong deletion is attributable and immediate, and the cost of retention is diffuse and invisible. Establishing that an item is dead requires evidence nobody has assembled. And the knowledge function is under-resourced everywhere, so a pruning exercise competes with creating the content people are currently asking for and always loses.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Make retirement evidence-based and reversible. Every content item carries usage, outcome and last-used data, which is already recorded. An item unused for a defined period is a retirement candidate, and the evidence is unambiguous. Retirement is staged rather than deleted: an item is archived, remains retrievable, and is monitored — if anyone looks for it, it comes back, and after a further period with no demand it is removed. That staging removes the asymmetry entirely, because a wrong retirement is now a recoverable inconvenience rather than a loss. Exclude the items that are rare by design — a compliance disclosure used twice a year is not dead — by marking them explicitly, which is a small deliberate act rather than a default. Report library health continuously: proportion live, proportion archived, concentration of usage, and the count of items with no owner. And exclude archived content from generative retrieval immediately, which is the fastest available improvement to answer quality and requires no deletion decision at all.

## Who Feels the Pain
Knowledge managers with a library they did not create and cannot prune; agents searching a corpus where most results are wrong; new agents trained on a dead canon; and every customer answered from stale content by a retrieval layer that cannot tell the difference.

## Impact If Fixed
Staged, reversible retirement removes the risk that has prevented pruning and lets a library be cut substantially in one exercise. Excluding archived content from generative retrieval is the immediate and largest effect, since it improves answer quality the same day without anyone having to decide what to delete.

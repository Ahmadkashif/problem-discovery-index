# Macro & Content Lifecycle

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/customer-support-platforms|Customer Support Platforms]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in support content is fighting to keep a library alive rather than merely large — and whoever can retire dead content with evidence takes the knowledge function.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$620M US spend attributable to knowledge and content management within support
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~4% of support software revenue, gating the quality of everything built on it
**Digital Adoption:** Low — content is created continuously and retired essentially never
**Target Buyer:** Knowledge managers and support operations leaders
**Automation Potential:** Very High — usage and outcome data per content item is fully recorded and unexamined

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Support content libraries grow monotonically. A mature organisation has several hundred macros and canned responses, of which agents use a few dozen; the rest are outdated, duplicated, superseded or specific to a product that no longer exists. The knowledge base has the same shape. Nothing is ever retired, because removing something might break a workflow somebody depends on and nobody can establish who that is — the same asymmetry that produces unpruned claim edit libraries, unused CRM required fields and unmaintainable routing trees elsewhere in this vault. The consequences here are specific and now more serious: agents search a library where most results are wrong and learn to use the handful they trust, new agents are trained on a corpus that is largely dead, and the generative answering layer retrieves from all of it indiscriminately — which means dead content has been promoted from clutter to an active source of wrong answers.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Every platform provides macro and article management with folders, tags and permissions; some provide usage counts. The gaps: usage is counted and outcomes are not, so a macro used a thousand times with a poor resolution rate looks better than one used twenty times well; duplication is not detected, so the same answer exists in nine variants with slight differences and agents pick arbitrarily; retirement has no evidence base, so it does not happen; ownership decays as authors leave; and nothing measures the proportion of the library that is actually live, which is the number that would make the problem visible.

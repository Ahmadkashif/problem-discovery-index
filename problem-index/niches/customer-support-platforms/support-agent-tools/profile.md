# Support Agent Tools

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/customer-support-platforms|Customer Support Platforms]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor building for support agents is fighting to measure and support whether the customer's problem was actually solved rather than how long it took — and whoever the agents experience as help rather than surveillance takes the deployment.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.7B US spend attributable to agent workspace, assistance and performance tooling
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~12% of support software revenue, against the workforce that does the work
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — agents have tools and are managed by metrics those tools generate
**Target Buyer:** Support leadership buys; agents live inside it for eight hours a day
**Automation Potential:** Very High — context assembly, drafting and after-work are all mechanical

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Support agents are managed by two metrics: average handle time, which is gameable and which rewards ending conversations rather than resolving them, and customer satisfaction, which at individual agent volumes is statistically meaningless — a handful of responses per month, heavily biased toward the extremes, treated as a performance measure. Both are well known to be inadequate and both persist because they are easy to compute and have always been there. The job they produce is one in which an agent is rewarded for speed and for the mood of whoever responded to a survey, in a role with high turnover, frequent exposure to distressed and sometimes abusive customers, and increasingly the experience of being the fallback behind an automated layer that handles the simple questions — which means the human queue is now disproportionately the difficult, the complex and the upset. Generative deflection has made the agent's job harder while the measurement of it has not changed at all.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Every platform provides an agent workspace with ticket context, macros, knowledge search and increasingly generative drafting; quality assurance and workforce management products surround it. The gaps: resolution — whether the customer's problem was solved — is not measured, so it cannot be managed or rewarded; the context an agent needs is assembled by opening tabs; after-conversation work is manual summarisation and categorisation; the emotional load of the role is unaddressed by any product despite being the dominant driver of attrition; and agents receive almost nothing back from the systems that observe them, which is the same pattern as the sales representative and the field technician elsewhere in this vault.

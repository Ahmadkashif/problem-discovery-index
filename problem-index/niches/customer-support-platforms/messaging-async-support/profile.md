# Messaging & Asynchronous Support

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/customer-support-platforms|Customer Support Platforms]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in asynchronous support is fighting to close an issue without the customer having to come back — and whoever raises first-contact resolution in a channel with no contact takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.9B US messaging, email and asynchronous support technology
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~14% of support software revenue
**Digital Adoption:** High — asynchronous channels dominate volume in most consumer businesses
**Target Buyer:** Support operations leaders; the platform vendors' core product
**Automation Potential:** Very High — the absence of time pressure is the single biggest advantage and is barely used

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Asynchronous support has a property nothing else in the category has: time. A customer writes and leaves. The organisation has minutes or hours rather than seconds, which means it can retrieve, verify, check the customer's account state, consult a specialist and compose a complete answer — and the industry has largely used that time to queue rather than to think. The contest is round trips: whether the issue closes on the first reply or requires the customer to come back with more information, which is the asynchronous equivalent of first-contact resolution and is the thing customers actually experience as good or bad support. Every additional round trip adds hours or days of elapsed time from the customer's perspective, which is why a two-exchange resolution feels dramatically worse than a single complete reply. The failure mode is a reply that asks for information the organisation could have looked up.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Zendesk, Intercom, Freshdesk, Front and Gorgias serve this channel as their core product, with routing, macros, collision detection and increasingly generative drafting. The gaps: replies are drafted without systematically checking what the organisation already knows about this customer, so the most common reply is a request for information; round trips per issue is not a reported metric anywhere, despite being the property that determines the customer's experience; asynchronous conversations are measured on first response time, which rewards a fast incomplete reply over a slower complete one and is precisely the wrong incentive in a channel with time; and the queue is worked in arrival order rather than by what is actually resolvable now.

## Why These Niches

Insurance DRP and specialty restoration represent the two dominant revenue models in collision repair with fundamentally different workflows. Rural shops and PDR operators remain digitally neglected despite viable addressable markets. Rideshare drivers and commercial vehicle operators are underserved audiences with distinct urgency and documentation needs. Supplement negotiation and photo-based estimating are the two most rule-heavy, automatable workflows in the industry — both currently consuming hours of skilled labor on pattern-matching tasks.

## Why These Pockets

This is the richest industry in the sweep so far, and the reason is structural: collision repair is an argument between two parties about money, conducted entirely through documents that a third party publishes. Every estimate in the country is written against a database somebody researched; every total loss is settled by a valuation report somebody produced; every OEM-compliant repair follows a procedure somebody structured. All three qualifiers sit in that referee position, all three sell the document as the product, and all three are the largest employers of researchers anywhere in the value chain — the estimating providers alone run research organizations several hundred people deep.

The common gap across all three is the same and unusually stark. Each holds a transaction platform that observes what happened after its research was published — supplements against estimates, challenges against valuations, search abandonment against procedures — and in each case that outcome data is organizationally severed from the research function that would use it. Estimating providers carry a large share of US collision claims end to end and set labor times by study rather than by observed outcome. Valuation providers absorb revisions without feeding them back into comparable selection. Procedure publishers log millions of failed technician searches and plan content by OEM release calendar instead. The pattern is consistent enough to be the industry's defining opportunity.

Ten pockets logged without qualifying. Two of them hold data as good as anything in the sweep and are disqualified by what they invoice for: paint manufacturers hold millions of field spectrophotometer readings mapping how every OEM colour actually drifts, given away to sell paint; the insurance loss research institutes pool loss experience across nearly every US auto insurer and are constituted specifically not to sell anything. MSO consolidators hold repair-level data across a thousand-plus locations serving a purely internal decision. And the gate-failures are the reverse case — diminished value appraisers are the purest insight-as-invoice shape in the industry, operating one person at a time.

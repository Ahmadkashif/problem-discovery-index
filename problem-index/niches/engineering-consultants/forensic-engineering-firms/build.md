# Challenge Outcomes as a Methodology Record

**Niche:** [[niches/engineering-consultants/forensic-engineering-firms/profile|Forensic Engineering Firms]]
**Industry:** [[industries/engineering-consultants|Engineering Consultants]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** The firm's opinions are attacked in deposition and at admissibility hearings across thousands of matters, and the record of which methodologies survived reaches only the expert who was on the stand.

## The Problem
A forensic opinion's value is that it holds up. Opposing counsel tests the methodology, the sampling, the assumptions, and the literature relied on, and courts rule on admissibility. Those outcomes are the only real measure of the firm's technical quality and they are dispersed — each one known to the expert involved, the engagement partner, and nobody else. The firm cannot say which methodologies have been successfully challenged, in which jurisdictions, on what grounds, or whether a proposed approach in a new matter resembles one that has been excluded before. In a practice where an excluded expert can lose a case and a reputation, the institutional memory of what survives is anecdotal.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Matters are managed individually and closed, with the outcome recorded as a commercial result rather than a technical one. Litigation outcomes also arrive slowly and unevenly, and much of the useful detail sits in transcripts and orders the firm holds but does not index. There is a defensive instinct as well: a systematic record of challenges is discoverable, and counsel would rather it did not exist — which is short-sighted, since the alternative is experts unable to know what their own firm has learned.

## What to Build
A methodology record capturing, per matter, the analytical approach used, the assumptions and literature relied on, and the challenge outcome at whatever granularity is available — accepted without challenge, challenged and admitted, limited, or excluded, with the stated ground. Built from materials the firm already receives. Across the practice, that answers questions nobody can answer today: which approaches attract challenge, which grounds succeed, how it varies by jurisdiction and subject matter, and whether a proposed methodology in a new engagement resembles one previously excluded — surfaced before the report is written rather than after. It also makes expertise transferable, so a junior expert inherits the firm's accumulated understanding of what survives cross-examination rather than acquiring it one deposition at a time.

## Target Customer
Practice leaders and managing directors at forensic engineering firms running 100-1,000 technical staff, and the counsel who retain them and currently assess methodological risk from an expert's personal reputation.

## Impact If Built
Converts scattered courtroom experience into institutional capital in a practice where methodological defensibility is the entire product. It is the same structural fix that forensic accounting needs and, as there, the record can only be assembled by the firm that generated it.

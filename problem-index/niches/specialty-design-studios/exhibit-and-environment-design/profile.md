# Exhibit & Brand Environment Design

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/specialty-design-studios|Specialty Design Studios]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is that every serious competitor is fighting to turn design intent into fabrication drawings a builder can execute without surprises — but for trade show programmes the surprise that costs money is on the show floor, show after show, and for museum exhibitions it is the fabrication bid and change orders on a one-off build. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.1B US design fees for trade show, corporate, museum and themed exhibits (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~24% of specialty design fees (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — 3D modelling and rendering are standard; rules, labour and property tracking are manual
**Target Buyer:** Exhibit house Design Director; Corporate Exhibit Manager; museum Exhibitions Director
**Automation Potential:** Medium — detailing, rules checking and estimating automate; concept and interpretation do not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Exhibit designers create three-dimensional environments — trade show booths, corporate showrooms, museum galleries, brand activations — and hand fabrication drawings to a builder, which is often their own exhibit house's shop. The design must be structurally buildable, shippable in crates, installable by a crew in a fixed window, and compliant with fire and venue rules. A custom booth for a 30x50 space can cost $150,000+ and travel to about twenty shows; a museum gallery is designed once and must last a decade.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Vectorworks, SketchUp, AutoCAD and Rhino for design and detailing; V-Ray or Twinmotion for renderings; job costing in an exhibit-house ERP or QuickBooks. Gaps: drawings disconnected from bills of materials and graphics files; no record linking design choices to install labour or fabrication cost.

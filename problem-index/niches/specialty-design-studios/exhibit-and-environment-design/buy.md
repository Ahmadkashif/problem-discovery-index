**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is that every serious competitor is fighting to turn design intent into fabrication drawings a builder can execute without surprises — but for trade show programmes the surprise that costs money is on the show floor, show after show, and for museum exhibitions it is the fabrication bid and change orders on a one-off build. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## The Problem
An exhibit touches many trades — millwork, metal, graphics, lighting, AV, electrical, rigging — and the venue's or building's own constraints. Conflicts between them surface late: a monitor bracket that hits a structural member, a hanging sign that clashes with the venue's lighting grid, a case that blocks an egress route.

## What Already Exists
Construction BIM coordination tools — Autodesk Navisworks and Revit, Solibri and similar — run clash detection, model federation and issue tracking across trades.

## The Customization Gap
Exhibits need: lightweight models from Vectorworks and SketchUp rather than Revit; venue and hall models (ceiling heights, rigging points, utility floor boxes) as the base layer; exhibit-specific clash rules (sightlines, hanging-sign clearances, egress widths at show density); and issue tracking that ends at the install, not at a building handover.

## Target Customer
Exhibit houses and museum exhibit firms running multi-trade builds of ~$250,000 and up (estimate).

## Impact If Solved
Clashes found in the model instead of on the floor or in the gallery, with fewer change orders and on-site fixes.

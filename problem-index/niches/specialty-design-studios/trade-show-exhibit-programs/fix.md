# The Labour Estimate Nobody Checks Against the Invoice

**Niche:** [[niches/specialty-design-studios/trade-show-exhibit-programs/profile|Trade Show Exhibit Programmes]]
**Industry:** [[industries/specialty-design-studios|Specialty Design Studios]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Installation labour is quoted before every show and billed after it, and the two numbers are never compared, so the estimate never improves.
**Tags:** #linear-regression #evaluation-metrics #feature-engineering #quick-win #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to deliver a client's whole show calendar at the lowest total cost per show — refurbishment, shipping, drayage, storage and install labour — while the exhibit still looks new, and whoever predicts and controls those floor costs best keeps the programme.

## The Problem
Before a show, the exhibit house estimates installation and dismantle hours from experience. After the show, actual hours arrive on a contractor's invoice, sometimes weeks later and addressed to the exhibitor. The overrun is paid and forgotten; the next show's estimate is made the same way.

## Why It's Still Broken
The estimate and the invoice sit with different parties and in different formats, and nobody owns post-show reconciliation.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A simple post-show loop: capture estimated and actual hours per show, per phase and per crew; attribute variance to venue, crew size, booth configuration and on-site changes; and feed a corrected rate back into the next estimate.

## Who Feels the Pain
Exhibit managers explaining overruns; account managers losing trust; designers whose choices are blamed without evidence.

## Impact If Fixed
Within a season, estimates calibrated to the house's own installs, at almost no cost beyond capturing two numbers per show.

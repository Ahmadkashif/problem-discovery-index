# Set, Window & Retail Display Design

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/specialty-design-studios|Specialty Design Studios]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn a concept into drawings that a fabricator can build and a crew can install right first time on a fixed date — across two hundred store windows or one shoot day — and whoever makes the design measurement-true to sites they cannot rehearse takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$400M US design fees for retail window and in-store display programmes, commercial and photo sets, and event sets (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~9% of specialty design fees (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — site surveys by tape measure and phone photo, foam-board and hand-built models, fabricator shops working from marked-up PDFs
**Target Buyer:** Retail Visual Merchandising Director; commercial Production Designer
**Automation Potential:** Medium — site capture and variant drawings automate; concept does not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Window and display designers create seasonal windows and in-store fixtures that roll out to dozens or hundreds of stores whose windows all differ in size; set designers for commercials, photography and events create one-off builds for a single day. In both, the design must fit spaces the designer has measured once, or not at all, and installation happens overnight or on the day, with no second chance. Fabrication is done by scenic and display shops that work from drawings and physical models.

## Current Tools & Gaps
SketchUp, Vectorworks and AutoCAD for drawings; Photoshop for concept boards; store window dimensions in spreadsheets; photo-audit apps for compliance after installation. Gaps: store-by-store variants drawn by hand; site measurements unreliable; no link between the master design and each store's installed reality.

# The Site Survey Done With a Tape Measure

**Niche:** [[niches/specialty-design-studios/set-window-and-display-design/profile|Set, Window & Retail Display Design]]
**Industry:** [[industries/specialty-design-studios|Specialty Design Studios]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A display is built to measurements taken once with a tape measure and a phone photo, and the build arrives a few inches too wide for the window.
**Tags:** #cnns #semantic-segmentation #quick-win #automation #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn a concept into drawings that a fabricator can build and a crew can install right first time on a fixed date — across two hundred store windows or one shoot day — and whoever makes the design measurement-true to sites they cannot rehearse takes the account.

## The Problem
Site surveys are done by whoever is available — a store manager, a junior designer — with a tape measure, a sketch and some photos. Obstructions such as sprinkler heads, glazing bars, security sensors and uneven floors are missed or mis-measured.

## Why It's Still Broken
Survey is treated as a chore, not a deliverable, and the tools for accurate capture have only recently reached ordinary phones.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A guided phone capture using depth sensing that produces a dimensioned model of the window or set space, with obstructions segmented and labelled, checked for completeness before the surveyor leaves.

## Who Feels the Pain
Fabricators rebuilding pieces; installers cutting on site; designers explaining why the window does not match the concept.

## Impact If Fixed
Measurement errors, the most common cause of display rework, removed at the point of capture.

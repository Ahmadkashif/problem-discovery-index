# One Record From Exhibition Script to Fabricated Label

**Industry:** [[industries/specialty-design-studios|Specialty Design Studios]]

**One-liner:** An exhibition is hundreds of objects, images, labels and interactives that each pass through script, design, rights clearance, conservation review and fabrication, and every one is tracked in a different spreadsheet.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #workflow-orchestration #data-integration #automation #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to hand a fabricator a drawing, object and content package complete enough to bid and build within a fixed, often grant-funded budget, and whoever brings the exhibition in on budget and open on the announced date wins the next commission.

## The Problem
A label written by a curator is edited by an educator, shortened by a designer to fit a panel, checked against the object record, and approved by a director; an image needs rights clearance and a high-resolution file; an object needs a mount, a case and light limits. When one item stalls, the fabrication package goes out incomplete, and the gap becomes a change order.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Museum software is built around collections records, and design software around drawings. The work between them — content and object tracking through design — is small per project and has been handled by project managers with spreadsheets.

## What to Build
A project record keyed to the exhibition script: each element (object, label, image, interactive, media piece) carries its status across content, rights, conservation and design, links to its position on the drawings, and generates label copy fitted to panel dimensions and reading-level targets. Language models draft label variants to length and flag text that drifts from the object record.

## Target Customer
Project managers at interpretive exhibit design firms and in-house museum exhibition departments.

## Impact If Built
Complete fabrication packages, fewer change orders, and less of the final month spent chasing approvals.

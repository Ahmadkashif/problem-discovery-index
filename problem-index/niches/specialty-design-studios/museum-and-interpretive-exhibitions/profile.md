# Museum & Interpretive Exhibitions

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/specialty-design-studios|Specialty Design Studios]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to hand a fabricator a drawing, object and content package complete enough to bid and build within a fixed, often grant-funded budget, and whoever brings the exhibition in on budget and open on the announced date wins the next commission.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$300M US design fees for museum, visitor-centre, science-centre and interpretive exhibits (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~7% of specialty design fees (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium-Low — drawings are digital; object lists, content and approvals run on spreadsheets and Word documents
**Target Buyer:** Museum Director of Exhibitions; National Park Service and visitor-centre project managers
**Automation Potential:** Medium — tracking, cost modelling and label production automate; interpretation does not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Interpretive exhibit design moves from an interpretive plan and exhibition script to schematic design, design development and a construction-document package that independent fabricators bid on. The designer coordinates curators, conservators, educators, media producers and the fabricator, and tracks every object, image, label and interactive through the process. Budgets are fixed early, frequently by grants or capital campaigns, and the opening date is announced months in advance.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Vectorworks, SketchUp and AutoCAD for drawings; collections management systems such as TMS or Axiell on the museum side; Excel object lists; Word scripts and label copy; email approvals. Gaps: no single record from script line to object to label to drawing; fabrication cost known only when bids arrive.

# Collections Management Extended Into Exhibit Design

**Niche:** [[niches/specialty-design-studios/museum-and-interpretive-exhibitions/profile|Museum & Interpretive Exhibitions]]
**Industry:** [[industries/specialty-design-studios|Specialty Design Studios]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** The museum's collections system already holds every object's dimensions, condition and loan terms; the exhibit designer retypes them into a spreadsheet.
**Tags:** #data-integration #feature-engineering #workflow-orchestration #compliance #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to hand a fabricator a drawing, object and content package complete enough to bid and build within a fixed, often grant-funded budget, and whoever brings the exhibition in on budget and open on the announced date wins the next commission.

## The Problem
Object dimensions, weights, light and humidity limits, mount requirements and lender conditions drive case design and layout. Designers receive them as exports or PDFs, and changes in the collections record after export never reach the drawings.

## What Already Exists
Collections management systems such as TMS (Gallery Systems) and Axiell hold object records, loans and conservation notes; some offer exhibition modules for object lists.

## The Customization Gap
Exhibit design needs: a live link from object records to positions on the drawings; case assignment with environmental compatibility checks (light, relative humidity, off-gassing materials); mount specifications per object; lender requirements surfaced as design constraints; and change alerts when a record changes after design freeze.

## Target Customer
Registrars and exhibition designers at museums with active temporary-exhibition programmes, and the firms they hire.

## Impact If Solved
Fewer layout reworks when an object changes, and conservation requirements designed in rather than discovered at installation.

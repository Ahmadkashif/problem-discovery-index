# The Fabrication Bid That Arrives Over Budget

**Niche:** [[niches/specialty-design-studios/museum-and-interpretive-exhibitions/profile|Museum & Interpretive Exhibitions]]
**Industry:** [[industries/specialty-design-studios|Specialty Design Studios]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The design is developed for months against a budget, and the first real cost signal is the fabricators' bids, when it is too late to redesign without missing the opening.
**Tags:** #gradient-boosting #linear-regression #confidence-intervals #feature-engineering #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to hand a fabricator a drawing, object and content package complete enough to bid and build within a fixed, often grant-funded budget, and whoever brings the exhibition in on budget and open on the announced date wins the next commission.

## The Problem
Exhibit designers estimate fabrication cost at schematic design from experience and per-square-foot rules of thumb. When bids come in over, the response is value engineering under deadline: cutting interactives, simplifying casework, deleting media — usually the parts the interpretive plan depended on.

## Why It's Still Broken
Firms hold past bids and drawings but have never joined them into a cost model; each project is estimated afresh by the most experienced person available.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A cost model built from the firm's past fabrication bids, keyed to element types (casework, graphics, interactives, media, lighting, mounts) and quantities extracted from schematic drawings, giving a range at each design phase and showing which elements drive the variance.

## Who Feels the Pain
Exhibition directors who must find money or cut content; designers forced to value-engineer their own work; fabricators asked to re-bid.

## Impact If Fixed
Cost surprises caught at schematic design, when cutting scope is a design decision rather than a crisis.

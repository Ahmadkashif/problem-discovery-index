# A Development Path Priced and Sequenced for a First Order

**Niche:** [[niches/specialty-design-studios/first-collection-founders/profile|First-Collection Brand Founders]]
**Industry:** [[industries/specialty-design-studios|Specialty Design Studios]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A first-time founder designs ten styles, spends their sampling budget on round two, and learns the factory's minimum per colour after the money is gone.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #k-nearest-neighbors #gradient-boosting #feature-engineering #workflow-orchestration #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get a founder with no product development experience from a mood board to a first production run at factory minimums they can afford, and whoever makes that first order arrive right takes the account and the founder's next collections.

## The Problem
Founders make decisions in the wrong order: they design a collection, then commission tech packs, then look for a factory, then discover minimum order quantities per style and colour, fabric minimums, sample charges and lead times that make the plan unaffordable. Each mistake costs a round and a month.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Studios guide founders by hand and charge for it; PLM vendors assume the user already knows the process; sourcing marketplaces match factories but do not plan development.

## What to Build
A planning assistant that starts from the founder's budget, launch date and categories; estimates sampling rounds and costs per style from comparable styles; matches factories by category, minimums and sample policy; narrows the collection to what the budget can carry; and sequences the steps — fabric sourcing, tech packs, protos, fit, pre-production — with the decisions each step needs from the founder.

## Target Customer
Pre-launch founders, sold directly or through studios that want to qualify and onboard founders efficiently.

## Impact If Built
Founders reach a first production order with fewer wasted rounds, and studios spend their time on design rather than on teaching the process.

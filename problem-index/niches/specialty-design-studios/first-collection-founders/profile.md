# First-Collection Brand Founders

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/specialty-design-studios|Specialty Design Studios]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get a founder with no product development experience from a mood board to a first production run at factory minimums they can afford, and whoever makes that first order arrive right takes the account and the founder's next collections.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$250M US design and development fees paid by pre-launch and first-season apparel and accessory brands (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~5% of specialty design fees (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — Instagram references, Google Docs and WhatsApp with a factory found online
**Target Buyer:** Founder
**Automation Potential:** High for guidance, documentation and factory matching

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Thousands of people start apparel and accessory brands each year, many through direct-to-consumer channels covered under [[industries/d2c-brand-operators|D2C Brand Operators]]. Before launch they need what a studio provides — designs, tech packs, patterns, samples, factory introductions — without knowing what any of those are, with a few thousand to a few tens of thousands of dollars, and at order sizes factories find unattractive. They are the studios' most numerous and least profitable clients: the most hand-holding, the most scope creep, the highest risk of non-payment.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Freelancer marketplaces, studio packages sold per tech pack, lightweight PLM tiers such as Backbone Lite and Techpacker, sourcing marketplaces and trade shows. Gaps: no tool that explains the process as it runs; sampling budgets set without knowing typical round counts; factory minimums discovered after design.

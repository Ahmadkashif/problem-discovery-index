# Grading & Spec Sheet Production

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/specialty-design-studios|Specialty Design Studios]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to produce a complete, graded, tolerance-correct spec and bill of materials from a sketch and a base block in minutes rather than hours, and whoever gets round-one factory questions closest to zero takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$300M US fees for tech pack assembly, measurement specs, grading and BOM preparation (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~7% of specialty design fees (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — done digitally but by re-keying between CAD, Illustrator and spreadsheets
**Target Buyer:** Head of Technical Design
**Automation Potential:** High — rule-bound, repetitive and checkable

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Every style needs a measurement spec with points of measure, tolerances and a grade across the size range; a bill of materials listing fabric, trims, labels and packaging; construction callouts; and labelling details (fiber content, country of origin, RN and care instructions required under FTC rules, plus tracking labels for children's products). Most of it is mechanical once the base block and size chart are known. It is the work studios most often price per tech pack and the work most often outsourced to lower-cost offshore technical design teams.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Grade rules in Gerber AccuMark, Lectra Modaris or Optitex; spec tables in Excel or PLM; flats in Illustrator; BOM libraries in PLM. Gaps: grading in CAD and spec tables in PLM maintained separately; tolerances inconsistent; labelling details re-typed per style.

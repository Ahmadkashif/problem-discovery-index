# The Failure-to-Supply Penalty Found on the Invoice

**Niche:** [[niches/pharmaceutical-manufacturers/mature-generic-supply-contracts/profile|Mature Generic Supply & Shortage Exposure]]
**Industry:** [[industries/pharmaceutical-manufacturers|Pharmaceutical Manufacturers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Wholesalers deduct failure-to-supply penalties from payments, and the maker learns which short shipments cost what only when finance reconciles the remittance.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #descriptive-statistics #data-integration #automation #quick-win #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep shipping a wholesaler's or GPO's awarded generic at an eroded price, and whoever ships the highest share of awarded volume in full takes the account.

## The Problem
Contract terms with wholesalers and GPOs specify penalties when awarded product is not supplied. The deductions arrive netted against invoices, coded inconsistently, weeks after the short shipment. Supply planners never see which shortfalls were expensive.

## Why It's Still Broken
Contracts, shipments and remittances sit in different systems owned by commercial, supply and finance teams, and penalties are treated as a finance reconciliation, not a supply signal.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Parse contract penalty terms and remittance deductions, match each deduction to the order and shortfall that caused it, dispute the invalid ones, and feed penalty cost per molecule back to planning so allocation favours the contracts where a short costs most.

## Who Feels the Pain
Finance teams reconciling deductions; planners allocating scarce batches blind to penalty exposure; commercial teams losing awards after repeated shorts.

## Impact If Fixed
Matching and disputing deductions could recover 20–40% of invalid penalties and redirects scarce supply to the costliest contracts, worth hundreds of thousands to millions a year at a mid-size maker (estimate).

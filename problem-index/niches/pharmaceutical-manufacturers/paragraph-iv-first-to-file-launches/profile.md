# Paragraph IV & First-to-File Launches

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/pharmaceutical-manufacturers|Pharmaceutical Manufacturers]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to file a substantially complete Paragraph IV ANDA first and clear the brand's patents, and whoever converts the most first-to-file positions into day-one launches takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.5B a year in patent analytics, Hatch-Waxman litigation, bioequivalence and CMC work on first-to-file programmes in the US (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~1% of US receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — dockets and patent data are digital; filing readiness and litigation strategy are tracked in spreadsheets and outside counsel's memos
**Target Buyer:** VP Intellectual Property & Litigation / Head of Portfolio at a generic maker
**Automation Potential:** Medium — tracking and document checks automate; claim construction and settlement strategy do not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The money in a generic molecule concentrates at launch. The first applicant with a substantially complete ANDA carrying a Paragraph IV certification is generally eligible for 180 days of exclusivity; if the brand sues within 45 days of notice, approval is generally stayed for 30 months unless the patent case resolves sooner (FDA). The contest is legal and regulatory speed: being ready to file on the first possible day, surviving FDA's completeness review, and winning or settling the patent case in time to launch. The firms competing are generic makers, their patent counsel and scientific experts, and the CMC and bioequivalence vendors that make the filing complete.

## Current Tools & Gaps
FDA's Paragraph IV Certifications List, the Orange Book, Cortellis and DrugPatentWatch for patent and filer data; Lex Machina and Docket Navigator for litigation analytics; outside counsel for strategy. Gaps: no system tracks a first-to-file position's exposure to forfeiture events, filing-completeness risk and litigation calendar together, and settlement terms across the industry are visible only in fragments.

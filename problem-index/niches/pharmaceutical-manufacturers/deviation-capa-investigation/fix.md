# Human Error, Retrain, Close

**Niche:** [[niches/pharmaceutical-manufacturers/deviation-capa-investigation/profile|Deviation & CAPA Investigation]]
**Industry:** [[industries/pharmaceutical-manufacturers|Pharmaceutical Manufacturers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** "Human error" is the easiest root cause to close on the deadline, and the retraining CAPA attached to it rarely stops the failure.
**Tags:** #bert #large-language-models #descriptive-statistics #compliance #quick-win #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to close each deviation with a root cause that holds, and whoever leaves the fewest repeat deviations from causes already declared fixed takes the account.

## The Problem
Under a closure target of ~30 days (estimate), an investigator who cannot quickly find a technical cause writes "operator did not follow procedure" and retrains the operator. FDA regularly criticises firms for attributing failures to human error without looking at why the error happened, and the same step produces the same error months later.

## Why It's Still Broken
Closure rate is what QA is measured on. The QMS accepts any root-cause category, and nothing shows that the same step has produced repeated human-error findings across operators, which would point to the procedure or the equipment.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A check at closure: when the root cause is human error, show how many times this step, procedure and equipment have produced human-error deviations, across how many operators, and require the investigator to address the procedure or equipment if the count crosses a threshold.

## Who Feels the Pain
Operators retrained for failures the process design caused; QA investigators reopening the same problem; site heads facing repeat 483 observations.

## Impact If Fixed
If a quarter of human-error closures pointed instead to a procedure or equipment fix (estimate), the repeat deviations attached to them would fall, and so would retraining hours that change nothing.

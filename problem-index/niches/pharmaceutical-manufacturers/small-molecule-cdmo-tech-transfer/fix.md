# The Tech-Transfer Package That Arrives as a Pile of PDFs

**Niche:** [[niches/pharmaceutical-manufacturers/small-molecule-cdmo-tech-transfer/profile|Small-Molecule CDMO Tech Transfer & Commercial Supply]]
**Industry:** [[industries/pharmaceutical-manufacturers|Pharmaceutical Manufacturers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The sponsor sends development reports, batch records and methods as a pile of documents, and CDMO engineers spend the first weeks finding the parameters that matter.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #feature-engineering #automation #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to bring a sponsor's process into its plant and through validation, and whoever passes the highest share of process performance qualification batches first time takes the account.

## The Problem
A tech-transfer package mixes development reports, executed batch records from the sponsor's site, analytical methods, specifications and risk assessments. Engineers read it to extract process parameters, ranges, equipment settings and known problems, then rebuild them in their own templates.

## Why It's Still Broken
There is no shared data standard for transfer packages, sponsors use their own templates, and nobody owns the extraction step.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Document models that extract parameters, ranges, equipment and in-process controls into a structured transfer record with links to the source page, and flag gaps and conflicts between documents before the kickoff meeting.

## Who Feels the Pain
MSAT engineers at CDMOs; sponsor project managers waiting on the gap assessment; quality reviewers checking transcriptions.

## Impact If Fixed
Cutting extraction from weeks to days (estimate) shortens every transfer's start and removes transcription errors that surface later as deviations.

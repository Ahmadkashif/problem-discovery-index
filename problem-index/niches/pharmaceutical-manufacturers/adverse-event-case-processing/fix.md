# The Literature Case Found Late in the Window

**Niche:** [[niches/pharmaceutical-manufacturers/adverse-event-case-processing/profile|Adverse Event Case Intake & Processing]]
**Industry:** [[industries/pharmaceutical-manufacturers|Pharmaceutical Manufacturers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The 15-day clock starts when a qualifying case appears in a journal the company screens, but the screening backlog means it is often found days later.
**Tags:** #bert #large-language-models #k-nearest-neighbors #compliance #quick-win #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn every incoming adverse event report into a coded, assessed case FDA accepts, and whoever leaves the fewest late or missed cases for an FDA inspection to find takes the account.

## The Problem
Literature screening for hundreds of molecules returns thousands of abstracts a week (estimate). Associates read them in batches, and a serious unexpected case buried in a case series is recognised late, leaving little time to obtain the article, code it and submit.

## Why It's Still Broken
Searches are tuned for recall, so most hits are irrelevant, and the screening is treated as a weekly task rather than a clocked intake channel.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Daily automated screening that ranks abstracts by the likelihood of a valid, serious, unexpected case for the company's products, retrieves full text for the top hits, and starts the case record with the clock date shown.

## Who Feels the Pain
Safety associates screening abstracts; safety heads explaining late submissions to inspectors.

## Impact If Fixed
Ranking hits so associates read the likely cases first could cut screening hours by more than half (estimate) and remove most late literature submissions.

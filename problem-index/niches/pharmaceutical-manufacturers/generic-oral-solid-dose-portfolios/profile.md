# Generic Oral Solid Dose Portfolios

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/pharmaceutical-manufacturers|Pharmaceutical Manufacturers]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is that every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to earn money on a molecule after the brand's exclusivity ends, but first-to-file launches and mature-molecule supply reward different things: a launch is won by whoever is first lawfully on the market with exclusivity, a mature molecule by whoever keeps shipping an awarded contract at an eroded price without a failure-to-supply penalty. ## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Generics filled 89% of US prescriptions in 2025 for 11.6% of spending, and FDA's catalogue holds 14,753 ANDAs, most of them tablets and capsules. A generic maker's business is a portfolio of molecules at different points in a lifecycle: identified years before patent expiry, filed (often with a Paragraph IV challenge), launched, then sold for years at prices that fall with each new entrant, until the maker exits or the molecule goes short. Teva, Viatris, Sandoz, Amneal, Hikma and the Indian majors (Sun, Zydus, Lupin, Aurobindo, Dr. Reddy's) compete molecule by molecule, and the work at each end of the lifecycle is different enough to split.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Molecule and patent data come from Clarivate's Cortellis Generics Intelligence, IQVIA, DrugPatentWatch and PharmaCompass; registrations from Veeva Vault RIM; supply plans from SAP IBP, Kinaxis or o9; awards from wholesaler programmes (McKesson OneStop, Cardinal SourceRx, Cencora PRxO). The gap is the molecule's full record: why it was chosen, what was forecast, what it actually earned, and when it stopped paying for its quality costs.

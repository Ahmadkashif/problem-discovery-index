# Audit Management Modules Pointed at FDA Inspections

**Niche:** [[niches/pharmaceutical-manufacturers/fda-inspection-483-response/profile|FDA Inspection Readiness & Form 483 Response]]
**Industry:** [[industries/pharmaceutical-manufacturers|Pharmaceutical Manufacturers]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** QMS audit modules manage internal and supplier audits well and treat an FDA inspection as just another audit.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #bert #compliance #workflow-orchestration #data-integration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to answer an FDA Form 483 so that the agency judges the corrective actions adequate, and whoever closes the highest share of 483s without a warning letter takes the account.

## The Problem
During an inspection, requests arrive by the hour and documents must be found, reviewed and presented; afterwards, commitments made in the response must be tracked for years. Firms run this outside their QMS.

## What Already Exists
Audit management modules in Veeva Vault Quality, MasterControl, TrackWise and ETQ; inspection-intelligence subscriptions from Redica; document management systems.

## The Customization Gap
The FDA case needs live request tracking with back-room review before anything reaches the investigator; pre-loaded intelligence on the investigator's past citations (Redica's profiles, FDA's public data); a link from each observation to the site's deviations and CAPAs; response drafting to the 15-business-day clock; and a commitment register that feeds progress updates to FDA.

## Target Customer
Site quality heads and corporate compliance teams already licensing a QMS.

## Impact If Solved
Running inspections and responses inside the existing QMS would save one to two weeks of QA effort per inspection on logging, chasing and reconciling (estimate) and keep every commitment to FDA tracked to closure, the first thing an investigator checks at re-inspection.

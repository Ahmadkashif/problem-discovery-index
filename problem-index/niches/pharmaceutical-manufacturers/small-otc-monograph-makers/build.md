**One-liner:** A small monograph maker carries the same registration, listing, reporting, testing and CGMP obligations as a large drug company, tracked by an owner from memory.

**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep a small maker's monograph products lawful to ship under FDA's reformed OTC system and CGMP inspection, and whoever keeps the most of a maker's SKUs on retail shelves without an FDA enforcement action takes the account.

## The Problem
The owner of a private-label sunscreen and antiseptic maker must keep establishment registration and drug listings current, file annual drug amount reports, track final monograph orders that change permitted ingredients and labelling, test at-risk products for impurities such as benzene, and run complaint handling, stability and annual product reviews under CGMP. Each obligation has its own date and format, and the retailer's quality team asks for proof of all of them.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Regulatory software is sold to firms with regulatory departments. Small makers buy consultants by the hour when something goes wrong, and their products sit on the line between drug and cosmetic, so generic compliance tools miss half the obligations.

## What to Build
A per-SKU obligation map built from the maker's listings and labels: which monograph applies, which ingredients and claims are permitted, which tests and reports are due, with reminders, pre-filled filings and a document pack the retailer's audit can accept. Monograph and guidance changes are read as they publish and mapped to the affected SKUs.

## Target Customer
Owners and operations heads of private-label OTC makers with 20–200 staff and dozens to hundreds of SKUs.

## Impact If Built
Keeping one retailer programme from being suspended over a missed filing or recall protects revenue that is often 20–40% of a small private-label maker's sales (estimate), at a software cost of a few thousand dollars a month (estimate).

# Small OTC Monograph & Private-Label Makers

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/pharmaceutical-manufacturers|Pharmaceutical Manufacturers]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep a small maker's monograph products lawful to ship under FDA's reformed OTC system and CGMP inspection, and whoever keeps the most of a maker's SKUs on retail shelves without an FDA enforcement action takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$300M a year in quality, testing, regulatory and software spend at small OTC monograph and private-label drug makers (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~0.2% of US receipts, but a large share of sites (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — paper batch records, spreadsheet complaint logs and an owner who doubles as the regulatory department
**Target Buyer:** Owner or VP Operations at a 20–200 person maker of sunscreens, hand sanitisers, antacids, analgesics, cough-cold or topical products, often for retailers' store brands
**Automation Potential:** Medium — calendars, registrations, reports and label checks automate; formulation and testing decisions need chemists

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
OTC monograph drugs need no approved application, so many of their makers are small firms with cosmetics or supplement roots. FDA's catalogue counts 2,755 sites (46% of the 5,953) that make only non-application products; most are medical gas makers, which leaves roughly 1,000 or more sites making OTC monograph, unapproved and homeopathic drugs (derived: up to 1,704 of the 2,755 are gas sites). The sector lags on basic obligations: the drug amount reporting rate for OTC monograph products was 52% in CY2024 against 80% for ANDAs, and FDA wrote to 9,571 registrants in October 2025 about missing reports before publishing a noncompliant list in March 2026. Benzene in aerosol sunscreens and antiperspirants has driven recalls since 2021, and FDA now funds testing programmes for benzene and for diethylene and ethylene glycol in OTC products. These makers face the same CGMP rules as a large generic maker with a fraction of the staff, and the boundary with cosmetics (the queued cosmetics-manufacturers industry) runs through their product lines.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Enterprise QMS and eBR systems priced and staffed for large firms; regulatory consultants by the hour; contract labs for testing. The gaps: no compliance calendar built for a small monograph maker, and nobody watches FDA's monograph orders, listing and reporting deadlines for them.

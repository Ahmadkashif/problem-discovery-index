# Nobody Will Sign Off the Deletion

**Niche:** [[niches/data-platform-integrators/retirement-and-rationalisation/profile|Retirement & Rationalisation]]
**Industry:** [[industries/data-platform-integrators|Data Platform Integrators]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The usage report identified two hundred unused assets and six months later all two hundred are still there.
**Tags:** #quick-win #compliance #workflow-orchestration #graph-theory #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #automation #data-integration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to actually remove an unused asset, which requires proving nothing depends on it and finding someone willing to say so — and whoever makes deletion safe takes the account.

## The Problem
The analysis gets done and nothing happens. A list of unused assets is produced, circulated, and stalls: nobody owns most of the assets, the people who might have depended on them cannot be identified, and no individual is willing to be the one who deleted something that turned out to matter. The rationalisation is reported as complete because the report was delivered, and the estate is exactly as large as it was.

## Why It's Still Broken
The downside is personal and the upside is collective — whoever approves a deletion carries the blame if it breaks something and receives none of the diffuse benefit if it does not, so the rational choice is always to leave it. No owner exists. Deletion is irreversible. And nobody is accountable for the estate's size.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Remove the personal risk from the decision. Deprecate with a notice period and monitoring rather than deleting, which is the fix and means no individual is making an irreversible call. Make removal reversible for a defined period, which lowers the stakes further and is technically straightforward. Assign the decision to a group or a policy rather than a person, so the accountability is institutional. Start with assets that are demonstrably untouched for a year and have no lineage children, since those are the uncontroversial cases and building the habit matters more than the first batch's size. Notify broadly before removing, which surfaces the hidden consumer cheaply. Record who was notified and what was found, so the process is defensible. Report the saving from what was removed, which builds support. Set a target for net estate growth rather than for deletions, as the inflow is the real problem. Require an owner at creation going forward, which prevents the next generation of orphans. And celebrate the removals, which is the cultural half and is usually missing.

## Who Feels the Pain
Platform teams maintaining an estate nobody will let them shrink; whoever would carry the blame for a wrong deletion; budgets funding unused compute; and the engineers whose every change is slowed by the accumulation.

## Impact If Fixed
Whoever approves a deletion carries the blame if it breaks something and receives none of the diffuse benefit if it does not, so the rational choice is always to leave it. A deprecation window with reversible removal takes the personal risk out.

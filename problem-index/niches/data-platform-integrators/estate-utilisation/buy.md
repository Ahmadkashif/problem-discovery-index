**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to establish which of the models and dashboards it has built are actually used, when the query log holds the answer and nobody has ever run it — and whoever answers it takes the account.

## The Problem
Enterprise architecture developed application portfolio management to answer this exact question about software estates: what exists, who uses it, what it costs, what depends on it, and what can be retired. The frameworks, the assessment methods and the tooling are established, and large organisations run rationalisation programmes on them regularly. Data estates are portfolios of the same kind, grow faster, and have never been managed that way.

## What Already Exists
Application portfolio inventories with ownership; usage and business value assessment; total cost of ownership per asset; dependency mapping for retirement; and rationalisation programme methodology.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to assets that are automatically enumerable and change weekly. It requires: (1) an inventory that can be extracted automatically with complete usage telemetry, where application portfolio management relies on surveys and interviews — this is the substantive difference and makes the analysis far cheaper and far more current; (2) thousands of small assets rather than hundreds of applications, so retirement must be routine rather than programmatic; (3) dependencies that are precise and machine-readable through lineage; (4) cost attributable per asset per query in consumption-priced platforms; and (5) an estate that regrows continuously, so a one-off rationalisation is worthless without a standing practice.

## Target Customer
Data platform integrators, client data leadership, platform vendors, and governance and portfolio management providers.

## Impact If Solved
Portfolio rationalisation established how to find what nobody uses, with frameworks and tooling. Complete usage telemetry and machine-readable lineage make the data version cheaper and current — and its continuous regrowth is why it must be a practice rather than a programme.

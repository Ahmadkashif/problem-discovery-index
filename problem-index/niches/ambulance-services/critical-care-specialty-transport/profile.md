# Critical Care & Specialty Care Transport Teams

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/ambulance-services|Ambulance Services]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to move ICU-level patients between hospitals without harm en route, and whoever has the fewest in-transport adverse events per 100 critical care transports takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$0.2B a year of quality, training, monitoring and documentation spend at ground critical care, neonatal and pediatric transport programmes (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~1% of 621910 receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium-Low — the team charts on an ePCR, but handoffs arrive on paper and adverse events are written into narrative
**Target Buyer:** Critical care transport programme director or hospital transport medical director
**Automation Potential:** Medium — handoff summaries, monitoring capture and event coding automate; the clinical care does not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Critical care transport teams, usually a nurse with a paramedic and sometimes a respiratory therapist or physician, move ventilated, pressor-dependent, neonatal and post-arrest patients between hospitals. Medicare bills this as specialty care transport (A0434, RVU 3.25 against 1.90 for ALS emergency in the CY2026 fee schedule). The teams are run by hospital systems, private operators and air programmes, and their patients are the sickest the industry carries. The risks are specific: unplanned extubation, hypotension on a pressor change, infusion errors, line loss, hypothermia in neonates. The field has its own quality benchmarking in GAMUT (Ground and Air Medical qUality in Transport), named here without figures. Competitors are hospital-based teams, private critical care divisions and air programmes flying ground missions. Pediatric appointment transport for Medicaid children is a different contest, at [[niches/non-emergency-medical-transport/pediatric-medical-transport/profile|🟠 Pediatric Medical Transport]].

## Current Tools & Gaps
ePCR critical care modules, transport ventilators and monitors that record data, and paper handoff packets. Gaps: monitor and pump data not joined to the chart, adverse events coded inconsistently, and no risk model that predicts which transports will deteriorate en route.

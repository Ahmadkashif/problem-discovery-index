# Tele-ICU Platforms Extended Into the Transport Vehicle

**Niche:** [[niches/ambulance-services/critical-care-specialty-transport/profile|Critical Care & Specialty Care Transport Teams]]
**Industry:** [[industries/ambulance-services|Ambulance Services]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Tele-ICU platforms give a remote intensivist eyes on an ICU bed; the riskiest stretch of a critical patient's stay is the 45 minutes in a truck between two ICUs, where nobody is watching.
**Tags:** #time-series-forecasting #gradient-boosting #data-integration #workflow-orchestration #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to move ICU-level patients between hospitals without harm en route, and whoever has the fewest in-transport adverse events per 100 critical care transports takes the account.

## The Problem
During transport the team is alone with the patient, and medical control is a phone call that describes numbers the physician cannot see.

## What Already Exists
Tele-ICU and remote patient monitoring platforms in hospitals, monitor manufacturers' telemetry from ambulances, and medical-control radio and phone lines.

## The Customization Gap
The transport version must stream from transport monitors and ventilators over cellular links that drop. It must give the receiving or medical-control physician the trend and the infusion state, not just a snapshot, and alert on early deterioration with thresholds suited to transport (movement artefact, vibration). Everything must be recorded into the ePCR as the run's record.

## Target Customer
Hospital systems running critical care transport and tele-ICU services together.

## Impact If Solved
Giving medical control the live trend on the 20% of transports that need a call (an estimate) would turn about 600 blind consults a year into informed ones for a 3,000-transport programme.

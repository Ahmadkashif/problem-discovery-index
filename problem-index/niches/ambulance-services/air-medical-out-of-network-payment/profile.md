# Air Medical Transport & Out-of-Network Payment

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/ambulance-services|Ambulance Services]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get paid for flights flown to patients whose health plans do not contract with the operator, and whoever collects the most per out-of-network transport after independent dispute resolution takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$0.3B a year of revenue cycle staff, IDR representation fees, administrative fees and billing software spent on air ambulance claims (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~1.5% of 621910 receipts (estimate); air medical itself is ~$5–6B of revenue (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — claims are electronic and disputes go through the federal IDR portal, but QPA disclosures, open-negotiation letters and offer briefs are assembled by hand
**Target Buyer:** Air operator chief revenue officer or vice-president of revenue cycle
**Automation Potential:** High for eligibility screening, deadline tracking and offer modelling; negotiation stays human

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Air ambulances are the one part of this industry inside the No Surprises Act. Patients cannot be balance-billed, so an out-of-network flight is paid at whatever the plan offers unless the operator disputes it through open negotiation and then federal IDR. Base rates are large: the CY2026 Medicare rotary-wing base is $4,489.71 before mileage at $29.23 a mile (CMS PUF), and commercial charges run far higher. Disputes are concentrated. In Q2 2025, 10,560 air ambulance disputes were initiated, 52% by Global Medical Response, 20% by PHI Air Medical and 10% by Air Methods. Operators prevailed in 89% of 12,263 determinations, at a median prevailing offer of 241% of the QPA (CMS supplemental tables). CMS cautions that unit-based QPAs can inflate that percentage. 47% of air disputes were challenged as ineligible. Competitors are the operators' own revenue teams, IDR representation firms (No Surprise Bill, HSI Health, Quick Med Claims) and billing vendors. On the plan side sit carriers and repricers such as Centene, BCBS Texas and Claritev. Charter operators flying non-medical trips are a separate queued industry, charter-air-operators.

## Current Tools & Gaps
IDR portal submissions, billing systems and spreadsheets of past determinations. Gaps: no model of which offer wins by payer, state and service; eligibility defects found after filing; and membership programmes (AirMedCare Network and others) tracked apart from claims.

# Exclusive Operating Area Bids & Contract Pricing

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/ambulance-services|Ambulance Services]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to price a county's exclusive 911 franchise from its call volume, payer mix and collection history, and whoever has the lowest share of won contracts losing money in their first full year takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$0.15B a year of corporate development staff, EMS consulting engagements and payer-mix data work spent on 911 bids and rebids (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~0.7% of 621910 receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low-Medium — county RFP data arrives as PDFs and CAD extracts; the bid model is a spreadsheet
**Target Buyer:** Operator vice-president of corporate development or regional president
**Automation Potential:** Medium — volume and payer-mix forecasting automate; strategy and the county relationship do not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A county 911 rebid happens every five to ten years (estimate), and the bidder must forecast net patient revenue years ahead. That means call volume, transport ratio, payer mix (Medicare, Medicaid, commercial, uninsured) and collection rate by payer, weighed against the unit-hours the response standard demands and any subsidy or franchise fee. A bid built on too rosy a payer mix wins and then loses money. Medicare's cost survey shows how wide the spread is: mean cost per transport ran from $918 in the highest-volume quartile to $2,878 in the lowest (MedPAC, June 2026). Competitors are operators' corporate development teams, EMS system consultancies such as Fitch & Associates and AP Triton (named from recall), and the billing firms whose collection data informs the payer-mix assumption. The winner is the bidder whose won contracts make money from the first full year, not the one with the lowest price.

## Current Tools & Gaps
County RFP data packs, historical CAD extracts, billing collection reports from existing contracts and consultants' spreadsheets. Gaps: no record of which bid assumptions turned won contracts into first-year losses, and payer mix assumed rather than modelled from the county's demographics and insurance coverage.

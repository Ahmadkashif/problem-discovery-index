# Shift Scheduling Apps Taught Paid-on-Call Coverage

**Niche:** [[niches/ambulance-services/rural-volunteer-squads/profile|Rural, Volunteer & Nonprofit Squads That Bill]]
**Industry:** [[industries/ambulance-services|Ambulance Services]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Public-safety scheduling software assumes paid staff on fixed rotations; a rural squad runs on availability windows, on-call pay and members who carry a pager to their day job.
**Tags:** #optimization-fundamentals #time-series-forecasting #worker-facing #automation #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep a staffed ambulance in service for a district that runs a few hundred to a couple of thousand calls a year, and whoever leaves the fewest hours a month with no crew in service takes the account.

## The Problem
Squads buy the scheduling tools sold to fire departments and paid EMS, then work around them with group texts because the tools cannot express "available from home within 6 minutes of the station between 6 p.m. and 6 a.m.".

## What Already Exists
Public-safety scheduling platforms, paging and response apps that show who is answering a call, and general shift apps.

## The Customization Gap
The squad needs availability windows rather than shifts, a response-distance rule per member, crew-pair rules (at least one EMT and a driver, or a paramedic for ALS), state certification expiries, on-call stipends, and a live "in service or not" status the county dispatch centre can see. It also needs reporting of uncovered hours and mutual-aid calls for the board and for grant applications.

## Target Customer
Squad chiefs and district administrators already paying for a paging or scheduling app.

## Impact If Solved
A tool that shows the county dispatch centre a live in-service status would end calls paged to a squad with nobody available, which cost ~5 minutes each before mutual aid is requested (an estimate).

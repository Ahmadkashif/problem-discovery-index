# Runs Never Billed Because the Treasurer Ran Out of Evenings

**Niche:** [[niches/ambulance-services/rural-volunteer-squads/profile|Rural, Volunteer & Nonprofit Squads That Bill]]
**Industry:** [[industries/ambulance-services|Ambulance Services]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** At small squads, runs go unbilled or are billed months late because the volunteer who handles billing has a day job, and the money that would pay for a paid daytime crew never arrives.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #automation #revenue-impact #quick-win #workflow-orchestration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep a staffed ambulance in service for a district that runs a few hundred to a couple of thousand calls a year, and whoever leaves the fewest hours a month with no crew in service takes the account.

## The Problem
A squad running 600 calls a year may bill through an outside firm or a volunteer treasurer. Missing insurance details, unsigned forms and charts with no signature from the patient or a representative stall claims. Timely filing limits pass and runs are written off. That revenue is what would fund a paid crew for the hours volunteers cannot cover.

## Why It's Still Broken
Billing firms price per claim and prefer larger clients. The squad has no one whose job it is to chase the gaps, and the ePCR does not flag a run as unbillable until someone tries to bill it.

## What a Fix Looks Like
An automatic check of each closed ePCR for what billing needs: demographics, insurance or a face sheet, the signature, the level documented. A short weekly list of fixable gaps goes to the chief, and complete runs flow to the biller the same week.

## Who Feels the Pain
Squad chiefs and boards short of money for paid staff; volunteer treasurers; and residents who get late, confusing bills.

## Impact If Fixed
If a squad running 600 calls a year at ~$500 average collection per billed run (an estimate) stopped writing off 60 of them (10% of its volume, an estimate), it would gain ~$30,000 a year, about the cost of covering 1,000 daytime hours with stipended crews (an estimate).

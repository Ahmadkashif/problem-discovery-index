# System Status Management & 911 Unit Deployment

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/ambulance-services|Ambulance Services]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep an available ambulance within reach of every zone of a county 911 contract around the clock, with the unit-hours the contract funds, and whoever has the fewest priority-1 calls arriving when the zone has no available unit (level-zero calls) per 1,000 priority-1 responses takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$0.6B a year of system status controllers, dispatch time spent on posting moves, CAD and deployment software, and AVL (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~3% of 621910 receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — CAD and AVL are digital; the posting plan is often a static table tuned by a senior controller
**Target Buyer:** Operator director of communications or operations manager for the contract
**Automation Potential:** High for forecasting and move recommendations; the controller keeps the final call

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
System status management means moving ambulances between street-corner posts through the day so that every zone always has an available unit within reach of its next priority-1 call. The controller balances coverage against unit-hours, crew fatigue, hospital wall time and the scheduled transfers sharing the same fleet. Medicare's cost survey found dynamic and hybrid deployment far cheaper per transport than static ($636 and $918 against $1,524), with 55.6% of organizations still static (MedPAC, June 2026). The county sets the response standard and the penalty per late call, so speed is the client's service level. What the operator controls is availability: whether a unit is free and posted within reach when a priority-1 call arrives. A call that arrives while its zone is at "level zero", with no available unit, is the event a posting plan exists to prevent, and CAD records it without reference to how fast any crew drove. The operator's contest is to keep those calls rare without buying unit-hours the contract does not fund. Competitors are the operators' own controllers, CAD and deployment software vendors, and EMS consultancies that design posting plans. Fire-based systems with fixed stations play a different game. Hospital capacity software is profiled at [[niches/general-hospitals/capacity-periop-optimization-vendors/profile|🔍 Capacity, Patient-Flow & Perioperative Optimization Vendors]].

## Current Tools & Gaps
CAD posting plans by hour and day, AVL, coverage maps and the senior controller's experience. Gaps: plans built on historical averages rather than probabilistic forecasts, wall-time delays not modelled, level-zero calls not counted as a measure of their own, and no record of which moves the controller overrode and why.

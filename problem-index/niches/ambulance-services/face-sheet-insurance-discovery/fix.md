# Face Sheets Requested by Fax From Forty Hospitals

**Niche:** [[niches/ambulance-services/face-sheet-insurance-discovery/profile|Face-Sheet Retrieval & Insurance Discovery]]
**Industry:** [[industries/ambulance-services|Ambulance Services]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A billing clerk keeps a binder of forty hospitals' face-sheet procedures and sends requests one patient at a time, and the answers come back days later or not at all.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #data-integration #automation #quick-win #workflow-orchestration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to find billable coverage for patients carried with no insurance details, and whoever converts the highest share of transports opened as self-pay into paid insured claims takes the account.

## The Problem
Each receiving hospital handles face-sheet requests differently: a portal login, a fax form, a phone call to registration, an email to health information management. A billing team serving a metro area keeps a list of procedures and sends hundreds of requests a week.

## Why It's Still Broken
Hospitals treat the requests as low-priority release-of-information work, and HIPAA permits sharing for payment, but each hospital sets its own process. No single vendor connects ambulance billing to every hospital's registration.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A face-sheet agent that knows each hospital's channel, sends structured requests automatically when a run is closed, reads the returned face sheet, extracts coverage and demographics into the claim, and escalates hospitals that do not respond.

## Who Feels the Pain
Billing clerks chasing paper; hospitals' registration staff answering repetitive requests; and patients who get a self-pay bill for a ride their insurance would have paid.

## Impact If Fixed
At ~5 minutes a request (an estimate), automating 400 requests a week saves a billing team ~33 hours a week.

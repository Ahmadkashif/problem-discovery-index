# County 911 Contracts & Exclusive Operating Areas

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/ambulance-services|Ambulance Services]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is that every serious competitor here is fighting to hold a county's or city's 911 ambulance contract. But the contract is won at bid, by pricing years of call volume, payer mix and collections into a subsidy or franchise fee, and kept month by month, by deploying units against the response-time standard. The two contests have different people, clocks and winners. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.2B a year of bid and contract-management work, dispatch and deployment staff, CAD and system status software, and EMS consulting tied to 911 contracts (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~6% of 621910 receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — CAD, AVL and compliance reports are digital; bid models are spreadsheets and deployment plans are tuned by a senior controller's experience
**Target Buyer:** Operator regional director and vice-president of operations; county EMS agency administrator on the other side
**Automation Potential:** Medium — demand forecasting and compliance reporting automate; bids and deployment rules stay human

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Many counties grant one operator an exclusive operating area for 911 ambulance transport, under state law that lets a local EMS agency franchise the service. The contract sets a response-time standard, commonly about 90% of priority-1 calls within 8–9 minutes in urban zones (estimate). It also sets penalties per late call, a subsidy paid or franchise fee charged, and clinical requirements. Private operators (AMR within GMR, Priority Ambulance and Falck, the last two named from recall, and regional independents), hospital-based services and fire departments compete for these areas, and fire-based EMS is both the commonest rival and, as first responder, a partner on every call. DocGo names "local fire departments and other local government providers" among its primary competitors (10-K). Government-owned organizations ran 38.5% of ground transports in Medicare's cost survey, for-profits 42.1% (MedPAC, June 2026). Municipal fleet maintenance for public ambulances is a different contest, at [[niches/fleet-managers/municipal-vehicle-fleets/profile|🟠 Municipal Vehicle Fleets]].

## Current Tools & Gaps
CAD with system status management modules, AVL, compliance dashboards and consultants' bid models. Gaps: bids priced on payer mix and collection rates nobody has checked against outcomes, and deployment plans that are static or rely on the controller's judgement.

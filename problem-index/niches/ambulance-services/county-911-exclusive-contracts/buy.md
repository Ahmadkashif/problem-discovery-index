But the contract is won at bid, by pricing years of call volume, payer mix and collections into a subsidy or franchise fee, and kept month by month, by deploying units against the response-time standard. The two contests have different people, clocks and winners. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## The Problem
A 911 ambulance contract contains dozens of measurable obligations. These include response standards by priority and zone (urban, suburban, rural), the clock start and stop rules, exemptions (weather, freeway closures, multi-casualty incidents, wrong addresses), penalties per late call and for outliers, clinical measures, and reporting deadlines. The compliance analyst recomputes them each month from CAD exports in spreadsheets.

## What Already Exists
Contract lifecycle management tools (Icertis, DocuSign CLM, Ironclad), CAD reporting modules, and business intelligence dashboards built on CAD data.

## The Customization Gap
The tool must turn contract clauses into executable rules: which timestamp starts the clock (call received, call answered or unit assigned), how zones map to addresses, how exemption categories are documented, and how penalties escalate. Then it must apply those rules to every CAD record and produce the county's report in the county's format. Each county writes its contract differently, so the rule layer has to be extracted from the contract text itself.

## Target Customer
Contract compliance managers and regional directors at operators holding several county contracts.

## Impact If Solved
Automating the monthly compliance computation would save each contract ~40 analyst-hours a month (an estimate) and show penalty exposure daily instead of after the month closes.

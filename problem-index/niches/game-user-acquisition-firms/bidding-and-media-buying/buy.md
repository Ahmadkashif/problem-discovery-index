## The Problem
Quantitative trading is the discipline of allocating capital across opportunities with uncertain returns, execution costs, capacity constraints and delayed feedback. Portfolio construction under uncertainty, capacity and market impact modelling, execution cost analysis, and explicit exploration to maintain signal are all mature. Media buying has the same structure — allocate a budget across sources with uncertain returns, diminishing capacity and delayed outcomes — and is done as campaign management.

## What Already Exists
Portfolio construction under return uncertainty; capacity and market impact modelling; execution cost analysis; explicit exploration versus exploitation allocation; and performance attribution across positions.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a market with opaque intermediaries and feedback measured in weeks. It requires: (1) an intermediary optimising for its own objective between the instruction and the outcome, which no execution venue does in the same way — this is the substantive difference and makes the plant genuinely unobservable; (2) returns realised over months rather than marked daily, so attribution is slow and noisy; (3) capacity constraints that are audience saturation rather than liquidity; (4) a return estimate that is itself a model output with large uncertainty, compounding the problem; and (5) no ability to exit a position, since an acquired cohort cannot be sold.

## Target Customer
UA teams and agencies, mobile publishers, bid management platforms, and quantitative optimisation consultancies.

## Impact If Solved
Quantitative trading solved allocation under uncertainty with capacity and execution costs. An intermediary optimising for its own objective between instruction and outcome, and positions that cannot be exited, is what has to be modelled here.

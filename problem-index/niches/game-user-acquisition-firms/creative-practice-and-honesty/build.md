# Measuring What the Misleading Ad Actually Buys

**Niche:** [[niches/game-user-acquisition-firms/creative-practice-and-honesty/profile|Creative Practice & Ad Honesty]]
**Industry:** [[industries/game-user-acquisition-firms|Game User Acquisition Firms]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** The practice persists because the metric it improves is measured immediately and the metric it destroys is measured separately.
**Tags:** #causal-inference #survival-analysis #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #compliance #hypothesis-testing #revenue-impact #descriptive-statistics
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to establish whether ads depicting mechanics the game does not have actually acquire anything of value after the churn they cause — and whoever measures that takes the account.

## The Problem
An ad showing a mechanic the game does not contain acquires installs at a low cost per install. Those users open the game, do not find what they were shown, and a large share leave almost immediately. Whether what remains is worth the spend is a measurable question. The industry has largely not asked it, because creative is evaluated on install metrics and value is evaluated on cohorts weeks later, and nobody joins the two.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Creative testing frameworks were built around install rate and cost per install because those are fast and unambiguous. Joining creative to long-horizon cohort value requires attribution that survives weeks. The practice works on the metric it is judged on. And establishing the answer might oblige a firm to stop.

## What to Build
Join creative to cohort outcome and evaluate on value, not installs. Attribute cohort value back to the creative that acquired it and evaluate creatives on that rather than on cost per install, which is the core and is the measurement whose absence sustains the practice. Measure early churn by creative specifically, since the expectation mismatch shows up in the first session and is unmistakable. Compare creatives on cost per retained player and cost per value rather than cost per install, which reorders the ranking substantially. Detect expectation mismatch directly by comparing what the creative depicts with what the player encounters, which is increasingly automatable. Model the brand and platform risk alongside the acquisition economics, as the regulatory exposure is real and is currently in nobody's model. Measure the effect on store ratings and reviews, which is a documented consequence and a compounding cost. Report the honest comparison even when the misleading creative wins on value, since the answer should be established rather than assumed. Track platform policy exposure per creative so the risk is visible to leadership. Test whether honest creatives can be made to perform, which is the constructive half and is under-invested. And make the value-based evaluation the default in the creative testing framework, which is the change that ends the practice or justifies it.

## Target Customer
UA teams and agencies, mobile publishers, platform policy teams, and advertising measurement and compliance vendors.

## Impact If Built
The practice persists because the metric it improves is immediate and the metric it destroys is measured separately and late. Attributing cohort value back to creative reorders the ranking and settles a question the industry has avoided.

# Creative Practice & Ad Honesty

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/game-user-acquisition-firms|Game User Acquisition Firms]]
**Category:** 🟠 Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to establish whether ads depicting mechanics the game does not have actually acquire anything of value after the churn they cause — and whoever measures that takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$6B US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~15% of category spend
**Digital Adoption:** Very low — unmeasured
**Target Buyer:** Marketing leadership
**Automation Potential:** High — long-horizon creative measurement

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Ads depicting mechanics that are not in the game acquire installs cheaply, the practice has drawn platform policy and consumer protection attention, and almost nobody has measured whether the installs are worth anything after the churn. It persists because the metric it is judged on — cost per install — improves, and the metric it damages — what those installs are worth — is measured separately and late. It is simultaneously an ethical problem, a regulatory exposure and an unexamined commercial question.

## Current Tools & Gaps
A creative testing framework measuring install rate and cost per install, and a platform policy document. The gaps: no measurement of creative effect on retention and value; no expectation-mismatch signal; no long-horizon creative evaluation; no policy position tied to evidence; and no accounting for the regulatory exposure.

## The Problem
Education and health outcomes research spent decades on exactly this question: how much of an outcome is attributable to the individual's starting characteristics and how much to the institution that served them. The answer was value-added modelling and risk adjustment — multilevel models separating intake composition from provider effects, used to compare schools and hospitals fairly and to allocate resources. The methods are public, contested, refined and widely applied. UA attributes the entire outcome to the acquisition.

## What Already Exists
Value-added and risk-adjusted outcome modelling; multilevel models separating individual and institutional effects; case-mix adjustment; provider comparison methodology; and explicit treatment of confounding between intake and provider.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a content cadence rather than an institution, with cohorts observed continuously. It requires: (1) the institutional effect being a sequence of content releases rather than a stable provider, so it varies continuously over a cohort's life — this is the substantive difference and makes the effect time-varying rather than fixed; (2) cohorts arriving daily rather than annually, giving far more comparison points; (3) complete behavioural observation rather than a single outcome measure; (4) commercial stakes where the decomposition reallocates accountability between internal functions; and (5) confounding where better content also attracts different players, which must be handled explicitly.

## Target Customer
UA and product leadership, mobile publishers, portfolio management, and analytics and econometrics consultancies.

## Impact If Solved
Education and health built value-added modelling to separate intake from institution, and the methods are public and refined. A content cadence that varies continuously over a cohort's life is what makes the effect time-varying rather than fixed.

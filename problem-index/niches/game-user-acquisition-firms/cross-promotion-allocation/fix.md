# Whoever Shouted Loudest This Quarter

**Niche:** [[niches/game-user-acquisition-firms/cross-promotion-allocation/profile|Cross-Promotion & Portfolio Allocation]]
**Industry:** [[industries/game-user-acquisition-firms|Game User Acquisition Firms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Cross-promotion inventory goes to the title with the most urgent install target, which is a decision about internal politics.
**Tags:** #quick-win #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #revenue-impact #confidence-intervals #optimization-fundamentals #hypothesis-testing #compliance
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to decide which of its own games should send players to which others, when the cost of moving a player between titles has never been measured — and whoever measures it takes the account.

## The Problem
Allocation of cross-promotion placements is settled in a meeting. The title with a launch, a target or a senior advocate gets the inventory. There is no analysis of which destination serves which players, no measure of what the source game gives up, and no record of what past allocations produced. It is one of the larger acquisition decisions a publisher makes and it is made on urgency and seniority.

## Why It's Still Broken
Nobody has produced a number — a decision with no measurement attached will be settled by whoever argues best, and there is nothing in the room that could contradict them. The channel appears free. The two teams have opposed interests. And nobody owns the portfolio outcome.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Produce the numbers that make the meeting shorter. Report what past cross-promotion transfers actually produced, by source and destination pair, which is the fix and takes one analysis against existing data. Measure the source game's loss alongside the destination's gain rather than counting installs, since that comparison is the whole question. Run a simple holdout on the next campaign, which is cheap and settles the cannibalisation argument for everyone. Record each allocation decision and its outcome so the meeting accumulates evidence rather than repeating. Identify which source-destination pairs have historically worked, as they differ enormously and nobody has tabulated it. Exclude high-value players in the source from promotions, which is the clearest immediate improvement. Report the portfolio-level result rather than the destination's numbers. Set a simple allocation rule based on the evidence rather than negotiating each time. Give both teams the same figures before the meeting. And treat the inventory as having a cost even if the price is nominal, because that alone changes how it is requested.

## Who Feels the Pain
Source games losing engaged players and receiving nothing; destination games receiving players who do not suit them; portfolio leaders with no basis for a decision; and the publisher, whose cheapest channel is allocated by argument.

## Impact If Fixed
A decision with no measurement attached will be settled by whoever argues best, and there is nothing in the room that could contradict them. One analysis of past transfers by source-destination pair gives the meeting a fact.

# Cross-Promotion & Portfolio Allocation

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/game-user-acquisition-firms|Game User Acquisition Firms]]
**Category:** 🟠 Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to decide which of its own games should send players to which others, when the cost of moving a player between titles has never been measured — and whoever measures it takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$4B US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~10% of category spend
**Digital Adoption:** Low — by negotiation
**Target Buyer:** Portfolio leadership
**Automation Potential:** High — transfer valuation and allocation

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A publisher's own games are its cheapest acquisition channel, allocation between them is decided by whoever needs installs this quarter, and nobody models what moving a player from one title to another actually costs. The transfer is not free: the player may have continued spending in the original game, may be worth less in the destination, and may end up engaged with neither. It is the one channel the publisher entirely controls and the one with no economics attached.

## Current Tools & Gaps
A cross-promotion placement system, an internal agreement about who promotes whom, and a quarterly negotiation. The gaps: no valuation of a transferred player's counterfactual; no measurement of cannibalisation; no portfolio-level optimisation; no matching of player to destination title; and no pricing of internal inventory.

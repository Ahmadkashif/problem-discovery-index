# The Lift Study the Network Ran on Itself

**Niche:** [[niches/game-user-acquisition-firms/incrementality-operations/profile|Incrementality Operations]]
**Industry:** [[industries/game-user-acquisition-firms|Game User Acquisition Firms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The incrementality evidence for the largest channel is a lift study designed, run and analysed by the channel.
**Tags:** #quick-win #hypothesis-testing #causal-inference #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #compliance #descriptive-statistics #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to run incrementality tests often enough to know what their spend is actually buying, when each one is currently a project — and whoever makes them routine takes the account.

## The Problem
Networks offer lift studies. They are free, convenient and run entirely by the party whose spend is being evaluated — the network defines the holdout, delivers the treatment, measures the outcome and reports the result. The methodology is usually not disclosed in enough detail to assess. Advertisers use these results to justify budgets, because the alternative is running their own study, which is a project.

## Why It's Still Broken
The convenient evidence is the conflicted evidence — a measurement produced entirely by the party being measured will be accepted whenever the independent alternative is expensive, and here it always is. Independent tests require holdouts. Methodology is opaque. And the result is usually favourable, which reduces the appetite to check.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Ask for the method, and run one independent check. Request the study's methodology in enough detail to assess, which is the fix and frequently changes how the result is weighted once seen. Compare the network's reported lift against the advertiser's own attributed figures for consistency. Run one independent geo holdout on the largest channel, which is a single project with a large payoff and is the honest minimum. Compare the independent result against the network's, since the size of the gap is the useful finding. Apply a discount to network-reported lift based on that comparison rather than accepting it whole. Ask every network the same methodological questions, as the differences between their answers are informative. Record which channels' claims have been independently checked and when. Use the independent result for allocation and the network's for context, not the reverse. Repeat the independent check annually on the largest channels. And state in internal reporting which figures are independently verified, which changes how they are used.

## Who Feels the Pain
Publishers allocating budget on conflicted evidence; UA teams defending numbers they did not produce; finance functions taking lift claims at face value; and the channels that are genuinely incremental and indistinguishable from those that are not.

## Impact If Fixed
A measurement produced entirely by the party being measured will be accepted whenever the independent alternative is expensive, and here it always is. One independent holdout on the largest channel gives every other claim a reference point.

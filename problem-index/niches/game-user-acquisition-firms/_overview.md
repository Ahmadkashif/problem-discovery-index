# Niche Analysis — Game User Acquisition Firms

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/game-user-acquisition-firms|Game User Acquisition Firms]]

## Niche Selection

This is the most quantitative marketing discipline in existence, optimising against a number that is a heavy-tail expectation estimated from a few days of behaviour, for a quantity determined mostly by what the game ships over the following six months. The eight niches below follow the money: the prediction everything is bid against, the buying itself, the creative that feeds it, the portfolio channel nobody models, and the people carrying the consequences of a number they do not control.

## Why These Niches

Lifetime value prediction and bidding carry the spend and the discipline's core machinery: everything downstream is an optimisation against a number, and the number is mis-specified. Creative practice and portfolio allocation are the two places where large decisions are made with no measurement behind them — ads depicting mechanics the game does not have, and cross-promotion allocated by whoever needs installs this quarter. The two underserved audiences are the manager measured on a payback number determined by a team they do not control, and the engineer building a complete small game to a five-megabyte budget in six formats to a brief describing something the product does not do. The last two are mechanical: producing the creative, and running the tests.

## Filter Notes

Seven of the eight are terminal — each names one contest that every serious competitor is fighting over, and decomposing further would produce features rather than markets.

**Lifetime value prediction** is not. It names a quantity, and underneath it are two problems that are different in kind. Heavy-tail estimation asks how to predict a distribution whose mass sits in a tail that early behaviour identifies poorly — a modelling problem over the UA function's own cohort data, where better tail accuracy directly improves bidding and nothing about the discipline's framing has to change. Player-content decomposition asks whether lifetime value is a property of the player at all, given that a cohort's realised value is determined largely by the content the game ships afterwards — a reframing that requires the content team's release history, changes what UA should be held to, and gives both functions evidence they currently lack to argue with each other. A UA team can buy a better tail model without anyone else's cooperation; the second requires the product organisation to participate and would change how both sides are measured, which is why nobody has built it.

Two adjacent candidates were rejected as belonging elsewhere: **designing the monetisation the value comes from** belongs to [[industries/mobile-game-publishers|Mobile Game Publishers]], and **operating the game the cohort arrives into** sits with [[industries/game-liveops-services|Game LiveOps Services]].

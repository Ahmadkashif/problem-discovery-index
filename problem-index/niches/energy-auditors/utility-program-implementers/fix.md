# The Contractor Network Is the Programme and Managing It Is a Relationship

**Niche:** [[niches/energy-auditors/utility-program-implementers/profile|Utility Efficiency Programme Implementers]]
**Industry:** [[industries/energy-auditors|Energy Auditors]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Savings targets are hit by contractors choosing to sell the programme, and what makes a contractor engage lives with the account manager who recruited them.
**Tags:** #tacit-knowledge-ml #evaluation-metrics #workflow-orchestration #worker-facing #data-integration

## The Problem
An efficiency programme does not install anything. Contractors do — HVAC companies, insulation crews, electricians — and they participate because the programme makes a sale easier or more profitable, not because they were asked.

So the implementer's real operating problem is contractor engagement: which contractors are active, which have gone quiet, which are frustrated with paperwork or slow payment, which will push a heat pump if the incentive covers the conversation, and which will quietly steer customers to a non-qualifying unit because the qualifying one is harder to get.

That knowledge sits with programme account managers. It is a relationship business conducted by phone and at trade counters, and what a manager knows about a hundred contractors is not written down beyond activity counts.

The consequences are structural. Participation forecasts rest on relationship intuition rather than on modelled engagement. Contractor attrition — the largest driver of a programme falling short — is noticed after a contractor stops submitting rather than before. And when a manager leaves, or a contract transfers to a new implementer at rebid, the network relationship resets while the target does not.

Underneath sits an unrecorded diagnosis. Managers know why contractors disengage — payment timing, documentation burden, incentive level, a bad audit experience, a competing programme in a neighbouring territory. Those reasons are the actionable content of the whole function and they exist as conversation.

## Why It's Still Broken
Account managers are measured on programme volume, and volume is delivered by talking to contractors, not by recording what they said.

The systems track applications, because applications are what get paid. A contractor is an attribute of an application rather than an entity with a state, a history and a trajectory.

And contract turnover cuts against investment. An implementer holding a three-year contract has limited incentive to build a durable network asset it may hand to a competitor at rebid — which is precisely why the asset never gets built and every transition is disruptive.

## What a Fix Looks Like
**Make the contractor the record.** Engagement history, submission quality, payment experience, training completed, stated frustrations, and territory — as an entity with a state rather than a column on an application.

**Structure the disengagement reason.** When a manager learns why a contractor has gone quiet, one controlled-vocabulary line. This is the single most actionable data in the programme and it is currently spoken aloud.

**Predict attrition before it shows in volume.** Submission cadence, quality drift, support contacts and payment delays precede disengagement and are all recorded already.

**Measure what the interventions do.** Training sessions, incentive changes, payment acceleration and co-marketing are run constantly and their effect on contractor activity is never estimated, though the variation is natural and abundant.

**Give managers their own network view.** Who is drifting, who is new, who has an unresolved payment issue — on a phone, before a trade counter visit. That is what makes the recording habit survive.

**Treat the network record as a transferable asset.** Written into the contract deliverables, it survives a rebid and makes the programme less fragile for the utility, which is an argument the utility will support.

## Who Feels the Pain
Account managers carrying a hundred relationships in their heads; contractors who disengage over solvable problems nobody logged; utilities whose savings targets depend on a network nobody can characterise; and the implementer, whose forecast accuracy and rebid position both rest on knowledge that walks out with an account manager.

## Impact If Fixed
Contractor engagement is the mechanism by which every efficiency target is actually met, and it is managed by memory and relationship. Making the contractor the unit of record turns attrition into a predicted event rather than a discovered one, makes programme interventions measurable for the first time, and protects the network through the contract transitions that currently reset it.

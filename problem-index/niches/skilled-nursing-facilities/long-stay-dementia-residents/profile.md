# Long-Stay Residents Living with Dementia

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/skilled-nursing-facilities|Skilled Nursing Facilities]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to cut falls with major injury and antipsychotic use among long-stay residents with dementia, and whoever moves the MDS long-stay falls and antipsychotic measures that feed SNF VBP and Five-Star takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$800M a year in falls technology, behavioural health and dementia-care programmes, and related staff training sold into nursing homes (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~0.4% of facility spending (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — behaviours are charted as checkboxes, falls are reviewed on paper incident reports, and most rooms have no sensors
**Target Buyer:** Director of Nursing and chief clinical or quality officer at a chain; Medicaid-heavy independents
**Automation Potential:** Medium — detection and pattern-finding automate; redirecting a distressed resident at 4 p.m. does not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Most SNF software is designed around the short-stay Medicare patient: admission, PDPM, therapy minutes, discharge. The long-stay resident with dementia, usually on Medicaid and living in the building for years, is the person the tools ignore. Yet this group drives some of the most consequential measures. Falls with Major Injury (Long-Stay) and Long Stay Hospitalization enter SNF VBP in FY 2027 (CMS). Antipsychotic use is a Five-Star quality measure. CMS's June 2026 risk-based survey data excluded 2,867 facilities (15.22%) for having two or more residents aged 65+ coded with schizophrenia after admission, a pattern regulators read as a way to hide antipsychotic use (CMS QSO-26-14-NH). Memory care in assisted living belongs to the queued assisted-living build; this niche is the certified nursing home's long-stay unit.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Fall-detection video and sensors from SafelyYou, Teton.ai and Inspiren, mostly proven in memory care; bed and chair alarms, which surveyors discourage; behaviour-tracking sheets; pharmacy consultant reviews of psychotropics. Gaps: no record ties behaviour patterns, time of day, staffing and falls together; post-fall reviews guess at mechanism; gradual dose reduction attempts are tracked on paper.

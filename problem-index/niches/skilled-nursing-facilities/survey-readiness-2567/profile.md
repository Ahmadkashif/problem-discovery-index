# State Survey Readiness & Plans of Correction

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/skilled-nursing-facilities|Skilled Nursing Facilities]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to predict which F-tags this state's survey team will cite before the team walks in, and whoever's mock-survey findings best match the CMS-2567 that follows takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$600M a year in survey-readiness consultants and mock surveys, compliance staff time, and plan-of-correction work (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~0.3% of facility spending (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — readiness lives in binders, paper audit tools and the memories of former surveyors; the CMS-2567 arrives as a document and the plan of correction is written in a word processor
**Target Buyer:** Administrator and Director of Nursing; VP of Clinical Operations or Compliance at a chain
**Automation Potential:** Medium-High for risk prediction, document assembly and plan-of-correction drafting; low for the on-site observation that surveyors themselves do

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The standard survey is the regulator's direct look at care, required at least every 15 months. Its results anchor the Five-Star overall rating, decide eligibility for the risk-based survey launched on 8 September 2026 (1,560 of 14,682 facilities qualified, CMS QSO-26-14-NH), and escalate to civil money penalties, denial of payment for new admissions or Special Focus Facility status. Findings are public word for word, by facility and F-tag, which makes this the rare regulated workflow where the regulator's own output is open data. State agencies differ widely. In June 2026, 1,410 facilities nationally had gone more than 18 months without a standard survey, and consultants speak of what "this state's teams look for".

## Current Tools & Gaps
Consultancies running mock surveys (Pathway Health, Health Dimensions Group, CMS Compliance Group and many regional firms); EHR report packs for the entrance conference; generic audit and policy tools; public 2567 data on Care Compare and data.cms.gov. Gaps: nobody turns the public 2567 corpus into state-level citation patterns; mock surveys are not graded against the real survey that follows; plans of correction are written from scratch.

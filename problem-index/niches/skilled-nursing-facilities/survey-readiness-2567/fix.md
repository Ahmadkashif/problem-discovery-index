# The Plan of Correction Written at Midnight

**Niche:** [[niches/skilled-nursing-facilities/survey-readiness-2567/profile|State Survey Readiness & Plans of Correction]]
**Industry:** [[industries/skilled-nursing-facilities|Skilled Nursing Facilities]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** After a survey the leadership team has days to answer a long CMS-2567, and writes each plan of correction from a blank page.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #compliance #quick-win #workflow-orchestration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to predict which F-tags this state's survey team will cite before the team walks in, and whoever's mock-survey findings best match the CMS-2567 that follows takes the account.

## The Problem
The plan of correction must say for each tag how the residents affected were fixed, how others at risk were identified, what systemic change was made and how it will be monitored, and it is due within ~10 days (recalled). The administrator and DON write it at night alongside running the building, and the language that the state accepted last time is in an old file somewhere.

## Why It's Still Broken
Each plan is treated as a one-off document. There is no library of accepted corrections by tag and state, and no tracking of which corrections held at revisit.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A drafting tool that reads the 2567, pulls the facility's prior plans and accepted language for the same tags, and proposes the four required elements with monitoring tasks scheduled in the facility's audit calendar. Track revisit outcomes so the library learns which corrections hold.

## Who Feels the Pain
Administrators and DONs after every survey; staff given vague in-services; residents if the fix is cosmetic.

## Impact If Fixed
Plans of correction for an average 2567 of 9.5 deficiencies (KFF) take ~2–3 hours instead of ~2 evenings (estimate), and corrections are built on what worked rather than what sounded right.

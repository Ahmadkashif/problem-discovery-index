# Hospital Referral Intake & Admissions

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/skilled-nursing-facilities|Skilled Nursing Facilities]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to decide on a hospital referral within the hour with the payer authorisation, the cost of care and the risk of readmission already known, and whoever says yes fastest to the referrals that will be authorised, paid and not readmitted takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.6B a year in admissions coordinators, hospital liaisons, referral software and the clinical review time spent on referral packets (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~0.7% of facility spending (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — referrals arrive as faxed or portal-uploaded packets of 30–100 pages (estimate), and the accept/decline decision is made by phone between the admissions coordinator, the DON and sometimes the administrator
**Target Buyer:** Director of Admissions and Administrator; VP of Business Development at a chain
**Automation Potential:** High for packet reading, payer checks and cost estimation; the decision to accept a complex resident stays human

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Short-stay admissions are where a facility earns its margin: a 24% fee-for-service Medicare margin against 2.1% all-payer (MedPAC). Hospital case managers send the same referral to several facilities at once, and the first credible yes usually wins. The facility must judge, from an unstructured packet, whether it can care for the patient (dialysis, IV antibiotics, bariatric, behaviours), what the stay will cost (drugs, especially, which PDPM bundles into the rate), whether the MA plan will authorise it, and whether the patient is likely to bounce back to hospital. With managed care now 42% of admissions (CLA), the authorisation step sits inside the admissions decision. This is distinct from continued-stay reviews after admission, which run on a weekly clock.

## Current Tools & Gaps
The hospital-side referral platforms, and the case managers who send referrals through them, are described under [[niches/general-hospitals/case-management-post-acute-placement/profile|🟠 Case Management & Post-Acute Placement]]; this niche is the receiving end. On that side: fax, operator CRMs such as Aline's Smart Referral CRM, and new AI intake vendors such as ExaCare and Skypoint's admissions agents. Gaps: packets are read by hand, drug and supply cost is estimated from memory, readmission risk is not scored, and after-hours referrals go unanswered.

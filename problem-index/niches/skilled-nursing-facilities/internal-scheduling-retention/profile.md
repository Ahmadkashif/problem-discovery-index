# Internal Scheduling, Float Pools & Retention

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/skilled-nursing-facilities|Skilled Nursing Facilities]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to fill the facility's schedule from its own employees and float pool before any outside labour, and whoever cuts agency hours furthest without letting reported hours per resident day fall below the staffing-star cut points takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.5B a year in workforce-management software, scheduler and float-pool coordination staff, and retention programmes (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~0.7% of facility spending (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — schedules are built in software, but call-offs and fills happen by phone and text
**Target Buyer:** Director of Nursing and staffing coordinator; regional VP of Operations for float pools
**Automation Potential:** High for forecasting and shift offers; low for retention, which is about supervisors and pay

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
This is the employer's side of the labour problem: keeping the people already on payroll on the schedule. The levers are forecasting call-offs, offering open shifts to the right employees first, running a float pool across nearby buildings, and keeping new CNAs past their first months. The winners are scheduling and workforce-management vendors and the operator's own processes. Agencies and marketplaces are what this niche is trying to avoid. Success shows in PBJ: steady hours per resident day by role, a falling contract share and lower turnover. Total Nurse Staffing and Nursing Staff Turnover are both SNF VBP measures from FY 2026 (CMS). The hospital version of this work, with central staffing offices and internal float pools at system scale, is [[niches/general-hospitals/nurse-staffing-office/profile|🟠 Nurse Staffing Office & Contingent Labour]].

## Current Tools & Gaps
SmartLinx, UKG and OnShift schedules; group texting; paper sign-up sheets for open shifts. Gaps: call-offs are not forecast; open shifts are offered to everyone rather than to the most likely acceptor; float pools are coordinated by phone; and early CNA attrition is not tracked by unit, supervisor or shift.

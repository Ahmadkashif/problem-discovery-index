# First-90-Day CNA Attrition Nobody Measures by Unit

**Niche:** [[niches/skilled-nursing-facilities/internal-scheduling-retention/profile|Internal Scheduling, Float Pools & Retention]]
**Industry:** [[industries/skilled-nursing-facilities|Skilled Nursing Facilities]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** New aides quit in their first weeks on particular units and shifts, and the facility only sees an overall turnover number.
**Tags:** #survival-analysis #descriptive-statistics #hypothesis-testing #worker-facing #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to fill the facility's schedule from its own employees and float pool before any outside labour, and whoever cuts agency hours furthest without letting reported hours per resident day fall below the staffing-star cut points takes the account.

## The Problem
Facilities recruit CNAs constantly, often through their own training programmes, and lose many before they settle in (estimate). The losses cluster on hard units, on nights and under particular supervisors, but turnover is reported as one annual percentage.

## Why It's Still Broken
HR systems record hire and termination dates, not unit, shift, preceptor or first-week assignment load. Nobody owns early attrition as a metric, so nobody acts on it.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A simple survival analysis of time to separation by unit, shift, preceptor, assignment size in the first two weeks and pay rate, refreshed monthly. Each DON gets a short list of where new hires are being lost. Pair it with a standard first-90-day plan (a fixed preceptor, a capped assignment, a check-in at day 30).

## Who Feels the Pain
New aides thrown into full assignments; preceptors who train people who leave; DONs who recruit to stand still.

## Impact If Fixed
Each new aide retained past 90 days saves a recruitment and orientation cycle of ~$3,000–5,000 (estimate) and some agency hours priced up to 80% above employed aides (ASPE, 2021). It also lowers the turnover measure CMS now publishes and pays on.

# One Departure Takes the Building's Reimbursement Memory

**Niche:** [[niches/skilled-nursing-facilities/independent-operators/profile|Independent & Small-Chain Operators]]
**Industry:** [[industries/skilled-nursing-facilities|Skilled Nursing Facilities]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** When an independent's only MDS coordinator leaves, the case-mix index drops and assessments go late until a replacement learns the building.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #descriptive-statistics #workflow-orchestration #quick-win #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to stand in for the regional nurse consultant a standalone facility cannot afford, and whoever flags the most problems before a surveyor, PBJ auditor or payment reviewer finds them takes the account.

## The Problem
The coordinator knows which physicians document poorly, which therapy evaluations to check, which residents are due an assessment and how the state's Medicaid case-mix review works. None of it is written down. Her replacement starts cold, and late or incomplete assessments cost money for months.

## Why It's Still Broken
There is no backup role in a single building, and the knowledge is tacit and procedural. Chains have regional consultants to cover; independents have nobody.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A running playbook generated from the coordinator's own work: the assessment calendar, recurring documentation gaps by physician and unit, state-specific rules she follows, and open audit items. It is kept current automatically from EHR activity and handed to a temporary consultant or new hire on day one.

## Who Feels the Pain
Administrators watching revenue fall; the new coordinator learning under pressure; residents whose assessments go late.

## Impact If Fixed
A coordinator vacancy that would otherwise mean ~2–3 months of late or thin assessments (estimate) costs weeks instead. On ~$1.8M of fee-for-service Part A revenue a year (MedPAC; derived), each month of a 3% case-mix dip costs ~$4,500 (estimate), and the building is less hostage to one person.

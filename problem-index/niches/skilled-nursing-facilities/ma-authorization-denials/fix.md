# Denials Nobody Has Time to Appeal

**Niche:** [[niches/skilled-nursing-facilities/ma-authorization-denials/profile|Medicare Advantage Authorisation & Continued-Stay Reviews]]
**Industry:** [[industries/skilled-nursing-facilities|Skilled Nursing Facilities]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Appealed SNF denials are nearly always overturned, yet many are never appealed because nobody in the building has the hours.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #workflow-orchestration #revenue-impact #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep every MA resident's authorised days matched to clinical need through weekly updates and appeals, and whoever wins the most continued-stay days that hold up on plan and QIO appeal takes the account.

## The Problem
When a plan cuts days, the facility must decide quickly whether to request a peer-to-peer review, help the resident file a fast-track appeal with the QIO, or accept the cut. Each appeal means assembling records and writing a clinical argument. OIG found nearly all appealed SNF denials at the largest MA insurers were overturned (June 2026), which means most unappealed denials were probably winnable.

## Why It's Still Broken
Appeals fall to the same MDS coordinator or business office manager who handles everything else. There is no record of which denials were appealed and won, so the business case for staffing appeals is never made.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Log every denial with plan, reason and reviewer. Auto-draft the appeal or peer-to-peer brief from the record within hours, and report monthly on denials, appeals filed and days recovered by plan.

## Who Feels the Pain
Residents discharged before they are ready; MDS coordinators; administrators losing covered days.

## Impact If Fixed
Nearly all appealed SNF denials at the largest MA insurers were overturned (OIG, June 2026), so appealing turns most denials into paid days. At ~$500 a day (estimate; NIC MAP reported Medicare revenue of $588 per patient day in February 2023), a building recovering ~50 days a year gains ~$25,000 (estimate). The data also shows which plans to escalate in contract talks.

# Section GG Scored by Whoever Was on Shift

**Niche:** [[niches/skilled-nursing-facilities/mds-pdpm-reimbursement/profile|MDS Assessment & PDPM Reimbursement]]
**Industry:** [[industries/skilled-nursing-facilities|Skilled Nursing Facilities]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Functional scores that set the PT, OT and nursing rate come from three days of entries by different aides, nurses and therapists who score the same resident differently.
**Tags:** #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #compliance #quick-win #workflow-orchestration #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn what the floor documents into MDS coding that captures each resident's full PDPM and Medicaid case-mix, and whoever captures the most case-mix that still survives Medicare and Medicaid MDS audits takes the account.

## The Problem
Section GG asks for the resident's usual performance on self-care and mobility over the first three days of the stay. The physical therapist scores a transfer as needing partial help. The night CNA, alone with twenty residents, documents the same transfer as dependent because that is how she did it at 3 a.m. The MDS coordinator picks a number from conflicting entries, often on the last day of the window.

## Why It's Still Broken
Each discipline documents in its own module, with its own definitions. No step in the workflow brings them together before the window closes. Coordinators lack the time to interview every discipline for every admission, and CNAs are rarely trained on what the GG codes mean.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A short, structured GG huddle record, generated automatically on day two. It lists each item, each discipline's entries and the spread between them, so the team agrees a usual-performance score with a one-line rationale before the window closes. Track inter-discipline disagreement by unit and shift to target training.

## Who Feels the Pain
MDS coordinators defending scores; therapists whose evaluations are overridden; CNAs blamed for "inaccurate" documentation; and administrators facing paybacks when an auditor finds the conflict.

## Impact If Fixed
Scores are reconciled before the window closes on each of the ~25–40 Part A admissions a month a typical coordinator handles (estimate). That protects the PT, OT and nursing components, which are among the largest drivers of the PDPM rate, and gives auditors a documented rationale instead of a contradiction.

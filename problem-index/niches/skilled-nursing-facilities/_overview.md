# Niche Analysis — Skilled Nursing Facilities

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/skilled-nursing-facilities|Skilled Nursing Facilities]]

## Niche Selection

A skilled nursing facility is paid on what its MDS assessments capture, judged on what its PBJ staffing file reports and what state surveyors write on the CMS-2567, and filled by hospital referrals that Medicare Advantage plans increasingly authorise a few days at a time. The eight niches below split the industry by what vendors, consultants and operators' own teams actually compete over. Market sizes are estimates of the staff time, services and software spent within each niche, not of facility revenue, which ran at ~$219.9B in 2024 for nursing care facilities and CCRCs combined (CMS NHE).

## Why These Niches

The two high-market-share niches are the facility's revenue engine and its largest cost. MDS coding sets both the Medicare PDPM rate and, since the Optional State Assessment ended in September 2025, most states' Medicaid case-mix. Nursing labour is the biggest expense and the most public quality signal. The two low-digitized niches are where paper, phone and memory still run high-stakes work: preparing for an unannounced survey whose findings are then published word for word, and deciding within the hour on a faxed hospital packet. The two underserved audiences are the long-stay resident with dementia, ignored by software built around the short-stay Medicare patient but now central to SNF VBP through the long-stay falls and hospitalisation measures, and the standalone operator without a chain's regional team. The two automatable niches are the most mechanical: weekly MA continued-stay updates and appeals, and the quarterly PBJ file, both rule-bound and deadline-driven.

## Filter Notes

Seven of the eight level-1 niches are terminal. **Nurse Staffing & Labour Supply** is not. The partial sentence, staffing every shift at the hours residents need with the least premium labour, produced two contests with different winners. Keeping the facility's own employees and float pool on the schedule is won by scheduling and workforce-management vendors and by the operator's own processes, and its scorecard is steady PBJ hours and falling turnover. Supplying a credentialed clinician for a same-day hole is won by marketplaces and agencies on fill speed and show-up reliability. The buyers overlap but the competitors, data and clocks do not, so the niche decomposes into **Internal Scheduling, Float Pools & Retention** and **Per-Diem Shift Marketplaces & Agency Labour**.

Four terminal calls were close. **MDS Assessment & PDPM Reimbursement** could have split into Medicare and Medicaid, but since state Medicaid programmes moved to PDPM groupings the same coded item moves both rates, and the same coordinator and auditors decide it, so one contest governs. **Hospital Referral Intake & Admissions** and **Medicare Advantage Authorisation** both touch MA plans. They are kept apart because admission is an hour-long accept-or-lose decision on an unstructured packet, while continued-stay review is a weekly documentation and appeal cycle with a different person doing it. **PBJ Submission** sits next to internal scheduling, but scheduling plans hours forward and PBJ reports them backward against a federal file specification and audit. Different tools fail in different ways.

**Independent & Small-Chain Operators** looks as if it bundles three sibling contests (PDPM coding, PBJ reporting, survey readiness), but the standalone buyer does not buy them separately. It buys one stand-in for the chain's regional nurse consultant and judges that relationship on one result: whether problems surface inside the building before a surveyor, auditor or payer finds them. The specialist contests, fought by vendors selling mainly to chains, stay in their own niches.

Four candidates were considered and rejected. **Contract therapy management** was a large niche before PDPM decoupled payment from therapy minutes in 2019 (recalled). Its remaining contest is the cost per therapy hour, a generic price contest that fails the filter. **Infection prevention and control** is a real compliance burden, but its contest is survey defence, already captured under survey readiness. **Medicare cost report preparation** belongs to the accounting firms already logged as a Pass 2 pocket under home health, and it is linked below rather than profiled again. **Memory care units** in assisted living are the queued assisted-living build's territory; the dementia niche here is limited to certified long-stay residents measured on the MDS.

## Why These Pockets

**No pocket qualifies**, and the reason is the same single fact found in home health: the industry's analytical labour is large and almost all of it works on protected resident records that belong to someone else. The two highest scores show it. The MDS and PDPM consultancies (48) do repeatable audit work at volume and hold the only cross-facility record of which coded items survive review. Every page they read is an identified resident's chart held under the facility's business associate agreement, as for their home-health twin [[niches/home-health-agencies/oasis-coding-review-outsourcers/profile|🔍 OASIS Coding & Review Outsourcers]] (50, kill switch). They score two points lower because only their analysts count toward the insight function and only the TPE response deadline is their own clock. The survey readiness consultancies (48) score like [[niches/home-health-agencies/home-health-compliance-consulting/profile|🔍 Home Health Regulatory & Survey Consulting]] (48). The plan of correction is literally the invoice and the deadlines are federal, but their only private asset, mock-survey findings paired with the real 2567, is client work product built from resident records under a BAA. The long-term care pharmacies (45), the state case-mix auditors (44), the EHR data teams (43), the compliance software vendors (43) and the operators' own analytics groups (43) repeat the pattern: rich, scoreable data, fenced by HIPAA, client ownership or both.

Several payer-side and adjacent pockets are already in the vault and score the same way, so they are linked rather than profiled again. Referral-pattern and market-share analytics for post-acute providers, including SNFs, are [[niches/home-health-agencies/post-acute-market-analytics/profile|🔍 Post-Acute Market & Benchmarking Analytics]] (49). The cost-report preparers whose annual CLA SNF cost comparison draws on ~11,800 FY 2025 cost reports are [[niches/home-health-agencies/medicare-cost-report-firms/profile|🔍 Medicare Cost Report Preparation]] (43). Medicare's own reviewers are [[niches/home-health-agencies/medicare-claims-review-contractors/profile|🔍 Medicare Claims Review Contractors]] (49, kill switch) and [[niches/medical-billing/cms-program-integrity/profile|🔍 Federal Programme Integrity Contractors]] (49, kill switch). The MA utilisation managers who cut SNF days are profiled from the home-health side as [[niches/home-health-agencies/ma-plan-utilization-management/profile|🔍 Medicare Advantage Home Health Utilization Management]] (46, kill switch), and from the plan's side as the contested sub-niche [[niches/health-insurance-carriers/inpatient-post-acute-review/profile|🎯 Inpatient & Post-Acute Concurrent Review]]. U.S. News's Best Nursing Homes ratings come from the same health ratings team that publishes its hospital ratings, so they are profiled under general hospitals as [[niches/general-hospitals/hospital-quality-ratings-publishers/profile|🔍 Hospital Quality Ratings Publishers]] (33). Nursing home negligence litigation runs through [[niches/personal-injury-law/medical-record-review-chronology/profile|🔍 Medical Record Retrieval & Chronology Services]] (52, kill switch). Federal payment policy research is [[niches/health-insurance-carriers/medpac-macpac/profile|🔍 MedPAC & MACPAC]] (30). Star forecasting on public data, the closest analogue to a Five-Star forecasting shop, is logged as [[niches/health-insurance-carriers/stars-forecasting-consultancies/profile|🔍 Star Ratings Forecasting & Quality Strategy Consultancies]] (43).

The strongest clean near-miss is senior care occupancy data (43). NIC MAP sells the analysis itself, from operator-contributed data published in aggregate by design, but on its own monthly calendar and with a small skilled nursing team. The other clean pockets (association research at 37, REIT and lender underwriting at 29, marketplace data teams at 27) and the watchdog projects that fail the gate show the rest of the map. PBJ daily staffing, Care Compare and CMS-2567 text are unusually rich, facility-identified and free, yet nobody with a 10-person team sells a graded product on them. For a research-insight engine that means no buyer here. For a builder it means the public-data products described in the industry's agents note face little incumbent competition, and REITs, lenders, MA plans and chains are the buyers.

# Agency Hours That Never Reach the PBJ File

**Niche:** [[niches/skilled-nursing-facilities/pbj-staffing-reporting/profile|PBJ Submission & Staffing Data Reconciliation]]
**Industry:** [[industries/skilled-nursing-facilities|Skilled Nursing Facilities]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A facility pays premium rates for agency shifts and then leaves them out of its staffing report, so it looks understaffed on the very days it spent most.
**Tags:** #data-integration #descriptive-statistics #compliance #quick-win #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn payroll, timeclock and agency invoices into a PBJ file that matches the hours actually worked, and whoever's submissions pass CMS's PBJ audits and never cost the facility a staffing star takes the account.

## The Problem
Agency invoices arrive weeks after the shifts, sometimes after the 45-day deadline, and in formats the payroll export cannot read. Payroll staff submit what they have. The missing hours depress the staffing measures for the quarter.

## Why It's Still Broken
The person who submits PBJ (payroll) is not the person who books agency (the staffing coordinator), and neither owns the reconciliation. Contracts rarely require agencies to deliver hours in PBJ-ready form.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Require daily hours files from every agency and marketplace in a standard layout as a contract term. Reconcile them weekly against sign-in records. Run a quarter-end check that compares invoiced contract hours with submitted contract hours before the deadline.

## Who Feels the Pain
Administrators whose staffing star drops for hours they paid for; payroll managers chasing invoices; families reading Care Compare.

## Impact If Fixed
Reported staffing matches actual staffing, recovering up to the ~5% of nursing hours that go unreported when agency invoices are missed (estimate, on LTCCC's 5.3% contract share). That protects the star, VBP staffing scores and risk-based survey eligibility at almost no cost.

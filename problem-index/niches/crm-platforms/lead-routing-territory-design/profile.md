# Lead Routing & Territory Design

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/crm-platforms|CRM Platforms]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in routing and territory software is fighting to assign a lead or an account to the representative who will actually convert it — and whoever can evidence that a design outperforms the incumbent one takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$980M US spend across lead routing, territory management and account assignment tooling
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~3% of CRM revenue, governing the allocation of every opportunity
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — the rules engines are mature and the logic inside them is politics
**Target Buyer:** Revenue operations, who own the rules and did not write most of them
**Automation Potential:** Very High — assignment is a matching problem with clean outcome data and it is a decision tree

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Every lead and every account is assigned to someone, and the assignment substantially determines whether it converts — representatives differ in skill, in segment fit, in relationship, in current load and in how quickly they respond. The mechanism that makes these assignments is a rules tree that has accumulated over years: geographic splits, named account carve-outs, segment definitions, round-robin fallbacks and exceptions granted to individuals. Revenue operations maintains it and is reluctant to change it, because the tree encodes a negotiated settlement between sales leaders about who owns what, and any change reopens the negotiation. So the routing logic is treated as a constraint rather than as a decision, and the question of whether a different design would produce more revenue is not asked — despite the organisation having thousands of assignments with outcomes attached, which is exactly the data required to answer it.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Routing is built into every CRM and is extended by specialist vendors — LeanData, Chili Piper, Distribution Engine — which handle matching, round-robin, capacity and speed-to-lead competently. Territory management and quota planning tooling exists. The gaps: no product evaluates routing outcomes, so no organisation knows whether its rules beat an alternative; representative fit — who converts which kind of lead — is not modelled despite being highly measurable; capacity is handled as a count rather than as an effect, so an overloaded representative receives leads at the same rate as an idle one and the conversion cost is invisible; and territory design is an annual planning exercise conducted in a spreadsheet with fairness as the objective rather than revenue.

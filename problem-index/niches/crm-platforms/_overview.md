## Why These Niches

Every CRM ships a forecast and every sales leader overrides it with a spreadsheet. That single fact defines the category's largest block: the system holds the pipeline, the activity and the outcomes, and its prediction is trusted less than a regional VP's instinct — because the input is a stage and a close date typed by someone compensated for selling rather than for accuracy. Forecasting from behaviour rather than from self-report is the contested capability, and the incumbents ceded it to a new category on data flowing through their own platforms.

That new category, revenue intelligence, **failed the filter as one niche**. Conversation intelligence records and analyses what was said in a call — a speech and language problem with consent, coaching and privacy dimensions that dominate the product design. Activity capture builds the engagement graph from email, calendar and other metadata — an entity resolution and completeness problem with no recording and an entirely different set of objections. Different data, different competitors, different failure modes. Decomposed below.

Field sales and nonprofit CRM are the two underdigitised segments, each because the horizontal product assumes a seller at a desk: a distributor's representative works from a vehicle, and a development office's central question is what caused a gift rather than what stage a deal is at. Representatives and customer success are the two underserved constituencies — the rep feeds the system and receives nothing back, and the whole category was built for acquisition with the post-sale motion bolted on afterwards.

The automation niches are the category's two permanent operational burdens: a routing and territory rules tree that encodes a political truce rather than an optimisation, and an account graph that enrichment vendors keep firmographically current and structurally wrong.

## Filter Notes

**Niche 2 failed the filter as stated and was decomposed.** "Revenue intelligence" bundles two products that share a buyer and nothing else. Conversation intelligence is built on recorded calls: its contested capability is turning what was said into coaching a manager will act on and deal signal a forecast can use, and its hardest constraints are consent, two-party recording law and the fact that representatives experience it as surveillance. Activity capture is built on email and calendar metadata: its contested capability is completeness of the engagement graph without any representative effort, and its hardest problems are contact and account resolution and a different privacy objection entirely. Both sub-niches are terminal and are written in full.

**Niches 1, 3–8 are terminal as stated.** Each names something testable: forecast accuracy against the leadership override, sales call capture without typing from a vehicle, attribution of a gift to its cause, whether a representative uses the system unprompted, renewal risk identified with enough lead time to act, territory design evaluated against outcomes, and the share of the account graph that is structurally correct.

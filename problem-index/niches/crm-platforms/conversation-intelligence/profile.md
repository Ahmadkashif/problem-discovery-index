# Conversation Intelligence

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/crm-platforms|CRM Platforms]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in conversation intelligence is fighting to turn a recorded sales call into coaching a manager acts on and deal signal a forecast can use — and whoever converts recordings into acted-upon change takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$2.1B US conversation intelligence and call analytics for revenue teams
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~6% of CRM revenue
**Digital Adoption:** High in enterprise sales, lower in mid-market and field organisations
**Target Buyer:** Sales leadership and enablement; legal and compliance as a gating stakeholder
**Automation Potential:** Very High — transcription and analysis are commodity and the downstream use is thin

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Recording sales conversations turned a previously unobservable activity into data, and the category that did it grew rapidly on that basis. The defining characteristics of the sub-niche all follow from the recording: consent is a legal requirement that varies by jurisdiction and applies to the customer as well as the representative; representatives experience being recorded differently from being tracked, and the objection is stronger and more legitimate; the data is rich but the volume per deal is small, since a deal may have six calls; and the analysis that matters is not what was said but what should have been said differently, which is a coaching judgement rather than an extraction. The competitors here are building speech and language products under a consent constraint, which is a different business from the metadata product in the sibling sub-niche and attracts different objections.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Gong and Chorus established the category and remain dominant; the conferencing platforms have added native recording and summarisation; the CRM incumbents integrate rather than compete directly. Transcription, speaker separation and topic detection all work well. The gaps: analysis is largely descriptive — talk ratios, question counts, topic coverage — which is measurable rather than useful, as the parent niche describes; coaching is delivered as clips and scorecards and its effect is unmeasured; competitor and objection intelligence, which is the most valuable content in the corpus, is surfaced by keyword search rather than synthesised; and consent handling is generally a configuration setting rather than a designed workflow, which in two-party jurisdictions leaves real exposure with the customer.

# Enterprise Sales Forecasting

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/crm-platforms|CRM Platforms]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in sales forecasting is fighting to produce a number a sales leader will submit without rebuilding it in a spreadsheet — and whoever forecasts most accurately from unfalsifiable behaviour rather than from self-reported stage takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$6.8B US attributable to forecasting, pipeline management and revenue planning within CRM
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~19% of CRM revenue
**Digital Adoption:** High — every platform ships a forecast and almost nobody submits it
**Target Buyer:** Chief revenue officers, sales leadership and revenue operations
**Automation Potential:** Very High — the outcome history is abundant and the behavioural predictors are already captured

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The forecast is the number a public company reports and a private one raises against, and it is produced by a process every participant knows to be unreliable. A representative sets a stage and a close date. A manager applies a discount they have developed by feel. A second-line leader applies another. Revenue operations assembles it in a spreadsheet in the last week of the quarter. The system that was bought to produce this number holds the pipeline, the activity history and the outcomes of tens of thousands of prior deals, and its own forecast is overridden as a matter of routine. The reason is upstream and well understood: the stage and the close date are opinions submitted by someone compensated for selling, and everything downstream inherits that. The behavioural signal — who engaged, how quickly they responded, how many people from the buying side are involved, whether momentum is accelerating — is unfalsifiable and is mostly flowing through the same platform.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Salesforce, HubSpot and Dynamics all ship forecasting modules with pipeline roll-ups, weighted stages and scenario views. Clari and the revenue intelligence vendors have built businesses specifically on forecasting from activity rather than from stage, which is the clearest possible statement about where the incumbents failed. The consistent gaps: forecast accuracy is not measured and published back, so nobody knows whether the system, the manager or the leader is the best predictor — which is a computable question every company could answer and almost none do; the manager's override is not recorded as a distinct prediction, so the judgement that actually produces the submitted number is never evaluated; and deal-level probability is presented as a stage weighting rather than as a calibrated estimate with an interval, which is why nobody treats it as information.

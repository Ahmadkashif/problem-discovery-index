# Field Sales & Distributor CRM

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/crm-platforms|CRM Platforms]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in field sales software is fighting to capture what happened on a call from a vehicle in under a minute without typing — and whoever the representatives actually use takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.9B US software spend across distributor, wholesale and consumer goods field sales organisations
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~5% of CRM revenue, against a very large field workforce
**Digital Adoption:** Low — the CRM is entered on a laptop at home in the evening, when it is entered at all
**Target Buyer:** Sales leadership at distributors, manufacturer representative organisations and consumer goods field teams
**Automation Potential:** Very High — the whole administrative load falls on someone in a vehicle

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Field sales is a different job from the one horizontal CRM was designed around. A distributor's representative visits fifteen accounts in a day, takes orders, checks stock, resolves a delivery problem, places point-of-sale material and notices what a competitor is doing on the shelf. A consumer goods field team does store visits with a compliance checklist. The transaction is frequently the order itself rather than an opportunity in a pipeline, and the relationship is continuous rather than a deal with stages. The representative works from a vehicle with a phone, often in a building with poor signal, with their hands occupied. Horizontal CRM assumes a seller at a desk moving a small number of large deals through stages, which describes almost nothing about this work — so the field organisation buys the CRM leadership wants and the representatives enter it at home, badly, or not at all.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Salesforce and its partner ecosystem serve larger field organisations with heavy customisation; specialist field sales and retail execution products serve consumer goods; many distributors run their ERP's sales module plus a spreadsheet. The gaps: capture in the field requires typing into forms designed for a desktop, so the record is created hours later from memory; order entry, which is the actual transaction, is frequently in a different system from the CRM; competitive and shelf observations — the most valuable thing a field representative sees and the reason the job exists — are captured as free text or not at all; and route and call planning is built from a territory list and the representative's habit rather than from any measure of which accounts need a visit.

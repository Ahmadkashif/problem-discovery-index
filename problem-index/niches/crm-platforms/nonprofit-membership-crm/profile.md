# Nonprofit & Membership CRM

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/crm-platforms|CRM Platforms]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in donor and member software is fighting to attribute a gift or a renewal to what actually caused it — and whoever answers that credibly takes the development office.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.4B US software spend across nonprofit development, membership and association management
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~4% of CRM revenue, against a very large number of organisations
**Digital Adoption:** Low-Medium — the database exists and the practice around it is manual
**Target Buyer:** Development directors, membership directors and executive directors
**Automation Potential:** High — attribution, lapse prediction and segmentation are all computable and are done by instinct

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A development office's entire question is different from a sales organisation's. There is no pipeline with stages and no close date. A donor gives, or does not, in response to some combination of an appeal letter, an event, a relationship with a board member, a programme story they read, a tax consideration, a life event and an accumulated sense of the organisation's worth. Attributing a gift to a cause is genuinely harder than attributing a sale to a campaign, and it matters more, because a development office allocates a small budget across appeals, events and relationship-building with no way to tell which works. Membership organisations have the parallel problem with renewals. Both segments are chronically under-resourced, buy software last, and are served by products that are competent databases and weak instruments.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Blackbaud anchors the enterprise nonprofit market; Salesforce's nonprofit offering, Bloomerang, Neon, Little Green Light and a long tail serve the middle and small end; association management systems serve membership organisations. All handle constituent records, gift entry, receipting and basic segmentation competently. The gaps: appeal attribution is single-touch or non-existent, so the annual appeal gets credit for gifts a major gift officer spent a year cultivating; lapse prediction — which donors are about to stop giving — is not offered, despite lapse being the sector's dominant retention problem and the giving history being a clean behavioural signal; major gift capacity and propensity are assessed by wealth screening vendors and by an officer's judgement, with no feedback on which assessments proved right; and the cost of fundraising by channel, which is the number boards actually ask about, is computed annually in aggregate if at all.

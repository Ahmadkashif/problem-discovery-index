# The System That Gives Something Back

**Niche:** [[niches/crm-platforms/sales-rep-tools/profile|Sales Representative Tools]]
**Industry:** [[industries/crm-platforms|CRM Platforms]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A representative types into the CRM for several hours a week and receives from it a set of required fields and a dashboard about themselves, which is why the data the entire industry's analytics rest on is entered badly.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #gradient-boosting #survival-analysis #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #workflow-orchestration #worker-facing #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor building for representatives is fighting to make the CRM something a seller opens because it helps them rather than because they are required to — and whoever the representatives use unprompted takes the seat.

## The Problem
A representative has a call in ten minutes with an account they last spoke to in June. What they need is the account's history, who was on the last call and what was said, what was promised and by whom, what the account bought and when it renews, what support tickets are open, and whether anything has changed on the customer's side. All of that is in the company's systems. Assembling it takes fifteen minutes across four tabs, so the representative does it for important calls and improvises for the rest. Meanwhile the same system requires them to update three fields afterwards for a report they will never see.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The purchase decision is made by leadership on visibility, and representative usefulness has never been a scored requirement in an evaluation. Vendors optimise for what wins deals. The sales engagement category grew rapidly by being genuinely useful to representatives, which demonstrates both that the demand exists and that the incumbents did not meet it. There is also a structural assumption worth naming: representatives are required to use the CRM, so there has never been commercial pressure to make them want to.

## What to Build
Invert the interface around the representative's own day. Meeting preparation assembled automatically before every external meeting on the calendar — account history, relationship map, prior commitments, open issues, renewal dates, recent news — delivered where the representative will see it rather than requiring them to go and get it. Their book analysed: which accounts have gone quiet relative to their own pattern, which commitments are overdue, where the buying committee has thinned, ranked by what it is worth. The day's priorities as a short list rather than a queue. Post-meeting capture as a by-product of the activity that already happened rather than as a form, which is the activity capture sub-niche's job and belongs here in delivery. Nothing that exists only to produce a management report should be asked of the representative at all; those fields should be derived or dropped. The measured objective is unprompted usage — whether representatives open the product when nobody requires it — which is the honest test and one no vendor currently reports.

## Target Customer
CRM platform vendors, sales engagement vendors extending their footprint, and directly the sales organisations whose data quality problem is a design problem wearing a compliance costume.

## Impact If Built
Data quality is the root constraint on every analytical capability in this industry and it is a consequence of asking people to do unpaid work for someone else's benefit. A system that helps the representative gets fed accurately as a by-product, which is worth more to the organisation than any enforcement policy and is the only durable fix.

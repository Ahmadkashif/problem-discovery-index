# Collections & Vehicle Recovery

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/rideshare-fleet-operators|Rideshare Fleet Operators]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Whether the response to a missed payment is chosen against what it will actually recover, or is just the next step in a sequence nobody has evaluated.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$480M — 8% of US rideshare fleet services
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~8%
**Digital Adoption:** Low — phone calls, a spreadsheet and a repossession agent
**Target Buyer:** Fleet collections staff and owners
**Automation Potential:** Moderate — sequencing and outcome measurement automate well; the conversations should not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche

When a payment is missed the operator has a sequence: call, call again, warn, disable the vehicle remotely, recover it. The sequence is the same regardless of the driver, the reason or the likely outcome, and it escalates on a calendar rather than on evidence.

The niche is distinct because the enforcement action destroys the repayment capacity. Recovering the vehicle from a driver who is two weeks behind ends their ability to earn, converts a recoverable arrears into a write-off, produces an idle asset, and costs the recovery fee. Sometimes it is the right call. It is currently made by default, at a fixed point in a sequence, without any estimate of what continuing would have recovered.

It separates from pricing because pricing determines how many arrears arise and this is about what to do once they have.

## Current Tools & Gaps

Payment systems with automatic debit and failure alerts. Starter-interrupt and GPS devices allowing remote disablement and location, widely deployed and legally constrained in several states. Third-party repossession agents. A spreadsheet or a fleet management module tracking arrears. The conversations are phone calls made by whoever has time.

The gaps are prediction and measurement. Nobody predicts which arrears will cure — which is highly predictable from payment history, telematics and tenure. Nobody measures which interventions worked, so the sequence is never refined. And the decision to disable or recover has no expected-value calculation behind it, despite being the most consequential and most reversible-until-taken action in the business.

# The Fleet Manager

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/rideshare-fleet-operators|Rideshare Fleet Operators]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Whether the person running the fleet's week spends it on foreseeable problems or on the ones that were already avoidable.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$600M — 10% of US rideshare fleet services
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~10%
**Digital Adoption:** Low — a phone, a spreadsheet and four systems that do not talk
**Target Buyer:** Fleet owners and operations managers
**Automation Potential:** High — most of the week is coordination that software does well

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche

A fleet manager spends the week phoning drivers about missed payments, arranging repairs, recovering vehicles that have stopped moving, and knowing most of it was foreseeable.

This is the operator's single largest labour cost and the bottleneck on growth: a fleet grows until the manager cannot hold it in their head, and then stops. The role is a coordination function across four unconnected systems — telematics, payments, maintenance and contracts — performed by a person with a phone and a spreadsheet, under constant interruption.

The niche is distinct because the subject is the work itself rather than any one of the domains it spans. The maintenance niche is about intervals; this is about the fact that the person scheduling the service is also the person taking the arrears call and meeting the new renter, and has no system that shows them what matters most this morning.

## Current Tools & Gaps

Telematics for vehicles, a payment system, a maintenance tracker or spreadsheet, a contract folder, and a phone. Larger operators use a fleet management platform that consolidates some of this. Communication with drivers is by call and text, largely unlogged.

The gaps are consolidation, prioritisation and memory. There is no single view of a driver showing their vehicle, payments, hours, maintenance and history together. There is no ranked list of what needs attention today. And there is no institutional record — when a manager leaves, everything they knew about which drivers are reliable and which vehicles are troublesome leaves with them, because none of it was ever written anywhere.

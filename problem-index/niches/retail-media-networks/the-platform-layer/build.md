# Everyone Licensed the Same Stack

**Niche:** [[niches/retail-media-networks/the-platform-layer/profile|The Platform Layer]]
**Industry:** [[industries/retail-media-networks|Retail Media Networks]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Every network below the top tier runs the same licensed auction and differentiates on data it has not learned to use.

## The Problem
A regional grocer launches a retail media network. They license a platform, connect their catalogue and identity graph, and open for business. So does the chain across the state, on the same platform, with the same auction, the same bidding controls, the same reporting and the same ranking logic. The only thing that differs is the shopper data, which the platform was not built to exploit and which the retailer has no way to use independently. Brands see interchangeable networks, buy on reach and price, and the retailer's genuine asset — knowing what their shoppers actually buy — produces no advantage at all.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Building a decisioning stack is expensive and licensing was the only way to launch quickly, which was correct at the time and has become a trap. Vendors serve many retailers and cannot build retailer-specific economics into a shared product without conflicts. Retailers lack the engineering capacity to extend what they licensed. And a network that is merely running produces revenue, which removes the urgency.

## What to Build
Give retailers a decisioning layer they actually own. Separate the auction mechanics, which are commodity, from the ranking objective, which is the retailer's own economics — this separation is the structural insight and it is what lets a retailer differentiate without rebuilding an exchange. Let the retailer express their true objective, combining relevance, margin, inventory position, private label strategy and long-run basket value, which is the first sub-niche's contest and is the thing no shared vendor platform will ever express. Make the shopper data usable in decisioning rather than only in audience building, since it is the retailer's only real asset and is currently a segmentation tool. Build the advertiser surface as a product rather than a console, which is the second sub-niche's contest and is where the brand's experience of the network is formed. Keep the retailer's data inside their own boundary, which matters commercially and increasingly legally, and is a real weakness of the licensed model. Support experimentation natively, since a network that cannot run a holdout cannot answer the category's central question. Provide the merchant-facing view alongside the advertiser-facing one, because the retailer has two customers and the licensed platforms serve one. Integrate with merchandising and supply systems, as the objective depends on margin and stock that live outside the media stack. Allow a phased migration from a licensed platform, since a rebuild is not a viable proposition for most retailers and an incremental path is the only sellable one. And measure what differentiation is worth — the yield difference between an objective-aware ranking and bid times predicted click — because that comparison is the entire business case.

## Target Customer
Retail media networks below the top tier, grocery and specialty retailers launching networks, and the platform vendors whose customers are undifferentiated by construction.

## Impact If Built
A retailer's only real asset is knowing what their shoppers buy, and the licensed stack turns it into a segmentation tool. Separating commodity auction mechanics from a retailer-owned ranking objective is what lets differentiation happen without rebuilding an exchange.

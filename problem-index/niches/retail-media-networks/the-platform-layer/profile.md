# The Platform Layer

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/retail-media-networks|Retail Media Networks]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** This niche is not terminal — what a sponsored slot should be ranked by and what a brand's media team needs to operate a network are different contests with different winners, and they are stated separately in the sub-niches below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$13B US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~22% of category spend
**Digital Adoption:** High — licensed and undifferentiated
**Target Buyer:** Retail media product leadership
**Automation Potential:** Very High — both halves are software contests

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Below the top tier, almost every network runs on a licensed platform from the same handful of vendors and differentiates on data it has not yet learned to use. The platform layer is therefore where the category's competitive position is actually decided: a retailer with better data and the same stack as everyone else is not differentiated, and the vendor's roadmap is the retailer's roadmap. This makes it the second-largest concentration of contested value in the category.

It is also **not terminal**. The label names a product category rather than a contest, and writing the contested statement produces two sentences describing different companies winning. One fight is over decisioning: every network licenses the same auction technology and ranks on bid times predicted click, while the retailer's actual objective is a mix of relevance, margin, inventory position, private label and long-run basket that no vendor platform expresses. The other is over the advertiser's working surface: whether a brand's media team can plan, buy, optimise and report across the network without a spreadsheet and a phone call, which is a workflow contest with a different buyer and a different winner. The two contests are stated in the sub-niches.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Licensed retail media platforms, self-service campaign consoles, keyword and product-level bidding, and standard reporting exports. The gaps: a ranking objective that cannot express the retailer's economics; no differentiation between retailers on the same stack; advertiser interfaces built for specialists; reporting that arrives as exports; and roadmaps set by a vendor serving competitors simultaneously.

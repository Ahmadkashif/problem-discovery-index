# The Roadmap You Do Not Control

**Niche:** [[niches/retail-media-networks/the-platform-layer/profile|The Platform Layer]]
**Industry:** [[industries/retail-media-networks|Retail Media Networks]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The retailer's competitive differentiation is a feature request in a vendor's backlog, prioritised against the same request from the retailer across the state.
**Tags:** #workflow-orchestration #data-integration #evaluation-metrics #compliance #revenue-impact #quick-win #descriptive-statistics #automation
**Contested on:** This niche is not terminal — what a sponsored slot should be ranked by and what a brand's media team needs to operate a network are different contests with different winners, and they are stated separately in the sub-niches.

## The Problem
The retailer identifies something that would genuinely differentiate their network — using basket affinity in ranking, or exposing a merchant-facing margin view, or a specific measurement their brands keep asking for. It is not a feature of the licensed platform. They file a request. The vendor serves forty retailers, several of whom compete with this one, and prioritises what benefits the most customers, which by definition is what benefits nobody in particular. Eighteen months later the feature ships to everyone simultaneously, at which point it is table stakes rather than a differentiator. The retailer's product strategy is an input to someone else's roadmap.

## Why It's Still Broken
The licensing decision was made as a launch decision, on speed and cost, without anyone framing it as a long-term strategic dependency — that framing gap is the root of it. Switching costs rise every quarter as more is built on top. Vendors reasonably optimise for their whole customer base. And the retailer has no engineering capacity to build an alternative, which makes the dependency feel unavoidable.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Take back the parts that matter and leave the rest. Identify which capabilities are genuinely differentiating and which are commodity, which is the fix and is a one-day exercise that most networks have never done — the answer is almost always that decisioning and measurement are differentiating and auction plumbing is not. Own the ranking objective specifically, even while continuing to license the auction, since the objective is the retailer's economics and can be expressed as a scoring layer on top of a vendor's candidate set. Keep shopper data inside the retailer's boundary, which is both a strategic and an increasingly legal requirement and is the thing hardest to reverse later. Negotiate data portability and export rights explicitly at renewal, because the practical lock-in is the data rather than the software. Build measurement independently, as it is the category's most contested output and should not be produced by a vendor serving competitors. Pool with non-competing retailers on shared needs, which is how smaller networks can reach parity on commodity capability without funding it alone. Insist on extension points rather than features, since a vendor can supply a hook far faster than a roadmap item. Cost the dependency explicitly in strategic terms at each renewal, which nobody does and which changes the negotiation. Stage any migration by capability rather than attempting a replacement, because a wholesale rebuild will not be approved. And measure yield against comparable networks, which is the only way to know whether the dependency is costing anything.

## Who Feels the Pain
Retail media networks that are indistinguishable from their competitors; brands choosing between identical offerings on price; and retailers whose best asset is inaccessible to their own product strategy.

## Impact If Fixed
A launch decision on speed and cost became a permanent strategic dependency because nobody framed it as one. Owning the ranking objective as a scoring layer over a licensed auction recovers the differentiating half without a rebuild.

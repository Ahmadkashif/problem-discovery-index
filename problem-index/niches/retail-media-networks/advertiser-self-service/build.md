# Eleven Consoles and a Spreadsheet

**Niche:** [[niches/retail-media-networks/advertiser-self-service/profile|Advertiser Self-Service]]
**Industry:** [[industries/retail-media-networks|Retail Media Networks]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A brand operates eleven retail networks with eleven taxonomies, eleven reporting schemas and eleven account managers, and consolidates spend wherever the paperwork is lightest.
**Tags:** #workflow-orchestration #data-integration #automation #evaluation-metrics #revenue-impact #worker-facing #descriptive-statistics #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to let a brand's media team plan, buy, optimise and report across a retail network without a spreadsheet and a phone call — and whoever does that gets the spend that currently goes wherever the paperwork is easiest.

## The Problem
A brand's retail media manager runs campaigns at eleven retailers. Monday is spent exporting reports from each console into a spreadsheet with a different shape each time, mapping products to each retailer's taxonomy, reconciling three definitions of a conversion and adjusting bids one console at a time. Planning a national promotion means eleven separate builds. Reporting to the brand's leadership means normalising eleven schemas by hand. The manager has capacity for perhaps four networks properly, so four networks get the spend, and which four has more to do with tooling than with performance.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Each network builds its own console and has no incentive to make cross-network operation easy, since friction concentrates spend in whoever the brand has capacity for. Standardisation attempts have stalled because the largest networks gain from incompatibility. Third-party aggregators depend on interfaces the networks control and can withdraw. And the burden falls on a brand-side manager with no budget authority.

## What to Build
Build the layer the brand actually needs. Normalise taxonomies, metrics and reporting across networks into one model, which is the foundation and is the unglamorous mapping work that everything else requires. Provide a single planning and build surface that deploys to every network, since a national promotion should be constructed once rather than eleven times and this alone recovers most of the manager's week. Optimise across networks rather than within them, because the brand's budget is one pool and the current structure forces it to be managed as eleven. Reconcile conflicting definitions explicitly and show which network measures what, rather than silently averaging numbers that mean different things. Automate the mechanical bid and budget work against a stated objective, which is the same argument as the media trader's and applies more acutely here. Support approval, budget and invoice workflows, since these are handled in email and are a real part of why a network gets dropped. Handle product taxonomy mapping automatically, as it is the single most repetitive task and is a matching problem rather than a judgement. Give the brand a cross-network incrementality view, connecting to the measurement work, since networks will never provide a comparable figure themselves. Make onboarding a new network cheap, which is the thing that decides whether a smaller retailer ever gets a budget. And measure operating hours per network, because that number explains the category's spend distribution better than performance does.

## Target Customer
Brand retail media teams and agencies, retail media networks below the top tier who lose spend to operational friction, and the aggregation vendors serving them.

## Impact If Built
A brand-side manager has capacity for four networks and the other seven lose the budget on paperwork rather than performance. One build deployed to every network, with taxonomies and metrics normalised, recovers the week and makes a smaller retailer viable to buy.

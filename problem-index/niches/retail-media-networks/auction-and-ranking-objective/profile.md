# Auction & Ranking Objective

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/retail-media-networks|Retail Media Networks]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to rank a sponsored slot by the retailer's real economics rather than by bid times predicted click — and whoever expresses that objective properly earns more from the same inventory while giving the shopper a better page.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$7B US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~12% of category spend
**Digital Adoption:** High — one objective, universally applied
**Target Buyer:** Retail media product and merchandising leadership
**Automation Potential:** Very High — a pure optimisation contest

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Every network licenses the same auction technology and ranks on bid times predicted click, while the retailer's actual objective is a mix of relevance, margin, inventory, private label and long-run basket that no vendor platform expresses. This is a self-contained optimisation contest with an unambiguous scoreboard — yield per page against experience cost — and it is won by whoever models the retailer's economics best. It requires no advertiser adoption, no new sales motion and no partnership, which is what separates it entirely from the advertiser-facing contest next door.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Generalised second-price auctions ranked on bid times predicted click, coarse relevance floors, manual slot reservations, and category-level ad load caps. The gaps: no margin in the objective; no inventory or local stock position; private label strategy handled by manual carve-outs; no long-run basket value; no accounting for displaced organic sales; and no way for the retailer to state a trade-off at all.

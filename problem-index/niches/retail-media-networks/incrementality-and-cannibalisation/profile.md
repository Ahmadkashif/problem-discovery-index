# Incrementality & Cannibalisation

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/retail-media-networks|Retail Media Networks]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to report what a sponsored placement actually caused, net of the organic sale it displaced — and whoever brands trust to produce that number sets the terms on which the category's spend is renewed.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$16B US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~27% of category spend
**Digital Adoption:** Low — perfect data, proxy metric
**Target Buyer:** Retailer measurement leadership and brand media finance
**Automation Potential:** Very High — the experiment is trivially runnable

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The retailer shows the ad and rings the sale in the same system, and still reports a return on spend that counts shoppers who searched the brand by name and subtracts nothing for the organic sale the advertisement displaced. This is the largest concentration of contested value in the category because it determines whether tens of billions of spend is renewed. It is also the category's most unusual contest: nothing technical stands in the way. The retailer can run the experiment on any Tuesday. What stands in the way is that the reported number funds a profit pool the retailer's earnings depend on, which makes independent, credible measurement a product with a buyer on the other side of the table.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Last-click attributed return on ad spend, platform-reported sales lift, occasional holdout studies run by the retailer, and clean rooms for brand-side analysis. The gaps: no subtraction of displaced organic sales; branded-search conversions counted in full; experiments run by the party being measured; no standard methodology across retailers; and no continuous measurement between studies.

# Ad Load Economics

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/retail-media-networks|Retail Media Networks]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to put a number on what advertising load costs in basket size and return visits over a year — and whoever measures that tells the category how much of its profit engine is borrowed from next year.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$5.5B US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~9% of category spend
**Digital Adoption:** Low — revenue instrumented, cost not
**Target Buyer:** Retailer executive and customer experience leadership
**Automation Potential:** Very High — the data is complete and the experiment is runnable

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Every sponsored placement costs something in shopper experience — a slot that would have shown a better-matched or better-margin product, a results page that is now a quarter advertising. That cost lands in basket size and return visits, over months, at a horizon no campaign report covers, while the revenue lands this week. This is the category's second-order question and the one nobody can answer about their own business. It is distinct from incrementality, which asks whether a campaign worked; this asks what the whole apparatus costs the retailer, and it is measurable by the only party with both sides of the ledger.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Ad load caps set by judgement, relevance floors, occasional experience surveys, and revenue reporting by placement. The gaps: no measurement of the long-run cost; experiments run over days rather than months; no link between exposure history and subsequent basket and visit behaviour; caps set by convention; and a horizon mismatch between where the revenue and the cost land.

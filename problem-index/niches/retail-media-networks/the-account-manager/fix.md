# The Advice Nobody Recorded

**Niche:** [[niches/retail-media-networks/the-account-manager/profile|The Account Manager]]
**Industry:** [[industries/retail-media-networks|Retail Media Networks]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The account manager makes a hundred recommendations a quarter, nobody records which were taken or what happened, so the network never learns what advice is worth giving.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #hypothesis-testing #workflow-orchestration #quick-win #worker-facing #confidence-intervals #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to take report-building and bid-tweaking off the account manager so they can give the advice the brand is paying for — and whoever does that changes how many brands one person can genuinely serve.

## The Problem
Across a network, account managers make thousands of recommendations a quarter: increase the budget here, shift to this keyword set, change this creative, extend into this category, reduce frequency. Some are taken, some are not. Some work, some do not. None of it is recorded. The network therefore has no idea which of its advice is good, which managers give better advice than others, or what to tell a new manager on their first day. Every account manager is independently rediscovering what works, in a business that has the outcome data to settle it and has never collected the input side.

## Why It's Still Broken
Recommendations live in slide decks and emails, which are not a data structure — this is the whole mechanical cause. Outcomes are measured at the campaign level and never linked back to a specific piece of advice. Account managers are measured on revenue and retention rather than on advice quality, so nobody asks. And the knowledge is assumed to be tacit rather than recoverable.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Record the recommendation and link it to what happened. Capture each recommendation as a structured record with its rationale, the expected effect and the account, which is the fix, takes seconds at the moment of giving it, and is the step that makes everything downstream possible. Record acceptance, since advice not taken is as informative as advice taken and currently vanishes. Link the recommendation to the subsequent change and its outcome, which the network can observe directly and is the only reason this data is worth collecting. Report which recommendation types work in which categories, which within two quarters produces a playbook grounded in evidence rather than in the most confident manager's opinion. Identify the managers whose advice performs and learn from them explicitly, rather than promoting on revenue and hoping the practice transfers. Build the induction material from the record, so a new manager starts with the network's accumulated experience instead of a deck. Feed the record into automated recommendation generation, which is what makes the build note's suggestions credible rather than generic. Show brands the track record of advice given to comparable accounts, which is a genuinely persuasive thing no network can currently offer. Flag advice that repeatedly fails so it stops being given. And report advice acceptance and outcome rates as a function metric, because a service business that cannot measure the quality of its service is measuring only its volume.

## Who Feels the Pain
Account managers whose good judgement is invisible and untransferable; brands receiving advice of unmeasured quality; and networks whose people are their differentiator and whose expertise never compounds.

## Impact If Fixed
Recommendations live in slide decks, which are not a data structure, so a business with perfect outcome data never collected the input side. A structured record captured in seconds and linked to what happened produces an evidence-based playbook within two quarters.

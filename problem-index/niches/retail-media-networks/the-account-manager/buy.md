# Customer Success Practice

**Niche:** [[niches/retail-media-networks/the-account-manager/profile|The Account Manager]]
**Industry:** [[industries/retail-media-networks|Retail Media Networks]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Software companies built customer success into a measured discipline with health scores, playbooks and automated touchpoints, and retail media staffs a ratio and hopes.
**Tags:** #worker-facing #gradient-boosting #evaluation-metrics #workflow-orchestration #automation #descriptive-statistics #revenue-impact #survival-analysis
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to take report-building and bid-tweaking off the account manager so they can give the advice the brand is paying for — and whoever does that changes how many brands one person can genuinely serve.

## The Problem
Software companies turned account management into a measured function: health scoring from usage and outcome signals, segmentation into high-touch and automated tiers, defined playbooks for defined situations, automated business reviews generated from product data, and churn prediction with early intervention. The tooling is mature and the practice is documented. Retail media account management, which serves a comparable book with comparable renewal dynamics, is staffed to a ratio and run on spreadsheets.

## What Already Exists
Customer health scoring from product and outcome signals; tiered engagement models with automated low-touch programmes; playbook libraries triggered by conditions; automated business review generation; and churn prediction with intervention workflows.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is from a software vendor's usage data to a retailer's commercial relationship with a supplier. It requires: (1) health signals drawn from category performance and share rather than from product usage, since a brand can be using the platform heavily and losing, which inverts the usual signal; (2) the relationship being entangled with a supply agreement, so churn is constrained in ways software relationships are not and the standard risk model does not apply; (3) the business review being a genuine analytical product about the brand's category rather than a usage summary, which is a substantially higher bar than the software equivalent; (4) the retailer's shopper data as the differentiating input, which has no analogue in software customer success and is where the value actually sits; and (5) tiering that accounts for suppliers whose media spend is small but whose supply relationship is large, a conflation the software model never faces.

## Target Customer
Retail media sales leadership, account management teams, and customer success platform vendors for whom retail media relationships are unserved.

## Impact If Solved
Software customer success reads usage, and here a brand can use the platform heavily while losing the category — the signal is inverted. The retailer's shopper data as the review's substance has no software analogue and is where the differentiation actually is.

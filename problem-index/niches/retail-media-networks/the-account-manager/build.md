# Forty Brands and the Same Deck Every Quarter

**Niche:** [[niches/retail-media-networks/the-account-manager/profile|The Account Manager]]
**Industry:** [[industries/retail-media-networks|Retail Media Networks]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Retail media account managers spend their weeks pulling keyword reports, adjusting bids in a spreadsheet and rebuilding the same quarterly business review for forty brands, none of which is the advice the brand is paying for.
**Tags:** #worker-facing #large-language-models #automation #workflow-orchestration #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #revenue-impact #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to take report-building and bid-tweaking off the account manager so they can give the advice the brand is paying for — and whoever does that changes how many brands one person can genuinely serve.

## The Problem
The account manager has forty brands. Each wants a quarterly business review, which means exporting the same reports, pasting them into the same template, writing the same observations about keyword performance, and presenting numbers the brand could have pulled themselves. Between reviews they adjust bids in a spreadsheet because the console makes bulk changes tedious. The thing they were hired for — knowing this retailer's shoppers, this category's dynamics, and what this brand should actually do — happens in the gaps, for the three or four accounts that shout loudest. The network's differentiation is supposed to be its people, and its people are doing data entry.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Account management is a cost line staffed to a ratio, and the ratio is set on accounts per head rather than on what happens in the accounts. Reporting automation is seen as a self-service feature for brands rather than as a tool for the internal team. The insight work is assumed to require a person from first principles, when most of it is pattern detection over data the network holds. And nobody measures the advice.

## What to Build
Automate the assembly and support the judgement. Generate the business review automatically from the network's own data — performance, category context, competitive movement, shopper behaviour — which is the fix for most of the week and is entirely mechanical work presented as analysis. Detect the findings rather than waiting for a person to notice them: a category share shift, a competitor's bid escalation, a product losing organic position, an audience saturating, a budget concentrated in branded terms. Rank accounts by what needs attention this week, since forty accounts cannot each receive equal attention and the current allocation is by volume of complaint. Automate the mechanical bid and budget work against a stated objective, which is the same argument as the media trader's and applies to the same hours. Give the manager the retailer's shopper insight in usable form, because that is the network's genuine differentiator and is currently locked in an analytics team's queue. Draft the recommendation with its evidence so the manager edits rather than composes, which is where the quality and the time both improve. Track which recommendations the brand accepted and what happened, which is the fix note's subject. Support the renewal conversation with the incrementality view honestly, since a manager defending an inflated number is in a weak position the moment the brand runs its own test. Free enough capacity to serve the long tail, connecting to the automation niche. And measure hours on assembly against hours on advice, because that ratio is the whole state of the function.

## Target Customer
Retail media sales and account management leadership, the account managers themselves, and the platform vendors whose tooling creates the assembly work.

## Impact If Built
The network's stated differentiation is its people and its people are doing data entry. Generating the review from data the network already holds and detecting findings automatically converts most of the week into the advisory work the brand is paying for.

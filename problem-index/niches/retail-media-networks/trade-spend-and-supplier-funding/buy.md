# Trade Promotion Optimisation Practice

**Niche:** [[niches/retail-media-networks/trade-spend-and-supplier-funding/profile|Trade Spend & Supplier Funding]]
**Industry:** [[industries/retail-media-networks|Retail Media Networks]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Consumer goods companies have spent decades measuring promotional lift and optimising trade spend, and retail media was added to the relationship without being added to the model.
**Tags:** #causal-inference #time-series-forecasting #convex-optimization #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #hypothesis-testing #revenue-impact #descriptive-statistics
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to show a supplier and a retailer what the total money between them actually bought, across trade funds and media spend together — and whoever does that settles the argument both sides are currently having blind.

## The Problem
Trade promotion optimisation is a real discipline with real software: baseline estimation, promotional lift measurement, incremental volume and profit calculation, post-event evaluation and optimisation of the promotional calendar against a budget. Consumer goods companies run it seriously because trade spend is frequently their largest line after cost of goods. Retail media has become a comparable line in a short space of time and sits entirely outside those models, evaluated by the retailer's own attributed return figure instead.

## What Already Exists
Trade promotion management and optimisation systems; baseline and lift estimation methods; incremental profit calculation; promotional calendar optimisation; and post-event evaluation frameworks.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to include a media instrument in a model built for price and placement. It requires: (1) media treated as a promotional instrument alongside price, display and feature, which is exactly the shape trade optimisation already handles and is why the transfer is natural — the instrument is new, the framework is not; (2) shopper-level rather than store-week data, which is far richer than the aggregate scanner data the practice was built on and permits measurement the existing methods cannot reach; (3) interaction effects between media and price promotion as a central rather than secondary term, since they are routinely run together and the combined effect is not additive; (4) the retailer's own attributed figure as a competing and conflicted measurement that must be reconciled rather than ignored, which the trade practice never had to handle; and (5) governance across sales and marketing functions that do not share systems, which is the deployment barrier rather than the modelling one.

## Target Customer
Consumer goods revenue growth management teams, retailer merchandising finance, and trade promotion vendors for whom retail media is an unmodelled instrument.

## Impact If Solved
Trade optimisation already handles instruments, baselines and interactions; media is a new instrument in an existing framework rather than a new problem. Shopper-level data permits measurement the store-week practice could never reach.

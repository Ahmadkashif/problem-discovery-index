# Two Ledgers, One Relationship

**Niche:** [[niches/retail-media-networks/trade-spend-and-supplier-funding/profile|Trade Spend & Supplier Funding]]
**Industry:** [[industries/retail-media-networks|Retail Media Networks]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A supplier and a retailer move hundreds of millions between them across trade funds and media spend, and neither side can see the two together or say what the total bought.
**Tags:** #data-integration #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #causal-inference #revenue-impact #confidence-intervals #workflow-orchestration #compliance
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to show a supplier and a retailer what the total money between them actually bought, across trade funds and media spend together — and whoever does that settles the argument both sides are currently having blind.

## The Problem
A supplier pays a retailer for promotional funding, end-cap placement, listing support, volume rebates and retail media. The first four are negotiated by the sales team against trade terms, tracked in a promotion management system and settled through deductions. The last is bought by the marketing team on a self-service console and invoiced separately. Both come from the supplier and both go to the retailer. Nobody on either side has a view of the total, so neither can answer whether the combined investment produced anything, and the annual negotiation is conducted on two separate sets of numbers by two sets of people who do not compare notes.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The two flows grew up in different functions with different systems and different vocabularies, and nothing forced them together — this organisational split is the whole cause and it exists identically on both sides. Retail media was deliberately positioned as marketing spend to reach a new budget rather than to compete with trade funds. Trade promotion systems and media platforms have no integration and no shared identifiers. And ambiguity favours whoever is negotiating better.

## What to Build
Join the two ledgers and measure them together. Build a single view of all money moving between a supplier and a retailer, by category, brand and period, which is the foundation and is arithmetic once the identifiers are mapped — the difficulty is organisational rather than technical. Measure return on the combined investment rather than on each stream separately, since a promotion and a media campaign supporting the same product interact and evaluating them independently misattributes both. Attribute outcomes to the mix, because the question either side actually has is whether the next dollar should go to price support, placement or media, and nobody can currently answer it. Reconcile deductions against media invoices, which is a genuine source of dispute and duplicated payment. Support the joint business planning conversation with evidence rather than with two positions, which is what both parties say they want. Model the interaction between a price promotion and a media campaign, since running both on the same product simultaneously is common and its combined effect is not the sum. Give the supplier's sales and marketing functions a shared view, which is the internal alignment problem and is the prerequisite for either function to use this. Handle the leverage question honestly, since media buying negotiated under supply pressure is a real feature of the category and pretending otherwise makes the analysis useless. Report per-supplier profitability to the retailer across both streams, which is a number merchandising needs and does not have. And track how the mix shifts over time, because the migration of trade funds into media is the category's defining financial story and nobody is measuring it.

## Target Customer
Supplier sales and marketing finance, retailer merchandising finance, and the trade promotion and retail media vendors on either side of the gap.

## Impact If Built
Both flows come from the same supplier to the same retailer, and an organisational split on both sides means neither can see the total. Joining the ledgers is arithmetic once identifiers are mapped, and it answers the question both parties actually have about where the next dollar should go.

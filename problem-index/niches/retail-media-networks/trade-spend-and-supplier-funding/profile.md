# Trade Spend & Supplier Funding

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/retail-media-networks|Retail Media Networks]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to show a supplier and a retailer what the total money between them actually bought, across trade funds and media spend together — and whoever does that settles the argument both sides are currently having blind.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$5B US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~8% of category spend
**Digital Adoption:** Very Low — spreadsheets and negotiation
**Target Buyer:** Retailer merchandising finance and supplier customer teams
**Automation Potential:** High — the reconciliation is arithmetic across two ledgers

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A large share of retail media budget comes from the same suppliers who fund trade promotions, listing fees, co-operative advertising and rebates — and increasingly from the same pot. Neither party can see the total. The retailer books media revenue in one system and trade income in another; the supplier books media spend in marketing and trade spend in sales, and they are negotiated by different people against different targets. The result is a commercial relationship worth hundreds of millions per supplier that neither side can evaluate as a whole, which is a distinct contest from measuring any campaign.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Trade promotion management systems, media platform billing, annual joint business plans, deduction and claim processes, and spreadsheets. The gaps: no joint view of trade and media spend; no common measure of return across the two; deductions and media invoices reconciled separately; media buying negotiated under supply leverage; and no attribution of outcomes to the combined investment.

# Niche Analysis — Retail Media Networks

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/retail-media-networks|Retail Media Networks]]

## Niche Selection

Retail media has the closed loop the rest of advertising spent twenty years failing to build — the same company shows the advertisement, owns the shelf, takes the order and records the purchase, in one system, under one identity — and points it at last-click attributed return on spend. The contests in this category are therefore unusual: almost none of them are blocked by missing data, and several are blocked by the fact that the honest number is smaller than the reported one. The eight niches below split the category by what competitors actually fight over.

## Why These Niches

Incrementality takes the largest share because it is the category's defining evasion: the retailer is the only party able to run the experiment, faces no external barrier to running it, and has a direct financial reason not to — which makes honest measurement a product opportunity rather than a technical one. The platform layer is second because every network below the top tier licenses the same technology and differentiates on data it has not learned to use. The two low-digitized niches are where the money comes from and where it goes next: the trade funds that quietly finance much of this spend, and the offsite extension every network now sells. The two underserved audiences are the account manager pulling keyword reports instead of advising, and the category merchant still accountable for category profit after their shelf was auctioned. The two automatable niches are the second-order question nobody in the category can answer about their own business — what ad load costs in basket and loyalty — and the thousands of small suppliers who cannot run a campaign at all.

## Filter Notes

Seven of the eight level-1 niches are terminal. The Platform Layer is not: the label names a product category — the licensed stack every non-top-tier network runs on — rather than a contest, and writing the contested statement produces two sentences describing different companies winning. One fight is over the decisioning itself: what a sponsored slot should be ranked by, given that the retailer's real objective is a mix of relevance, margin, inventory position, private label and long-run basket that bid-times-predicted-click cannot express. The other is over the advertiser's working surface: whether a brand's media team can plan, buy, optimise and report across a network without a spreadsheet and an account manager, which is a workflow contest with different winners and different buyers. A competitor can lead decisively on either while being ordinary at the other. It therefore decomposes into **Auction & Ranking Objective** and **Advertiser Self-Service**.

Two candidates were considered and rejected. **The brand's own demand-side buying stack** is a real contest but it belongs to [[industries/programmatic-ad-platforms|Programmatic Ad Platforms]], where it is already analysed. **Loyalty programme operations** supplies the identity substrate this whole category runs on, and the contest over it belongs to [[industries/retail-pos-platforms|Retail POS Platforms]] rather than here.

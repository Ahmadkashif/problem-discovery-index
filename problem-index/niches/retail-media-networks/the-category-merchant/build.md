# Accountable for a Shelf You No Longer Control

**Niche:** [[niches/retail-media-networks/the-category-merchant/profile|The Category Merchant]]
**Industry:** [[industries/retail-media-networks|Retail Media Networks]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** The category merchant is still accountable for category profit after the top of their search results was sold to the highest bidder, and nobody has given them a number for what it cost.
**Tags:** #worker-facing #causal-inference #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #revenue-impact #confidence-intervals #hypothesis-testing #convex-optimization
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to give the category merchant a number for what the sponsored placements in their category cost them — and whoever produces it gives the retailer's other half a seat at a table it has been excluded from.

## The Problem
A merchant is responsible for the profit of their category. The top four results for the category's most valuable searches are now sold to whoever bids most, frequently a supplier whose product carries lower margin than the one it displaced, sometimes ahead of the retailer's own private label. The media revenue is booked centrally. The cost — the margin difference, the shopper who did not find what they wanted, the private label sale that did not happen — lands in the merchant's numbers with no label on it. They are told their category is underperforming and have no way to show that part of the reason was sold.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Retail media was built as a separate revenue line reporting to a different executive, and the accounting followed the organisational chart rather than the economics — that split is the whole cause. Attributing media revenue and cost to a category requires a join nobody has needed to make. Merchants have no standing in the media organisation. And the network's growth is a headline number that an internal cost analysis would complicate.

## What to Build
Give merchandising its side of the ledger. Attribute media revenue to the category that generated it, which is the easy half and immediately reframes the conversation from central revenue to category economics. Measure the cost of displacement per category — the margin difference between what was promoted and what would have ranked organically, plus the conversion effect — which is the hard half, is computable through suppression experiments, and is the number that gives the merchant standing. Report category profit inclusive of both, so the merchant is accountable for a complete picture rather than half of one. Show which suppliers are buying position and at what price, since a merchant negotiating annual terms with a supplier who is simultaneously buying their shelf placement is negotiating blind. Give merchants a constraint surface over the auction — private label protection, minimum organic visibility, stock-aware suppression — which connects to the ranking objective work and is what a seat at the table actually means. Model the substitution, because a promoted product that takes a sale from a higher-margin alternative is a loss dressed as media revenue. Include the long-run effect on category loyalty, connecting to the ad load work, since shoppers who stop finding what they want leave the category rather than the page. Let merchandising and media share an objective and a report rather than competing, which is the organisational outcome the tooling should be designed to produce. Handle private label explicitly, since it is where the conflict is sharpest and is currently managed by unexamined carve-outs. And measure whether categories with heavier ad load perform better or worse on total profit, which is the empirical question nobody has run.

## Target Customer
Merchandising leadership and category merchants, retailer finance, and the retail media organisations that need internal legitimacy to keep growing.

## Impact If Built
Media revenue is booked centrally and its costs land unlabelled in the merchant's numbers, because the accounting followed the organisational chart rather than the economics. Measuring displacement cost per category through suppression gives the merchant a number and therefore standing.

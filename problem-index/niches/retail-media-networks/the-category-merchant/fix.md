# The Supplier Who Bought the Top of the Page

**Niche:** [[niches/retail-media-networks/the-category-merchant/profile|The Category Merchant]]
**Industry:** [[industries/retail-media-networks|Retail Media Networks]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The merchant negotiates annual terms with a supplier who is simultaneously buying the top of that merchant's category page, and the merchant cannot see it.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #revenue-impact #compliance #quick-win #workflow-orchestration #confidence-intervals #hypothesis-testing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to give the category merchant a number for what the sponsored placements in their category cost them — and whoever produces it gives the retailer's other half a seat at a table it has been excluded from.

## The Problem
A merchant sits down to negotiate next year's terms with a supplier. They have the supplier's sales, margin, promotional support and service performance. They do not have what that supplier spent on media in their category, what position it bought, what it displaced, or what the retailer earned from it. The supplier's own team has all of it. So the merchant negotiates on a partial view against a counterparty with a complete one, in a relationship where media spend has become a material part of the total value exchanged — and the information gap exists entirely inside the retailer's own four walls.

## Why It's Still Broken
Media data sits in the network's systems and merchandising has no access, which was a reasonable separation when media was small and is now a self-inflicted negotiating disadvantage. Some networks argue that advertiser confidentiality prevents sharing, which does not apply to the retailer's own data about its own suppliers. Nobody owns the merchant's information needs in the media organisation. And the annual negotiation cycle is slow enough that the gap is never felt acutely.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Give the merchant the supplier's full picture. Report each supplier's media spend, positions bought and the retailer's revenue from them, by category and period, which is the fix, is data the retailer already owns about itself, and closes an information asymmetry that exists for no defensible reason. Show what the supplier's placements displaced, since that is the merchant's cost and the supplier's benefit in the same transaction. Include media revenue in the supplier's total value to the category, which is the number the negotiation should actually be about. Flag suppliers whose media spend is buying position their terms did not earn, which is a straightforward comparison and is currently impossible. Show the counterfactual on margin, since a supplier buying placement over a higher-margin alternative is a cost that should appear in their scorecard. Time the reporting to the negotiation calendar rather than to the media reporting cycle, because a figure that arrives after terms are agreed is decorative. Train merchants on what the numbers mean, as media metrics are unfamiliar and an unexplained figure will be ignored. Establish a single supplier view across merchandising and media, which is the organisational fix underneath the reporting one. Extend to the supplier conversation itself, where discussing both streams together benefits both parties and is currently prevented by the retailer's own structure. And measure whether merchants with the full view negotiate better terms, since that is the argument for the whole change.

## Who Feels the Pain
Merchants negotiating against counterparties who know more about the retailer's business than they do; the retailer, losing terms it could have had; and suppliers dealing with two uncoordinated parts of the same company.

## Impact If Fixed
The information asymmetry exists entirely inside the retailer's own walls, and confidentiality arguments do not apply to a retailer's own data about its own suppliers. Reporting each supplier's media spend and displaced margin before the negotiation closes a gap that costs the retailer terms.

# Category Management Practice

**Industry:** [[industries/retail-media-networks|Retail Media Networks]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Retail category management has decades of method for assortment, space and supplier economics, and none of it was updated when the shelf started being auctioned.

**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to give the category merchant a number for what the sponsored placements in their category cost them — and whoever produces it gives the retailer's other half a seat at a table it has been excluded from.

## The Problem
Category management is a developed discipline: assortment optimisation, space allocation by productivity, price architecture, supplier scorecards, and category profit accounting that includes trade income and shrink. It is applied rigorously to physical shelves, where a decision to give a product a facing is evaluated on its return. The digital shelf, where placement is now auctioned in real time, sits entirely outside those frameworks, so the most dynamic part of the category is the part with no category management applied to it.

## What Already Exists
Assortment and space optimisation; productivity-per-facing analysis; category profit accounting inclusive of trade income; supplier scorecards and joint planning; and planogram compliance measurement.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is from a shelf set quarterly to a shelf set per query. It requires: (1) placement decided per shopper per query rather than per planogram, so productivity analysis must be computed continuously over millions of arrangements rather than evaluated on one — this dynamism is the structural break with the whole practice; (2) media revenue treated as category income alongside trade funds, which the accounting frameworks already have a slot for and simply have not been given the data to fill; (3) displacement measured rather than assumed, since a physical facing decision has an obvious counterfactual and a ranking decision does not; (4) the merchant setting constraints rather than making selections, which is a different kind of control and requires an interface concept that does not exist; and (5) reconciliation between physical and digital shelf strategy, since a category promoted one way in store and another online is a coherence failure nobody currently owns.

## Target Customer
Category merchants and merchandising leadership, retailer finance, and category management vendors for whom the auctioned digital shelf is unaddressed.

## Impact If Solved
Category management is applied rigorously to a shelf set quarterly and not at all to one set per query. Its accounting already has a slot for media income, and the missing pieces are measured displacement and a constraint interface the merchant can operate.

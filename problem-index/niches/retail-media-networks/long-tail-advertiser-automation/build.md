# Nine Tenths of the Supplier Base Cannot Participate

**Niche:** [[niches/retail-media-networks/long-tail-advertiser-automation/profile|Long-Tail Advertiser Automation]]
**Industry:** [[industries/retail-media-networks|Retail Media Networks]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A retailer has thousands of suppliers and a few hundred with media teams, and the network is built entirely for the few hundred.
**Tags:** #automation #workflow-orchestration #gradient-boosting #large-language-models #evaluation-metrics #revenue-impact #convex-optimization #confidence-intervals
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to let a supplier with no media team run effective campaigns without touching a console — and whoever does that opens the nine tenths of the supplier base that cannot participate at all.

## The Problem
A regional food producer sells eleven products through the retailer. They would happily spend a modest amount to get those products found. To do that they must understand campaign structure, keyword match types, bidding, budget pacing and a reporting interface designed for someone who does this professionally. They have a founder and a sales person. They try once, spend the budget in four days on irrelevant terms, conclude retail media does not work for them, and never return. Multiply by several thousand suppliers and the network has excluded most of its own supplier base from a product they would have bought.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The console was built for the professional buyers who arrived first, and every subsequent feature deepened that assumption. Small accounts cannot be served by account managers economically, so they were left with the self-service tool as the fallback. Minimum spends were set to keep support costs manageable, which formalised the exclusion. And revenue concentration among the large accounts means the long tail is nobody's target.

## What to Build
Remove the expertise requirement rather than simplifying the interface. Build campaigns automatically from the supplier's catalogue and a stated objective, so the supplier says what they sell and what they want rather than constructing anything — this is the product and the distinction from a simplified console is the whole point. Choose targeting, bids, budgets and pacing automatically, since every one of those is a decision the network can make better than a novice and none of them should be asked. Express the objective in the supplier's language — sell more of this, clear this stock, support this new listing, defend this shelf — because keywords and bids are implementation details they should never see. Set sensible budget defaults and prevent the four-day burn, which is the fix note's subject and is the single most common first experience. Report in plain terms — this is what you spent, this is what sold, this is what it means — since a report a supplier cannot interpret is a report that ends the relationship. Lower or remove minimum spends, which are the formal barrier and are only defensible while support is manual. Integrate with the supplier's existing touchpoints rather than requiring a new login, since the supplier portal they already use is where this belongs. Onboard automatically at listing time, so the offer reaches every supplier rather than the ones who ask. Handle seasonality and promotional calendars automatically, which small suppliers care about and cannot operate. And measure activated supplier count and retention rather than revenue alone, because the long tail's value compounds and its first-campaign failure rate is what currently caps it.

## Target Customer
Retail media growth teams, the thousands of suppliers excluded from the channel, and the platform vendors whose consoles define who can participate.

## Impact If Built
The console was built for the professionals who arrived first and every feature deepened the assumption, excluding most of the supplier base. Constructing campaigns from a catalogue and a stated objective removes the expertise requirement rather than simplifying it, which is the difference that matters.

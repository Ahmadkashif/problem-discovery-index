# The Amended K-1 That Reaches the Beneficiary's CPA Too Late

**Niche:** [[niches/trust-companies/fiduciary-tax-compliance/profile|Fiduciary Tax Compliance]]
**Industry:** [[industries/trust-companies|Trust Companies]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** When a correction forces an amended K-1, the beneficiary's accountant has often already filed, and the trust company finds out when the beneficiary calls.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #workflow-orchestration #compliance #quick-win #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to file every trust's 1041 and K-1s correctly by the April deadline despite late and corrected tax information, and whoever finishes the most returns without an amendment takes the account.

## The Problem
Amended K-1s go out by mail with no explanation of what changed or whether the beneficiary needs to act. Beneficiaries and their CPAs call the trust officer, who cannot answer and forwards the call to tax.

## Why It's Still Broken
The tax unit sees returns, not relationships; the trust officer sees relationships, not returns; and no one owns the communication.

## What a Fix Looks Like
For every amended K-1, an automatic impact summary — which lines changed, by how much, and whether the change is large enough to warrant amending the beneficiary's own return — sent to the beneficiary and, with consent, their accountant, with a copy and a call script for the officer.

## Who Feels the Pain
Trust officers fielding confused calls; beneficiaries paying their CPAs twice; tax staff interrupted in their busiest weeks.

## Impact If Fixed
Most calls avoided, faster action by beneficiaries' accountants, and a better client experience from the same correction.

# Trust Account Acceptance & Onboarding

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/trust-companies|Trust Companies]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn a signed trust instrument and a transfer of assets into a correctly coded, KYC-cleared account in days, and whoever onboards fastest without an acceptance error takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$250M a year in onboarding, document-abstraction and KYC tooling and services bought by trust companies (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~0.5% of fiduciary income (estimate), touching every new account
**Digital Adoption:** Low-Medium — e-signature and online forms at the front, scanned instruments and manual system setup behind
**Target Buyer:** Head of Trust Operations / Head of New Business
**Automation Potential:** High

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Every new trust passes the same gate: pre-acceptance review of the instrument and the assets, KYC and beneficial-ownership checks on every party, transfer of assets from the prior trustee or custodian, and setup of the trust accounting record — followed by the prompt post-acceptance review of assets in discretionary accounts. Directed trusts, where an outside adviser holds investment or distribution authority, have multiplied the parties and roles each acceptance must sort out. Onboarding speed is how directed-trust companies compete for referrals from advisers, and setup accuracy decides decades of correct administration. Competitors are the onboarding modules of trust accounting vendors, contract-abstraction and KYC vendors, and the trust companies themselves competing on time to open. The successor trustee's job of retitling a decedent's assets into a trust is a different workflow, covered from the attorney's side in [[niches/estate-planning/trust-administration-funding/profile|⚡ Trust Administration & Funding]].

## Current Tools & Gaps
E-signature and intake forms, imaging systems, bank KYC platforms and manual setup screens. The gaps: instruments read by hand, KYC tools that model companies and individuals but not trust roles, cost basis lost in transfers, and acceptance memos written from scratch.

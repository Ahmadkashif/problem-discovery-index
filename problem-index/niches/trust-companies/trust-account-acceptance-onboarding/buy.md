# Bank KYC Platforms Taught Trust Structures

**Niche:** [[niches/trust-companies/trust-account-acceptance-onboarding/profile|Trust Account Acceptance & Onboarding]]
**Industry:** [[industries/trust-companies|Trust Companies]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Bank KYC platforms screen individuals and companies; a trust brings a grantor, trustees, a protector, directed advisers and classes of beneficiaries who change over time.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #graph-theory #data-integration #compliance #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn a signed trust instrument and a transfer of assets into a correctly coded, KYC-cleared account in days, and whoever onboards fastest without an acceptance error takes the account.

## The Problem
Trust onboarding stalls on identifying and screening every party in the right role, and again whenever a beneficiary is born, dies or comes of age.

## What Already Exists
Customer due diligence and screening platforms licensed by banks for onboarding individuals and legal entities, with sanctions, adverse-media and beneficial-ownership checks.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs: a trust party model with roles and powers (who can direct, who can remove the trustee, who can appoint), roles extracted from the instrument rather than typed in, screening depth set by role and power, foreign beneficiaries and protectors, periodic refresh triggered by life events and distributions, and an audit trail linking each check to the provision that created the party's role.

## Target Customer
Trust companies already using a bank KYC platform whose trust onboarding still runs on spreadsheets beside it.

## Impact If Solved
KYC on a trust completed in hours rather than days, with role-based depth that satisfies examiners without over-screening minor beneficiaries.

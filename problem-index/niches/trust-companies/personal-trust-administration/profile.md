# Personal Trust Administration

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/trust-companies|Trust Companies]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to make each discretionary decision on an old instrument consistent, documented and defensible at a caseload no officer can hold in memory, and whoever makes the senior officer's judgement repeatable and reviewable takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$900M a year in tooling, data and outsourced services bought by personal trust units (estimate), against ~$6.0B of gross fiduciary income from personal trust and agency accounts at FDIC-insured institutions in 2025
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~11% of FDIC-reported fiduciary income ($6.0B of $55.6B), and the large majority of trust officers (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — ledgers, statements and fees are digital; instruments are scans and decisions live in memos and email
**Target Buyer:** Chief Fiduciary Officer / Head of Personal Trust
**Automation Potential:** Medium — drafting, retrieval and review are automatable; the discretionary decision must stay with the trustee

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Personal trust is the fiduciary core: ~553,600 managed personal trust and agency accounts holding ~$996B at insured institutions at the end of 2025, led by Bank of America, Northern Trust, Wells Fargo, PNC and JPMorgan Chase, with independent trust companies in South Dakota, Nevada, Delaware and Wyoming growing beside them. The work is discretion exercised under an instrument — distributions under support standards, impartiality between income and remainder beneficiaries, and the documentation that protects the trustee from surcharge — reviewed by a distribution or fiduciary committee. The contest among vendors is not the ledger, which is solved, but the decision layer above it: who makes discretion consistent across officers and survivable when the senior ones retire. Trust accounting vendors (FIS, SEI, SS&C Innovest), wealth CRM vendors and new AI drafting tools all compete for that layer.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Trust accounting platforms record distributions; imaging systems hold instruments and memos; wealth CRMs hold contact notes. The gaps: no structured record of why a request was approved or declined, no retrieval of precedent decisions under similar instrument language, no join between decisions and later complaints or asset departures, and declines that are often never written down.

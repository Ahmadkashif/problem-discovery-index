# Special Asset Administration

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/trust-companies|Trust Companies]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to value, manage and report the farms, buildings, family companies and mineral interests held in trusts with evidence an examiner and a remainder beneficiary will accept, and whoever produces defensible special-asset reviews without a specialist visiting every asset every year takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$250M a year in management, valuation and tooling spend on special assets held by trust companies (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~3% of managed personal trust and investment agency assets by value (~$60B of real estate and ~$108B of miscellaneous assets within ~$5.1T at insured institutions, December 2025), and a far larger share of officer time (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — paper leases, division orders, check stubs, appraisal PDFs and spreadsheets kept by one officer
**Target Buyer:** Head of Special Assets / Chief Fiduciary Officer
**Automation Potential:** Medium — document reading, income reconciliation and valuation triggers automate; site judgement and negotiation do not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Families leave trusts more than securities: a farm rented to a neighbour on a cash lease, a strip mall, a minority stake in the family company, royalty interests in a county the beneficiaries have never visited, timber, notes owed by relatives. The trustee must manage each prudently, allocate its receipts between income and principal under state law, insure it, keep it out of environmental and liability trouble, and value it often enough to report fairly to beneficiaries and examiners. Large trust banks run special-asset units; most trust departments have one officer who knows the farms. Competitors for the work are those in-house units, farm management companies, mineral managers, property managers and valuation firms, each covering one asset class.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Trust accounting systems carry special assets at a book or last-appraised value with a free-text description. Property management and royalty accounting software exist for operators, not trustees. The gaps: no structured record of leases, production and condition; royalty income deposited without reconciliation; valuations refreshed on a fixed calendar rather than when something changes; and asset knowledge that leaves with the officer.

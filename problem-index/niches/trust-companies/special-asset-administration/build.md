# A Special-Asset Review Built From the Asset's Own Paper

**Niche:** [[niches/trust-companies/special-asset-administration/profile|Special Asset Administration]]
**Industry:** [[industries/trust-companies|Trust Companies]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** The annual review of a farm or a royalty interest is written from memory and last year's memo, though the leases, statements and production records that should drive it are already in the file.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #change-point-detection #time-series-forecasting #feature-engineering #evaluation-metrics #compliance #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to value, manage and report the farms, buildings, family companies and mineral interests held in trusts with evidence an examiner and a remainder beneficiary will accept, and whoever produces defensible special-asset reviews without a specialist visiting every asset every year takes the account.

## The Problem
A trust department may hold hundreds of special assets, each reviewed annually. The review should ask whether rent is at market, whether the lease has lapsed, whether production has fallen, whether the business interest's value has moved, and whether the asset still suits the trust. In practice the officer updates last year's memo, because assembling the evidence for each asset takes longer than the review allows.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Each asset class has its own documents and vendors, and no one vendor serves trustees across all of them. The volume per trust company is modest, and the experts who know the assets are scarce and busy.

## What to Build
A review engine that reads each asset's documents — leases, division orders, operator statements, entity financial statements and K-1s, appraisals, insurance certificates — into a structured asset record; monitors income streams for breaks and step changes against expected rent or production; flags leases near expiry, overdue appraisals and coverage gaps; and drafts the annual review with each finding cited, so the officer judges rather than assembles. Valuation refreshes are triggered by events rather than by calendar.

## Target Customer
Heads of special assets and chief fiduciary officers at trust banks and independent trust companies with significant real estate, farm, mineral or closely held holdings.

## Impact If Built
Assembling a review in minutes rather than hours (estimate) lets a small team cover hundreds of assets properly, catches lapsed leases and underpaid royalties within months rather than years, and gives examiners and remainder beneficiaries a documented basis for every hold decision.

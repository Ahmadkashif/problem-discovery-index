# Community Bank Trust Departments

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/trust-companies|Trust Companies]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to give a trust department of three to ten people the account review, fiduciary audit and tax capacity examiners expect of a large trust company, and whoever makes a sub-$1B department pass its examination without adding staff takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$150M a year in trust platform, outsourcing and advisory spend by small trust departments (estimate); 727 insured institutions reported under $1B of fiduciary assets at the end of 2025, holding ~$134B and earning ~$0.8B of fiduciary income
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~1.4% of FDIC-reported fiduciary income, but over 70% of the institutions that report fiduciary assets
**Digital Adoption:** Low-Medium — trust accounting rented from a service bureau; reviews, committee minutes and audit evidence in spreadsheets and binders
**Target Buyer:** Trust Department Head (often also the senior trust officer) / bank CEO
**Automation Potential:** High for reviews, committee documentation and tax preparation

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The median small department earned ~$0.3M of fiduciary income in 2025 and administers ~30 managed personal accounts, yet it carries the same obligations as a large trust company: pre-acceptance and annual account reviews, fiduciary audit, a trust committee, fiduciary tax returns, and a separate trust examination. Its staff are few, senior and hard to replace, and many departments have sold their books or referred clients to larger trust companies when a key officer retired. The banks themselves are profiled under [[industries/community-banks|Community Banks]], whose niches cover lending, deposits and BSA but not the trust department. Competitors for this audience are trust platform vendors offering service-bureau processing, outsourced trust operations providers, larger trust companies offering to take the accounts, and the consultants and CPA firms that perform fiduciary audits.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Service-bureau trust accounting, outsourced investment models, and compliance software built for the bank's lending and deposit rules. The gaps: no workflow that turns account reviews, committee decisions and audit evidence into an examination-ready record; tax preparation outsourced at a cost the department struggles to carry; and continuity that depends on one or two people.

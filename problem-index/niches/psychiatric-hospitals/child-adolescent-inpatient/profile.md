# Child & Adolescent Inpatient Psychiatry

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/psychiatric-hospitals|Psychiatric Hospitals]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to stabilise children and adolescents in crisis and return them to their families, and whoever has the lowest 30-day readmission rate for discharged youths takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$0.3B a year of tooling, family-engagement, education-liaison and aftercare-coordination spend on youth units (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~1% of receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — family sessions, school contacts and aftercare referrals are arranged by phone
**Target Buyer:** Medical director of child and adolescent services; chief executive of a freestanding hospital with youth units
**Automation Potential:** Low-Medium — coordination automates; the clinical and family work does not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Youth units serve patients who cannot consent for themselves, whose families and schools are part of every decision, and who are restrained under tighter rules: an order for an adolescent lapses after two hours and for a child under nine after one (42 CFR 482.13). Beds are scarce, so youths board in emergency departments, and the outpatient child psychiatrists who should see them after discharge are scarcer still. The stakes of failure are high: a jury awarded $60M in compensatory damages, plus trebled and punitive damages, against UHS's Cumberland Hospital for Children and Adolescents in 2024 over inappropriate sexual contact during examinations by its former medical director, an independent contractor (UHS 10-K). Competitors are chain-owned youth hospitals and units, children's hospital psychiatric units and academic programmes, with residential treatment centres (queued as their own industry) as the next level. Tools built for adult units ignore families and schools.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Behavioral EHRs, phone and fax referrals to outpatient providers, and school liaison by a social worker. Gaps: no structured record of family engagement, school re-entry plans or whether the first outpatient visit happened, and no readmission analysis by age band.

# Discharges With No Child Psychiatrist Appointment

**Niche:** [[niches/psychiatric-hospitals/child-adolescent-inpatient/profile|Child & Adolescent Inpatient Psychiatry]]
**Industry:** [[industries/psychiatric-hospitals|Psychiatric Hospitals]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Youths leave the unit with a prescription and a phone number instead of a booked appointment, because outpatient child psychiatrists and therapists are booked for weeks and the social worker has no shared view of openings.
**Tags:** #workflow-orchestration #data-integration #worker-facing #automation #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to stabilise children and adolescents in crisis and return them to their families, and whoever has the lowest 30-day readmission rate for discharged youths takes the account.

## The Problem
The median facility saw only 35.5% of all discharged patients in outpatient care within seven days (FAPH-7, IPFQR), and for youths the specialist supply is thinner still.

## Why It's Still Broken
Outpatient practices do not publish openings, many do not take Medicaid, and hospital social workers find slots by phone one discharge at a time.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A shared referral list of outpatient youth providers with openings and payer acceptance, a booked bridge visit (telehealth if needed) before discharge, and a reminder to the family.

## Who Feels the Pain
Hospital social workers, families and the youths themselves.

## Impact If Fixed
If booked visits before discharge rise from ~40% to ~70% of youth discharges (estimate), a unit discharging 800 youths a year sends ~240 more home with an appointment.

# Involuntary-Hold & Commitment Paperwork

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/psychiatric-hospitals|Psychiatric Hospitals]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep every involuntary hold, certification and court petition on its statutory clock, and whoever has the fewest holds ended by a procedural defect rather than a clinical decision takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$0.2B a year of psychiatrist, social worker and medical-records time on legal-hold paperwork, plus outside counsel and hearing logistics (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~0.6% of receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — state forms are filled by hand or as PDFs, and hold expiries are tracked on whiteboards
**Target Buyer:** Medical director; risk manager or general counsel at a chain
**Automation Potential:** High for clocks, drafting and packets; the clinical opinion and testimony stay with the psychiatrist

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
No other part of healthcare holds patients against their will under a statutory clock. Emergency holds commonly run 72 hours, after which the hospital must certify, petition a court or release, and medication over objection needs its own hearing (estimate; rules differ by state). Each state has its own forms, standards and deadlines, and a defect ends the hold whatever the clinical picture. Since 2024, press reporting and a federal investigation of the largest chain have centred on patients held without proper legal basis (search summary; Acadia 10-K), so the record of each hold's legality is now a compliance exposure as well as a clinical one. Competitors are hospitals' medical-records and risk teams, chains' legal departments and healthcare law firms, with EHR form libraries in support. The patient's side is [[niches/public-defenders/mental-health-court/profile|🟣 Mental Health Court Defense]].

## Current Tools & Gaps
State PDF forms, EHR document templates, court e-filing portals in some counties, and whiteboards. Gaps: no legal-status register per patient with clocks, no draft that cites the chart behind each statutory element, and no record of why holds ended.

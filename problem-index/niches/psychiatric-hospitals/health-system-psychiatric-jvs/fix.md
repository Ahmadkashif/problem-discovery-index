# Partner EDs That Cannot See the JV's Open Beds

**Niche:** [[niches/psychiatric-hospitals/health-system-psychiatric-jvs/profile|Health-System Joint Ventures & Hospital-Based Psychiatric Units]]
**Industry:** [[industries/psychiatric-hospitals|Psychiatric Hospitals]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** An emergency physician at the partner system phones the JV hospital, waits for the intake clinician, faxes records, and often sends the patient to a competitor who answered first.
**Tags:** #workflow-orchestration #data-integration #automation #worker-facing #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to run a health system's inpatient psychiatric capacity for it, and whoever keeps the largest share of the system's psychiatric admissions in the system's own beds takes the account.

## The Problem
The JV was built to serve the partner's EDs, but the referral process is the same phone-and-fax routine used with any outside hospital.

## Why It's Still Broken
The JV runs its own behavioral EHR, separate from the partner's, and the partner's ED staff rotate and do not know the JV's intake rules.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A shared referral queue in the partner's EHR that shows the JV's real availability by unit type, sends the referral packet electronically, and returns a decision with reasons.

## Who Feels the Pain
ED physicians and nurses, ED social workers, the JV's intake clinicians, and patients waiting in the ED.

## Impact If Fixed
If ~15% of the partner's outside psychiatric transfers happen only because the JV could not be reached or answered late (estimate), a system sending 1,200 a year could keep ~180 more patients.

# Health-System Joint Ventures & Hospital-Based Psychiatric Units

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/psychiatric-hospitals|Psychiatric Hospitals]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to run a health system's inpatient psychiatric capacity for it, and whoever keeps the largest share of the system's psychiatric admissions in the system's own beds takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$0.6B a year of joint-venture management fees, unit management contracts, development and planning services (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~2% of receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — the partner system's EHR and bed board are electronic, but psychiatric demand and transfers out are tracked in spreadsheets
**Target Buyer:** Health system chief strategy officer or service-line vice president; the JV board
**Automation Potential:** Medium — demand modelling and referral routing automate; the partnership does not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
General hospitals see psychiatric patients in their emergency departments and often cannot place them. Chains now build and run freestanding psychiatric hospitals jointly with health systems. In 2025 alone Acadia opened five JV facilities with Henry Ford Health, Geisinger Health, Ascension Seton, Fairview Health Services and ECU Health (10-K), and UHS runs JVs of its own (10-K). Lifepoint Behavioral Health and Oceans Healthcare manage units and JVs (named from recall), and many systems run their own units. Hospital-based psychiatric units belong to NAICS 622110 and are a boundary case here. This niche holds only what is specific to psychiatric capacity, and the system's general bed flow stays at [[niches/general-hospitals/case-management-post-acute-placement/profile|🟠 Case Management & Post-Acute Placement]].

## Current Tools & Gaps
Health-system capacity platforms, the JV operator's own census reports and consultants' bed-need studies. Gaps: no one measures how many of the system's psychiatric patients end up in someone else's beds, and the JV's admissions team does not see the partner's ED queue.

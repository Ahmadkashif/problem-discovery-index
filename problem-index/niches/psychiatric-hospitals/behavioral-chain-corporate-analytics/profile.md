# Behavioral Hospital Chain Corporate Analytics

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/psychiatric-hospitals|Psychiatric Hospitals]]
**Category:** Insight Layer
**Value-Chain Position:** Aggregator / rollup platforms

## What They Do
The chains that own most freestanding psychiatric hospitals run central analytics, quality, revenue cycle and compliance teams across their facilities. Acadia operated 277 facilities with over 12,500 beds at the end of 2025 and reported $3,312.8M of revenue, 55% from acute inpatient psychiatric facilities; UHS ran 182 US inpatient behavioral facilities, with behavioral health ~43% of its net revenues (10-Ks). Their analysts track admissions, length of stay, payer denials, restraint and incident rates, staffing and JV performance for executives, boards and investors.

## Insight Function
**Size:** ~20–60 analysts and data staff per chain (estimate)
**Output:** Internal dashboards, facility performance reviews, JV business cases, investor metrics (same-facility admissions, length of stay, revenue per patient day)
**Proprietary data:** Patient-level clinical, utilization and payer records across hundreds of facilities, the largest psychiatric inpatient corpus outside payers
**Clock:** Quarterly 10-Q and annual 10-K reporting (Acadia's next 10-Q was not yet filed on 29 October 2026), CMS reporting deadlines that bind their facilities, and the DOJ investigation Acadia disclosed
**Buyer:** Chief financial officer or chief operating officer of the chain

**Kill switches:** **HIPAA and 42 CFR Part 2.** The corpus is the chains' own patients' behavioral and substance-use records, the most restricted class of health data, and any outside system touching it faces a compliance review severe enough to stall procurement, sharpened by the investigations both chains have faced.
**Verdict:** Logged — kill switch. The insight is spent on running the chain and never invoiced (Q1 1), and the field is a handful of chains (Q5 2).

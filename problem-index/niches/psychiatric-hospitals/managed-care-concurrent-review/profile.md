# Managed-Care Concurrent Review of Psychiatric Days

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/psychiatric-hospitals|Psychiatric Hospitals]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to win payer approval at each concurrent review for the psychiatric days the record supports, and whoever ends the year with the lowest share of reviewed patient days denied takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$0.6B a year of UR coordinator time, outsourced review, peer-to-peer physician time and review software (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~2% of receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — reviews are phone calls read from notes; decisions come back by portal, fax or voicemail
**Target Buyer:** Director of utilization review; regional revenue cycle director at a chain
**Automation Potential:** High for packet drafting and scheduling; the conversation with the reviewer stays human

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Commercial plans, Medicare Advantage plans, Medicaid managed care plans and their behavioral health organisations review inpatient psychiatric stays every one to three days (estimate). The criteria are behavioral (danger, grave disability, failed lower levels of care, active treatment), written in each payer's own guideline or InterQual or MCG, with LOCUS or ASAM in some states. Competitors are the hospital's own UR team, outsourced behavioral UR firms such as SimiTree and Coronis Health (search summary), physician-advisor services, and revenue cycle AI vendors moving in from acute care (AKASA, Candid Health, Trellis AI, all hiring engineers; see the agents note). The payer's side is [[niches/health-insurance-carriers/inpatient-post-acute-review/profile|🎯 Inpatient & Post-Acute Concurrent Review]], and the payers' criteria units are [[niches/behavioral-health-clinics/behavioral-managed-care-policy/profile|🔍 Behavioral Managed Care Clinical Policy Units]].

## Current Tools & Gaps
EHR UR modules, payer portals, licensed criteria and spreadsheets of review due dates. Gaps: no evidence extraction from psychiatric notes, no record of what the reviewer was told against what was approved, and no learning across thousands of reviews about which arguments hold for which payer.

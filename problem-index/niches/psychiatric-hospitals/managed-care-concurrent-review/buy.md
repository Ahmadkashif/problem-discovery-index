# Acute-Care Revenue Cycle Agents Taught Psychiatric Criteria

**Niche:** [[niches/psychiatric-hospitals/managed-care-concurrent-review/profile|Managed-Care Concurrent Review of Psychiatric Days]]
**Industry:** [[industries/psychiatric-hospitals|Psychiatric Hospitals]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Revenue cycle AI vendors already automate authorizations and appeals for acute hospitals, and they need a behavioral layer before they can argue a psychiatric day.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #data-integration #workflow-orchestration #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to win payer approval at each concurrent review for the psychiatric days the record supports, and whoever ends the year with the lowest share of reviewed patient days denied takes the account.

## The Problem
Health systems that already buy revenue cycle automation for their acute floors leave the psychiatric unit's reviews to a coordinator with a phone.

## What Already Exists
AKASA (whose customers handle 1 in 10 US inpatient discharges by its own count), Candid Health and Trellis AI automate prior authorization, coding and appeals; acute denial tools are profiled at [[niches/medical-billing/denial-management-analytics-vendors/profile|🔍 Denial Management Analytics Vendors]].

## The Customization Gap
- behavioral criteria sets and LOCUS or ASAM dimensions;
- evidence from nursing and technician notes, not only physician notes;
- review cadences of one to three days with phone delivery and voicemail decisions;
- legal-hold status and 42 CFR Part 2 consent for detox patients.

## Target Customer
Health systems with psychiatric units, and chains whose central UR team handles reviews for many hospitals.

## Impact If Solved
For a health system with 200 psychiatric beds, adding the behavioral layer to a tool it already licenses could spare ~2 coordinator FTEs, ~$160,000 a year (estimate).

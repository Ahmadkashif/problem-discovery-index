# Peer-to-Peer Calls That Land During Rounds

**Niche:** [[niches/psychiatric-hospitals/managed-care-concurrent-review/profile|Managed-Care Concurrent Review of Psychiatric Days]]
**Industry:** [[industries/psychiatric-hospitals|Psychiatric Hospitals]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** When a payer's reviewer refers a psychiatric day to its physician, the hospital's attending must call back inside a narrow window, often while rounding or in a commitment hearing, and a missed window is a denied day.
**Tags:** #workflow-orchestration #large-language-models #worker-facing #automation #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to win payer approval at each concurrent review for the psychiatric days the record supports, and whoever ends the year with the lowest share of reviewed patient days denied takes the account.

## The Problem
The coordinator leaves a sticky note: "P2P with the plan, call by 2 p.m." The psychiatrist is in a hearing until 2:30 and calls back without the notes she needs.

## Why It's Still Broken
Psychiatrists are often independent contractors (Acadia 10-K) with no protected time for payer calls, and each payer runs its own scheduling line and window.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A queue that books the peer-to-peer into the attending's day around rounds and hearings, sends her a one-page cited brief beforehand, and records the outcome and the reviewer's stated reason.

## Who Feels the Pain
Attending psychiatrists, UR coordinators and the revenue cycle director who sees the denials.

## Impact If Fixed
If one in four missed peer-to-peers is saved (estimate), and a 100-bed hospital misses ~100 a year at ~3 days each, it keeps ~75 days, ~$75,000 at ~$1,000 a day.

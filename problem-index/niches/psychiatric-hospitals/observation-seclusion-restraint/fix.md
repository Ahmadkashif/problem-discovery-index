# Restraint Debriefs Filed and Never Analysed

**Niche:** [[niches/psychiatric-hospitals/observation-seclusion-restraint/profile|Observation Rounds, Seclusion & Restraint Records]]
**Industry:** [[industries/psychiatric-hospitals|Psychiatric Hospitals]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** After every restraint the team writes a debrief of what led up to it and what might have prevented it, and the forms go into a binder or an incident system that nobody reads for patterns.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #k-means-clustering #compliance #worker-facing #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep agitated and suicidal patients safe with the least coercion, and whoever has the fewest combined restraint and seclusion hours per 1,000 patient hours takes the account.

## The Problem
The same triggers recur, such as the shift-change hour, the phone call before visiting time or a particular patient pairing, but each debrief is read once by the unit manager.

## Why It's Still Broken
Incident systems were designed for falls and medication errors, with fixed categories that do not fit escalations, and the free-text debrief is where the useful detail sits.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Read every debrief and restraint record, cluster the antecedents and times, and give each unit's monthly meeting its top three recurring triggers with examples.

## Who Feels the Pain
Nurse managers, performance-improvement directors and the technicians who carry out the restraints.

## Impact If Fixed
If addressing the top three triggers removes ~10% of a hospital's ~300 restraint episodes a year (estimate), that is ~30 fewer restraints and the staff injuries that come with some of them.

# Observation Rounds, Seclusion & Restraint Records

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/psychiatric-hospitals|Psychiatric Hospitals]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep agitated and suicidal patients safe with the least coercion, and whoever has the fewest combined restraint and seclusion hours per 1,000 patient hours takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$2B a year of technician time on 15-minute rounds and 1:1 observation, plus de-escalation training, observation technology and incident software (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~6% of receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — rounding sheets are paper on many units, and restraint start and stop times are handwritten
**Target Buyer:** Chief nursing officer; director of performance improvement
**Automation Potential:** Medium — documentation and video observation automate; the decision to restrain or release stays with staff

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Restraint and seclusion are regulated minute by minute. Orders lapse after four hours for adults, two for adolescents and one for children under nine, a face-to-face evaluation is due within one hour, and deaths go to CMS the next business day (42 CFR 482.13). Hours are published per facility. In the 2024 data the median was 0.05 restraint hours per 1,000 patient hours and the 90th percentile 0.63 (IPFQR). Joint Commission NPSG 15.01.01 adds environmental ligature-risk assessment (search summary). Competitors are each hospital's own nursing leadership and programmes, de-escalation trainers (CPI, Mandt), observation technology (AvaSure, Oxehealth; search summary) and digital rounding apps. The same contest for group homes is [[niches/idd-group-homes/behavior-support-data/profile|🟠 Behaviour Support Plans & Restrictive Intervention Data]], and hospital security staff are [[niches/security-guard-firms/hospital-healthcare/profile|🟣 Hospital & Healthcare Security]].

## Current Tools & Gaps
Paper or tablet rounding sheets, incident reporting systems built for falls and medication errors, and annual de-escalation training. Gaps: no link between the observation record, the escalation that followed and the restraint, and no unit-level analysis beyond the annual IPFQR number.

# The Missing Accessory on the Rooftop

**Niche:** [[niches/building-material-dealers/roofing-siding-exterior-distribution/profile|Roofing, Siding & Exterior Products Distribution]]
**Industry:** [[industries/building-material-dealers|Building Material Dealers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A roof crew on a stripped house finds the ridge vent or drip edge missing, and the distributor sends a pickup across town at full cost.
**Tags:** #k-nearest-neighbors #logistic-regression #quick-win #worker-facing #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to land the complete roof or siding system a contractor ordered — matched to the job's measurement report and on the rooftop the morning of the tear-off — and whoever misses the fewest rooftop drops when a storm multiplies demand takes the account.

## The Problem
Short accessories are the commonest rooftop failure: a few bundles of ridge cap, a roll of underlayment, a box of vents. The crew stops, the contractor calls, and a branch driver makes an unplanned run.

## Why It's Still Broken
Orders are built from the contractor's list or the salesperson's memory, and short-ship causes aren't coded, so the same omissions repeat.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A completeness check at order entry that compares each roof order with the measurement report and with completed orders for similar roofs, flags missing system components, and asks the salesperson to confirm before the load is built.

## Who Feels the Pain
Roofing crews waiting on a bare deck, branch drivers on hot-shot runs, and contractors facing a weather risk.

## Impact If Fixed
If accessory shorts cause about half of second trips (estimate) and a busy branch makes ~10–20 unplanned rooftop runs a week in storm season (estimate), a completeness check removes ~5–10 of them a week at ~$75–150 each in driver time and fuel (estimate), and fewer crews wait on a bare deck.

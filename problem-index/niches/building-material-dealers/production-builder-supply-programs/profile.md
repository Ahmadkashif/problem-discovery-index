# Production Builder Supply Programmes

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/building-material-dealers|Building Material Dealers]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to supply every start of a production builder's plans complete and on the builder's schedule at the per-plan price agreed for the period, and whoever runs the fewest short, late or variance-PO starts per hundred at that price takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$45B a year of dealer sales of framing packages, components, windows, doors, trim and installed services to production builders (estimate: roughly half of ~939,000 single-family starts in 2025 (NAHB, search summary) built by production builders, at ~$80,000–$100,000 of dealer-supplied material and installation per house)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~9% of the codes' receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — POs and schedule releases arrive through builder portals, but per-plan pricing, variance POs and back-charges are reconciled by hand
**Target Buyer:** Builder division purchasing manager (the customer); the dealer's VP of national or builder accounts
**Automation Potential:** Medium-High for schedule-driven ordering and reconciliation; low for the price negotiation

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
This is the post-award contest. A production builder takes off each plan once, bids it to two or three dealers for a period, and then releases starts plan by plan through its portal. The dealer wins or loses the division on execution: every start supplied complete, on the day the framer is scheduled, at the agreed price, with variance POs (extras the builder approves) and back-charges (costs the builder claws back) kept low. Lumber moves during the period, so the price terms — fixed, indexed to a composite or repriced monthly — decide who carries the market. Builders FirstSource said in its 2025 results that it serves 48 of the top 50 US metro areas (CBSAs), which is the footprint national builders buy against; US LBM, QXO's Kodiak (~40% of its ~$2.4B 2025 revenue in Florida and Texas) and regional dealers with their own truss plants compete for the same divisions. It differs from takeoff, whose contest ends at award, and from [[niches/building-material-dealers/job-site-delivery-dispatch/profile|Job-Site Delivery Dispatch & Yard Management]], which serves every customer; here the measure is per start and per plan.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Builder-supplier portals (Hyphen SupplyPro), LBM ERPs, the dealers' component plants and installed-framing crews. The gaps: per-plan margin tracked against the market only in spreadsheets, variance POs coded loosely, and start schedules that change without the dealer's yard seeing them.

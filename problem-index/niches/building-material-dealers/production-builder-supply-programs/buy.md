# Schedule Releases Turned Into Yard Plans

**Niche:** [[niches/building-material-dealers/production-builder-supply-programs/profile|Production Builder Supply Programmes]]
**Industry:** [[industries/building-material-dealers|Building Material Dealers]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Builder portals publish start schedules; dealers read them as orders, not as a forecast for trucks, truss slots and inventory.
**Tags:** #time-series-forecasting #survival-analysis #data-integration #workflow-orchestration #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to supply every start of a production builder's plans complete and on the builder's schedule at the per-plan price agreed for the period, and whoever runs the fewest short, late or variance-PO starts per hundred at that price takes the account.

## The Problem
A builder's schedule moves daily: a start slips a week, three lots release at once, a plan is swapped. The yard learns at the last minute and builds loads in a rush.

## What Already Exists
Builder-supplier portals carry POs and schedule releases; LBM ERPs and dispatch tools plan from confirmed orders; general demand-planning software forecasts SKUs.

## The Customization Gap
A forecast of next-week and next-month demand by plan built from the builder's release history (how often its "scheduled" starts slip, by division and superintendent), translated into truss-plant slots, framing packages to stage and trucks to book. The customisation is reading the builder's own slippage pattern, which generic demand planning doesn't model.

## Target Customer
Operations managers at dealers and component plants serving production builders.

## Impact If Solved
If rush loads and missed truss slots fall by half (estimate), a yard serving ~1,000 starts a year (estimate) saves hundreds of extra truck runs and supplies starts on the day the framer arrives.

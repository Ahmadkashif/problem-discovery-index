# Route Planning Taught Boom Trucks and Load Order

**Niche:** [[niches/building-material-dealers/job-site-delivery-dispatch/profile|Job-Site Delivery Dispatch & Yard Management]]
**Industry:** [[industries/building-material-dealers|Building Material Dealers]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** General routing software optimises stops and time windows; a lumber dealer also needs truck capability, load length and weight, and the order the load must come off.
**Tags:** #optimization-fundamentals #dynamic-programming #gradient-boosting #workflow-orchestration #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get each load to a ready site, built in the right order and dropped in the right spot on the day promised, and whoever runs the fewest failed or second-truck deliveries per hundred drops takes the account.

## The Problem
Dispatchers plan around constraints no generic router knows: which truck has a boom that reaches a second-storey drop, which loads fit on one flatbed, which job needs a moffett, and that a framing package must be loaded so the floor system comes off first.

## What Already Exists
Route-optimisation and delivery-management software, ERP dispatch boards, and telematics.

## The Customization Gap
Truck and attachment capabilities, load length, weight and bundle constraints, reverse-sequence loading, yard pick times by item, site constraints (overhead lines, soft ground, narrow streets) and builder delivery windows, all inside the dealer's ERP order flow.

## Target Customer
Dealers with mixed fleets and multiple yards.

## Impact If Solved
If plans that know truck capability and load order remove the wrong-truck and wrong-sequence deliveries, ~1–2 per hundred drops (estimate), a 20-truck fleet making ~80 drops a day (estimate) saves one or two second trips daily, and the dispatcher stops overriding the plan.

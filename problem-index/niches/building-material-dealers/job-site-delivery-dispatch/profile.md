# Job-Site Delivery Dispatch & Yard Management

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/building-material-dealers|Building Material Dealers]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get each load to a ready site, built in the right order and dropped in the right spot on the day promised, and whoever runs the fewest failed or second-truck deliveries per hundred drops takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$12B a year of delivery and yard load-out cost at US building-material dealers — trucks, boom and forklift equipment, drivers, yard crews and dispatch (estimate: ~3–5% of the codes' ~$300B of contractor-facing sales)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~2.5% of the codes' receipts in cost (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low-Medium — larger dealers run dispatch and GPS tools, but site readiness, drop location and load order are arranged by phone
**Target Buyer:** Operations manager or dispatcher; the general manager when the case is fleet cost
**Automation Potential:** High for routing, load sequencing, site confirmation and proof of delivery; low for the driving and unloading

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Job-site delivery is what a pro dealer sells besides material, and it is a different problem from parcel or freight delivery. Loads are long, heavy and built in the order the framer will use them; trucks carry booms, moffett forklifts or conveyors; drop sites are unaddressed lots, muddy subdivisions and occupied homes; and a failed drop wastes a truck-morning and often a framing crew's day. The work is mechanical and repeatable — sequence orders into routes, build loads in reverse drop order, confirm sites, prove delivery — which is why it is the most automatable corner of the dealer's operation. Parcel-side routing and proof-of-delivery are mapped at [[niches/last-mile-delivery/route-optimization-vendors/profile|Route Optimization Vendors]] and [[niches/last-mile-delivery/proof-of-delivery-documentation/profile|Proof-of-Delivery Documentation & Disputes]]; the dealer-specific difference is site readiness and load order.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Dispatch modules in LBM ERPs, general route-planning and delivery-management software, GPS telematics, and driver phones. The gaps: site readiness confirmed by phone if at all, failure causes uncoded, load order built from memory, and yard pick status invisible to dispatch.

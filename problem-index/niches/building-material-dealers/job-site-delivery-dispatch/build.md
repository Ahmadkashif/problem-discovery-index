# Site Readiness Confirmed Before the Truck Leaves

**Niche:** [[niches/building-material-dealers/job-site-delivery-dispatch/profile|Job-Site Delivery Dispatch & Yard Management]]
**Industry:** [[industries/building-material-dealers|Building Material Dealers]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** The cheapest failed delivery is the one that never leaves the yard; a confirmation loop with the superintendent the evening before prevents most of them.
**Tags:** #gradient-boosting #logistic-regression #large-language-models #worker-facing #workflow-orchestration #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get each load to a ready site, built in the right order and dropped in the right spot on the day promised, and whoever runs the fewest failed or second-truck deliveries per hundred drops takes the account.

## The Problem
Loads go out to sites where the slab isn't cured, the lot is flooded, the previous trade's materials block the drop, or nobody can say where the framer wants it. The truck comes back and the yard rebuilds the load.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Dispatch tools plan routes from orders; they don't talk to superintendents, and the dealer's outside salesperson is the only link, by phone.

## What to Build
An agent that texts each superintendent or framer the evening before with the delivery window and asks for readiness, a drop pin and a placement note or photo; predicts failure risk from site history, weather and builder behaviour; and reschedules automatically when the answer is no. Replies flow to the driver's app. A two-week demo needs synthetic orders and a texting sandbox.

## Target Customer
Dispatchers and operations managers at dealers running ~10+ trucks.

## Impact If Built
If failed drops fall from ~5–10% to ~2–3% (estimate), each truck recovers several runs a month and drivers stop absorbing site problems.

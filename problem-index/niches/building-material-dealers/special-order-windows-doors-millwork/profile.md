# Special-Order Windows, Doors & Millwork

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/building-material-dealers|Building Material Dealers]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get each special-order window, door and millwork unit configured, ordered and delivered right the first time against the house's openings, and whoever posts the lowest remake rate on special-order units takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$40B a year of dealer sales of windows, exterior and interior doors, and millwork (estimate; Builders FirstSource reported $3.8B, 25.3% of its 2025 sales, in this category)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~8% of the codes' receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — each manufacturer's configurator is digital, but the hand-off from plan to configurator to dealer ERP to acknowledgement is re-keyed and checked by eye
**Target Buyer:** Window and door department manager or millwork manager at a pro dealer
**Automation Potential:** High for schedule reading, re-keying and acknowledgement checks; medium for design-stage selection

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Special-order units are made to a dealer's order and can't be returned, so an error is a remake with a second lead time. The work is a translation chain: the plan's window and door schedule and elevations become configured units in five or six manufacturers' tools, then dealer orders, then manufacturer acknowledgements, each step re-keyed. A pre-hung interior door package adds handing and jamb width; a whole-house package can run to dozens of units (estimate). The contest is first-time-right, measured as remakes per hundred units, which separates it from commodity lumber, where the contest is quantity and price. The makers selling through dealers are the queued millwork manufacturers build; custom architectural millwork shops are the queued cabinet and millwork shops build.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Manufacturer configurators and dealer portals (Andersen, Pella, Marvin, JELD-WEN and others), quote integrators such as Saberis, takeoff services that pass window takeoffs to quoting (Paradigm), and dealer ERPs. The gaps: no check of configured units against the house's openings, acknowledgements compared by eye or not at all, and remake causes uncoded.

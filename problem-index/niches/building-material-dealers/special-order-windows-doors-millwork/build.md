# Every Unit Checked Against Its Opening

**Niche:** [[niches/building-material-dealers/special-order-windows-doors-millwork/profile|Special-Order Windows, Doors & Millwork]]
**Industry:** [[industries/building-material-dealers|Building Material Dealers]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A configurator validates a window against its own catalogue; nothing validates it against the hole in the wall it is going into.
**Tags:** #object-detection #large-language-models #transformers #random-forests #data-integration #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get each special-order window, door and millwork unit configured, ordered and delivered right the first time against the house's openings, and whoever posts the lowest remake rate on special-order units takes the account.

## The Problem
Remakes come from mismatches between unit and house: a jamb for a 2x4 wall on a 2x6 house, a bedroom window too small for egress, a missing tempered requirement, a handing reversed against the floor plan.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Manufacturers check their own rules, dealers' ERPs store the order, and the plan set sits in the salesperson's email. No product reads all three.

## What to Build
A checker that extracts the window and door schedule, wall thickness, room types and swing directions from the plan set, matches each configured unit to its opening, applies code rules (egress, safety glazing, wind or energy requirements the jurisdiction sets) and flags mismatches with the sheet as evidence before the order is placed. A two-week demo works on public plan sets and sample configurator exports.

## Target Customer
Window and door departments at pro dealers and millwork distributors.

## Impact If Built
If remakes fall from ~3% of units to ~1% (estimate), the dealer saves the unit, the freight and weeks of a wrapped opening on site.

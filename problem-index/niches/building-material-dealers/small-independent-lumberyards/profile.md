# Small Independent Lumberyards & Building Supply Dealers

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/building-material-dealers|Building Material Dealers]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get a small yard, where the owner still prices most quotes, to answer every customer request — a walk-in or phoned list, an emailed list or a plan set — on the day it arrives, and whoever lifts the yard's same-day quote rate highest takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$37.6B in 2022 receipts at 17,047 firms under 20 employees in NAICS 444190, the 2017 code that became 444180 (Census SUSB 2022); ~$72.0B including the small wholesalers in 4233
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~8% of the codes' receipts (~15% with small wholesalers)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — a small ERP or a co-op's point-of-sale system, paper tickets in the yard, and plans read on the owner's laptop
**Target Buyer:** Owner-operator or general manager
**Automation Potential:** Medium — quoting, pricing and credit are automatable, but integration budgets are small and data is thin

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Most building-material firms are small. In the retail code, ~84% of firms have fewer than 20 employees; they are family lumberyards in rural counties and small towns, serving local builders, remodelers, farmers and homeowners, often as members of a co-op such as LBM Advantage or Do it Best. The owner is often the estimator, the credit manager and the commodity buyer. Vendors selling AI to distributors target the national rollups and the large regionals; their integration work and pricing pass these yards by, and the rollups buying their neighbours (Builders FirstSource, US LBM, QXO's Kodiak) bring estimating desks and digital ordering the small yard cannot match. The contest is turnaround across everything a small yard is asked to price — counter and phoned lists from remodelers and farmers, emailed lists, and the occasional plan set — measured as the share of requests answered the same day. That is a different measure from plan-set takeoff's hit rate: most small-yard requests are short lists, and the bottleneck is that only the owner knows the prices, not that the drawings are hard. The industrial and electrical counterparts are [[niches/industrial-distributors/small-independent-distributors/profile|Small Independent Industrial Distributors]] and [[niches/electrical-distributors/small-independent-electrical-distributors/profile|Small Independent Electrical Distributors]].

## Current Tools & Gaps
Co-op programmes and catalogues, small-dealer ERPs (ECI Spruce is positioned for yards with up to ~200 employees by one review site, search summary), outsourced takeoff (Paradigm Estimate, STACK offered through LBM Advantage), and the owner's spreadsheets. The gaps: quotes that wait for the owner, prices set from last month's invoice, no record of how many requests went unanswered that day, and no estimator to hire.

# Contractor Credit, Job Accounts & Lien Rights

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/building-material-dealers|Building Material Dealers]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep a dealer's losses on contractor credit down while it keeps selling on account, and whoever delivers the lowest write-offs as a share of credit sales takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$3B a year of bad debt, receivables carrying cost, credit and collections labour, and notice and lien services at US building-material dealers (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~0.6% of the codes' receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — credit applications arrive as PDFs, trade references are phoned, and notice deadlines are tracked in spreadsheets or outsourced
**Target Buyer:** Credit manager or controller at a pro dealer
**Automation Potential:** High for deadlines, notices and application intake; medium for the credit decision itself

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Pro dealers are their customers' bank. Most contractor sales go on account at 30 days or longer (estimate), to small builders, remodelers and framers with thin balance sheets, and a single failed builder can leave a dealer holding a whole subdivision's materials. The dealer's protection is statutory: mechanics' lien and bond rights, kept alive by notices served on clocks that start at first delivery — in California, a preliminary notice within 20 days of first furnishing (Civil Code §8204) — and different in every state. The credit manager therefore runs two jobs at once: deciding whom to trust and for how much, and making sure every job's notice is served before its window closes, from delivery dates that live in another system. Both jobs are scored by one number, write-offs as a share of credit sales: a bad credit call becomes an uncollectable balance, and a lapsed notice turns a recoverable one into a write-off. The notice services themselves are logged as a gate failure at [[niches/electrical-distributors/construction-notice-lien-services/profile|Construction Notice & Lien Services]], material financing at [[niches/electrical-distributors/contractor-material-financing/profile|Contractor Material-Financing Platforms]], and supplier trade credit generally at [[niches/credit-bureaus/commercial-trade-credit/profile|Commercial Trade Credit Reporting]].

## Current Tools & Gaps
Credit-application and monitoring platforms (Nuvo, used by Kodiak's Jones Heartz Building Supply to cut decisions from 4–5 days to 1–2 by Nuvo's account, search summary), notice services (Procore's Levelset and others), commercial credit bureaus, materials financing (Billd), and ERP job accounts. The gaps: job accounts opened late, first-delivery dates keyed by hand, and lien waivers tracked in folders.

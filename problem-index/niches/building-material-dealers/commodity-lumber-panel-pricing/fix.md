# Duty Changes Passed Through a Month Late

**Niche:** [[niches/building-material-dealers/commodity-lumber-panel-pricing/profile|Commodity Lumber & Panel Pricing]]
**Industry:** [[industries/building-material-dealers|Building Material Dealers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** When softwood duty rates or Section 232 tariffs change, dealers update costs when the next invoice arrives rather than when the rule takes effect.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #compliance #quick-win #revenue-impact #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to reprice every open quote, builder price sheet and counter price to the latest lumber and panel market before the material ships, and whoever lets the fewest packages ship below replacement cost without losing bids to stale prices takes the account.

## The Problem
Commerce's administrative review results and tariff proclamations change the landed cost of Canadian lumber on a date set in the Federal Register. Dealers often learn through the next supplier invoice.

## Why It's Still Broken
Duty is buried in suppliers' delivered prices, and no one at a dealer owns the trade calendar.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A watch on Federal Register notices for the softwood lumber orders and Section 232 actions, translated into a cost adjustment by product origin and applied to price lists and open quotes on the effective date.

## Who Feels the Pain
Commodity buyers caught between suppliers' new prices and customers' old quotes.

## Impact If Fixed
Duty changes priced on their effective date. The seventh-review rates cut the All Others deposit by ~10 points, so a month's lag on a busy yard's Canadian lumber sales is real money in either direction, fixed with a few hours of set-up.

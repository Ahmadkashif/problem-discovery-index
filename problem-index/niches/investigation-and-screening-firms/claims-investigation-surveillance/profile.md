# Insurance Claims Investigation & Surveillance

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/investigation-and-screening-firms|Investigation & Background Screening Firms]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to put claim-changing evidence on the file before the claim's next decision date — the independent medical exam, the deposition, the hearing or the settlement conference — inside the carrier's hour caps, and whoever produces the most usable activity per billed hour takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$2B a year of surveillance, activity checks, field and desk investigations bought by carriers, TPAs and self-insured employers (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~25% (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — assignments, reports and video move through portals; field work, timing and the investigator's read do not
**Target Buyer:** Director of the Special Investigation Unit at a carrier or TPA; claims vendor manager
**Automation Potential:** Medium — desk research, logging and reporting are automatable; field presence is not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Workers' compensation, auto bodily injury, disability and general liability claims generate the industry's steadiest investigation work: surveillance of claimants, activity checks, recorded statements, scene canvasses, background and social media reports. Carriers buy through approved-vendor panels with billing guidelines, and the assignments are timed against claim events set by others — exam dates, hearing calendars, statutory fraud-reporting windows such as California's 60 days. The examination-under-oath record that follows some of these cases is analysed at [[niches/court-reporting-firms/insurance-euo-reporting/profile|Insurance EUO Reporting]].

## Current Tools & Gaps
Regional and national investigation firms, many PE-backed roll-ups; carriers' claims systems (Guidewire, Duck Creek) with vendor assignment; investigator case management (Trackops, CaseFleet); video and report portals. Gaps: carriers allocate work among panel vendors by habit and price, not by yield; hour caps force mid-surveillance phone calls for re-authorisation; and the result rarely flows back to the order.

# County Court Record Retrieval

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/investigation-and-screening-firms|Investigation & Background Screening Firms]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to return a county criminal search with the full disposition and the identifiers needed to match it inside the screener's turnaround window, from courts that restrict or refuse online access, and whoever covers the most slow and restricted counties without a re-pull takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$700M a year of county and state court searches fulfilled by court-runner networks, researcher firms and direct court integrations, most of it sold to screening firms (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~9% (estimate; largely an input to the screening niches)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — many courts allow searches only at a public terminal or the clerk's counter, and results come back as typed notes or screenshots
**Target Buyer:** VP of Fulfilment or Court Operations at a screening firm
**Automation Potential:** Medium — online courts automate; terminal-only courts need a person

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The criminal search is the core of a background report, and its speed is set by the slowest court in the address history. Some counties publish searchable portals; others restrict identifiers (California courts stopped allowing date-of-birth searches after *Hamrick* in 2021, and Los Angeles removed month and year of birth in 2024, leaving some checks waiting weeks or months); many require a runner at the courthouse. Retrievers are small firms and networks of independent runners paid per search, coordinated by researchers who know each court's habits. The screener-side orchestration problem is analysed at [[niches/hr-consultants/background-screening-providers/buy|Court Record Retrieval Across Three Thousand Jurisdictions]]; this niche is the retriever's own business.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Runner networks and researcher firms, wholesale court-record vendors, direct court integrations, and email or portal exchanges with screeners. Gaps: nobody keeps a shared, current map of what identifiers each court shows; runners' observations about backlogs and missing dispositions are lost; and dispatch is by spreadsheet and text message.

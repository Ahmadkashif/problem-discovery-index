# Criminal Record & Booking Data Aggregators

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/investigation-and-screening-firms|Investigation & Background Screening Firms]]
**Category:** Insight Layer
**Value-Chain Position:** Data & benchmark vendors

## What They Do
Wholesale vendors assemble court, corrections, sex-offender registry and jail booking records into national criminal databases and alert feeds that screening firms resell as "national criminal" searches and continuous monitoring. Equifax bought Appriss Insights, described as the nation's most comprehensive incarceration data network, for $1.825B in 2021; LexisNexis Risk Solutions and a few smaller wholesalers sell the rest. Screeners treat database hits as pointers to confirm at the source court.

## Insight Function
**Size:** ~30-150 data acquisition, normalisation and quality analysts per aggregator (estimate)
**Output:** Database search results and booking alerts priced per search or per monitored person
**Proprietary data:** Bulk court, corrections and booking records collected and normalised over years under source agreements; the records themselves are public
**Clock:** None set by a third party; FCRA §613's "strict procedures" standard is continuous, not a deadline
**Buyer:** General Manager / Head of Data Acquisition for criminal data

**Verdict:** Logged — below threshold (32). Lookup rather than analysis (Q1 3, as for [[niches/directory-and-list-publishers/people-search-directories/profile|People-Search & Public-Record Directories]], 34), public inputs cap Q3 at 3, and a handful of wholesalers make a thin market (Q5 2). Screening and tenant pockets built on similar court records score Q3 4–5 because they also hold their own adjudication, outcome and dispute history; the wholesaler sells the record and never learns what it led to.

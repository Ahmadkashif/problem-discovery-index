# Corporate Due Diligence & Executive Vetting

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/investigation-and-screening-firms|Investigation & Background Screening Firms]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to surface the disqualifying fact about a person or counterparty that a database screen misses before the signing or appointment date, and whoever delivers the most corroborated red flags inside that date takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.5B US integrity due diligence, executive and board vetting and pre-transaction investigation revenue, much of it at firms coded outside NAICS 561611 (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~10% of the activity counted here (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low-Medium — registries and databases are searched online, but foreign court files, local press archives and discreet source inquiries run on retained local researchers, phone calls and the analyst's memory
**Target Buyer:** General Counsel, Chief Compliance Officer, private equity deal partner, or executive search partner
**Automation Potential:** Medium — retrieval and assembly automate; source work and judgement do not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Clients hire these firms to learn what a screening hit or a clean database result does not show: a founder's undisclosed litigation abroad, a distributor's ownership by a government official, an executive candidate's departure from a prior employer under investigation. Kroll, Control Risks, Nardello, Mintz Group, K2 Integrity and hundreds of boutiques compete. Executive vetting is a volume line inside the same firms: Mintz has been the AESC's due diligence partner for more than 12 years, and in the AESC–Mintz 2024 survey screenings ended a candidacy or raised concern in 22% of searches. Executive search firms left this work to this industry ([[industries/executive-search-firms|Executive Search Firms]]).

## Current Tools & Gaps
Screening databases (World-Check, Dow Jones), ownership data (Moody's Orbis, Sayari), litigation and press archives, national registries, and retained researchers abroad. Gaps: each report is scoped from scratch; the firm's knowledge of which sources pay off in which country is not recorded; and executive vetting usually starts only at the finalist stage.

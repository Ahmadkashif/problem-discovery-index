# Executive Vetting That Starts After the Shortlist

**Industry:** [[industries/investigation-and-screening-firms|Investigation & Background Screening Firms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Executive due diligence is ordered on the finalist, weeks into a search, so a disqualifying finding lands when the board has already chosen.

**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to surface the disqualifying fact about a person or counterparty that a database screen misses before the signing or appointment date, and whoever delivers the most corroborated red flags inside that date takes the account.

## The Problem
In a typical [[industries/executive-search-firms|executive search]] the in-depth background investigation is ordered once a finalist is chosen, with the appointment date already pencilled in. If it finds litigation, a misstated degree or a prior departure under investigation, the board must restart or accept the risk under time pressure. The AESC–Mintz 2024 survey found screenings ended a candidacy or raised concern in 22% of searches — findings that would have been cheaper earlier.

## Why It's Still Broken
Full investigations are expensive and need the candidate's consent, so search firms wait; nobody offers a light, consented, public-source review priced for a long list of ~8–12 candidates (estimate).

## What a Fix Looks Like
A staged product: at long-list, a consented public-record and media review per candidate in a day, with a short structured flag list; at finalist, the full investigation scoped by what the light review found. Search firms build the stages into the search timeline and fee.

## Who Feels the Pain
Search partners whose placements fall through late; boards and nominating committees deciding under deadline; candidates whose issues could have been explained early.

## Impact If Fixed
Disqualifying findings arrive before the shortlist hardens, failed placements fall, and the full investigation is spent where it matters.

# Candidates With Records

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/investigation-and-screening-firms|Investigation & Background Screening Firms]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get a candidate's wrong, sealed or incomplete record corrected or explained before the employer's response window closes, and whoever resolves the most candidate challenges inside that window takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$300M a year of dispute handling, adjudication, fair-chance assessment and candidate-support services inside screening contracts, plus legal aid and record-clearing services (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~4% (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — candidates dispute by phone, email and uploaded court papers; sealing orders travel by mail
**Target Buyer:** Head of fair-chance hiring or Talent Acquisition at a large employer; VP of Consumer Relations at a screening firm
**Automation Potential:** Medium

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The person the report is about is not the customer. A candidate with a record — or with someone else's — gets a pre-adverse notice and a short window: under California law at least five business days to respond and five more if disputing accuracy. They must obtain court papers, prove a sealing or expungement, show rehabilitation for the individualized assessment, and file a dispute the screener must reinvestigate — the bureau-side version of that process is [[niches/credit-bureaus/fcra-dispute-reinvestigation/profile|FCRA Dispute Reinvestigation]]. Employers with fair-chance hiring programmes want these candidates to get through; NELP counts 37 states and over 150 cities and counties with ban-the-box rules. The CFPB's 2024 opinion on sealed records and missing dispositions was withdrawn in May 2025, leaving the standard to state law and litigation.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Screeners' candidate portals and dispute centres, legal aid expungement clinics and eligibility tools, and record-clearing laws that seal some records automatically. Gaps: candidates do not know what a report will show before applying; sealing orders reach the court but not every database holding the old record; and mitigation evidence arrives as scattered uploads.

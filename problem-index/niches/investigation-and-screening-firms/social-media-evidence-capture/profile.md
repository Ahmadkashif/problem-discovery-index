# Social Media & Open-Source Evidence Capture

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/investigation-and-screening-firms|Investigation & Background Screening Firms]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to find a subject's public posts and preserve them with provable authenticity before they are deleted or locked, and whoever delivers the most captured posts that survive an authentication challenge takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$300M a year of social media canvasses, online activity reports and open-source investigations bought by insurers, TPAs, law firms and employers (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~4% (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — searching is digital; capture is still screenshots, screen recordings and manual URL logs at many firms
**Target Buyer:** SIU director or claims vendor manager; litigation partner; investigation firm owner
**Automation Potential:** High

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A claimant who says they cannot work may post photos from a softball game; a witness may have described an accident in a public comment; a litigant may have deleted a review. Desk investigators search platforms, identify the subject's accounts, capture public content and write it up, often before surveillance is ordered and as a cheaper alternative to it. The work is repetitive and screen-based, but it has two hard edges: proving the account belongs to the subject, and proving the capture is authentic and complete if it reaches a hearing. Ethics rules bar deceptive "friending", so only public content is fair game.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Open-source investigation tools (Skopenow, ShadowDragon and others), web capture and archiving tools, social listening platforms built for brands, and manual search. Gaps: account attribution is a judgement written in prose; captures lack hashes, metadata and context that would support self-authentication; and posts vanish between discovery and capture.

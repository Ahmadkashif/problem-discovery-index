# Niche Analysis — Investigation & Background Screening Firms

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/investigation-and-screening-firms|Investigation & Background Screening Firms]]

## Niche Selection

The industry sells findings about people: whether a candidate has a reportable record and a true work history, whether a claimant is as injured as claimed, whether a counterparty or an executive carries a hidden problem. Screening firms assemble those findings from court indexes, databases and phone calls under the FCRA; investigators gather them in the field, online and from sources, for insurers, lawyers and companies. The eight niches below split the industry by what operators and vendors actually compete over. Market sizes are estimates of revenue or spend within each niche, not of the Census total; several include firms coded outside NAICS 561611, and the court retrieval niche is largely an input sold to the screening niches.

## Why These Niches

Employment screening takes the largest share because it is most of the industry's revenue — First Advantage alone reported $1.57B in 2025 and the 32 largest firms in the code took ~46% of its receipts — and claims investigation is second because insurers, TPAs and self-insured employers are the steadiest buyers of investigation hours, under billing caps and claim deadlines they set. The two low-digitized niches are where courthouse terminals, retained researchers abroad and phone calls still carry the work: county court searches, which set every screening report's turnaround, and due diligence on counterparties and executives, where the finding that matters is the one a database misses. The two underserved audiences are the person the screening report is about, who is not the customer and has days to correct it, and the owner-investigator running one of the 2,806 firms with fewer than five employees. The two automatable niches are the most mechanical, screen-bound work in the industry: closing verifications, and finding and preserving a subject's public posts.

## Filter Notes

Seven of the eight level-1 niches are terminal. Enterprise Employment Background Screening is not: its contested sentence produced two contests with different winners. The high-volume hourly and gig employer renews on the share of applicants cleared within a day, because a slow report is a lost hire; the regulated employer — hospital, bank, carrier — renews on never having an excluded, unlicensed or newly charged person on shift, found by a surveyor or payer audit. Different buyers, evidence, clocks and products (same-day decisioning versus continuous eligibility records), so it decomposes into **High-Volume Hourly & Gig Screening** and **Regulated-Workforce Screening & Monitoring**.

Three terminal calls were close. **Corporate Due Diligence & Executive Vetting** joins compliance-driven counterparty due diligence and executive and board vetting; the buyers differ (compliance officers and deal teams versus [[industries/executive-search-firms|search firms]] and boards), but the same firms do both with the same method, and both contests are decided by the red flag a database misses before a signing or appointment date, so one sentence governs. **Employment, Education & Licence Verification** overlaps the credit-bureau niche [[niches/credit-bureaus/employment-income-verification/profile|Employment & Income Verification]]; it is kept because that contest is the database's coverage across lenders, landlords and agencies, while this one is the screener's job of closing every verification, database or not. **Insurance Claims Investigation & Surveillance** could be split into surveillance and field or desk investigation, but the carrier buys all three through one panel, on one set of billing guidelines, against the same claim dates.

Four candidates were considered and rejected. **Skip tracing and locate work** is done by investigators, but its operator contest is already analysed as [[niches/collections-agencies/skip-tracing-operations/profile|Skip Tracing Operations]], for process servers as [[niches/legal-support-services/hard-to-serve-skip-trace-service/profile|Hard-to-Serve & Skip-Trace Service]] for recovery agents as [[niches/repossession-services/skip-assignments-location/profile|Skip Assignments & Location Work]], and its data layer as [[niches/collections-agencies/identity-skip-trace-data-providers/profile|Identity Resolution & Skip Trace Data Providers]]; claiming it here would duplicate siblings. **Tenant screening** uses the same court records but belongs to [[industries/credit-bureaus|Credit Bureaus]] as [[niches/credit-bureaus/tenant-screening-decisions/profile|Tenant Screening Reports & Decisions]]. **Domestic and private-client investigations** (infidelity, custody, missing persons) were rejected as a niche because the honest contest — discretion and the investigator's personal reputation with family lawyers — is relationship selling, not a contest a product decides. **Retail loss prevention**, where BLS places 34% of private detectives, is in-house work at retailers rather than this industry's. The queue's boundary neighbours are kept out: confirming that an applicant is who they claim at onboarding is [[industries/identity-verification-vendors|Identity Verification Vendors]], and testing candidates' skills is [[industries/talent-assessment-platforms|Talent Assessment Platforms]].

## Pass 2 — Insight-Layer Discovery

Second, orthogonal sweep. This pass sweeps seven positions in the value chain — aggregator/rollup, payer & intermediary, data & benchmark vendors, association research arms, specialist advisory & valuation, regulatory bodies, and suppliers selling into the industry — looking for a dedicated insight function of 10+ people whose analytical output is the billable deliverable. Full method in `_direction.md`. Pockets already profiled under other industries are named and linked below rather than profiled again.

**Kill-switch judgement for this industry.** Following the contributed-data rule set in batch 7: data furnished under FCRA furnishing or membership terms, or held in trust for contributors, is a live switch; data supplied on terms that give the vendor product rights — including public records bought under bulk-data agreements — is not. Work commissioned through counsel or performed on client-owned files (a carrier's claim file, a client's confidential engagement) is a live "data isn't theirs" switch, the vault's majority rule. Statutory confidentiality of fraud referrals is live; government procurement is not.

## Why These Pockets

The sweep adds no qualifier, and that is the central finding. Most of the operators fail the gate: 2,806 of 3,767 employer firms have fewer than five employees, and even the national claims investigation and litigation firms employ field, desk and case investigators who produce one report or one day of surveillance at a time — production, not an insight function — so their desk units and litigation teams are logged as gate failures. The exception is the national screeners, which are this industry's operators too and do clear the gate; they are already profiled elsewhere (below). Where analytical teams exist outside the screeners, they mostly work on data that is not theirs: corporate investigation firms (42) sell insight as the invoice, but each report belongs to the commissioning client and much internal-investigation work runs through counsel, the same ruling as [[niches/expert-networks/commercial-due-diligence-advisors/profile|Commercial Due Diligence Advisors]] (50, kill switch) — and most of the gap between 42 and 50 is rule drift, since that pocket took Q4 5 on bid deadlines, a client's clock, and would sit at ~46 now; state fraud bureaus (31) hold referrals under statutory confidentiality.

The industry's best-scored insight pocket is already in the vault and is linked, not re-profiled: [[niches/hr-consultants/background-screening-providers/profile|Background Screening Providers]] (55, indexed) profiles Sterling, HireRight, Checkr and their peers — this industry's own screening operators — with crossref copies under [[niches/staffing-agencies/background-screening-crossref/profile|staffing agencies]], [[niches/it-staffing-firms/background-screening-crossref/profile|IT staffing]], [[niches/security-guard-firms/background-screening-crossref/profile|security guard firms]], [[niches/tutoring-centers/background-screening-crossref/profile|tutoring centers]] and [[niches/youth-sports-orgs/background-screening-crossref/profile|youth sports]]. Its assembled court records and adjudication and dispute history are the screener's own under the rule above, so it carries no switch. Its score needs a caveat, logged for Stage E: it counts 300–2,000 researchers, verifiers and adjudicators (largely production headcount) as the insight function and takes Q4 5 on hiring turnaround, the employer's schedule rather than a third-party deadline; under the current rules it would sit at ~49, just below the line. The neighbouring data and analytics pockets are linked too: [[niches/collections-agencies/identity-skip-trace-data-providers/profile|Identity Resolution & Skip Trace Data Providers]] (54, indexed) is the TLOxp and Accurint layer investigators search, and red violet's idiCORE (10,022 customers at end-2025) belongs with it; [[niches/customs-brokers/sanctions-screening-data-providers/profile|Sanctions & Restricted Party Screening Data]] (55, indexed) is the screening data due diligence firms start from; [[niches/property-management/tenant-screening-data/profile|Tenant Screening Data & Scoring]] (54, indexed) and [[niches/childcare-centers/childcare-background-check-vendors/profile|Childcare Background Check & Screening Vendors]] (44) are the rental and childcare versions of screening; [[niches/directory-and-list-publishers/people-search-directories/profile|People-Search & Public-Record Directories]] (34) is the consumer end of public-record lookup; insurer fraud work is [[niches/chiropractic-practices/insurance-fraud-investigation-bureaus/profile|Insurance Fraud Investigation Bureaus]] (37), [[niches/insurance-tpa/claims-fraud-analytics-vendors/profile|Claims Fraud Analytics Vendors]] (46) and [[niches/public-adjusters/claims-data-fraud-analytics/profile|Property Claims Data & Fraud Analytics]] (47); the medical evidence that surveillance is timed against is [[niches/chiropractic-practices/ime-report-firms/profile|Independent Medical Examination Firms]] (51, kill switch); FCRA enforcement research is [[niches/collections-agencies/cfpb-consumer-research/profile|Federal Consumer Finance Research]] (34); and digital forensics after breaches is [[niches/cybersecurity-mssp/breach-forensics-firms/profile|Incident Response & Breach Forensics Firms]] (54, kill switch).

The strongest near-miss is the executive vetting practice (42, no switch, on the same footing as the screeners): a same-shaped report per candidate, a corpus of recurring executives and an outcome that later events grade — held back by public inputs, the board's own dates and a market of a handful of firms. The best clean pocket is criminal record and booking data aggregators (32): no switch, because public records bought in bulk are the vendor's to sell, but lookup rather than analysis, public inputs and a thin wholesale market keep it far below the line. The vendor programmes at carriers and TPAs fail the gate, yet they are the natural buyers for the yield tooling in niche 2.

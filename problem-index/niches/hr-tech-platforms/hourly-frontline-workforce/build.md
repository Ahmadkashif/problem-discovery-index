# A Record the Worker Can Verify

**Niche:** [[niches/hr-tech-platforms/hourly-frontline-workforce/profile|Hourly & Frontline Workforce]]
**Industry:** [[industries/hr-tech-platforms|HR Tech Platforms]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** An hourly worker's pay depends on hours recorded by the employer, accruals computed by the employer and premiums determined by the employer, and the worker's view of all three is a total on a pay statement.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #compliance #workflow-orchestration #automation #worker-facing #data-integration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in frontline HR software is fighting to make an hourly worker's schedule, hours, accruals and pay visible and provable to the worker themselves — and whoever the workers actually use takes the employer.

## The Problem
A worker's pay statement shows 71.25 hours at a base rate, 3.5 at overtime, a shift differential and a sick leave balance. Whether the 71.25 matches the shifts they worked, whether a clock-in that failed was corrected, whether the overtime threshold was applied on the right basis, whether the differential applied to every qualifying shift and whether the sick leave accrued at the rate their city requires are all questions they cannot answer. The employer's system computed all of it correctly or incorrectly, and the worker's only recourse is to ask a manager who will look at the same system.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Workforce management systems are bought by operations leaders to control labour cost, and worker-facing transparency was never a scored requirement. Building it means exposing the arithmetic, which some employers would rather not do — not usually because the arithmetic is wrong, but because exposing it invites questions and questions take manager time. And the workforce in question has little purchasing influence and, in most of these settings, limited practical ability to insist.

## What to Build
A worker-held record that shows the arithmetic. Every shift as scheduled, as clocked and as paid, with any correction visible and attributed. Hours totalled with the overtime calculation shown against the applicable threshold. Premiums and differentials itemised against the shifts that triggered them. Accrual balances with the accrual events that produced them and the rate applied, including the jurisdictional basis — which is the point at which the multi-jurisdiction sub-niche's failures become visible to the person they affect. Predictive scheduling entitlements, where they apply, computed and shown alongside the schedule changes that triggered them. Everything in the worker's language, on a phone, without a company email address. A discrepancy is raised against a specific shift or entitlement and resolved against a record rather than a recollection. For the employer this is fewer disputes and better compliance evidence; for the worker it is the first independent view of a pay calculation that determines their income.

## Target Customer
Workforce management and frontline HCM vendors, employers with large hourly workforces and wage-and-hour exposure, and the worker advocacy and legal services organisations who see the disputes.

## Impact If Built
Wage and hour is among the most litigated areas of employment law and the disputes are overwhelmingly about records that one party holds and the other cannot see. Showing the arithmetic prevents most of them, gives the employer a contemporaneous defensible record, and gives the worker something they have never had — which is the ability to check.

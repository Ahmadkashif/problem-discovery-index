# Compensation & Pay Equity

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/hr-tech-platforms|HR Tech Platforms]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in compensation software is fighting to check a pay decision for equity and market position before it is made rather than after — and whoever moves the check to the point of decision takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.9B US compensation management, pay equity and total rewards software
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~8% of HR technology revenue, rising with pay transparency regulation
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — compensation planning cycles are systematised, individual decisions are not
**Target Buyer:** Total rewards leaders; increasingly general counsel, as pay transparency and reporting obligations expand
**Automation Potential:** Very High — equity analysis and market positioning are computable at the moment of decision and are performed annually

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Pay decisions are made continuously — at hire, at promotion, at retention, at market adjustment — and they are checked annually, in aggregate, by a pay equity analysis conducted by an external consultant under privilege. That sequencing guarantees the problem: disparities are created one decision at a time throughout the year and detected once at the end, by which point correcting them means a remediation exercise rather than a different decision. Meanwhile pay transparency regulation has expanded quickly — posted salary ranges, reporting obligations, restrictions on salary history — which turns what was a fairness question into a compliance question with an audit trail. The data required for the check at the point of decision is the same data used in the annual analysis, the calculation is the same, and it runs twelve months too late.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Compensation planning modules in every HCM handle merit cycles, budgets and approvals; specialist compensation platforms offer market benchmarking and range management; pay equity analysis is largely a consulting engagement supported by statistical software. The gaps: the equity check is annual, retrospective and privileged, which means it is designed to be discoverable as little as possible rather than to change decisions; the individual approving a pay decision sees a budget and a range and not the equity implication; range placement and market position are shown where compensation platforms are deployed and are absent for most employers; pay transparency compliance — that a posted range matches what is actually paid — is not verified; and compression against new hires, which is the single most consequential and most computable driver of both inequity and attrition, is monitored almost nowhere.

# Regression Methodology the Consultants Already Use

**Industry:** [[industries/hr-tech-platforms|HR Tech Platforms]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Pay equity analysis is a well-specified regression exercise with an established methodology, published guidance and decades of litigation practice behind it, and it is delivered to employers once a year as a consulting report.
**Tags:** #linear-regression #hypothesis-testing #confidence-intervals #evaluation-metrics #bayesian-inference #descriptive-statistics #compliance #cross-validation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in compensation software is fighting to check a pay decision for equity and market position before it is made rather than after — and whoever moves the check to the point of decision takes the account.

## The Problem
The methodology is not a secret. Pay is regressed on the legitimate explanatory factors within comparable groups, the residual variance associated with protected characteristics is examined, and the results are interpreted with attention to group sizes and to which factors are themselves potentially tainted. Employment economists have done this for decades, regulators have published guidance on it, and it has been litigated extensively. An employer receives the result annually as a report from a consultant and has no capability to run it themselves, which means they cannot check a proposed change, cannot monitor between analyses, and cannot investigate a specific finding without commissioning more work.

## What Already Exists
Regression methodology is elementary and free. The pay equity analysis approach is documented in regulatory guidance, in academic literature and in practitioner publications. Statistical software implements everything required. Market benchmarking data is commercially available from several providers. Job grouping frameworks exist. Every component of the analysis is available; what employers lack is it being run continuously inside their own systems.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to continuous internal use rather than an annual external report. It requires: (1) comparable group construction that is defensible and stable, since the grouping choice drives the result more than the regression does and an unstable grouping makes period-over-period comparison meaningless; (2) careful treatment of factors that may themselves carry disparity — prior salary, performance ratings, and time in grade are all commonly used explanatory variables and all can encode the very thing being measured, which is a methodological judgement that must be explicit rather than buried; (3) small-group handling, since most employers have many groups too small to analyse and reporting a spurious finding on eleven people is worse than reporting nothing; (4) scenario analysis, so a proposed set of pay decisions can be evaluated before it is executed, which is the capability that makes this an instrument rather than a report; and (5) an explicit position on privilege, since running the analysis internally changes its discoverability and the organisation should make that choice knowingly rather than stumble into it.

## Target Customer
Compensation platform vendors, total rewards functions, and the employment law and consulting firms who could deliver continuous monitoring rather than annual engagements.

## Impact If Solved
The methodology is public and the barrier has been delivery model rather than capability. Continuous internal analysis with scenario testing lets an organisation prevent disparities rather than discover them, and the explicit treatment of tainted explanatory factors is the methodological point that most annual analyses handle quietly and that determines whether the result means anything.

# Employee-Facing HR Tools

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/hr-tech-platforms|HR Tech Platforms]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor building employee-facing HR software is fighting to let an employee answer a question about their own employment without asking a person — and whoever answers accurately from the employee's own record takes the deployment.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$2.2B US spend attributable to employee self-service, HR service delivery and workforce experience
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~9% of HR technology revenue, against every employee in every customer
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — portals are universal and are used to submit tickets
**Target Buyer:** HR leaders buy; every employee uses; the HR service desk feels the consequences
**Automation Potential:** Very High — the questions are repetitive and the answers are in the employee's own record

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Every employee is a user of HR systems and almost none of them is a customer of one. The questions they have are simple, personal and repetitive: how much leave do I have and when does it expire, what is my parental leave entitlement, what happens to my equity if I leave, is this expense claimable, how do I add a dependent, what is my notice period, why was my pay different this month. Each has a correct answer that depends on the individual's own record, their location, their plan elections and the policy version in force. The employee asks an HR generalist, who looks it up, and answers — which is a competent process that consumes a substantial share of the HR function's capacity and delivers the answer in hours or days rather than seconds. HR service desks in large organisations handle enormous volumes of this, and the vast majority of it is a lookup.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Every HCM ships a self-service portal; HR service delivery platforms — ServiceNow's HR module and its competitors — handle case management and knowledge bases; chatbots have been widely deployed with mixed results. The gaps: knowledge bases contain policy documents rather than personal answers, so an employee searching for their leave entitlement finds the policy and must apply it to themselves; the chatbots that have been deployed mostly retrieve policy text rather than reasoning over the individual's record, which is why they are widely disliked; the answer is rarely traceable to the record and the policy version that produced it, so an employee cannot check it; and nothing measures whether employees got a correct answer, only whether a ticket was closed.

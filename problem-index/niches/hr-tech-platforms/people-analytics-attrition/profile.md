# People Analytics & Attrition

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/hr-tech-platforms|HR Tech Platforms]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in people analytics is fighting to diagnose the structural conditions that produce attrition months before the resignations — and whoever names the condition rather than scoring the person takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$3.9B US people analytics, workforce planning and retention software
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~16% of HR technology revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — dashboards are widespread, diagnosis is not
**Target Buyer:** Chief people officers, people analytics leaders and the executives who receive the turnover number
**Automation Potential:** Very High — the longitudinal employment record is the cleanest organisational dataset that exists and is used for reporting

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Voluntary attrition is the most expensive recurring event in most companies once replacement cost, ramp time and lost institutional knowledge are counted, and it is reported as a percentage after it happens. The drivers are structural and visible well in advance: pay that has compressed against what new hires in the same role are being offered, managers carrying spans of control that make attention impossible, job families with no internal mobility path so the only promotion available is at another company, cohorts whose promotion velocity has stalled relative to their peers, and reorganisations that left people reporting to someone who did not hire them. Every one of those is computable from the system of record, months ahead. The distinction that defines the niche is what the product does with it: a diagnosis of conditions is an organisational instrument, and a flight-risk score attached to individual employees is a surveillance product that will be used in ways nobody sanctioned, and the two are built from the same data.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Workday, SAP SuccessFactors and Oracle hold the record; Visier, ChartHop and One Model exist as a category precisely because the HCM systems do not answer analytical questions. Turnover reporting, headcount planning and org visualisation are well served. The gaps: attrition is reported rather than predicted, and where prediction exists it is usually an individual risk score with no prescribed action; the structural drivers are not surfaced as diagnosable conditions, so a leader is told the number and not what produced it; internal mobility — the strongest known retention lever and one entirely within the company's control — is unmeasured in most organisations; and nobody tracks whether a retention intervention worked, so the whole practice runs on belief.

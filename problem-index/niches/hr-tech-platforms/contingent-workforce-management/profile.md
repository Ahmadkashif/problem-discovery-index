# Contingent Workforce Management

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/hr-tech-platforms|HR Tech Platforms]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in contingent workforce software is fighting to give an employer one accurate view of everyone doing work for it, employee or not — and whoever can state total workforce and its classification exposure takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.7B US software spend across vendor management, contingent labour and workforce visibility
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~7% of HR technology revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Low-Medium — large enterprises run vendor management systems; most employers do not
**Target Buyer:** Ambiguous by design — procurement owns the spend, HR owns the risk, and neither owns the population
**Automation Potential:** High — the data exists across procurement, payroll and access systems and is never assembled

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Many organisations cannot state how many people work for them. The employees are in the HCM system. The contractors are in accounts payable. The staffing agency workers are in a vendor management system if the organisation is large enough to have one, and in a purchase order if it is not. The offshore development team is a line item in a services contract. Consultants, freelancers, and the people working through a services firm on site are scattered across procurement records. The consequences are substantial and specific: classification exposure, where someone treated as a contractor meets the tests for employment and the liability is retroactive; access and security risk, where people with system access are not in any joiner-mover-leaver process; cost blindness, where the true cost of a function is unknown because half of it is in a different budget; and a co-employment risk that grows with tenure and with how closely the work is directed. It is a workforce visibility problem that neither HR nor procurement owns.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Vendor management systems — SAP Fieldglass, Beeline and their competitors — serve large enterprises with requisition, rate and timesheet management for agency labour. Freelancer platforms handle their own populations. Procurement systems hold the spend. HCM systems hold employees. The gaps: no single view of total workforce, which is the thing every one of these problems requires; classification risk is assessed at engagement by a form and never re-assessed as the engagement extends and the working relationship changes, which is exactly when the risk grows; tenure and co-employment exposure is not tracked; and access provisioning for non-employees is frequently ad hoc, so leavers among the contingent population are the largest source of orphaned access in most organisations.

# Classification Assessed Once at Engagement

**Niche:** [[niches/hr-tech-platforms/contingent-workforce-management/profile|Contingent Workforce Management]]
**Industry:** [[industries/hr-tech-platforms|HR Tech Platforms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Whether someone is properly a contractor depends on how the work is actually directed and performed, which changes as an engagement extends, and it is assessed once on a form at the beginning and never again.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #hypothesis-testing #confidence-intervals #compliance #workflow-orchestration #automation #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in contingent workforce software is fighting to give an employer one accurate view of everyone doing work for it, employee or not — and whoever can state total workforce and its classification exposure takes the account.

## The Problem
A contractor is engaged for a three-month project with a defined deliverable, working independently with their own equipment. The engagement is assessed at the outset and is clearly correct. Twenty months later they are still there, they attend the team's daily meetings, their work is directed day to day by an internal manager, they use company equipment and systems, they have no other clients, and their engagement has been renewed six times without review. The classification tests that were satisfied in month one are not satisfied now. Nobody reassessed, because the assessment was an onboarding form, and the liability if the classification is challenged is retroactive across the whole period.

## Why It's Still Broken
Classification is treated as an engagement gate rather than as an ongoing property, which matches how the paperwork works and not how the risk behaves. The attributes that determine it — direction, integration, exclusivity, equipment, duration — are not recorded anywhere after the initial form, so reassessment would require gathering them afresh. And the organisational incentives run against looking: the hiring manager values the arrangement, procurement values the flexibility, and the function that would bear the liability is not in the loop.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Make classification a monitored property with the attributes captured continuously. Duration, renewal count, exclusivity, equipment provision, system access breadth, meeting and reporting-line integration, and whether the worker appears in team structures are all observable from systems the organisation already runs, and together they form a risk indicator that rises as an engagement drifts toward employment in substance. Trigger a reassessment on thresholds rather than on calendar, so a contractor approaching a duration or integration threshold surfaces for review while the position can still be corrected. Report the population-level exposure — how many engagements exceed each threshold, and what the aggregate retroactive liability would be — which is the number a general counsel wants and no organisation computes. Frame it as risk management rather than as a judgement about individuals, and be clear that the remedy is frequently conversion to employment, which is usually the right outcome for the worker as well.

## Who Feels the Pain
Workers in long contractor engagements who are functionally employees without the entitlements; general counsel carrying a retroactive exposure whose size nobody has computed; and hiring managers who did nothing wrong and created the risk by renewing.

## Impact If Fixed
Classification exposure is retroactive and grows with every month an engagement drifts, which makes early detection disproportionately valuable. The attributes needed are observable from existing systems, and threshold-triggered reassessment converts an unreviewed accumulation into a managed queue — while frequently identifying people who should have been made employees a year ago.

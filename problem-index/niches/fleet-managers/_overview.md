## Why These Niches

Delivery and service fleets represent the two largest segments with fundamentally different operational patterns — delivery optimizes for route density while service optimizes for response time. Municipal and small owner-operator fleets are digitally neglected due to budget constraints and low IT support. EV transition and aging fleet management represent emerging underserved needs that existing fleet tools don't address. DOT compliance and preventive maintenance are the two most rule-based, automatable workflows consuming disproportionate manual labor.

## Why These Pockets

**No qualifiers**, and the reason is that this industry's insight layer had already been found. Fleet management sits at the intersection of four industries swept earlier, and the four strongest pockets serving it — driver risk data, transportation compliance publishing, fleet maintenance analytics, and vehicle valuation — were logged under those. What remains once those are excluded is a set of competent but sub-threshold positions, which is the correct result rather than a thin one.

The pattern in what remains is familiar. Fuel card networks hold transaction-level fuel and maintenance purchasing across a large share of the US commercial fleet — a near-real-time economic dataset nobody else has — and fund a payments business. Lease residual analytics hold the link between a vehicle's actual operating history and its realized resale price, which is precisely what the vehicle valuation publishers indexed elsewhere do not have, serving a balance sheet position instead. And the two positions that do sell analysis as the invoice — cost-of-ownership data and fleet benchmarking consultancies — top out around eighty people each.

One near miss is worth noting for its clock. Fuel tax and registration reporting is high-volume repeatable filing work against quarterly statutory deadlines with audit consequences, scoring 47 on everything except the corpus: the jurisdictional rule knowledge is real but thin, and the mileage data belongs to the fleet.

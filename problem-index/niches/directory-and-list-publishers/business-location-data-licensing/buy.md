# Open Places Data as the Base Layer

**Niche:** [[niches/directory-and-list-publishers/business-location-data-licensing/profile|Business Location Data Licensing]]
**Industry:** [[industries/directory-and-list-publishers|Directory & List Publishers]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** An open places layer now gives every licensee a free baseline; a compiler that builds on it rather than against it can sell only what it adds.
**Tags:** #k-nearest-neighbors #word-embeddings #evaluation-metrics #data-integration #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to be the licensee's reference file for which businesses exist where right now — catching openings, moves and closures first — and whoever scores freshest in the licensee's own ground-truth audit takes the contract.

## The Problem
Licensees can download Overture's places layer at no cost, so a compiler's licence fee has to be justified by coverage, freshness and verification the open layer lacks, especially for small US businesses without a social-media presence.

## What Already Exists
Overture Maps' places dataset (59M+ places at its 2023 launch, from Meta and Microsoft contributions, under CDLA Permissive 2.0), with a published schema and identifiers.

## The Customization Gap
A compiler needs: a conflation pipeline that matches its own records to open identifiers so licensees can join them; a "delta" product that delivers only what the compiler verifies differently — closures, new openings, corrected phones and categories — with evidence; coverage reporting for small and home-based businesses the open layer under-represents; and licence terms that respect the open layer's permissive licence while protecting the compiler's verified additions.

## Target Customer
Place-data compilers repositioning against free baselines; platform data teams that want verified deltas rather than a second full file.

## Impact If Solved
The compiler sells the part of the data that is actually scarce, and licensees stop paying twice for the same baseline.

# The Ad Proof With the Wrong Phone Number

**Niche:** [[niches/directory-and-list-publishers/print-local-directories/profile|Print Local Directories]]
**Industry:** [[industries/directory-and-list-publishers|Directory & List Publishers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A transposed digit in a printed ad costs the advertiser a year of calls from the book and the publisher the account.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #evaluation-metrics #workflow-orchestration #quick-win #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to prove to a local advertiser that the printed book still rings the phone — tracked calls per heading, per edition — and whoever proves it keeps the renewal.

## The Problem
Ad copy changes are taken in conversations and emails during the canvass and keyed under close-date pressure. Errors — a wrong digit, an old address, a lapsed contractor licence number, a service area the business no longer covers — reach print, and the book cannot be corrected for a year. The result is a credit claim and usually a lost advertiser.

## Why It's Still Broken
Proofs are checked by eye, by the advertiser and the consultant, both rushed; nothing compares the copy with the publisher's own business record or with licence boards.

## What a Fix Looks Like
An automatic proof check that extracts phones, addresses, licence numbers and service claims from the ad, compares them with the compiled record, state licence lookups and the previous edition, and blocks release until every mismatch is confirmed.

## Who Feels the Pain
Advertisers who lose a year of calls, consultants who lose accounts and commission, and publishers paying credits.

## Impact If Fixed
The most damaging class of print errors is caught before the press, at almost no cost.

# Calls per Heading per Edition, Proven

**Niche:** [[niches/directory-and-list-publishers/print-local-directories/profile|Print Local Directories]]
**Industry:** [[industries/directory-and-list-publishers|Directory & List Publishers]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A print publisher sells an ad on the promise that the book rings the phone and almost never shows the advertiser how often it did.
**Tags:** #linear-regression #causal-inference #evaluation-metrics #feature-engineering #revenue-impact #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to prove to a local advertiser that the printed book still rings the phone — tracked calls per heading, per edition — and whoever proves it keeps the renewal.

## The Problem
A towing company has bought a half-page in the same book for years. Its owner thinks calls are down and asks to cancel. The consultant believes the heading still works in this county but has no numbers, and the conversation ends in a discount or a loss.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Print publishers built their systems for selling and producing ads, not measuring them; call tracking was added ad by ad as an upsell, and nobody aggregated it by heading, edition and distribution area.

## What to Build
A call-evidence model for each book: tracked calls where numbers exist, extrapolated to untracked ads by heading, ad size, position and distribution area, with seasonality and edition effects estimated, and comparable advertisers' trends shown. The output is a one-page renewal sheet per advertiser — expected calls at each ad size, and where the book no longer pays, an honest recommendation to shrink or switch spend to digital.

## Target Customer
Sales directors at print directory publishers, from national publishers' remaining print divisions to independent regional books.

## Impact If Built
Renewal conversations carry evidence, advertisers right-size rather than cancel, and the publisher learns which headings and areas still justify printing.

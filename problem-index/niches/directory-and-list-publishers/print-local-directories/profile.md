# Print Local Directories

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/directory-and-list-publishers|Directory & List Publishers]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to prove to a local advertiser that the printed book still rings the phone — tracked calls per heading, per edition — and whoever proves it keeps the renewal.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$400M US print directory advertising, still declining (estimate: Thryv, the largest print publisher, booked $324.0M across its whole print-and-digital Marketing Services segment in 2025; adding regional and independent publishers gives ~$300–500M. The Census figure of $1.78B for directory advertising space in 2020 also counts online directories)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~5% of the ~$8B US buyer spend on directories, lists and compiled business data, including directory advertising (estimate; see overview)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — annual canvass by consultants, paper or PDF proofs, close dates, printing and doorstep delivery
**Target Buyer:** Publisher's sales director; the advertiser is a local trade or service owner
**Automation Potential:** Medium — proofing, renewal evidence and canvass planning automate; the relationship sale does not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The printed phone book survives as a business, mainly in smaller and rural markets and among older households. Thryv still prints The Real Yellow Pages inside a Marketing Services segment that fell 32.6% to $324.0M in 2025; independent publishers print regional and community books. Revenue is advertising: plumbers, lawyers, towing, funeral homes and other local services buying display ads and listings by heading. Publishers have a constitutional right to deliver — Seattle's opt-out and per-book fee ordinance was struck down in Dex Media West v. City of Seattle (9th Cir. 2012) — but no right to a renewal. The decisive question in every renewal is whether the book still produces calls for this advertiser, and most of the evidence is the owner's memory.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Ad order and pagination systems, proofing workflows, tracked phone numbers on some display ads, and digital bundles sold alongside print. Gaps: call tracking is partial and reported per ad rather than per heading and edition; comparable-advertiser trends are not used in renewal conversations; and proof checks for phone, address and licence errors are manual under close-date pressure.

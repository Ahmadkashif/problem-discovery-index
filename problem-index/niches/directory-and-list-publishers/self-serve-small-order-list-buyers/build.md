# A Count That Is the Delivery

**Niche:** [[niches/directory-and-list-publishers/self-serve-small-order-list-buyers/profile|Self-Serve Small-Order List Buyers]]
**Industry:** [[industries/directory-and-list-publishers|Directory & List Publishers]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A small buyer is quoted a count, pays, and receives a file that shrinks after hygiene and bounces after mailing; the count should already be the clean, guaranteed delivery.
**Tags:** #gradient-boosting #logistic-regression #evaluation-metrics #feature-engineering #revenue-impact #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to hand a small buyer a few thousand names they can trust with no contract and no minimum — a count that matches what is delivered and a deliverability guarantee that actually pays out — and whoever makes the small order safe takes the buyer.

## The Problem
A landscaper selects ~5,000 homeowners on a list store, pays, and finds that a few hundred fail address hygiene and more come back undeliverable. Claiming the guarantee means counting returned pieces and emailing the compiler, which most small buyers never do.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Showing post-hygiene counts makes the compiler's universe look smaller against competitors, and automatic payouts on guarantees cost money compilers currently keep through friction.

## What to Build
A list builder that shows only records predicted deliverable at a stated confidence, prices on that count, de-duplicates against the buyer's uploaded customer list free of charge, and pays out the guarantee automatically from USPS tracking and email bounce data rather than buyer paperwork. Per-record deliverability probabilities come from the compiler's verification history, so the guarantee is priced, not hoped.

## Target Customer
Compilers and resellers running self-serve list stores.

## Impact If Built
Small buyers get the list they paid for, repeat orders rise, and the compiler competes on trust instead of raw counts.

# Market Universe & Firmographic Counts

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/directory-and-list-publishers|Directory & List Publishers]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to give a revenue team the complete, de-duplicated and correctly sized universe of accounts in its market, and whoever's count survives the customer's own match test — no dead, duplicate or mis-banded firms — becomes the system of record for targeting and territories.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.5B US spend on company-level firmographic data for targeting, market sizing and territory design (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~19% of the ~$8B US buyer spend on directories, lists and compiled business data, including directory advertising (estimate; see overview)
**Digital Adoption:** High — delivered as files, APIs and CRM enrichment
**Target Buyer:** Revenue Operations leader; Marketing Operations director; strategy and market-sizing analysts
**Automation Potential:** High — entity resolution and modelled size estimates are machine work once ground truth exists

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The unit sold is the account: a business with an industry code, an employee band, a sales estimate, locations and corporate links. Buyers use it to size a market ("how many dental practices with five or more chairs in Texas"), score and tier accounts, and carve territories that will set reps' quotas for a year. The test is a match: the buyer runs its CRM and its won-and-lost history against the vendor's universe and counts dead firms, duplicates and companies in the wrong band. Dun & Bradstreet's Hoovers and corporate linkage, Data Axle's business file and ZoomInfo's company records compete here. Most employee and sales figures for private firms are modelled, so the size band — the most-used select after industry — is an estimate presented as a fact.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Universe files with modelled size fields, corporate family trees, CRM enrichment connectors and master data management on the buyer side. Gaps: counts come with no stated error, modelled sizes carry no interval, a re-model can move thousands of accounts across bands without notice, and the universe rewards keeping marginal records because buyers compare raw counts. Hierarchy inside the buyer's CRM is the separate problem described under [[niches/crm-platforms/account-data-hygiene/profile|CRM Platforms: Account Data Hygiene]].

# Niche Analysis — Directory & List Publishers

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/directory-and-list-publishers|Directory & List Publishers]]

## Niche Selection

A directory or list publisher sells the claim that a record is true — that a business exists here, a person can be reached there, a household just moved — and every record decays from the day it is verified. Around that claim sit two ways of selling (by count, per thousand names, and by subscription or licence), a shrinking print business sold on an annual canvass, and a legal perimeter of deletion mandates, phone-calling rules and duties to vet who buys. The eight niches below split the industry by what competitors actually fight over. Market sizes are estimates of US buyer spend on each niche's data, listings and services. Every niche's share is stated against one denominator: ~$8B of US buyer spend on directories, lists and compiled business data, including directory advertising (estimate). That runs above the ~$4.6B of employer revenue Census counts for this code because the largest business-data sellers (Dun & Bradstreet, ZoomInfo, Experian) are partly classified elsewhere; the eight niches cover ~$7B of it, and the remainder is mainly consumer compiled lists and smaller specialist compilations not built as niches.

## Why These Niches

B2B sales intelligence is where the money in compiled data now sits, sold as annual seats and renewed or lost every year; business location licensing is the other growing pool, the compiled file licensed whole to the map, search and analytics platforms that show it to the public, and it now outweighs print, which is shrinking fast — Thryv's print-carrying segment fell 32.6% in 2025. The two low-digitized niches are where paper and personal contact still run the record: the printed local book sold by canvass and proof sheet, and directories compiled from questionnaires the listees are asked to return each edition. The two underserved audiences are buyers the platforms ignore — the small buyer of a few thousand names who cannot test a list before paying postage — and the immigrant communities whose in-language directories are invisible to English-first compilation and listings tools. The two automatable niches are the most mechanical products in the industry: trigger lists inferred from public records each week, and appending compiled fields to a client's file.

## Filter Notes

Seven of the eight level-1 niches are terminal. B2B Sales Intelligence Subscriptions is not: writing its contested sentence produced two sentences with different winners. Sales development teams judge a platform on whether dials connect and emails land on the named person this week — a reachability contest on perishable person records, measured in the dialer. Marketing and revenue operations judge it on whether the vendor's universe of accounts is complete, de-duplicated and correctly sized for targeting and territory design — a coverage-and-correctness contest on company records, measured by a match test against the CRM. The buyers sit in the same company and often sign one contract, but they leave for different competitors, so the niche decomposes into **Direct-Dial & Email Contact Data** and **Market Universe & Firmographic Counts**. Account hierarchy inside the buyer's own CRM is a separate contest already covered under [[niches/crm-platforms/account-data-hygiene/profile|CRM Platforms: Account Data Hygiene]].

Three terminal calls were close and are recorded honestly. **Business Location Data Licensing** borders the listings-management work profiled as [[niches/marketing-agencies-smb/local-listings-reputation-data/profile|🔍 Local Listings & Reputation Data]]; it is kept here because the compiler's contest — being the licensee's freshest reference file, above all on closures — is about compiling the place universe, not syndicating one business's details. **Trade, Professional & Membership Directories** mixes library reference directories, industrial supplier directories and association rosters; one contest governs all of them, getting listees to confirm their own entries each edition, so it stays whole. **Data Append & File Enhancement** borders merge/purge and hygiene for mailings under [[niches/direct-mail-agencies/mail-file-data-processing/profile|Direct Mail Agencies: Mail File Data Processing]]; the contests differ (a compiled field matched correctly versus a clean mail file by the list due date), so the boundary holds.

Four candidates were considered and rejected. **Consumer compiled mailing lists** as a standalone niche: its contest splits across trigger lists, append and the response modelling that belongs to the data cooperatives under [[industries/direct-mail-agencies|Direct Mail Agencies]], and what remains — the cheapest names per thousand — is a generic price contest that fails the filter. **People-search directories** sell consumer lookups whose contest is subscription conversion at the edge of the Fair Credit Reporting Act; they are logged as a Pass 2 pocket below. **Online review and local search directories** compete with general search engines for consumers' clicks, an advertising and marketplace contest rather than a compilation one. **Health plan provider directories** carry a regulator's clock but the records belong to the plans that commission the work, so they are logged as a Pass 2 pocket rather than built as a niche.

## Pass 2 — Insight-Layer Discovery

Second, orthogonal sweep. This industry is itself a data business, so the sweep looks at the research functions inside and beside the compilers as well as above them — aggregator/rollup, payer & intermediary, data & benchmark vendors, association research arms, specialist advisory & valuation, regulatory bodies, and suppliers selling into the industry — for a dedicated insight function of 10+ people whose analytical output is the billable deliverable. Verification callers, industry coders and compilation clerks are production headcount and do not count towards the gate. The operators fail it: 1,375 private establishments employed 14,671 people in 2024, and at the national compilers most of the headcount is verification production. Full method in `_direction.md`.

## Why These Pockets

No new pocket qualifies, and that is the finding. Compiled-record businesses are not excluded by nature: elsewhere in the vault they qualify when an outside clock governs the record and a fragmented market buys it — [[niches/environmental-consultants/environmental-records-database-providers/profile|🔍 Environmental Records Database Providers]] (58) because the due-diligence report gates a property closing, [[niches/contract-manufacturing/electronic-component-data-providers/profile|🔍 Electronic Component Data Providers]] (56) because end-of-life notices and regulatory declarations stop shipments, and in this code's own territory the commercial credit bureaus and drug compendia (both 56). The pockets swept here fall short on the same two questions. No third party sets a deadline on their work — contact records decay, intent scores refresh weekly, rankings follow the publisher's own cycle — so Q4 sits at 2 for most of them; and each is a market of a handful of vendors, so Q5 sits at 2–3. The compilers' own teams fail a step earlier, because most of their headcount verifies and codes records, which is production, and the small data science teams that remain produce a field inside the record rather than an invoiced analysis.

The strongest near-miss is the legal directories (44). Chambers alone runs 320+ researchers through 350,000+ interviews and surveys a year — a labour-scaled, repeatable research operation on a confidential corpus no one else holds. It misses on three counts: the rankings are read free and firms pay for promotion around them (Q1 3), every deadline in the cycle is the publisher's own (Q4 2), and the buyer market is a handful of guides (Q5 3). B2B intent cooperatives (42) have the cleanest insight-as-invoice shape and a unique pooled corpus, but their output scales with models rather than analysts and nothing external times it. Healthcare provider directory validation (41) is the one pocket with a regulator's clock on a directory — the No Surprises Act's 90-day verification cycle — and it carries the vault's most common kill switch: the rosters being validated belong to the health plans that commission the work. The regulators fail the gate outright: summed across agencies they look like a function, but no single agency has ten people researching data brokers, and nothing they produce is sold.

For an insight engine, this industry is a supplier of raw material and a seller of verification labour, not a buyer of analysis. The commercial opening it does offer is operational and sits in Pass 1: the verification ledger, accuracy measurement and call triage described in the high-impact note, sold to the national and mid-size compilers whose products would become measurably better.

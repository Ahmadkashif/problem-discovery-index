# Self-Verification the Listee Actually Completes

**Niche:** [[niches/directory-and-list-publishers/trade-and-professional-directories/profile|Trade, Professional & Membership Directories]]
**Industry:** [[industries/directory-and-list-publishers|Directory & List Publishers]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Directory update questionnaires ask busy organisations to retype what the publisher already knows, so most are never returned.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #change-point-detection #evaluation-metrics #automation #workflow-orchestration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get the listed organisations to confirm their own entries every edition cycle at near-zero cost, and whoever publishes the highest share of entries verified this edition keeps the library and industry subscribers.

## The Problem
A reference directory editor sends thousands of update requests per edition. Many go unanswered; many that return say "no changes" when the website shows a new address and a different chief executive. The editorial team chases the most important entries by phone and carries the rest forward.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The publishers are small, their budgets are shrinking with library spend, and generic survey vendors have no reason to model a directory's entry structure or edition cycle.

## What to Build
A pre-filled verification flow: before each edition, crawl each listee's website, filings and news, propose the specific changes the evidence suggests ("your website lists a new headquarters address — confirm?"), and send a one-click confirm-or-correct request to a verified contact. Entries that change without a response are flagged for an editor, with the evidence attached, and every entry carries a "verified by the organisation on" date that the published product displays.

## Target Customer
Publishers and editorial directors of trade, reference, government-contact and association directories.

## Impact If Built
More entries confirmed per edition at lower cost, and a verified-date the publisher can sell as the quality measure buyers lack.

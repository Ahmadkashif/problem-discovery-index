# Trade, Professional & Membership Directories

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/directory-and-list-publishers|Directory & List Publishers]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get the listed organisations to confirm their own entries every edition cycle at near-zero cost, and whoever publishes the highest share of entries verified this edition keeps the library and industry subscribers.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$700M US subscriptions, editions and listing fees for trade, reference, professional, government-contact and membership directories (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~9% of the ~$8B US buyer spend on directories, lists and compiled business data, including directory advertising (estimate; see overview)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — compiled by mailed or emailed update questionnaires, phone follow-up and editors' spreadsheets, even where the product is now a database
**Target Buyer:** Publisher or editorial director of the directory; buyers are librarians, procurement teams and industry sales staff
**Automation Potential:** Medium — update capture and change detection automate; editorial selection and verification of authority stay human

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
These are directories compiled from the listees themselves: industrial supplier directories (Thomasnet, bought by Xometry for $300M in 2021, reports 500,000+ sellers; its print ancestor stopped in 2006), state manufacturer directories, government contact directories, association and alumni directories, and reference directories sold to libraries by publishers such as Grey House and Gale. The record is maintained by an annual or edition-cycle update: the publisher sends each organisation its current entry and asks for corrections. Response rates decide quality, and an entry that is not returned is often carried forward unchanged. The product's value to a library or a buyer is that an entry was confirmed recently by the organisation it describes.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Survey and form tools, email campaigns, phone follow-up by small editorial teams, and content management for the published database. Gaps: update requests arrive blank or generic rather than pre-filled with what the publisher already suspects has changed; nobody tracks how many editions an entry has gone unverified; and respondent authority (is this person entitled to change the entry?) is not checked.

# Engagement Quality From Online Community Research

**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to measure whether a developer community is working, when member count and message volume rise as it fills with unanswered questions — and whoever measures it properly takes the account.

## The Problem
There is a substantial research literature on what makes online communities survive: newcomer retention, the experience of a first contribution, reciprocity, the concentration of contribution among few members, moderation load, and the dynamics by which communities collapse. The findings are consistent and published. Developer community management is largely practised without reference to any of it, using metrics that the same literature identifies as uninformative.

## What Already Exists
Newcomer socialisation and retention findings; contribution concentration measurement; reciprocity and help-network analysis; community lifecycle and collapse dynamics; and validated engagement quality measures.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a community that is also a commercial channel and a support surface. It requires: (1) a community operated by a vendor whose product is the subject, so the incentive structure differs from a volunteer community and the staff-to-member answer ratio is itself a health signal — this is the substantive difference; (2) members whose primary goal is solving a technical problem rather than belonging; (3) a commercial outcome the community is expected to serve; (4) platforms whose analytics are built for engagement rather than for help; and (5) a support function whose load the community absorbs.

## Target Customer
Developer relations and community functions, community platform vendors, developer platform providers, and community analytics vendors.

## Impact If Solved
Community research established what makes communities survive and which metrics are uninformative. A vendor-operated community that is also a support surface, serving people with a technical problem, is what the measures have to be adapted to.

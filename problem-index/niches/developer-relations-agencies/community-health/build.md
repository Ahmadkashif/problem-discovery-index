# Whether the Community Is Working

**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** The metrics rise whether the community is thriving or dying, which makes them worse than none.
**Tags:** #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #survival-analysis #data-integration #confidence-intervals #graph-theory #automation #k-means-clustering
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to measure whether a developer community is working, when member count and message volume rise as it fills with unanswered questions — and whoever measures it properly takes the account.

## The Problem
Member count rises as people join and never falls as they stop participating. Message volume rises when questions go unanswered and get repeated. Both numbers go up while a community degrades from a place where developers help each other into a queue of unanswered questions that new arrivals read before deciding not to stay. The reporting says the community is growing and the community is dying, and the data to tell the difference is in the same platform.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Community platforms report the metrics that are easy to count and flattering to report. Nobody has defined what a healthy developer community looks like in measurable terms. The vanity metrics are what leadership asks for. And the deterioration is gradual.

## What to Build
Measure whether people get help and come back. Measure the proportion of questions that receive a useful answer, which is the core and is the single number that describes whether a community is functioning. Measure time to first response, since a question answered in an hour and one answered in a week are different communities. Track whether members return after their first question, which is the retention signal that member count conceals. Measure member-to-member help rather than staff responses, because a community where only employees answer is a support desk. Identify and support the contributors who answer, as a small number of people carry most communities and their departure is the failure mode. Track unanswered questions as a backlog with an owner rather than as message volume. Distinguish newcomers from regulars in every metric, since their experiences differ completely and the average hides it. Report the health metrics rather than the size metrics, which is the change that makes the rest matter. Detect the deterioration early, when it is a rising answer time rather than an empty channel. And benchmark against comparable communities where data allows, so a number has meaning.

## Target Customer
Developer relations agencies and in-house community functions, community platform vendors, developer platform providers, and community analytics vendors.

## Impact If Built
Member count and message volume rise whether a community is thriving or dying, which is worse than reporting nothing. Answer rate, response time and return rate describe whether people are actually being helped.

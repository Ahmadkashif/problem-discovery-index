# Incrementality From Marketing Measurement

**Niche:** [[niches/developer-relations-agencies/adoption-attribution/profile|Adoption Attribution]]
**Industry:** [[industries/developer-relations-agencies|Developer Relations Agencies]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Marketing measures untrackable channels with geographic holdouts and mix models, and developer relations counts talks.
**Tags:** #causal-inference #hypothesis-testing #confidence-intervals #evaluation-metrics #revenue-impact #time-series-forecasting #descriptive-statistics #data-integration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to show that advocacy drove adoption, when the moment of influence carries no identifier and the conversion arrives months later — and whoever demonstrates it takes the account.

## The Problem
Marketing has long had channels it cannot track to an individual — television, outdoor, sponsorship, brand — and measures them anyway, with geographic holdouts, media mix models and incrementality tests. The methods exist precisely because the absence of a click identifier is not an excuse for measuring nothing. Developer relations has the same structure, describes its measurement problem as unique, and reports activity.

## What Already Exists
Geographic and audience holdout designs; media mix modelling for untrackable channels; incrementality testing; long-lag effect modelling; and reporting effects with uncertainty rather than exact attribution.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to small volumes and a long, diffuse influence path. It requires: (1) far smaller numbers than consumer marketing — a conference reaches hundreds, not millions — so statistical power is the binding constraint and designs must pool across events and periods, which is the substantive difference; (2) an influence path of months with several touches, none identifiable; (3) an audience that is professionally sceptical of being tracked, making some standard techniques inappropriate as well as impractical; (4) adoption that may be by an individual developer or by an enterprise that individual works for; and (5) programmes that are frequently too small to hold out without visibly abandoning a community.

## Target Customer
Developer relations agencies and in-house functions, marketing leadership, developer platform vendors, and marketing measurement providers.

## Impact If Solved
Marketing measures untrackable channels with holdouts and mix models rather than declaring them unmeasurable. Audiences of hundreds rather than millions is what makes statistical power the binding constraint here.

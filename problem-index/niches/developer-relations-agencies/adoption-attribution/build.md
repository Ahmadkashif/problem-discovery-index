# A Claim That Survives a Budget Review

**Niche:** [[niches/developer-relations-agencies/adoption-attribution/profile|Adoption Attribution]]
**Industry:** [[industries/developer-relations-agencies|Developer Relations Agencies]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A function whose central claim is almost certainly true is cut first because nobody can show it.
**Tags:** #causal-inference #hypothesis-testing #confidence-intervals #evaluation-metrics #revenue-impact #descriptive-statistics #data-integration #compliance
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to show that advocacy drove adoption, when the moment of influence carries no identifier and the conversion arrives months later — and whoever demonstrates it takes the account.

## The Problem
A developer hears a tool explained credibly, tries it in a workshop, gets helped in a community, and adopts it months later through a self-serve signup or as a name on a deal that sales sourced. None of those moments carries an identifier and the conversion cannot be traced back. So the function reports inputs — talks, articles, members, stars — everyone involved knows they are inputs, and the budget conversation each year is conducted on faith.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The individual-level join is genuinely hard across pseudonymous developer identities and raises privacy questions the field is right to be careful about. The aggregate alternative sacrifices the precision people expect from marketing measurement. Nobody has pushed the aggregate version as the answer. And the activity metrics are easy to produce.

## What to Build
Measure at the level where the evidence is obtainable and defensible. Design cohort and geographic comparisons — adoption in markets, segments or periods with and without advocacy activity — which is the core and is the version that survives both a budget review and a privacy review. Run deliberate holdouts where the programme can be varied, since an experiment settles what a decade of argument has not. Measure exposure at the aggregate rather than tracking individuals, which is both more defensible and sufficient for the question being asked. Model the delay between exposure and adoption explicitly, because a measurement window shorter than the lag will show nothing regardless of the truth. Use self-reported attribution at signup, which is imperfect and is the only direct signal available and is frequently not asked for. Separate the advocacy effect from the product and market effects it coincides with. Report an effect with an interval rather than a number, which is honest and is what a sceptical reviewer needs. Accumulate across programmes and clients so the evidence base grows beyond one company's anecdote. Publish the method, since the field's problem is partly that no credible method exists to adopt. And stop reporting activity as though it were outcome, which is the change that makes the rest necessary.

## Target Customer
Developer relations agencies and in-house functions, marketing and product leadership, developer platform vendors, and measurement providers.

## Impact If Built
A function whose claim is probably true is cut first because it reports inputs everyone knows are inputs. Cohort and holdout measurement at the aggregate is the version that survives both the budget review and the privacy review.

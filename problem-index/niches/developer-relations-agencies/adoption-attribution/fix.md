# Reporting Talks Given

**Niche:** [[niches/developer-relations-agencies/adoption-attribution/profile|Adoption Attribution]]
**Industry:** [[industries/developer-relations-agencies|Developer Relations Agencies]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The quarterly report says forty talks, twelve articles and eight thousand community members, and the next slide asks for budget.
**Tags:** #quick-win #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #confidence-intervals #revenue-impact #causal-inference #data-integration #hypothesis-testing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to show that advocacy drove adoption, when the moment of influence carries no identifier and the conversion arrives months later — and whoever demonstrates it takes the account.

## The Problem
The DevRel report is a list of activities. It demonstrates effort rather than effect, and everyone in the room knows it, which makes the budget conversation an exercise in trust rather than evidence. The team is not being dishonest — they genuinely cannot trace the influence — but activity reporting actively harms them, because it invites the comparison with functions that can show a number and offers nothing to defend.

## Why It's Still Broken
Activity is what can be counted — a function that reports what it can measure rather than what matters will report activity indefinitely, because the alternative requires admitting the outcome is unknown. No method is agreed. The reporting cadence is quarterly. And a weak number feels worse than no number.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Add whatever outcome evidence exists, however imperfect, and label it honestly. Ask new users where they heard about the product at signup, which is the fix and is a single field that most products do not have. Report the self-reported attribution alongside the activity, with its limitations stated. Compare adoption in regions and segments where the programme is active against those where it is not, which is a chart from data already held. Track the lag between a programme in a market and adoption there, which frequently makes the pattern visible. Report the qualitative evidence deliberately — named accounts that cite advocacy, community members who became customers — rather than leaving it anecdotal. Separate the activity from the outcome in the report so the two are not conflated. State what the function cannot currently show and what it would take, which is more persuasive than a weak claim. Run one deliberate comparison, since a single experiment is worth years of argument. Agree with leadership in advance what evidence would justify the budget, which is the conversation nobody has. And stop leading with activity, which is what invites the comparison the function loses.

## Who Feels the Pain
DevRel teams cut first in every downturn; leaders who believe in the function and cannot defend it; advocates whose work is genuinely effective and unprovable; and the companies that cut a function that was working.

## Impact If Fixed
A function that reports what it can measure rather than what matters will report activity indefinitely, and activity invites a comparison it loses. A signup question and a regional comparison are the smallest honest outcome evidence available.

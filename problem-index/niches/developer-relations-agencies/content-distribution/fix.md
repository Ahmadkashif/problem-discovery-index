# The Talk Nobody Recorded

**Niche:** [[niches/developer-relations-agencies/content-distribution/profile|Content Distribution & Syndication]]
**Industry:** [[industries/developer-relations-agencies|Developer Relations Agencies]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The best explanation the company has ever produced was given once, to eighty people, and no recording exists.
**Tags:** #quick-win #automation #workflow-orchestration #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #data-integration #worker-facing #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get one piece of advocacy work in front of the developers who would want it, across a dozen surfaces, without doing it by hand each time.

## The Problem
Advocacy produces excellent explanatory material that exists only as a live performance. A talk that explains a difficult concept better than any documentation page is given to a room, the conference does not record it or records it badly, and the explanation is gone. The advocate moves to the next talk. The company's best explanation of its own product is not written down anywhere.

## Why It's Still Broken
Capture is the event's responsibility rather than the programme's — a recording that depends on a conference's audiovisual arrangements will exist sometimes, and nobody treats the material as the company's asset to secure. Writing it up is a separate task. The advocate has moved on. And nobody notices the loss.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Capture it yourself and write it down while it is fresh. Record every talk independently rather than relying on the event, which is the fix and needs a phone and a microphone. Write the talk up as a post or documentation page while it is fresh, which is far easier immediately after than months later. Publish the recording promptly rather than waiting for the conference's version. Treat the talk's core explanation as documentation material, since an explanation that works live usually works written. Reuse the talk at other events rather than writing a new one, which is normal practice everywhere else and oddly rare here. Keep the slides and the sample as maintained artefacts rather than event ephemera. Capture the questions the audience asked, which is free research about what people do not understand. Build the write-up into the talk's preparation so it is a deliverable rather than a follow-up. Measure how many talks produced a durable artefact, which is usually a small fraction. And treat a talk with no artefact as unfinished work rather than a completed event.

## Who Feels the Pain
Developers who would have understood if they had seen it; advocates whose best work evaporates; documentation teams who could have used the explanation; and the programme, producing excellent material with a one-day shelf life.

## Impact If Fixed
A recording that depends on the event's arrangements will exist sometimes, and nobody treats the material as the company's asset. A phone and a write-up while it is fresh turns a performance into a durable artefact.

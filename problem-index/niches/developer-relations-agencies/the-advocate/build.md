# A Role Somebody Can Do for Five Years

**Niche:** [[niches/developer-relations-agencies/the-advocate/profile|The Developer Advocate]]
**Industry:** [[industries/developer-relations-agencies|Developer Relations Agencies]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** The role requires constant travel, public performance and permanent availability, and nobody has designed for that.
**Tags:** #worker-facing #workflow-orchestration #evaluation-metrics #automation #descriptive-statistics #data-integration #tacit-knowledge-ml #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to make a role built on constant travel, public performance and an always-on presence sustainable — and whoever does takes the account.

## The Problem
An advocate travels to conferences, speaks publicly, maintains a personal presence developers trust, answers questions at all hours in several timezones, and produces content continuously. Each element is reasonable and the combination is not sustainable for long. The function loses experienced people to burnout at a rate everyone acknowledges and nobody measures, and each departure takes a community relationship with it.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The role grew organically and was never designed. The always-on presence is part of the credibility, so limiting it feels like limiting the value. Travel is how the job has always been done. And the advocate's own ambition drives the load as much as the employer's expectations.

## What to Build
Manage the load and get more from each piece of work. Track and cap travel and speaking load as a managed quantity rather than as whatever fits in a calendar, which is the core and is the single change that most affects how long someone lasts. Set explicit expectations about availability rather than leaving an implicit always-on norm, which is what erodes people quietly. Get leverage from each piece of work — a talk becomes a post, a video, a workshop, a sample — so the output is not linear in the advocate's presence. Build community relationships as team relationships rather than individual ones where possible, which addresses the succession problem without pretending credibility is transferable. Recognise that the reputation is partly the advocate's own, and structure the relationship accordingly rather than assuming the employer owns it. Support the public exposure, since a public-facing technical role attracts hostility that nobody has resourced. Rotate the travel and the event load across the team rather than concentrating it on whoever is best at it. Measure the load and report it, because an unmeasured burden is never managed. Provide a path that does not require the same intensity indefinitely. And design the role deliberately rather than letting it be whatever the calendar allows.

## Target Customer
Developer relations agencies and in-house functions, DevRel leadership, developer advocates themselves, and professional communities in the field.

## Impact If Built
The role burns people out reliably and each departure takes a community relationship with it, and nothing measures the load. Capped travel, explicit availability and leverage from each piece of work is what makes a five-year career possible.

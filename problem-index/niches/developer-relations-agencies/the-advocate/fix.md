# The Community Left With the Advocate

**Niche:** [[niches/developer-relations-agencies/the-advocate/profile|The Developer Advocate]]
**Industry:** [[industries/developer-relations-agencies|Developer Relations Agencies]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The advocate resigned and the community's relationship with the company went with them.
**Tags:** #worker-facing #quick-win #workflow-orchestration #tacit-knowledge-ml #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #data-integration #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to make a role built on constant travel, public performance and an always-on presence sustainable — and whoever does takes the account.

## The Problem
Advocacy relationships are personal, so when an advocate leaves, the community's trust goes with them rather than staying with the company. The successor starts from nothing, the community's regulars follow the departed advocate to their next role, and years of relationship-building evaporates in a fortnight. Everyone knows this happens and it is treated as an inherent property of the function rather than as a succession failure.

## Why It's Still Broken
The relationship was only ever with one person — a community whose only strong tie to the company runs through one individual will lose that tie when the individual does, and nothing in how the function operates builds any other tie. Credibility feels non-transferable. Introducing a second person reads as dilution. And departures are not planned for.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Build more than one tie, deliberately and early. Involve more than one person in every significant community relationship rather than one owner per community, which is the fix and is a staffing pattern rather than a system. Introduce a successor before a departure where notice permits, which is the standard practice everywhere else and is rarely done here. Make the company's own presence credible rather than relying entirely on individuals, which is a harder and more durable investment. Document the relationships, the context and the commitments so a successor inherits knowledge rather than a channel. Recognise the community's regulars directly so their tie is to the community rather than to one advocate. Leave on good terms and say so publicly, since a departing advocate who speaks well of the company retains some of the relationship for it. Accept that some of the relationship is genuinely theirs and plan accordingly rather than resenting it. Track which communities depend on a single person, which is a risk register nobody keeps. Rotate involvement periodically rather than at a departure. And treat a departure as a foreseeable event rather than a surprise.

## Who Feels the Pain
Companies losing a community they invested years in; successors starting from nothing; community members who lose their point of contact; and the advocate, whose departure is treated as a betrayal.

## Impact If Fixed
A community whose only strong tie to the company runs through one person will lose that tie when the person goes, and nothing builds another. Two people on every significant relationship is a staffing pattern, not a system.

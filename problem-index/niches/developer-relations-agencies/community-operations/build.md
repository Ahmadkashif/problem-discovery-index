# The Mechanical Half, Automated

**Industry:** [[industries/developer-relations-agencies|Developer Relations Agencies]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** The relationship work is the point and the mechanical work is what fills the day.

**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to run the mechanical half of a developer community — onboarding, moderation, routing, reporting — without a person doing it by hand, and whoever automates that takes the account.

## The Problem
Community work divides into relationship work, which requires a person, and operational work, which does not: welcoming newcomers, pointing people at the right channel, enforcing conduct, tidying, tagging, recognising contributors, compiling reports. The operational half scales with the community's size and is done by hand, so a growing community consumes its staff's capacity before the relationship work — the actual value — gets any.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Community platforms provide primitives and leave the operations to bots somebody writes. Each community's conventions differ. Automation is feared as making a community feel corporate. And the work is invisible, so its cost is never totalled.

## What to Build
Automate the operations and protect the character. Onboard newcomers with a structured path that orients them and gets them a first successful interaction, which is the core — the first experience determines retention and it is currently improvised. Support moderation at volume with detection and triage rather than manual reading, keeping human judgement on the decisions. Route questions and topics to the right channel or person automatically. Organise and tag content so the community's own history is searchable, which is where most communities lose their accumulated value. Recognise contributors automatically, since the people who answer are the community's infrastructure and are usually unacknowledged. Generate the reporting rather than compiling it, which is a meaningful recurring cost. Surface community content into the documentation where it answers a recurring question, which is a loop nobody closes. Keep the tone human, because automation that makes a community feel like a product surface defeats the purpose. Measure the operational load so the staffing can be argued for. And leave the relationship work entirely to people, which is the division that makes automation acceptable here.

## Target Customer
Developer relations and community functions, community platform vendors, developer platform providers, and community tooling developers.

## Impact If Built
The operational half scales with the community and the relationship half is the value, so a growing community consumes its own staff. Automating onboarding, moderation and reporting returns the capacity to the work that requires a person.

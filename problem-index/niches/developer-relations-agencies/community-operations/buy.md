# Moderation Tooling From Large Platforms

**Industry:** [[industries/developer-relations-agencies|Developer Relations Agencies]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Large platforms built moderation and onboarding tooling at scale, and small developer communities do it by hand.

**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to run the mechanical half of a developer community — onboarding, moderation, routing, reporting — without a person doing it by hand, and whoever automates that takes the account.

## The Problem
Large online platforms built extensive community operations tooling: automated moderation with human review, newcomer onboarding flows tested for retention, content organisation and routing, contributor recognition systems, and health reporting. The techniques are documented and the tooling patterns are well known. Small developer communities — where the same work occurs at a scale one person cannot cover — use the platform's defaults and a bot somebody wrote.

## What Already Exists
Automated moderation with escalation to humans; onboarding flows optimised for newcomer retention; automatic content routing and organisation; contributor recognition mechanics; and community health reporting.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a small, technical, vendor-operated community where tone is the asset. It requires: (1) a community small enough that heavy-handed automation is immediately noticeable and reads as corporate, which is exactly the perception the function exists to avoid — this is the substantive difference; (2) content that is technical questions and code rather than social posts, so routing and organisation need domain understanding; (3) conduct enforcement in a professional community where the participants know each other; (4) operation by a vendor whose product is the subject, changing the moderation dynamic; and (5) staff of one or two rather than a moderation function.

## Target Customer
Developer relations and community functions, community platform vendors, developer platform providers, and community tooling developers.

## Impact If Solved
Large platforms built moderation, onboarding and recognition tooling and documented the patterns. A small technical community where automation immediately reads as corporate is what makes the adaptation about restraint as much as capability.

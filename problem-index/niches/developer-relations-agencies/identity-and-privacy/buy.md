## The Problem
Privacy engineering established how to build systems that use personal data legitimately: purpose limitation, data minimisation, consent that is specific and revocable, aggregation before analysis, retention limits, and transparency about what is collected. The practice is mature, regulated in many jurisdictions, and has a vendor ecosystem. Developer relations measurement has neither the practice nor a stated position, and consequently does nothing rather than doing it properly.

## What Already Exists
Purpose limitation and minimisation frameworks; specific and revocable consent mechanisms; aggregation and differential privacy techniques; retention and access controls; and transparency and subject access practice.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to an audience that is unusually attentive to tracking and whose goodwill is the asset. It requires: (1) a population that is professionally sceptical of data collection and whose trust is the function's entire working capital, so the standard must exceed legal compliance rather than meet it — this is the substantive difference; (2) deliberately separate pseudonymous identities across surfaces, where linking defeats a choice the person made; (3) data spread across a company's systems, a community platform and event registration systems with different controllers; (4) a purpose that is the function's own measurement rather than a service to the user, which is the weakest possible consent basis; and (5) a community that will discuss any perceived overreach publicly and immediately.

## Target Customer
Developer relations and data leadership, privacy and legal functions, community platform vendors, and privacy engineering providers.

## Impact If Solved
Privacy engineering made consent, minimisation and purpose limitation into design practice with a vendor ecosystem. An audience whose trust is the function's working capital is why the standard here must exceed compliance rather than meet it.

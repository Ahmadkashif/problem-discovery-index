# Nobody Decided What Is Acceptable

**Niche:** [[niches/developer-relations-agencies/identity-and-privacy/profile|Identity Linking & Privacy Design]]
**Industry:** [[industries/developer-relations-agencies|Developer Relations Agencies]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Half the team assumes linking community handles to accounts is obviously fine and half assumes it is obviously not, and nobody has written anything down.
**Tags:** #quick-win #compliance #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #data-integration #workflow-orchestration #confidence-intervals #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to connect the same pseudonymous developer across a conference, a community, a piece of content and a signup — and whoever does that acceptably takes the account.

## The Problem
Organisations have no stated position on what developer data may be linked for measurement. Individuals make ad hoc decisions: someone exports community members and matches them against a customer list; someone else refuses to. Neither is following a policy because none exists. The organisation is simultaneously doing some of this and unable to do it systematically, which is the worst of both — the risk without the benefit.

## Why It's Still Broken
Nobody owns the question — a decision that sits between marketing, data, legal and community is made by whoever acts first, and nobody has been asked to write a position. The question feels difficult. Nobody has been challenged on it. And the ad hoc practice is invisible.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Write the position down before building anything, and audit what is already happening. Establish and publish an internal position on what developer data may be linked and for what purpose, which is the fix and is a document that costs a few meetings. Audit what linking is already happening informally, which is usually more than leadership expects. Involve legal and the community team, not only marketing, since the community relationship is what is at risk. State the position publicly where the community can see it, which is both right and a differentiator. Prefer opt-in association over inference in the policy, so the default is consent. Set retention and access limits for anything that is linked. Define what will not be linked, which is the part a policy driven by measurement appetite tends to omit. Review the position when the programme or the regulation changes. Give the community a route to ask what is held about them. And be prepared to conclude that some measurements are not worth making, which is a legitimate position and the one the field's instinct is pointing at.

## Who Feels the Pain
Developers whose separate identities were joined without their knowledge; teams acting inconsistently because no policy exists; legal functions discovering practice after the fact; and the community relationship, which is the function's only real asset.

## Impact If Fixed
A decision sitting between four functions is made by whoever acts first, and nobody has been asked to write a position. A stated policy, published where the community can see it, costs a few meetings and prevents the failure that matters.

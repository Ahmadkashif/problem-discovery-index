## The Problem
Marketing science built a rigorous toolkit for measuring untrackable activity: matched geographic experiments, synthetic control groups constructed from untreated regions, difference-in-differences designs, and statistical machinery for small numbers of large units. It exists because advertisers needed to measure television and outdoor without a click. Developer relations faces the same measurement structure and has not adopted any of it.

## What Already Exists
Matched market experiment design; synthetic control construction; difference-in-differences estimation; power analysis for small unit counts; and inference methods for few treated units.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to programmes whose units are conferences and communities rather than markets. It requires: (1) treatment units that are events, communities and content pieces rather than geographies, so the matching must be on programme-relevant characteristics with no established framework — this is the substantive difference; (2) outcome lags of months against a measurement window that leadership wants quarterly; (3) treated units in the tens rather than the hundreds, pushing the inference methods to their limits; (4) contamination, since a talk reaches an online audience outside any geography; and (5) a holdout that means visibly withdrawing from a community, which has costs a marketing holdout does not.

## Target Customer
Developer relations agencies and in-house functions, marketing leadership, developer platform vendors, and measurement consultancies.

## Impact If Solved
Marketing science built matched experiments and synthetic controls to measure what cannot be tracked. Treatment units that are events and communities, in the tens, with contamination across geography, is what the designs must be rebuilt for.

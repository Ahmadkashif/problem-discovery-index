# Nobody Held Anything Out

**Niche:** [[niches/developer-relations-agencies/cohort-measurement/profile|Cohort-Level Measurement]]
**Industry:** [[industries/developer-relations-agencies|Developer Relations Agencies]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The programme runs in every region for every segment, so there is nothing to compare it against.
**Tags:** #quick-win #hypothesis-testing #causal-inference #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #confidence-intervals #revenue-impact #workflow-orchestration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to establish an advocacy effect without identifying a single developer — and whoever designs those comparisons takes the account.

## The Problem
The reason nobody can measure developer relations is partly that nobody designed it to be measurable. Programmes run everywhere, for everyone, continuously. There is no region without a meetup, no segment without an advocate, no period without activity. Any comparison is confounded by everything else happening, and the absence of variation is a design choice nobody made deliberately and nobody has revisited.

## Why It's Still Broken
Coverage is the instinct — a programme designed to reach everyone leaves no group unreached, and therefore nothing to compare against, which is a measurement consequence nobody considered when designing for reach. Withholding activity feels like abandoning a community. Nobody has asked for variation. And the confounding is accepted as inherent.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Find or create some variation, which is usually easier than it sounds. Look for the variation that already exists — regions the programme reached later, segments it has not prioritised, periods when activity paused — which is the fix and needs no new decisions. Stagger new programme launches across regions rather than launching everywhere, which produces a comparison at no cost. Vary intensity rather than presence where withdrawal is unacceptable, since a difference in degree is measurable too. Plan the comparison before the programme rather than seeking one afterwards. Record precisely when and where each activity happened, which is frequently not kept and is the prerequisite for any of this. Use the natural experiments that occur — a cancelled event, an advocate's departure, a region temporarily unstaffed — which are informative and currently unexamined. Keep one segment lightly served deliberately, with the reasoning documented. Compare against an external baseline where no internal one exists. Report the comparison honestly including its confounds. And ask, before the next programme is designed, how anyone will know whether it worked.

## Who Feels the Pain
DevRel teams unable to defend a programme they believe in; leaders funding on faith; advocates whose effective work is unprovable; and the next budget review.

## Impact If Fixed
A programme designed to reach everyone leaves nothing to compare against, which is a measurement consequence nobody considered. Staggering a launch produces the comparison at no cost.

# Comparisons That Need Nobody's Identity

**Industry:** [[industries/developer-relations-agencies|Developer Relations Agencies]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** The effect is establishable at the aggregate and the field has been arguing about individual attribution for a decade.
**Tags:** #causal-inference #hypothesis-testing #confidence-intervals #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #time-series-forecasting #bayesian-inference #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to establish an advocacy effect without identifying a single developer — and whoever designs those comparisons takes the account.

## The Problem
The field's measurement debate is stuck on connecting an individual's exposure to their eventual adoption, which is hard and privacy-fraught. The aggregate question — does adoption rise where and when advocacy happens, relative to where and when it does not — is answerable with data both the company and the programme already hold, and almost nobody designs the comparison. The programme is run everywhere at once, so there is nothing to compare against.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Programmes are designed for coverage rather than for measurement, so no variation exists to exploit. Aggregate methods feel weak next to the precision marketing claims elsewhere. Small numbers make naive comparisons noisy, which discourages the first attempt. And nobody has built the designs for this specific setting.

## What to Build
Create the variation deliberately and analyse it properly. Design programmes with deliberate variation — regions, segments or periods with and without — which is the core and is what makes any comparison possible at all. Model the lag between activity and adoption explicitly, since a comparison over the wrong window will find nothing whatever the truth. Pool across events, regions and clients to gain the statistical power that any single programme lacks, which is the technique that makes small numbers workable. Use before-and-after designs with matched comparisons where a holdout is not acceptable. Report effects with intervals and be explicit about power, which is what makes a modest finding credible rather than dismissible. Include the qualitative evidence alongside rather than instead, since a named account that cites a workshop is real evidence. Measure the right outcome — trial, first meaningful use, retention — rather than signups alone. Accumulate a cross-client evidence base, which is an agency's unique position. Publish the designs so the field has something to adopt. And accept a wide interval, because a defensible range beats a precise fiction in the conversation this is for.

## Target Customer
Developer relations agencies and in-house functions, marketing and product leadership, developer platform vendors, and measurement providers.

## Impact If Built
The aggregate question is answerable with data both parties already hold, and programmes are run everywhere at once so nothing can be compared. Deliberate variation with pooled analysis is what turns a decade of argument into a finding.

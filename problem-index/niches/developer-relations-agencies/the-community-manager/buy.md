## The Problem
Support platforms solved the operational problem of many people asking questions: a queue with state, routing by skill, service expectations, suggested answers from a knowledge base, and escalation paths. The tooling is commodity and every support organisation runs on it. The community channel handles a comparable volume of the same product's questions with a chat client, because it is not officially support and therefore was never given any of it.

## What Already Exists
Queues with state tracking; skill-based routing; service level expectations and alerting; knowledge base suggestion at the point of answering; and defined escalation paths.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a conversational public channel that must not become a ticket system. It requires: (1) preserving the community's conversational character, since the reason developers ask there rather than in support is precisely that it is not a ticket system — this is the substantive difference and rules out most of the support interface; (2) questions interleaved with conversation, so detection rather than submission starts the process; (3) answers from community members as well as staff, which the queue must accommodate rather than override; (4) public visibility, so the handling is itself a signal to prospective users; and (5) no customer identity or entitlement, since many askers are not customers.

## Target Customer
Developer relations and community functions, community platform vendors, support operations, and community tooling providers.

## Impact If Solved
Support platforms made queues, routing and answer reuse commodity. Preserving a conversational public channel — the reason developers ask there at all — is what rules out simply installing them.

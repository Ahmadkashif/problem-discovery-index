# Remembering Which Questions Are Open

**Niche:** [[niches/developer-relations-agencies/the-community-manager/profile|The Community Manager]]
**Industry:** [[industries/developer-relations-agencies|Developer Relations Agencies]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Tracking what is unanswered in the community is a mental task performed continuously by one person.
**Tags:** #worker-facing #quick-win #automation #workflow-orchestration #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #data-integration #large-language-models
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to support the person running an unofficial support desk with no support tooling and no ticket queue — and whoever equips them takes the account.

## The Problem
Questions scroll past. The community manager holds in their head which are still open, which they have escalated, which are waiting on an engineer, and which they intended to come back to. The channel does not track any of it. A question missed is a person ignored, so the manager stays vigilant continuously, cannot be away without things falling through, and carries the load as constant low-level attention rather than as work anyone can see.

## Why It's Still Broken
The channel has no state — a conversation platform has no concept of an open item, so the only place the queue can exist is in a person's head. Support tooling does not fit. Nobody has counted the questions. And a missed question looks like nothing happened.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Get the queue out of the manager's head and into something shared. Use a lightweight mark — a reaction, a thread convention, a bot command — to flag a question as open and resolved, which is the fix and needs no platform change. Generate a daily list of open items from those marks, so the state is visible rather than remembered. Let anyone mark and resolve, which spreads the load and involves the community. Show how long each item has been open, which prioritises without judgement. Route persistent items to a named person rather than back into the channel. Cover the handover when the manager is away, which currently does not exist. Count the weekly question volume and resolution rate, which is the evidence that the role is understaffed. Reuse previous answers by pointing at them rather than retyping. Convert the recurring ones into documentation with a link to reuse. And treat the queue as a team artefact rather than one person's vigilance.

## Who Feels the Pain
Community managers who cannot switch off; developers whose question scrolled past; teams who lose the thread when the manager is away; and the company, whose most visible support surface depends on one person's memory.

## Impact If Fixed
A conversation platform has no concept of an open item, so the queue exists only in a person's head. A reaction convention and a daily digest moves it somewhere shared.

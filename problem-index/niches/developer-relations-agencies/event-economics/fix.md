# Sponsoring It Because We Sponsored It

**Niche:** [[niches/developer-relations-agencies/event-economics/profile|Event & Programme Economics]]
**Industry:** [[industries/developer-relations-agencies|Developer Relations Agencies]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The conference sponsorship renews annually and nobody has asked what last year's produced.
**Tags:** #quick-win #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #revenue-impact #confidence-intervals #data-integration #workflow-orchestration #causal-inference
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to decide which conferences, workshops and sponsorships are worth doing, when the spend is substantial and nobody has compared one against another — and whoever prices them takes the account.

## The Problem
Event sponsorships renew. The organiser sends the package, the relationship is good, the team went last year, and the decision is made without anyone asking what the previous year produced. Across a calendar this is a substantial budget allocated by inertia. Some of those events are genuinely valuable and some are not, and nothing in the process distinguishes them, so both continue indefinitely.

## Why It's Still Broken
There is no review step — a renewal that arrives as an invoice rather than as a decision will be paid, because nothing in the process asks the question. Outcomes were not recorded. The relationship makes declining awkward. And the alternative events are unknown because none were tried.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Make the renewal a decision with the previous year's evidence in front of it. Record what each event produced at the time — conversations, workshop attendance, follow-ups, community joins — which is the fix and is a form filled in on the flight home. Include the full cost with advocate time, which frequently changes the picture entirely. Review each renewal against that record rather than against the relationship. Try one new event a year in place of a renewal, which is how the comparison set grows. Compare the same event year on year, which is the cleanest comparison available. Ask the advocates who attended what they thought, which is qualitative evidence nobody records systematically. Follow up event contacts consistently so there is something to measure. Report the calendar as a portfolio with costs, which makes the allocation visible to whoever funds it. Decline gracefully where the evidence does not support renewal, which is a relationship skill rather than a measurement one. And write the decision down, so next year starts from a position rather than a habit.

## Who Feels the Pain
Programme leaders allocating a large budget by inertia; advocates travelling to events that do nothing; smaller events that would be better and are never tried; and the budget review, which finds an unexamined line.

## Impact If Fixed
A renewal that arrives as an invoice rather than as a decision will be paid, because nothing asks the question. A form filled in after each event turns next year's renewal into a choice.

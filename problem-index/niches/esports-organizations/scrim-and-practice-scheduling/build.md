# Practice Arranged by Something Other Than a Group Chat

**Niche:** [[niches/esports-organizations/scrim-and-practice-scheduling/profile|Scrim & Practice Scheduling]]
**Industry:** [[industries/esports-organizations|Esports Organizations]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** The core of competitive preparation is arranged by whichever coach replies to a message first.
**Tags:** #optimization-fundamentals #workflow-orchestration #automation #evaluation-metrics #data-integration #worker-facing #descriptive-statistics #k-nearest-neighbors
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get practice matches against the right opposition at the right time, across timezones, through a process that runs entirely in private group chats — and whoever systematises it takes the account.

## The Problem
Professional teams practise against other professional teams, and the arrangement of those sessions is entirely informal. A coach messages contacts, negotiates times across timezones, and takes whoever is available. The opposition is not matched to what the team needs to work on, the blocks collide with travel and content commitments, weaker teams struggle to get sessions at all, and nothing is recorded afterwards in a form that supports review.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Access to opposition is a competitive advantage that established teams have no interest in democratising. The market per title is small. Coaches regard the relationship network as part of the job. And no neutral party exists to run the matching.

## What to Build
Match on need and constraints, then keep the record. Match teams by declared practice objective and comparable strength rather than by who answered, which is the core — practising the wrong thing against the wrong opposition is a real and invisible cost of the current process. Resolve timezone and availability constraints automatically across both sides, since that negotiation is most of the coach's overhead. Detect collisions with travel, competition and sponsor obligations before confirming, as those collisions currently surface on the day. Record every session with opposition, objective and outcome, which turns practice into something reviewable. Attach review workflow so the footage and notes sit with the session. Support substitutes and partial rosters, which is the normal case and breaks informal arrangements. Handle the reciprocity norms of the scene rather than ignoring them, because a system that disrupts the social contract will not be adopted. Give lower-ranked teams a route to sessions, which is where the unmet demand is. Keep team identity and objectives private to the counterparty, since disclosure is the obvious objection. And run it per title with the scene's own conventions encoded, as these differ substantially.

## Target Customer
Esports organisations and coaching staff, leagues and title publishers, academy and tier-two teams, and scheduling platform vendors.

## Impact If Built
Practising the wrong thing against the wrong opposition is a real and invisible cost of arranging sessions by group chat. Matching on objective and strength with automatic constraint resolution recovers both the time and the practice quality.

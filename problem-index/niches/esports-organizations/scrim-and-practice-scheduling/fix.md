# The Scrim Block That Collided With the Flight

**Niche:** [[niches/esports-organizations/scrim-and-practice-scheduling/profile|Scrim & Practice Scheduling]]
**Industry:** [[industries/esports-organizations|Esports Organizations]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Practice was booked for Thursday afternoon, the team flies Thursday morning, and two people found out an hour before.
**Tags:** #quick-win #workflow-orchestration #automation #worker-facing #evaluation-metrics #data-integration #descriptive-statistics #compliance
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get practice matches against the right opposition at the right time, across timezones, through a process that runs entirely in private group chats — and whoever systematises it takes the account.

## The Problem
The team's schedule lives in several places: the coach arranges practice in a chat, the manager books travel in a spreadsheet, the content team schedules filming in a calendar, and sponsor commitments arrive by email. They collide constantly. Practice blocks are cancelled at short notice, players lose rest, the counterparty team is let down, and the reciprocity that governs access to good opposition erodes.

## Why It's Still Broken
There is no single schedule — commitments made in four different systems by four different people will collide, and the collision is discovered by whoever is closest to it on the day. Nobody owns the composite calendar. The coach arranges practice without seeing travel. And the cancellations are absorbed socially rather than tracked.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Build one calendar and check against it before committing anything. Create a single team calendar containing competition, travel, practice, content and sponsor commitments, which is the fix and is a shared calendar rather than a product. Require confirmation against that calendar before a practice block is agreed, since checking takes seconds and the cancellation costs a relationship. Protect rest and travel days explicitly rather than leaving them as whatever is left over. Alert on a collision when it is created rather than on the day. Give the counterparty team as much notice as possible when something must move, because the reciprocity norms are the actual currency here. Track cancellations by cause, as the pattern usually points at one recurring conflict. Include the content and sponsor obligations, which are the commonest surprise. Publish the calendar to players so they can plan their own lives. Assign one person to own it, which is the part that makes it work. And review it weekly as a standing item rather than reactively.

## Who Feels the Pain
Coaching staff losing practice they negotiated; players with no notice and no rest; counterparty teams let down repeatedly; and an organisation whose access to good opposition quietly degrades.

## Impact If Fixed
Commitments made in four different systems by four different people will collide, and the collision is found by whoever is closest to it on the day. One shared calendar checked before committing costs nothing and protects the reciprocity the practice schedule runs on.

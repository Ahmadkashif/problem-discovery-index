# A Career Built to End

**Niche:** [[niches/esports-organizations/the-player-career/profile|The Player Career]]
**Industry:** [[industries/esports-organizations|Esports Organizations]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A career that starts at seventeen and ends in the mid-twenties has no plan attached to it at either end.
**Tags:** #worker-facing #workflow-orchestration #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #compliance #revenue-impact #confidence-intervals #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to make a career that starts at seventeen and ends in the mid-twenties into something a person can survive and leave, and whoever builds that takes the account.

## The Problem
A professional player signs their first contract at seventeen or eighteen, often without independent advice, trains at an intensity that would concern any other sport, earns for perhaps five to eight years, and finishes in their mid-twenties with no qualification, no plan and frequently no savings strategy. In most titles there is no players' association. The organisation's interests and the player's diverge precisely at the points that matter most, and nobody represents the second.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The players are individually young and unorganised and cannot fund a service. The organisations have no incentive to build it. No association exists in most titles to act as the buyer. And the sector's contraction removed the money that might have paid for it.

## What to Build
Serve the player as the customer, starting with the record. Build a portable career record — matches, performance, earnings, contracts, audience — owned by the player rather than by the organisation, which is the core and is the asset that makes everything downstream possible. Benchmark contract terms against the market so a seventeen-year-old is not negotiating blind, since the information asymmetry at first signing is the single largest transfer of value in the sector. Provide plain-language contract review, as most players sign documents they have not understood. Support financial planning for a compressed earning window, which is a different problem from ordinary financial advice and nobody presents it that way. Monitor health for the specific risks of this career — repetitive strain, sleep, sedentary load, mental health under public scrutiny — because the practice schedules are genuinely extreme. Plan the transition from the first contract rather than from the last, which is the difference between a landing and a fall. Record the audience the player built, since that is portable value they usually do not realise they own. Connect to education and training that fits an unusual schedule. Aggregate anonymised terms across players to build the benchmark, which requires collective participation and is why an association matters. And make it free to the player, funded by leagues, sponsors or associations rather than by the people who have the least.

## Target Customer
Professional players, player associations where they exist, leagues with welfare obligations, and athlete career services providers.

## Impact If Built
The information asymmetry at first signing is the single largest transfer of value in the sector, and the player is seventeen. A portable career record with contract benchmarking is what makes the rest of the relationship negotiable.

# Sold Out in an Hour and Restocked in Six Weeks

**Niche:** [[niches/esports-organizations/merchandise-and-fan-commerce/profile|Merchandise & Fan Commerce]]
**Industry:** [[industries/esports-organizations|Esports Organizations]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The jersey sold out on the night of the win and came back long after anybody cared.
**Tags:** #quick-win #time-series-forecasting #descriptive-statistics #revenue-impact #evaluation-metrics #automation #confidence-intervals #workflow-orchestration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to convert a large, engaged, global fanbase into direct revenue when the merchandise operation is outsourced, generic and timed by nobody — and whoever converts it takes the account.

## The Problem
The predictable merchandise failure: a competitive result drives a surge of purchase intent, the available stock clears within hours, and the restock arrives six weeks later into an audience that has moved on. The revenue lost is not the stock that sold — it is the far larger quantity that would have sold in that window. Nobody forecast the surge, nobody pre-positioned inventory, and nobody captured the demand that could not be filled.

## Why It's Still Broken
Nobody captures unmet demand — a stockout that records nothing leaves the organisation believing it sold well, when what actually happened is that most of the demand was turned away invisibly. Restock lead times are set by an outsourced partner. Surges follow results nobody plans around. And the loss never appears in any report.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Capture the demand you cannot fill, and pre-position for the results you can foresee. Offer pre-order or waitlist the instant something sells out, which is the fix and converts an invisible loss into a recorded order. Pre-position inventory ahead of major competitive events, since the surge is predictable in timing even when the result is not. Keep core items on a print-on-demand path so they never fully stock out, which trades margin for availability at exactly the right moment. Record every stockout with its duration and traffic, as that is the number that makes the case for everything else. Forecast size distribution from past drops rather than guessing, because the wrong sizes are the commonest form of the same failure. Shorten restock lead times with the fulfilment partner, which is a negotiation rather than a project. Announce the restock date at the point of stockout so intent is retained. Cover the international audience in the pre-order, since that is where the unserved demand concentrates. Report lost revenue estimates alongside actual revenue. And plan drops around the competitive calendar rather than around whoever remembered.

## Who Feels the Pain
Organisations losing the largest revenue moment of their season; fans who wanted to buy and could not; commercial staff reporting a success that was a shortfall; and the cash position that needed it.

## Impact If Fixed
A stockout that records nothing leaves the organisation believing it sold well, when most of the demand was turned away invisibly. A waitlist at the moment of stockout converts that into recorded orders.

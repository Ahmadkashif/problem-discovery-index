# The Same Fan Counted Five Times

**Niche:** [[niches/esports-organizations/cross-surface-attention/profile|Cross-Surface Attention Aggregation]]
**Industry:** [[industries/esports-organizations|Esports Organizations]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The headline reach figure counts one person once per surface they visited, and everyone in the room knows it.
**Tags:** #quick-win #descriptive-statistics #data-integration #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #automation #revenue-impact #probability-distributions
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn a broadcast, a dozen streams, several short-form platforms and a set of social accounts into one comparable unit of attention — and whoever defines that unit takes the account.

## The Problem
The standard reach claim in an esports deck is a sum across surfaces. The audience overlaps heavily — the committed fan watches the match, follows two players' streams, sees the clips and follows the social accounts — so the figure overstates unique reach by a large and unknown multiple. Sponsors apply an arbitrary discount because they cannot compute the real number either, and the organisation is penalised for an inflation it did not benefit from.

## Why It's Still Broken
Nobody computes overlap — a total presented without an overlap estimate cannot be interpreted by either party, so the buyer substitutes a guess and the guess is always unfavourable. Deduplication is assumed to need identity data. The inflated number is the sector's norm. And nobody wants to publish a smaller figure unilaterally.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Estimate the overlap approximately and say so. Publish a stated overlap assumption alongside the total rather than leaving it implicit, which is the fix and immediately outperforms a number the buyer discounts arbitrarily. Estimate overlap from the signals available — concurrent engagement, geography, timing, follower intersections where exposed — since a rough estimate beats no estimate decisively here. Report unique reach as a range rather than a point, which is honest and still usable. Show per-surface figures alongside the composite so the buyer can do their own arithmetic. Compare against a sponsor's own audience data where they will share it, as that converts an argument into a joint exercise. Use a consistent method across every deck, which matters more than the method's precision. Benchmark the overlap against comparable organisations where data exists. Present the smaller honest number as a credibility position rather than apologising for it. Track it over time, since the trend is unaffected by the level. And state the method in the deck, which is what stops the reflexive discount.

## Who Feels the Pain
Partnership teams discounted on arrival; sponsors unable to compare offers; organisations penalised for a sector-wide habit; and the credibility of every number in the deck.

## Impact If Fixed
A total presented without an overlap estimate cannot be interpreted by either party, so the buyer substitutes a guess and the guess is always unfavourable. A stated assumption and a range beat an inflated point figure in every negotiation.

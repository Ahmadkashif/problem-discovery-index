# The Deliverables Nobody Tracked Until Renewal

**Niche:** [[niches/esports-organizations/sponsorship-sales-and-packaging/profile|Sponsorship Sales & Packaging]]
**Industry:** [[industries/esports-organizations|Esports Organizations]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The sponsor arrived at the renewal meeting with a list of things that were contracted and never delivered, and nobody on the organisation's side had checked.
**Tags:** #quick-win #workflow-orchestration #automation #compliance #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #revenue-impact #data-integration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to package and price inventory whose value nobody can establish, against sponsors who have become far more demanding since the contraction — and whoever can price it credibly takes the account.

## The Problem
A sponsorship contract lists dozens of commitments: social posts per month, content pieces per quarter, appearances, broadcast mentions, jersey placement. Delivery is handled by several people across a year and tracked by nobody. At renewal the sponsor — who has been tracking — presents the shortfall. The organisation has no counter-record, loses negotiating position entirely, and sometimes loses the partnership over items that would have taken an afternoon to deliver.

## Why It's Still Broken
The commitments live in a PDF — obligations recorded only in a contract document are invisible to the people who have to perform them, so nobody misses a deadline that was never on a calendar. Delivery is spread across content, social and team staff. Nobody owns fulfilment. And the sponsor's tracking is better than the seller's.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Extract the commitments into a calendar and count them. Pull every contracted deliverable into a tracked list with owners and dates, which is the fix and is an afternoon per contract. Count delivery against commitment continuously, as the running total is what prevents the surprise. Alert on anything at risk with enough time to deliver it, since most shortfalls are recoverable. Capture the evidence at the moment of delivery — the post, the clip, the appearance — because reconstructing it later is what makes the meeting go badly. Send the sponsor a monthly fulfilment summary, which converts them from an auditor into a partner. Assign each deliverable to a named person rather than to a team. Flag commitments made verbally that never reached the contract, as those cause the worst disputes. Reconcile before the renewal meeting rather than during it. Keep the record across years so patterns are visible. And write the next contract's commitments at a level the organisation can actually deliver, which is the real lesson.

## Who Feels the Pain
Partnership managers ambushed at renewal; sponsors who did not get what they paid for; content and social staff blamed for untracked obligations; and organisations losing revenue over recoverable gaps.

## Impact If Fixed
Obligations recorded only in a contract document are invisible to the people who have to perform them, so nobody misses a deadline that was never on a calendar. Extracting the commitments into a tracked list is an afternoon that saves the renewal.

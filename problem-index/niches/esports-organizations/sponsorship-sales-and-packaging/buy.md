# Rights Management From Sports Sponsorship Sales

**Niche:** [[niches/esports-organizations/sponsorship-sales-and-packaging/profile|Sponsorship Sales & Packaging]]
**Industry:** [[industries/esports-organizations|Esports Organizations]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Sports rights holders run inventory, pricing and fulfilment on dedicated platforms, and esports organisations run it on a spreadsheet.
**Tags:** #workflow-orchestration #revenue-impact #data-integration #automation #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #confidence-intervals #compliance
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to package and price inventory whose value nobody can establish, against sponsors who have become far more demanding since the contraction — and whoever can price it credibly takes the account.

## The Problem
Sports sponsorship sales is a mature software category. Rights holders manage structured inventory, build packages against it, price from benchmarks, track fulfilment of every contracted deliverable, and generate sponsor-facing reporting automatically. Leagues and clubs of moderate size run on it. Esports organisations sell into the same budgets, face the same buyers, and manage the whole process in decks and spreadsheets — largely because the platforms are priced for organisations several times their size.

## What Already Exists
Structured inventory and availability management; package builder tooling; pricing benchmarks; deliverable fulfilment tracking; and automated sponsor reporting.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to inventory that lives on platforms the organisation does not own and moves when a player transfers. It requires: (1) inventory attached to individual players' own channels, which leaves with the player — no traditional rights holder has inventory that can resign, and this is the substantive difference; (2) digital surfaces with no equivalent of a stadium's fixed asset inventory; (3) content series and creator-style activations as core products rather than as extras; (4) a price point suitable for an organisation with two salespeople, which rules out the incumbent platforms; and (5) fulfilment evidence assembled from platform analytics rather than from a match-day report.

## Target Customer
Esports organisations, leagues, sponsorship agencies, and sports rights management vendors seeking a smaller tier.

## Impact If Solved
Sports rights management is mature software priced for larger organisations. Inventory that can resign with a player, and a price point for a two-person sales team, are what make this a different product.

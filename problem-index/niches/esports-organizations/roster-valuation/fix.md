# The Signing Whose Numbers Did Not Come With Him

**Niche:** [[niches/esports-organizations/roster-valuation/profile|Roster Valuation]]
**Industry:** [[industries/esports-organizations|Esports Organizations]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The organisation signed the league's statistical leader, he performed like an average player, and the buyout cost more than the salary saved.
**Tags:** #quick-win #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #causal-inference #hypothesis-testing #revenue-impact #bayesian-linear-regression
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to establish what a player will contribute to a different team, when every statistic available depends on the patch, the meta, the role and the teammates they had — and whoever solves the transfer takes the account.

## The Problem
The expensive mistake in this sector is signing a player on numbers that belonged to their circumstances. They led the league on a team whose composition fed them opportunities, in a meta that suited their style, against weaker opposition. On the new roster, in a new role, after a patch, the numbers are unremarkable. The contract is multi-year, the buyout is expensive, and the organisation absorbs both while the roster underperforms.

## Why It's Still Broken
Nobody adjusts for anything — a comparison between two players in different situations, made on unadjusted numbers, is not a comparison at all, and the sector makes it in every signing meeting. Past signings are not reviewed against their justifications. Highlight reels are more persuasive than context. And the decision is made under transfer-window time pressure.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Adjust roughly and check the record before signing anything. Apply crude adjustments for role, teammate quality and league strength rather than none, which is the fix and is far better than the unadjusted comparison in use. Split the target's statistics by patch and meta, since a player whose numbers came from one specific meta is the classic failure and is visible immediately. Compare against players in similar roles rather than across the whole league. Look specifically at performance when circumstances changed — a role switch, a roster change, a major patch — as that is the closest available evidence of transferability. Review the organisation's own past signings against what was claimed at the time, which usually reveals a consistent bias. Write down the expected contribution before signing so it can be checked later. Put an interval on the expectation rather than a point, given the sample sizes involved. Weight consistency across contexts above peak performance. Structure contracts with shorter terms or performance conditions where the uncertainty is high. And ask what specifically will transfer, which is a question that frequently has no good answer and is rarely asked.

## Who Feels the Pain
Organisations carrying underperforming contracts; players signed into roles that do not suit them; coaching staff handed a roster that does not fit; and the finances of a sector already under pressure.

## Impact If Fixed
A comparison between two players in different situations, made on unadjusted numbers, is not a comparison at all, and the sector makes it in every signing meeting. Crude role, teammate and league adjustments beat none decisively.

# Roster Valuation

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/esports-organizations|Esports Organizations]]
**Category:** 🟠 Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to establish what a player will contribute to a different team, when every statistic available depends on the patch, the meta, the role and the teammates they had — and whoever solves the transfer takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$200M US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~13% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Medium, non-transferable
**Target Buyer:** General managers
**Automation Potential:** High — contribution modelling

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The roster is the largest controllable cost in the business. It is assembled on in-game statistics that are heavily dependent on patch, meta, role, teammates and league strength — meaning a player's numbers on one team in one season are a poor guide to what they will contribute on another. Multi-year contracts are signed on that basis and buyouts are paid to correct them. The analytical sophistication that exists in these organisations is pointed at winning matches, not at valuing the people who play them.

## Current Tools & Gaps
In-game statistics, match VODs, a coach's judgement and a scout's network. The gaps: no adjustment for patch and meta; no separation of individual contribution from team effect; no league strength normalisation; no role-adjusted comparison; and no record of past signings against outcomes.

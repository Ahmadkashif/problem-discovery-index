# The Visa That Did Not Arrive Before the Major

**Niche:** [[niches/esports-organizations/team-operations-and-logistics/profile|Team Operations & Logistics]]
**Industry:** [[industries/esports-organizations|Esports Organizations]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The filing went in late, the appointment was three weeks out, and the team plays the most important event of the year without its starter.
**Tags:** #quick-win #compliance #workflow-orchestration #automation #evaluation-metrics #worker-facing #data-integration #descriptive-statistics
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to run an international competitive operation — visas, travel, bootcamps, contracts, welfare, sponsor deadlines — through one person with a spreadsheet, and whoever gives that person a system takes the account.

## The Problem
This is a known and recurring failure in the sector: a player cannot travel to a tournament because immigration paperwork was filed too late, an appointment could not be obtained, or a document had expired. The competitive and commercial cost is enormous — a season's qualification, a sponsor's headline moment, a player's career year. The cause is almost always that a deadline sat in one person's head alongside forty other things.

## Why It's Still Broken
Nothing tracks the deadlines — a date that exists only in a person's memory will eventually be missed, and no amount of diligence changes that when the same person is also booking flights and handling a sponsor deadline. Processing times vary and nobody models the buffer. The calendar and the paperwork live in different places. And the failure is treated as bad luck each time it happens.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Put every date in one place with a buffer in front of it. Build a single register of every player's documents with expiry and required filing dates, which is the fix and is an afternoon's work with permanent value. Work backwards from the competitive calendar with a realistic processing buffer, since the deadline that matters is the filing date rather than the travel date. Alert well ahead and escalate to someone other than the manager, because the manager is the person who is already overloaded. Track processing times actually observed per jurisdiction rather than the published ones, as the gap between them is where teams get caught. Identify the players most at risk by nationality and document status, which concentrates the attention correctly. Keep copies of every document accessible from anywhere, since the emergency always happens in transit. Plan contingency for critical events — a substitute, a remote arrangement — rather than assuming success. Review the register monthly as a standing item. Share visibility with leadership so it is not one person's private risk. And record every near-miss, because the pattern shows exactly where the buffer is too thin.

## Who Feels the Pain
Players who miss the event of their career; organisations losing qualification and sponsor moments; team managers carrying a risk nobody else sees; and teammates competing short-handed.

## Impact If Fixed
A date that exists only in a person's memory will eventually be missed, and no amount of diligence changes that. A document register worked backwards from the competitive calendar with a real processing buffer is an afternoon's work against a season-ending risk.

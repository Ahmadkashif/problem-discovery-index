# A System for the Person Holding Everything

**Niche:** [[niches/esports-organizations/team-operations-and-logistics/profile|Team Operations & Logistics]]
**Industry:** [[industries/esports-organizations|Esports Organizations]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** An international competitive operation runs on one person, a spreadsheet and their memory.
**Tags:** #workflow-orchestration #automation #compliance #data-integration #worker-facing #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to run an international competitive operation — visas, travel, bootcamps, contracts, welfare, sponsor deadlines — through one person with a spreadsheet, and whoever gives that person a system takes the account.

## The Problem
The team manager's remit is immigration, international travel, accommodation, employment contracts, tournament registration, scrim scheduling, player welfare and sponsor deliverables, for a group of young people living together in a foreign country. None of it is in a system. Deadlines live in one person's head. A missed visa filing can eliminate a player from a major tournament, and this happens regularly enough to be a known sector risk.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The organisations are small and the role is one person, so there is no internal advocate for tooling. The requirements span immigration, HR, travel and facilities, which no single product covers. Off-the-shelf tools assume a corporate structure these organisations do not have. And the sector's financial pressure has cut exactly this kind of overhead.

## What to Build
Put the deadlines in a system and make the handover possible. Track immigration documents, expiries and filing deadlines against the competitive calendar with escalating alerts, which is the core — a visa deadline missed by a week costs a season and is the one failure nobody can absorb. Hold the competitive calendar as the spine and derive travel, accommodation and filing requirements from it, since everything else follows the schedule. Maintain a document register per player covering contracts, permits, insurance and medical, which is currently a folder somebody hopes is complete. Manage travel and accommodation bookings against that calendar rather than in a separate inbox. Record welfare items — housing, hours, rest, wellbeing checks — as first-class rather than informal, given the age of the people involved. Register sponsor obligations that require player time, because those collide with the competitive schedule and nobody sees both. Make the whole thing handoverable, as the single point of failure is the person and not the spreadsheet. Support multiple nationalities and jurisdictions natively, since that is the normal case here. Alert on collisions between competition, travel, content and rest. And keep it usable by one non-specialist, which is the actual constraint.

## Target Customer
Esports organisations and team managers, leagues, player agencies, and global mobility and HR vendors.

## Impact If Built
A visa deadline missed by a week costs a season and is the one failure nobody can absorb. Tracking immigration and travel against the competitive calendar, in something handoverable, removes the single point of failure.

# The Promo Code Nobody Used

**Niche:** [[niches/esports-organizations/sponsor-outcome-attribution/profile|Sponsor Outcome Attribution]]
**Industry:** [[industries/esports-organizations|Esports Organizations]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The only outcome measurement in the contract is a discount code, it captured a fraction of the effect, and the renewal is being judged on it.
**Tags:** #quick-win #causal-inference #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #confidence-intervals #revenue-impact #data-integration #hypothesis-testing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to show that the attention delivered produced something for the sponsor, using outcome data the sponsor holds and the organisation cannot see — and whoever gets that loop closed takes the account.

## The Problem
Esports sponsorship contracts commonly include one measurable element: a promo code or a tracked link. Its redemption rate becomes the de facto measure of the whole partnership. It captures only the fraction of the audience who converted immediately through that specific path, misses everything upper-funnel, and undercounts by a large margin. Renewals are decided on it, and organisations that delivered genuine brand value lose partnerships on a number that was never designed to measure them.

## Why It's Still Broken
It is the only thing that was measurable, so it became the measure — a partnership with exactly one number attached will be judged by that number regardless of what it represents. Nothing else was written into the contract. Neither party proposed an alternative. And the sponsor's marketing team needs some figure to report internally.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Add cheap complementary evidence and state the code's limits. Write down what the code does and does not capture and put it on the report, which is the fix and reframes the entire renewal conversation. Add a simple survey question to the sponsor's existing customer intake, since a single "where did you hear about us" line costs nothing and captures a far wider effect. Compare traffic and sign-ups during activation windows against baseline, which is weak evidence but far better than nothing and uses data the sponsor already has. Measure branded content engagement separately, as that is the activation quality the code cannot see. Agree the measurement approach at contract time rather than at renewal, which is the durable fix. Use an audience-matched comparison where the sponsor can run one. Report leading indicators monthly rather than one figure annually. Ask for aggregate outcome data rather than record-level, which is usually acceptable when record-level is not. Benchmark against the sponsor's other channels, since the alternative is being judged against a perfect-attribution channel. And propose the alternative measurement yourself, because the sponsor will not.

## Who Feels the Pain
Organisations losing renewals they earned; sponsors making decisions on a fragment; partnership managers with no case to make; and the sector's pricing, which is set by this number.

## Impact If Fixed
A partnership with exactly one number attached will be judged by that number regardless of what it represents. A survey line in the sponsor's intake and a stated limitation on the code change the renewal conversation at almost no cost.

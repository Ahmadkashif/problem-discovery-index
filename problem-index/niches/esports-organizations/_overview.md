# Niche Analysis — Esports Organizations

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/esports-organizations|Esports Organizations]]

## Niche Selection

An esports organisation earns almost nothing from winning. Its revenue is sponsorship, content, merchandise and league distributions, which means the product is audience attention across surfaces the organisation does not own. Its largest controllable cost is a roster valued on statistics that do not transfer between teams. The eight niches below follow those two facts — what the organisation sells and cannot measure, what it buys and cannot evaluate — and then the operational and human machinery that carries both.

## Why These Niches

Audience measurement and sponsorship sales are where all the revenue is and where the sector's credibility problem sits: the organisations spent their growth years selling a story about scale and their contraction years being asked for evidence they never built the instruments to produce. Roster valuation and team operations are the two places where consequential decisions are made on judgement with no supporting system — a multi-year contract signed on statistics that do not transfer, and an entire international operation run by one person with a spreadsheet. The two underserved audiences are the people the sector's restructuring landed on: players with careers that end in their mid-twenties, and the small content team that now produces more of the business's value than the competition does. The last two are mechanical revenue and mechanical scheduling.

## Filter Notes

Seven of the eight are terminal — each names a single contest that every serious competitor is fighting over, and going further would produce features rather than markets.

**Audience measurement** is not. It names a capability rather than a contest, and underneath it are two problems with different customers and different data. Cross-surface attention aggregation asks how to turn incompatible numbers from a broadcast, a dozen player streams, several short-form platforms and social accounts into one comparable unit — a data acquisition and normalisation problem, solvable entirely with data the organisation can reach, and sold to the organisation itself. Sponsor outcome attribution asks whether that attention produced anything for the sponsor — a causal measurement problem that requires conversion and brand data held by the sponsor, not the organisation, and is therefore sold into the sponsor relationship rather than into the organisation's stack. One is plumbing the organisation can build alone; the other cannot exist without the counterparty's cooperation, which is precisely why the sector has neither.

Two adjacent candidates were rejected as belonging elsewhere: **representing individual players and streamers as talent** is its own industry at [[industries/creator-talent-agencies|Creator Talent Agencies]], and **buying audience through paid media** belongs to [[industries/game-user-acquisition-firms|Game User Acquisition Firms]].

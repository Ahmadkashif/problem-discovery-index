# Asking for the Metric Before the Study Runs

**Niche:** [[niches/player-research-firms/outcome-telemetry-linkage/profile|Outcome Telemetry Linkage]]
**Industry:** [[industries/player-research-firms|Player Research Firms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Nobody agreed what number would show the finding was right, so afterwards there is no number to look at.
**Tags:** #quick-win #evaluation-metrics #hypothesis-testing #confidence-intervals #descriptive-statistics #causal-inference #workflow-orchestration #compliance
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to measure whether the change a finding produced actually improved anything, using telemetry the client holds — and whoever gets that arrangement working takes the account.

## The Problem
Studies are commissioned to answer a question and nobody specifies what observable outcome would indicate the answer was right. Afterwards, if anyone thinks to look, there is no agreed measure — so the conversation becomes a search for a number that supports the conclusion, which is worse than no measurement at all. Specifying it in advance costs one conversation at kickoff and is never had.

## Why It's Still Broken
Nobody has the conversation — a measure that is not agreed before the study will be chosen afterwards to fit whatever happened, and everyone involved knows this and still does not have the conversation. The engagement is scoped around the study rather than the outcome. Clients are not asked. And there is no template for asking.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Add one question to the kickoff and write down the answer. Ask at kickoff what observable metric would change if the finding is acted on and correct, which is the fix and takes five minutes. Record it in the study brief so it is part of the engagement rather than an afterthought. Ask what the current value is, since a baseline is needed and the client usually has it to hand. Agree the window over which the change would be expected, as that is where post-hoc disagreements concentrate. Note what else is likely to change in the same period, which determines whether any measurement will be readable. Ask whether the client will share the metric afterwards, which is a much smaller ask than telemetry access and is usually granted. Record the answer even when the client declines, since the pattern of refusals is itself informative. Use the same question across studies so results become comparable. Report back to the client with the comparison, which is a service they value and rarely receive. And accept a rough measure over a perfect one, because an approximate agreed metric beats an exact unagreed one entirely.

## Who Feels the Pain
Researchers who cannot demonstrate impact; clients who cannot justify the research budget; firms competing on reputation rather than evidence; and every subsequent study that could have learned from this one.

## Impact If Fixed
A measure that is not agreed before the study will be chosen afterwards to fit whatever happened, and everyone involved knows this. One question at kickoff makes the outcome measurable at all.

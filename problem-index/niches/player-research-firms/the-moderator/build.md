# Support for the Person in the Room

**Niche:** [[niches/player-research-firms/the-moderator/profile|The Moderator]]
**Industry:** [[industries/player-research-firms|Player Research Firms]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** The most demanding role in the study is performed back to back and followed by the analysis.
**Tags:** #worker-facing #workflow-orchestration #automation #evaluation-metrics #large-language-models #data-integration #descriptive-statistics #tacit-knowledge-ml
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to make back-to-back moderated sessions sustainable for the person running them, who is also the person writing them up — and whoever does that takes the account.

## The Problem
Moderating is sustained attention under constraint: stay neutral, follow the protocol, notice what matters, manage a participant who is frustrated or bored or over-helpful, and capture enough to reconstruct it later. Doing this six times in a day is genuinely depleting, and the same person then produces the analysis. The scarce resource in this field is experienced moderators, and the way the work is structured uses them up.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The role is regarded as inherently manual and the fatigue as inherent to it. Research operations tooling addresses scheduling and recruitment, not the session itself. The firm's economics favour more sessions per day. And moderators are not the people who buy tools.

## What to Build
Take the capture load off the moderator and separate the roles. Provide structured timestamped capture during the session so the moderator marks moments rather than writing notes, which is the core and returns their attention to the participant. Let observers contribute to the same timeline, since the observation load should not sit on the person also running the session. Separate moderation from analysis as distinct roles with a proper handover, as combining them is the structural cause of the overload. Generate an automatic session summary from the capture, so the write-up starts from something rather than nothing. Set and enforce daily session limits based on the work's actual demands rather than on scheduling convenience. Build recovery time into the schedule between sessions rather than treating it as slack to be removed. Support the moderator after difficult sessions, which happen and are currently absorbed privately. Rotate moderators across studies rather than assigning one person to a long block. Capture protocol deviations automatically, which is currently a memory task during a demanding one. And report moderator load as an operational metric, since an unmeasured load is never managed.

## Target Customer
Games user research firms and platforms, research operations leadership, in-house research functions, and research tooling vendors.

## Impact If Built
The scarce resource in this field is experienced moderators and the way the work is structured uses them up. Structured in-session capture plus a real moderation-to-analysis handover removes the double load.

# Load and Support Practice From Clinical Interviewing

**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)

**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to make back-to-back moderated sessions sustainable for the person running them, who is also the person writing them up — and whoever does that takes the account.

## The Problem
Professions built on sustained one-to-one attention — clinical psychology, social work, qualitative interviewing, counselling — established practices to make the work sustainable: caseload limits, mandatory recovery time, supervision and debriefing, structured note-taking that does not compete with attention, and explicit recognition that the work is depleting. These are professional norms with evidence behind them. Session moderation is the same kind of work with none of the norms.

## What Already Exists
Caseload and session limits; mandated recovery intervals; supervision and debriefing structures; structured note-taking protocols; and recognition of emotional labour in workload planning.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to commercial research on a project schedule rather than to a clinical caseload. It requires: (1) session counts driven by a client's study timeline rather than by professional standards, so limits must be commercially justified rather than mandated — this is the substantive difference and is why none of it has transferred; (2) the same person performing analysis afterwards, which no clinical profession requires; (3) sessions observed live by clients, adding performance pressure with no clinical analogue; (4) participants who are consumers rather than patients, so the emotional labour is real but unrecognised; and (5) no professional body to set or enforce standards.

## Target Customer
Games user research firms, research operations leadership, in-house research functions, and professional bodies in user research.

## Impact If Solved
Professions built on sustained attention established caseload limits and supervision because the work depletes people. Limits that must be commercially justified rather than professionally mandated is what has to be argued here.

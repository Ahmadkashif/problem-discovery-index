# Three Days to Watch Forty Hours

**Niche:** [[niches/player-research-firms/session-data-analysis/profile|Session Data Analysis]]
**Industry:** [[industries/player-research-firms|Player Research Firms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The readout is Thursday, the material is forty hours long, and the researcher will see perhaps a third of it.
**Tags:** #quick-win #automation #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #workflow-orchestration #worker-facing #data-integration #large-language-models
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn forty hours of gameplay video, think-aloud audio and observer notes into findings in the three days before the readout — and whoever automates that coding takes the account.

## The Problem
The arithmetic does not work. Sessions finish, the readout is scheduled, and there is not enough time to review the material properly. The researcher prioritises, watches what they can, leans on observer notes taken in real time, and produces findings. The findings are usually good, because the researchers are good. They are also produced from a partial reading, and the deck does not say which third was watched.

## Why It's Still Broken
The schedule is set before the material exists — an analysis window fixed at proposal time cannot know how much material the sessions will produce, and the readout date does not move when they produce more. Observer notes substitute for review. Nobody reports coverage. And the finding is accepted because it is usually right.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Take the mechanical work out of the three days and be honest about coverage. Transcribe and index the material automatically as sessions complete rather than afterwards, which is the fix and returns most of the window to actual analysis. Mark candidate moments from telemetry during the session so review starts targeted. Improve observer note-taking with structured timestamped capture, since those notes are doing the heavy lifting and are currently freeform. Review across participants by moment rather than session by session, which is far faster and matches how findings form. Report coverage in the deck — how much material informed each finding — which is uncomfortable and correct. Schedule the analysis window from the actual session count rather than from the proposal. Start analysis during the session block rather than after it, which is the largest available structural gain. Prioritise by research question rather than chronologically, which most researchers do implicitly and no tool supports. Flag findings based on thin coverage so the team can weight them. And propose a later readout when the material warrants it, which is a conversation firms rarely have and clients usually accept.

## Who Feels the Pain
Researchers producing findings from a third of the evidence; clients acting on conclusions weighted more heavily than warranted; moderators whose sessions are underused; and the discipline's claim to rigour.

## Impact If Fixed
An analysis window fixed at proposal time cannot know how much material the sessions will produce, and the readout date does not move when they produce more. Automatic transcription and indexing during the session block returns the window to analysis.

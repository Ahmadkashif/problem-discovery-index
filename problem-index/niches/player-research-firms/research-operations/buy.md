**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get the right participants into the right sessions at the right time without consuming the research team, and whoever automates that takes the account.

## The Problem
Clinical trial operations solved participant management comprehensively because regulation demanded it: eligibility screening against structured criteria, electronic informed consent with audit trails, visit scheduling across sites and constraints, retention and reminder programmes, and participant payment. The software category is mature and handles far more complexity than any user research study requires. Games user research does a simpler version of the same job by hand.

## What Already Exists
Structured eligibility screening; electronic consent with audit trails; multi-constraint visit scheduling; retention and reminder programmes; and participant payment administration.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a lighter, faster process with confidentiality rather than safety at its centre. It requires: (1) non-disclosure over unreleased games as the central compliance concern rather than patient safety, which changes what the consent flow must do — this is the substantive difference; (2) study timelines of weeks and recruitment in days, where trials plan over months; (3) device, platform and facility requirements as scheduling constraints, with no clinical equivalent; (4) far lighter regulatory burden, so the tooling must be proportionate rather than comprehensive; and (5) small firms and solo researchers as the buyer, requiring a price and a simplicity clinical software does not offer.

## Target Customer
Games user research firms, in-house research functions, playtesting platforms, and research operations vendors.

## Impact If Solved
Clinical operations automated screening, consent and payment because regulation demanded it, and the software is mature. Non-disclosure over unreleased titles rather than patient safety, at a small-firm price, is what the adaptation requires.

# Three Hundred Studies and No Record of Any of Them

**Niche:** [[niches/player-research-firms/findings-outcome-loop/profile|Findings Outcome Loop]]
**Industry:** [[industries/player-research-firms|Player Research Firms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The firm has run hundreds of studies and cannot search what it recommended, let alone what happened.
**Tags:** #quick-win #data-integration #workflow-orchestration #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #automation #tacit-knowledge-ml #confidence-intervals
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to find out what happened to the findings it delivered, when the evidence lives on the other side of an organisational boundary — and whoever closes that loop takes the account.

## The Problem
The firm's own body of work is a folder of decks. There is no searchable record of what was recommended, to whom, about what kind of game, or what was found. A researcher starting a new study on a familiar problem cannot find what the firm learned about it three years ago on another title. The accumulated knowledge of hundreds of studies exists as individual memory and slide files.

## Why It's Still Broken
The deliverable is a deck — a body of knowledge stored as presentation files is not a body of knowledge, because nothing in it can be queried, compared or counted. No one owns the archive. Client confidentiality is assumed to prevent reuse, usually more broadly than it actually does. And each study closes and the team moves on.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Make the firm's own findings searchable before worrying about outcomes. Record each study's findings and recommendations as structured entries rather than only in a deck, which is the fix and takes minutes per study going forward. Tag by game type, platform, research question and method, so retrieval is possible at all. Backfill the most recent year rather than the whole archive, which is achievable and captures most of the value. Make it searchable by every researcher in the firm, since the point is reuse. Record what the client said they would do at the readout, which is free and is the first half of the outcome loop. Separate what is confidential from what is generalisable method, as the second is usually shareable and is assumed not to be. Note the study's sample size and design alongside the finding, so its weight is visible on retrieval. Review recurring findings across studies, which is where the field-level insight is. Use it in proposals, which is where it pays commercially. And make recording it part of study close rather than an extra task.

## Who Feels the Pain
Researchers rediscovering what the firm already knew; clients paying for findings the firm has produced before; research leads with no view of their own body of work; and the discipline, which accumulates nothing.

## Impact If Fixed
A body of knowledge stored as presentation files is not a body of knowledge, because nothing in it can be queried, compared or counted. Structured findings plus tagging makes hundreds of studies searchable and starts the outcome loop for free.

**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to give the single researcher inside a studio the peer review, method support and standing that an agency team has by default — and whoever supplies that takes the account.

## The Problem
Professions where practitioners work alone — sole-practitioner clinicians, independent auditors, solo legal practice — built structures precisely because isolation degrades quality and wellbeing. Peer supervision groups, mandatory continuing education, published standards to cite, practice review schemes and professional indemnity all exist to give a lone practitioner something to lean on. Solo in-house researchers have a discipline with real standards and none of the supporting apparatus.

## What Already Exists
Peer supervision and review groups; continuing professional development requirements; published practice standards; practice review and accreditation schemes; and professional body backing.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to an employed practitioner inside a commercial team rather than an independent professional. It requires: (1) an employer whose interests can conflict with the finding, so the support must give standing against internal pressure rather than assuring external clients — this is the substantive difference; (2) no licensure or regulatory requirement, so participation must be voluntary and immediately useful; (3) confidentiality over unreleased games, which constrains what can be peer-reviewed and how; (4) a practitioner with no protected time for professional development; and (5) a field young enough that the standards themselves are still being established.

## Target Customer
In-house research functions, research agencies offering support, professional bodies in user research, and continuing education providers.

## Impact If Solved
Solo practitioner fields built supervision and standards because isolation degrades quality. An employed practitioner whose employer can prefer a different finding needs standing, not assurance — which is what the adaptation has to supply.

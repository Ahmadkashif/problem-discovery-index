# Nobody to Check the Study Design

**Niche:** [[niches/player-research-firms/the-in-house-researcher/profile|The In-House Researcher of One]]
**Industry:** [[industries/player-research-firms|Player Research Firms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The study ran with a leading task instruction and the researcher found out from the results.
**Tags:** #worker-facing #quick-win #evaluation-metrics #workflow-orchestration #descriptive-statistics #confidence-intervals #tacit-knowledge-ml #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to give the single researcher inside a studio the peer review, method support and standing that an agency team has by default — and whoever supplies that takes the account.

## The Problem
Design errors in research are ordinary and are caught by a second pair of eyes: a leading question, a task instruction that gives away the answer, a screener that selects the wrong people, a session order that creates a learning effect. An agency team catches these in a design review that takes twenty minutes. The solo researcher has no such review, runs the study, and discovers the problem in the data — when the sessions have already happened and cannot be re-run.

## Why It's Still Broken
There is no reviewer — a design flaw that twenty minutes of another professional's attention would catch instead costs an entire study, because the twenty minutes are not available in the building. Nobody in the studio knows enough to review it. Asking outside raises confidentiality concerns. And the schedule has no slack for review.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Find twenty minutes of another researcher's attention, somehow. Establish a peer review exchange with researchers at other studios, which is the fix and costs both parties twenty minutes per study. Use a design checklist covering the recurring error types, which catches a meaningful share without any second person. Pilot every study with one participant before the block, since a pilot exposes most design errors and is frequently skipped for time. Have a non-researcher colleague read the task instructions aloud, which catches leading language surprisingly well. Share designs rather than findings to keep confidentiality manageable, as the design is usually not the sensitive part. Keep a personal record of errors caught and missed, which is how a solo practitioner improves without feedback. Build review time into the study schedule explicitly rather than hoping for it. Use standard designs for recurring study types instead of designing each from scratch. Ask an agency for a paid design review on high-stakes studies, which is cheap relative to the study. And treat the pilot as mandatory, which is the single most effective habit available to someone working alone.

## Who Feels the Pain
Researchers who lost a study to a fixable error; studios acting on findings from a flawed design; participants whose time produced nothing; and a researcher's confidence, working alone with no way to check.

## Impact If Fixed
A design flaw that twenty minutes of another professional's attention would catch instead costs an entire study, because the twenty minutes are not available in the building. A peer exchange and a mandatory pilot are the two habits that replace a team.

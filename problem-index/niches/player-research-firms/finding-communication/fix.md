# The Caveat That Was Said Out Loud

**Niche:** [[niches/player-research-firms/finding-communication/profile|Finding Communication]]
**Industry:** [[industries/player-research-firms|Player Research Firms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The researcher explained the limitation clearly in the readout, and the deck went to twenty people who were not in the room.
**Tags:** #quick-win #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #confidence-intervals #workflow-orchestration #worker-facing #data-integration #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to report a finding definitely enough to be acted on and honestly enough to be true — and whoever resolves that takes the account.

## The Problem
Readouts are careful. The researcher says which findings are strong, which are suggestive, which rest on two participants, and what the sample could not tell them. Then the deck circulates. It reaches a producer, a director and a publisher who were not in the room, and every finding on it carries the same apparent weight. Decisions are made on slides that were never intended to stand alone.

## Why It's Still Broken
The caveats live in the talk and not in the artefact — a document that omits the qualifications its author stated will be read as though there were none, and it is always the document that travels. Slides are written for presentation. Adding caveats to slides feels like weakening them. And nobody tracks where the deck goes.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Put the caveats in the artefact that travels. Write the key limitation on the slide rather than saying it, which is the fix and costs a line per finding. State the participant count supporting each finding on the finding itself, since a number travels where a spoken caveat does not. Mark findings as observed or inferred, which is a single word and prevents most misreading. Include the sample description on an early slide rather than in an appendix. Add a standing limitations slide that cannot be skipped, which is standard elsewhere and absent here. Assume the deck will be forwarded without the researcher, because it will be. Separate the strong findings from the suggestive ones structurally rather than by ordering, as ordering does not survive extraction. Link findings to clips so a reader can check rather than trust. Ask the client how the findings will be circulated, which frequently changes how the deck is written. And write the deck for the reader who was not in the room, since that is most of its audience.

## Who Feels the Pain
Teams acting on a two-participant observation as though it were established; researchers whose careful caveats vanished; clients making decisions on partial information; and the finding that turns out to be wrong with nobody's name on the caveat.

## Impact If Fixed
A document that omits the qualifications its author stated will be read as though there were none, and it is always the document that travels. A line per finding and a participant count is the whole fix.

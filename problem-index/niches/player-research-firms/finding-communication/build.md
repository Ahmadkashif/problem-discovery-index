# Honest Confidence That Still Gets Acted On

**Niche:** [[niches/player-research-firms/finding-communication/profile|Finding Communication]]
**Industry:** [[industries/player-research-firms|Player Research Firms]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A hedged finding does not get acted on, so findings are not hedged, and the confidence in the deck is not the confidence in the room.
**Tags:** #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #descriptive-statistics #large-language-models #worker-facing #data-integration #hypothesis-testing #workflow-orchestration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to report a finding definitely enough to be acted on and honestly enough to be true — and whoever resolves that takes the account.

## The Problem
A researcher observes something in five of eight sessions, understands exactly how much weight that supports, and writes a finding that a development team will act on. The two are in tension: an accurately hedged statement gets deprioritised, and a decisive one overstates. The discipline resolves this in the room, verbally, with a caveat that does not travel when the deck is forwarded to someone who was not there.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The deck is the medium and a deck expresses emphasis rather than evidence. Confidence language is read as weakness by teams making decisions under pressure. The researcher is rewarded for influence. And no format exists that conveys both strength and basis at a glance.

## What to Build
Attach the evidence to the finding so the confidence is inspectable rather than asserted. Link every finding to the specific moments that support it, which is the core — a finding a team can watch is believed without needing to be overstated. Show how many participants exhibited the behaviour, plainly, as a count is read correctly where a confidence word is not. Distinguish observation from inference explicitly, since conflating them is where most overstatement happens. Express strength in terms a development team can use — how likely this is to affect players, how confident, how costly to ignore — rather than in research language. Show disconfirming evidence alongside, which builds trust rather than undermining the finding. Produce a format that survives forwarding, because the caveat said in the room is lost by the second person who sees the deck. Separate the finding from the recommendation, as the evidence supports the first and judgement supports the second. Report the sample the finding rests on next to the finding itself. Test whether teams act differently on well-evidenced findings, which is how the format is validated. And give researchers language for genuine uncertainty that does not read as hedging, which is a craft problem as much as a tooling one.

## Target Customer
Games user research firms, in-house research functions, development teams receiving research, and research reporting tooling vendors.

## Impact If Built
A caveat said in the room is lost by the second person who reads the deck, so the confidence that travels is always the overstated one. Linking findings to watchable moments lets a finding be believed without being overstated.

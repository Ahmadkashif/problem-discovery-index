# Eight Participants, All the Same Person

**Niche:** [[niches/player-research-firms/participant-sampling/profile|Participant Sampling]]
**Industry:** [[industries/player-research-firms|Player Research Firms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The study recruited eight players and seven of them were experienced, available and already fans of the genre.
**Tags:** #quick-win #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #hypothesis-testing #data-integration #automation #probability-distributions
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to generalise from whoever answered the screener and turned up, when those people are systematically not the people the game is for — and whoever fixes the sample takes the account.

## The Problem
A study intended to test whether a new player can understand a game recruits eight participants, most of whom are experienced in the genre because those are the people on the panel and those are the people who say yes. The findings say the onboarding works. The actual new players — the ones the study was for — were not there. The participant table in the appendix records this accurately and nobody reads it as the caveat it is.

## Why It's Still Broken
The screener does not exclude hard enough — a recruitment process that fills the slots is judged to have succeeded, and who filled them is recorded in an appendix rather than treated as a result. Genre-experienced participants are easier to recruit. The deadline rewards filling slots. And the finding reads the same either way.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Report the sample as a result rather than as an appendix. Put the sample's composition against the intended profile on the first page, which is the fix and changes how every finding in the deck is read. Screen on the dimension the research question depends on rather than on general profile, since a study about new players needs new players and nothing else matters as much. Reject participants who do not fit even when slots go unfilled, which is a discipline the schedule punishes and the finding requires. Recruit outside the standard panel for hard-to-reach profiles, budgeting for it explicitly. Record who was screened out and who declined, as the pattern shows where the bias enters. Recruit fresh participants rather than repeat panel members, which is a known and tolerated problem. State in the findings which participants each conclusion rests on. Run a smaller correct sample rather than a larger convenient one, which is the right trade and is rarely taken. Track composition across a client's studies to show the cumulative skew. And say plainly when the sample cannot support the question asked, which is the professional obligation.

## Who Feels the Pain
Clients acting on findings about a population that was not studied; researchers who knew and said it quietly; new players whose experience was never observed; and the discipline's credibility when the launch contradicts the research.

## Impact If Fixed
A recruitment process that fills the slots is judged to have succeeded, and who filled them is recorded in an appendix rather than treated as a result. Putting composition against intent on the first page changes how every finding is read.

## The Problem
Cybersecurity developed a whole apparatus for sharing indicators of attack between organisations: standard formats, sharing communities, confidence and traffic-light protocols, indicator expiry, and a shift from sharing atomic indicators to sharing behaviours because indicators are cheap for attackers to rotate. Fraud consortia are proprietary, closed, incompatible, and still largely built on atomic identifiers.

## What Already Exists
Structured threat intelligence formats; information sharing and analysis organisations; confidence scoring and handling protocols; indicator lifecycle and expiry practice; and behaviour-over-indicator methodology.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a commercial consortium with a competitive moat. It requires: (1) sharing that is a competitive asset rather than a collective defence, which is the substantive difference — security shares because everyone benefits and fraud vendors compete on exclusivity; (2) indicators about consumers rather than about infrastructure, which brings privacy and fair treatment obligations security sharing does not carry; (3) an atomic identifier whose false positive falls on a real person, making expiry a consumer protection matter rather than hygiene; (4) behaviour-level sharing that would generalise better and would also be harder to keep proprietary; and (5) no neutral sharing body, since the consortium operators are the vendors themselves.

## Target Customer
Network and data leadership, merchants, regulators examining consumer impact, and threat intelligence platform vendors.

## Impact If Solved
Security learned that atomic indicators rotate and behaviours persist, and built lifecycle discipline around it. Fraud consortia have neither, and the expiry question here affects real people rather than infrastructure.

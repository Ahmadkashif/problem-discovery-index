## The Problem
Credit strategy functions do this properly. A cutoff change is analysed with swap-set analysis showing exactly which applicants would flip and what their expected performance is, scenario simulation across economic conditions, portfolio-level forecasting, and a documented rationale that a regulator will read. It is a mature discipline with tooling and standards. Fraud strategy performs the same kind of decision with a slider and a dashboard.

## What Already Exists
Swap-set analysis; cutoff optimisation with profit curves; scenario and stress simulation; portfolio forecasting; and documented policy rationale standards.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a decision with a missing outcome side and an adversary. It requires: (1) swap-set analysis where one side has no performance data, which is exactly what the randomised sample supplies and is the substantive dependency; (2) an adversary who responds to the policy, so a projection assuming a stable population will overstate the benefit of tightening; (3) decisions made in days rather than quarterly, which needs simulation to be instant rather than an analytical project; (4) a merchant whose commercial interest differs from the vendor's guarantee exposure, making the cost function contested; and (5) no regulatory requirement for documented rationale, so the discipline must be adopted for its own sake.

## Target Customer
Risk leadership and strategists, merchants, guarantee underwriters, and credit strategy tooling vendors.

## Impact If Solved
Credit strategy's swap-set analysis is exactly the right instrument and needs performance data on both sides of the cutoff. Supplying the missing side is what makes the whole mature discipline transferable.

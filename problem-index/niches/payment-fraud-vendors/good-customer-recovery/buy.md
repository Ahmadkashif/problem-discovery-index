# Step-Up Authentication and Cart Recovery

**Niche:** [[niches/payment-fraud-vendors/good-customer-recovery/profile|Good-Customer Recovery]]
**Industry:** [[industries/payment-fraud-vendors|Payment Fraud Vendors]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Authentication built risk-based step-up and ecommerce built cart recovery, and the declined transaction sits between them with neither applied.
**Tags:** #workflow-orchestration #gradient-boosting #evaluation-metrics #revenue-impact #automation #confidence-intervals #compliance #logistic-regression
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to give a wrongly declined customer a way through instead of a dead end — and whoever recovers them turns the category's largest invisible loss into revenue.

## The Problem
Two mature practices bracket this problem. Risk-based authentication established that friction should be proportionate to risk and that a step-up challenge resolves uncertainty far better than a refusal. Ecommerce established that an abandoned cart is recoverable with a well-timed message and that recovery rates are substantial. The declined transaction is an abandoned cart caused by the merchant's own system, and neither practice is applied to it.

## What Already Exists
Risk-based and adaptive authentication with step-up; strong customer authentication frameworks; abandoned cart recovery programmes; alternative payment method fallback; and conversion measurement practice.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a customer the merchant's own system rejected. It requires: (1) recovery messaging that does not reveal a fraud decision, which is a genuine constraint since telling a possible fraudster why they were stopped is unwise — this is the substantive design problem; (2) step-up chosen by predicted customer type rather than by a uniform risk band, so the challenge lands where it resolves rather than where it annoys; (3) coordination between the fraud vendor and the merchant's commerce platform, which currently have no shared flow; (4) an abandonment caused by the merchant rather than by the customer, which makes the recovery both more justified and more delicate; and (5) outcomes that feed back as labels, which cart recovery never had a use for.

## Target Customer
Merchant success and product leadership, merchants, commerce platforms, and authentication and conversion vendors.

## Impact If Solved
Both bracketing practices are mature and neither is applied to a cart the merchant's own system abandoned. The design constraint is recovering the customer without telling a possible fraudster anything, which is solvable and unsolved.

**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to assemble the evidence a network's reason code demands, inside its deadline, only for the cases that can actually be won — and whoever predicts winnability stops spending effort on disputes that were lost before they started.

## The Problem
Insurance claims operations and litigation management both solved this: assess the merits early, estimate the expected recovery net of cost, settle or decline the weak cases, and concentrate resources on the ones worth contesting. Both have models, triage rules and portfolio-level reporting on where effort goes. Chargeback representment has the same economics — a case with a probability, a value and a cost to pursue — and is run as a processing queue.

## What Already Exists
Claims triage and early merit assessment; litigation outcome prediction and portfolio management; settlement decision frameworks; evidence management systems; and cost-of-pursuit reporting.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a very high volume of very small cases with rigid deadlines. It requires: (1) triage that costs almost nothing per case, since the case values are small and any manual assessment destroys the economics — this is the substantive constraint; (2) a rigid network deadline rather than a negotiable timeline, which removes the option of waiting; (3) rules set by card networks and changing without negotiation, unlike litigation where procedure is stable; (4) evidence held by the merchant rather than by the party contesting, requiring retrieval across systems; and (5) an outcome decided by an issuer applying network rules rather than by an adjudicator weighing arguments, which makes the prediction problem more tractable than litigation's.

## Target Customer
Dispute operations leadership, merchants, guarantee providers, and claims and legal operations vendors with no payments presence.

## Impact If Solved
Both adjacent disciplines triage on expected value and this one does not. The constraint is that triage must cost nothing per case, which is exactly what makes it a modelling problem rather than a staffing one.

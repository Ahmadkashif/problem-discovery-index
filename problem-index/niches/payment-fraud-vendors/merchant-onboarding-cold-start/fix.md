# Judged on the Worst Ninety Days

**Niche:** [[niches/payment-fraud-vendors/merchant-onboarding-cold-start/profile|Merchant Onboarding & Cold Start]]
**Industry:** [[industries/payment-fraud-vendors|Payment Fraud Vendors]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The merchant's entire impression of the product is formed during the period when the model knows least about them, and nobody told them that would happen.
**Tags:** #quick-win #worker-facing #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #workflow-orchestration #automation #confidence-intervals #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to know a new merchant's definition of normal before it has any history — and whoever transfers that from merchants already on the network wins the first ninety days the customer judges them on.

## The Problem
The merchant goes live expecting the performance the demo showed. Instead they get false positives on their most valuable customers, review queues they were not staffed for, and a tuning conversation they did not know was coming. Nobody explained that the model needs history, what the early period would look like, or how long it lasts. By the time performance is good, the relationship has a scar and the renewal conversation is difficult.

## Why It's Still Broken
The sales process demonstrates steady-state performance, so the expectation is set at the wrong level — a demo on mature data cannot honestly represent week one and nobody corrects it afterwards. The onboarding period is owned by implementation rather than by anyone measuring performance. Complaints are handled case by case. And nobody measures time to acceptable performance, so the period's length is not even known internally.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Set the expectation and manage the period. Tell the merchant explicitly what the first weeks will look like and how long the learning period is, which is the fix and converts a shock into a plan. Measure and publish time to acceptable performance from past merchants, since the number exists in the data and would make the explanation credible. Widen review bands deliberately during the early period rather than declining, as a review is recoverable and a decline is not. Agree a tuning cadence in advance with named owners on both sides, because the conversation currently happens reactively after a complaint. Flag the merchant's likely early false positive patterns from similar merchants, which lets both sides prepare. Review the first weeks' declines together, since the merchant knows which were good customers and the vendor cannot know otherwise. Staff review capacity for the early period explicitly, as the volume is predictably higher. Track complaints and escalations by merchant age, which will show the pattern clearly. Do not benchmark a new merchant against mature ones in reporting, because it is misleading and damages trust. And ask new merchants at ninety days what surprised them, since the answer is consistent and nobody collects it.

## Who Feels the Pain
New merchants losing customers during onboarding; customer success teams managing avoidable escalations; review teams absorbing unplanned volume; and vendors whose churn concentrates in the first year.

## Impact If Fixed
A demo on mature data cannot honestly represent week one, and nobody corrects the expectation afterwards. Publishing the real time-to-performance and widening review bands during the learning period turns a scar into a managed phase.

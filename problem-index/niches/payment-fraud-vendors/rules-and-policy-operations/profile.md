# Rules & Policy Operations

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/payment-fraud-vendors|Payment Fraud Vendors]]
**Category:** 🟠 Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to manage a rule layer that grows after every incident and is never pruned — and whoever measures what each rule actually does takes back the decisions the model should be making.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$700M US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~10% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Low — rules written under pressure
**Target Buyer:** Risk strategy leadership
**Automation Potential:** High — measurement and lifecycle management

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Every model sits under a rule layer. Rules are written in response to a loss spike or a merchant complaint, by someone under pressure, and almost none are ever removed. Over years the layer accumulates into hundreds of overlapping conditions whose combined effect nobody can describe, frequently overriding a model that would now decide correctly. It is a distinct discipline from modelling — configuration management for a policy artefact — and it has no tooling.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Rule builders, versioning if any, and a change log. The gaps: no measurement of individual rule impact; no expiry or review; overlapping and contradictory rules undetected; no simulation before deployment; and no view of the rule layer's aggregate effect.

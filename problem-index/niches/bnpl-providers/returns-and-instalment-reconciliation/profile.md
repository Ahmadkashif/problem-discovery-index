# Returns & Instalment Reconciliation

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/bnpl-providers|BNPL Providers]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to reconcile a merchant's return policy with a consumer's instalment schedule — and whoever does that removes the most common and most infuriating failure in the product.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$14B US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~13% of originations
**Digital Adoption:** Low — two systems that do not talk
**Target Buyer:** Operations leadership
**Automation Potential:** Very High — the reconciliation is rule-based

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The provider sits between a merchant's return policy and a consumer's instalment schedule, and must reconcile two systems that were never designed to talk. A partial return, a return after two of four payments, an exchange, a refund to a card rather than to the plan, a return processed after the plan completed — each produces a reconciliation the merchant's system and the instalment schedule handle differently and neither handles jointly. The consumer experiences it as continuing to be charged for something they sent back.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Merchant refund notifications of varying timeliness, manual adjustment of instalment schedules, customer service intervention, and reconciliation spreadsheets. The gaps: refunds arriving late or not at the plan level; partial returns not mapping to a payment schedule; consumers charged after returning; exchanges unmodelled; and the whole reconciliation handled case by case.

# Six Apps and a Calendar

**Niche:** [[niches/bnpl-providers/the-consumer-with-six-plans/profile|The Consumer With Six Plans]]
**Industry:** [[industries/bnpl-providers|BNPL Providers]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** The consumer is the only party who could see all six plans at once, and has no tool that shows them.
**Tags:** #worker-facing #data-integration #time-series-forecasting #confidence-intervals #evaluation-metrics #compliance #automation #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to give a consumer a view of what they have actually committed to across providers — and whoever does that serves the only party who could see all of it and has no tool for it.

## The Problem
Someone has plans with four providers. Payments fall on the eighth, the twelfth, the fifteenth and the twenty-second, against a debit card, in amounts they agreed to individually and have not added up. Each provider's app shows that provider's schedule. Nothing shows the total. The consumer forms an impression from memory, which is systematically optimistic because each individual commitment felt small, and finds out the true position when a payment fails, a fee is charged and their card is declined at a shop. The information exists in four apps and the consumer's own bank statement, and nothing assembles it.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Each provider builds for its own relationship, and an aggregate view is a product for a customer the provider shares with competitors — the commercial logic points away from building it. Aggregation requires either consumer-permissioned data access or a sector mechanism, neither of which exists for these obligations. Banks see the payments and do not categorise them as instalment obligations. And the consumer who most needs it is the least likely to seek it out.

## What to Build
Give the consumer the whole picture. Aggregate obligations across providers through consumer-permissioned access, which is the foundation and can be built by a third party or by a provider willing to serve the relationship rather than only the account. Detect instalment payments in bank transaction data where direct access is unavailable, since the payments are recognisable and this reaches consumers who will never connect six accounts manually. Forecast the coming weeks against expected income, which is the product — a consumer who can see that the fifteenth is a problem can act on the fourteenth. Warn before a payment that will fail, which is the fix note's subject and is the moment the harm is preventable. Show the total committed rather than the individual amounts, since the whole mechanism of the harm is that each commitment looked small. Support restructuring, including contacting providers on the consumer's behalf, which is what they actually need and cannot do across four relationships. Make it available to people with no financial sophistication, since that is the population and any product requiring effort will not reach them. Connect to debt advice where the position warrants it. Be honest about the provider's conflict where a provider builds it, since a tool that discourages further borrowing is not commercially obvious and should be a deliberate choice. And measure whether users end up in fewer difficulties, because that is the outcome and the product should be judged on it rather than on engagement.

## Target Customer
Consumers holding plans across providers, consumer finance and budgeting vendors, banks who see the payments, and the providers willing to serve the person rather than the account.

## Impact If Built
Each provider builds for its own relationship and the aggregate view serves a customer they share with competitors, so the commercial logic points away from it. Detecting instalment payments in bank data reaches the consumers who will never connect six accounts, and the forecast is what makes the fifteenth actionable on the fourteenth.

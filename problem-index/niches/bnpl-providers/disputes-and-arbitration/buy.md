# Online Dispute Resolution Practice

**Niche:** [[niches/bnpl-providers/disputes-and-arbitration/profile|Disputes & Arbitration]]
**Industry:** [[industries/bnpl-providers|BNPL Providers]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Marketplaces built online dispute resolution at enormous volume with structured evidence and consistent outcomes, and instalment providers decide by agent.
**Tags:** #workflow-orchestration #evaluation-metrics #compliance #confidence-intervals #automation #descriptive-statistics #hypothesis-testing #graph-theory
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to adjudicate between a consumer and a merchant on evidence neither supplies — and whoever builds that evidence base decides a dispute rather than splitting the difference.

## The Problem
Large marketplaces resolve buyer-seller disputes at enormous volume and have developed real practice for it: structured evidence requirements by dispute type, automated resolution for clear cases, consistency measurement across decisions, seller and buyer history as inputs, and an appeal route. They built it because the alternative — a person reading two accounts and deciding — did not scale and produced outcomes participants experienced as arbitrary. Instalment providers face the same problem at similar volume and are at the earlier stage.

## What Already Exists
Structured dispute intake by type; evidence requirements specified per dispute category; automated resolution for clear cases; participant history as decision input; and consistency measurement with appeal.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a three-party structure where the arbiter also holds a credit obligation. It requires: (1) the arbiter having a financial position in the outcome, since the provider holds the instalment plan and a decision for the consumer means a loss the provider absorbs or recovers from the merchant — a conflict marketplaces do not have and which must be handled explicitly; (2) the merchant being a commercial partner whose relationship the provider values, unlike a marketplace seller who is a participant; (3) a payment schedule that continues during the dispute, so the timing of the decision has consequences a marketplace refund does not; (4) regulatory obligations on how a credit provider treats disputed amounts, which a marketplace is not subject to; and (5) the provider's lack of visibility into the transaction, since a marketplace holds the listing, the message history and the payment while the provider holds only the plan.

## Target Customer
Provider operations and compliance leadership, and dispute resolution vendors for whom the three-party instalment structure is an unserved case.

## Impact If Solved
Marketplaces built this because a person reading two accounts did not scale and produced arbitrary outcomes, and the practice exists. The arbiter holding a financial position in the outcome, and lacking visibility into the transaction, are the two adaptations the marketplace model does not cover.

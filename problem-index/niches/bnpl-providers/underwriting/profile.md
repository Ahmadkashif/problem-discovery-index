# Underwriting

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/bnpl-providers|BNPL Providers]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** This niche is not terminal — inferring capacity from a thin file and seeing what a consumer owes elsewhere are different contests with different winners, and they are stated separately in the sub-niches below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$30B US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~27% of originations
**Digital Adoption:** High — modern stack, fragmented evidence
**Target Buyer:** Credit risk leadership
**Automation Potential:** Very High — decisioning is the product

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The provider approves a purchase split into instalments, inside a checkout, in a few hundred milliseconds, on an applicant who frequently has no usable credit file. The repayment outcome arrives over the following weeks. This decision is the business — it determines the loss rate, the approval rate and therefore the merchant relationship — which makes it the largest concentration of contested value in the category.

It is also **not terminal**. Two problems live here with different solutions. Thin-file decisioning asks what can be inferred about a person the bureaus cannot describe, from device signals, behaviour, the basket and any history the provider has — a modelling contest a provider can win alone with better data and better science. Cross-provider accumulation asks how much this consumer already owes elsewhere, which no modelling can answer from one provider's data and which requires furnishing, a bureau mechanism or a consortium. One is solvable inside a company and the other is not solvable inside any company. The two contests are stated in the sub-niches.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Bureau data where it exists, device and behavioural signals, basket and merchant context, the provider's own repayment history, and a millisecond decision budget. The gaps: bureau files absent for a large share of applicants; concurrent plans at other providers invisible; outcomes fragmented across providers and bureaus; furnishing partial and inconsistent; and the sector's central risk unmeasurable by anyone.

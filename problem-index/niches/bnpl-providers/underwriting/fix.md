# Underwriting as the Only Lender

**Niche:** [[niches/bnpl-providers/underwriting/profile|Underwriting]]
**Industry:** [[industries/bnpl-providers|BNPL Providers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Every provider underwrites as if it were the consumer's only lender, because the sector's partial furnishing makes concurrent plans across providers invisible to all of them at once.
**Tags:** #compliance #evaluation-metrics #data-integration #confidence-intervals #descriptive-statistics #quick-win #graph-theory #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** This niche is not terminal — inferring capacity from a thin file and seeing what a consumer owes elsewhere are different contests with different winners, and they are stated separately in the sub-niches.

## The Problem
Each provider's affordability assessment implicitly assumes the payments it is about to schedule are the only ones the consumer has committed to. They frequently are not. A consumer can hold six plans from six providers with overlapping payment dates, and no participant can see more than their own. The assessment each provider makes is defensible in isolation and collectively produces an exposure nobody intended and nobody can measure. This is the sector's central risk, it is a property of the structure rather than of any provider's decisions, and every participant would benefit from it being fixed.

## Why It's Still Broken
Furnishing is voluntary and partial, and the products that furnish are not the ones where accumulation concentrates — the reporting gap is precisely aligned with the risk, which is why it has not resolved itself. A provider that furnishes alone gives competitors visibility of its own customers and gains little in return, which is a classic collective action problem. Bureau infrastructure was not designed for six-week obligations. And the consumers most exposed are the least able to make the case.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Make the exposure visible to somebody. Furnish consistently, including the short instalment products, which is the fix and is what every provider would benefit from if all of them did it — the collective action problem is the obstacle rather than any technical difficulty. Support a real-time or near-real-time sharing mechanism, since six-week obligations taken on the same afternoon are not addressed by monthly reporting cycles. Work with the bureaus on a format that represents these obligations, because the existing structures are a poor fit and that poorness is used as a reason for inaction. Build a consortium where bureau infrastructure cannot move fast enough, which is a route the sector could take collectively and has not. Measure accumulation internally at least, since a provider can see a consumer's repeat behaviour with itself and that alone is informative and frequently unused. Publish sector-level accumulation statistics, which would inform the regulatory conversation with better data than a regulator will otherwise use. Support the consumer's own view of their obligations, connecting to that niche, since they are the one party who could see all of it and have no tool. Move before it is mandated, because it will be mandated and the terms will be better if the sector proposes them. Handle the privacy and competition questions explicitly rather than using them as reasons for delay. And report what the sector cannot currently see, because naming the gap plainly is the first step in a conversation that is otherwise had entirely in generalities.

## Who Feels the Pain
Consumers accumulating obligations nobody can see; providers whose losses come from a risk they cannot measure; and a sector whose central question will be answered by a regulator using worse data.

## Impact If Fixed
The reporting gap is precisely aligned with the risk, which is why it has not resolved itself, and no single provider benefits from furnishing alone. Consistent furnishing including short instalment products is the fix and the obstacle is collective action rather than technology.

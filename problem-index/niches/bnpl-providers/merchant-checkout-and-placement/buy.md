**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to make the offer appear at the right moment in a checkout the provider does not control — and whoever does that wins the conversion that decides which provider a merchant keeps.

## The Problem
Checkout conversion optimisation is one of the most heavily practised areas in ecommerce. Every element of the flow has been tested repeatedly, the effects of placement, wording, timing and friction are well documented, and serious merchants run continuous experimentation programmes. The instalment offer sits inside that flow, materially affects conversion, and is positioned according to an integration guide rather than according to any test the provider has run.

## What Already Exists
Checkout experimentation programmes; placement and messaging testing methodology; funnel instrumentation; statistical testing infrastructure; and documented evidence on checkout friction effects.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to an element inside someone else's funnel. It requires: (1) the experiment spanning thousands of independent merchant sites rather than running within one, which gives statistical power no individual merchant has and is the provider's genuine advantage — it is also a governance question since the merchant owns the checkout; (2) the outcome being a split between payment methods rather than a conversion or abandonment, so the measurement must distinguish taking the provider's option from converting at all; (3) merchant consent for variation, which is a commercial conversation rather than a technical capability; (4) effects that differ by basket value and category, requiring segmented results rather than a single finding; and (5) an eligibility dimension, since showing an offer to someone who will be declined is worse than not showing it and no conventional checkout element has that property.

## Target Customer
Provider commercial and product teams, merchants and commerce platforms, and conversion optimisation practitioners for whom cross-merchant experimentation is unusual territory.

## Impact If Solved
Two decades of checkout experimentation exists and this element is placed from a guide. Running one experiment across thousands of merchant sites is statistical power no individual merchant has, and eligibility gives the element a property no conventional checkout component has.

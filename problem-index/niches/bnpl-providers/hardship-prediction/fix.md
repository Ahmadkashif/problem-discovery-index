# Help That Requires Asking For It

**Niche:** [[niches/bnpl-providers/hardship-prediction/profile|Hardship Prediction]]
**Industry:** [[industries/bnpl-providers|BNPL Providers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The payment plan, the pause and the fee waiver all exist, and every one of them requires the consumer to telephone and explain that they are in financial difficulty.
**Tags:** #compliance #workflow-orchestration #evaluation-metrics #automation #quick-win #worker-facing #descriptive-statistics #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to identify a consumer heading into difficulty before the missed payment rather than after it — and whoever does that intervenes while it still helps and while the regulator is still watching rather than acting.

## The Problem
The provider has forbearance options. They are real and they help. Accessing any of them requires the consumer to find the right contact route, initiate a conversation, and explain that they cannot pay — which is a difficult thing to do, is disproportionately hard for the people who most need it, and happens after the missed payment and the fee because until then there is nothing to report. The help exists, is genuinely offered, and is gated behind an act of self-disclosure that filters out a large share of the people it was designed for.

## Why It's Still Broken
Forbearance was designed as a response to a request because that is how lending processes work, and nobody asked whether the request is a barrier — the process shape was inherited and the filtering effect it produces is invisible to the provider, who sees only the people who called. Proactive offers feel like an admission or an intrusion. Verifying hardship without a disclosure is awkward. And the people filtered out simply default, which looks like a credit outcome rather than a process failure.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Offer before being asked. Present the forbearance options proactively to consumers on the difficulty trajectory, which is the fix and reaches the people the disclosure requirement filters out. Make the options visible and self-serve in the app, so taking a pause does not require a conversation at all — this is the single largest accessibility improvement and requires no prediction. Remove the need to explain, since requiring a justification is the barrier and the provider does not need the reason to move a payment date. Offer at the moment of difficulty rather than after the failure, connecting to the prediction work. Word the offer carefully, since this is the most sensitive communication the provider sends and a clumsy version does harm. Make the first use frictionless and later ones supported, which balances accessibility against the small number who would misuse it. Waive fees where the provider's own scheduling contributed, without requiring the consumer to argue. Signpost debt advice plainly, which is both appropriate and increasingly expected. Measure uptake against predicted need, which reveals the size of the filtered-out population and is the number that justifies the change. And report outcomes for consumers offered forbearance proactively against those who asked, because if the proactive group does better the disclosure requirement is costing people money and the sector should say so.

## Who Feels the Pain
Consumers who needed help and could not bring themselves to ask for it; agents meeting people only after the damage; and providers whose default rate includes people their own process could have caught.

## Impact If Fixed
The process shape was inherited from lending and the filtering effect is invisible because the provider sees only the people who called. Making the options self-serve in the app, with no explanation required, reaches the filtered-out population and needs no prediction at all.

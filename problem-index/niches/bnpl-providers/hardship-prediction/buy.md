**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to identify a consumer heading into difficulty before the missed payment rather than after it — and whoever does that intervenes while it still helps and while the regulator is still watching rather than acting.

## The Problem
Retail banking has developed early warning practice for customers in or approaching financial difficulty, substantially because regulators required it. Indicators are defined from account behaviour, thresholds trigger review, outreach is structured, forbearance options are prepared, and vulnerable customer treatment is a documented obligation. The practice is imperfect and it exists, it is audited, and the expectations continue to tighten. Instalment providers serve a population with more financial fragility than a typical banking book and largely wait for disclosure.

## What Already Exists
Early warning indicator frameworks; behavioural triggers from account activity; structured outreach and forbearance pathways; vulnerable customer policies and training; and regulatory reporting on difficulty identification.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a provider who sees only part of the consumer's position and has a short relationship. It requires: (1) signals from instalment repayment behaviour rather than from a current account, which is a narrower window but a more frequent one — the provider sees a payment decision every fortnight where a bank sees a balance, and the higher frequency partly compensates for the narrower view; (2) a consumer whose difficulty is frequently driven by obligations at other providers, which the bank's view of a main account would partly capture and this provider's cannot; (3) a short relationship with limited history, so the model must work on thin behavioural files as well as thin credit files; (4) interventions that are product changes rather than account restructuring, since the instrument is a payment schedule; and (5) a regulatory expectation that is forming rather than settled, which is an argument for moving early rather than complying late.

## Target Customer
Collections and compliance leadership at instalment providers, vulnerable customer specialists, and regulators developing expectations for the sector.

## Impact If Solved
Banking built this because regulators required it and the sector serves a more fragile population while waiting for a phone call. A payment decision every fortnight is a narrower but far more frequent window than a bank's balance view, which partly compensates.

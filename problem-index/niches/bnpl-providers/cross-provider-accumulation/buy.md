## The Problem
Credit reporting exists precisely because lenders individually cannot see a borrower's total obligations and collectively suffer when they cannot. The bureaus, the furnishing conventions, the data standards, the dispute mechanisms and the regulatory framework around them were built to solve this exact collective action problem, and they work. A new lending sector grew up alongside that infrastructure, furnishes into it partially and inconsistently, and has reproduced the problem the infrastructure was created to solve.

## What Already Exists
Credit bureau infrastructure and furnishing standards; reciprocity principles governing access; dispute and correction mechanisms; regulatory frameworks around accuracy and use; and decades of operational practice.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to obligations that are created and discharged faster than the reporting cycle. It requires: (1) timeliness measured in hours rather than in the monthly cycles the infrastructure was built around, since the accumulation happens in an afternoon and monthly reporting cannot address it — this timing mismatch is the substantive gap and it is what makes existing infrastructure insufficient rather than merely unused; (2) a data format representing a six-week four-payment obligation, which the existing schemas fit poorly; (3) obligations that are frequently discharged before they would ever appear, so the reporting must capture the exposure during its life rather than its history; (4) a reciprocity model appropriate to a sector where the data's competitive value differs from traditional lending; and (5) consumer-facing visibility as a design goal, which the bureau model treats as a right of access rather than as a product.

## Target Customer
The credit bureaus, sector industry bodies, regulators, and the data infrastructure operators who could build the timely layer the bureaus do not.

## Impact If Solved
Credit reporting exists to solve exactly this collective action problem and a new sector reproduced it alongside working infrastructure. A timing mismatch measured in hours against monthly cycles is what makes the existing bureaus insufficient rather than merely unused.

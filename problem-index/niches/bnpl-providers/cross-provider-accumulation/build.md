# Five Plans on the Same Afternoon

**Niche:** [[niches/bnpl-providers/cross-provider-accumulation/profile|Cross-Provider Accumulation]]
**Industry:** [[industries/bnpl-providers|BNPL Providers]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A consumer can take six instalment plans from six providers in an afternoon and no participant, including all of them together, can see it.
**Tags:** #data-integration #compliance #graph-theory #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #workflow-orchestration #revenue-impact #descriptive-statistics
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to make concurrent obligations across providers visible to somebody before the regulator makes it visible to everyone — and whoever assembles that sharing defines the sector's terms rather than receiving them.

## The Problem
Each provider's decision is reasonable. The consumer's total position is not. Six providers each approve a modest instalment plan, each believing it to be manageable, and the consumer has committed a substantial share of their next month's income across obligations none of the lenders can see and which the bureaus will report, if at all, after the risk has already materialised. The sector's own structure produces this, every participant would prefer it did not, and no individual participant can fix it. It is the clearest collective action problem in consumer finance and the one most likely to be resolved by legislation.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
A provider that furnishes alone exposes its own customers to competitors' targeting while gaining nothing, which is a first-mover penalty that keeps everyone still — the structure rewards exactly the behaviour that produces the risk. Bureau formats were built for monthly-reported longer-term credit. Real-time sharing raises competition and privacy questions nobody has worked through. And the sector's growth has outpaced its governance.

## What to Build
Build the sharing mechanism the sector cannot build individually. Establish a real-time or near-real-time obligation registry, which is the core and must operate on a timescale that matches how the accumulation happens — a monthly reporting cycle does not address an afternoon. Design it so that no provider gains a competitive advantage from participation, which is the condition under which collective action becomes possible and is a governance design problem rather than a technical one. Include the short instalment products, since those are where accumulation concentrates and where the current reporting gap sits exactly. Work with the bureaus where they can adapt and around them where they cannot, since the infrastructure question is about timeliness and format rather than about the bureaus' willingness. Limit the data shared to what the purpose requires, because a registry that becomes a marketing dataset will not survive scrutiny and would deserve not to. Give the consumer visibility of their own obligations through it, connecting to that niche, which is both the strongest fairness argument and a genuine product. Produce sector-level statistics on accumulation, which would inform the regulatory conversation with real evidence and is currently entirely absent. Move ahead of mandate, since the mechanism will be required and a sector-designed version will fit the product better than an imposed one. Handle the privacy and competition law questions explicitly with counsel and regulators rather than treating them as blockers. And measure the risk the registry reveals, because the sector's central number is currently unknown to everyone including the sector.

## Target Customer
Sector leadership and industry bodies, the credit bureaus, regulators forming a view, and the consortium operators who could run the mechanism.

## Impact If Built
The structure rewards exactly the behaviour that produces the risk, since furnishing alone is a first-mover penalty. A near-real-time registry designed so participation confers no competitive advantage is a governance design problem, and it is the only thing that addresses an accumulation that happens in an afternoon.

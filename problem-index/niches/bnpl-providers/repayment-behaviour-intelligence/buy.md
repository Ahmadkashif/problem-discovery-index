## The Problem
Consumer credit research is a real field. Economists and credit researchers study repayment behaviour, credit access, the effects of credit expansion on households, and the predictive value of alternative signals, using bureau panels, survey data and occasional lender datasets. The methods are developed and the standards are high. The largest new source of evidence about thin-file consumers sits inside operating companies that use it for scoring and publish nothing, which means the field's understanding of this population is being formed without the best available data.

## What Already Exists
Consumer credit research methodology; bureau panel analysis; causal designs around credit access changes; household finance survey linkage; and peer review standards for credit research.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a proprietary operational dataset inside a commercial firm. It requires: (1) research access arrangements that protect commercial and personal confidentiality while permitting rigorous work, which is a governance design problem and is the practical obstacle rather than any methodological one; (2) linkage to outcomes outside the provider's own product, since the interesting questions are about the consumer's broader financial life; (3) causal identification in an observational dataset where the provider's own approval decisions created the sample — the selection is severe and must be addressed rather than assumed away; (4) a commercial firm's timelines against academic ones, which requires a working arrangement neither side is used to; and (5) findings that may be commercially or politically uncomfortable, which the arrangement must anticipate rather than discover.

## Target Customer
Provider data and policy leadership, academic credit researchers, regulators, and the research institutions who could host the collaboration.

## Impact If Solved
The field's understanding of this population is being formed without the best available data because that data sits inside firms that do not publish. Research access arrangements are a governance design problem, and the provider's own approval decisions created a selection that must be addressed rather than assumed away.

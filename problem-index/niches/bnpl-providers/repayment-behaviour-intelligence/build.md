# New Empirical Ground, Used to Score the Next Approval

**Niche:** [[niches/bnpl-providers/repayment-behaviour-intelligence/profile|Repayment Behaviour Intelligence]]
**Industry:** [[industries/bnpl-providers|BNPL Providers]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** The sector built one of the largest repayment datasets on thin-file consumers in existence and has used it almost entirely to score its own next approval.
**Tags:** #survival-analysis #gradient-boosting #causal-inference #confidence-intervals #evaluation-metrics #hypothesis-testing #descriptive-statistics #bayesian-inference
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to establish what small-instalment repayment behaviour actually predicts about a person's capacity — and whoever answers that owns genuinely new empirical ground about a population the bureaus cannot describe.

## The Problem
Tens of millions of people, many with no usable credit history, making small frequent payment decisions with fully observed outcomes. That is a dataset of a kind that has not previously existed, about a population the conventional credit system describes as unscoreable, generated in under a decade. The sector uses it to decide whether to approve the next two-hundred-pound purchase. The questions it could answer — what completing four instalments actually says about a person's capacity, how that compares with what a bureau score would predict, whether this behaviour is a better foundation for credit access than the absence of a file — are open, answerable and unasked.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The data is a commercial asset used for an immediate commercial purpose, and research is not what an operating credit function is staffed for — the use was determined by the department that owns it. Answering the broader question requires outcomes beyond the provider's own product, which means linking to external data. Publishing findings would inform competitors. And the sector has been growing too fast to do research.

## What to Build
Treat the dataset as the research asset it is. Study what instalment repayment behaviour predicts about subsequent credit outcomes, by linking to bureau or other outcome data where permitted, which is the core question and has an answer nobody has established. Compare the predictive power of this behaviour against bureau scores for the same people, which is the finding that would matter most and would determine whether this data deserves a place in the wider credit system. Characterise the population properly, since the sector's customers are described anecdotally and a rigorous description would change several conversations including the regulatory one. Study the accumulation effect, which requires the sharing mechanism and is the sector's most important unanswered question about its own customers. Distinguish the products, since pay-in-four and longer instalments attract different people and behave differently. Examine the trajectory, because whether repeat use is a sign of healthy cash-flow management or of deepening difficulty is an empirical question with a real answer and is currently argued on both sides without evidence. Validate against outcomes rather than against bureau agreement, which is the fix note's subject. Publish, since credibility with regulators and with the credit system is worth more to the sector than the competitive value of withholding, and a sector facing scrutiny with published evidence is in a different position from one facing it with assertions. Partner with academic researchers, who will do this properly and whose independence is part of the value. And support financial inclusion claims with evidence, because the sector makes them and has not tested them.

## Target Customer
Credit and data leadership at instalment providers, the bureaus and credit system participants, regulators, and researchers studying thin-file credit.

## Impact If Built
The use of the dataset was determined by the department that owns it, which is an operating credit function rather than a research one. Establishing what instalment repayment predicts relative to a bureau score is genuinely new ground about a population the credit system calls unscoreable.

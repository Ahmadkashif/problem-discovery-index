# Hybrid & Non-Hyperscaler Estates

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/cloud-cost-management|Cloud Cost Management]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor here is fighting to produce one total cost for a workload that spans cloud, colocation, licensed software and owned hardware — and whoever does that takes the enterprise, because no tool currently sees more than a fraction of it.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$380M US attributable to hybrid and non-cloud technology cost management
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~13% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Very Low — the tooling stops at the cloud bill
**Target Buyer:** Enterprise technology finance and infrastructure leadership
**Automation Potential:** Medium-High — the data exists across several systems and none of it is joined

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The category is named for cloud and most enterprises are not only cloud. A workload's real cost includes the cloud bill, and also the colocation contract, the owned hardware being depreciated, the database and middleware licences, the observability and security software attached to it, the network circuits, and the people operating it. No product in the category sees more than the first. The consequences are specific: migration business cases compare a complete cloud cost against an incomplete on-premise one and are wrong in a predictable direction; repatriation decisions are made on the same faulty comparison in the opposite direction; and nobody can answer what a service costs in total, which is the question the business asks. This is underdigitised because each component lives in a different system — asset management, procurement, contract management, licensing, the general ledger — and nobody has joined them.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Cloud cost tools for the cloud portion; IT asset management for hardware; software asset management for licences; procurement and contract systems for the rest; and spreadsheets to combine them. The gaps: no product produces a total cost of ownership per service, which is the number every architectural decision depends on; depreciation, licence and contract costs are annual or monthly figures that must be allocated to workloads and are not; migration and repatriation cases are built by hand with inconsistent assumptions; and software licence cost in particular is frequently larger than the infrastructure it runs on and is invisible in every infrastructure cost view.

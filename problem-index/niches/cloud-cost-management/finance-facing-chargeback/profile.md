# Finance-Facing Allocation & Chargeback

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/cloud-cost-management|Cloud Cost Management]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor here is fighting to produce a cloud allocation that reconciles to the invoice, survives audit and forecasts accurately enough to plan against — and whoever does that takes the finance account, because a number that does not add up is worse than no number.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$640M US finance-facing cloud financial management
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~21% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** High — this is the half the category has served
**Target Buyer:** Finance, financial planning and technology business management
**Automation Potential:** High for forecasting and variance; allocation accuracy is the binding constraint

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Finance needs cloud spend to behave like every other cost line: allocated to a cost centre, reconciled to the invoice, forecast for the plan, and explained when it varies. Cloud spend resists all four. It is consumption-based and therefore genuinely variable. It arrives with a discount structure — commitments, negotiated rates, credits — that makes the effective rate different from the list rate and different again per account. It is incurred by engineers who do not see it and is not approved in advance. And it must be accrued before the invoice arrives. The contest here is accuracy and defensibility: whether the allocation reconciles exactly, whether the forecast is close enough to plan against, and whether a variance can be explained to an executive in one sentence. These are unglamorous requirements and they are the ones this buyer is actually judged on.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Allocation and chargeback features, amortisation of commitments, budget and alert mechanisms, forecasting by extrapolation, and integrations to financial systems. The gaps: forecasts are extrapolations of the recent trend, which is the least informative predictor available for a business that knows its own plans; variance explanation is a list of the largest movers rather than an account of why; discount and commitment amortisation is complex enough that finance frequently cannot reconcile the tool's number to the invoice; unallocated spend undermines chargeback credibility; and accruals before invoice are estimated crudely.

# Accountable for a Number They Cannot Change

**Niche:** [[niches/cloud-cost-management/the-finops-practitioner/profile|The FinOps Practitioner]]
**Industry:** [[industries/cloud-cost-management|Cloud Cost Management]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** FinOps is measured on total cloud spend, which only engineers can change, so the function is held to an outcome it has no authority over and can demonstrate no value.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #hypothesis-testing #confidence-intervals #evaluation-metrics #causal-inference #worker-facing #quick-win #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor that takes this seriously is fighting to remove the chasing and the variance archaeology from a FinOps practitioner's week — and whoever does that takes the function, because those two activities are most of what it currently does.

## The Problem
The cloud bill rose this year because the business grew, launched two products and acquired a company. The FinOps function is asked why costs are up. Their actual achievements — a commitment strategy that improved the effective rate, a set of realised optimisations, an attribution improvement that let three teams see their spend for the first time — are invisible in a total that went up for reasons they do not control. Within two years the function is regarded as ineffective, which is both unfair and the predictable result of measuring it on the wrong number.

## Why It's Still Broken
Total spend is the number the executive sees, so it becomes the metric by default, and nobody has defined an alternative. Isolating the function's contribution requires a counterfactual — what spend would have been without their work — which nobody constructs. The realised savings from an optimisation disappear into a growing total and become invisible within a month. And the practitioner community's maturity model describes capability rather than supplying an effectiveness measure the function can be judged on.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Measure the function on what it controls. Realised savings tracked individually and cumulatively, with each optimisation's effect measured against the counterfactual of the previous run-rate, carried forward so that a saving achieved in March is still visible in November rather than being absorbed into growth. Effective rate improvement, which is the commitment and discount work and is entirely within the function's control and directly measurable. Efficiency measures rather than absolute ones: cost per unit of business output, which separates growth from waste and is the honest framing for a growing organisation. Coverage and attribution quality, since making spend visible is a real achievement and is currently unrewarded. Variance explanation latency, which is a service-quality measure of the function's core output. And decompose the total explicitly into growth, price, efficiency and waste, so the executive conversation distinguishes a bill rising because the business grew from one rising because nobody is managing it — which is the distinction the whole function exists to make and which its own headline metric obscures.

## Who Feels the Pain
FinOps practitioners judged on an outcome they cannot control; executives who cannot tell whether a rising bill is a problem; and organisations that disband a function whose value they never measured.

## Impact If Fixed
Carrying realised savings forward against a counterfactual is straightforward and makes the function's contribution visible in a growing total. Decomposing the total into growth, price, efficiency and waste is the distinction the function exists to make and is absent from its own reporting.

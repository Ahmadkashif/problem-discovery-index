# Workflow Automation the Practice Has Not Adopted

**Industry:** [[industries/cloud-cost-management|Cloud Cost Management]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Routing a task to an owner, tracking it and escalating is what every workflow and ticketing product does, and FinOps follow-up runs on a spreadsheet and a reminder in a calendar.

**Contested on:** Every serious competitor that takes this seriously is fighting to remove the chasing and the variance archaeology from a FinOps practitioner's week — and whoever does that takes the function, because those two activities are most of what it currently does.

## The Problem
The practitioner has a list of things that need doing by other people: tag these resources, review this recommendation, explain this variance, confirm this commitment. Assigning work to an owner with a due date and an escalation path is exactly what workflow and ticketing systems do, at enormous scale, in every other function. FinOps does it with a spreadsheet, a recurring meeting and a series of polite messages.

## What Already Exists
Workflow and ticketing platforms with assignment, due dates and escalation; approval and review workflows; integration into the chat and issue tools engineers already use; automated reminder and escalation logic; and change-point detection for the variance half. All commodity.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a function with responsibility and no authority. It requires: (1) delivery into the engineer's existing tools rather than into a cost portal, since the recipient will not adopt a new system for somebody else's priority — this is the difference between a request that gets actioned and one that does not; (2) requests that are specific and small, because a task saying "improve your tagging" is not actionable and one saying "these four resources need an owner, here is the suggested value, confirm or correct" is; (3) suggested answers included, since confirming an inference takes seconds and supplying information takes a meeting, and this single design choice determines the response rate; (4) escalation that is evidence-based rather than social, so a non-response escalates with the cost consequence attached rather than as a complaint; and (5) closure tracking with the saving realised, because the practitioner's credibility depends on demonstrating that the requests led somewhere.

## Target Customer
FinOps functions, cost management vendors, and the workflow and developer platform vendors into whose tools these requests should be delivered.

## Impact If Solved
Commodity workflow machinery addresses the chasing that consumes a practitioner's week, and the adaptation is entirely about delivering into the engineer's own tools with a suggested answer attached. That combination is what turns a request into a response.

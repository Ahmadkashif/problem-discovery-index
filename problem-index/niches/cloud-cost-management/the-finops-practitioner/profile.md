# The FinOps Practitioner

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/cloud-cost-management|Cloud Cost Management]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor that takes this seriously is fighting to remove the chasing and the variance archaeology from a FinOps practitioner's week — and whoever does that takes the function, because those two activities are most of what it currently does.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$190M US attributable to FinOps practice tooling and workflow
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~6% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Low — the practice runs on spreadsheets and persuasion
**Target Buyer:** FinOps and cloud economics leadership; the beneficiary is the practitioner
**Automation Potential:** Very High — both the chasing and the investigation are automatable

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The FinOps practitioner sits between finance and engineering and is the human integration layer that the products do not provide. Their week is spent asking engineering teams to tag resources, explaining variances they cannot investigate themselves, assembling reports from several tools, chasing teams about recommendations nobody has acted on, and translating between two functions that do not share a vocabulary. Almost none of this is the analysis the role was created for. The position is also structurally weak: they are accountable for a number they cannot change, since only engineers can change spend, and they have persuasion rather than authority. This is a distinct contested surface because the specific activities consuming the week — tag chasing, variance investigation, report assembly, follow-up — are individually automatable and collectively constitute the job.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Cost platforms with reporting and alerting, tagging compliance dashboards, spreadsheets for everything the platforms do not do, and a practitioner community with a maturity framework. The gaps: tagging compliance is reported and the chasing is manual, which is the largest single time consumer; variance investigation requires context from engineering that the practitioner must request rather than obtain; reporting is assembled by hand from several sources because no tool covers the whole estate; recommendation follow-up is a spreadsheet of who has not acted; and the practitioner's own effectiveness is unmeasured, so the function is judged on total spend, which it does not control.

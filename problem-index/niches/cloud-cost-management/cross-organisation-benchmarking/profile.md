# Cross-Organisation Benchmarking

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/cloud-cost-management|Cloud Cost Management]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor that gets here is fighting to tell a customer what a workload of this shape should cost and what comparable organisations actually pay — and whoever does that holds the only asset in this category a competitor cannot obtain from the same billing export.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$290M US, almost entirely latent
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~10% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** None — the corpus is unused
**Target Buyer:** The vendors themselves; secondarily cloud economics and procurement
**Automation Potential:** Very High — the data is structured, comparable and already ingested

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
These vendors sit across the infrastructure spend of thousands of organisations and observe what everyone actually runs: at what utilisation, on which instance families, with which commitment coverage, at what effective rate after discounts, for workloads of recognisable shapes. That is a cross-organisational benchmark nobody else holds and no customer can assemble. It answers the questions customers ask constantly and cannot currently answer — is our effective rate good, is this workload expensive for what it does, what do organisations of our size and shape spend on observability or data transfer or storage, is our commitment coverage normal. The category instead resells the customer's own bill back to them in a nicer interface, which is precisely why every product in it looks the same: they are all built on an input every competitor also has.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Occasional published industry surveys, practitioner community benchmarks based on self-reported data, and negotiated-rate intelligence from resellers and consultancies. The gaps: no vendor has operationalised its own corpus, so the one differentiating asset in the category is idle; effective-rate benchmarking in particular would tell customers whether their negotiated agreement is competitive, which is the question procurement most wants answered and no independent party will address; workload-shape comparison — what should a workload like this cost — is attempted nowhere; and the governance design that would make cross-customer analysis acceptable has not been articulated by anyone, so the topic is avoided rather than resolved.

# The Benchmarking Product Pattern, Proven Elsewhere

**Niche:** [[niches/cloud-cost-management/cross-organisation-benchmarking/profile|Cross-Organisation Benchmarking]]
**Industry:** [[industries/cloud-cost-management|Cloud Cost Management]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Compensation, procurement pricing and payroll benchmarking all pool customer data under established governance and sell the comparison back, and cloud cost vendors sit on a richer corpus and do not.
**Tags:** #k-means-clustering #dimensionality-reduction #descriptive-statistics #bayesian-inference #hypothesis-testing #confidence-intervals #compliance #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor that gets here is fighting to tell a customer what a workload of this shape should cost and what comparable organisations actually pay — and whoever does that holds the only asset in this category a competitor cannot obtain from the same billing export.

## The Problem
The contribute-to-receive benchmarking model is proven repeatedly: compensation surveys, procurement price benchmarks, payroll and benefits comparisons, advertising rate benchmarks. Each pools sensitive customer data under established norms about aggregation thresholds and anonymisation, and sells the comparison back as the primary product. Cloud cost vendors hold a corpus that is larger, more structured and updated continuously, and sell a rendering of each customer's own bill.

## What Already Exists
The benchmarking product pattern with its governance conventions; statistical disclosure control with a formal literature; hierarchical estimation for small cohorts; clustering and dimensionality reduction for defining comparable groups; and differential privacy where stronger guarantees are required. All mature and all proven commercially in adjacent markets.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to infrastructure workloads and negotiated rates. It requires: (1) a workload similarity model, since the cohort must be defined by what the workload does rather than by the customer's industry — which is the piece that has no analogue in compensation benchmarking and is the foundational work here; (2) disclosure control for effective rates in particular, because a rate reveals a negotiated agreement and small cohorts could identify a specific customer's discount, which is commercially sensitive in a way compensation data is not; (3) hierarchical estimation, since the useful cohorts are narrow — this workload shape, this scale, this region — and shrinkage toward broader groups is what makes narrow reporting possible; (4) normalisation across providers and over time, since instance families and prices change and a benchmark comparing across generations is misleading; and (5) an explicit reciprocity model, because customers will ask what they contribute and what they receive, and a clear answer is the condition of participation.

## Target Customer
Cost management vendors with large installed bases, procurement and sourcing advisory firms, and the benchmarking vendors already running this model in adjacent categories.

## Impact If Solved
A commercially proven pattern maps onto a richer corpus than any of its existing applications and has not been attempted. Workload similarity is the novel piece, and disclosure control on negotiated rates is the constraint that determines what can be published.

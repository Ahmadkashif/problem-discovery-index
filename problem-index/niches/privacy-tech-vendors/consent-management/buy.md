**Contested on:** Whether the consent record reflects an informed choice, or an interface optimised until enough people clicked accept.

## The Problem

Informed consent is not a concept digital privacy invented. Medical research developed it over decades, in response to serious failures, and built a substantial apparatus around it: disclosure requirements about what the participant will undergo, comprehension assessment to check they understood, voluntariness requirements ensuring the choice is free of coercion, ethics committee review of the consent materials, and readability standards for the documents.

Crucially, medical research learned that a signature is not consent. Studies repeatedly found participants signing forms they did not understand, which led to comprehension testing — asking participants to state in their own words what they had agreed to — becoming standard practice in many settings.

Digital consent adopted the signature and none of the rest. There is a disclosure, of sorts, in a layered panel most people never open. There is no comprehension assessment. Voluntariness is compromised by designs that make acceptance dramatically easier than refusal. There is no independent review of the consent materials. And readability standards are not applied to privacy notices that routinely require advanced reading levels.

## What Already Exists

Research ethics: institutional review board processes; informed consent documentation standards; comprehension assessment instruments, several of them validated and published; readability requirements and plain-language guidance; the extensive literature on what participants actually understand from consent forms, which is consistently less than researchers assume.

Clinical practice: teach-back methods, where the patient states their understanding back; decision aids for complex choices; and shared decision-making frameworks.

Behavioural science: the dark patterns literature, which documents how interface design shapes choice, and the consumer protection work that has drawn on it.

Digital consent: consent management platforms with configurable interfaces, purpose granularity and record-keeping at very large scale.

## The Customization Gap

**Comprehension assessment has no digital equivalent and easily could.** The instruments exist and are validated. Administering a short comprehension check to a sample of users after consent is entirely feasible on the web, would produce the measure nobody has, and has never been tried at scale.

**Scale changes the method, not the principle.** Research consent involves a conversation; web consent involves millions of interactions and seconds of attention. That argues for sampling and for telemetry-based proxies validated against direct assessment, not for abandoning the requirement.

**Voluntariness is where the digital case is weakest.** Research ethics takes coercion seriously and examines whether the participant could genuinely decline. A banner where accepting is one click and rejecting is four, or where declining degrades the service, would not pass a voluntariness review.

**Independent review of consent materials is entirely absent.** Ethics committees review consent documents before use. Digital consent interfaces are reviewed by the customer's own marketing and legal functions, whose interests are not aligned with the person consenting.

**Readability standards are applied in one field and not the other.** Research consent documents are held to reading-level requirements. Privacy notices commonly are not, and the disparity is not defensible on any principled ground.

**The bundling problem has a direct analogue.** Research ethics addressed consent to multiple procedures and concluded they should be separable. Purpose bundling in digital consent is the same issue and is handled far less rigorously.

## Target Customer

The consent platforms, for whom comprehension assessment and readability tooling would be a genuine differentiator as regulatory attention on consent quality increases.

Privacy counsel at organisations that want a defensible position rather than a maximal acceptance rate, which is a growing group as enforcement decisions accumulate.

Regulators and academic researchers as collaborators, since a validated digital comprehension instrument would be a public good and would likely be adopted as a reference.

## Impact If Solved

Seventy years of hard-won practice about what consent actually requires reaches a domain that borrowed the word and left the substance behind.

Comprehension assessment at sample scale is achievable now, uses validated instruments, and would produce the first real evidence about whether digital consent means anything.

And applying voluntariness and readability standards — both well established, both currently ignored — would address the two dimensions on which digital consent is most obviously weakest, without requiring any new research at all.

# Buy: Environmental and Safety Assessment Practice

**Niche:** Assessments & Documentation
**Industry:** [[industries/privacy-tech-vendors|Privacy Tech Vendors]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Environmental impact assessment learned decades ago that an assessment without monitoring and enforcement of its conditions is a document, and privacy impact assessment has not.
**Tags:** #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #compliance #workflow-orchestration #hypothesis-testing #change-point-detection
**Contested on:** Whether an impact assessment changes what gets built, or documents a decision already taken.

## The Problem

Impact assessment as a regulatory instrument was developed for the environment, and privacy borrowed the concept without borrowing what makes it work.

Environmental impact assessment has a mandatory trigger based on project characteristics rather than on someone choosing to initiate one. It requires assessment before consent, so the assessment can affect whether and how the project proceeds. It produces conditions attached to the permission. Those conditions are monitored during and after construction. Non-compliance has enforcement consequences. And the assessment is subject to public scrutiny, which is a powerful quality mechanism.

Privacy impact assessment has the form and none of the apparatus. The trigger depends on someone recognising it applies. The assessment typically occurs after the design is fixed. The mitigations are commitments in a document with no monitoring. There is no enforcement of the conditions the organisation set itself. And nobody outside the organisation sees it.

The same drift happened in environmental practice early on and was corrected by making conditions enforceable and monitoring mandatory. Privacy has not made that correction.

## What Already Exists

Environmental impact assessment: statutory triggers by project type and scale, scoping, alternatives analysis, mitigation hierarchy, conditions of consent, monitoring requirements, and enforcement.

Health and safety: risk assessment practice with the hierarchy of controls — eliminate, substitute, engineer, administer, protect — which is a genuinely useful framework for reasoning about mitigations and is unknown in privacy.

Clinical and financial: ethics committee review before a study proceeds; model risk management with validation and ongoing monitoring.

Privacy: assessment templates and workflow in the privacy platforms, with regulatory guidance on when assessments are required.

## The Customization Gap

**The trigger should be objective, not discretionary.** Environmental triggers are defined by project characteristics. Privacy triggers depend on someone judging that the processing is high risk, which means the processing nobody examined is never assessed. Objective triggers derived from observed characteristics — new data category, new third party, new automated decision, scale threshold — would close this.

**Conditions must be enforceable to matter.** Environmental consent conditions are binding and monitored. Privacy mitigations are self-imposed commitments with no monitoring, which is the gap that makes assessments documentary.

**Timing determines whether it is a design instrument.** Assessment before consent is what gives environmental assessment its force. Privacy assessment before build would do the same and almost never happens.

**The mitigation hierarchy is missing and would help.** Safety practice ranks controls by robustness: eliminate the hazard, then substitute, then engineer it out, then rely on procedure. Privacy mitigations are listed without this ranking, so procedural safeguards are treated as equivalent to not collecting the data — which they are not.

**Alternatives analysis is absent.** Environmental assessment requires considering alternatives including doing nothing. Privacy assessment evaluates the proposed processing as given, which removes the most useful question: is there a less intrusive way to achieve this.

**Public scrutiny is the quality mechanism privacy lacks.** Environmental assessments are published and contestable. Privacy assessments are internal, so their quality is unexamined by anyone with an interest in it.

## Target Customer

Privacy counsel and programme leadership, for whom the mitigation hierarchy and alternatives analysis are immediately usable improvements to how assessments are conducted.

The privacy platforms, for whom objective triggering and condition monitoring are product features borrowed from a mature regulatory tradition.

Regulators as the eventual audience, since the environmental precedent for making conditions monitorable and enforceable is directly available and would strengthen an instrument that is currently weak.

## Impact If Solved

An instrument borrowed from environmental practice would acquire the parts that made it work there — objective triggers, binding conditions and monitoring.

The mitigation hierarchy alone would improve assessment quality substantially, by making it visible when an organisation has chosen a procedural safeguard where an engineering one was available.

And alternatives analysis would restore the question the instrument exists to ask: whether the processing needs to happen in this form at all, which the current practice reliably skips.

# Buy: Assurance Practice From Audit and Finance

**Niche:** The Privacy Officer
**Industry:** [[industries/privacy-tech-vendors|Privacy Tech Vendors]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Finance built a complete apparatus for an individual who must attest to something they cannot personally verify, and privacy asks its officer to sign with none of it.
**Tags:** #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #hypothesis-testing #compliance #workflow-orchestration #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Whether the person who signs the processing record has any means of establishing that it is accurate.

## The Problem

A chief financial officer attests to the accuracy of financial statements they did not personally prepare, about transactions they did not personally see, in an organisation too large for them to inspect. This is structurally identical to the privacy officer's position and finance solved it.

The solution is an apparatus: internal controls over reporting with documented design and tested operation, internal audit providing independent assurance, external audit providing a further independent opinion, sub-certification where each business unit head attests to their own area, a materiality framework defining what matters, and a documented basis on which the attestation rests.

The privacy officer has none of it. No internal controls framework over the accuracy of the record. No independent testing. No sub-certification from system owners. No materiality concept. No documented basis. They sign because signing is required, on the strength of a survey.

The apparatus is not exotic. It is standard practice in a neighbouring function inside the same organisations.

## What Already Exists

Financial control frameworks: internal control over financial reporting, with control design documentation, operating effectiveness testing and deficiency classification.

Sub-certification: the cascade of representations where each unit head attests to their area, giving the signing officer a documented basis composed of attestations from people who do know.

Internal audit: an independent function providing assurance on controls, reporting to the audit committee rather than to management — the organisational answer to the independence problem.

Materiality: a defined threshold for what is significant enough to matter, which lets an attestation be meaningful without being exhaustive.

Audit committee governance: the reporting line that makes independence structural rather than aspirational.

## The Customization Gap

**Sub-certification is the most transferable and most obviously missing.** System owners attesting to the accuracy of their own entries in the record, formally and in writing, would give the privacy officer the documented basis finance takes for granted — and it changes the quality of questionnaire responses considerably when a signature is attached.

**No materiality concept exists in privacy.** Every processing activity is treated as equally requiring accuracy, which is unachievable and therefore achieved nowhere. A materiality framework — by data sensitivity, volume and subject impact — would let the officer concentrate verification where it matters and state a defensible basis.

**Internal audit rarely covers privacy substantively.** The function exists in most large organisations and privacy is seldom in its plan. Adding it would provide independent testing of the record's accuracy at no new institutional cost.

**Controls over the record itself are unframed.** Finance has controls over the reporting process. Privacy has no equivalent concept of controls ensuring the record is accurate and current — the record is treated as an output rather than as something with a control environment.

**Independence lacks the structural support.** Finance solved this with audit committee reporting. Privacy officers are formally independent and report to management, which is the same problem finance had before the governance reforms.

**Deficiency classification is absent.** Finance classifies control failures by severity with defined remediation and disclosure consequences. Privacy findings have no equivalent grading, so everything is either fine or a crisis.

## Target Customer

General counsel and audit committees at organisations where the privacy officer is formally designated, and where the parallel with financial attestation is immediately recognisable to the people who already run the financial version.

Internal audit functions, for whom privacy record accuracy is a natural addition to an existing plan and requires no new institution.

The privacy platforms, for whom sub-certification workflow and a materiality framework are straightforward features that would meaningfully change the officer's position.

## Impact If Solved

Sub-certification gives the signing officer a documented basis composed of attestations from people who actually know, which is the single structural change that most improves their position — and it improves the underlying data quality at the same time.

A materiality framework makes the attestation achievable, by concentrating verification where consequence is concentrated rather than requiring uniform accuracy across everything.

And bringing internal audit into privacy would provide independent testing using an institution that already exists in most organisations and is currently pointed elsewhere.

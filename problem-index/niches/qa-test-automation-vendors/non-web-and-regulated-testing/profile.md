# Non-Web & Regulated Testing

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/qa-test-automation-vendors|QA & Test Automation Vendors]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor here is fighting to bring modern test automation to software that runs on hardware and must produce regulatory evidence — and whoever does that takes those industries, because the category's tooling assumes a browser and they do not have one.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$680M US attributable to embedded, industrial and regulated software testing
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~17% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Very Low — much of it is still manual with a signed record
**Target Buyer:** Medical device, automotive, industrial and financial engineering functions
**Automation Potential:** High for execution and evidence; the constraints are hardware and regulation

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A large body of consequential software is not a web application. It runs on a device, in a vehicle, on a factory floor or inside a regulated financial system, and its testing has requirements the category's tooling does not contemplate: real hardware in the loop, timing and concurrency behaviour that emulation does not reproduce, safety cases requiring traceability from hazard to requirement to test to evidence, and a regulator who will ask for the record. The consequence of an escaped defect is a recall, an injury or an enforcement action rather than a rollback. These organisations therefore test extensively, manually, with a documentation burden that consumes a substantial share of the engineering effort — which is where a modern approach would help most and is served least.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Hardware-in-the-loop rigs, simulation environments, specialist test management tools built around traceability, and a great deal of manual testing with signed records. The gaps: test management tooling in these industries is oriented to producing evidence rather than to running tests, so the automation and the traceability live in different systems; evidence generation is manual even where the test execution is automated, which is the largest single waste; simulation fidelity for timing and concurrency is the determining constraint and is not characterised; the traceability matrix is maintained by hand at considerable cost; and the modern testing ecosystem's tooling is unusable here for reasons of environment rather than of principle.

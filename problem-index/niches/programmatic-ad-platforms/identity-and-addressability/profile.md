# Identity & Addressability

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/programmatic-ad-platforms|Programmatic Ad Platforms]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** This niche is not terminal — assembling a legitimate deterministic graph and performing well with no identifier at all are different contests with different winners, and they are stated separately in the sub-niches below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$5B US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~17% of vendor-layer revenue
**Digital Adoption:** High — heavily invested, structurally unsettled
**Target Buyer:** Platform identity and partnership leadership
**Automation Potential:** High — resolution and modelling are both automatable

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Third-party cookie deprecation, app tracking policy and fragmentation into competing identifiers have made deterministic reach measurement impossible while the industry's pricing models still assume it. Identity is the second-largest concentration of value in the category because everything else — frequency, reach, audience, attribution, suppression — is defined in terms of a person the system can no longer reliably recognise.

It is also **not terminal**. The label names a technology category rather than a contest, and writing the contested statement produces two sentences describing different companies winning. One fight is over resolving a real person across devices and properties with deterministic or near-deterministic confidence, which is an interoperability, consent and partnership contest — won by whoever assembles the largest legitimate graph and gets it adopted on both sides of the market. The other is over performing well with no identifier at all, using page context, cohorts and modelled reach, which is a pure modelling contest won by whoever extracts the most signal from the moment and the aggregate. A competitor can lead decisively on either while being ordinary at the other, and several do. The two contests are stated in the sub-niches.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Third-party cookies in decline, competing universal identifiers, publisher first-party data, browser-provided topic signals, contextual targeting, and probabilistic matching. The gaps: no interoperability between graphs; consent provenance rarely attached to an identifier; reach and frequency reported as though identity were complete; contextual treated as a downgrade rather than a discipline; and no honest accounting of addressable coverage.

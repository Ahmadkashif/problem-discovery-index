# Cohort & Contextual Targeting

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/programmatic-ad-platforms|Programmatic Ad Platforms]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to bid well on an impression where nothing about the person is known — and whoever extracts the most from the page, the moment and the aggregate wins the majority of inventory that is no longer addressable.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$2.2B US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~7% of vendor-layer revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — keyword-era tooling, modern problem
**Target Buyer:** Platform data science and buy-side strategy
**Automation Potential:** Very High — a pure modelling contest

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Most inventory now arrives with no usable identifier, and the question is what can be inferred from everything else: the page, its content and sentiment, the moment, the device, the aggregate behaviour of the property, the position, the weather, the time. This is a modelling contest with a clear scoreboard and no partnership dependency — a competitor can win it alone, in a quarter, with better representations, which makes it a completely different business from assembling a graph. Contextual targeting is still largely sold as keyword blocklists and category taxonomies built in an earlier era, which is roughly where visual search was before embeddings.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Keyword and category contextual taxonomies, brand safety blocklists, browser-provided topic signals, seller-defined audiences, and cohort approximations. The gaps: page understanding limited to keyword matching; no modelling of the moment or the reader's state; brand safety implemented as crude exclusion that destroys reach; no bridge between contextual signals and outcome data; and contextual treated as a downgrade rather than as a discipline.

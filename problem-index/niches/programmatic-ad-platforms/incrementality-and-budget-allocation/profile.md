# Incrementality & Budget Allocation

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/programmatic-ad-platforms|Programmatic Ad Platforms]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to tell an advertiser what their spend actually caused rather than what it was credited with — and whoever does that decides where the category's budget goes.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$2.3B US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~8% of vendor-layer revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Low — attribution everywhere, causality nowhere
**Target Buyer:** Advertiser measurement and finance leadership
**Automation Potential:** Very High — experimentation can be continuous

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Attribution assigns credit; incrementality establishes cause. The two routinely disagree by large margins, and almost every budget decision in the category is made on the first. This is a distinct contest from bid-level outcome feedback: that one asks what an individual impression is worth, this one asks whether the channel, campaign or budget produced anything at all, and answers it with experiments and causal models rather than with joins. It is the measurement that determines how much money enters the category, which makes it the one every incumbent has the least interest in answering honestly.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Last-touch and multi-touch attribution, occasional geo holdout experiments, marketing mix modelling on a quarterly or annual cadence, and platform-reported conversion lift. The gaps: experiments run rarely and manually; mix models too slow and too coarse to allocate with; lift studies run by the party being measured; no continuous experimentation infrastructure; and no reconciliation between the three measurement approaches that all disagree.

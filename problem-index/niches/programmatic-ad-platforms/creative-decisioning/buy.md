## The Problem
Producing and analysing visual and textual content is now a commodity capability: generation of images and video, understanding of what is in them, copy generation with controlled tone and constraints, and brand-consistent stylisation are all available and cheap. Advertising is the most natural application imaginable. What is deployed in programmatic creative is largely a template with slots, filled from a product feed, which is where the category was a decade ago.

## What Already Exists
Image and video generation with style and brand conditioning; multimodal understanding for content extraction; controlled text generation; brand-consistent asset pipelines; and automated resizing and format adaptation.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is from producing assets to deciding between them under measurement. It requires: (1) generation directed by a performance hypothesis rather than by a prompt, since the value is in testing an attribute-level claim rather than in producing another image — this reframing is what turns commodity generation into optimisation; (2) hard brand and legal constraints enforced structurally rather than requested, because a regulated claim or an off-brand asset is a serious incident and probabilistic compliance is not acceptable; (3) format and placement adaptation across dozens of specifications with the message preserved, which is mechanical and enormous and is where most creative production effort currently goes; (4) closed-loop measurement feeding back into generation, which no creative tool provides and which is the entire point; and (5) approval workflow that a brand team will accept, since the organisational barrier is larger than the technical one and ignoring it is why generative pilots stall.

## Target Customer
Creative optimisation vendors, demand-side platforms, agency production and strategy teams, and generative tooling vendors for whom performance-directed creative is unserved.

## Impact If Solved
Commodity generation becomes optimisation only when it is directed by a performance hypothesis rather than a prompt. Structural enforcement of brand and legal constraints, and an approval path brand teams accept, are what decide whether any of it ships.

# Supply Path & Inventory Quality

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/programmatic-ad-platforms|Programmatic Ad Platforms]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to tell a buyer which of the fourteen paths to the same impression is real and which sites exist only to carry advertisements — using that buyer's own spend rather than a generic score.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$4B US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~13% of vendor-layer revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Low — generic scores, per-buyer questions
**Target Buyer:** Buy-side supply quality and trading leadership
**Automation Potential:** High — path analysis is mechanical

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Every buyer needs to know which of the fourteen paths to the same impression is real and which sites exist only to carry ads, and the tools that answer it score the open web generically rather than the buyer's own spend. A meaningful share of programmatic spend reaches made-for-advertising inventory, and a buyer's exposure to it is entirely specific to their own supply paths, their own inclusion lists and their own bidding behaviour — which is exactly what a generic industry score cannot express. This is distinct from the money question, which is about fees and reconciliation; here the question is whether the impression was worth buying at all.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Verification vendors with generic site scores, supply path optimisation reports, inventory quality lists, ads.txt and sellers.json, and periodic audits. The gaps: scoring the open web rather than the buyer's delivery; no path-level economics; no detection of resold and rebrokered inventory; no measurement of what a path costs versus what it delivers; and remediation that is a list update rather than a bidding change.

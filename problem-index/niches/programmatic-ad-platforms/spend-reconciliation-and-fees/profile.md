# Spend Reconciliation & Fees

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/programmatic-ad-platforms|Programmatic Ad Platforms]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to account for the gap between what the buyer paid and what the publisher received, impression by impression — and whoever can enumerate it recovers a quarter of the money for whoever hired them.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$2.5B US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~8% of vendor-layer revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Very Low — spreadsheets and audits
**Target Buyer:** Advertiser finance and procurement
**Automation Potential:** High — the reconciliation is arithmetic over logs

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The reconciliation between what a buyer paid and what a publisher received still routinely loses a quarter of the money to fees nobody can enumerate. This is a distinct contest from inventory quality: the impression may be entirely legitimate, on a real site, seen by a real person, and a quarter of the money still did not reach the publisher and cannot be accounted for. It is a financial transparency problem with a clear beneficiary — the advertiser who commissioned the analysis — and it has resisted resolution because every party between the two ends earns from the opacity.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Platform fee disclosures, agency contracts with audit rights, periodic third-party audits, log-level data access where granted, and spreadsheets. The gaps: log-level data withheld or supplied in incompatible forms; fees taken at layers that do not report; no impression-level join between buy and sell side; audits that are annual and retrospective; and no standard for what a disclosed fee includes.

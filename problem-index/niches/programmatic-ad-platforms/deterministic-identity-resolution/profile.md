# Deterministic Identity Resolution

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/programmatic-ad-platforms|Programmatic Ad Platforms]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to assemble the largest graph of real people that both buyers and publishers will actually adopt and regulators will accept — and whoever does that sets the addressable currency of the market.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$2.6B US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~9% of vendor-layer revenue
**Digital Adoption:** High — many graphs, no consensus
**Target Buyer:** Platform identity leadership and publisher partnerships
**Automation Potential:** High — matching automatable, adoption is commercial

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
This contest is won by adoption and legitimacy rather than by algorithm. Several identifiers are technically adequate; none has the two-sided coverage to function as a currency, because a graph is worth nothing unless both the publisher supplying inventory and the buyer valuing it recognise the same person. Layered on that is consent: an identifier that cannot demonstrate lawful basis and permitted use is unusable by any serious buyer regardless of its coverage. The contest is therefore an interoperability, partnership and governance fight with a matching engine attached, which makes it a different business from extracting signal without identity.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Competing universal identifiers, publisher first-party graphs, hashed email matching, authenticated traffic solutions, and data clean rooms for join operations. The gaps: no bridging between graphs; consent provenance rarely travelling with the identifier; match rates reported as adoption; no shared measurement of graph accuracy; and authenticated inventory that remains a minority of supply.

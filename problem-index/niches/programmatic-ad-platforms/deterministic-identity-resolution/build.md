# Several Currencies, No Money

**Niche:** [[niches/programmatic-ad-platforms/deterministic-identity-resolution/profile|Deterministic Identity Resolution]]
**Industry:** [[industries/programmatic-ad-platforms|Programmatic Ad Platforms]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Several identity graphs are technically adequate and none has two-sided coverage, so the market has multiple currencies and no medium of exchange.
**Tags:** #graph-theory #compliance #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #data-integration #bayesian-inference #revenue-impact #workflow-orchestration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to assemble the largest graph of real people that both buyers and publishers will actually adopt and regulators will accept — and whoever does that sets the addressable currency of the market.

## The Problem
A buyer targets an audience keyed to one identifier. A publisher passes a different one. Neither can use the other's without a translation that loses coverage and confidence at every hop. The result is a market where a substantial share of inventory is nominally addressable and practically not, because the two sides speak different identity languages. Every participant has invested in their own graph, each is individually rational, and the collective outcome is that the industry's core unit of account does not exist. Meanwhile the regulatory position of most of these graphs is asserted rather than demonstrated.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
A graph is a commercial moat and interoperability dissolves it, which is the whole reason this has not resolved and is not going to resolve on its own. Each participant expects to be the winner and waits. Consent provenance is expensive to carry and nobody is checking, so nobody carries it. And the intermediary who would bridge the graphs is trusted by none of the owners.

## What to Build
Build the exchange layer rather than another currency. Bridge between graphs with explicit confidence at each hop, so a buyer can value inventory in whatever identity language it arrives in — this is the durable position precisely because it does not require anyone to abandon their own graph. Carry consent, lawful basis and permitted use with every identifier as a first-class field, since an identifier that cannot state what it may be used for is a liability rather than an asset and this is where the market is heading regardless. Verify rather than assert that provenance, because self-declared consent is worth nothing under scrutiny and a verified chain is the differentiator. Measure graph accuracy against outcomes rather than against match rates, which is the fix note's subject and is the only way to tell a large graph from a good one. Support authenticated and unauthenticated regimes side by side, as the honest picture is a mixed market for the foreseeable future. Make publisher participation cheap and immediately valuable, since publisher-side adoption is the binding constraint and integration cost is what blocks it. Handle household and shared-device semantics explicitly, which is where connected television inventory actually lives and where person-level graphs quietly fail. Price the confidence, so a high-confidence match and a weak one are not sold as the same product — the market currently cannot express this difference at all. Give regulators and publishers an auditable record of how a match was made. And publish coverage and accuracy openly, because a currency nobody can inspect will not be adopted as one.

## Target Customer
Identity providers, publishers and supply-side platforms, demand-side platforms, and the advertisers whose addressable buying is limited by translation loss.

## Impact If Built
Every participant's graph is individually rational and the collective result is a unit of account that does not exist. A bridging layer with per-hop confidence and verified consent is durable precisely because it asks nobody to abandon their own graph.

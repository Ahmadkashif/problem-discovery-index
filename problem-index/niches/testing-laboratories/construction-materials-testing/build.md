# Deciding Which Pours Need an Early Warning

**Niche:** [[niches/testing-laboratories/construction-materials-testing/profile|Construction Materials Testing & Special Inspection]]
**Industry:** [[industries/testing-laboratories|Testing Laboratories]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A low 28-day cylinder break arrives a month after the slab was poured and loaded, when the testing firm held the information to flag it within days.

**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to have a certified technician on every pour and compaction test the schedule demands and a signed result with the engineer of record before the next placement, and whoever staffs the field reliably and reports the same day wins the contractor's and owner's next project.

## The Problem
Concrete acceptance rests on 28-day compressive strength. When a set breaks low, the engineer of record must decide on cores, load tests or remediation for a structure that may already carry the floors above. The testing firm had the mix design, the batch tickets, the field slump, air and temperature, the curing record and the seven-day breaks — but it reads them one set at a time, against the specification, and rarely asks which placements are drifting toward a low result.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Field data is on paper in many firms, so the joined history needed for prediction doesn't exist. Maturity sensors estimate in-place strength from temperature, which is useful but answers a different question from cylinder acceptance, and needs sensors installed in advance. Firms sell tests, not forecasts.

## What to Build
A risk score for every placement, updated as early breaks arrive, predicting the 28-day result with an interval from the mix, the producer's recent performance on the firm's projects, field measurements, weather and curing conditions. Borderline placements trigger an extra early break, a maturity check or a call to the engineer while there is still time to act.

## Target Customer
Construction services practice leaders at testing firms; engineers of record on large structural projects who would pay for earlier warning.

## Impact If Built
A low break discovered at seven days rather than 28 days lets the engineer and contractor act before the structure is loaded — the kind of avoided rework that wins a testing firm the owner's trust.

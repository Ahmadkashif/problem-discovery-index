# Cylinders That Lose Their Identity Between Site and Lab

**Niche:** [[niches/testing-laboratories/construction-materials-testing/profile|Construction Materials Testing & Special Inspection]]
**Industry:** [[industries/testing-laboratories|Testing Laboratories]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A concrete cylinder is cast, left on site, collected, cured and broken by different people, and its label is a marker scrawl on a plastic mold.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #workflow-orchestration #worker-facing #quick-win #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to have a certified technician on every pour and compaction test the schedule demands and a signed result with the engineer of record before the next placement, and whoever staffs the field reliably and reports the same day wins the contractor's and owner's next project.

## The Problem
Cylinders must cure on site under controlled conditions and be collected within the standard's window, then demolded, cured and broken at set ages. Labels smear, pickups are missed, sets are mixed up, and a low break on a mislabelled cylinder is worthless as evidence.

## Why It's Still Broken
Cylinders are cheap and numerous, the chain of custody is informal, and field and lab systems are often separate.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Scannable labels issued at casting, linked to the placement and the field record; pickup tasks scheduled automatically with curing-window alerts; scans at pickup, demolding and break; and the break result attached to the right placement without retyping.

## Who Feels the Pain
Field technicians, lab technicians, and engineers of record who must rely on the results.

## Impact If Fixed
Fewer lost or mixed-up sets and break results that stand up when a pour is disputed.

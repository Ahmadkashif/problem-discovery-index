# Product Safety, EMC & Certification

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/testing-laboratories|Testing Laboratories]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is that every serious competitor here is fighting to get a product to market with the compliance evidence its target markets demand — but safety certification accounts are won by whose mark retailers, insurers and inspectors require and how lightly its factory follow-up sits on the manufacturer, while EMC and wireless accounts are won by chamber availability and getting a failing product through retest fastest in a lab regulators still recognise. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$5.5B US product safety, EMC, wireless and certification testing revenue (estimate; UL Solutions alone reported $2.66B of Industrial and Consumer revenue worldwide in 2025)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~20% (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium-High — test equipment and automation software are digital; scoping, technical files and certification records are documents and spreadsheets
**Target Buyer:** VP of compliance engineering at a manufacturer; general manager of a product testing lab or certification body
**Automation Potential:** Medium — test execution is automated; scoping, diagnosis and report assembly are not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Manufacturers of electrical, electronic and connected products must show safety, electromagnetic compatibility and radio compliance before they can sell, and must keep showing it as designs and standards change. The labs that do this — UL Solutions, Intertek, TÜV, CSA, SGS, Bureau Veritas, Element, Nemko and hundreds of independents — sell testing, certification marks and ongoing factory follow-up. Regulation is tightening at the border: CPSC's eFiling rule now requires certificate data at entry from July 2026, and the FCC has withdrawn recognition from test labs owned or controlled by foreign adversaries.

## Current Tools & Gaps
EMC test automation (Rohde & Schwarz EMC32 and similar), lab LIMS and project tools, certification databases, PLM on the manufacturer's side. The gaps: no single record of a product's compliance evidence across markets; no link between a design change and the certifications it might invalidate; and standards revisions tracked by memory.

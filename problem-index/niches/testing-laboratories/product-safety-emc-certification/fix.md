# The Standard Revision Nobody Mapped to the Portfolio

**Niche:** [[niches/testing-laboratories/product-safety-emc-certification/profile|Product Safety, EMC & Certification]]
**Industry:** [[industries/testing-laboratories|Testing Laboratories]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Standards are revised on multi-year cycles with transition deadlines, and manufacturers discover which products are affected when a market stops accepting the old edition.

## The Problem
Safety, EMC and radio standards change edition, sometimes wholesale, as when IEC 62368-1 replaced the older IT and audio-video safety standards. Each change carries a date after which the old edition stops giving market access. Manufacturers and labs track this by newsletters and memory, and products certified to superseded editions surface late.

## Why It's Still Broken
Standards publishers announce editions; nobody maps those announcements onto a specific company's certified products, because that mapping needs the products' compliance records in structured form.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Monitor standards and regulatory notices, extract the transition dates and the changed clauses, and match them against each product's certification record to produce a dated worklist: which products, which markets, which tests are likely needed, and by when.

## Who Feels the Pain
Compliance managers at manufacturers; lab account managers who could have sold the update early; distributors left holding stock that can no longer be placed on the market.

## Impact If Fixed
Transitions become planned projects rather than shipment holds, and labs gain a predictable pipeline of update work.

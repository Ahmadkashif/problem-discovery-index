# EMC & Wireless Compliance Testing

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/testing-laboratories|Testing Laboratories]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this sub-niche is fighting to get a product through emissions, immunity and radio testing on the first chamber booking, and whoever turns a failing scan into a diagnosed fix fastest — in a lab the FCC still recognises — wins the launch.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$2B US EMC, radio and wireless compliance testing revenue (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~7% (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** High in test execution — scans and automation are software-driven; Low in diagnosis, scheduling and report formatting
**Target Buyer:** EMC lab manager or wireless lab director; on the client side, the hardware compliance engineer
**Automation Potential:** High for execution and reporting; medium for diagnosis

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Every electronic product must meet emissions and immunity limits, and every radio product must meet spectrum rules and obtain authorisation — in the US an FCC grant issued through a telecommunication certification body on the basis of a recognised lab's report. Chamber time is the scarce asset, and many products fail their first emissions scan. The winner is the lab that gets a product from failing scan to passing retest fastest. Recognition itself is now contested: the FCC has stopped recognising labs owned or controlled by foreign adversaries — 23 test labs according to FCC 26-28 — and a law-firm summary says about three quarters of new certified devices had been tested in labs located in China, so work is moving.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Test receivers and automation software (Rohde & Schwarz EMC32 and others), chamber booking spreadsheets, and report templates per regulator. The gaps: diagnosis of failures depends on a senior engineer reading the spectrum; chamber scheduling cannot absorb unpredictable retests; and the same data is retyped into different regulators' report formats.

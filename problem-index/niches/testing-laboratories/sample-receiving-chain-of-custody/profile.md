# Sample Receiving, Login & Chain of Custody

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/testing-laboratories|Testing Laboratories]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn a cooler and a handwritten chain-of-custody form into correctly logged, preserved and scheduled work within minutes of arrival, and whoever logs fastest with the fewest transcription errors controls the turnaround the client actually experiences.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1B in receiving and login labour and tooling across US environmental, food and product labs (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~4% (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — most samples still arrive with handwritten or printed chain-of-custody forms that are keyed into the LIMS by hand
**Target Buyer:** Laboratory operations manager or client services manager
**Automation Potential:** High — reading forms, checking rules and drafting logins all automate; unpacking does not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Every sample's legal and analytical life starts at the receiving desk. Staff unpack coolers, check temperatures and preservation, compare bottles against the chain-of-custody form, assign sample IDs and enter clients, projects, matrices, methods and collection times into the LIMS. Errors here propagate everywhere: a wrong collection time breaks the holding-time clock; a wrong method wastes a sample; a missed discrepancy becomes a dispute later. Volumes peak on Monday mornings and Friday afternoons, when samplers come back from the field. The receiving desk is usually staffed by the lab's lowest-paid people, under the most time pressure, keying data from forms written in the rain.

## Current Tools & Gaps
LIMS login screens and barcode printers; some LIMS offer client portals and electronic chain-of-custody forms; email and phone for discrepancies. The gaps: field samplers rarely use the portals; handwritten forms are retyped; holding-time and preservation checks happen in the clerk's head; and discrepancy follow-up depends on who answers the phone.

# Client Portals Adapted to How Samplers Actually Work

**Niche:** [[niches/testing-laboratories/sample-receiving-chain-of-custody/profile|Sample Receiving, Login & Chain of Custody]]
**Industry:** [[industries/testing-laboratories|Testing Laboratories]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** LIMS client portals already offer electronic chain-of-custody forms; field samplers standing in a ditch with gloves on don't use them.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #data-integration #worker-facing #workflow-orchestration #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn a cooler and a handwritten chain-of-custody form into correctly logged, preserved and scheduled work within minutes of arrival, and whoever logs fastest with the fewest transcription errors controls the turnaround the client actually experiences.

## The Problem
Labs have invested in portals so clients can submit electronic forms. Adoption is low because the person filling the form is a field technician working offline, often for a consultant with several labs, under weather and time pressure.

## What Already Exists
LIMS client portals and electronic chain-of-custody modules; generic mobile forms apps used by consultants.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs: forms pre-filled from the project quote and bottle order so the sampler only adds times and initials; offline mobile capture with barcode scanning of bottle labels; voice entry for gloved hands; export in the formats consultants' own field apps use; and a fallback that reads paper forms the same way when the app isn't used.

## Target Customer
Client services managers at labs serving environmental consultants and utilities.

## Impact If Solved
More samples arrive with structured data, login becomes confirmation rather than typing, and consultants see the lab as easier to work with.

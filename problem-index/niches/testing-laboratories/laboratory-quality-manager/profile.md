# The Laboratory Quality Manager

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/testing-laboratories|Testing Laboratories]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep a lab continuously assessment-ready — corrective actions closed, uncertainty budgets current, every proficiency result answered — without the quality manager rebuilding the evidence before each visit, and whoever makes readiness continuous wins the quality budget.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$700M in quality staff time, quality software and accreditation fees across US testing labs (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~3% (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low-Medium — documents in SharePoint or a QMS built for manufacturers; uncertainty and proficiency tracking in spreadsheets
**Target Buyer:** Quality manager or quality director
**Automation Potential:** High for evidence assembly, uncertainty updates and tracking; low for the judgement in corrective actions

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
ISO/IEC 17025 requires every accredited lab to name a person responsible for the management system. That person maintains document control, training and competence records, equipment calibration and traceability, method validation files, measurement uncertainty budgets, proficiency testing and responses to unsatisfactory results, corrective actions, internal audits, management reviews and impartiality risk assessments — and hosts the assessor. A2LA alone held 4,350 accreditation certificates in 2024, and TNI-based state programmes require proficiency tests twice a year per field of testing. In a mid-size lab the quality manager is often one person, also doing bench work, and in a small lab it is the owner. Software built for manufacturers' quality systems ignores most of what makes a lab's quality system different.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Manufacturer-oriented QMS (MasterControl, Qualio, ETQ), document management in SharePoint, LIMS quality modules, proficiency providers' portals, spreadsheets for uncertainty. The gaps: uncertainty budgets recalculated by hand once a year; proficiency results tracked by email; and evidence rebuilt for every assessment.

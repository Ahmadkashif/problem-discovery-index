# Lease Obligations & HBP Tracking

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/oil-and-gas-producers|Oil & Gas Producers]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep every signed lease in force until the operator chooses to release it, and whoever loses the fewest net acres to a missed rental, shut-in payment or continuous-development deadline takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$300M a year of US spend on land-administration software, lease abstracting and staff time spent tracking obligations (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~0.1% of producer receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — obligations live in land modules typed from scanned leases and in spreadsheets of dates
**Target Buyer:** Land administration manager or VP of Land
**Automation Potential:** High — once clauses are abstracted, status is a rule applied to production and unit data

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
After a lease is signed, the land department's job is to keep it. A lease survives its primary term only through production or operations, and many carry clauses with their own deadlines: continuous development, shut-in royalty payments, Pugh releases outside units, depth severance and delay rentals. Whether a lease is held by production depends on wells, units and shut-in months that live in other systems. A missed deadline can terminate acreage the operator paid thousands of dollars an acre for, and the first sign may be a competitor's top lease. The clock is each lease's own terms, and the winner is whoever loses the fewest acres.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Land modules from Quorum, W Energy, PakEnergy and Pandell; generic contract-lifecycle tools; GIS acreage maps. Gaps: clauses never abstracted into data, held-by-production status computed by hand from production reports, and drilling schedules that change without anyone checking which obligations they now miss.

# Non-Op Working Interest Investors

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/oil-and-gas-producers|Oil & Gas Producers]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to decide each AFE election inside the joint operating agreement's window on better information than the operator's own proposal, and whoever's elected wells return the most cash per dollar committed takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$300M a year of US spend on non-op deal evaluation, AFE analytics, JIB review and partner audit services (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~0.1% of producer receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — public non-op companies run data teams; private and family non-op owners work from PDFs
**Target Buyer:** CEO, CFO or head of engineering at a non-operating investor; family offices holding working interests
**Automation Potential:** High for document intake and benchmarking; the participate-or-not decision stays human

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A non-operator owns part of a well it does not run. Under the joint operating agreement it receives an authority for expenditure (AFE) for each proposed well and must elect to participate or go non-consent within a fixed window (~30 days in common forms, estimate), then pays its share of costs through monthly joint interest bills it has the right to audit. Northern Oil and Gas, the largest public non-op company, held interests in more than 7,000 wells with about 95 operators (search summary). Private funds and families hold smaller versions of the same book. Every operator sends AFEs, JIBs and revenue statements in its own format, and the non-op's edge is judging operators and wells across all of them.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Accounting systems' non-op modules, offset data from Enverus and Novi Labs, outsourced COPAS audits, and spreadsheets of AFEs. Gaps: no cross-operator record of AFE estimates against actual costs and production, document intake by hand, and JIB review by sampling.

# Mineral & Royalty Aggregators

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/oil-and-gas-producers|Oil & Gas Producers]]
**Category:** Insight Layer
**Value-Chain Position:** Aggregator / rollup platforms

## What They Do
Public and private companies that buy mineral and royalty interests under other companies' wells and collect a share of revenue without paying costs. Viper Energy closed its all-equity purchase of Sitio Royalties on 19 August 2025, valued at about $4.0B, and reported 96,003 net royalty acres with 43,355 gross productive wells on its acreage. It has no employees of its own: Diamondback provides management and operating services (Viper 10-K). Kimbell Royalty Partners reports interests in more than 133,000 gross wells across 28 states (search summary), and Black Stone Minerals, Texas Pacific Land and Dorchester Minerals are other public examples (recalled), alongside many private-equity-backed buyers. Their investors pay for yield and growth, not for analysis.

## Insight Function
**Size:** ~10-40 engineers, geologists, land and A&D analysts per larger aggregator (estimate); Viper relies on Diamondback staff
**Output:** Acquisition evaluations, development forecasts for acreage operated by others, and owner-side revenue checks, all used internally
**Proprietary data:** Two regimes. Monthly check detail from hundreds of operators across tens of thousands of wells (realised prices, deductions, payment timing by operator) is the aggregator's own. Seller data rooms for acquisitions come under confidentiality agreements.
**Clock:** Self-set acquisition processes and seller bid dates
**Buyer:** Chief Executive or head of acquisitions — internal function

**Verdict:** Logged — below threshold. The cross-operator check detail is a genuinely unusual dataset, a payer-by-payer record of realised prices and deductions no operator sees. But the insight is monetised by owning royalties, not invoiced, and the teams are small.

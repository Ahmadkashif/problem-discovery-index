# Mineral Title & Lease Administration

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/oil-and-gas-producers|Oil & Gas Producers]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is that every competitor here is fighting to keep the operator's right to drill and produce intact. Two contests with different winners sit inside it. Before a well, the contest is building drilling title from county records, won by whoever's ownership schedules need the fewest corrections after first production. After the lease is signed, it is keeping leases alive, won by whoever loses the fewest net acres to a missed obligation. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$2.5B a year of US spend on contract landmen, abstracting, drilling title opinions, land software and land-administration staff (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~0.5% of producer receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — deed books, courthouse trips, scanned leases and spreadsheets of expiration dates
**Target Buyer:** VP of Land at an operator
**Automation Potential:** Medium — extraction and calendaring automate; title judgement and negotiation do not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
An operator's right to produce rests on two records: who owns the minerals under each tract, built up from a century of recorded instruments, and what each signed lease requires to stay in force. Both are kept by land departments and contract landmen, mostly on paper or in scanned images. The first is per-tract research before a well; the second is ongoing administration afterwards. They share people and systems but not their clock, their data or their vendors. That is why the niche splits.

## Current Tools & Gaps
County records online in some counties and on paper in others; title plants in some markets; Enverus Courthouse for images and indexes; Quorum, W Energy, PakEnergy and Pandell land modules; contract land brokerages and oil and gas title attorneys. Gaps: runsheets built by hand, title requirements tracked in opinions rather than systems, lease clauses never abstracted into data, and land and division-order records kept separately. Residential-market courthouse work is [[niches/title-and-settlement-offices/courthouse-abstracting/profile|🟠 Courthouse Abstracting in Non-Plant Counties]].

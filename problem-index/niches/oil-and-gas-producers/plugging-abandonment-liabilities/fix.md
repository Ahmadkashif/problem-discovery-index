# The Idle-Well List Nobody Owns

**Niche:** [[niches/oil-and-gas-producers/plugging-abandonment-liabilities/profile|Plugging & Abandonment Liabilities]]
**Industry:** [[industries/oil-and-gas-producers|Oil & Gas Producers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Wells that stopped producing years ago sit on no one's list until the state sends a notice.
**Tags:** #change-point-detection #feature-engineering #compliance #workflow-orchestration #quick-win #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to estimate what each idle well will cost to plug and when the regulator will force it, and whoever's plugging estimates land closest to the final plugging invoices takes the account.

## The Problem
Operations stops visiting a dead well; accounting keeps its ARO; regulatory files nothing until the commission flags it as inactive. Under state inactive-well rules (Texas's Statewide Rule 14, recalled) the operator must plug, test or justify each one by a deadline, and an acquisition brings hundreds more without a list.

## Why It's Still Broken
Idle status is visible in production data, but no department owns the decision to plug, sell or reactivate a well.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A monthly idle-well register built from production and commission records: months inactive, state deadline, estimated plugging cost, reactivation potential and bond exposure, with an owner assigned to each well and a quarterly decision meeting.

## Who Feels the Pain
Regulatory analysts receiving notices, controllers carrying stale ARO, and buyers inheriting surprises.

## Impact If Fixed
For an operator with ~10% of its wells idle (estimate), a register turns surprise notices into a planned plugging programme and prevents penalties on wells nobody knew were due.

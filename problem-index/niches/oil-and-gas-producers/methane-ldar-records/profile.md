# Methane Leak Detection & OOOOb Records

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/oil-and-gas-producers|Oil & Gas Producers]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to find and repair every leak and malfunctioning control device before it counts against the operator, and whoever has the fewest violations found at an EPA or state inspection takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.5B a year of US spend on LDAR survey crews, continuous monitors, aerial and satellite surveys, and emissions-records software at producers (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~0.3% of producer receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — detection is increasingly sensor-based, while survey logs and repair tracking still run on uploads, spreadsheets and work orders
**Target Buyer:** VP of EHS or emissions programme manager at an operator
**Automation Potential:** High — clip filing, plume flagging, repair tracking and report assembly are repetitive and rule-bound

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Methane rules turned leak detection into a record-keeping business. EPA's 2024 rules for new sources (OOOOb) and the model rules states must adopt for existing sites (OOOOc) require fugitive surveys, control-device monitoring and repair deadlines: at sites with major production and processing equipment, OGI at least quarterly, audio-visual-olfactory checks at least bimonthly, and a first repair attempt within 30 days of an OGI find (40 CFR 60.5397b). An interim final rule published 31 July 2025, and finalised in November 2025, moved several compliance dates to 22 January 2027, and the state-plan deadline from 9 March 2026 to the same date (EPA). The Waste Emissions Charge was pushed back to 2034 emissions (search summary), and some states already run their own rules. An operator's programme must turn OGI clips, continuous-monitor alerts, aerial detections and work orders into a defensible record per site. That record is what an inspector checks.

## Current Tools & Gaps
LDAR contractors with OGI crews; continuous monitors (Project Canary, Qube); aerial surveys (Bridger Photonics, Insight M); satellites ([[niches/satellite-operators/satellite-methane-analytics/profile|🔍 Satellite Methane & Emissions Analytics]], 40); emissions data platforms (Validere, Highwood); and maintenance systems. Gaps: detections from different methods never fused into one site history, repairs tracked in a CMMS that knows nothing of regulatory deadlines, and surveys that cannot be proved after the fact. Annual greenhouse gas reporting is [[niches/oil-gas-field-services/regulatory-reporting-automation/profile|⚡ Regulatory Reporting Automation]] under field services.

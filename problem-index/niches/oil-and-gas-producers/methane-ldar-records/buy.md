# Maintenance Work Orders Adapted to Leak-Repair Deadlines

**Niche:** [[niches/oil-and-gas-producers/methane-ldar-records/profile|Methane Leak Detection & OOOOb Records]]
**Industry:** [[industries/oil-and-gas-producers|Oil & Gas Producers]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Every operator already runs a maintenance system that issues and closes work orders; leak repairs need regulatory deadlines, proof of repair and re-survey built in.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #feature-engineering #compliance #workflow-orchestration #automation #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to find and repair every leak and malfunctioning control device before it counts against the operator, and whoever has the fewest violations found at an EPA or state inspection takes the account.

## The Problem
A leak found by a technician becomes a work order in the maintenance system, where it competes with every other job. The system does not know the repair deadline, the re-monitoring requirement, or the conditions under which a repair can be delayed, so overdue repairs are found when the annual report is assembled.

## What Already Exists
Computerised maintenance management systems (IBM Maximo, SAP Plant Maintenance, eMaint, Fiix) issue, schedule and close work orders with mobile apps for field crews.

## The Customization Gap
Leak records with the OGI clip and component attached; regulatory deadlines by rule and state; delay-of-repair justification and tracking; re-survey scheduling after repair; and exports formatted for annual reports and inspections. The maintenance system keeps scheduling; the compliance layer keeps the clock.

## Target Customer
Operators that already run a CMMS across their field operations.

## Impact If Solved
Putting regulatory clocks on leak work orders can take overdue repairs toward zero. For an operator logging ~5,000 leaks a year (estimate), even a 2% overdue rate is 100 potential violations.

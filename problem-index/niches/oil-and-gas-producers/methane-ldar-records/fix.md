# Surveys That Happened but Cannot Be Proved

**Niche:** [[niches/oil-and-gas-producers/methane-ldar-records/profile|Methane Leak Detection & OOOOb Records]]
**Industry:** [[industries/oil-and-gas-producers|Oil & Gas Producers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A technician surveyed the site, but the clip was not saved, the wind speed was not logged or a component was missed, so on paper the survey never happened.
**Tags:** #cnns #object-detection #feature-engineering #compliance #worker-facing #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to find and repair every leak and malfunctioning control device before it counts against the operator, and whoever has the fewest violations found at an EPA or state inspection takes the account.

## The Problem
Survey records are assembled after the fieldwork from camera files, notes and site lists. Gaps are found months later, when an annual report or an inspection asks for proof, and by then nothing can be done but report a deviation.

## Why It's Still Broken
Completeness checks happen at the end of the year, not at the end of the survey, and the technician has no tool that tells them on site what is missing.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A completeness check that runs as clips upload, or on the camera tablet on site: every component on the site's list has footage, conditions are logged, and any gaps are shown to the technician before they leave the pad.

## Who Feels the Pain
LDAR technicians whose names are on the record, programme managers writing deviation reports, and operators facing inspectors.

## Impact If Fixed
If ~3–5% of surveys today have a documentation gap (estimate), an on-site check removes nearly all of them, turning reported deviations into ordinary records.

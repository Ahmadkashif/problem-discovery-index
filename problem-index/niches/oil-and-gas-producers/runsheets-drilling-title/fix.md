# Title Requirements Lost Between the Opinion and the Spud

**Niche:** [[niches/oil-and-gas-producers/runsheets-drilling-title/profile|Runsheets & Drilling Title]]
**Industry:** [[industries/oil-and-gas-producers|Oil & Gas Producers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A drilling title opinion lists dozens of curative requirements in prose, and the ones nobody tracked become suspended revenue a year later.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #feature-engineering #workflow-orchestration #compliance #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to deliver a complete runsheet and ownership schedule for each drilling unit before the spud date, and whoever's ownership schedules need the fewest corrections after first production takes the account.

## The Problem
The attorney's opinion ends with requirements: obtain an affidavit of heirship, record a missing assignment, ratify a lease, confirm a probate. Landmen work some before the spud and defer others. The list lives in a PDF, and when the well comes on line, unresolved requirements put owners in suspense.

## Why It's Still Broken
Requirements are written as prose, each opinion in its author's style, and nothing turns them into tasks with owners and due dates.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Extract every requirement from each opinion into a task list linked to the tract and owner it affects, assign it, and track it to resolution, with a pre-first-production report of what will go into suspense if unresolved.

## Who Feels the Pain
Title managers, division-order analysts and the owners whose payments are held.

## Impact If Fixed
If ~5% of a new well's owner decimals go into suspense for unmet requirements (estimate), clearing half of them before first sales avoids months of held payments and the calls and interest that follow.

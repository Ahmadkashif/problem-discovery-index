# Title Plant Search Adapted to Severed Mineral Estates

**Niche:** [[niches/oil-and-gas-producers/runsheets-drilling-title/profile|Runsheets & Drilling Title]]
**Industry:** [[industries/oil-and-gas-producers|Oil & Gas Producers]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Title plants index every surface conveyance by parcel for home sales; mineral title needs the same index for estates that were split from the surface a century ago.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #bert #graph-theory #data-integration #automation #compliance
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to deliver a complete runsheet and ownership schedule for each drilling unit before the spud date, and whoever's ownership schedules need the fewest corrections after first production takes the account.

## The Problem
Residential title searches typically go back a few decades to the last insured policy. Mineral title goes back to the patent, and the minerals may have been severed, divided among heirs and conveyed separately for a hundred years. A residential plant's parcel index does not follow the mineral estate.

## What Already Exists
Title plants and search software (RamQuest, Integrity title plants, county-level plants run by title companies) index recorded documents by parcel and produce searches for closings.

## The Customization Gap
Mineral specifics: indexing by section, township, range or abstract and survey rather than street parcel; tracking severed mineral, royalty and non-participating royalty interests separately from the surface; term royalties and depth limits; and outputs as runsheets and ownership schedules rather than title commitments. Enverus's own contract title-examiner posting names RamQuest and Integrity plant experience, showing the overlap.

## Target Customer
Title plants and abstract companies in oil and gas counties wanting to sell to land departments, and operators licensing plant data.

## Impact If Solved
Re-indexing a plant's existing images for mineral estates lets one county's records serve a second market with no new collection, cutting search time per tract in plant counties by an estimated ~50%.

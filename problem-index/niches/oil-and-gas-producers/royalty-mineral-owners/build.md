# A Royalty Check Auditor for the Small Owner

**Niche:** [[niches/oil-and-gas-producers/royalty-mineral-owners/profile|Royalty & Mineral Owners]]
**Industry:** [[industries/oil-and-gas-producers|Oil & Gas Producers]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** An owner with a few wells has no practical way to check whether the volumes, prices and deductions on their check match public production data and their lease.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #gradient-boosting #feature-engineering #evaluation-metrics #data-integration #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get mineral and royalty owners paid what their leases and decimals say they are owed, and whoever recovers the most underpaid, suspended or escheated royalty dollars per owner takes the account.

## The Problem
Royalty audits exist, but auditors take cases where the dollars justify a contingency fee. Owners with small interests across several operators get nothing beyond the stub. Underpayment, whether wrong decimals, missed months, deductions the lease prohibits or prices below what the operator realised, goes unnoticed.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The owner's data is on paper or behind separate portals, and the comparison needs public production by well or lease, posted and index prices, and the lease's deduction clause, which the owner may never have read.

## What to Build
An owner-side tool: photograph or upload stubs and leases, extract volumes, prices, taxes and deductions per well and month, and compare them with state-reported production, an implied decimal from the unit and the lease, regional price benchmarks and the lease's deduction language. Flag months and lines that look wrong, with a draft letter to the operator.

## Target Customer
Individual owners and family mineral offices; trustees and mineral managers with many small interests.

## Impact If Built
If ~5–10% of small owners' checks carry correctable errors (estimate), a tool that finds them for a flat fee recovers money that would never justify a contingency audit across millions of owners.

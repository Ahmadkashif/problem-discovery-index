# Device Protection & Trade-In Programme Administrators

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/wireless-carriers|Wireless Carriers]]
**Category:** Insight Layer
**Value-Chain Position:** Payer & intermediary layer

## What They Do
Asurion and Assurant run carriers' device insurance, extended warranty, repair and trade-in programmes. They price the cover, file rates and forms where states require it, carry or place the risk, pay claims, repair or replace phones, and grade and resell traded-in devices. Customers pay monthly premiums on their carrier bill, and carriers share in programme revenue. Assurant publishes a periodic report on trade-in values and upgrade behaviour as marketing. The counter-side grading of trade-ins at dealer stores is a separate niche, [[niches/wireless-retail-agents/trade-in-intake-promo-eligibility/profile|⚡ Trade-In Intake & Promo Credit Eligibility]], under wireless retail agents.

## Insight Function
**Size:** ~50-200 actuarial, pricing, claims analytics and device valuation staff per firm (estimate)
**Output:** Programme pricing and rate filings, loss, reserving and fraud analytics, trade-in valuation grids; published reports are marketing
**Proprietary data:** Claims, failure, repair and graded-device records across tens of millions of phones and every major model, a cross-brand damage and reliability record no handset maker or carrier assembles
**Clock:** State insurance rate and form filings and statutory reserving cycles; device launch calendars drive trade-in pricing
**Buyer:** Chief actuary or chief analytics officer

**Kill switches:** none — the administrator prices and carries the risk, and the claims, repair and grading records are its own. Only subscriber CPNI belongs to the carrier, and the pricing and loss analysis doesn't depend on it. This is the same ruling as the vehicle-side shape, [[niches/auto-dealers-independent/fi-product-actuarial-teams/profile|F&I Product Actuarial & Claims Research]] (46, no kill switch).
**Verdict:** Logged — below threshold. The record is unique and rate filings set real clocks, but the analysis prices an insurance and logistics product rather than being sold, and the market is two large administrators. The F&I teams score higher on Q2 and Q5 because more administrators compete there.

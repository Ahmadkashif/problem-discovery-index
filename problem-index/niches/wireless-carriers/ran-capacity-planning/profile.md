# RAN Capacity Planning & Spectrum Deployment

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/wireless-carriers|Wireless Carriers]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to predict, sector by sector, when mobile and fixed wireless demand will exhaust capacity and which fix — a new carrier, a sector split, a small cell or a cap on fixed wireless sales — buys the most relief per dollar, and whoever forecasts exhaustion earliest and most accurately takes the planning budget.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$2B a year in planning tools, capacity analytics and RF engineering services, steering most of the ~$30B annual network capex (estimate; capex from CTIA 2026 Annual Survey)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~1% of revenue directly, ~10% through the capex it allocates (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** High — counters, forecasts and planning tools are digital; the final call on which fix goes where is a spreadsheet and a planning meeting
**Target Buyer:** VP RAN Engineering / Director of Capacity Planning
**Automation Potential:** High for forecasting and option ranking; Low for site build

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Mobile data grew 20% in 2025 to 159.3 trillion MB, and nearly 16 million fixed wireless homes now share the same sectors as phones. A fixed wireless household uses many times the data of a phone (estimate), so each FWA sale consumes capacity that mobile customers will want later. Capacity planning is where the carrier decides, years ahead and sector by sector, whether to light new spectrum, split a sector, add a small cell or stop selling fixed wireless at an address. The spectrum itself arrives on the FCC's schedule: AWS-3 licences won in Auction 113 in June 2026 and the 3.45 GHz and 600 MHz spectrum AT&T bought from EchoStar must be deployed under buildout conditions.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Forsk Atoll and Infovista Planet for planning, vendor capacity dashboards, in-house forecasting in Python or spreadsheets. The gaps: forecasts are trend lines per sector rather than distributions; FWA sales caps are set separately from mobile forecasts; and the relief options are compared by rule of thumb.

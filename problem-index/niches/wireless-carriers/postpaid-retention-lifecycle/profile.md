# Postpaid Phone Retention & Upgrade Lifecycle

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/wireless-carriers|Wireless Carriers]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche (decisioning platforms, retention analytics vendors and the carriers' own lifecycle teams) is fighting to pick, line by line, the cheapest save offer that actually changes a postpaid customer's decision to port out, and whoever measures save-offer uplift most accurately takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$15B a year in retention device credits, loyalty offers, save-desk care time and lifecycle analytics across the national and regional carriers (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~5% of carrier revenue (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** High — every line's billing, device and usage history is digital; the decision about who gets which offer is still rules and campaign calendars
**Target Buyer:** SVP Consumer Retention / Chief Customer Lifecycle Officer
**Automation Potential:** High for targeting and offer choice; Medium for save conversations

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Postpaid phone lines are where carrier revenue and margin sit, and churn near 0.9% a month (Q3 2025: 0.92% at AT&T, 0.89% at T-Mobile) means a national carrier must win back or replace millions of lines a year. Retention here is not generic subscription churn. Number portability lets a customer leave in minutes, device payment plans and 24–36-month promo credits hold customers until a known date and then release them, and competitors target those dates with switching offers. The work is choosing, line by line and month by month, whether to spend on keeping a customer and how much.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Customer data and decisioning platforms (Salesforce, Pega Customer Decision Hub, Adobe), billing and promotion engines from Amdocs and Netcracker, and in-house churn models. The gaps: churn models predict who leaves, not whom an offer changes; promotions are rarely tested against holdouts; and device-credit end dates, network experience and competitor offers sit in separate systems.

# Cell Site Field Operations & Fault Diagnosis

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/wireless-carriers|Wireless Carriers]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to resolve a site fault on the first visit or without one, and whoever turns alarms and site history into the right part, person and decision before the truck leaves takes the field operations contract.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$6B a year in carrier field technician labour, contracted site maintenance, generator and power service, and field service software (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~2% of carrier revenue (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — alarms are digital, but site access notes, gate codes, landlord rules, equipment quirks and repair history live in technicians' phones, spreadsheets and memory
**Target Buyer:** VP Field Operations / Director of Network Operations
**Automation Potential:** Medium for triage and dispatch; Low for the physical repair

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Carrier technicians own the ground-level work at each site: shelters and cabinets, power plant, batteries and generators, baseband units, backhaul hand-offs and the first diagnosis. Work above the cabinet goes to contracted tower crews, whose construction and climbing trade belongs to [[industries/utility-contractors|Utility Contractors]]; the carrier's work is deciding whether, when and with what to send someone. Rural territories, storm restoration and FCC outage reporting make each wasted trip costly.

## Current Tools & Gaps
ServiceNow, Salesforce Field Service and IBM Maximo for work orders; vendor alarm consoles; tower-owner portals for climb approvals; generator telemetry where installed. The gaps: no prediction of whether an alarm self-clears; no structured site memory; parts chosen by guesswork; and storm fuel runs dispatched by phone.

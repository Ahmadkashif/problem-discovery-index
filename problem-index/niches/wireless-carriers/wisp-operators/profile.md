# Fixed Wireless ISPs (WISPs)

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/wireless-carriers|Wireless Carriers]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is that every serious competitor is fighting to deliver advertised speeds to rural homes over fixed wireless, but the contest splits in two. Before the sale it is qualifying a specific roofline correctly; after the sale it is keeping evening-peak experience inside the plan on shared and often unlicensed spectrum. The data, people, clocks and vendors differ, so the niche is decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$450M a year in software, radio management, coverage prediction and engineering services bought by ~2,000 WISPs serving ~9 million customers (estimate; operator and customer counts from WISPA)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~2% of industry revenue (estimate: ~9M customers at ~$55–65 a month)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — radios report telemetry and billing is online, but qualification, installs and troubleshooting lean on the owner's and installers' experience
**Target Buyer:** Owner-operator or network engineer of a WISP
**Automation Potential:** Medium — prediction and monitoring automate; installs and tower work don't

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
WISPs are small regional operators that beam broadband from towers, grain elevators and water tanks to antennas on rural homes, using unlicensed 5 GHz and 6 GHz bands, CBRS and some licensed spectrum. Typical staff is a few people. They now compete with T-Mobile, Verizon and AT&T fixed wireless (14.7 million customers after Q3 2025), subsidised fibre and low-orbit satellite. The national carriers' tools assume large RF teams and licensed spectrum. WISPs need the same answers at a fraction of the cost.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Ubiquiti UISP and Cambium cnMaestro for radios; Tarana and others for next-generation fixed wireless; TowerCoverage and Cambium cnHeat for coverage prediction; Preseem for subscriber experience; Sonar, Splynx or Visp for billing. Each covers one step, and none connects the pre-sale prediction to the post-sale experience.

# WISP Address Qualification & Installs

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/wireless-carriers|Wireless Carriers]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche (coverage-prediction tools, LiDAR heat-map vendors and radio makers' planning software) is fighting to tell, before the truck rolls, whether a specific roofline can hold the advertised speed from a specific sector, and whoever qualifies addresses most accurately takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$150M a year in coverage prediction, LiDAR and imagery data, survey tools and install workflow software for WISPs (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** under 1% of industry revenue (estimate); a large share of a WISP's sales and install cost
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — coverage tools exist, but the final call is often an installer's site survey and the owner's memory of the area
**Target Buyer:** WISP owner or sales and install manager
**Automation Potential:** High for prediction; Low for the install itself

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Fixed wireless to a home depends on an unobstructed path between the customer's antenna and the sector: trees, terrain, buildings and seasonal foliage decide it, roofline by roofline. A wrong "yes" costs a truck roll, an installer's half-day and often a customer who cancels in the first month. A wrong "no" loses a customer to the national carriers' fixed wireless, which qualifies on capacity rather than line of sight, or to subsidised fibre. The clock is the install appointment, and the label (did the install hit the speed?) arrives the same day.

## Current Tools & Gaps
TowerCoverage, Cambium cnHeat (LiDAR-based heat maps) and vendor-specific planning tools; installer site surveys; Google Earth. The gaps: predictions aren't calibrated against the WISP's own install outcomes; summer foliage isn't modelled; failed installs aren't fed back.

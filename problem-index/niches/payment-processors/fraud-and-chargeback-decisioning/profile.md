# Fraud & Chargeback Decisioning

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/payment-processors|Payment Processors]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to block the fraudulent transaction without blocking the customer — and whoever balances that properly stops merchants losing more to declined good orders than to fraud.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$5B US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~4% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — widely deployed, poorly balanced
**Target Buyer:** Risk and merchant leadership
**Automation Potential:** Very High — a well-posed prediction with abundant labels

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Fraud screening at the transaction level decides whether an order proceeds, and the merchant's losses from declined legitimate orders routinely exceed their fraud losses while only one of the two is reported as a loss. Chargeback management is the other half: which disputes to contest, with what evidence, and what the outcome record says about which are winnable. Both are prediction problems with abundant labels, and both are configured with thresholds and rules on the merchant's behalf.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Fraud scoring vendors, rules engines, manual review queues, chargeback representment workflows, and liability shift through authentication. The gaps: false declines unmeasured and uncosted; thresholds set by merchants without evidence; review queues measured on throughput; representment decisions made without evidence of what wins; and chargeback outcomes not fed back into screening.

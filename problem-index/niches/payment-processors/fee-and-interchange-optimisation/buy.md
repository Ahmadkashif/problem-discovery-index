**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)

**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get transactions qualifying at the interchange rate they are entitled to — and whoever does that returns money merchants are currently paying for data they had and did not send.

## The Problem
Complex published tariffs with qualification conditions are a familiar commercial structure, and every industry with one developed optimisation practice. Energy procurement models tariff structures against consumption patterns, freight audits carrier invoices against contracted rates and recovers overcharges routinely, and telecoms expense management is an established category. The practice exists because a complex schedule rewards whoever reads it carefully. Interchange is exactly this kind of schedule and is largely navigated by convention and occasional consulting engagements.

## What Already Exists
Tariff modelling against usage patterns; automated invoice auditing against contracted rates; overcharge recovery processes; rate optimisation recommendations; and continuous monitoring against schedule changes.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a tariff where qualification depends on how the transaction was constructed. It requires: (1) qualification determined by data submitted at the moment of the transaction rather than by consumption after the fact, so optimisation is a change to the integration rather than a change to a contract — this makes it an engineering fix rather than a procurement one, which is the substantive difference; (2) hundreds of rates with interacting conditions across multiple networks and card products, which is more complex than most tariff structures; (3) blended pricing concealing the underlying rate, so the merchant frequently cannot see the thing being optimised; (4) schedules that change on a published cadence, requiring continuous maintenance; and (5) the processor as both the party applying the fee and the party who could fix it, which is an alignment problem energy and freight do not have.

## Target Customer
Merchant finance teams, processors competing on total cost, and expense audit and recovery firms for whom interchange is an adjacent market.

## Impact If Solved
Every industry with a complex published tariff built optimisation practice, and payments left it to consultants. Qualification determined by how the transaction was constructed makes this an engineering fix rather than a procurement one.

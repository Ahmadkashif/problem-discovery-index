# Merchant Underwriting

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/payment-processors|Payment Processors]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to decide whether a business is safe to process for, against the same registries everyone else uses, and to learn from the outcome — and whoever closes that loop stops grading a decision against losses it never attributes back.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$22B US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~18% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Low — rebuilt independently everywhere
**Target Buyer:** Risk and onboarding leadership
**Automation Potential:** Very High — verification and prediction are both automatable

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Every acquirer independently rebuilds business verification and risk underwriting against the same public registries, and grades it against fraud losses it never attributes back to the decision. The same company is verified separately by every processor it approaches, using the same sources, producing different answers. Meanwhile the decision — approve, decline, approve with reserve — is the one that determines the acquirer's loss exposure, and its accuracy is unmeasured because losses arrive months later attributed to a merchant rather than to the decision that admitted them.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Registry lookups, sanctions and watchlist screening, website review, processing history where available, and risk scoring by rules. The gaps: verification rebuilt per acquirer against identical sources; decisions not joined to subsequent losses; reserve and limit decisions set by category convention; no measurement of declined-but-safe merchants; and onboarding time treated as the primary metric.

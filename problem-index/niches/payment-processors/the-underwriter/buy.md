**Industry:** [[industries/payment-processors|Payment Processors]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Commercial credit analysts work to structured frameworks with documented rationale and portfolio review, and merchant underwriters read a website against a clock.

**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to give the underwriter evidence and feedback rather than a website and a clock — and whoever does that turns an unmeasured judgement into a skill that can improve.

## The Problem
Commercial credit analysis is a structured profession. Analysts work to defined frameworks, gather specified evidence, document their rationale, present to a committee for larger exposures, and have their portfolio reviewed periodically with outcomes fed back. Training is deliberate and the craft accumulates. Merchant underwriting carries genuine credit exposure and is performed as a rapid review with no framework, no documented rationale, no committee for larger exposures and no outcome review.

## What Already Exists
Structured credit analysis frameworks; documented rationale requirements; credit committee processes for larger exposures; portfolio review with outcome feedback; and analyst training and accreditation.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a decision made in minutes at enormous volume. It requires: (1) the framework encoded in the tooling rather than applied by a trained analyst, since a commercial credit process takes days and this takes minutes — the structure must be automated rather than taught, which is the central adaptation; (2) exposure that is a chargeback liability tail rather than a loan balance, which is a less familiar shape and is frequently reasoned about qualitatively; (3) the business model itself being the main uncertainty, which a website now largely answers and which credit analysis obtains from financial statements that do not exist here; (4) committee review reserved for the small number of large exposures, with the rest needing an automated equivalent; and (5) outcome feedback that is available in months rather than years, giving a far faster learning loop than commercial credit enjoys.

## Target Customer
Acquirer risk leadership, underwriting teams, and credit training and framework providers for whom merchant acquiring is an unserved application.

## Impact If Solved
Commercial credit is a structured profession with documented rationale and portfolio review, and merchant underwriting has none of it despite carrying real exposure. Encoding the framework in tooling rather than teaching it is the adaptation a minutes-long decision requires.

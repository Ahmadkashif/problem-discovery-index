# Matchmaking

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/game-hosting-providers|Game Hosting Providers]]
**Category:** 🔵 High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to trade match quality against wait time against latency without knowing what any of the three costs in whether the player comes back — and whoever establishes that currency takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$800M US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~27% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** High, unvalidated
**Target Buyer:** Platform and game leadership
**Automation Potential:** High — measurement and policy optimisation

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Every matchmaker in the industry makes the same trade: a better-balanced match takes longer to assemble and may require a more distant server. The weights governing that trade are set by hand, adjusted by intuition, and never validated against what players actually experience. Teams tune toward shorter queues because queue length is visible on a dashboard and churn caused by a bad match is not. The record that would settle it is complete and sitting in the provider's systems.

### Contested sub-niches
This niche is not terminal. It names a system, and two separable businesses sit inside it:
- [[niches/game-hosting-providers/match-outcome-measurement/profile|🎯 Match Outcome Measurement]] — what quality, wait and latency actually cost
- [[niches/game-hosting-providers/matchmaker-objective-design/profile|🎯 Matchmaker Objective Design]] — what the matchmaker should therefore optimise

## Current Tools & Gaps
A skill rating system, hand-set weights, a queue time dashboard and a latency threshold. The gaps: no measured cost of a bad match; no measured cost of a long queue; no latency tolerance curve; no population-aware policy; and no validation of any weight against retention.

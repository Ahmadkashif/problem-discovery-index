# CMRA Compliance & Form 1583 Records

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/private-mail-centers|Private Mail Centers]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep every holder's Form 1583, IDs, terminations and remail addresses current in the USPS database by each quarterly certification date, and whoever lets a store certify on 15 January, April, July and October with no expired ID and no evening of re-keying takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$60M a year of staff time and software spent on CMRA record-keeping across ~12,000 registered CMRAs (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~1% of receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — paper forms and photocopied IDs, re-keyed and uploaded to a USPS database
**Target Buyer:** Store owner or manager; compliance lead at a virtual-mailbox network
**Automation Potential:** High — extraction, expiry tracking and certification preparation are mechanical

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The CMRA rules in DMM 508.1.8 make each mailbox a regulated record. The store must collect one Form 1583 per addressee with primary and secondary ID, witnessed in person or by real-time video by the store or a US-commissioned notary, and must deny any application where the documents do not match. Since July 2023 it must upload everything to the CMRA Customer Registration Database and certify every quarter. A store that fails gets 30 days' written notice before delivery is suspended. The USPS Inspector General's 2025 audit found the programme's own records patchy, which means enforcement could tighten.

## Current Tools & Gaps
The USPS database itself, point-of-sale mailbox modules, generic ID verification (Persona, Onfido) and online notarisation (Proof, Notarize), and virtual-mailbox networks' own onboarding flows. Gaps: no tool tracks ID expiry per holder against the certification dates, prepares database entries, or produces the pre-certification exception list.

# The ID That Expired Two Quarters Ago

**Niche:** [[niches/private-mail-centers/cmra-compliance-records/profile|CMRA Compliance & Form 1583 Records]]
**Industry:** [[industries/private-mail-centers|Private Mail Centers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A holder's driver's licence expired in March, nobody noticed, and the store has certified twice since that no ID on file is expired.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #compliance #automation #quick-win #workflow-orchestration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep every holder's Form 1583, IDs, terminations and remail addresses current in the USPS database by each quarterly certification date, and whoever lets a store certify on 15 January, April, July and October with no expired ID and no evening of re-keying takes the account.

## The Problem
ID expiry dates sit on photocopies in a binder or as images in the database. Holders rarely volunteer a renewed licence, and the store has no list sorted by expiry. The DMM treats an expired ID upload as non-compliance.

## Why It's Still Broken
The expiry date is never captured as a field at intake, so nothing can sort or alert on it; collecting a new ID means catching the holder at pickup, which depends on who is on shift.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Capture expiry as a field at intake (or extract it once from existing images), and keep a 90-day expiry list. Text holders a link to upload a renewed ID, and flag the box at pickup so the associate asks in person.

## Who Feels the Pain
Owners signing certifications they cannot verify; associates asked to chase holders; holders surprised by a suspension notice that affects everyone at the store.

## Impact If Fixed
A one-field change and a monthly list close the most common certification gap at near-zero cost (estimate).

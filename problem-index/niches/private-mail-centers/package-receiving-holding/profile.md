# Package Receiving & Holding

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/private-mail-centers|Private Mail Centers]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to release every received package to the right person with a scan at each hand-off, and whoever can settle an "it says delivered" dispute from the record in seconds takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$250M a year of package-acceptance fees and the package-handling share of mailbox rent (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~5% of receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low-Medium — some point-of-sale systems log arrivals and send notices; many stores use handwritten slips and shelf memory
**Target Buyer:** Store owner; franchisor's operations lead
**Automation Potential:** High — label reading, matching, notification and release logging are mechanical

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Every carrier delivers to the store each day, for mailbox holders and for non-holders who pay a per-package fee to avoid porch theft. The store becomes the custodian. It must match each label to a holder (often with a missing "PMB" number), return mail for anyone without a Form 1583 on file as the DMM requires, keep remailing for former holders, shelve, notify and release. The competing options are parcel lockers, apartment package rooms and carrier access points, so the store's advantage is a person and a record. A dispute ("tracking says delivered") is where the account is won or lost.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Package-receiving modules in PostalMate, ShipRite and franchise systems; ShipRite Smart Mailboxes for after-hours pickup; locker networks (Amazon Hub, Quadient Parcel Pending) as substitutes. Gaps: label-to-holder matching by eye, no scan at shelving or release in many stores, and no chain-of-custody report for a dispute.

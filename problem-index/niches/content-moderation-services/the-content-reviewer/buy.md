# Buy: Emergency Services Peer Support, Adapted

**Niche:** The Content Reviewer
**Industry:** [[industries/content-moderation-services|Content Moderation Services]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Emergency services built structured peer support for trauma-exposed workers over forty years, and content moderation offers a counselling hotline and a confidentiality agreement that forbids talking about the work.
**Tags:** #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #hypothesis-testing #survival-analysis #worker-facing #compliance
**Contested on:** Whether the protections a reviewer is promised in a contract are the protections they actually receive on a shift.

## The Problem

Emergency services, military medicine and child protection have spent decades developing support for workers exposed routinely to traumatic material. The consistent finding across all of them is that the effective mechanism is not individual clinical counselling offered on request. It is structured peer support: trained colleagues who have done the same work, embedded in the team, available immediately, with clear escalation into clinical care when needed. Clinical support matters and works best as the apex of a structure, not as the whole of it.

Content moderation has essentially the opposite arrangement. Clinical counselling is the primary offer, available on request, often external, frequently not in the reviewer's language, and requiring the reviewer to identify themselves as struggling in order to access it. Peer support — the mechanism with the strongest evidence behind it — is not merely absent but partly foreclosed, because confidentiality agreements restrict what reviewers may say about the content they handle, including to each other and to family.

The industry has adopted the least effective component of a well-understood model and blocked the most effective one.

## What Already Exists

Emergency services peer support: Critical Incident Stress Management programmes, trained peer supporter networks in fire, police and ambulance services, and the substantial evidence base on what helps and what does not — including robust findings on the harms of poorly designed single-session debriefing, which any adaptation must respect.

Clinical: trauma-focused therapy protocols; screening instruments validated in occupational trauma populations; occupational mental health surveillance in first responder services.

Platforms: employee assistance programme providers; digital mental health services such as Lyra, Spring Health and Modern Health, which have scale and multilingual reach; and peer support platforms built for healthcare workers.

Adjacent: military and child protection agency programmes, which handle the specific problem of workers who cannot discuss their work outside a cleared circle — the closest existing analogue to the confidentiality constraint.

## The Customization Gap

**Confidentiality has to be solved, not worked around.** The single largest adaptation. Reviewers need a cleared circle within which they can discuss what they have seen — the model child protection and military intelligence use — rather than a blanket restriction that forecloses peer support entirely. This is a contractual and policy change as much as a product one, and nothing in the existing support category addresses it.

**The exposure is chronic, not incident-based.** Emergency services models are built around discrete critical incidents with a clear before and after. Moderation exposure is continuous, cumulative and unremarkable day to day, which changes both the trigger for support and its cadence. The programmes need to be routine and scheduled rather than incident-triggered.

**Multilingual and multi-country at real depth.** Reviewers are deliberately staffed across many markets, and clinical support in a second language with no cultural competence for the reviewer's context is substantially less effective. Digital mental health platforms have the widest reach and their depth in the relevant languages is thin.

**Employer administration compromises uptake.** Support accessed through the employer, in a role with high turnover and easy replacement, will be under-used by exactly the people who most need it. Independent administration is close to a functional requirement and no existing provider is structured that way for this industry.

**Turnover breaks the peer supporter model.** Emergency services peer support assumes long-tenured colleagues. With moderation attrition measured in months, the trained-peer population has to be continuously rebuilt, which changes the training economics and argues for a much lighter, faster certification than the emergency services version.

**Do no harm.** The evidence that some debriefing approaches worsen outcomes is strong and specific. Any adaptation must be built on the current evidence rather than on the intuitive version of peer support, which is precisely the mistake a new entrant would make.

## Target Customer

The digital mental health platforms — Lyra, Spring Health, Modern Health — have the scale and multilingual infrastructure and lack the occupational trauma specialism. A partnership with an emergency services peer support organisation is the most credible route to a product.

Buyers are the moderation vendors and, more usefully, the platforms specifying supply-chain welfare terms. The same adaptation serves every screen-delivered trauma workforce: emergency dispatch, child protection, claims handlers, investigative journalism verification and in-house platform trust and safety teams.

## Impact If Solved

The mechanism with the best evidence becomes available in an industry that currently offers the one with the weakest. That is a large change in expected outcome for a modest change in structure.

Resolving the confidentiality constraint would be the most consequential single act. It costs nothing, requires only a policy decision about a cleared circle, and it unblocks the support that people in every comparable profession rely on most.

And support that is routine and structural rather than requested removes the self-identification barrier that currently filters out the people in the worst position.

# Project Performance as the Missing Half of Qualification

**Niche:** [[niches/general-contractors/subcontractor-prequalification-data/profile|Subcontractor Prequalification Data]]
**Industry:** [[industries/general-contractors|General Contractors]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Qualification assesses whether a subcontractor can afford to do the work and has not been hurting people; it says nothing about whether they show up, which is what the Pass 1 analysis says general contractors actually need to know.

## The Problem
Prequalification answers financial capacity and safety record, because those are documentable from statements and federal data. The question a project manager actually has is the one Pass 1 names: which subs show up, do quality work, and do not inflate change orders. That knowledge exists — every general contractor forms it on every project — and it lives in individual superintendents' heads, is not shared across the firm, and is lost when they leave. The qualification platform sits between many general contractors and the same subcontractor population, which makes it the only party positioned to aggregate performance, and it collects none of it.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Performance assessment is subjective and contested in a way financial statements are not: a subcontractor rated poorly may dispute it, and a platform publishing performance ratings takes on a role its general contractor customers may not want to be seen supporting. Contributing performance data also reveals something about the contributing general contractor's own project management. And there is no established taxonomy for what performance means, so contributions would be incomparable without one being designed first.

## What to Build
Structured performance capture at project close, designed to be quick and comparable: schedule adherence, manpower against commitment, rework incidence, change order behaviour, and closeout responsiveness — factual measures rather than opinions, recorded against a defined scale. Contributed in exchange for access to the aggregate, which is the only structure that makes participation rational and is exactly how the financial side of these platforms already works. Aggregation must protect the contributor, so ratings are reported at a level that cannot be attributed to a single general contractor. On that base, qualification becomes what customers actually want: financial capacity and safety alongside a performance profile built from many projects rather than from one superintendent's memory. And the joined data answers a question nobody can currently ask — which qualification signals actually predict project performance, which is the validation this product category has never had.

## Target Customer
VPs of product and chief risk officers at qualification platforms, and the general contractor operations leaders whose subcontractor knowledge Pass 1 describes as informal and lost with departures.

## Impact If Built
Completes a product that currently answers half the question, and does it with data only a platform sitting between many general contractors could ever assemble. It also converts the industry's most cited tacit-knowledge problem into an institutional asset, which is the underlying purpose of the whole category.

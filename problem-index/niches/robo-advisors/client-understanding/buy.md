## The Problem
Behavioural finance is an established field with robust findings: loss aversion, recency bias, the behaviour gap between fund returns and investor returns, the effect of framing on allocation decisions. The findings were established in surveys, experiments and aggregate fund flow data. The digital advice platform has the population, the instrumentation and the intervention channel to apply them individually, and applies them as marketing copy.

## What Already Exists
Behavioural finance research; investor behaviour gap measurement from fund flows; risk tolerance questionnaire instruments with validation literature; advisor coaching frameworks; and nudge and choice architecture practice.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is from population findings to individual management. It requires: (1) individual prediction rather than population effects, since knowing that investors on average panic does not identify which client will — this is the substantive adaptation and is what the platform's data uniquely supports; (2) intervention as a measurable action rather than as advice given in a meeting, which makes the effect estimable for the first time; (3) real-time operation during a drawdown rather than retrospective analysis; (4) a regulatory frame where advice is supervised, so behavioural intervention must be designed to be defensible; and (5) a fee model where retention rather than transaction revenue is the outcome, which aligns the platform's interest with the client's in a way the brokerage context never did.

## Target Customer
Product, investment and research leadership, behavioural finance researchers with no access to individual data, and advice platform vendors treating risk tolerance as a form field.

## Impact If Solved
The findings are population-level and the platform needs individual prediction. Its behavioural record is the first dataset capable of supplying it, and the field has been waiting for exactly this data.

# Advice That Fits the Balance

**Industry:** [[industries/robo-advisors|Robo-Advisors]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** At four thousand dollars the allocation barely matters and the contribution rate is everything, and the product spends all its intelligence on the allocation.

**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to deliver something worth having to a client whose fee revenue is a few dollars a year — and whoever makes that economically real owns the segment every competitor is quietly trying to grow out of.

## The Problem
For a client with a small balance, the difference between a good and a mediocre allocation is a few dollars a year. The difference between contributing fifty dollars a month and two hundred is the entire outcome. So is capturing an employer match they are leaving on the table, paying down a credit card before investing, or having enough set aside not to liquidate at the first emergency. The platform optimises the part that does not matter much and says nothing about the parts that do, because the product was designed around portfolio management.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The product was built portfolio-first and the segment was treated as a smaller version of the same client, so nobody asked what actually drives outcomes at that balance. Advice about debt and saving is not obviously within the advisory relationship. The segment's revenue does not justify bespoke development on its own. And success is measured in assets gathered, which makes small balances a pipeline rather than an audience.

## What to Build
Advise on what actually moves the outcome. Model which decision has the largest effect for this client given their balance, income and contribution pattern, which is the core and will usually not be the allocation. Surface employer match capture, since leaving a match uncaptured is the largest single loss available to this segment and is frequently invisible to the client. Address the ordering question honestly — emergency fund, high-interest debt, match, then investing — because a platform that only ever recommends investing is not advising. Focus the product on contribution behaviour, as that is where the leverage is and where behavioural design actually works. Detect the client heading for a liquidation because of a cash shortfall rather than a market event, since the remedy is completely different. Keep the cost to serve low enough that the segment is genuinely viable, which is the constraint the whole design exists inside. Measure outcomes in progress toward a goal rather than in returns, because returns on a small balance are noise. Make the advice legible without financial literacy, since the audience is the one the industry never served. Resolve the scope question deliberately, as guidance on saving and debt can be delivered as education where advice is constrained. And measure the segment on client outcomes rather than on conversion to larger balances, which is the change that makes the rest possible.

## Target Customer
Product and growth leadership, clients the traditional industry never served, workplace plan providers reaching the same people, and financial wellness vendors adjacent to the problem.

## Impact If Built
The product was built portfolio-first and the segment was treated as a smaller version of the same client. At this balance the contribution rate and the uncaptured match dominate the outcome, and neither is part of the product.

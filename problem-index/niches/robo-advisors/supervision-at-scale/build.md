# Supervising the Template, Not the Send

**Niche:** [[niches/robo-advisors/supervision-at-scale/profile|Supervision at Template Scale]]
**Industry:** [[industries/robo-advisors|Robo-Advisors]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** The unit of risk is the template and the personalisation rule, and the unit of review is the individual message.
**Tags:** #compliance #large-language-models #evaluation-metrics #workflow-orchestration #automation #descriptive-statistics #confidence-intervals #hypothesis-testing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to supervise communications where one approved template reaches two hundred thousand people — and whoever redesigns supervision around the template rather than the message wins a function that currently samples the wrong thing entirely.

## The Problem
A template goes out to two hundred thousand clients with personalised balances, projections and allocations generated by rules. If the template is fine, every one of those messages is fine. If a personalisation rule produces a misleading projection for a particular client shape, thousands of messages are wrong and a percentage sample will catch it only by luck. Supervision reviews individual messages after they are sent, which is the wrong unit, at the wrong time, in the wrong volume.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The sampling rule came from a regulatory framework written for individual advisors, so the control was adopted as specified rather than adapted to the medium — compliance moved to the new context and the control did not. Testing generated variants before a send requires engineering that compliance cannot commission. Reviewers are measured on volume sampled. And nobody has argued the case to a regulator for a different control.

## What to Build
Move supervision upstream to the template and the rule. Review templates and personalisation logic as the supervised artefacts, which is the core and matches the control to where the risk actually is. Generate the variant space before sending and test it, since a rule's edge cases are enumerable and are where the failures live. Detect the generated output that is misleading for a client segment — a projection that implies certainty, a comparison that flatters, a figure that is technically true and practically wrong — because that is what a reviewer would catch and cannot at this volume. Sample by variant rather than by message, which concentrates review where the content differs. Direct the freed reviewer time to genuine one-off communications, since those are the unexamined risk today. Analyse the whole send for suitability across segments, as a message that is fine for most clients and wrong for a subset is the characteristic failure of the medium. Keep an auditable record of what was reviewed and why the control is designed this way, which is what makes it defensible. Make the case to the regulator explicitly rather than layering a new control on the old one. Monitor for drift when templates are edited, because a small copy change can alter meaning. And measure supervision coverage in terms of risk rather than in percentage of messages, which is the metric that would justify the whole redesign.

## Target Customer
Compliance and supervision leadership, reviewers spending their days on duplicates, regulators examining a control that no longer fits, and supervision vendors whose products count messages.

## Impact If Built
Compliance moved to a new medium and the control did not. Supervising the template and its personalisation logic puts review where the risk is and frees the time currently spent reading the same email twenty thousand times.

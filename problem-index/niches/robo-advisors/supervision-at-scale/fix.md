# Reviewing the Same Email Twenty Thousand Times

**Niche:** [[niches/robo-advisors/supervision-at-scale/profile|Supervision at Template Scale]]
**Industry:** [[industries/robo-advisors|Robo-Advisors]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The reviewer's sample is ten percent of sends, and ninety-nine percent of that sample is one template repeated.
**Tags:** #worker-facing #compliance #quick-win #automation #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #workflow-orchestration #large-language-models
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to supervise communications where one approved template reaches two hundred thousand people — and whoever redesigns supervision around the template rather than the message wins a function that currently samples the wrong thing entirely.

## The Problem
The supervision queue is generated by a sampling rule applied to all outbound communications. Because bulk sends dominate volume, the sample is overwhelmingly copies of the same few templates. The reviewer reads the market commentary email, then reads it again with a different name and balance, then again. Meanwhile the genuinely individual communications — a service associate's written reply, an unusual one-off notice — are a vanishing fraction of the sample and are where an actual problem would be.

## Why It's Still Broken
The sampling rate is expressed as a percentage of messages, so the queue composition follows message volume mechanically — and nobody looked at what the queue was actually made of. Reviewer productivity is measured in items dispositioned, which the duplicates flatter. Changing the sampling basis feels like weakening a control. And the reviewers who notice are not the ones who set the rule.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Deduplicate the queue and redirect the attention. Group identical template sends into one review item, which is the fix and immediately reveals how little unique content is being reviewed. Report the queue's composition by template versus individual, since that single breakdown makes the problem undeniable. Stratify the sample so individual communications are represented properly, because they are the ones a reviewer's judgement is actually for. Keep the percentage satisfied while changing what the sample contains, as the two are separable and the objection is about the number rather than the content. Show the reviewer the personalised variants that differ rather than those that do not, which is where template risk concentrates. Review templates before the send as a separate queue, since that is the moment a finding can prevent anything. Track findings by source, which will show how few come from bulk sends. Let reviewers flag a template rather than a message, because they currently have no way to raise a systemic issue. Measure time spent per unique item, as that is the true productivity number and is invisible today. And document the change for the regulator, since the case is straightforward and being able to make it is the point.

## Who Feels the Pain
Reviewers reading the same email all day; compliance leadership whose coverage is nominal; and firms whose real communication risk is outside the sample.

## Impact If Fixed
The sampling rate follows message volume mechanically and nobody examined the queue's composition. Deduplicating template sends and stratifying the sample satisfies the same percentage while reviewing something real.

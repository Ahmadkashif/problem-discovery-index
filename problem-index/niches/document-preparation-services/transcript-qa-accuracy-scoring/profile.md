# Transcript QA & Accuracy Scoring

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/document-preparation-services|Document Preparation Services]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to produce an accuracy score that the client, the vendor and the editor all accept as the basis for service credits and pay, and whoever scores every transcript by error severity, not a hand-checked sample, at auditor-level agreement takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$150M US spend on transcript QA staff, third-party accuracy audits and QA tooling across medical, general and evidentiary transcription (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~4% (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — auditors re-listen and mark errors on spreadsheets or score sheets
**Target Buyer:** Director of Quality at a documentation vendor or transcription platform; HIM director commissioning audits
**Automation Potential:** Very high for detecting and classifying differences; human review reserved for disputed severity

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Accuracy is what the client pays for and what the worker is paid on, and it is measured by sampling. AHDI's 2025 best practices set the medical scheme: 98.0 minimum, critical errors at three points, two scoring methods (one fails any report with a critical error, the other divides errors by lines and can let one through in a long report), random samples such as the "5/3 method", and pages on keeping auditors consistent. Platforms run their own rubrics for verbatim style and speaker attribution. The same score feeds vendors' service credits, editors' incentive pay and platforms' worker levels, so disagreement about it is disagreement about money.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Score sheets and spreadsheets, vendors' internal QA modules, word-error-rate scripts that ignore severity, and manual double audits for calibration. The gaps: a few percent of reports scored, severity judged inconsistently between auditors, and no link between audit findings and the patterns that cause them.

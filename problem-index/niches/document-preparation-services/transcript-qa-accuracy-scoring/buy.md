# Contact-Centre Quality Management for Transcripts

**Niche:** [[niches/document-preparation-services/transcript-qa-accuracy-scoring/profile|Transcript QA & Accuracy Scoring]]
**Industry:** [[industries/document-preparation-services|Document Preparation Services]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Contact centres moved from sampling calls to scoring all of them with speech analytics; transcript QA still samples the way contact-centre QA did before speech analytics.
**Tags:** #transformers #large-language-models #evaluation-metrics #workflow-orchestration #compliance
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to produce an accuracy score that the client, the vendor and the editor all accept as the basis for service credits and pay, and whoever scores every transcript by error severity, not a hand-checked sample, at auditor-level agreement takes the account.

## The Problem
Transcript QA teams sample, re-listen, score, calibrate and coach, a workflow contact centres have already largely automated.

## What Already Exists
Contact-centre quality-management and conversation-analytics suites (NICE, Verint, Observe.AI and others) that score every interaction against configurable rubrics, run calibration sessions, route disputes and drive coaching.

## The Customization Gap
The unit of work is a document, not a conversation, and the rubric is about fidelity to audio. The adaptation compares the transcript against the recording and its draft rather than scoring an agent's behaviour. It encodes severity schemes such as AHDI's critical and noncritical categories and the client's chosen scoring method, and supports report-level fail rules. It must run inside business-associate agreements for medical work, and feed disputes from editors back into rubric calibration.

## Target Customer
Quality leads at documentation vendors and platforms that already understand contact-centre tooling.

## Impact If Solved
A proven QA workflow, from calibration and dispute handling to coaching, applied to transcripts without building it from scratch.

# Niche Analysis — Document Preparation Services

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/document-preparation-services|Document Preparation Services]]

## Niche Selection

NAICS 561410 is the trade that turns speech and rough drafts into accurate, formatted text for a fee per line, per audio minute, per page or per document. It took ~$3.51B in 2022 at 3,139 employer firms, and three of every four establishments employ fewer than five people. Two forces shape every niche. A machine now writes the first draft, so the human product is the correction. And the client owns the source material: a health system's dictation, a police department's evidence, a job seeker's career history, a doctoral candidate's dissertation. The eight niches below split the industry by what operators and their vendors actually compete over. Market sizes are estimates of what US buyers spend on the service and its tooling within each niche, including home-based freelancers and offshore-delivered work. They overlap and are not shares of Census receipts.

## Why These Niches

Medical transcription takes the first market-share slot because healthcare dictation is still the largest single body of paid transcription. It is also where the industry's economics are most exposed: per-line pricing, AHDI's 98.0 quality floor and a buyer moving to front-end speech recognition and ambient scribes. General transcription platforms take the second because per-minute, hybrid human-and-machine transcription for business, research, media and education is where volume has concentrated, and where the human tier now has to justify itself against machine output at an eighth of the price. The two low-digitized niches still run on mailed drives, foot pedals, phone interviews and Word files. Evidentiary transcription carries discovery and trial clocks and a chain-of-custody record. Resume writing turns a phone interview into a document whose outcome nobody measures. The two underserved audiences are people the general tools ignore: doctoral candidates, many of them writing in a second language, who must pass a graduate school's format review without crossing its line on third-party editing; and federal and transitioning military applicants who since September 2025 must fit a career into OPM's two-page limit against a specialized-experience screen. The two automatable niches are the most rule-bound work in the trade: scoring transcripts against a severity scheme, and bringing documents into line with a named style guide.

## Filter Notes

Seven of the eight level-1 niches are terminal. Resume Writing & Career Documents is not. Its contested sentence produced two contests with different winners. At volume, platforms and their contractor networks compete to turn a questionnaire and an old resume into a keyword-matched, ATS-readable document in the fewest paid writer-hours, and the winner is whoever holds revisions and refunds down at a flat per-order writer fee. At the senior end, boutiques compete to position a leader's record so retained recruiters and boards shortlist them, and the winner is whoever turns a long discovery interview into quantified, verifiable leadership evidence. The inputs, prices (tens versus thousands of dollars), channels and measures of success differ. It decomposes into **Volume Resume Platforms & Writer Networks** and **Executive & Senior-Career Resume Boutiques**.

Three terminal calls were close. **Medical Transcription** could have split into acute-care hospital dictation and physician-practice work. It was kept whole because one contest, turnaround and an AHDI score at the lowest edit cost per line, governs both. The practices moving to ambient scribes are leaving the industry for the software in [[industries/healthcare-practice-software|Healthcare Practice Software]]. **General Transcription Platforms** and **Evidentiary Transcription** share workers and tools. They are separate because the evidentiary contest is won on a transcript's defensibility under cross-examination and its custody record, not on routing price. **Transcript QA & Accuracy Scoring** overlaps the medical niche, where AHDI's scheme lives. It is kept as its own function because scoring runs across medical, general and evidentiary work and is bought separately, as audits health systems commission and as worker-level scoring platforms need.

Five candidates were considered and rejected. **Accessible document remediation** (tagging PDFs to WCAG and PDF/UA ahead of DOJ's Title II dates) is already a niche in [[niches/digital-accessibility-firms/document-and-media-accessibility/profile|Document & Media Accessibility]] under Digital Accessibility Firms, and this industry does not claim it again. **Film and television captioning** belongs to [[niches/postproduction-studios/captions-subtitles-and-accessibility/profile|Captions, Subtitles & Accessibility Files]], whose contest runs on platform timed-text specs and FCC rules. **Legal form preparation for self-represented customers** is [[niches/legal-support-services/self-represented-document-preparers/profile|Self-Represented Litigants & Legal Document Preparers]] under Legal Support Services. Census codes some of these preparers here, but the unauthorised-practice line is the contest, and it belongs there. **Typing and secretarial services** fail the filter: the remaining demand is general remote assistance analysed under [[industries/virtual-assistant-services|Virtual Assistant Services]], and the residual contest is a generic "lowest hourly rate". **Transcripts of court proceedings** (depositions, hearings, digital court recordings) are NAICS 561492 and belong to [[industries/court-reporting-firms|Court Reporting Firms]].

## Why These Pockets

No pocket qualifies, and the operators themselves fail the gate first. A transcription, resume or editing shop sells text, not research; the largest employ QA editors and formatters, who are production staff, not an insight function. Every rich corpus in the industry belongs to someone else. Dictation and clinical audio are protected health information held under business-associate agreements, which puts a HIPAA kill switch on the ambient documentation vendors (27) and keeps the medical outsourcers' QA departments production work. Evidence recordings belong to agencies, unpublished manuscripts to their authors (the author-services firms, 27, kill switch), parsed resumes to employers and staffing firms (24, kill switch), and API customers' audio to the customers (22, kill switch). OPM's assessment units (28) hold applicant records for agencies under the Privacy Act. The jail-call analytics vendors that sit beside evidentiary transcription are already logged under Public Defenders as [[niches/public-defenders/correctional-communications-analytics/profile|🔍 Correctional Communications & Data Analytics]] (39, kill switch), and dissertation abstracting under Libraries & Archives as [[niches/libraries-and-archives/scholarly-abstracting-indexing-databases/profile|🔍 Scholarly Abstracting & Indexing Databases]] (42); neither is profiled twice.

The clean near-miss is healthcare IT performance research (44). It holds what the vault looks for: a provider-interview corpus no competitor has, research that is itself the invoice, and coverage of exactly the speech recognition and ambient tools that are hollowing out medical transcription. It falls short on the market around it. In its segment it is effectively one firm with a self-set publishing calendar, which caps Q4 and Q5. Its general-IT counterpart, [[niches/cloud-infrastructure-consultants/it-research-advisory-firms/profile|🔍 IT Research & Advisory Firms]], is indexed at 54 on a market of several large houses. The insight business that does qualify near the career-document half of the industry is already indexed elsewhere: [[niches/corporate-training/labor-market-skills-data/profile|🔍 Labour Market & Skills Taxonomy Data Providers]] (54) sells the posting and skills data resume writers and parsers key against. Under IT Staffing Firms the same kind of vendor is scored at 49 as [[niches/it-staffing-firms/labor-market-analytics-providers/profile|🔍 Labour Market Analytics Providers]]. Outplacement firms (32) hold the best outcome data on career documents, how fast displaced workers land, but sell transition services rather than that insight.

The rest fail the gate honestly. AHDI, the resume and editorial associations and the style-manual teams each run on fewer than ten people, which puts AHDI well below the coding bodies already logged under Medical Billing as [[niches/medical-billing/coding-certification-bodies/profile|🔍 Coding Certification Bodies]] (44). Thesis offices and HHS's HIPAA investigators are reviewers and enforcers, which is production. The resume platforms' "career studies" are marketing content from small teams. The vault's favourite shape, a dated judgement whose outcome the producer holds and never grades, sits inside the operators. Every edit of a machine draft is an expert prediction that a QA audit later scores, and every resume is a bet on interviews the writer never hears about. That is a product opportunity for selling to the larger operators, set out in [[niches/document-preparation-services/transcript-qa-accuracy-scoring/build|Severity Scoring on Every Edit Diff]]. It is not an insight business those operators could sell.

# Two Pages for a Twenty-Year Career

**Niche:** [[niches/document-preparation-services/federal-transition-job-seekers/profile|Federal & Military-Transition Job Seekers]]
**Industry:** [[industries/document-preparation-services|Document Preparation Services]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The federal resume went from an often ~3–5-page document (estimate) to a two-page cap at a fixed date, and writers and applicants are cutting by instinct.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #worker-facing #quick-win #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get an applicant rated qualified on an announcement's specialized-experience screen inside OPM's two-page limit, and whoever translates a career, often a military one, into the grade's qualification language in two pages takes the account.

## The Problem
Since September 2025 USAJOBS refuses resumes over two pages. Applicants with long careers, especially retiring service members and senior civil servants, must choose what to keep. Many cut the wrong things: the evidence for the specialized experience, the dates and hours that prove duration, or the most relevant recent work.

## Why It's Still Broken
The rule is new, writers' templates were built for long resumes, and nobody knows yet which compressions HR specialists credit.

## What a Fix Looks Like
For each announcement, rank every element of the long master resume by how much it contributes to the qualification evidence. Build the two-page version from the top down, keep the dates and duration statements the screen needs, and show the applicant what was left out and why.

## Who Feels the Pain
Senior and transitioning applicants; federal resume writers rebuilding every client's template at once.

## Impact If Fixed
Two-page resumes that keep the qualifying evidence, built in minutes per announcement instead of an evening.

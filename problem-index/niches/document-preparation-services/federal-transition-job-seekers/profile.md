# Federal & Military-Transition Job Seekers

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/document-preparation-services|Document Preparation Services]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get an applicant rated qualified on an announcement's specialized-experience screen inside OPM's two-page limit, and whoever translates a career, often a military one, into the grade's qualification language in two pages takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$80M US spend on federal resumes, USAJOBS application support and military-to-civilian resume translation (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~2% (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — writers work from announcements, OPM qualification standards and their own experience of how HR specialists screen
**Target Buyer:** Federal resume writer or small federal-careers firm; transition programme managers as a channel
**Automation Potential:** Medium-High for matching experience to announcement language; low for judging what an HR specialist will credit

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Federal hiring runs on rules private-sector resume advice ignores. An announcement sets minimum qualifications, usually a year of specialized experience at the next lower grade, and an HR specialist checks the resume against them before any hiring manager sees it. Since 27 September 2025 OPM has required hiring resumes submitted through USAJOBS to be no longer than two pages, and USAJOBS blocks longer uploads. Under the Merit Hiring Plan, competitive-service announcements at GS-05 and above also carry four short essay questions. Federal resumes had often run to ~3–5 pages or more (estimate), with hours and grades for every job. Transitioning service members face both the federal rules and the translation of military roles into civilian terms.

## Current Tools & Gaps
The USAJOBS resume builder, OPM's applicant guidance, military skills translators, generic keyword matchers and writers' own templates. The gaps: no tool that maps a career to a specific announcement's specialized-experience language, nothing that helps choose what survives the cut to two pages, and no feedback on why an applicant was rated not qualified.

# Specialized Experience Matched to the Announcement

**Niche:** [[niches/document-preparation-services/federal-transition-job-seekers/profile|Federal & Military-Transition Job Seekers]]
**Industry:** [[industries/document-preparation-services|Document Preparation Services]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Federal applicants are rated not qualified because their resume doesn't show the announcement's specialized experience in words an HR specialist will credit, and nothing checks before they apply.
**Tags:** #bert #transformers #large-language-models #feature-engineering #evaluation-metrics #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get an applicant rated qualified on an announcement's specialized-experience screen inside OPM's two-page limit, and whoever translates a career, often a military one, into the grade's qualification language in two pages takes the account.

## The Problem
Every announcement lists the specialized experience required at the grade, often as several duty statements. The HR specialist reads the resume for evidence of each, at the right level, over the required period. A logistics NCO with exactly the right experience describes it in military terms, and the specialist, who can't credit what isn't written, rates them not qualified.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Federal resume writing is a small craft market, and the rules changed sharply in 2025. Matching tools are built for private-sector keyword screens, not for a human specialist applying a qualification standard.

## What to Build
A matcher that parses the announcement's specialized-experience statements and the applicant's career history (including military evaluations and job descriptions). It links each requirement to evidence at the right level and period, flags requirements with no evidence or evidence at the wrong grade, and proposes civilian phrasing for the evidence that exists. It is trained and checked on writers' records of which resumes were rated qualified for which announcements.

## Target Customer
Federal resume writers and small federal-careers firms; transition programmes serving separating service members.

## Impact If Built
Fewer qualified applicants screened out on wording, and writers able to serve more clients under the two-page rule.

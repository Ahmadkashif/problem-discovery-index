# Executive & Senior-Career Resume Boutiques

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/document-preparation-services|Document Preparation Services]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn a senior leader's record into a resume, LinkedIn profile and bio that retained recruiters and boards shortlist, and whoever converts a long discovery interview into quantified, verifiable leadership evidence fastest takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$300M US spend on executive resumes, bios, board CVs and personal-branding packages (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~9% (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — long interviews, bespoke Word documents, referral-driven sales
**Target Buyer:** Principal of an executive resume or career-branding boutique
**Automation Potential:** Medium-Low — research and drafting assist; positioning and the client relationship stay human

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A vice president, a C-suite candidate or an aspiring non-executive director buys more than a resume: a positioning narrative, a LinkedIn presence that retained search researchers will find, a bio for board submissions and sometimes coaching for the search process itself. Packages often run to thousands of dollars (estimate), sold on referral, with discovery interviews running for hours. The audience is the shortlist builder at a retained search firm (see [[industries/executive-search-firms|Executive Search Firms]]) or a board's nominating committee. They read for scope, P&L, transformation evidence and verifiable claims against profile databases such as those under [[niches/executive-search-firms/executive-board-profile-data/profile|Executive & Board Profile Data Providers]].

## Current Tools & Gaps
Word, long recorded interviews, public company filings and press for context, LinkedIn, and the writer's judgement. The gaps: hours turning interview transcripts into quantified evidence, claims that can't be checked against public records before a recruiter checks them, and no way to see how search researchers actually find and screen candidates.

# Doctoral Candidates & Multilingual Researchers

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/document-preparation-services|Document Preparation Services]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get a dissertation through the graduate school's format review and the committee's reading on the first submission without the editing crossing into authorship under the university's policy, and whoever leaves an edit record the committee would accept takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$300M US spend on dissertation and thesis editing, proofreading and formatting, plus manuscript editing for early-career researchers (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~9% (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — all work is in Word or LaTeX, but rules, disclosures and deadlines are tracked by hand
**Target Buyer:** Owner of a dissertation editing and formatting service; graduate school thesis office as a channel
**Automation Potential:** Medium — formatting and mechanical language fixes automate; the line between editing and authorship needs judgement

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
US universities awarded 58,131 research doctorates in 2024, and temporary visa holders earned 38% of science and engineering doctorates, a majority in computer science, engineering and mathematics. Many write a book-length document in a second language against a graduate school's manual and deposit deadline. Universities limit what a third-party editor may do, typically allowing proofreading and formatting but not rewriting arguments, and some ask students to disclose paid editing. AI writing tools and detectors have made the line sharper. Editors serving this audience, from solo academic editors to firms such as Editage, Enago, AJE (now owned by MPS) and Scribendi, sell help that must stay visibly on the right side of it.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Word tracked changes, PerfectIt, reference managers, LaTeX templates, Paperpal, Writefull and Grammarly, iThenticate at deposit. The gaps: no record separating language edits from substantive ones, format checks that ignore the school's manual, and no help for students choosing an editor who is allowed to do what they need.

# A Disclosed Edit Ledger for the Committee

**Niche:** [[niches/document-preparation-services/doctoral-multilingual-researchers/profile|Doctoral Candidates & Multilingual Researchers]]
**Industry:** [[industries/document-preparation-services|Document Preparation Services]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A committee asked whether a dissertation was edited can see that it was, but not what the editor changed, so honest students and honest editors carry the same suspicion as those who crossed the line.
**Tags:** #bert #transformers #large-language-models #evaluation-metrics #compliance #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get a dissertation through the graduate school's format review and the committee's reading on the first submission without the editing crossing into authorship under the university's policy, and whoever leaves an edit record the committee would accept takes the account.

## The Problem
University policies allow proofreading and formatting by a third party but not rewriting the argument, analysis or findings. A dissertation goes through an editor's thousands of tracked changes, which are accepted and gone by the time the committee reads it. If a question is raised, by an advisor, an AI detector or a whistle-blower, nobody can show which changes were language and which were content.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Editors' records are Word files on their own computers. Universities write policies but have no standard for evidence. Editors fear that exposing their changes invites scrutiny of normal work.

## What to Build
A ledger that classifies every tracked change into categories that map onto typical university policy: spelling and grammar, word choice, sentence restructuring, formatting and citation style, and content flags (claims, reasoning, results) that an editor should comment on but not change. It produces a summary the student can disclose to the committee: counts by category, samples, and a statement of what the editor did not do. Policies differ, so the category-to-policy map is configurable per institution.

## Target Customer
Dissertation editors and editing firms that want to sell compliance as a feature; graduate schools that want a standard for disclosure.

## Impact If Built
A defensible record for honest work, a clearer line for editors, and a basis for universities to permit language help instead of fearing it.

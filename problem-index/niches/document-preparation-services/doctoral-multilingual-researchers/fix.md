# The Deposit Deadline Missed for a Margin

**Niche:** [[niches/document-preparation-services/doctoral-multilingual-researchers/profile|Doctoral Candidates & Multilingual Researchers]]
**Industry:** [[industries/document-preparation-services|Document Preparation Services]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A defended dissertation goes back and forth with the format reviewer over page numbers and captions until the term's deposit deadline passes.
**Tags:** #cnns #object-detection #automation #quick-win #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get a dissertation through the graduate school's format review and the committee's reading on the first submission without the editing crossing into authorship under the university's policy, and whoever leaves an edit record the committee would accept takes the account.

## The Problem
After the defence come revisions, then deposit. The reviewer returns a list: roman numerals missing on the abstract, a landscape table with the page number in the wrong place, a figure caption below instead of above, a heading not matching the table of contents. Each round takes days, and the deposit deadline decides whether the student graduates this term. For an international student, a slipped term can affect visa status and job start dates.

## Why It's Still Broken
Manuals differ by institution, templates break when chapters are pasted in, and formatters check by eye against a PDF manual. The same faults recur every term and nobody encodes them.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A pre-deposit check against the school's own manual on the rendered PDF, listing violations by page in the reviewer's wording, with mechanical fixes applied automatically and the rest explained, and run early enough that the first submission is the last.

## Who Feels the Pain
Doctoral candidates near the finish; formatters handling peaks before every deadline; thesis office reviewers repeating the same corrections.

## Impact If Fixed
One review round instead of several, and fewer graduations slipping a term over formatting.

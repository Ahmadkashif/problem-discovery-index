**One-liner:** Academic writing assistants fix language well, but they don't know the student's discipline style or what the student's university lets an assistant change.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #compliance #automation #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get a dissertation through the graduate school's format review and the committee's reading on the first submission without the editing crossing into authorship under the university's policy, and whoever leaves an edit record the committee would accept takes the account.

## The Problem
Students writing in a second language use writing assistants heavily, and editors use them to speed their own passes. The tools offer to paraphrase whole paragraphs as readily as they fix an article, and that is exactly the kind of help many university policies forbid.

## What Already Exists
Paperpal, Writefull, Grammarly and Trinka for academic language; reference managers for citation style; generic language models.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation adds a policy mode that limits suggestions to what the student's institution permits (grammar, spelling, word choice, citation format) and withholds paraphrase and content generation. Discipline profiles enforce the relevant manual (APA 7, AMA, Chicago, IEEE) and the field's terminology. Every suggestion accepted is logged for the edit ledger, and the tool runs inside Word and Overleaf where theses are actually written.

## Target Customer
Graduate schools licensing tools for students; editing firms equipping their editors; writing centres.

## Impact If Solved
Language help students can use without fear and editors can document, and fewer integrity cases that start with a well-meant paraphrase.

# The Third Revision Round

**Niche:** [[niches/document-preparation-services/volume-resume-platforms/profile|Volume Resume Platforms & Writer Networks]]
**Industry:** [[industries/document-preparation-services|Document Preparation Services]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Revision rounds are where flat-fee orders lose money, and most trace back to a target role nobody pinned down before drafting.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #workflow-orchestration #worker-facing #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn a questionnaire and an old resume into a keyword-matched, ATS-readable resume in the fewest paid writer-hours, and whoever holds revision and refund rates down at a flat per-order writer fee takes the account.

## The Problem
A client asks for changes, then more, then a different direction entirely: they meant product management, not project management; they want to apply to two kinds of role. The writer, paid per order, absorbs the hours. The platform's revision policy limits time, not scope, and support handles the dispute.

## Why It's Still Broken
Intake records what the client has done, not precisely what they are applying for. Target-role ambiguity surfaces only when the client reads a draft written for one interpretation.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Before drafting, require the client to confirm two or three real target postings and a one-paragraph positioning statement the writer drafts from intake. Any later change of target is classed as a new scope with its own price, and revision requests are logged by cause so the platform can see which intake gaps produce them.

## Who Feels the Pain
Contract writers working unpaid rounds; support staff mediating; clients frustrated that the first draft "missed the point".

## Impact If Fixed
Fewer rounds per order, clearer scope when a client changes course, and data on which intake questions prevent revisions.

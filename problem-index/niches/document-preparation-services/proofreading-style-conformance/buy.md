# Consistency Checkers Loaded With the Client's Style

**Niche:** [[niches/document-preparation-services/proofreading-style-conformance/profile|Proofreading & Style-Guide Conformance]]
**Industry:** [[industries/document-preparation-services|Document Preparation Services]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Consistency checkers already catch variant spellings and inconsistent capitalisation; they need each client's style sheet and terminology to be useful at scale.
**Tags:** #feature-engineering #large-language-models #automation #data-integration #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to bring a document into line with a named style guide while changing nothing the guide doesn't require, and whoever returns a change log the client can accept without rereading the document takes the account.

## The Problem
Editors run consistency checks on long documents, but each check starts from generic settings. Client-specific rules are added by hand each time or not at all.

## What Already Exists
PerfectIt (consistency checking with style sheets for common manuals), Word macros shared among editors, and grammar tools with limited custom dictionaries.

## The Customization Gap
The firm-level adaptation keeps a managed style-sheet library per client: rules, term lists, product names, legal defined terms. It is versioned when the client updates its guide, shared across every editor on the account, and connected to the firm's job intake so the right sheet loads automatically. Each job also produces a report of rule violations found and fixed, which the firm can show the client as evidence of conformance.

## Target Customer
Multi-editor proofreading and editing firms with recurring corporate and institutional clients.

## Impact If Solved
Consistent output across editors on the same account, and less time spent setting up each job.

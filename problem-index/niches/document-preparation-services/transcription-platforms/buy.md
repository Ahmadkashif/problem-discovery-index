# Contact-Centre Workforce Management for Transcript Queues

**Industry:** [[industries/document-preparation-services|Document Preparation Services]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Contact centres solved forecasting work against a service level decades ago; transcription platforms still staff a turnaround promise by watching the queue.
**Tags:** #time-series-forecasting #exponential-smoothing #optimization-fundamentals #workflow-orchestration #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to justify a human-verified tier priced near eight times the machine transcript, file by file, as recognisers improve, and whoever predicts which minutes actually need a human, and prices and routes them that way, takes the account.

## The Problem
Uploads spike after conferences, earnings seasons and the end of university terms, and customers choose 24-hour, 3-day or rush turnaround. The human pool is freelance and logs in when it chooses. Coordinators raise bonuses when the queue looks long, which is late and expensive.

## What Already Exists
Workforce management suites from the contact-centre world (NICE, Verint, Calabrio) forecast arrivals, compute required staffing against a service level and schedule agents. Gig-marketplace tooling handles pay incentives.

## The Customization Gap
Transcription queues differ in three ways the suites don't model. Work items have deadlines rather than wait-time targets. Their handling time varies with audio difficulty, so arrivals must be forecast in predicted editor-minutes, not files. And the workforce is freelance, so the lever is posted pay and queue visibility, not schedules. The adaptation forecasts editor-minutes by turnaround band, predicts freelancer supply by hour from login history, and sets targeted bonuses on specific hard files early enough to work.

## Target Customer
Operations managers at transcription platforms and at captioning and transcription vendors serving universities.

## Impact If Solved
Turnaround held with smaller, earlier, better-aimed incentives instead of late across-the-board bonuses.

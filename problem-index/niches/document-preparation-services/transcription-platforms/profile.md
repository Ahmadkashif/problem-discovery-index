# General Transcription Platforms (Per-Minute, Hybrid)

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/document-preparation-services|Document Preparation Services]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to justify a human-verified tier priced near eight times the machine transcript, file by file, as recognisers improve, and whoever predicts which minutes actually need a human, and prices and routes them that way, takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$900M US spend on per-minute business, research, media, education and general legal transcription, human and hybrid (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~25% (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** High — upload, recognition, freelancer queues and delivery are all online
**Target Buyer:** COO or Head of Operations at a transcription platform; media, research or legal-operations lead buying at volume
**Automation Potential:** Very high on clean audio; the residual is multi-speaker, accented, technical and noisy recordings

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Rev, GoTranscript, TranscribeMe, Verbit and 3Play Media sell transcripts by the audio minute to podcasters, journalists, market researchers running focus groups and interviews, academic researchers, enterprises, universities and law firms, with the human work done by home-based contractors in a task queue. Rev's public prices show the squeeze: $1.99 a minute human-verified against $0.25 for its machine tier, and its own recogniser, Reverb, was trained on 200,000 hours of transcripts its human workforce produced. The machine tier is good enough on clean audio, so the platform's business rests on selling the human pass only where it changes the result. Public universities' Title II accessibility date (26 April 2027 for large entities) adds education demand for accurate transcripts.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Speech-to-text engines (in-house, Whisper-class open models, Deepgram, AssemblyAI), diarisation, freelancer marketplaces with QA levels, and style guides for clean versus true verbatim. The gaps: no difficulty prediction before a file is priced or claimed, hard files sitting unclaimed near deadline, and no evidence for customers about which minutes the human changed.

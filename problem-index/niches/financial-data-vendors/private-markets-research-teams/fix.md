# The Researcher Who Leaves With the Contacts

**Niche:** [[niches/financial-data-vendors/private-markets-research-teams/profile|Private Markets Research & Collection Teams]]
**Industry:** [[industries/financial-data-vendors|Financial Data Vendors]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Response rates depend on a researcher knowing which person at which GP answers, how they like to be asked and what they disclosed last time, and that knowledge leaves with them.
**Tags:** #tacit-knowledge-ml #large-language-models #k-nearest-neighbors #worker-facing #workflow-orchestration #evaluation-metrics
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor's private-markets research team is fighting to grow coverage faster than the private universe grows, from sources that must be asked rather than parsed — and whoever automates the chase while keeping the GP and LP relationships intact wins the coverage race.

## The Problem
Private-markets research is relationship work at volume. Experienced researchers know which contacts respond, which pension records officers process requests quickly, which GPs will confirm a figure but not volunteer it. Turnover in the research team resets those relationships and coverage dips.

## Why It's Still Broken
Outreach history sits in individual inboxes and CRM notes that nobody structures; the knowledge is tacit and never captured as a pattern.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Structured outreach records per counterparty — who was asked, how, what was given, how long it took; retrieval of that history and of what worked with similar counterparties when a new researcher picks up coverage; drafted requests that reference the counterparty's prior disclosures; and response-likelihood estimates to plan the quarter.

## Who Feels the Pain
New researchers inheriting cold coverage, and the vendor's coverage metrics after every attrition wave.

## Impact If Fixed
Relationship knowledge becomes institutional, coverage stops dipping with turnover, and ramp-up time for new researchers shrinks.

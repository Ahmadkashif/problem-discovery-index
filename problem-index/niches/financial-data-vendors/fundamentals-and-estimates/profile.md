# Fundamentals & Estimates Data

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/financial-data-vendors|Financial Data Vendors]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Not terminal as stated — competitors here are fighting either to standardise a new filing correctly within hours and explain every derived number, or to hold the broadest contributed broker estimates and clean them into a trusted consensus; these are different contests with different winners, stated separately in the sub-niches.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$3.5B
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~20% of the segment
**Digital Adoption:** High — delivered digitally, produced substantially by hand
**Target Buyer:** Chief content officer / head of company data
**Automation Potential:** High — large, repeatable collection workloads with decades of labelled history

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Company fundamentals (as-reported and standardised financial statements, segments, KPIs) and broker estimates (contributed forecasts and the consensus built from them) are the numbers every equity model, screen, comp table and quant backtest rests on. Together they are the vendors' second-largest product shelf after the desktop itself.

It is **not terminal**. Fundamentals are produced from issuer filings by collection analysts; the contest is speed, mapping correctness and explainability. Estimates are produced from broker contributions; the contest is getting brokers to contribute at line-item detail and cleaning what they contribute. Different sources, different labour, different competitors — Visible Alpha's rise was a fight over detailed estimate contributions, not over who reads a footnote best.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Internal keying and validation tools, XBRL pre-population, broker contribution feeds and portals, rules-based consensus construction, sample QA. The gaps: collector and specialist judgement not recorded as decisions; no measured consistency; point-in-time history patchy for the earlier decades; and limited ability to show a client how a number was derived.

# Entitlements & Exchange Reporting

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/financial-data-vendors|Financial Data Vendors]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to prove, per user and per use, exactly what each client was entitled to and declared — and whoever makes the exchange audit an uneventful lookup keeps both the exchanges and the clients' market data managers on side.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$600M (tooling, staff and audit exposure across vendors and redistributors)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~3% of the segment
**Digital Adoption:** Low — entitlement systems are digital, the policy layer is spreadsheets
**Target Buyer:** Head of exchange relations / market data compliance
**Automation Potential:** High

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Exchanges license their data under policies that distinguish display, non-display and derived use, device counts and professional status, and audit vendors and clients against them. Vendors run entitlement systems such as LSEG's DACS and Bloomberg's EMRS; clients run inventory tools such as TRG Screen and Calero-MDSL. The monthly declaration and the periodic audit are where money changes hands, and they are prepared by a few specialists. Related contract-term problems for alternative datasets sit in [[niches/data-marketplace-brokers/licence-term-enforcement/profile|Licence Term Enforcement]].

## Current Tools & Gaps
Entitlement systems, declaration templates and portals, inventory platforms, spreadsheets. Gaps: policies stored as PDFs; no versioned rules engine; non-display declarations self-reported and unchecked; audit reconstruction manual.

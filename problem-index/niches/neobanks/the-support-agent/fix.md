# The Escalation That Goes Nowhere

**Niche:** [[niches/neobanks/the-support-agent/profile|The Support Agent]]
**Industry:** [[industries/neobanks|Neobanks]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The agent escalates the case to the risk team, the queue has no service commitment, and the member calls back three times before anyone looks at it.
**Tags:** #workflow-orchestration #compliance #evaluation-metrics #automation #quick-win #worker-facing #descriptive-statistics #confidence-intervals
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to let the person a frozen member reaches explain what happened and do something about it — and whoever does that turns the category's worst interaction into a resolvable one.

## The Problem
The agent believes the restriction is wrong. The member has explained themselves credibly and supplied a document. The agent escalates. The escalation enters a risk queue with no service commitment, prioritised by whatever the risk team is prioritising, which is not this. The member calls back the next day and speaks to a different agent who can see an escalation exists and nothing else. They call again. Eventually someone reviews it and restores the account. The escalation path exists on the org chart and functions as a place where cases wait.

## Why It's Still Broken
The escalation crosses from support into risk, and the receiving team's priorities are set by risk objectives rather than by member impact — a queue with no commitment across an organisational boundary is a queue that absorbs rather than resolves. Nobody measures time-to-resolution on escalations. Support cannot see the escalation's status, so each callback restarts. And the member's distress is not a metric in the receiving team's review.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Make the escalation a commitment rather than a queue. Set and publish a service commitment for restricted-account escalations, which is the fix and is a management decision rather than a technical one — a queue with a deadline behaves completely differently from one without. Prioritise by member impact, since a person who cannot pay rent and a person who noticed a limit are in different situations and currently wait the same. Show the agent the escalation's status and expected time, so a callback is informative rather than a restart. Let the agent attach evidence that clears the common cases directly, which removes a share of escalations entirely rather than speeding them up. Auto-resolve the cases that meet clear criteria, since some restrictions clear on a document and require no human judgement at all. Route to a reviewer with the case fully assembled, connecting to the analyst niche, so the review is fast when it happens. Report escalation volume and duration to both teams, which creates shared accountability for something currently owned by neither. Track repeat contacts per case, because that number measures the member's experience precisely and is the argument for the commitment. Give the member a status they can see themselves, which removes a large share of the callbacks. And measure hardship cases separately, since those are where the harm concentrates and an average resolution time conceals them entirely.

## Who Feels the Pain
Members calling three times about the same frozen account; agents escalating into a queue they know is slow; and institutions whose complaint volume and churn are driven by a handover with no commitment.

## Impact If Fixed
A queue with no commitment across an organisational boundary absorbs cases rather than resolving them, and the receiving team's priorities are not the member's. A published service commitment prioritised by member impact is a management decision, and letting the agent clear the common cases removes the escalation entirely.

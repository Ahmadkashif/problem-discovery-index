## The Problem
Cash flow forecasting is a serious discipline in corporate treasury. Expected receipts and payments are modelled with timing uncertainty, scenarios are run, minimum balance requirements are managed, and the forecast drives real decisions about borrowing and timing. Businesses do this because running out of cash is catastrophic. Consumers face the same problem with far higher stakes relative to their resources, and the tooling available to them is a balance, a transaction list and occasionally a spending pie chart.

## What Already Exists
Treasury cash forecasting with receipts and payments modelling; scenario analysis; minimum balance and liquidity management; timing uncertainty handling; and variance analysis against forecast.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a consumer with irregular income and no treasury function. It requires: (1) forecasting from observed transactions rather than from known contractual obligations, since a business knows its payables and a consumer's commitments must be inferred from the ledger — that inference is the substantive work and it is entirely feasible; (2) highly irregular income for a large share of the population, where gig, shift and multiple-job patterns break the simple periodic model every consumer budgeting tool assumes; (3) output that is an action rather than a report, since the user is not a treasurer and a forecast they cannot act on is noise; (4) forecast errors that cause real harm, so calibration and conservatism matter more than in a treasury context with a credit line; and (5) a provider whose fee income may depend on the customer running short, which is a conflict corporate treasury never has.

## Target Customer
Product teams at digital banks, financial health and budgeting vendors, and treasury practitioners for whom consumer cash flow is an unserved application.

## Impact If Solved
Treasury forecasts rigorously because running out of cash is catastrophic, and consumers face the same problem with higher relative stakes and a balance screen. Inferring commitments from the ledger rather than reading them from contracts is the substantive work and is feasible on a complete transaction record.

# Regulatory Reporting Practice

**Niche:** [[niches/neobanks/sponsor-bank-compliance/profile|Sponsor Bank Compliance]]
**Industry:** [[industries/neobanks|Neobanks]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Banks built regulatory reporting factories that generate many returns from one governed data layer, and fintechs assemble each partner's pack by hand.
**Tags:** #compliance #data-integration #workflow-orchestration #automation #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #confidence-intervals #graph-theory
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to produce compliance evidence once and satisfy any sponsor bank with it — and whoever does that removes the binding constraint on every product decision in the category.

## The Problem
Regulatory reporting in banking is an industrialised function. A governed data layer feeds many different returns, definitions are held centrally and versioned, lineage is traceable from a reported figure back to its source records, changes are controlled, and the whole thing is built to survive examination. Banks invested in this because producing dozens of returns by hand became impossible. Fintechs produce a comparable volume of evidence for their sponsor banks using spreadsheets and manual pulls.

## What Already Exists
Regulatory reporting platforms with governed data layers; centralised definition management; lineage from report to source; controlled change processes; and examination-ready evidence retention.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a recipient who is a commercial partner rather than a regulator. It requires: (1) requirements set by a partner's interpretation rather than by a published rule, so the specification is negotiated and changes with the partner's own examination experience — this moving and unpublished target is the substantive difference from regulatory reporting; (2) multiple partners with different interpretations of the same underlying obligation, requiring a mapping layer regulatory reporting does not need; (3) partner migration as a realistic event, which has no regulatory analogue and has become common in this category; (4) scale and cost suited to a small compliance team rather than a bank's reporting function; and (5) the reporting being a commercial gate on product launches, which gives it an operational urgency regulatory returns do not carry.

## Target Customer
Fintech compliance teams, sponsor banks overseeing multiple programmes, and regulatory reporting vendors for whom the sponsor-bank model is an unserved market.

## Impact If Solved
Banks industrialised reporting because manual production became impossible, and fintechs are at that point with spreadsheets. Requirements set by a partner's negotiated interpretation rather than a published rule is the moving target regulatory reporting never faces.

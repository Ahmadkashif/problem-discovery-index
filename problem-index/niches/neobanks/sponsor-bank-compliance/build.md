# The Same Pack, Rebuilt Per Partner

**Niche:** [[niches/neobanks/sponsor-bank-compliance/profile|Sponsor Bank Compliance]]
**Industry:** [[industries/neobanks|Neobanks]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Every neobank builds the same compliance reporting pack from scratch in the format its particular sponsor bank happens to want, and rebuilds it when the bank changes.
**Tags:** #compliance #workflow-orchestration #automation #data-integration #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #quick-win #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to produce compliance evidence once and satisfy any sponsor bank with it — and whoever does that removes the binding constraint on every product decision in the category.

## The Problem
The sponsor bank requires monthly reporting: transaction monitoring statistics, alert dispositions, customer risk ratings, complaint volumes, dispute metrics, adverse action counts, and a dozen other things. The substance is essentially the same at every sponsor bank because it derives from the same regulatory expectations. The format, the definitions, the cut dates and the delivery mechanism are specific to this bank. A small compliance team assembles it by hand every month, and if the fintech changes sponsor — which many have been forced to do — the whole apparatus is rebuilt for the next bank's preferences.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The reporting is treated as a relationship deliverable rather than as a product, which means it is shaped by whatever the partner asked for and never abstracted — the bespoke framing prevents anyone seeing that the underlying evidence is identical. Sponsor banks have no incentive to standardise and each has their own examination history driving their requests. Compliance teams are small and busy. And the industry has no body that could define a common pack.

## What to Build
Separate the evidence from the format. Model the underlying compliance evidence once — the events, the dispositions, the ratings, the metrics — and generate any partner's pack from it, which is the core and is the abstraction the category has never made despite the substance being common. Generate continuously rather than assembling monthly, so the pack is a view rather than a project and an examination request can be answered the same day. Map each partner's definitions explicitly, since the definitional differences are where the manual work actually is and they are enumerable. Monitor against the partner's thresholds continuously, so a metric heading toward a breach is visible rather than discovered in the monthly pack. Retain the evidence with lineage, which is what an examination actually requires and what a spreadsheet cannot provide. Support partner migration as a configuration change rather than a rebuild, which is the fix note's subject and has become a live operational need across the category. Give the sponsor bank a direct view rather than a document, which reduces their work as well and is a genuine relationship improvement. Handle the ad hoc request, which consumes more compliance time than the scheduled reporting and is entirely unmodelled. Push toward a common standard through an industry body, since every fintech and every bank is currently paying for the same bespoke work. And measure compliance effort per month, because it is a real cost that scales with partner count and nobody tracks it.

## Target Customer
Compliance and partnership leadership at fintechs, sponsor banks receiving bespoke packs from every programme, and the compliance platform vendors serving the segment.

## Impact If Built
The reporting is framed as a relationship deliverable rather than a product, which prevents anyone noticing the underlying evidence is identical everywhere. Modelling the evidence once and generating any partner's format turns a monthly project into a continuous view and a migration into a configuration change.

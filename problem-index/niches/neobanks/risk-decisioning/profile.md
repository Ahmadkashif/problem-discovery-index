# Risk Decisioning

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/neobanks|Neobanks]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** This niche is not terminal — judging a stranger at the door and judging an existing customer from their own ledger are different contests with different winners, and they are stated separately in the sub-niches below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$2.8B US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~28% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** High — vendor scores and tuned thresholds
**Target Buyer:** Risk and product leadership
**Automation Potential:** Very High — decisioning is the product

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The decisioning that matters — who is approved, whose account is frozen, whose deposit is held — is a mixture of vendor scores, purchased rules and internally tuned thresholds. It is the largest concentration of contested value in the category because it determines both the fraud losses and the customer experience, and because the competitive question in consumer fintech is which institution can distinguish a fraudster from a customer having a bad month.

It is also **not terminal**. Two contests live here. At onboarding the subject is a stranger, the evidence is third-party identity data, device signals and a document, there is no behavioural history, and the adversary is organised application fraud — a data-vendor and identity fight. In ongoing account risk the subject is an existing customer, the evidence is the institution's own ledger and is abundant, the adversary is different, and the error is freezing somebody's wages rather than declining an application. Different data, different adversaries, different consequences, different vendors. The two contests are stated in the sub-niches.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Third-party identity and fraud scores, rules engines, device intelligence, transaction monitoring, and thresholds tuned internally. The gaps: scores whose accuracy on this population is unmeasured; rules inherited and rarely retired; thresholds set once; no feedback from outcomes into any of it; and the two decision types treated as one system.

## The Problem
Relationship banking is an established commercial discipline. Banks measure share of wallet, track product penetration per household, identify primacy indicators, run structured onboarding programmes designed to embed a relationship in its first ninety days, and manage attrition with early warning indicators. The practice exists because the difference between a primary and a peripheral relationship is the difference between a profitable customer and an unprofitable one. Digital challengers face the same economics with better data and manage it with an incentive campaign.

## What Already Exists
Share of wallet estimation; primacy indicator frameworks; structured onboarding and activation programmes; product penetration and cross-hold analysis; and attrition early warning systems.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a relationship with no branch, no relationship manager and a customer who may hold four other accounts. It requires: (1) primacy established through a payroll change rather than through a relationship conversation, which makes the critical action an administrative task at a third party — removing that friction is the substantive problem and traditional practice has nothing to say about it; (2) far better observational data, since every transaction is visible and the traditional practice relied on inference — the data advantage is real and unexploited; (3) a customer base for whom financial precarity is common, so the interventions that work differ from those designed for a mass-affluent branch customer; (4) no human relationship to fall back on, meaning every intervention is a product or a message; and (5) a risk system that can destroy primacy instantly, which traditional banking's friction-based controls did not do.

## Target Customer
Commercial and product leadership at digital banks, and relationship banking practitioners for whom the digital challenger model is an unserved application.

## Impact If Solved
Traditional banking manages primacy as a discipline because it determines profitability, and challengers manage it with a bonus. The critical action being an administrative task at a third-party payroll system is the problem traditional practice never had to solve.

# Identity Assurance Practice

**Niche:** [[niches/neobanks/onboarding-identity-decisioning/profile|Onboarding & Identity Decisioning]]
**Industry:** [[industries/neobanks|Neobanks]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Government digital identity programmes built assurance levels, published error rates and formal frameworks, and commercial onboarding buys a score and picks a cut-off.
**Tags:** #compliance #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #hypothesis-testing #graph-theory #descriptive-statistics #logistic-regression #object-detection
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to judge a stranger from third-party data and a document against an organised adversary — and whoever does that well decides who can open a bank account at all.

## The Problem
Identity assurance has a formal apparatus in government and standards work: defined assurance levels, specified evidence requirements for each, published false acceptance and false rejection rates for biometric and document checks, equity analysis across demographic groups, and accessible alternative routes for people who cannot satisfy the primary path. It exists because refusing someone access to a public service on an identity check is recognised as a serious harm. Commercial account opening makes an equivalent decision using purchased scores with no published error rates and no alternative route.

## What Already Exists
Identity assurance level frameworks; published biometric and document check error rates; demographic equity analysis of identity systems; defined evidence hierarchies; and exception handling routes for people who fail automated checks.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a commercial decision with an adversary and a profit motive. It requires: (1) an active adversary, which government identity frameworks address less centrally and which changes the design from a static assurance level to a moving contest — this adversarial dimension is the substantive difference; (2) error rates measured on this population rather than published by a vendor, since demographic and behavioural composition differ enormously and vendor figures do not transfer; (3) an economic trade-off rather than a policy standard, so the institution must choose a point on the curve deliberately and can be held to that choice; (4) an exception route that is commercially viable, since manual review costs money and refusing is free, which is why the alternative path rarely exists; and (5) equity analysis that is expected but not mandated, making it a governance choice rather than a requirement.

## Target Customer
Risk and compliance leadership at digital banks, identity verification vendors, and the regulators and standards bodies whose frameworks could inform commercial practice.

## Impact If Solved
Government frameworks publish error rates and provide alternative routes because refusing someone a service on an identity check is a serious harm. The adversary is the substantive difference, and error rates measured on this population rather than the vendor's do not transfer.

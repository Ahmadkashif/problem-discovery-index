**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Insurance industrialised claims intake, triage and evidence assembly at enormous volume, and bank disputes are read one at a time.
**Tags:** #workflow-orchestration #large-language-models #automation #compliance #evaluation-metrics #gradient-boosting #confidence-intervals #descriptive-statistics
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn a member's description of what went wrong into a coded claim before a regulatory clock expires — and whoever does that handles volume growth without either breaching deadlines or writing off valid claims.

## The Problem
Insurance handles the same shape of problem industrially: a claimant describes an event in their own words, the claim is classified and triaged, evidence is gathered from multiple sources, a decision is made against defined criteria within a service standard, and the whole thing is instrumented and optimised. Straight-through processing handles the simple majority automatically and routes the complex minority to a human. Bank dispute handling has the same structure, comparable volumes, a harder deadline, and is largely manual.

## What Already Exists
Claims intake with automated classification; triage and complexity routing; straight-through processing for simple claims; evidence gathering automation; and fraud detection within claims.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a hard regulatory clock and a third-party network process. It requires: (1) a statutory timetable that does not flex with volume, so capacity planning must be against a deadline rather than a service level — this externally fixed clock is the central operational difference from insurance's negotiable standards; (2) an outcome determined by a card network's rules rather than by the institution's own policy, which means the decision is a prediction about a third party's adjudication; (3) provisional credit obligations that put money out before the outcome is known, changing the economics of speed; (4) evidence held in the institution's own transaction systems rather than supplied by the claimant, which makes automated assembly far more feasible than in insurance; and (5) claim values that are small, ruling out the investigative cost insurance tolerates.

## Target Customer
Dispute operations at digital banks and card issuers, dispute processing vendors, and claims automation vendors for whom bank disputes are an adjacent market.

## Impact If Solved
Insurance industrialised the same shape at greater volume and bank disputes are read one at a time. The statutory clock that does not flex with volume is the central operational difference, and evidence held in the institution's own systems makes automation more feasible than in insurance.

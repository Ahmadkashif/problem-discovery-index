**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Retail returns management is a developed industry with disposition engines, grading and recovery optimisation, and dropshipping merchants refund and move on.
**Tags:** #convex-optimization #gradient-boosting #evaluation-metrics #workflow-orchestration #revenue-impact #automation #descriptive-statistics #compliance
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to resolve a return on an item that cannot economically go back where it came from — and whoever makes that decision correctly per order captures the margin the whole category currently writes off.

## The Problem
Returns management is a real industry. Retailers run disposition engines that decide per item whether to restock, refurbish, liquidate, donate or dispose; they grade condition at intake, recover value through secondary channels, and measure recovery rate as a managed number. Vendors sell all of this. Dropshipping merchants, whose return economics are the worst in retail because the goods are far away and low value, use none of it and treat every return as a write-off.

## What Already Exists
Returns disposition engines with per-item routing rules; condition grading at intake; secondary market and liquidation channels; recovery rate measurement; and returns authorisation and fraud screening.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a merchant with no warehouse, no intake and no physical custody. It requires: (1) disposition decided before the item moves rather than at intake, since there is no intake — which inverts the entire model and makes the decision a prediction from the return reason and item rather than an inspection; (2) consolidation across many small merchants to reach the volume any recovery channel requires, which is the structural piece and the reason no individual merchant can adopt the practice; (3) supplier chargeback as a disposition option, which has no retail equivalent and is where the largest recoverable value sits; (4) keep-the-item outcomes treated as a legitimate disposition with its own economics rather than as a failure, since it is frequently optimal and is currently chosen by default rather than by calculation; and (5) cross-border duty and customs treatment on anything that does move, which retail returns systems handle domestically and largely ignore.

## Target Customer
Dropshipping merchants and aggregators, sourcing platforms, and returns management vendors for whom the custody-free merchant is unserved.

## Impact If Solved
Every disposition engine decides at intake and there is no intake here, which turns inspection into prediction. Consolidating many small merchants is what reaches recovery-channel volume, and supplier chargeback is a disposition option retail has no equivalent for.

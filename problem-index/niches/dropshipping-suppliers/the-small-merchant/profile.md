# The Small Merchant

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/dropshipping-suppliers|Dropshipping Suppliers]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to give a one-person storefront the information a buying team would have — and whoever does that decides whether small merchants survive their first year, which is where the category's supply of customers comes from.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$900M US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~7% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Low — spreadsheets and forums
**Target Buyer:** Platform merchant success
**Automation Potential:** High — most of the missing work is analysis

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The typical dropshipping merchant is one person, often part-time, carrying a customer relationship for goods they have never seen, sourced from a party they have never met, shipped from a country they have never visited. A retailer doing the same thing at scale would have a buying team, a quality function, a logistics analyst and a finance department. The merchant has a spreadsheet and a forum. This is a distinct niche because the contest is over replacing an absent organisation rather than improving a specific function, and it is where the category's merchant churn — extremely high and almost entirely unexamined — actually comes from.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Storefront platforms, advertising dashboards, supplier marketplaces, spreadsheets, and community forums. The gaps: no true unit economics including returns, refunds and chargebacks; no supplier or product risk view; no cash flow visibility across advertising and settlement cycles; no benchmark against comparable merchants; and no early warning before the business fails.

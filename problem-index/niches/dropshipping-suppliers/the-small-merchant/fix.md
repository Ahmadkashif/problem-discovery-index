# The Product That Was Never Profitable

**Niche:** [[niches/dropshipping-suppliers/the-small-merchant/profile|The Small Merchant]]
**Industry:** [[industries/dropshipping-suppliers|Dropshipping Suppliers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The merchant scales the product with the best sales, which is the product with a nineteen percent return rate and a chargeback problem, and finds out four months later.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #revenue-impact #confidence-intervals #quick-win #hypothesis-testing #time-series-forecasting #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to give a one-person storefront the information a buying team would have — and whoever does that decides whether small merchants survive their first year, which is where the category's supply of customers comes from.

## The Problem
The merchant looks at the dashboard. One product is selling far better than the rest, so they put more advertising behind it. What the dashboard does not show is that this product is returned nineteen percent of the time, generates three times the support messages, has a chargeback rate that threatens the payment account, and has a landed cost that rose last month. It has never made money. The merchant scales it for four months, spends everything they have on it, and discovers the truth when their payment provider writes to them. Every input to that calculation was recorded somewhere in their own systems.

## Why It's Still Broken
Revenue and orders are what every dashboard shows, because they are the easiest to compute and the most flattering — this framing is the root of it. Returns and refunds arrive weeks after the sale and are never attributed back to the product. Advertising, storefront, supplier and payment data sit in four systems nobody joins. And the merchant has no accounting background and does not know the number is missing.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Report profit per product, not revenue. Attribute every cost back to the product that caused it — goods, shipping, advertising, payment fees, refunds, returns, chargebacks and support time — which is bookkeeping rather than analytics and produces a number that frequently reverses the merchant's entire ranking; this is the fix. Attribute refunds and returns to the original order date so the product's true performance is not flattered by a four-week lag. Show contribution after all variable costs as the headline figure, replacing revenue, since whichever number is at the top is the one that gets optimised. Flag products whose profit is negative while their sales are growing, which is the specific pattern that destroys these businesses and is trivially detectable. Track chargeback rate per product with a warning threshold, because payment account termination is the single most common sudden death in the category and it is preceded by weeks of visible signal. Alert on a landed cost increase, since supplier prices move and merchants do not re-check. Include the merchant's own time at a stated rate, which changes the conclusion on low-value high-support products entirely. Compare against similar merchants' benchmarks so an eleven percent return rate is recognisable as bad. Recommend the specific action — raise price, change supplier, drop the product — because a diagnosis without an action is not usable by someone with two hours. And default the whole thing on, since the merchants most in need of it are the least likely to configure anything.

## Who Feels the Pain
Merchants scaling products that lose money; payment providers absorbing chargebacks; and platforms losing merchants who conclude the model does not work when it was one product.

## Impact If Fixed
Revenue is at the top of every dashboard because it is easiest and most flattering, and it reverses the true ranking. Full cost attribution is bookkeeping rather than analytics, and the negative-profit-growing-sales pattern that kills these businesses is trivially detectable once the number exists.

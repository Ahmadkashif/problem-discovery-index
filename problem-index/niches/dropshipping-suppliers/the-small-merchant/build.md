# A Buying Team of One

**Niche:** [[niches/dropshipping-suppliers/the-small-merchant/profile|The Small Merchant]]
**Industry:** [[industries/dropshipping-suppliers|Dropshipping Suppliers]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A one-person storefront does the work of a buying team, a quality function, a logistics analyst and a finance department, with a spreadsheet and a forum.
**Tags:** #worker-facing #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #revenue-impact #gradient-boosting #confidence-intervals #workflow-orchestration #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to give a one-person storefront the information a buying team would have — and whoever does that decides whether small merchants survive their first year, which is where the category's supply of customers comes from.

## The Problem
The merchant is selecting products, assessing suppliers, setting prices, buying advertising, writing listings, answering customers, handling returns and managing cash, alone, in evenings. Every one of those is a job with a profession behind it. They make each decision on a forum post, a video and a guess, and most of them are gone within a year — not because dropshipping cannot work, but because nobody can do eight jobs competently at once with no instrumentation. The platforms serve them a supplier catalogue and an order feed, which addresses one of the eight.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Small merchants pay little and churn fast, which makes them an unattractive segment on the usual metrics — this is the reason, and it is a misreading, because they are the source of the large merchants. The information they need spans storefront, advertising, supplier and payment systems that nobody joins. Educational content fills the gap profitably without solving anything. And churn is treated as an inherent property of the segment rather than as a consequence of what they were given.

## What to Build
Supply the missing organisation as software. Compute true unit economics per product — landed cost, advertising, payment fees, refunds, returns, chargebacks and support time — which is the single most valuable thing a merchant can have and is what almost none of them possess, since the naive margin they track omits most of the costs. Show cash position across the advertising-to-settlement cycle, because running out of cash while nominally profitable is a leading cause of failure and is entirely predictable. Give a risk view per supplier and product, drawing on the platform's reliability and return data, so product selection stops being a guess. Benchmark against comparable merchants, which is the only way a solo operator can tell whether a four percent conversion rate is good. Warn before the failure rather than reporting it after — declining margin, rising returns, a slipping supplier, a cash crunch — since each is visible weeks ahead in data the merchant already generates. Prioritise the day: the merchant has two hours and needs to know which of forty things matters most, which is a scheduling problem nobody has framed as one. Automate the mechanical work — listing generation, order routing, tracking updates, routine support replies — because time is the binding constraint and every hour returned is spent on judgement instead. Explain in plain language rather than presenting a dashboard, since the user is not an analyst and a chart they cannot interpret is worse than a sentence. And measure merchant survival as the product's own metric, because that is the outcome and current tooling is not judged on it.

## Target Customer
Solo and small dropshipping merchants, dropshipping and storefront platforms whose merchant base churns, and the aggregators recruiting from it.

## Impact If Built
Eight professions performed by one person with no instrumentation is the actual cause of the churn the category treats as inherent. True unit economics including refunds and chargebacks, and a cash-cycle view, are both computable and are what the naive margin hides.

# The Description That Was Machine Translated Twice

**Niche:** [[niches/dropshipping-suppliers/listing-content-differentiation/profile|Listing Content Differentiation]]
**Industry:** [[industries/dropshipping-suppliers|Dropshipping Suppliers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The listing says high quality fashion breathable material suitable for many occasions, in that order, and the merchant publishes it because there are four thousand more like it.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #bert #evaluation-metrics #quick-win #automation #revenue-impact #descriptive-statistics #workflow-orchestration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn one supplier's photograph and paragraph into ten thousand listings that are each worth buying — and whoever does that decides whether a merchant competes on anything other than price.

## The Problem
Supplier descriptions arrive translated from the source language, often via an intermediate one, into text that is grammatically broken, keyword-stuffed and entirely uninformative. It does not say what size the thing is, what it is made of, or whether it fits the buyer's purpose. Buyers read two lines and leave. Merchants publish it anyway because rewriting four thousand listings by hand is not possible and the tools that offer to rewrite them produce text that is fluent, equally uninformative and sometimes wrong. The listing is simultaneously the merchant's only sales surface and the one part of their business they have given up on.

## Why It's Still Broken
Rewriting inherits the source's emptiness — if the original does not state the material, a fluent rewrite will either omit it or invent it, which is why the obvious remedy has not worked. Merchants cannot verify claims about products they have never seen, so they avoid making any. Bulk tools optimise for fluency because that is what is easy to check. And the harm is a conversion rate nobody has a baseline for.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Fix the information, not the prose. Extract the real attributes from wherever they exist — the supplier's images, specification tables, variant names and the raw description — and build the listing from those, which is what makes the output informative rather than merely fluent and is the difference between this and generic rewriting. Flag missing critical attributes to the merchant rather than writing around them, since an unknown material on a garment is a question to ask the supplier, not a gap to paper over. Ask the supplier directly for the missing fields, which no platform does at scale and which most suppliers will answer. Refuse to generate unverifiable claims, because an invented specification produces returns and marketplace sanctions and is the failure mode that discredits the whole approach. Use comparable listings of the same item from other sellers as corroboration, which is freely available and is the fastest route to a complete attribute set. Mine customer questions and returns for what buyers actually needed to know, which is the highest-value content input and is entirely unused. Structure for the channel's attribute schema so the listing is filterable, since an unfilterable listing is invisible regardless of how it reads. Localise measurements and conventions for the destination, as a size chart in the wrong system is a guaranteed return. And measure conversion against the original text, which gives the merchant the baseline they have never had.

## Who Feels the Pain
Merchants whose only sales surface is unreadable; buyers who cannot establish basic facts about a product; and platforms whose category-wide conversion is dragged down by the content they supply.

## Impact If Fixed
Rewriting inherits the source's emptiness and either omits or invents, which is why fluent rewriting failed. Extracting real attributes from images and specification tables, corroborating against other sellers' listings, and asking the supplier for the gaps is what makes the listing informative.

# Channel CPQ With the Eligible Services List Built In

**Niche:** [[niches/it-value-added-resellers/erate-schools-libraries/profile|K-12 Schools & Libraries Buying Through E-rate]]
**Industry:** [[industries/it-value-added-resellers|IT Distributors & Value-Added Resellers]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** The reseller's quoting tool already prices the district's network refresh; it doesn't know which lines E-rate will pay for.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #feature-engineering #compliance #data-integration #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to deliver E-rate category two equipment and services that USAC reimburses in full, and whoever has the lowest share of invoiced dollars reduced or denied takes the account.

## The Problem
E-rate quotes are built in the same CPQ as commercial quotes, then reviewed by the reseller's E-rate specialist or the district's consultant line by line.

## What Already Exists
Channel CPQ tools, distributor feeds, USAC's open data and the E-rate Productivity Center, and consultants' tools such as Funds For Learning's E-rate Manager.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs: eligibility tags per SKU with the rule that justifies each; cost-allocation fields for mixed products; the discount rate and category two budget per school; the Form 470 and 471 numbers carried on the quote; and invoice method (BEAR or SPI) driving the paperwork.

## Target Customer
Resellers with education practices and the consultants who advise their district customers.

## Impact If Solved
Building eligibility into the quote removes most line-by-line review time (estimate: ~2–4 hours per district quote) and stops ineligible lines reaching the Form 471.

# Public-Sector Contract Vehicles

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/it-value-added-resellers|IT Distributors & Value-Added Resellers]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to quote every public-sector order from the right contract vehicle at an allowed contract price, and whoever has the lowest share of orders rejected or modified for off-contract items or prices takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$60B a year of US federal, state, local and education IT products and attached services bought through resellers on contract vehicles (estimate); CDW's Public segment alone sold $8.5B in 2025
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~15% of receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — vehicle catalogues are online, but the reseller's awarded price lists, modifications and sales reports run on spreadsheets
**Target Buyer:** VP of public sector or contracts director at the reseller
**Automation Potential:** High — SKU-to-contract matching, ceiling checks and sales reports are rule-bound

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Government and education buyers mostly order through pre-competed vehicles: GSA's Multiple Award Schedule, NASA's SEWP (SEWP VI named 2,115 winners and opens for orders on 1 November 2026), state cooperatives such as NASPO ValuePoint, and education and cooperative vehicles such as E&I, Sourcewell and OMNIA Partners. Each sets which SKUs a reseller may sell, at what ceiling, to whom, and how sales are reported. The vendors' SKU churn — end-of-sale servers, renamed licences, new bundles — keeps putting quotes off-contract. GSA's OneGov strategy adds pressure by making OEMs the prime contractors, with resellers as subcontractors and authorised partners. E-rate buying by schools and libraries is a different regime with its own reimbursement clock, mapped separately in [[niches/it-value-added-resellers/erate-schools-libraries/profile|K-12 Schools & Libraries Buying Through E-rate]].

## Current Tools & Gaps
Channel CPQ with distributor feeds, GSA Advantage and eBuy, the SEWP portal, cooperative catalogues, bid intelligence from GovSpend and Deltek GovWin, and buyer-side procurement suites mapped under [[niches/procurement-spend-platforms/public-sector-procurement/profile|Public Sector Procurement]]. The gaps: no reseller-side catalogue that knows every vehicle's awarded lines and current modification, SKU replacements mapped by hand, and sales reports assembled quarterly from invoices. The industry note is [[problems/it-value-added-resellers/low-impact-2|Contract-Vehicle Price Lists Kept in Spreadsheets]].

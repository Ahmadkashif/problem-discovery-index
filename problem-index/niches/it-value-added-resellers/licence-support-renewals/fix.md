# The Support Contract That Won't Co-Term

**Niche:** [[niches/it-value-added-resellers/licence-support-renewals/profile|Licence & Support Contract Renewals]]
**Industry:** [[industries/it-value-added-resellers|IT Distributors & Value-Added Resellers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Every hardware sale adds a support contract with its own end date, until a customer has dozens expiring in different months and renews them one by one.
**Tags:** #optimization-fundamentals #feature-engineering #workflow-orchestration #quick-win #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to renew each customer's expiring software licences, cloud subscriptions and hardware support contracts before the vendor's renewal date, and whoever renews the largest share of expiring contract value on time takes the account.

## The Problem
Support contracts bought with each project start on the shipping date. Over a few years a customer accumulates contracts on scattered dates, each needing a quote, a PO and a renewal, and some lapse unnoticed until a device fails out of support.

## Why It's Still Broken
Co-terming requires prorating each contract to a common date, and the vendor's install-base report, the reseller's records and the customer's view rarely agree on what is covered.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Reconcile the install base against contracts, propose a co-term date that minimises cost and admin, price the prorations, and quote one consolidated renewal; then default new hardware support to the customer's co-term date at the point of sale.

## Who Feels the Pain
Renewals reps, customers' IT managers, and field engineers who find a failed device out of support.

## Impact If Fixed
Consolidating a customer's scattered contracts into one or two renewal dates removes most of the lapses and cuts renewal admin per customer by ~half (estimate).

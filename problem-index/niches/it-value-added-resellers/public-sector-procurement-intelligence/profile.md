# Public-Sector IT Procurement Intelligence

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/it-value-added-resellers|IT Distributors & Value-Added Resellers]]
**Category:** Insight Layer
**Value-Chain Position:** Data & benchmark vendors

## What They Do
Firms that track government and education buying and sell it to the resellers and vendors chasing it: Deltek's GovWin IQ, GovSpend, HigherGov and Bloomberg Government. They collect solicitations, awards, contract-vehicle holders and, in GovSpend's case, purchase orders obtained from state and local agencies, then sell searchable intelligence, forecasts and alerts. Public-sector teams at resellers, OEMs and distributors pay annual subscriptions; the vehicles they track are those in [[niches/it-value-added-resellers/public-sector-contract-vehicles/profile|Public-Sector Contract Vehicles]].

## Insight Function
**Size:** ~20–60 market analysts and forecasters at the largest provider (estimate); staff who collect and key bid documents and purchase orders are production and are not counted
**Output:** Opportunity forecasts, expiring-contract and incumbent analysis, market reports, alerts
**Proprietary data:** Years of solicitation, award and purchase-order records assembled from public sources and records requests, enriched and linked to vendors and contracts — the assembly is theirs, the underlying records are public
**Clock:** Self-set publication and alert cadence; the bid due dates subscribers chase belong to the subscribers
**Buyer:** Head of research or content at the provider

**Kill switches:** none — the corpus is built from public procurement records and records requests, and the analysis and linkage are the provider's own product.
**Verdict:** Logged — below threshold. The intelligence is the invoice and the archive is hard to rebuild, but a data vendor's own release calendar scores Q4 2, the analytical team is modest once collection staff are set aside, and a handful of providers makes Q5 a 2. Its one unused asset is the outcome label: the archive records which opportunities each subscriber pursued only if subscribers tell it, so forecasts of who will win are rarely graded against awards it already holds.

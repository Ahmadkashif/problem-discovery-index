# Institutional Evidence Without an Institutional Back Office

**Niche:** [[niches/asset-managers/emerging-and-boutique-managers/profile|Emerging & Boutique Managers]]
**Industry:** [[industries/asset-managers|Asset Managers]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A boutique with a strong track record fails consultant due diligence because it cannot document its research process, risk controls and operations to the standard of a firm a hundred times its size.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #data-integration #evaluation-metrics #compliance #automation #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to give a sub-$10B manager institutional-grade evidence — clean track record, consultant-ready answers, credible risk and research process — without an institutional-sized operations team, and whoever does that gets boutiques through consultant screens that currently filter them out on infrastructure, not performance.

## The Problem
Consultants assess process as much as performance. Large managers evidence process with research systems, risk reports, committee minutes and polished DDQ answers. A boutique has the process in the founders' heads and the evidence in scattered files.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Enterprise vendors sell to large firms; boutique-focused vendors sell point tools. Nobody packages the evidence a consultant wants as one product.

## What to Build
A lightweight evidence layer: research notes and decisions captured as events, risk reports generated from the administrator's holdings, investment committee minutes structured, and a DDQ answer set drafted from these records. The output is a due diligence pack that can be refreshed each quarter.

## Target Customer
Founders and COOs of independent managers in emerging-manager programmes or seeking first institutional mandates.

## Impact If Built
Boutiques compete on investment merit rather than on the size of their operations team.

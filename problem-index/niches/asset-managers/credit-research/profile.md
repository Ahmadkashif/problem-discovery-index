# Credit Research

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/asset-managers|Asset Managers]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to read covenant and offering documents across hundreds of issuers per analyst and catch credit deterioration before the rating agencies do — and whoever does that avoids the downgrade and default losses that decide a fixed income manager's ranking.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$5–7B estimated annual global spend on long-only credit research staff, data and tools
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~12% of active-manager investment cost (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low to Medium — prices and spreads digitised, documents read by hand
**Target Buyer:** Head of Credit Research / Fixed Income CIO
**Automation Potential:** Very High — document reading and surveillance are repeatable

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A credit analyst is paid to avoid losses, not to find winners. She covers far more issuers than an equity analyst — often a hundred or more — and the decisive inputs are legal documents: indentures, credit agreements and offering memoranda whose covenant packages, baskets and carve-outs determine what an issuer can do to bondholders. Surveillance across that universe — liquidity, leverage drift, refinancing walls, covenant headroom — is a monitoring problem at a volume no team reads fully. Equity tools do not transfer.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Bloomberg and ICE pricing, rating agency research, covenant research services such as Covenant Review (part of CreditSights, within Fitch Group) and Octus (formerly Reorg), issuer financials, internal credit scoring models, spreadsheets. The gaps: internal ratings and their rationales unrecorded as events; covenant analysis done per deal and not stored structurally; surveillance by rotation rather than by risk; and no comparison of internal ratings against agency ratings and subsequent spreads.

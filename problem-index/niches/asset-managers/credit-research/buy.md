# Early-Warning Systems From Commercial Lending

**Niche:** [[niches/asset-managers/credit-research/profile|Credit Research]]
**Industry:** [[industries/asset-managers|Asset Managers]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Banks run early-warning systems over their loan books; long-only credit managers hold thousands of issuers and watch them in rotation.
**Tags:** #gradient-boosting #survival-analysis #logistic-regression #change-point-detection #evaluation-metrics #compliance
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to read covenant and offering documents across hundreds of issuers per analyst and catch credit deterioration before the rating agencies do — and whoever does that avoids the downgrade and default losses that decide a fixed income manager's ranking.

## The Problem
A credit team covering a thousand issuers reviews each on a cycle — quarterly results, annual deep dive — and in between relies on news and price moves to prompt attention. Deterioration that is visible in filings, liquidity or market signals weeks earlier is noticed late.

## What Already Exists
Commercial banks operate early-warning systems on their lending books: default probability models, watch-list triggers on financial ratios, behavioural signals from account activity, and governed escalation to credit officers. Structural and market-implied default models are widely sold.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs: (1) public-market signals — spreads, equity moves, rating outlooks — rather than account behaviour, which a bond investor never sees; (2) the manager's own internal rating as an input and a target, so the system measures where the firm's view lags the evidence; (3) issuer-level rather than loan-level aggregation across many bonds and loans in a capital structure; (4) mark-to-market losses from spread widening and downgrades, which hurt a fund long before any default; and (5) integration with portfolio limits so an alert reaches the PM holding the risk.

## Target Customer
Credit research leads and fixed income risk teams at asset managers.

## Impact If Solved
Analyst attention goes where deterioration is emerging rather than where the calendar points, which is what decides losses in a credit portfolio.

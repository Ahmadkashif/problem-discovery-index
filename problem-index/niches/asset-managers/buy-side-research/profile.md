# Buy-Side Research

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/asset-managers|Asset Managers]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** This niche is not terminal — an equity analyst forecasting upside from management and a credit analyst underwriting downside from documents are different contests with different winners, and they are stated separately in the sub-niches.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$15–20B estimated annual global spend on buy-side research staff, data and tools at long-only managers
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~30% of active-manager investment cost (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — data terminals everywhere, research records almost nowhere
**Target Buyer:** Director of Research / Chief Investment Officer
**Automation Potential:** High — the reading, monitoring and record-keeping layers, not the judgment

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The research floor is what an active manager charges for. Analysts cover companies or issuers, build models, meet management, and pass recommendations to portfolio managers. Its tooling spend — terminals, fundamentals, transcripts, research management systems, alternative data, and now AI research assistants — is the largest discretionary technology line in the investment function, and the competitive question is which manager turns that spend into better decisions than the index.

It is **not terminal**. Two contests live inside it. Fundamental equity research is an upside forecast built on management access, earnings models and the analyst's read of people; the winning tool is one that captures and grades that read. Credit research is a downside underwriting exercise built on legal documents, covenants, capital structures and recovery analysis across far more issuers per analyst; the winning tool is one that reads documents and monitors deterioration. Different inputs, different error costs, different vendors.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Bloomberg and FactSet terminals, transcript and document search (AlphaSense, which absorbed Tegus), research management systems (FactSet RMS, Bipsync, SS&C Tamale), Excel models, sell-side research under MiFID II budgets, and expert-network calls. The gaps: recommendations and rationales not recorded as events; no join to outcomes; knowledge that leaves with analysts; and AI tools aimed at external documents rather than the firm's own research record.

# The Sell Decision Nobody Reviews

**Niche:** [[niches/asset-managers/investment-decision-analytics/profile|Investment Decision Analytics]]
**Industry:** [[industries/asset-managers|Asset Managers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Buys are debated in investment committee; sells happen quietly, and nobody checks what the sold positions did afterwards.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #hypothesis-testing #quick-win #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn a portfolio manager's own trade history into a measured account of where their decisions add and lose value — sizing, timing, selling — that the PM accepts as fair, and whoever does that becomes the feedback loop active management never had.

## The Problem
Research on active managers suggests selling decisions are often weaker than buying decisions, yet the review process concentrates on new ideas.

## Why It's Still Broken
Sold positions disappear from holdings reports; nobody owns their post-sale tracking.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A standing report of every exit with its subsequent factor-adjusted return over set horizons and the stated reason, reviewed quarterly. It needs only trade history and returns.

## Who Feels the Pain
CIOs and PMs; clients paying for decisions nobody evaluates.

## Impact If Fixed
Selling becomes a reviewed decision, and the cheapest available improvement in active returns becomes visible.

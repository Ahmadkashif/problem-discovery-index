# AI Coding Assistants

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/developer-tools-vendors|Developer Tools Vendors]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor here is fighting to be the assistant an engineering organisation actually runs on — and that contest is decided twice, by the developer and by the security function, which is why this niche is not terminal and is decomposed below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$4.6B US coding assistants and agentic development tooling
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~35% of category revenue and rising
**Digital Adoption:** High and moving faster than any prior shift in the category
**Target Buyer:** Developers adopt; platform and security functions approve or block
**Automation Potential:** Very High, and the contest is about trust rather than capability

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Coding assistants have repriced this category in about three years, moving from completion to agentic editing and compressing both expectations and roadmaps. It is the largest block of spend and the fastest-moving competitive surface in developer tooling. It is also the clearest case in the industry of a purchase decided in two places at once: an assistant that developers do not keep switched on is worthless regardless of what was procured, and an assistant that security will not approve is unavailable regardless of what developers want.

The filter fails here. "AI coding assistants" names a product category rather than a contest, and the two contests are fought against different competitors with different measures of success. One is a fast, bottom-up, consumer-shaped competition for the developer's own default. The other is a compliance-shaped competition for approval from people who can block the tool the whole engineering organisation is asking for. The two contested sub-niches below carry the terminal statements.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Assistants from the hosting platforms and from specialist vendors, editor extensions, agentic editing tools, and code search products extending into generation. The gaps: acceptance rate is the industry's reported metric and measures whether a suggestion was taken rather than whether it was right, which is a different and far less flattering question; provenance and licence exposure are asserted rather than demonstrated; the effect on code that nobody wrote by hand is unmeasured; and the review burden created by generated code lands on humans whose capacity did not change.

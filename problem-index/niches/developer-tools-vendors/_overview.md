## Why These Niches

The category sells productivity and cannot measure it, which is the largest contested surface and is now urgent rather than philosophical: enormous assistant spend is being committed on demonstrations and enthusiasm while the effect on throughput, defects and maintenance burden is genuinely contested. Whoever can attribute an engineering change to a tool settles the category's central commercial argument.

AI coding assistants **failed the filter as one niche**. The contest for the developer's own adoption is latency, editor fit and whether the suggestion is right often enough to keep on — decided by individuals, bottom-up. The contest for enterprise deployment is provenance, licence contamination, data boundaries and audit — decided by security and platform functions who can block a tool the whole engineering organisation wants. Different buyers, different competitors, different definitions of winning. Decomposed below.

The two underdigitised areas are where tooling stops. The Language Server Protocol solved the plumbing and the fifteenth-most-popular ecosystem still gets navigation that half works, because somebody has to build and maintain the server. And the enormous estates of decades-old code that run banks, insurers and governments are served by almost nothing modern, which is where comprehension is worth most and is attempted least.

The two underserved constituencies are the vendor's own support engineer, reproducing failures in environments they cannot see, and every developer losing a week to environment setup that appears in no metric anywhere.

The automation niches are scale performance — where every tool degrades and the affected customers are the largest and least able to switch — and the corpus itself, which records how software is actually written across millions of repositories and is used to render diffs.

## Filter Notes

Seven of the eight level-1 niches are terminal. **AI Coding Assistants** is not: it names a product category in the middle of a repricing event rather than a contest, and the two contests inside it are fought against different competitors in front of different buyers. Developer-adopted assistants are won on latency, editor integration and suggestion quality, by whoever the developer keeps switched on — a consumer-shaped contest with bottom-up distribution. Enterprise assistant governance is won on code provenance, licence contamination, data boundaries, attribution and audit, by whoever a chief information security officer will approve — a compliance-shaped contest where the developer's preference is an input rather than the decision. Decomposed into two contested sub-niches.

Two candidates were rejected. *Code search and navigation* was folded into Legacy Codebase Comprehension and Long-Tail Language Ecosystems, since its contest only becomes distinct where the code is unfamiliar or the ecosystem unsupported. *Code hosting* was rejected as finished — it is a settled duopoly competing on bundling rather than on any capability.

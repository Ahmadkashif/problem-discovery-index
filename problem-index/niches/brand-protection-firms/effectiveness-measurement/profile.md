# Enforcement Effectiveness Measurement

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/brand-protection-firms|Brand Protection Firms]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Whether anyone can show that enforcement reduced counterfeit sales, or only that it produced takedowns.

## Profile

**Market Size:** ~$480M
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~12%
**Digital Adoption:** Very low — activity reported as outcome
**Target Buyer:** Brand leadership, procurement, finance
**Automation Potential:** High — the proxies are measurable

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche

The contract is priced and renewed on listings removed, sites taken down and sellers actioned. Those numbers are reported monthly and used to justify the spend.

None of them is an outcome. Whether counterfeit sales fell, whether infringing supply shrank, whether consumers were less likely to encounter a fake — none is measured by the firm, the brand or anyone else.

The pattern practitioners describe privately is that removed listings are replaced within days by the same operators under new accounts, which means the count measures activity on both sides rather than progress on either. A rising takedown number is consistent with a growing problem, a shrinking one, and a stable one, and nothing distinguishes them.

The proxies that would help are measurable. The prevalence of infringing listings in a sampled category over time. The time to re-emergence after an operator-level action. The share of search results for a brand term that are infringing. Test purchase success rates. Brand-side signals — warranty claims on non-genuine goods, customer complaints, seizure data — that the brand already holds.

The category sells assurance and reports activity, which places it in the same structural position as every other assurance business in this cluster.

## Current Tools & Gaps

Monthly dashboards reporting takedown counts by platform and category. Some prevalence sampling by a minority of firms. Brand-side market research commissioned separately and not joined. Customs seizure data held by the brand's legal function. Test purchases performed for evidence.

The gaps are the whole niche. Prevalence is not tracked over time, so nobody knows whether the infringing population is growing or shrinking. Re-emergence is not measured, which is the same absence that runs through operator attribution. No baseline or control exists, so a change cannot be attributed to enforcement rather than to seasonality or platform policy. Brand-side outcome signals are not joined to enforcement activity. And no firm publishes any measure other than volume, which means brands cannot compare vendors on anything else.

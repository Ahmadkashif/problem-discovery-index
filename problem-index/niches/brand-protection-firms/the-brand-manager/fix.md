# Fix: An Authorised Reseller Has Been Suspended Again

**Niche:** The Brand's Own Manager
**Industry:** [[industries/brand-protection-firms|Brand Protection Firms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The vendor the brand pays to protect it has suspended one of the brand's own authorised partners, and the manager finds out from an angry email.
**Tags:** #evaluation-metrics #compliance #worker-facing #data-integration #workflow-orchestration #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Whether the person accountable for the brand protection programme can say what it achieved, or only what it did.

## The Problem

An email arrives from a regional sales director. An authorised reseller has had their listings removed and their account restricted. The reseller is furious, the sales relationship is damaged, and the enforcement was filed by the vendor the brand pays.

The manager now has to unwind it. Confirm the reseller is authorised, which requires the channel list that is maintained elsewhere and is out of date. Contact the vendor, who filed the notice on the basis of a photograph. Get the notice withdrawn. Ask the platform to restore. Apologise to the reseller and to the sales director. And explain internally how the brand's own enforcement programme attacked the brand's own channel.

It happens repeatedly. The same reseller may be actioned again months later, because the correction did not reach the vendor's detection or determination records, and because the authorised list still has not been updated.

The manager spends a meaningful share of their time on this. It is invisible in the vendor's report, which counts the original action and not the reversal. And it is the single largest source of internal reputational damage to the programme they run.

## Why It's Still Broken

**The authorised seller list is owned elsewhere and is stale.** Channel management maintains it for commercial purposes, at a cadence that suits them, and the brand protection manager depends on it without controlling it.

**Corrections do not propagate.** A withdrawn notice removes the action and does not update the vendor's determination record, so the same seller is re-evaluated identically next time.

**The vendor does not report its own errors.** The monthly report counts actions, not reversals, so the incident exists only as an internal complaint.

**The reseller cannot reach the filer.** They escalate through the sales relationship, which is why the manager learns about it from a sales director rather than from the vendor.

**Nobody counts them.** The frequency of channel-partner incidents is not tracked, so the pattern is experienced as a series of one-offs.

**Detection cannot easily distinguish them.** An authorised reseller's listing looks like any other third-party listing, and without a current list the determination is a guess.

## What a Fix Looks Like

**Make the authorised seller list a live, jointly-owned asset.** Current, complete, including regional and marketplace-specific seller identities, fed to the vendor continuously rather than annually. This prevents most of these incidents and is a data ownership problem rather than a technical one.

**Require a pre-action check against the list.** No notice against a seller matching the authorised list without an explicit override and a human decision. A simple gate that would stop the most damaging class of error outright.

**Propagate corrections into the vendor's records.** A withdrawn notice must update the seller's determination record, or the same seller is actioned again — which is the specific pattern that most infuriates channel partners.

**Count the incidents and report them.** Channel-partner actions per quarter, with resolution time, in the vendor's own report. Making the error visible in the report that measures the vendor is what creates the pressure to reduce it.

**Give resellers a direct route.** An escalation path from an affected authorised partner to the brand and the vendor, rather than through the sales relationship — which currently means the manager hears about it last.

**Include it in the vendor's performance terms.** Channel-partner error rate as a contracted metric with consequences, which is the mechanism procurement would apply to any other vendor causing damage to the business it serves.

**Extend the list beyond resellers.** Authorised repairers, refurbishers and service partners are actioned for the same reason and are almost never on any list the vendor holds.

## Who Feels the Pain

The authorised reseller or repair partner, a legitimate business attacked by the brand it partners with, losing income and standing while it is sorted out.

The manager, spending substantial time unwinding errors caused by a vendor they manage, and absorbing the internal reputational cost.

The sales relationship, which takes real damage each time and is the reason the incident escalates the way it does.

And the programme itself, whose internal credibility is set by these incidents far more than by any takedown count.

## Impact If Fixed

A live, jointly-owned authorised seller list with a pre-action check would prevent most of these incidents outright, and it is a data ownership decision rather than a technology project.

Propagating corrections into the vendor's determination records stops the repeat actions against the same partner, which is the part that causes the most relationship damage.

And putting channel-partner error rate into the vendor's reported metrics with contractual consequence is what would make the vendor care about an error that currently costs them nothing and costs the brand a great deal.

# Build: The Programme View, Not the Vendor Dashboard

**Niche:** The Brand's Own Manager
**Industry:** [[industries/brand-protection-firms|Brand Protection Firms]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A brand-side view that joins vendor activity to the organisation's own outcome signals, tracks channel-partner incidents, and produces the case the manager has to make upward.
**Tags:** #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #time-series-forecasting #causal-inference #data-integration #revenue-impact #worker-facing #workflow-orchestration
**Contested on:** Whether the person accountable for the brand protection programme can say what it achieved, or only what it did.

## The Problem

The manager's tooling is the vendor's dashboard. It reports the vendor's activity, organised the way the vendor organises it, with the metrics the vendor is paid on.

It cannot answer any of the questions the manager is actually asked. Is the counterfeit problem getting better — not addressed. Is this vendor better than the alternative — only comparable on volume and price. Why was our authorised reseller in Germany suspended — arrives as an internal complaint rather than as a vendor-reported error. What would another two hundred thousand of budget buy — no basis whatsoever.

The information that would answer them exists inside the brand and is scattered. Warranty claims on units that turned out not to be genuine sit with customer service. Complaints about fakes sit in the support system. Seizure data sits with legal. Grey-market observations sit with commercial. The authorised seller list sits with channel management. And sales data that might show a counterfeit effect sits with finance.

Nobody has joined any of it to the programme, because the programme's owner is one person in legal with a vendor dashboard and no mandate over the other functions.

## Why Nobody Has Built This

**The tooling market serves the vendor.** Products in this category are sold to brand protection firms and built for their operations. The brand-side manager is a downstream recipient of a report.

**The manager has no budget for tooling.** Their spend is the vendor contract, and internal tooling would come from a different budget they do not control.

**The data is in other functions.** Joining warranty, complaint, seizure and channel data requires cooperation from four teams for whom this is not a priority.

**Outcome measurement is uncomfortable internally too.** A manager who discovers the programme has no measurable effect has a problem with their own programme, not only with their vendor.

**The role is small and dispersed.** One or two people per brand, across many brands, with no community and no shared practice.

**Vendors have no reason to build it.** A brand-side view that compares vendors and measures outcome is against the interest of the party best placed to build it.

## What to Build

**Join the brand's own signals into one view.** Warranty claims on non-genuine units, customer complaints about counterfeits, returns, seizure volumes, grey-market observations. Each is noisy and together they bound the trend, and they are already collected for other purposes.

**Track channel-partner incidents explicitly.** Every authorised reseller, repairer or distributor actioned by the vendor, with the resolution and the time it took. This is the manager's most painful recurring internal problem and it is currently anecdotal.

**Maintain the authorised seller list as a shared operational asset.** Owned jointly with channel management, current, and fed to the vendor continuously. A stale list is the largest cause of the channel-partner incidents above, and the manager depends on a list they do not control.

**Compare vendors on more than volume.** Where a brand uses several vendors or is considering a change, a comparison on prevalence in their categories, re-emergence rates and channel-partner error rates rather than on notice counts.

**Produce the upward narrative.** The manager must present this to leadership. A view built around what leadership asks — is it getting better, what is it costing, what would more buy — rather than around what the vendor did.

**Support the budget case.** Programme cost against measured prevalence trend and against brand-side harm signals. Currently the case is a large activity number and an assertion.

**Give the role a peer community.** One person per brand, facing identical problems, with no forum. A practice community would improve this role faster than any software.

## Target Customer

Brand protection and IP managers at brands spending meaningfully on enforcement, who are the users and would need an internal sponsor for the budget.

Brand leadership and finance as the sponsors, since the artefact they receive at present is uninterpretable and they are the ones authorising the spend.

Independent providers rather than the enforcement vendors, since a view that compares vendors and measures outcome cannot credibly come from one of them.

## Impact If Built

The person accountable for the programme gets a view of the programme rather than a view of the vendor's activity.

Tracking channel-partner incidents would turn the manager's most painful recurring internal problem into a measured one with a vendor-facing consequence.

And joining the brand's own warranty, complaint and seizure signals costs internal cooperation rather than money, and would give the manager the first outcome-shaped evidence anyone in this chain has had.

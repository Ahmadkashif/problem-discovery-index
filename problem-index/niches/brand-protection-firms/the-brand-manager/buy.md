# Buy: Vendor Performance Management, Applied Here

**Niche:** The Brand's Own Manager
**Industry:** [[industries/brand-protection-firms|Brand Protection Firms]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Procurement has a mature discipline for managing service vendors on outcomes rather than activity, and brand protection contracts are managed on a count the vendor produces.
**Tags:** #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #hypothesis-testing #revenue-impact #workflow-orchestration #compliance
**Contested on:** Whether the person accountable for the brand protection programme can say what it achieved, or only what it did.

## The Problem

Managing an outsourced service on measurable performance is a developed procurement discipline. Service level agreements define what good looks like. Key performance indicators are agreed and independently verifiable. Outcome-based contracting ties payment to results rather than to effort. Vendor scorecards compare providers on common terms. Benchmarking establishes what the market delivers. And periodic competitive review tests whether the incumbent is still the right choice.

Brand protection contracts are managed on a metric the vendor generates, defines and is paid on, with no independent verification and no outcome component. The service level, where one exists, concerns response times rather than results. And the vendor scorecard, if there is one, is the takedown count.

Procurement functions that would never accept this arrangement for a logistics provider or a call centre accept it here, because the category's metric is industry convention and nobody has proposed an alternative.

## What Already Exists

Procurement practice: service level agreements, outcome-based contracting, vendor scorecards, benchmarking and competitive review, with extensive tooling and established professional practice.

Vendor management platforms: performance tracking, contract management and spend analysis across a supplier portfolio.

Outcome-based contracting: mature in facilities management, logistics and increasingly in professional services, where payment is tied to measured results.

Marketing procurement: the specific discipline of buying agency services whose output is hard to measure, which faced exactly this problem and developed independent verification and incrementality requirements.

Brand protection: contracts priced on notice volume with activity reporting.

## The Customization Gap

**The metric is vendor-generated and unverified.** Procurement normally requires independently verifiable performance data. Here the vendor counts its own notices and is paid on the count.

**No outcome component exists to contract on.** Outcome-based contracting requires a measurable outcome, which is the gap in [[niches/brand-protection-firms/effectiveness-measurement/profile|🟠 Enforcement Effectiveness Measurement]] — and until prevalence or re-emergence is measurable, there is nothing to tie payment to.

**No benchmarking data exists.** Procurement compares against market norms. There are no published norms for brand protection performance on anything but price.

**Marketing procurement already solved the analogous problem.** Buying a service whose output is hard to attribute is what marketing procurement does, and it responded with independent verification, incrementality requirements and media audit — none of which has been applied here.

**The manager is not procurement.** The contract is frequently managed by legal or IP rather than by a procurement function with this discipline, so the practice does not reach it.

**Switching costs are high and unexamined.** Changing vendor means rebuilding detection coverage and recordation, which discourages competitive review — and nobody has assessed how high the cost actually is.

## Target Customer

Procurement functions at brands with significant enforcement spend, who would recognise the arrangement as one they would not accept elsewhere and have the professional practice to change it.

Brand protection managers, as the internal advocate who needs procurement's discipline and lacks the authority to impose it alone.

Marketing procurement specialists, whose experience with hard-to-attribute services is the closest available precedent and is directly transferable.

## Impact If Solved

A mature procurement discipline applies to a category currently managed on a self-reported activity count, and the gap is recognisable to any procurement professional the moment it is described.

Requiring independently verifiable performance data is the first step and would immediately change what vendors report, because it is a contract condition rather than a request.

And marketing procurement's experience with unattributable services — independent verification, incrementality requirements, periodic audit — is the closest available template for a category with exactly the same measurement problem.

# Build: The Operator Graph

**Niche:** Operator Attribution
**Industry:** [[industries/brand-protection-firms|Brand Protection Firms]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Resolve accounts to operators across every monitored surface using the signals that are expensive to vary, so enforcement, measurement and prioritisation all operate on the entity that matters.
**Tags:** #graph-neural-networks #graph-theory #contrastive-learning #k-means-clustering #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #automation #data-integration
**Contested on:** Whether the hundreds of accounts behind a counterfeit operation are recognised as one entity.

## The Problem

Everything in this industry is limited by the absence of the operator as an entity.

Enforcement is per-listing because there is no operator to enforce against. Prioritisation is by match confidence because there is no operator scale to prioritise by. Effectiveness cannot be measured because re-emergence cannot be detected without knowing who returned. And the strongest enforcement levers — payment, infrastructure, legal — require identifying an entity and are therefore unavailable.

The firm holds the data. Every detected listing carries an image, a price, a description, a seller identity, a shipping origin where a test purchase was made, and a timestamp. Across hundreds of thousands of listings, the operators leave patterns: the same product photographs appearing under twenty seller names, the same fulfilment origin, descriptions built from one template, prices moving together, accounts created in batches, new accounts appearing within days of removals.

Some of those signals the operator can vary cheaply, and does. Images are re-shot, descriptions rewritten, account details separated. The signals that survive are the expensive ones — where the goods physically ship from, how the operation is organised, what its operational cadence looks like — and those are exactly the ones nobody has built a model around.

## Why Nobody Has Built This

**The pipeline has no entity above the listing.** Detection, enforcement and reporting are all listing-shaped, so there is nowhere for an operator to live and nothing downstream that would consume one.

**Volume pricing does not reward it.** Clustering does not increase notice count and may reduce it, and the contract is priced on notices.

**The adversary separates deliberately.** Operators know that shared images and addresses connect accounts and take steps, which makes naive clustering weak and discourages the attempt.

**Clustering errors are costly in both directions.** Merging two distinct sellers into one operator can lead to enforcement against an innocent party; splitting one operator into many loses the benefit. Both matter and both require careful confidence handling.

**Physical origin data is collected for evidence, not for clustering.** Test purchases establish shipping origin for legal purposes and the results are filed with the case rather than fed back.

**Cross-platform data is fragmented.** Different surfaces are monitored by different systems and sometimes different vendors, which prevents the clustering that matters most.

## What to Build

**Build the graph from the data already held.** Accounts as nodes; edges from shared or near-duplicate images, template-similar descriptions, coincident creation timing, correlated pricing, shared contact fragments, common shipping origin and temporal succession after enforcement.

**Weight edges by how expensive they are to fake.** A shared image is cheap to avoid. A shared fulfilment origin is not. A model that treats all edges equally will be defeated by the operators who matter, and one that weights by adversary cost will not.

**Use succession as a first-class signal.** A new account with matching characteristics appearing within days of an enforcement action is nearly always the same operator. This is the strongest available link, it is generated by the firm's own enforcement, and nobody records it.

**Feed test purchase results back.** Physical origin, packaging characteristics, product batch markings and payment destination from test purchases are the highest-quality clustering evidence available, and they currently stop at the case file.

**Express confidence and act accordingly.** High-confidence clusters support coordinated enforcement; lower-confidence ones support prioritisation only. Acting on an uncertain cluster is how an innocent seller gets caught in an operator-level action.

**Make the operator the reporting unit.** Operators detected, actioned, and re-emerged. This changes what the contract measures and is the reporting counterpart to the whole capability.

**Share the graph between brands.** The same operators sell many brands, so a shared operator registry would be dramatically stronger than any single brand's view — and is the intervention requiring cooperation that does not yet exist.

## Target Customer

Brand protection firms willing to compete on effect rather than volume, for whom the operator graph is the prerequisite for every other improvement in this industry.

Brands with organised counterfeit problems, who would immediately recognise the difference between a report of four thousand listings and a report of thirty operators.

Marketplace platforms, whose own seller integrity teams have better data for exactly this clustering and a different objective.

## Impact If Built

The entity that matters becomes visible, and with it prioritisation by operator scale, coordinated cross-surface enforcement, the harder enforcement levers, and re-emergence measurement — none of which is possible without it.

Using post-enforcement succession as a clustering signal is free, uses data the firm's own enforcement generates, and is the strongest single link available.

And reporting operators rather than listings would reframe the entire commercial conversation, from a count that rises indefinitely to a population that can actually be reduced.

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche

Detection is the engine. Marketplaces, social platforms, app stores and domain registrations are scanned continuously, images and keywords are matched against the brand's assets, and thousands of candidates a day are surfaced.

A human then decides infringement on each, from a photograph, a price, a seller name and a title. That decision determines whether a notice is filed, and a notice can remove a listing, suspend a seller and end a small business's income.

Two contests run through this at once and pull in opposite directions. The recall contest is against sellers who have learned precisely what the matching keys on and perturb it — altered images, misspelled brand names, product shots that show no logo, listings that reference the brand only in the description. The precision contest is legal: a great deal of what matches is legitimate, and distinguishing counterfeit from lawful resale, parallel import, repair, refurbishment, compatibility claims, parody or criticism is a judgement no matcher makes.

Improving one makes the other harder. A better matcher surfaces more borderline cases, and the reviewer deciding them has three seconds and a photograph.

## Current Tools & Gaps

Image matching against brand asset libraries, keyword and misspelling matching, marketplace and social crawling at scale, price anomaly detection, seller profile heuristics, and review interfaces where analysts work a queue of candidates. Some machine-assisted pre-classification.

The gaps run through both halves. Matching keys on the features sellers have learned to alter, so the operators who matter are systematically under-detected while naive listings are over-represented. Review provides the listing and rarely the context that would determine legitimacy — purchase history, seller history, authorised distribution status, prior determinations on the same seller. Nothing distinguishes the legal categories that matter, so resale, repair and parody are surfaced identically to counterfeit. Reviewer accuracy is unmeasured, so the error rate in either direction is unknown. And nothing records the determination rationale, so an appeal cannot be evaluated against what was actually decided.

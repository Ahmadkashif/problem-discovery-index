# Build: A Report That Answers the Question

**Niche:** Reporting & Account Operations
**Industry:** [[industries/brand-protection-firms|Brand Protection Firms]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Reporting built around what the client is asked internally — is it getting better, what did it cost, what would more buy — with activity as an operational appendix rather than as the headline.
**Tags:** #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #time-series-forecasting #descriptive-statistics #revenue-impact #automation #workflow-orchestration
**Contested on:** Whether the monthly report answers the client's question or restates the count the contract was priced on.

## The Problem

The monthly report leads with a number. Four thousand listings removed, with a breakdown by platform and category and a comparison to last month.

The client's manager then has to translate it into an answer to a question it does not address. Their director asks whether the counterfeit problem is improving. Their commercial colleagues ask why an authorised reseller was suspended. Finance asks what another two hundred thousand would achieve. The report answers none of them, so the manager constructs an answer from the number and their own judgement, and presents it upward with more confidence than the evidence supports.

Meanwhile the things the report does not show are the things that most matter to the relationship. Channel-partner errors, which are the vendor's most damaging failures, appear nowhere in the vendor's own reporting. Re-emergence is invisible, so the same operator being actioned monthly looks like sustained productivity. And nothing shows where the problem has moved, so the scope stays where it was set at onboarding.

The report is well produced and answers the wrong question, because it was designed around the metric the contract is priced on rather than around the decision the client has to make.

## Why Nobody Has Built This

**The report justifies the invoice.** It is built around the billable metric because that is its commercial function.

**An outcome report needs outcome measurement.** Prevalence, re-emergence and exposure are the measurements described in [[niches/brand-protection-firms/effectiveness-measurement/profile|🟠 Enforcement Effectiveness Measurement]] and do not exist, so there is nothing to report.

**Showing errors is unattractive.** A report including channel-partner actions and reversal rates is a report that shows the vendor's failures, which no vendor volunteers.

**The client asks for the count.** Brands specify reporting in terms of takedowns at contracting, so the report delivers what was asked for.

**Comparisons would invite competition.** A report structured for vendor comparison makes switching easier, which is not in the incumbent's interest.

**Nobody has offered an alternative.** A brand cannot buy outcome reporting from anyone, so the count is the only option rather than the preferred one.

## What to Build

**Lead with the trend, not the count.** Prevalence in monitored categories over time, with its uncertainty. Even a rough estimate is a better headline than a volume number, because it is the shape of the answer the client needs.

**Show re-emergence.** Operators actioned and the proportion that returned within thirty days. This makes the difference between disruption and churn visible, which is the industry's central unexamined question.

**Include the errors.** Channel-partner actions, reversals and their resolution times. A vendor reporting its own errors is making a credibility claim no competitor currently can, and it is also the thing the client most needs to see.

**Answer the budget question.** What the current spend covers, where the gaps are, and what an increment would extend to. Currently the budget conversation has no analytical basis at all.

**Show where the problem moved.** Which surfaces, categories and geographies have grown, so scope can be revisited rather than persisting where it was set.

**Keep activity as an operational appendix.** Takedown counts are useful for operations and should not be the headline. Separating the operational view from the executive view is most of the fix.

**Make it comparable.** Reporting on a common structure that allows comparison between vendors is against an incumbent's interest and in the buyer's — and the firm confident in its performance benefits from it.

## Target Customer

Brand protection firms competing on effect, for whom outcome-shaped reporting is a differentiator that requires the measurement to exist first.

Brands and their procurement functions, who should specify the reporting structure at contracting rather than accepting the vendor's default.

Independent reporting providers, since a comparable, outcome-shaped view across vendors cannot credibly come from one of them.

## Impact If Built

The client's manager gets a report that answers the question they are asked, instead of one they must translate and then defend.

Including channel-partner errors and reversals in the vendor's own reporting would create accountability for the failures that currently exist only as internal complaints.

And separating the executive view from the operational appendix is most of the fix, because the count is genuinely useful for running the operation and genuinely misleading as a headline.

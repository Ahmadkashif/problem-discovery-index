# Buy: Client Reporting Practice From Agency Services

**Niche:** Reporting & Account Operations
**Industry:** [[industries/brand-protection-firms|Brand Protection Firms]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Marketing agencies spent a decade being forced from activity reporting to outcome reporting by clients who stopped accepting impressions as a result, and brand protection still reports impressions.
**Tags:** #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #causal-inference #revenue-impact #descriptive-statistics #workflow-orchestration
**Contested on:** Whether the monthly report answers the client's question or restates the count the contract was priced on.

## The Problem

Reporting an activity metric as though it were a result is a pattern marketing agencies were pushed out of, and the mechanism by which it happened is instructive.

Agencies reported impressions, reach and clicks. Clients accepted it until marketing procurement professionalised, independent verification appeared, and brands began asking for incremental effect. Agencies that could show outcome differentiated; the ones that could not lost share. Media auditing became a normal function. Reporting standards emerged. And the conversation moved from activity to effect, unevenly and substantially.

Brand protection reports takedowns and is where agencies were before that shift. The client accepts it, there is no independent verification, no auditing function exists, and no vendor differentiates on outcome because none can demonstrate it.

The transition in marketing was driven by the buyer rather than by the sellers, which is the relevant lesson.

## What Already Exists

Agency reporting practice: outcome-oriented reporting frameworks, incrementality reporting and the structures that emerged after procurement pressure.

Media auditing: independent verification of agency-reported delivery and value, now a standard function at large advertisers.

Marketing procurement: the specific discipline of buying services whose output is hard to attribute, with its own professional body of practice.

Measurement standards: industry bodies defining how delivery and effect are reported so that vendors are comparable.

Brand protection: vendor dashboards and monthly takedown reporting.

## The Customization Gap

**No auditing function exists.** Media auditing verifies what an agency reported. Nobody verifies a brand protection vendor's takedown count, its accuracy or its coverage, and the count is entirely self-reported.

**No reporting standard exists.** Marketing bodies define reporting terms so that vendors can be compared. Brand protection has no standard, so every vendor reports its own way and comparison is impossible.

**Procurement is not involved.** The contract is usually managed by legal or IP rather than by a procurement function with the discipline that drove the change in marketing.

**The outcome is harder to measure here.** Marketing eventually had sales data. Counterfeit sales are unobservable, which makes the proxies more important and the transition harder — though not impossible, as the prevalence measures show.

**No independent verification is possible today.** A brand cannot check a takedown count, cannot check accuracy, and has no third party to ask.

**The buyer has not pushed.** Marketing's shift happened because buyers organised and demanded it. Brand protection buyers have not.

## Target Customer

Brand procurement and marketing procurement functions, who have already been through this transition in another category and would recognise the pattern immediately.

Industry bodies and anti-counterfeiting coalitions, as the natural home for a reporting standard that individual vendors would be penalised for adopting alone.

Independent verification providers, for whom brand protection auditing is an adjacent service to media auditing with the same structure and no incumbent.

## Impact If Solved

A transition that already happened in an adjacent category, driven by buyers, offers a direct template — including the sequence in which it occurred.

A reporting standard is the enabling artefact, because it makes vendors comparable and comparison is what creates the pressure to report outcome.

And an independent verification function would give brands their first ability to check what they are being told, which in marketing was the change that made everything else follow.

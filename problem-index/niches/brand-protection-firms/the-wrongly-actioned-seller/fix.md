# Fix: Nobody Bears a Cost for Being Wrong

**Niche:** The Wrongly-Actioned Seller
**Industry:** [[industries/brand-protection-firms|Brand Protection Firms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A wrong notice costs the filer nothing, costs the platform an appeal, and costs the seller their income — which is exactly the incentive structure that produces a high error rate.
**Tags:** #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #compliance #revenue-impact #worker-facing #hypothesis-testing
**Contested on:** Whether a legitimate seller removed by mistake has any practical route to redress.

## The Problem

Consider the incentives at the moment a determination is made.

If the reviewer actions a genuine counterfeit, the brand is served and the count increases. If they action a legitimate seller, the count still increases, the platform handles the appeal, the seller loses income, and the filer learns nothing because the reversal does not return to them.

So over-enforcement is free to the party doing it and expensive to a party with no voice in the process. That is not a description of anyone behaving badly; it is a description of an incentive structure that will reliably produce a high error rate regardless of who is operating it.

Every other consequence in this niche follows from it. Precision is not measured because nothing requires it. Evidence standards are met at the minimum because the minimum is sufficient. Lawful categories are not routed separately because routing them costs volume. And appeal outcomes are not requested because nobody's metrics would use them.

Introducing any cost for a wrong notice — reputational, commercial or legal — would change all of it. Nothing currently does.

## Why It's Still Broken

**The harm falls outside every commercial relationship.** The seller is not a customer of the filer, the brand or, in a meaningful sense, the platform's enforcement function.

**Notice processes are designed for speed on the rights holder's side.** Actioning on notice with contest afterwards is the design, and it makes the cost of a wrong notice fall entirely on the recipient.

**No misrepresentation penalty operates in practice.** Copyright regimes have one in principle and it is rarely used. Trademark notices on marketplaces mostly have no equivalent at all.

**Error rates are unpublished and largely uncomputed.** Without a number there is no reputational cost, and without reversal feedback there is not even a private one.

**Brands do not ask.** A brand procuring enforcement asks for volume and coverage. Asking for precision would change the market and nobody asks.

**The affected sellers are dispersed and unorganised.** The parties with the strongest interest in change have the least ability to coordinate, which is beginning to shift and has not yet.

## What a Fix Looks Like

**Publish reversal rates by notice sender.** Platforms hold this data. Publishing it creates a reputational cost for high error rates and lets brands see which vendors are wrong most often. This is the single highest-leverage change and it requires one party to decide.

**Return every reversal to the filer.** Free, immediate, and it converts an invisible error into private feedback at minimum.

**Make brands ask for precision at procurement.** A brand requiring a reported wrongful-action rate changes what firms measure. Brands also carry the reputational risk when a wrongful action becomes public, so the interest is real once it is named.

**Apply escalating consequences to repeat error.** A notice sender with a persistently high reversal rate should face additional evidence requirements or rate limits. Platforms already do this for other abuse patterns and not for this one.

**Require reasons with every notice.** A notice stating the category and the basis is checkable. One asserting infringement is not, and the assertion standard is what makes cheap wrong notices possible.

**Give the seller a route to the filer.** Direct contactability creates the cost most reliably, because a firm that hears from the people it wrongly actions will reduce the rate.

**Anticipate the regulation.** Platform accountability regimes are imposing notice accuracy and redress obligations, and building ahead of them is cheaper than retrofitting under enforcement.

## Who Feels the Pain

The legitimate seller, whose livelihood absorbs the entire cost of an error that costs the party responsible nothing.

The platform, which handles the appeals and the reputational consequences of an enforcement decision it did not make.

The brand, which carries the public risk when its enforcement vendor suspends a well-known legitimate reseller, and has no way to assess that risk in advance.

And the firms that are genuinely careful, who bear the cost of that care in a market where precision is invisible and volume is rewarded.

## Impact If Fixed

Published reversal rates by notice sender is the fix. It creates a cost where none exists, it requires one party's decision, and it uses data platforms already hold.

Returning reversals to the filer costs nothing and would give firms their first private measurement of their own error rate.

And requiring reasons with every notice would raise the floor on evidence at the moment of filing, which is where the error is made and where nothing currently asks anything at all.

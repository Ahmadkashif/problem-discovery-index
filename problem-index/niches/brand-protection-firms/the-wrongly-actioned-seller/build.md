# Build: A Redress Path That Reaches the Filer

**Niche:** The Wrongly-Actioned Seller
**Industry:** [[industries/brand-protection-firms|Brand Protection Firms]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A redress route that connects the affected seller to the party that filed the notice, resolves the common legitimate categories quickly, and returns the outcome to the filer so the error does not recur.
**Tags:** #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #compliance #workflow-orchestration #bert #automation #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Whether a legitimate seller removed by mistake has any practical route to redress.

## The Problem

A seller is actioned. They do not know who filed the notice, cannot contact them, and can only appeal to the platform.

The appeal asks them to prove legitimacy. For a genuine reseller of goods bought at retail, that means invoices they may not have kept. For a repair service, it means explaining a legal doctrine to a support queue. For a parallel importer, it means a jurisdictional argument. Each is a legal position that is probably correct and that the seller has to articulate without counsel, into a form, with their income suspended.

Meanwhile the party that made the wrong determination is not in the process at all. They do not learn of the appeal, do not learn of the reversal, and will apply the same logic to the same seller next month.

The information that would resolve most of these quickly exists. Whether the seller is an authorised distributor is a list the brand holds. Whether the listing describes a repair service is determinable from its text. Whether this seller has been determined legitimate before is in the filer's own records. And whether the goods are genuine is frequently establishable from serialisation the brand maintains.

None of it reaches the appeal, because the appeal is between the seller and the platform and the evidence is held by the brand and the filer.

## Why Nobody Has Built This

**The harmed party is nobody's customer.** The seller has no relationship with the filer or the brand, and is a cost centre for the platform. No commercial actor benefits from serving them.

**The current asymmetry favours every party with power.** The filer bears no cost, the brand gets enforcement, and the platform gets a defensible process. Only the seller is worse off, and they have the least leverage.

**Connecting seller to filer exposes the filer.** A firm contactable by everyone it actions receives complaints and learns its own error rate, which is useful and unwelcome.

**Evidence sits with parties who are not in the appeal.** Authorised seller status and serialisation are held by the brand, which is not a participant in the platform's appeal process.

**Volume makes individual handling expensive.** Platforms process enormous numbers of notices and appeals, and a richer process costs money against a party with no commercial weight.

**No regulation currently requires it, though that is changing.** Platform accountability regimes imposing redress obligations are arriving, and the industry has not prepared.

## What to Build

**Give every notice a route back to the filer.** The seller should be able to contact the party that made the determination, with a defined response obligation. This is the structural change: it makes the error correctable at source and gives the filer the feedback that would reduce the rate.

**Resolve the common legitimate categories automatically.** Authorised distributor status checked against a current list, repair and compatible-part listings identified from text, prior legitimate determinations on the same seller surfaced. A large share of wrongful actions would be reversed within hours by these checks alone.

**Put the brand's evidence into the appeal.** Authorised seller lists and serialisation data, accessible to the appeal process, so the seller is not asked to prove something the brand can confirm.

**Return the outcome to the filer, always.** A reversal should reach the party who filed, correct the seller's record, and mark the detection pattern. Without this the same seller is actioned again.

**Provide an accessible evidence standard.** A genuine reseller may not have invoices for goods bought years ago. Purchase evidence, serial numbers, supplier correspondence and platform transaction history should all be acceptable, because a standard that only a sophisticated party can meet is a standard that excludes the sellers most affected.

**Restore properly on reversal.** Listing restoration is not enough where an account was suspended and search ranking lost. Reinstatement should address the account's standing, not only its existence.

**Report the rate.** Platforms publishing reversal rates by notice sender would create the accountability that currently does not exist anywhere in this chain.

## Target Customer

Platforms, who absorb the appeals, face growing regulatory pressure on notice accuracy and redress, and are the only party positioned to build the connection between seller and filer.

Regulators implementing platform accountability regimes, for whom notice-and-action redress is an explicit obligation and the current practice is plainly inadequate.

Seller associations and marketplace seller communities, as the constituency, and increasingly as an organised voice.

## Impact If Built

A route from the seller to the filer is the structural fix, because it makes the error correctable at source and gives the party who made it the only feedback that would change their rate.

Automatic checks against authorised-seller and prior-determination data would reverse a large share of wrongful actions within hours, using information that already exists.

And published reversal rates by notice sender would let brands choose enforcement vendors on accuracy — creating, for the first time, a commercial cost for getting it wrong.

# Infringement Determination

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/brand-protection-firms|Brand Protection Firms]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Whether a flagged listing is actually infringing, which is a legal judgement that no image match settles and that currently gets three seconds.

## Profile

**Market Size:** ~$440M
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~11%
**Digital Adoption:** Low — a reviewer, a photograph and a queue
**Target Buyer:** IP counsel, brand protection leadership, platforms, regulators
**Automation Potential:** Moderate — routing and evidence automate, the judgement does not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche

This is the precision half and it is not a detection problem. A listing that matches a brand's imagery may be a counterfeit, or it may be lawful resale of genuine goods, a parallel import, a refurbishment, a compatible part correctly described, a repair service naming what it repairs, a review, a parody, or an unrelated business with a similar name.

Several of those are lawful, some depend on jurisdiction, and none is distinguishable from a photograph and a price. The determination is a legal judgement, and it is made by a reviewer with a throughput target from the least informative possible evidence.

That is what separates it from its sibling. [[niches/brand-protection-firms/adversarial-detection/profile|🎯 Adversarial Detection]] is a modelling contest with a measurable objective where better is always better. Determination is a judgement problem where improving detection actively makes things worse — a more sensitive matcher surfaces more borderline cases and the same three seconds are spread across more of them.

It is also where the documented harm sits. A wrongly-actioned seller loses a listing, sometimes an account and an income, and contests it through a process built for the party with counsel. The firms' own error rates in this direction are not published and mostly not computed.

## Current Tools & Gaps

Review interfaces presenting the listing. Brand-supplied guidance on what constitutes infringement. Authorised seller lists, where the brand maintains one. Escalation to IP counsel for contested cases. Platform notice forms with their own evidence requirements.

The gaps concentrate on evidence and process. The lawful categories are not distinguished before review, so a repair service and a replica arrive identically. Authorised seller lists are stale, incomplete or absent, and their absence is the single largest cause of wrongful action against legitimate resellers. Jurisdiction is not modelled, though parallel import legality varies by it. No rationale is recorded, so an appeal contests an unrecorded reasoning. Accuracy is unmeasured in the direction that harms sellers. And appeal outcomes do not return to the firm, so the clearest evidence of error is discarded.

## The Problem
Insurance underwriting is the discipline of pricing an uncertain future cost from a history of past ones. It uses risk factors observable at underwriting time, a loss distribution rather than an expected value, explicit loading for uncertainty, exclusions for risks that should not be carried, and continuous monitoring of loss ratios against prediction. A fixed-price port is an insurance contract in everything but name: the studio accepts an uncertain cost for a fixed premium.

## What Already Exists
Risk factor models from historical loss data; loss distribution rather than point estimation; uncertainty loading and reinsurance; exclusions and conditions; and loss ratio monitoring against predicted.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a portfolio of dozens rather than thousands, where the insurer also performs the work. It requires: (1) a project count in the dozens, so the model must be simple, heavily pooled and honest about its own uncertainty — this is the substantive difference from any actuarial practice; (2) a studio that both prices and executes, meaning its own capability is a risk factor; (3) losses that are effort overruns rather than claims, with no independent verification; (4) a competitive bid process where a correctly priced risk loses to an underpriced one; and (5) exclusions that must be negotiated with a client who is buying certainty.

## Target Customer
Porting and co-development studios, commercial leadership, publishers, and services pricing and risk vendors.

## Impact If Solved
Underwriting prices uncertainty from loss history as routine practice. A portfolio of dozens rather than thousands, where the insurer also does the work, is what makes the model simple, pooled and honest about itself.

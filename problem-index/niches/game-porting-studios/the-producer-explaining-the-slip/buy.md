## The Problem
Construction contracting solved the client-keeps-changing-things problem contractually and procedurally. Variation orders record every change with its cost and time impact and require sign-off before work proceeds. Delay analysis attributes schedule slip to specific causes and parties. Extension-of-time provisions exist precisely because a contractor should not bear a delay the client caused. The practice is standard, documented and enforceable. Porting studios face the same situation and absorb it.

## What Already Exists
Variation order processes with cost and time impact; delay analysis methodologies; extension-of-time provisions; contemporaneous record-keeping standards; and dispute resolution frameworks.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a client whose ordinary work is itself the variation. It requires: (1) client-side development that is expected and continuous rather than an exceptional change order, so a per-variation process would be absurd and the mechanism must be cumulative — this is the substantive difference; (2) impact measured in merge and rework effort rather than in materials and labour; (3) a commercial relationship where raising variations aggressively damages a repeat-business relationship, unlike a one-off build; (4) records that are code and test artefacts rather than site diaries; and (5) a schedule whose critical path runs through verification rather than through physical sequencing.

## Target Customer
Porting studios, production leadership, publishers, and contract and project management vendors.

## Impact If Solved
Construction built variation orders because clients change things mid-build and contractors should not bear it. Continuous client development being normal rather than exceptional is what makes the mechanism cumulative rather than per-change.

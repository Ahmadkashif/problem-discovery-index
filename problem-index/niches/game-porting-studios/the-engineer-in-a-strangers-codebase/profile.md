# The Engineer in a Stranger's Codebase

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/game-porting-studios|Game Porting Studios]]
**Category:** 🟣 Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to make a large unfamiliar codebase comprehensible to an engineer with no access to its authors and no documentation of why anything is the way it is — and whoever does that takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$200M US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~10% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Low — no support
**Target Buyer:** Engineering leads
**Automation Potential:** High — code comprehension support

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The job is to make a large unfamiliar codebase work on hardware it was never written for, with no access to the people who wrote it and no documentation of why anything is the way it is. The engineer must infer intent from code, distinguish deliberate design from accident, and avoid breaking behaviour they do not understand. It is cognitively demanding in a specific way — every question that would take thirty seconds to ask has to be answered by reading — and nothing supports it.

## Current Tools & Gaps
An IDE, a debugger, the code, and whatever the client answers by email eventually. The gaps: no orientation to an unfamiliar codebase; no history or intent recovery; no map of what is safe to change; no way to ask a question without a round trip to the client; and no capture of what the engineer learns.

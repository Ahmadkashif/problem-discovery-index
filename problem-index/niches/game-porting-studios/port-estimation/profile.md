# Port Estimation

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/game-porting-studios|Game Porting Studios]]
**Category:** 🔵 High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to quote a fixed price and a fixed date for work whose difficulty depends entirely on properties of a codebase they cannot see until after the contract is signed — and whoever estimates it accurately takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$550M US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~27% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Very low — judgement
**Target Buyer:** Commercial and technical leadership
**Automation Potential:** High — measurement plus a historical model

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The business is priced on estimation and the estimation is a guess. A port is quoted before the studio has meaningful access to the codebase, as a fixed price against a fixed date, and the actual effort depends on things invisible at bid time. Estimation error comes straight out of margin. The studios that are good at this are good because of a small number of experienced people whose judgement is neither transferable nor checkable.

### Contested sub-niches
This niche is not terminal. It names a function, and two different problems sit inside it:
- [[niches/game-porting-studios/codebase-assessment/profile|🎯 Codebase Assessment]] — what is measurable about the code that predicts effort
- [[niches/game-porting-studios/effort-model-and-pricing/profile|🎯 Effort Model & Bid Pricing]] — turning measurement and history into a price with risk

## Current Tools & Gaps
A scoping call, a demo build, a spreadsheet and a technical director's judgement. The gaps: no measurement of the codebase at bid time; no record of past estimates against actuals; no risk pricing; no contingency method; and no way to check an expert's judgement against outcomes.

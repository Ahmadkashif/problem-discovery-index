## The Problem
Broadcast media solved the currency problem institutionally. Radio and television audience measurement is conducted by a body separate from the sellers, governed by a joint industry committee representing buyers and sellers, with published methodology, audited procedures and stated error properties. Nobody pretends the panel is perfect; everybody agrees it is the currency, and the independence is the reason it functions. Digital audio's currency is a count produced by the platforms selling the inventory, with the independent measurement providers now owned by those platforms.

## What Already Exists
Panel-based audience measurement with published methodology; joint industry committee governance; independent audit of measurement procedures; agreed currency definitions; and error and reliability reporting as standard.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a census-scale digital channel with no institutional structure. It requires: (1) governance established from nothing, since there is no joint industry committee and creating one is a coordination problem rather than a measurement one — this institutional gap is the substantive obstacle and no vendor can solve it alone; (2) census-level server data rather than a panel, which is a far richer input and could produce better measurement than broadcast ever had if the incentives allowed it; (3) exposure inference rather than presence, since a download is not a listen and the panel model has no equivalent problem; (4) attribution to online conversion as an expectation, which broadcast currency never attempted and which digital advertisers demand; and (5) fragmented publishers with no shared body, unlike broadcasters who were few and already organised.

## Target Customer
Advertiser and agency bodies, publishers seeking a credible currency, and audience measurement organisations for whom digital audio is an unclaimed territory.

## Impact If Solved
Broadcast currencies work because the measurer is separate from the seller, and digital audio has the opposite arrangement. Census server data could produce better measurement than broadcast ever had, and the missing piece is the governance rather than the method.

# A Currency That Counts File Requests

**Niche:** [[niches/audio-adtech-networks/the-measurement-layer/profile|The Measurement Layer]]
**Industry:** [[industries/audio-adtech-networks|Audio Adtech Networks]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** The industry's currency is the download, which counts a file request rather than a listen, and certainly not a listen that reached the advertisement.
**Tags:** #bayesian-inference #survival-analysis #confidence-intervals #evaluation-metrics #causal-inference #revenue-impact #descriptive-statistics #hypothesis-testing
**Contested on:** This niche is not terminal — establishing that a listener reached the advertisement and establishing that hearing it caused anything are different contests with different winners, and they are stated separately in the sub-niches.

## The Problem
An advertiser buys on downloads. The standard defines a download carefully and it remains a count of file requests. Dynamic insertion then places the advertisement at a position in the episode, which may sit past where most listeners stop — so the impression is served and counted whether or not anybody reached it. Attribution is a household IP match against a later website visit, which degrades under shared networks and address rotation and cannot see a listener who converts on another device. The channel's entire commercial case rests on two numbers, neither of which measures what an advertiser believes they are buying.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Downloads were the only available count when the industry formed, and a currency once established is defended by everyone who has priced against it — changing it restates every historical comparison and reduces every reported number. The telemetry that would establish exposure sits with streaming platforms who are also sellers and have no reason to publish a smaller figure. The independent measurement layer that might have forced it has been acquired. And advertisers have accepted the arrangement for a decade.

## What to Build
Build the measurement the channel does not have. Model exposure from listening telemetry rather than counting requests, which is the first sub-niche's contest and is tractable because the data exists and is simply not used for this. Report exposure as the currency alongside downloads with the gap explained, since the honest number will be lower and a transition that restates history is the only way a market accepts it. Improve attribution beyond household matching and publish its error rate, which is the second sub-niche and is where the governance question lives. Establish independence structurally, because every current referee is also a seller and no methodological improvement survives that conflict. Combine exposure and attribution into a single reported outcome, since an advertiser needs one number and currently receives two weak ones. Validate against panels, surveys and experiments where available, which is the external check the channel has never had. Report cross-device and out-of-household loss explicitly, so the known blind spot is quantified rather than ignored. Standardise through an industry body, since a single vendor's better number is incomparable and will be dismissed. Make the method inspectable, because the credibility problem is the product problem here. And measure the channel's honest reach and effect, since an audio industry that could state those credibly would be bought with far more confidence than one that cannot.

## Target Customer
Advertisers buying audio on faith, publishers whose inventory is undersold because it cannot be evidenced, and the industry bodies who could establish a currency.

## Impact If Built
A currency established when nothing better existed is defended by everyone who priced against it, and it counts file requests. Modelling exposure from telemetry that already exists produces a lower and honest number, which is the only basis on which the channel can be bought with confidence.

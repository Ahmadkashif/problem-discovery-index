# The Referee Who Sells the Inventory

**Niche:** [[niches/audio-adtech-networks/the-measurement-layer/profile|The Measurement Layer]]
**Industry:** [[industries/audio-adtech-networks|Audio Adtech Networks]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The independent measurement companies that verified audio advertising were acquired by the platforms selling it, and one of the main ones was shut down, leaving nobody outside the trade to check the numbers.

## The Problem
The measurement of audio advertising is now substantially performed by the parties selling it. The independent providers that grew up to verify attribution were acquired, and one of the significant ones was closed in 2024. An advertiser asking whether a campaign worked receives an answer computed by the seller, using a method the seller controls, with an error rate nobody publishes. In any other market this arrangement would be regarded as a structural failure rather than a measurement inconvenience, and in this one it is simply the current state.

## Why It's Still Broken
Independent measurement is a hard business to sustain — it is expensive, its customers are advertisers who buy it reluctantly, and the platforms can offer an equivalent free, which is what produced the consolidation. Advertisers accept free measurement because the alternative has a price. No industry body has intervened. And the channel is small enough relative to others that scrutiny has not arrived.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Re-establish an external check. Fund independent measurement collectively through an advertiser or industry body, which is the fix and is the only structure in which independence survives — a commercial independent measurement business has repeatedly failed against free measurement from sellers, and the funding model is the problem rather than the product. Require methodology disclosure as a condition of buying, which advertisers can impose individually and have not. Publish error rates as a market norm, so a seller's measurement can at least be evaluated. Run advertiser-side verification where possible, using the advertiser's own conversion data and their own experiments, which is independent by construction. Standardise the attribution method through a body so that seller-produced numbers are at least comparable. Audit the platforms' measurement periodically, which is a well-established mechanism in other media. Separate measurement from sales organisationally within platforms, which is a partial remedy platforms could adopt and would differentiate on. Preserve the historical record from the closed providers, since the comparison against a period with independent measurement is valuable evidence. Report the share of measurement that is seller-produced, which names the situation plainly. And build the case with advertisers, because they are the only party with the interest and the money to fix this and have not yet been shown what it is costing them.

## Who Feels the Pain
Advertisers buying on numbers produced by sellers; publishers whose honest inventory is indistinguishable from inflated inventory; and the channel, whose growth depends on a credibility it has removed the means to demonstrate.

## Impact If Fixed
A commercial independent measurement business fails repeatedly against free measurement from sellers, so the funding model is the problem rather than the product. Collective funding through an advertiser body is the only structure in which independence survives, and advertiser-side verification is independent by construction.

# Brand Suitability & Transcripts

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/audio-adtech-networks|Audio Adtech Networks]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to judge whether an episode is suitable for a brand rather than whether it contains a word — and whoever does that stops audio repeating the mistake that defunded news on the open web.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$700M US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~9% of category spend
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — transcription deployed, judgement absent
**Target Buyer:** Advertiser brand safety and platform policy
**Automation Potential:** Very High — the transcripts exist and language models are commodity

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Transcription made audio content readable, and the industry used it to build the same blunt keyword blocklists that defunded news on the open web. The transcript is a far richer artefact than a web page's text — it captures tone, context, the speaker, the segment structure — and the category's first application of it was a word list. This is a distinct contest because the input is unusually good, the mistake is well documented from another medium, and the correction is available now rather than requiring a capability that does not exist.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Automated transcription, keyword blocklists applied to transcripts, category classification of shows, and manual review. The gaps: word matching without meaning; suitability judged at show level when episodes vary; no advertiser-specific suitability; no assessment of tone or framing; and the same defunding of serious content that the open web produced.

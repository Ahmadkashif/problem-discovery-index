# The Show Blocked for Its Subject Matter

**Niche:** [[niches/audio-adtech-networks/brand-suitability-and-transcripts/profile|Brand Suitability & Transcripts]]
**Industry:** [[industries/audio-adtech-networks|Audio Adtech Networks]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A show is excluded from a brand's buy because its subject matter triggers a keyword, and the entire back catalogue is demonetised for years with no notification and no appeal.
**Tags:** #evaluation-metrics #compliance #descriptive-statistics #hypothesis-testing #quick-win #revenue-impact #large-language-models #bert
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to judge whether an episode is suitable for a brand rather than whether it contains a word — and whoever does that stops audio repeating the mistake that defunded news on the open web.

## The Problem
A podcast covering health, politics, crime or current affairs discusses its subject. Keywords appear. Blocklists exclude the show, frequently across its entire catalogue, from a large share of programmatic demand. The publisher sees revenue fall and does not know why — there is no notification, no visible reason and no route to contest it. They may adjust their content, avoiding the subjects that appear to cost money, which is the outcome nobody intended and which the open web has already demonstrated. The show was never unsuitable; it contained words.

## Why It's Still Broken
Exclusion is invisible to the excluded party, so the feedback that would correct the error never reaches anyone who could act — a decision with no notification is a decision with no error correction. Advertisers setting lists never see the inventory they removed. The cost falls entirely on the publisher. And the blocklist is the only control most buying interfaces expose.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Make the exclusion visible and contestable. Tell publishers when and why they are excluded, which is the fix and is the step that creates any feedback at all — an exclusion the publisher cannot see cannot be corrected by anyone. Provide an appeal route with a human decision, since the false positives are numerous and identifiable and currently have nowhere to go. Show advertisers what their rules exclude before the rules are applied, including examples, which prevents the worst additions at the moment they are made. Report the reach and quality cost of each rule, so the trade-off is a decision rather than a default. Assess at episode rather than show level, since the catalogue-wide exclusion is the largest and most avoidable harm. Prune lists by testing each term's actual effect, which reliably shows that most terms exclude nothing unsuitable. Replace matching with meaning-based assessment, which is the build note's subject and is now cheap. Track exclusion rates by publisher and by content category, so systematic defunding of journalism is visible in the data rather than only in complaints. Publish aggregate figures through an industry body, since the open web's experience shows this becomes entrenched quickly once it is normal. And give publishers a suitability profile they can show buyers, which turns a defensive position into a commercial one.

## Who Feels the Pain
Publishers of serious audio losing revenue for covering their subject; advertisers paying more for worse inventory; and listeners whose journalism podcasts lose their funding.

## Impact If Fixed
An exclusion the publisher cannot see cannot be corrected by anyone, which removes every feedback path. Notification with an appeal route creates the correction, and episode-level assessment prevents a catalogue being demonetised for years over one word.

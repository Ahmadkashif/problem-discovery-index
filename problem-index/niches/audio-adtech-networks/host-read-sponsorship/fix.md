# Verifying the Read by Listening to It

**Niche:** [[niches/audio-adtech-networks/host-read-sponsorship/profile|Host-Read Sponsorship]]
**Industry:** [[industries/audio-adtech-networks|Audio Adtech Networks]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Confirming that a sponsor's read aired, covered the agreed points and named the offer correctly is done by a person listening to the episode.
**Tags:** #transformers #large-language-models #automation #evaluation-metrics #compliance #quick-win #workflow-orchestration #descriptive-statistics
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to run the host-read business as a market rather than as a set of email threads — and whoever does that prices the channel's most valuable and least systematised product.

## The Problem
A campaign runs across eleven shows for eight weeks. Somebody must confirm that each read aired, in the right episode, covering the agreed talking points, with the correct offer and promotional code, and without a prohibited claim. This is done by a person listening — or more commonly by spot-checking a few and assuming the rest. Reads get missed entirely, offers get misstated, codes get read incorrectly so the tracking fails, and a prohibited claim occasionally airs. The verification is the sponsor's only assurance that they received what they paid for, and it is a person with headphones and a checklist.

## Why It's Still Broken
Verification has always been manual because it required listening, and transcription only became cheap and accurate recently while the process did not change — the capability arrived and the workflow did not notice. Nobody owns verification as a function; it falls to whoever has time. Spot-checking feels sufficient because problems are occasional. And a missed read is usually discovered by the sponsor, which is the worst way to find out.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Verify from the audio automatically. Transcribe every published episode and check for the read, which is the fix, is now cheap and accurate, and turns a spot-check into complete coverage. Confirm the agreed talking points were covered, since that is the contractual obligation and is checkable against the brief. Verify the offer and promotional code were stated correctly, because a misread code silently destroys the campaign's only tracking and is a common and completely preventable failure. Flag prohibited claims, which is a compliance requirement in regulated categories and is currently enforced by hope. Record the timestamp and position of the read, which connects to exposure and lets a sponsor know where in the episode they appeared. Alert when a read is missing from a scheduled episode, immediately rather than at reconciliation. Produce the sponsor's delivery evidence automatically, which is the artefact they actually want and which is currently assembled by hand if at all. Track read variation across episodes and hosts, since a host who improvises differently each time is a data point about the product's consistency. Feed the transcripts into performance analysis, since what was actually said is the creative variable and is currently unrecorded. And measure the miss rate, because a network that can state its delivery accuracy has something to sell that its competitors cannot.

## Who Feels the Pain
Sponsors paying for reads that did not air; operations staff listening to episodes with a checklist; and networks discovering misses when a client complains.

## Impact If Fixed
Verification was manual because it required listening, and transcription became cheap while the workflow did not notice. Automatic transcript checking turns a spot-check into complete coverage and catches misread promotional codes that silently destroy a campaign's only tracking.

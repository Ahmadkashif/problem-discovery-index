**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)

**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to forecast and price a perishable back-catalogue-heavy inventory that dynamic insertion created — and whoever does that properly stops publishers selling next month on last month's downloads.

## The Problem
Revenue management exists because perishable inventory with uncertain demand is worth managing carefully. Airlines and hotels built demand forecasting, booking curve analysis, overbooking policy, price fencing and capacity allocation across fare classes, and it transformed their economics. The methods are documented and the software is mature. Audio advertising inventory is perishable in exactly the same way — a slot unserved is revenue gone — and is managed by trend extrapolation and a rate card.

## What Already Exists
Demand forecasting with booking curves; capacity allocation across fare or rate classes; overbooking policy with measured spoilage; dynamic pricing and price fencing; and yield measurement against theoretical maximum.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to capacity that is itself forecast rather than fixed. It requires: (1) uncertain supply as well as uncertain demand, since an airline knows it has a hundred and eighty seats and a publisher does not know how many downloads next month's episodes will produce — forecasting both sides simultaneously is the central difference and makes the problem harder than the classical one; (2) a back catalogue producing capacity indefinitely, which has no airline analogue and behaves like a separate business; (3) demand classes that are genuinely different products, since a host-read sponsorship and a programmatic spot are not fare classes on the same seat; (4) publishers who are frequently individuals or small teams, so the practice must be embedded in a platform rather than operated; and (5) inventory whose value depends on an exposure estimate rather than on a known seat, which adds a measurement dependency airlines never had.

## Target Customer
Audio publishers and networks, hosting and monetisation platforms, and revenue management vendors for whom audio inventory is an unserved application.

## Impact If Solved
Revenue management transformed industries with perishable inventory and audio manages the same shape with a trend line. Uncertain supply as well as uncertain demand is the central difference, and a back catalogue producing capacity indefinitely has no airline analogue.

# The Listening Curve for Your Own Show

**Niche:** [[niches/audio-adtech-networks/the-podcast-host/profile|The Podcast Host]]
**Industry:** [[industries/audio-adtech-networks|Audio Adtech Networks]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The platform knows exactly where the host's audience stops listening and shows the host a download count.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #worker-facing #quick-win #confidence-intervals #revenue-impact #survival-analysis #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to tell a host what their endorsement is actually worth — and whoever does that lets the person supplying the channel's only irreplaceable asset price it.

## The Problem
The hosting platform records where every listener stops. It knows that this show's audience listens to the end and that one's falls away at twelve minutes, which episode formats hold attention and which lose it, where in an episode people skip. The host sees a download number and perhaps a completion percentage. They cannot see the curve, cannot tell whether their new format is working, cannot tell an advertiser how many people reach a mid-roll, and cannot make the case that their engaged audience is worth more than a larger disengaged one. The information is about their own audience, it exists, and it is not shown to them.

## Why It's Still Broken
Platforms built analytics for their own operations and surfaced a download count to creators, because the download is what the business runs on — the creator-facing view followed the commercial metric rather than the creator's questions. Detailed curves would let hosts argue for exposure-based pricing, which is not in every platform's interest. Nobody has asked loudly. And the analytics screen was built once and never revisited.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Show the host their own data. Publish the position-level listening curve per episode and per show, which is the fix, requires no new collection, and is the single most useful thing a host could be given about their own audience. Show where listeners stop and where they skip, so format decisions are informed rather than guessed. Report exposure at each insertion point, which is what an advertiser is buying and what the host should be pricing. Compare against similar shows so a curve has meaning, since a host looking at their own curve alone cannot tell whether it is good. Track the curve over time and against format changes, which turns it into a feedback loop for the show rather than a static fact. Show the back catalogue's continuing listening, which is a substantial part of many shows' value and is invisible in a monthly download figure. Give the host an exportable view they can take to an advertiser, which converts the data into negotiating evidence. Explain what the numbers mean, since most hosts are not analysts and an unexplained curve is not usable. Make it available to independent hosts rather than only to networked ones, because the independents have the least support and the most need. And measure whether hosts who see their curves make better shows, since that is the outcome and it is checkable.

## Who Feels the Pain
Hosts making format decisions blind about their own audience; advertisers unable to buy on engagement because the data is not exposed; and shows with excellent retention priced identically to shows without.

## Impact If Fixed
The creator-facing view followed the commercial metric rather than the creator's questions, so the most basic fact about their own audience is withheld. Publishing the position-level curve requires no new collection and turns a download number into both a format feedback loop and negotiating evidence.

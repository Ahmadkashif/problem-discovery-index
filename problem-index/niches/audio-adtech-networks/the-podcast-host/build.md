# Lending Credibility, Paid on Downloads

**Niche:** [[niches/audio-adtech-networks/the-podcast-host/profile|The Podcast Host]]
**Industry:** [[industries/audio-adtech-networks|Audio Adtech Networks]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A podcast host lends their credibility to a product, is paid on a download number that overstates who heard it, and has no way to know what their endorsement is actually worth.
**Tags:** #worker-facing #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #descriptive-statistics #causal-inference #revenue-impact #survival-analysis #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to tell a host what their endorsement is actually worth — and whoever does that lets the person supplying the channel's only irreplaceable asset price it.

## The Problem
A host reads an endorsement for a product they have agreed to stand behind. Their audience trusts them, which is why the advertiser is paying a premium over a programmatic spot. The payment is calculated from downloads. The host cannot see how many listeners reached the read, cannot see whether their audience responded better than another show's, cannot see what comparable hosts are paid, and has no evidence of the effect their endorsement produced beyond a promotional code that captures a fraction of it. They negotiate against a network that has all of this and is not obliged to share it.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The data sits with platforms and networks whose commercial interest is in the host not having it, which is the same information asymmetry as in influencer marketing and persists for the same reason. Hosts are individually small and have no collective mechanism. The download metric is established and convenient for everyone paying on it. And nobody has built a product for which a host is the customer.

## What to Build
Give the host the evidence about their own audience. Show the listening curve for their own show, which is the fix note's subject and is the most basic fact about their audience that they cannot currently see. Report exposure rather than downloads, connecting to that niche, so the host knows how many people actually reached their read and can price on it — which will benefit hosts with engaged audiences and is the correct market outcome. Build rate benchmarks by show size, category and format from contributed and observed deal data, since hosts will share what they were paid in exchange for benchmarks and no such resource exists. Measure the endorsement's effect beyond promotional codes, using the advertiser's aggregate response where they will share it, which is the number that justifies the premium and nobody computes. Give the host negotiating evidence from their own performance history, which is their only real leverage. Track which advertisers pay on time and behave well, which hosts trade informally and would use. Support the host's own operations — briefs, approvals, invoicing, schedules — since they are running a business with no tooling. Show what a comparable show earns, which is the single most requested and most withheld fact. Keep the host as the customer, since a product funded by networks will serve networks. And measure whether hosts' realised rates improve, because that is the point.

## Target Customer
Podcast hosts and independent creators, creator business vendors, and the talent representatives negotiating on their behalf.

## Impact If Built
The data sits with the parties whose interest is in the host not having it, which is the same asymmetry as in influencer marketing. Showing a host their own listening curve and pricing on exposure rather than downloads moves the premium toward the hosts whose audiences actually listen.

# Everything the Enterprise Tier Requires an Implementation For

**Niche:** [[niches/contract-lifecycle-platforms/mid-market-clm/profile|Mid-Market CLM]]
**Industry:** [[industries/contract-lifecycle-platforms|Contract Lifecycle Platforms]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Extraction, clause classification and standard template content are all commodity now, and the mid-market cannot buy any of it because it is packaged as an enterprise implementation.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #bert #transfer-learning #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #cross-validation #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor here is fighting to deliver contract management value to a company with no legal operations function and no implementation budget — and whoever does that takes a market the category has never reached, because everyone else requires a project first.

## The Problem
Contract extraction works. Clause classification works. Standard agreement templates are freely published by bar associations, industry bodies and law firms. Renewal date tracking is trivial. Every component of a useful mid-market product exists and is cheap, and the market cannot buy it because every vendor packages it behind a configuration phase designed for customers with a legal operations team.

## What Already Exists
Contract extraction models and services; clause type classifiers with public benchmarks; published standard templates and model clauses from credible sources; language models that classify agreement types and summarise terms; and cloud storage APIs that make pointing at a shared drive trivial. All of it is available at costs compatible with mid-market pricing.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to zero configuration and a non-legal user. It requires: (1) an opinionated fixed schema rather than a configurable one, since configuration is the thing being removed and a dozen well-chosen fields cover what this customer needs; (2) defaults supplied as content, including templates and standard positions, with an explicit boundary between describing what is customary and advising what to do — which needs to be stated plainly in the product rather than buried in terms; (3) output aimed at a finance lead or office manager rather than a lawyer, which changes the language, the prioritisation and the definition of an alert; (4) confidence routing with a light review path, since there is no legal team to check extractions and the product must be honest about what it is unsure of; and (5) a price and onboarding that assume self-service throughout, including the initial estate load, which is the moment most likely to fail.

## Target Customer
CLM and e-signature vendors seeking a mid-market motion, accounting and finance platforms whose customers hold these estates, and the extraction vendors who could package directly.

## Impact If Solved
Every component is commodity and the packaging is the only obstacle, which is an unusually clean gap. The fixed schema and the supplied legal content are what remove the implementation, and the honest confidence routing is what makes the absence of a legal reviewer acceptable.

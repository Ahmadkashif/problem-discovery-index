# Mid-Market CLM

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/contract-lifecycle-platforms|Contract Lifecycle Platforms]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor here is fighting to deliver contract management value to a company with no legal operations function and no implementation budget — and whoever does that takes a market the category has never reached, because everyone else requires a project first.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$520M US addressable below the enterprise tier
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~17% of category revenue, almost entirely unserved
**Digital Adoption:** Very Low — contracts live in shared drives and email
**Target Buyer:** A general counsel of one, an office manager, a finance lead
**Automation Potential:** Very High — the value must be automatic because there is nobody to configure it

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
CLM adoption is concentrated in large enterprises with legal operations functions, and the reason is structural rather than a matter of price. The products deliver value after a configuration project: clause libraries populated, playbooks encoded, workflows designed, the repository migrated, integrations built. That project requires a legal operations team, and the overwhelming majority of companies with a meaningful contract estate do not have one — they have a general counsel with no staff, or no lawyer at all and a finance lead who handles contracts among six other responsibilities. These companies have the same problems: they do not know what they have agreed, they miss renewals, they sign counterparty paper without a real review, and they have no library. They cannot buy the solution because the solution requires a function they do not have. The contest is delivering the value with no configuration at all.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Enterprise CLM at enterprise prices and implementation timelines; lightweight repository tools; e-signature products extending into agreement management; and shared drives, which remain the honest incumbent. The gaps: every product's value curve starts after a setup phase, which is where mid-market adoption dies; templates and playbooks are expected as customer inputs rather than provided; pricing assumes a legal team; and nobody has built for the buyer who will evaluate this in an afternoon and abandon it in a week if it has not already helped.

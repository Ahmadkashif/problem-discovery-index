# The Shared Drive Is the Incumbent

**Niche:** [[niches/contract-lifecycle-platforms/mid-market-clm/profile|Mid-Market CLM]]
**Industry:** [[industries/contract-lifecycle-platforms|Contract Lifecycle Platforms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The product every mid-market CLM sale actually competes against is a folder structure, and no vendor has bothered to understand why it keeps winning.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #k-means-clustering #bert #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #quick-win #data-integration #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor here is fighting to deliver contract management value to a company with no legal operations function and no implementation budget — and whoever does that takes a market the category has never reached, because everyone else requires a project first.

## The Problem
A company keeps its contracts in a shared drive organised by counterparty, with filenames that mostly include a date. It does not answer questions, it does not track renewals and it loses amendments. It also requires no training, no migration, no licence, no administrator, and it works exactly the same way as every other folder in the business. Vendors treat it as an absence of a solution and lose to it repeatedly without ever asking what it provides that their product does not.

## Why It's Still Broken
The category's competitive analysis is against other CLM vendors, because that is who appears in enterprise evaluations, and the mid-market's actual incumbent is invisible in that frame. Migration is also treated as a precondition, which forces the customer to abandon the thing that works before receiving anything, and that ordering is the reason so many of these deals stall at the pilot. And nobody has built for the possibility that the drive stays.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Work with the drive instead of replacing it. Index it in place, with no migration and no change to how anyone files anything, so the customer gets answers without giving anything up — which removes the abandonment step entirely and is the single most important design decision. Add structure invisibly: recognise agreement families across files, identify types, extract dates and terms, and detect duplicates and near-duplicate versions, which is where these estates are genuinely broken. Deliver value through channels the customer already uses — a renewal reminder in their calendar and their email rather than in a portal they must remember to open. Improve the filing gently by suggesting names and locations consistent with what is already there, rather than imposing a taxonomy. Support the folder's real strengths, which are universal access and zero training, and be honest that a migration is only worth it once the product has already proven useful. And measure what the drive cannot do — the renewals missed, the versions in conflict, the agreements nobody can find — which is the argument, made with the customer's own data.

## Who Feels the Pain
Companies losing money to missed renewals in a filing system nobody defends; vendors losing mid-market deals to a folder; and the person who maintains the drive and knows it is inadequate and has no route to anything better.

## Impact If Fixed
Indexing in place removes the migration precondition that stalls most of these deals, and it lets the product prove itself before asking for anything. Measuring what the drive cannot do converts a vague sense of inadequacy into a specific and quantified case.

# First-Pass Redlining

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/contract-lifecycle-platforms|Contract Lifecycle Platforms]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in automated redlining is fighting to make the routine edits correctly without counsel and to know reliably when a provision is not routine — and whoever holds that boundary takes the legal function, because being wrong once on the wrong clause ends the deployment.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$430M US automated review and redlining
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~14% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Medium and rising quickly
**Target Buyer:** General counsel and legal operations, measured in counsel hours returned
**Automation Potential:** Very High for the routine share; the contest is the boundary

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The measurable prize here is counsel hours. A large share of contract review is the mechanical application of a known position to a recognised provision, and the technology to do that now exists. The contest is not whether the edits can be generated — they can, adequately — but whether the system knows which provisions it should not touch. A redlining tool that handles ninety percent of provisions correctly and silently mishandles the indemnity on a large deal has produced a net loss, because the lawyer must now review everything to find out whether it was one of the ninety. The product is therefore judged on its boundary behaviour rather than on its average accuracy, which is an unusual and demanding requirement and is why the incumbents' features are used tentatively. Whoever establishes that boundary credibly wins, because the alternative — a lawyer reading every provision to check — captures none of the value.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Playbook-based review features in the CLM suites, specialist contract review products, word processor add-ins, and general-purpose models used informally by counsel. The gaps: confidence is rarely exposed, so the lawyer cannot tell which suggestions to check; the interaction is generate-then-review, which preserves the reading burden the tool was meant to remove; deviation from policy is reported without the consequence explained, so counsel must reconstruct why it matters; the systems do not learn from the lawyer's corrections; and nobody measures the outcome that matters, which is hours returned rather than suggestions accepted.

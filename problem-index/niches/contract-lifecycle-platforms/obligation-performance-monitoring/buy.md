**Contested on:** Every serious competitor here is fighting to join contract terms to operational reality and say whether a commitment is actually being met — and whoever does that takes the commercial function, because knowing the obligation and knowing whether it is honoured are different products and only the first exists.

## The Problem
Compliance and audit solved this shape of problem: a requirement is expressed as a testable condition, joined to an authoritative data source, evaluated continuously, and evidenced automatically. Security compliance platforms do it for controls, and financial controls monitoring does it for accounting. Contract obligations — which are requirements with data sources and evidence needs — are handled with a task and a reminder.

## What Already Exists
Continuous control monitoring platforms and methodology; security compliance automation with evidence collection from connected systems; service level monitoring in every observability product; anomaly and change-point detection for trend warning; and integration frameworks for the connections required. The pattern is proven and the components are commodity.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to obligations expressed in prose and negotiated individually. It requires: (1) translating a contractual clause into a testable condition, which is the core work — "99.9 percent availability measured monthly excluding scheduled maintenance" must become a specific query against a specific source, and the exclusions are where the disputes live; (2) per-contract definitions rather than a standard control set, since compliance frameworks have uniform controls and every contract defines its terms slightly differently, which makes the mapping the bulk of the effort and the reason it must be assisted rather than manual; (3) measurement-basis disputes anticipated explicitly, because the counterparty's measurement and the company's frequently differ and the monitoring should compute both where the contract is ambiguous; (4) financial consequence attached, since credits, penalties and termination rights are what make a breach matter and control monitoring has no analogue for them; and (5) evidence retained in a form usable in a dispute, which is a higher standard than internal assurance.

## Target Customer
CLM vendors, compliance automation platforms with an adjacent market, observability vendors whose data answers the service level question, and commercial operations functions.

## Impact If Solved
An established monitoring discipline maps onto obligations with the clause-to-condition translation as the only genuinely new work. Anticipating measurement-basis disputes and attaching financial consequence are what make the output commercially useful rather than merely accurate.

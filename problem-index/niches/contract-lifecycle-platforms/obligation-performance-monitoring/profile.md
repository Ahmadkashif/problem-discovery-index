# Obligation Performance Monitoring

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/contract-lifecycle-platforms|Contract Lifecycle Platforms]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor here is fighting to join contract terms to operational reality and say whether a commitment is actually being met — and whoever does that takes the commercial function, because knowing the obligation and knowing whether it is honoured are different products and only the first exists.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$230M US obligation and performance monitoring
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~8% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Very Low — obligations are extracted and then not monitored
**Target Buyer:** Commercial, delivery and finance functions rather than legal
**Automation Potential:** Very High — the operational data exists in adjacent systems

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Extracting an obligation and monitoring its performance are different problems, and the category has attempted only the first. A contract commits the company to a service level, a quarterly report, an insurance certificate, a volume floor, a price review, a security attestation, a data deletion within thirty days of termination. Knowing these exist is useful; knowing whether they are being met requires joining them to the systems where the answer lives — the monitoring platform, the delivery records, the billing system, the security programme. That join is what nobody has built, which is why obligation registers are populated once during implementation and then decay into a list nobody opens. The buyer is also different: legal wants to know what was promised, and commercial, delivery and finance want to know whether it is being honoured, which is a different conversation and a different budget.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Obligation registers inside CLM products, task and reminder assignment, and manual attestation. The gaps: obligations are tracked as tasks with due dates rather than as conditions to be verified, so the register records that somebody ticked a box; nothing connects to the operational systems that hold the evidence; the counterparty's obligations to the company are monitored even less than the company's own, which is where the recoverable money usually is; and breach is discovered when the counterparty raises it, which is the worst possible moment.

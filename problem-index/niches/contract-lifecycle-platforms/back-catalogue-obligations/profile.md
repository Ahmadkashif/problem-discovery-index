# Back Catalogue Obligations

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/contract-lifecycle-platforms|Contract Lifecycle Platforms]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to make the executed back catalogue answerable — what have we promised, to whom, and where — and whoever does that takes the account, because it is the question the category was bought to answer and the one every implementation declares out of scope.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$780M US attributable to legacy contract structuring and obligation discovery
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~26% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Very Low — the back catalogue remains a folder of PDFs at most companies
**Target Buyer:** General counsel, legal operations and enterprise risk
**Automation Potential:** Very High — extraction quality is now adequate and the corpus is static

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A company's commitments are not in its new contracts. They are in the several thousand agreements executed over the past decade — the service levels promised to a customer in 2019, the exclusivity granted in an early partnership, the most-favoured-nation clause somebody agreed to close a quarter, the data commitments made before the current policy existed, the indemnity cap that was negotiated away. When a question arises — can we change this pricing, does this customer have a right to audit us, are we exposed on this — the answer requires a lawyer to read. CLM systems were sold precisely to end this and in practice manage new contracts forward while the back catalogue, where the liability lives, stays a folder. The implementation failure rate in this category is notorious and this is why: the value was always downstream of structuring the legacy estate, and structuring it was scoped out of every project because it was hard, unglamorous and had no obvious owner.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Extraction and repository products, CLM systems with migration tooling, managed services that read contracts manually, and legal process outsourcers. The gaps: migration is priced and scoped as a project with a deadline rather than as a continuous process, which is why it is cut; extraction accuracy is reported in aggregate when the operationally relevant question is which extractions are reliable; nobody prioritises the estate, so a company that can afford to structure a fifth of its contracts structures the wrong fifth; and amendment chains are handled poorly everywhere, so even a structured agreement frequently does not reflect the operative terms.

# Business-Side Requesters

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/contract-lifecycle-platforms|Contract Lifecycle Platforms]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor that takes this seriously is fighting to let the person who needs a contract get one — or know exactly when they will — without asking a lawyer, and whoever does that takes the deployment, because the requester decides whether a CLM system is used or routed around.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$170M US attributable to self-service contracting and requester experience
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~6% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Low — the requester's experience is a form and silence
**Target Buyer:** Legal operations buys it; sales, procurement and operations use it
**Automation Potential:** Very High — most requests are standard and could be self-served

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
CLM systems are designed for the legal function and used by everybody else. A salesperson who needs an order form, a procurement manager who needs a supplier agreement signed, a marketing lead who needs a sponsorship contract — these are the people who generate the volume, and their experience is a form with fields they do not understand, followed by silence of unpredictable duration, followed by a document with tracked changes they cannot evaluate. The predictable consequence is that they route around the system: they use an old contract from a shared drive, they ask a lawyer they know directly, or they proceed without one. Every governance problem in the category downstream of that — unapproved terms, invisible agreements, no repository record — originates in a requester experience nobody designed. The contest is self-service for the requester, which is simultaneously the highest-leverage adoption lever and the least-attended surface in the category.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Intake forms, request portals, status fields, and in the more mature products a self-service path for simple agreement types. The gaps: self-service is limited to a narrow set and requires the requester to know which applies; there is no estimate of when a contract will be ready, which is the requester's only real question; redlines are returned with no explanation of what changed or why it matters commercially; the requester cannot see where their request is; and nothing measures how often people bypass the system, which is the metric that would reveal the size of the problem.

# Nobody Counts the People Who Went Around

**Niche:** [[niches/contract-lifecycle-platforms/business-side-requesters/profile|Business-Side Requesters]]
**Industry:** [[industries/contract-lifecycle-platforms|Contract Lifecycle Platforms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A contract that never entered the CLM system leaves no trace in it, so the metric that would reveal the system's real adoption is structurally invisible.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #logistic-regression #hypothesis-testing #confidence-intervals #evaluation-metrics #data-integration #quick-win #compliance
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor that takes this seriously is fighting to let the person who needs a contract get one — or know exactly when they will — without asking a lawyer, and whoever does that takes the deployment, because the requester decides whether a CLM system is used or routed around.

## The Problem
The CLM dashboard reports four hundred contracts processed this quarter, cycle times improving, and healthy adoption. Elsewhere in the company, sales executed sixty order forms from an old template, a regional office signed a dozen supplier agreements directly, and a marketing team used a sponsorship contract from two years ago with the dates changed. None of those appear anywhere in the system, so the reported adoption is a percentage of a denominator nobody has measured. Legal believes it has visibility. It has visibility of the contracts that came to it.

## Why It's Still Broken
The system can only count what it processes, and nobody has gone looking for the rest. The evidence exists in other systems — signature platforms, accounts payable, closed opportunities, the shared drives — and joining it requires an initiative nobody owns. There is also a mild disincentive: the number would show that a well-reported deployment is covering a fraction of the contracting activity, which is uncomfortable for whoever sponsored it.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Measure the denominator from outside the system. Reconcile against the signature platform, which holds every executed agreement including the ones that bypassed legal, and is usually the single most revealing comparison available. Reconcile against finance, since a supplier being paid implies an agreement and an agreement not in the repository is a gap with a name attached. Reconcile against closed opportunities, since a closed deal implies a contract. Scan the shared drives for executed agreements, which the mid-market niche describes and which applies here too. Report coverage by business unit and agreement type, which localises the problem — bypass is almost never uniform and is usually concentrated in one or two teams with a specific reason. Ask those teams why rather than enforcing, since the reason is nearly always that the sanctioned path was too slow or did not fit, and that is a product finding rather than a compliance one. And track coverage over time as the primary adoption metric, replacing volume processed, because volume rewards a system that handles more of what already came to it.

## Who Feels the Pain
Legal functions with a false picture of their own coverage; the business units bypassing a process that does not work for them; and companies carrying obligations in agreements legal has never seen.

## Impact If Fixed
Reconciling against the signature platform and finance is ordinary data work and typically reveals a coverage figure far below the reported adoption. Localising bypass by team turns an abstract compliance concern into a specific and usually fixable product problem.

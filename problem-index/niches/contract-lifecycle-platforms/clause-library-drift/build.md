# The Library Describes a Position Nobody Takes

**Niche:** [[niches/contract-lifecycle-platforms/clause-library-drift/profile|Clause Library Drift]]
**Industry:** [[industries/contract-lifecycle-platforms|Contract Lifecycle Platforms]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** The playbook says the company requires a mutual liability cap, the company has not obtained one in fourteen months, and every tool that checks against the playbook reports a deviation nobody intends to act on.
**Tags:** #bert #word-embeddings #descriptive-statistics #change-point-detection #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #compliance #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor here is fighting to keep the clause library and the playbook aligned with what the company has actually been agreeing to — and whoever closes that gap takes legal operations, because a library that describes a fiction makes every feature built on it wrong.

## The Problem
The playbook, written three years ago by a general counsel who has since left, states that the company requires a mutual limitation of liability and will not accept an indemnity without a cap. In practice, since entering a new segment eighteen months ago, the company has accepted a one-way cap in most agreements, because that is the market norm there and holding the position loses deals. Every automated review flags this as a deviation. Counsel overrides it every time. New joiners are trained on the playbook and are confused within a fortnight. The executed agreements record the real position unambiguously and nothing compares the two.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Library maintenance is nobody's standing responsibility — there is a change approval process but no currency owner — so the library is updated when a problem is noticed rather than when practice moves. Comparing stated positions against executed outcomes requires normalising both into comparable form, which is the same work the benchmarking sub-niche needs and which nobody has done. And the drift is comfortable: the playbook states the aspiration and everybody knows the reality, which functions adequately until something is automated against it, at which point the fiction becomes operational.

## What to Build
Continuous conformance between the library and the executed estate. Normalise each library position into a comparable form, and extract the corresponding position from every executed agreement — which is the same normalisation the negotiation evidence needs and should be built once. Compare, and report per provision: this is what the playbook says, this is what we agreed in the last hundred and twelve contracts, and this is the gap. Detect the point at which practice moved, using change-point methods over the executed series, since knowing that the position shifted in the second quarter of last year usually explains it immediately. Segment the comparison, because the position frequently differs legitimately by market, deal size or counterparty type and a single global position was the error rather than the drift. Propose the update explicitly for approval, since the correct response to drift is sometimes to change the playbook and sometimes to enforce it, and that is a legal judgement the product should inform rather than make. And report the aggregate: this proportion of the playbook is aligned with practice, which is a currency measure no legal function has and which would justify making someone responsible for it.

## Target Customer
Legal operations and general counsel at companies with an established playbook, and the CLM vendors whose review and deviation features are only as good as the library beneath them.

## Impact If Built
Every automated capability in the category inherits the library's errors, which makes currency a foundational property rather than a housekeeping one. The executed estate is a complete and immediate record of the true position, and comparing against it is mechanical once the normalisation exists.

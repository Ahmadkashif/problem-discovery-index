# Legal Operations Triage

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/contract-lifecycle-platforms|Contract Lifecycle Platforms]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor here is fighting to tell a routine agreement from a genuinely dangerous one at the moment it arrives — and whoever does that takes legal operations, because everything downstream depends on the first thirty seconds being right.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$190M US attributable to intake, triage and routing
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~6% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Low — intake is a queue and a person reading
**Target Buyer:** Legal operations leadership; the beneficiary is counsel
**Automation Potential:** Very High — risk signals are present in the document and the request

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Legal operations is the front door, and the front door is a queue where a routine mutual non-disclosure agreement and an agreement with an uncapped indemnity and a five-year exclusivity arrive looking identical: both are a request with a document attached. Somebody must decide what each one is, how urgent it is, who should handle it and whether it needs senior attention. That decision is made by reading, at a rate of a few minutes each, by a person who is not usually a lawyer, under time pressure. Getting it wrong in one direction wastes senior counsel's time on an NDA; getting it wrong in the other direction sends a materially dangerous agreement to a junior reviewer with a two-day queue. This is a distinct contest because it is a classification problem at the front of the process whose accuracy determines the value of everything after it, and because it is the specific work that makes legal operations a routing desk rather than a function.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Intake forms, request queues, routing rules based on requester or contract type, and service-level dashboards. The gaps: routing rules use declared attributes — the requester's own selection of contract type — which is frequently wrong and is the input the whole chain depends on; nothing reads the attached document to assess risk before assignment; urgency is whatever the requester claimed, which is always high; complexity and expected effort are not estimated, so workload balancing is impossible; and the triage decision is never evaluated afterwards, so nobody knows how often it was wrong.

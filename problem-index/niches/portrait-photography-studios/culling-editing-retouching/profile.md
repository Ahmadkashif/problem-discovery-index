# Culling, Editing & Retouching

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/portrait-photography-studios|Portrait Photography Studios]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to reproduce a photographer's own culling and editing choices on a 2,000–5,000-frame wedding closely enough that the photographer stops checking every frame, and whoever earns that trust takes the subscription.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$600M in US spend on AI culling and editing software, outsourced editing services and in-house editor time at session studios (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~7% (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** High — entirely on screen, with AI tools now mainstream
**Target Buyer:** Owner-photographer; the post-production lead at an associate studio
**Automation Potential:** Very high for selection and global edits; medium for retouching judgement

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Post-production is the most mechanical part of session photography and the most hated. A wedding produces 1,500–5,000 raw frames and 400–600 delivered images after three to five hours of culling and editing per hour of coverage. The tools have arrived: Aftershoot sells culling, editing and retouching for $10–45 a month, Imagen edits at five cents a photo with culling as an add-on, and outsourced editing houses offer per-image rates. The contest has moved from "can software cull" to "can it cull and edit like me", because a photographer's look is their brand and a missed moment or an off-colour skin tone in a delivered gallery is their reputation.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Lightroom remains the editing base; Aftershoot, Imagen, Narrative Select and Evoto add AI culling, editing profiles and retouching; outsourced editors handle overflow. Gaps: calibrated confidence so the photographer reviews only uncertain frames; studio styles shared across associate shooters; retouching that follows each client's stated preferences.

# Preschool & Childcare Centre Photography

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/portrait-photography-studios|Portrait Photography Studios]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to photograph infants, toddlers and preschoolers in a centre with no office staff and no student information system, and whoever makes picture day cost the centre director nothing — no roster cleanup, no flyers, no package distribution — keeps the centre.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$300M in US preschool and childcare picture-day receipts (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~3.5% (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low-Medium — centres run on childcare apps, but picture day still runs on paper flyers, sign-up sheets in the lobby and packages left in cubbies
**Target Buyer:** Centre director or owner; the volume or preschool-specialist studio owner
**Automation Potential:** Medium — scheduling, rosters and ordering automate; photographing a ten-month-old does not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Preschool photography is the segment volume tools were not designed for. Centres are small (often 40–150 children, estimate), have no student information system to export a roster from, and their director is also covering classrooms. The subjects range from infants who cannot sit to four-year-olds who will not, so the 25-second school rhythm breaks: sessions are slower, need props and patience, and parents care intensely about expression. Large studios run preschool lines (Lifetouch listed preschool photography among its businesses at its 2018 sale, and posts seasonal preschool photographer roles), but small centres are low-revenue stops and are served last. Centres themselves are covered under [[industries/childcare-centers|Childcare Centers]].

## Current Tools & Gaps
Centres manage enrolment and parent messaging in childcare apps such as Brightwheel and Procare; studios use school-oriented volume platforms. Gaps: no link between the two; rosters typed from the centre's lists; ordering flyers sent home in backpacks; packages sorted into cubbies by staff.

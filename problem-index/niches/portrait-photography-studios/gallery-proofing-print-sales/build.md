# Gallery Sales Sequences Learned From Outcomes

**Niche:** [[niches/portrait-photography-studios/gallery-proofing-print-sales/profile|Online Proofing, Gallery Sales & Print Fulfilment]]
**Industry:** [[industries/portrait-photography-studios|Portrait Photography Studios]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Every gallery gets the same reminder schedule and the same product suggestions, though the platform can see which clients, images and offers actually lead to orders.
**Tags:** #gradient-boosting #survival-analysis #cnns #evaluation-metrics #revenue-impact #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn a delivered session gallery into print, album and wall-art orders before the client's attention fades, without the photographer chasing, and whoever converts the most galleries into lab orders takes the studio's subscription.

## The Problem
A newborn gallery and a wedding gallery behave differently; a client who favourited forty images in the first hour is not the same as one who downloaded everything and left. Default sequences treat them alike.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Platforms offer sequence templates and leave tuning to the photographer, who has neither the data volume nor the time. Platform-level learning is possible but raises questions about using one studio's client data to tune another's sales.

## What to Build
Per-gallery timing and offers learned from the studio's own history plus opt-in pooled models: when to send the next reminder, which images to feature in a wall-art preview (ranked by favourites, views and image content), which product to suggest, and when an expiry extension is worth offering. It reports conversion by session type so the photographer sees where print revenue comes from.

## Target Customer
Session photographers relying on print and album revenue; gallery platforms as the delivery channel.

## Impact If Built
More galleries converted without more chasing, and print revenue that does not depend on the photographer's memory.

# Outside Portraits That Miss the Yearbook Spec

**Niche:** [[niches/portrait-photography-studios/high-school-senior-portraits/profile|High School Senior Portraits]]
**Industry:** [[industries/portrait-photography-studios|Portrait Photography Studios]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Seniors who used an independent photographer send a horizontal, low-resolution or heavily filtered image the week of the deadline, and the yearbook adviser has to chase every one.
**Tags:** #object-detection #cnns #evaluation-metrics #automation #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to book seniors' portrait sessions ahead of the independent photographers who market to them directly, before the yearbook's fall deadline, and whoever books the most seniors while delivering yearbook-compliant images on time keeps the high school.

## The Problem
Schools allow outside photographers if the image meets the adviser's rules: head-and-shoulders, vertical, plain background, high-resolution JPEG, sometimes sent by the photographer directly and with proof of rights. Submissions arrive by email in every format, many non-compliant, and many in the last week.

## Why It's Still Broken
The adviser is a teacher with a yearbook class, not a production department; email has no validation; and the contract studio has no incentive to help competitors' images through.

## What a Fix Looks Like
An upload portal that checks orientation, resolution, crop and head size on submission, tells the family immediately what to fix, records rights confirmation, and feeds compliant images into the same yearbook export as the contract studio's. The contract studio offers it as a service to the school.

## Who Feels the Pain
Yearbook advisers in deadline week; seniors whose photo is replaced by an ID picture; the contract studio blamed for missing pages.

## Impact If Fixed
Deadline week stops being a chase, and the contract studio earns credit for a complete senior section.

# Photographer CRMs Adapted to Date-Bound Selling

**Niche:** [[niches/portrait-photography-studios/wedding-photography/profile|Wedding Photography]]
**Industry:** [[industries/portrait-photography-studios|Portrait Photography Studios]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Creative-business CRMs treat every lead alike; a wedding photographer's leads compete for a fixed set of Saturdays.
**Tags:** #gradient-boosting #logistic-regression #workflow-orchestration #revenue-impact #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to sell a fixed calendar of peak-season Saturdays — each date sold once and worthless once it passes — to the right couple at the right price a year or more ahead, and whoever fills the prime dates at full price earliest takes the season.

## The Problem
Two couples inquire about the same June Saturday; one is a ~$6,000 full-day wedding three hours away, the other a ~$2,500 elopement nearby (illustrative figures). A CRM shows two leads. The photographer needs to know which to chase, what to quote for travel, and whether to hold the date for a retainer.

## What Already Exists
HoneyBook, Dubsado and Studio Ninja provide pipelines, templates, contracts, invoices and scheduling for creative businesses.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs: date-level inventory with peak and off-peak pricing; lead scoring by likelihood to book and value, learned from the studio's own history; travel and second-shooter costs computed into quotes; date holds that expire unless a retainer arrives; and marketplace lead sources tracked to bookings so the studio knows which listings pay.

## Target Customer
Solo and associate wedding studios on an existing CRM.

## Impact If Solved
Fewer prime dates sold cheaply or left empty, and marketing spend moved to the sources that actually book.

# The Date Sold Without the Edit Week Behind It

**Niche:** [[niches/portrait-photography-studios/wedding-photography/profile|Wedding Photography]]
**Industry:** [[industries/portrait-photography-studios|Portrait Photography Studios]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Every Saturday a photographer sells also sells 24–40 hours of editing and a contracted delivery date, and nobody checks the second calendar before signing the first.
**Tags:** #time-series-forecasting #optimization-fundamentals #evaluation-metrics #workflow-orchestration #worker-facing #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to sell a fixed calendar of peak-season Saturdays — each date sold once and worthless once it passes — to the right couple at the right price a year or more ahead, and whoever fills the prime dates at full price earliest takes the season.

## The Problem
Contracts promise delivery in a set window, commonly six to eight weeks. The booking calendar shows Saturdays; it does not show that four weddings in five weeks in September, plus engagement sessions and albums, add up to more editing hours than exist before the contracted dates. The breach shows up in October as late galleries, apologetic emails and reviews that mention the wait, which are exactly what the next couple reads before choosing.

## Why It's Still Broken
Post-production hours are invisible at the moment of sale: they depend on coverage hours, shot counts, retouching requests and whether an editor or AI tool is available, none of which the CRM records against the date. Turning down a June Saturday feels like losing money, so photographers book first and work it out later.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A second calendar beside the first: every booked date projects its editing load and delivery deadline from the photographer's own history (hours per coverage hour, outsourcing used, album turnaround), and a new booking shows whether it pushes any contracted delivery past due. The photographer can then price the date up, schedule outsourced editing, or adjust the delivery terms before signing.

## Who Feels the Pain
Owner-photographers in peak season; couples waiting past the promised date; associates and outsourced editors handed a backlog at the last minute.

## Impact If Fixed
Delivery promises kept through peak season, fewer reviews that mention delays, and dates priced for the work they actually create.

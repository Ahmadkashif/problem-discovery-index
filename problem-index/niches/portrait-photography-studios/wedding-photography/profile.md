# Wedding Photography

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/portrait-photography-studios|Portrait Photography Studios]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to sell a fixed calendar of peak-season Saturdays — each date sold once and worthless once it passes — to the right couple at the right price a year or more ahead, and whoever fills the prime dates at full price earliest takes the season.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$3.5B in US wedding photography receipts (estimate: ~2.0M weddings × ~65% hiring a paid photographer × ~$2,800 median)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~40% (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium-High — inquiries, contracts, galleries and editing are digital; the sale is still won in conversation
**Target Buyer:** Owner-photographer of a solo or small associate studio
**Automation Potential:** High for pricing, inquiry handling, contracts and post-production; none for the day itself

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
About 2.0 million US weddings took place in 2025 (The Wedding Report estimate), and couples in The Knot's 2026 study paid an average of $3,000 for a photographer (median $2,800, upper quartile $4,700). What sets wedding work apart from other session photography is perishable inventory. A family or newborn photographer can add a weekday session whenever demand appears; a wedding photographer sells a fixed set of peak-season Saturdays, each once, a year or more ahead, and an empty Saturday in June is revenue that never comes back. Every booking decision is therefore a yield decision: hold the date for a larger wedding that may inquire later, or take the smaller one now; price the prime dates up and the off-season down; and commit, with each date sold, to 24–40 hours of post-production and a contracted delivery window. Most studios are one or two photographers who are shooting on exactly the days inquiries peak. Wedding videography is a sibling business covered under [[industries/video-production-smb|SMB Video Production]], and wedding planning under [[industries/event-planning|Event Planning]].

## Current Tools & Gaps
HoneyBook, Dubsado and Studio Ninja manage inquiries, contracts and invoices; Pixieset, ShootProof and Pic-Time deliver galleries; marketplaces sell leads. Gaps: date-level pricing and holds, which live in the photographer's head; replies that quote the right price for the date asked; and booking that ignores the editing capacity each date consumes.

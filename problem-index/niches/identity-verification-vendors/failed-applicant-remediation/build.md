# A Route for the Person Who Failed

**Niche:** [[niches/identity-verification-vendors/failed-applicant-remediation/profile|Failed-Applicant Remediation]]
**Industry:** [[industries/identity-verification-vendors|Identity Verification Vendors]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** The product ends at the rejection, and for the person rejected that is where the problem begins.
**Tags:** #workflow-orchestration #compliance #evaluation-metrics #worker-facing #automation #data-integration #confidence-intervals #large-language-models
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to give a person who could not be verified a documented route to prove who they are — and whoever builds that route stops the category's silent failure from being permanent.

## The Problem
Automated verification will always fail for some genuine people: an unusual document, a poor capture, a thin file, a recent move, a name that does not transliterate cleanly. That is a normal property of any automated decision. What is not normal is that there is frequently no second path at all — no way to submit other evidence, no human to speak to, no appeal, and no explanation. A technical limitation becomes a permanent exclusion from a bank account, and the product treats it as a completed transaction.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The vendor's customer is the institution and the rejected applicant is not a party to the relationship, so nobody owns their outcome — a product whose user and whose subject are different people optimises for the user. Manual paths cost money per case. Rejections produce no measurable loss to anyone in the transaction. And building an appeal implies the decision might be wrong, which nobody wants to concede.

## What to Build
Build the second path deliberately. Offer an alternative evidence route — a different document, a chip read, a utility or employment record, a video session — which is the core and resolves most genuine failures. Tell the person what failed in terms they can act on, since a specific problem is fixable and a generic rejection is not. Provide a documented appeal with a defined response time, because a decision that cannot be contested is not a decision anyone should accept. Hand off to the institution with the evidence rather than ending the flow, as the institution can accept risk the vendor cannot and is frequently willing to. Preserve progress so a second attempt is not a restart, which is where much of the abandonment happens. Route repeated failures to a human rather than looping, since the loop is what makes the exclusion permanent. Track abandonment and appeal outcomes, which is the measurement that makes the whole problem visible. Make the path accessible to people without the newest device or the strongest connection, because they are disproportionately the ones here. Record the reason for each failure in a form the institution can audit, since they carry the regulatory obligation. And measure how many rejected applicants were eventually verified, which is the direct estimate of the false reject rate nobody has.

## Target Customer
Product and support leadership, institutions accountable for access, applicants excluded by a technical failure, and regulators examining the gate to financial services.

## Impact If Built
A product whose user and whose subject are different people optimises for the user, so nobody owns the rejected applicant's outcome. An alternative evidence route both resolves genuine failures and produces the first direct estimate of the false reject rate.

## The Problem
Forensic document examination is a real profession: structured methodology, reference collections of genuine documents, known forgery libraries, proficiency testing, and standards for expressing conclusions with a stated level of confidence. Border and law enforcement examiners are trained and tested. Commercial verification reviewers do comparable work at vastly higher volume, with a fraction of the training and no proficiency measurement at all.

## What Already Exists
Document examination methodology; genuine and fraudulent reference collections; security feature taxonomies; proficiency testing programmes; and conclusion scales expressing confidence.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a minute per case on a photograph. It requires: (1) examination of a digital image rather than a physical document, which removes tactile, ultraviolet and infrared evidence the discipline depends on — this is the fundamental constraint and determines what conclusions are even possible; (2) sixty seconds rather than an hour, so reference material must be delivered rather than consulted; (3) reviewers trained in weeks rather than years, meaning the system must supply the expertise; (4) proficiency testing at scale, which is achievable with seeded cases and is entirely absent; and (5) graded conclusions rather than binary decisions, which the discipline uses and the product does not support.

## Target Customer
Review operations leadership, reviewers, institutions relying on the decisions, and document examination training and tooling providers.

## Impact If Solved
A trained discipline with proficiency testing exists and commercial review has neither. Seeded proficiency testing and graded conclusions are both achievable at scale and would make reviewer quality measurable for the first time.

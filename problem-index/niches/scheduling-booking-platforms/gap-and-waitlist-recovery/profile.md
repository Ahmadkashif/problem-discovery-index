# Gap & Waitlist Recovery

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/scheduling-booking-platforms|Scheduling & Booking Platforms]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor here is fighting to refill a slot in the hours after it is released, to the right customer, without spamming everyone — and whoever does that takes the operator, because the recovered slot is pure margin on capacity already paid for.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$210M US attributable to capacity recovery and waitlist management
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~14% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Low — a cancelled slot is usually left open
**Target Buyer:** Any operator whose capacity is perishable and whose costs are fixed
**Automation Potential:** Very High — the slot, the candidates and the response history are all recorded

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A cancellation twenty hours before an appointment is a different problem from a no-show: the slot is knowably empty and there is time to sell it again. In most businesses it is simply left open, or the operator messages a couple of people they can think of. Meanwhile the platform knows exactly who might take it: the person who asked for that time and could not get it, the client whose usual interval has elapsed, the one who has accepted a short-notice offer twice before, the one who lives nearby and books at short notice habitually. Refilling is a ranking and offer problem with a clean outcome, and the economics are unusually attractive because the practitioner and premises are already paid for, so a recovered slot is close to pure margin. The category treats the waitlist as a list somebody reads rather than as a recovery mechanism.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Waitlists in some products, manual messaging, and a cancellation notification to the operator. The gaps: waitlists are unranked and require the operator to work through them; offers go to everyone at once, which produces a race, an awkward apology to the losers, and waitlist fatigue that degrades future response; nothing predicts who will accept, so the offer is untargeted; the time window is not used, so an offer for tomorrow's ten o'clock goes out at five in the afternoon when nobody is looking at their phone; and gaps arising from other causes — an over-run resolved, a shortened appointment, a no-show already identifiable — are never treated as recoverable at all.

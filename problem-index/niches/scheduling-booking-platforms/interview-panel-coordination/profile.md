# Interview Panel Coordination

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/scheduling-booking-platforms|Scheduling & Booking Platforms]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor here is fighting to place a multi-person interview panel across busy calendars in one pass, with the sequence and eligibility constraints satisfied — and whoever does that takes the talent acquisition account, because panel scheduling is the coordinator's entire week.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$220M US interview scheduling and coordination
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~15% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — tooling exists and coordinators still broker most panels by hand
**Target Buyer:** Talent acquisition operations and recruiting leadership
**Automation Potential:** Very High — a well-formed constraint problem with structured inputs

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A final-stage interview is five people, in sequence, on one day, each for a defined duration, where each interviewer must be eligible for their assigned competency, at least one must be senior enough to make a recommendation, none may have a prior relationship with the candidate, some must be in the candidate's time zone, and the whole thing must happen within the window before the candidate accepts another offer. It is a constraint satisfaction problem of entirely ordinary difficulty and it is solved by a coordinator sending messages, holding provisional time, and rebuilding the plan each time an interviewer declines. The recruiting cost of getting it wrong is concrete and well understood: candidates withdraw during the scheduling delay, and the delay is usually days rather than hours. The contest is placing the panel in one pass, and repairing it in one pass when something moves.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Interview scheduling modules inside applicant tracking systems, specialist interview coordination products, calendar integration, and interviewer pool management. The gaps: most tools present overlapping availability and leave the selection to a person, which is the hard part; eligibility and competency constraints are maintained as spreadsheets outside the tool; interviewer load and fatigue are not modelled, so the same willing people are booked repeatedly until they stop agreeing; the candidate's own availability and time zone are gathered by email; and rescheduling restarts the whole negotiation, which is where most of the coordinator's time actually goes.

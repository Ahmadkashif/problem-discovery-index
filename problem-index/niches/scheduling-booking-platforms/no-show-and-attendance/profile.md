# No-Show & Attendance

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/scheduling-booking-platforms|Scheduling & Booking Platforms]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to predict which bookings will not be honoured and intervene on the ones worth intervening on — and whoever raises attendance takes the account, because the slot cannot be sold twice and the operator counts the loss every week.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$420M US attributable to attendance, deposits and booking assurance
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~28% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Universal in form — everyone sends a reminder — and untested in substance
**Target Buyer:** Any business that sells time: clinics, salons, studios, trades, professional services
**Automation Potential:** Very High — the booking, the history and the outcome are all recorded

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
For a business that sells time, a no-show is the one loss that cannot be recovered. The inventory is perishable at a fixed moment: the slot passes, the practitioner was idle or paid anyway, and the revenue does not come back later. Rates reach double digits in several sectors and the entire industry's response is a reminder sent to everybody at the same interval before the appointment, which was chosen by convention rather than by evidence and has never been tested against an alternative by any vendor. Meanwhile the platform holds exactly what a prediction needs: how far ahead the booking was made, whether this customer has attended before, what service, what time of day, what day of the week, what channel they booked through, whether they have rescheduled once already, whether they paid anything. Attendance is among the most predictable consequential behaviours in small business and is treated as weather.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Reminder emails and text messages on a configurable schedule, deposits and card-on-file at booking, cancellation policies, and per-customer no-show counters in some products. The gaps: nothing is predicted, so every intervention is applied uniformly and the expensive ones are applied to everybody or to nobody; deposits are the strongest lever available and are used as an all-or-nothing setting because operators fear the booking friction; the cost of a no-show varies enormously by slot and nothing accounts for that; and the outcome of every intervention is recorded and never analysed, so no operator or vendor learns anything from a decade of data.

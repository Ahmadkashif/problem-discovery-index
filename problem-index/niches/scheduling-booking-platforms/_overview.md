## Why These Niches

The category solved putting an appointment on a calendar and never addressed whether the appointment happens, which makes attendance the largest contested surface and an almost completely unworked one. The universal answer — one reminder, twenty-four hours before, to everyone — has never been tested by anybody against anything, which is remarkable for a feature deployed in every product in the category.

Team and enterprise scheduling **failed the filter as one niche**. Routing an inbound lead to the right seller in seconds is a speed-and-correctness contest bought by revenue operations, and coordinating a five-person interview panel across calendars is a constraint-satisfaction contest bought by talent acquisition. Different buyers, different incumbents, different definitions of winning. Decomposed below.

The underdigitised areas are both places where reality exceeds the product. A real business has staff with different skills, rooms and equipment that are also constrained, service-specific buffers and travel time — and expressing that defeats the configuration interface, so businesses simplify their availability and sell less than they could. And a large share of bookings never touch any platform at all, arriving by phone, at a counter or in a direct message, which makes the platform's record of the day incomplete in exactly the way that breaks everything built on it.

The two underserved constituencies are the solo practitioner, whose scheduling admin is the only unpaid hour in a day measured in booked time, and the coordinator who rebuilds the whole day by hand every time one thing moves.

The automation niches are the category's two standing omissions: a reminder feature nobody has ever measured, and a cancelled slot nobody tries to refill.

## Filter Notes

Seven of the eight level-1 niches are terminal. **Team & Enterprise Scheduling** is not: it names a customer size rather than a contest, and the two contests inside it are unrelated. Revenue meeting routing is won by whoever gets an inbound prospect onto the right seller's calendar in the seconds after they express interest, correctly, against territory and account rules — a routing and latency problem sold to revenue operations. Interview panel coordination is won by whoever can place a multi-person panel across several busy calendars in one pass without a coordinator brokering it — a constraint-satisfaction problem sold to talent acquisition. Decomposed into two contested sub-niches.

Two candidates were rejected. *Payments and deposits at booking* was folded into No-Show & Attendance, because a deposit is one of the interventions that contest is fought with rather than a contest of its own. *Calendar integration* was rejected outright: it is mature, commoditised and genuinely finished, and nobody is competing on it.

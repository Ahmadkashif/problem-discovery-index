# Revenue Meeting Routing

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/scheduling-booking-platforms|Scheduling & Booking Platforms]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor here is fighting to get an inbound prospect onto the right seller's calendar in the seconds after they raise their hand — and whoever holds both the correctness and the latency takes the revenue operations account, because both halves are required and each vendor currently has one.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$290M US inbound routing and revenue scheduling
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~19% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Medium-High — routing exists and is mostly static rules
**Target Buyer:** Revenue operations and demand generation
**Automation Potential:** Very High — ownership, territory and availability are all structured data

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A prospect fills in a form on a pricing page. The value of that moment decays measurably by the minute, and the research on inbound response time is consistent and well known. What has to happen is a chain: identify the company from an email domain, look up whether it is an existing account and who owns it, check territory and segment rules, confirm the right seller is available, and put a meeting on the calendar — all before the person navigates away. Routing products do the identification and ownership half and hand off to a scheduling link. Scheduling products do the calendar half and accept whatever routing decision arrives. Nobody holds the chain, which is why the common failure modes persist: the prospect is routed to a round-robin because ownership lookup failed, or gets a link and books four days out, or is assigned to someone whose calendar is full. The contest is the whole chain, executed in seconds, correctly.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Lead routing products with rules engines, scheduling platforms with team pages and round-robin, customer relationship systems holding ownership and territory, and enrichment vendors supplying firmographic data. The gaps: the handoff between routing and scheduling loses information, so the scheduler does not know why this seller was chosen or whether a fallback is acceptable; ownership lookup failures default to round-robin silently, which is the single most common and most costly failure; nothing accounts for the meeting's likely value when choosing between an immediate slot with a less suitable seller and a later one with the right seller; and outcome attribution stops at the meeting booked rather than the meeting held or the opportunity created.

# Reminder Sequence Optimisation

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/scheduling-booking-platforms|Scheduling & Booking Platforms]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor here is fighting to establish what a reminder should actually say, when, and through which channel, for whom — and whoever produces that evidence takes the category, because a feature deployed in every product has never been tested by anybody.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$130M US attributable to reminder delivery and messaging within the category
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~9% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Universal in deployment and zero in measurement
**Target Buyer:** Every operator; the evidence would belong to the vendor
**Automation Potential:** Very High — a well-formed experimentation problem with millions of trials available

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Every product in this category sends appointment reminders. Every one of them defaults to roughly twenty-four hours before, by email or text, with wording written by whoever built the feature. That default exists because it is what everybody else does, and nobody in the industry — not one vendor, across millions of appointments a year — has tested it against an alternative and published or even internally established what works. The questions are simple and entirely answerable: does a second reminder help or annoy, does a reminder that requires a reply outperform one that does not, does timing depend on lead time and appointment hour, does channel matter by age or sector, does including the cancellation link reduce no-shows by enabling cancellation or increase them by suggesting it. Each is a two-arm experiment with abundant traffic and a clean outcome. The contest is simply being the vendor who finds out.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Configurable reminder timing and channel, template editing, delivery reporting, and in some products a second reminder. The gaps: no vendor runs experiments, so the defaults are folklore; operators configure by intuition with no feedback; delivery is reported and effect is not, so a message that arrives and changes nothing looks identical to one that works; content is never varied systematically; and personalisation is absent, so the same message goes to a four-year regular and a first-time booking.

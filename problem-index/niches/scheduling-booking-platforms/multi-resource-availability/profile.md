# Multi-Resource Availability

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/scheduling-booking-platforms|Scheduling & Booking Platforms]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor here is fighting to let a real business express what it can actually deliver — staff with different skills, constrained rooms and equipment, service-specific buffers and travel — and whoever does that takes the account, because businesses currently simplify their availability and sell less than they could.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$260M US attributable to resource-constrained scheduling
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~17% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Low — most businesses simplify rather than express their real constraints
**Target Buyer:** Multi-practitioner clinics, salons, studios, workshops and mobile services
**Automation Potential:** High — the constraints are knowable and the solving is standard

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A one-person consultancy's availability is a calendar and a working day, which every product handles. A real service business is not that. It has six practitioners with overlapping but different skills, three treatment rooms of which one has the equipment for two of the services, cleaning and setup times that vary by service, a rule that two particular services cannot run simultaneously because they share a machine, and for mobile work a travel time between appointments that depends on where the previous one was. Expressing that defeats every configuration interface in the category, so the business does what people do when a system cannot represent their reality: they simplify it. They restrict bookable services, pad every slot, take bookings by phone for anything complex, and leave capacity unsold — a loss that is invisible because it never appears as a missed booking. The contest is representing the business accurately enough that it can sell what it can actually deliver.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Staff calendars with services attached, basic resource objects in the more capable products, fixed buffers per service, and round-robin across a pool. The gaps: resources are modelled as a second calendar rather than as constraints, so combinations are not solved; skill matrices are either absent or a flat list without proficiency or service-time variation; travel time is a fixed buffer if it exists at all, rather than a function of the actual locations; configuration is expressed through forms that cannot represent conditional rules; and nothing tells the operator what their simplifications are costing, so the loss stays invisible.

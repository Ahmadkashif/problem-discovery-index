# Contract Funding & Stipulation Verification

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/sales-finance-companies|Sales Finance Companies]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to fund a clean contract the same day without funding a defective or fraudulent one, and whoever cuts days-to-fund while catching every defect before the money moves takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.8B a year in funder staff, verification services and post-funding audit across indirect lenders (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~6% of sales finance companies' non-interest operating spend (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — eContracts cover part of the flow; scanned and couriered paper packages, pay stubs and titles are read by eye
**Target Buyer:** VP of Funding Operations
**Automation Potential:** High for rules checks; human for genuine exceptions

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Funding is where the approval becomes money, and where a lender's dealer reputation is made. A funder checks the contract against the approval, recomputes the disclosures, applies state fee and add-on caps and the dealer agreement, verifies stipulations and confirms the lien will be recorded. Defects go back to the dealer; good dealers wait behind bad ones. Speed and accuracy pull against each other with a clock the dealer sets by phoning daily. The competitors are in-house funding departments, stipulation-verification vendors such as Informed.IQ, eContracting through RouteOne and Dealertrack, and outsourced funding processors.

## Current Tools & Gaps
eContracting and eVaulting through the dealer networks; origination systems holding the approved terms; Informed.IQ and similar stipulation verification; generic document capture. Gaps: rules for state contract forms and caps maintained in funders' heads and binders; defects returned one at a time over several cycles; no measure of which dealers' packages are reliably clean.

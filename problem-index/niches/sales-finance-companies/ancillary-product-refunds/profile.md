# Ancillary Product Cancellations & Refunds

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/sales-finance-companies|Sales Finance Companies]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to cancel and refund every add-on product on every early-terminated contract by that product's own terms and the state's rule, automatically and provably, and whoever closes the refund gap with an audit trail an examiner accepts takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$300M a year in servicing staff, administrator fees, outsourced processing and remediation cost for add-on cancellations at sales finance companies (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~1% of sales finance companies' non-interest operating spend (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — product terms sit on contract images; refunds are computed and chased by hand
**Target Buyer:** Director of Loan Servicing
**Automation Potential:** Very high — every refund is a deterministic calculation once the product terms are structured

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
GAP, vehicle service contracts, tyre-and-wheel and other add-ons are sold by the dealer, financed in the contract and administered by third parties. When a contract ends early — payoff, refinance, total loss, repossession — the unearned portion must be cancelled and refunded by the product's own method and the state's rule. Regulators have cited servicers for failing to do this, and some states have codified the obligation. The pricing side of these products sits with the administrators' actuaries ([[niches/auto-dealers-independent/fi-product-actuarial-teams/profile|F&I Product Actuarial & Claims Research]]); this niche is the lender's refund duty. The competitors are loan servicing system vendors, F&I product administrators' cancellation portals and outsourced cancellation processors.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Loan servicing systems (Shaw Systems, Nortridge, LoanPro); administrator portals; dealer F&I menu records. Gaps: products not structured at funding; termination events not wired to cancellation; refund methods applied inconsistently; dealer commission chargebacks chased by email.

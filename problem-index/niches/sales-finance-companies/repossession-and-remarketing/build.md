# Net Recovery Routing by Vehicle and Market

**Niche:** [[niches/sales-finance-companies/repossession-and-remarketing/profile|Repossession, Remarketing & Deficiency]]
**Industry:** [[industries/sales-finance-companies|Sales Finance Companies]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Recovered vehicles go to the same auction by habit when the net recovery — after transport, reconditioning, fees and days held — varies by hundreds of dollars across channels and markets.
**Tags:** #gradient-boosting #survival-analysis #optimization-fundamentals #evaluation-metrics #revenue-impact #data-integration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn a defaulted contract into the highest net recovery through a repossession and sale that survive each state's notice rules, and whoever recovers the most per charged-off dollar without a wrongful-repossession or notice defect takes the account.

## The Problem
A recovery coordinator sends a repossessed sedan to the nearest physical auction because that is the contract. A digital auction, a different lane, a light reconditioning or a dealer-direct sale might net more. Over thousands of units a year the gap is material, and losses on non-prime paper are high enough that recovery rate moves the book.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Sale results, fees, transport and reconditioning costs and days-to-sale sit with different vendors; condition reports are unstructured; and remarketing contracts favour volume commitments over per-unit optimisation.

## What to Build
A model of net recovery by channel for each unit, using VIN, mileage, condition report text, location, season and recent channel results, with days-held as a cost. Recommend the channel and reconditioning decision per unit, and learn from realised results.

## Target Customer
Recovery and remarketing directors at finance companies selling thousands of repossessed units a year.

## Impact If Built
Higher net recovery per unit and shorter holding periods, which directly lower charge-off severity.

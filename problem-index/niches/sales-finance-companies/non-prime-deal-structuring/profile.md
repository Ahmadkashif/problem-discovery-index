# Non-Prime Deal Structuring

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/sales-finance-companies|Sales Finance Companies]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to return a counteroffer on advance, term, down payment and stipulations that the dealer can close and the borrower can pay, and whoever structures the most closable deals that still perform takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$2.5B a year in credit analysts, structuring desks, fraud screening and dealer risk staff for non-prime indirect paper (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~8% of sales finance companies' non-interest operating spend (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — the application and score are digital; the structure is a human negotiation by screen and phone
**Target Buyer:** Chief Credit Officer at a non-prime lender
**Automation Potential:** Medium — suggestion and screening, with the analyst deciding

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Non-prime deals are rarely approved as submitted. The analyst cuts the advance against book value, shortens the term, asks for more down or requires proof of income and residence, and the dealer either restructures and resubmits or takes the deal elsewhere. The contest is the counteroffer, and the scarce input is the senior analyst's read of the borrower, the vehicle and the dealer. Losses here are high — subprime auto ABS averaged 8.88% annualised net losses in 2025 (S&P) — so a small edge in structure is worth a great deal. The competitors are Credit Acceptance, Westlake, Exeter, Santander Consumer, regional finance companies and the structuring desks inside banks.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Decision engines and program grids; dealer-risk tiering; fraud screens (Point Predictive, bureau products); income verification; deal-jacket images. Gaps: no record of which counter structures close and perform; counteroffers composed by hand under time pressure; dealer gaming of program grids detected late.

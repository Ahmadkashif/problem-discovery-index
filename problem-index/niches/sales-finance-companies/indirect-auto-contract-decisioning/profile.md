# Indirect Auto Contract Decisioning

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/sales-finance-companies|Sales Finance Companies]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is "every serious competitor is fighting to give the dealer a workable decision before another lender does," but prime and non-prime paper are won differently — prime by deciding the most applications automatically at a stable loss rate, non-prime by structuring counteroffers the dealer can close and the borrower can pay — with different inputs, staff and winners. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$4B a year in decisioning staff, bureau data, decision engines and fraud screening across indirect auto lenders (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~13% of sales finance companies' non-interest operating spend (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** High — applications arrive electronically through the dealer networks and engines score every one; the manual queue is where adoption stops
**Target Buyer:** Chief Credit Officer
**Automation Potential:** High for prime, medium for non-prime, where the counteroffer is judgement

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
This is the money decision: which dealer-submitted contracts the finance company buys, on what terms. Applications are shotgunned to several lenders at once through Dealertrack and RouteOne, and the dealer books with whichever source returns a closable answer first. Captives (43.4% of new-vehicle loans in Q1 2026) win prime new-car paper with subvened rates and speed; independent non-prime lenders such as Credit Acceptance, Westlake, Exeter and Santander Consumer win on structure and appetite. The credit judgement in the manual queue is the industry's tacit core.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Origination and decision engines (defi SOLUTIONS, MeridianLink, Origence's CUDL, in-house systems), bureau scores and auto-specific scores, fraud screens from Point Predictive and the bureaus, and income verification from Informed.IQ. The gaps: no record of why an analyst overrode or countered; no join between the counteroffer and its outcome; dealer drift visible only in the vintage report.

# Regulatory Reporting Engines Adapted to Facility Definitions

**Niche:** [[niches/sales-finance-companies/warehouse-and-abs-reporting/profile|Warehouse & ABS Collateral Reporting]]
**Industry:** [[industries/sales-finance-companies|Sales Finance Companies]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Banks run regulatory reporting engines that turn one data model into many prescribed reports; finance companies rebuild each facility's report in its own workbook.
**Tags:** #data-integration #descriptive-statistics #large-language-models #automation #compliance #workflow-orchestration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to produce borrowing base, servicer and asset-level reports from one reconciled loan-level dataset with evidence that each contract exists and is pledged once, and whoever makes the finance company's numbers verifiable by its banks and investors takes the account.

## The Problem
Each warehouse agreement and indenture defines eligibility, delinquency, charge-off and concentration slightly differently, and each new facility adds a workbook.

## What Already Exists
Regulatory reporting platforms used by banks (AxiomSL, now Adenza within Nasdaq, Wolters Kluwer OneSumX and similar) that map a single data model to many prescribed templates with lineage and validation.

## The Customization Gap
Finance companies need facility and deal definitions as configuration, drafted from the credit agreement or indenture text by a language model and confirmed by the analyst; borrowing base, excess-spread and trigger calculations; ABS-EE schema output; cross-report consistency checks; and pricing for a company with one analyst rather than a bank's reporting department.

## Target Customer
Capital markets teams at finance companies with three or more facilities or outstanding deals.

## Impact If Solved
New facilities onboarded by configuration, monthly reporting cut from days to hours, and reports that agree with each other.

# Warehouse & ABS Collateral Reporting

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/sales-finance-companies|Sales Finance Companies]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to produce borrowing base, servicer and asset-level reports from one reconciled loan-level dataset with evidence that each contract exists and is pledged once, and whoever makes the finance company's numbers verifiable by its banks and investors takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$700M a year in capital markets reporting staff, trustee and custodian reporting fees, verification and reporting software across sales finance companies (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~2% of sales finance companies' non-interest operating spend (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — servicing data is digital; the reports are assembled in workbooks per facility and per deal
**Target Buyer:** Treasurer / Head of Capital Markets
**Automation Potential:** Very high — every report is a deterministic transformation of loan-level data

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Finance companies fund on borrowed money, and the borrowing is governed by reports: monthly borrowing base certificates and eligibility tests for warehouse lines, servicer reports for each securitisation on its determination date, and for public deals Form ABS-EE with 72 asset-level fields per auto loan filed with the 10-D. US auto loan ABS reached a record ~$127B in 2025 (S&P). The Tricolor case — collateral allegedly pledged to more than one lender — has turned "prove the collateral" from an audit formality into a monthly requirement. The competitors are in-house capital markets teams working in spreadsheets, structured-finance reporting vendors, trustees' and custodians' reporting services and outsourced backup servicers.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Servicing systems' standard reports; Excel workbooks per facility; trustee portals; structured-finance analytics (Intex, dv01) on the investor side; custodians holding contract files. Gaps: definitions duplicated across workbooks; reconciliations repeated monthly; no link from tape row to contract image and title; no cross-lender view of duplicate pledges.

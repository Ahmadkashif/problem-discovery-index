# Residuals Graded Against Realised Resale

**Niche:** [[niches/sales-finance-companies/equipment-dealer-finance-programs/profile|Equipment Dealer & Vendor Finance Programs]]
**Industry:** [[industries/sales-finance-companies|Sales Finance Companies]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Equipment lessors set residual values years ahead and rarely score the forecast when the machine comes back and is sold.
**Tags:** #gradient-boosting #survival-analysis #time-series-forecasting #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to approve a small-business buyer at the dealer's counter on thin financials while setting a residual the used-equipment market will honour, and whoever decides fastest with credit and residual losses on plan takes the account.

## The Problem
A 36- or 60-month lease on a combine or wheel loader carries a residual set at signing. If the market softens, the lessor takes the loss at return or the dealer's buyback absorbs it. Residual committees use guides, auction comparables and experience; the forecast is rarely joined systematically to the eventual sale.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Returns trickle in years after signing, are remarketed through dealers, auctions or retail at varying prices, and are recorded in remarketing systems separate from origination. Hours, attachments and condition at return are captured inconsistently.

## What to Build
A residual ledger: every residual set, its inputs and the committee's adjustment, joined to the realised sale price, channel, hours and condition. Models for expected resale by model, hours and region with calibrated ranges, and a scorecard of committee overrides showing whether adjustments improved the forecast.

## Target Customer
Residual and asset management heads at captives and equipment lessors.

## Impact If Built
Residuals priced with evidence, residual losses anticipated by cohort, and committee judgement graded rather than assumed.

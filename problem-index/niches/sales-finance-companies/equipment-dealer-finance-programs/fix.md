# Telematics the Captive Owns and Credit Never Sees

**Niche:** [[niches/sales-finance-companies/equipment-dealer-finance-programs/profile|Equipment Dealer & Vendor Finance Programs]]
**Industry:** [[industries/sales-finance-companies|Sales Finance Companies]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Modern machines report hours, location and utilisation to the manufacturer, and the manufacturer's own finance arm finds out a borrower is in trouble when the payment is missed.
**Tags:** #change-point-detection #gradient-boosting #time-series-forecasting #data-integration #evaluation-metrics
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to approve a small-business buyer at the dealer's counter on thin financials while setting a residual the used-equipment market will honour, and whoever decides fastest with credit and residual losses on plan takes the account.

## The Problem
A financed machine that stops running for six weeks in peak season, or moves out of state, is an early warning and a residual signal. The data exists in the manufacturer's telematics platform; portfolio management at the captive works from payment history.

## Why It's Still Broken
Telematics belongs to the product organisation, customer consent terms were written for service and uptime, and nobody owns the data-sharing agreement between the two halves of the same company.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A consented, contractually clear feed of utilisation and location for financed units into portfolio monitoring, with change-point alerts on utilisation drops and relocation, and hours-at-return feeding the residual ledger.

## Who Feels the Pain
Portfolio managers and collectors at captives; residual teams; dealers asked to repossess machines that went quiet months ago.

## Impact If Fixed
Earlier, gentler interventions with struggling borrowers and better residuals, from data the group already holds.

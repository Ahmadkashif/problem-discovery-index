# Equipment Dealer & Vendor Finance Programs

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/sales-finance-companies|Sales Finance Companies]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to approve a small-business buyer at the dealer's counter on thin financials while setting a residual the used-equipment market will honour, and whoever decides fastest with credit and residual losses on plan takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$2.5B a year in credit, documentation, residual and remarketing staff and tools for dealer- and vendor-sourced equipment contracts (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~8% of sales finance companies' non-interest operating spend (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — dealer portals submit applications; small-ticket credit is scored, larger deals still go to an analyst with tax returns and a site visit
**Target Buyer:** VP of Dealer & Vendor Programs / Chief Credit Officer at a captive
**Automation Potential:** High for small-ticket approvals; medium for residual setting

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The equipment side of sales finance buys installment contracts and leases on tractors, excavators, trucks and machine tools sold through dealers, inside a ~$1.3T equipment leasing and finance industry (ELFA). The borrower is a small business or farm, the collateral's value depends on hours, attachments and season, and the captive's program is part of the manufacturer's sales strategy, with subvened rates tied to model-year and quarter-end targets. Credit and residual judgements are made together, at the dealer's counter. The competitors are the manufacturer captives (John Deere Financial, Cat Financial, CNH Industrial Capital, Kubota Credit), vendor-finance specialists such as DLL and bank equipment finance arms.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Captive dealer portals; small-ticket scoring using commercial bureaus and PayNet-style payment data; residual guides and auction data from EquipmentWatch, Sandhills and RB Global's Rouse; lease and loan servicing systems (NetSol, Odessa and in-house). Gaps: farm and construction borrowers whose financials are seasonal; residuals set by committee with little feedback from realised resale; telematics data the manufacturer holds but credit rarely uses.

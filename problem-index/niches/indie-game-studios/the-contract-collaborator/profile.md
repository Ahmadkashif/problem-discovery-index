# The Contract Collaborator

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/indie-game-studios|Indie Game Studios]]
**Category:** 🟣 Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to make the freelance artist, composer and contractor who staffs most indie projects a predictable part of a three-year schedule rather than its largest variable — and whoever does it takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$250M US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~6% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Low — email, invoices and trust
**Target Buyer:** Studio leads and the contractors themselves
**Automation Potential:** Medium — scoping, scheduling and payment

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Most indie games are made by a core of two or three people plus a rotating set of contract artists, composers, animators, writers and QA. The contractor is not an employee and not a vendor, engagements are scoped in a paragraph, milestones slip on both sides, and payment terms depend on the studio's runway. It is the part of the schedule that is least visible and most often the reason a project overruns — and the contractor, who carries the cash flow risk, is the person nobody builds for.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Email, a shared drive, a spreadsheet, an invoice template and a contract someone adapted once. The gaps: scoping that does not survive contact with iteration; no shared view of dependency between a contractor's delivery and the core team's schedule; late payment with no recourse; asset handoff by file transfer; and no rights and credit record when the game ships.

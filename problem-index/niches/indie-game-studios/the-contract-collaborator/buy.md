# Freelance Management From Creative Agencies

**Niche:** [[niches/indie-game-studios/the-contract-collaborator/profile|The Contract Collaborator]]
**Industry:** [[industries/indie-game-studios|Indie Game Studios]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Creative agencies run large freelance rosters against deadlines with scoping, resourcing and payment tooling, and game studios run theirs on email.
**Tags:** #workflow-orchestration #automation #data-integration #worker-facing #evaluation-metrics #revenue-impact #linear-regression #confidence-intervals
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to make the freelance artist, composer and contractor who staffs most indie projects a predictable part of a three-year schedule rather than its largest variable — and whoever does it takes the account.

## The Problem
Advertising, design and production agencies have run the same structure for decades: a small permanent core plus a large freelance roster, engaged per project, scoped, scheduled, reviewed and paid on terms. The software exists — resourcing, scoping templates, approval rounds, timesheets, purchase orders and payment. Game studios have the same structure, a longer timeline and none of the tooling, and it is a leading cause of schedule overrun.

## What Already Exists
Agency resource management; freelance roster and rate management; scoping and estimate templates; review and approval rounds; and purchase order and contractor payment.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a three-year project rather than a six-week campaign, with revision counts nobody can bound. It requires: (1) a multi-year engagement where the specification changes as the game is designed, unlike a brief fixed at the outset — this is the substantive difference; (2) iteration rounds on creative assets that cannot be capped the way an agency caps them, because the game's design is still moving; (3) dependency between contractor deliverables and internal development work, which agencies do not have in the same form; (4) a studio whose ability to pay is itself uncertain, where an agency has a client paying; and (5) rights, credit and residual terms that matter at ship in a way campaign work does not.

## Target Customer
Indie studios, publishers running external development, contract creative professionals, and agency resourcing vendors.

## Impact If Solved
Agencies solved the permanent-core-plus-freelance-roster structure decades ago. Adapting it to a three-year project whose specification is still moving — and to a studio whose ability to pay is itself uncertain — is the work.

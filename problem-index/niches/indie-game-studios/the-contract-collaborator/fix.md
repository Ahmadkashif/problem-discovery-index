# The Invoice Nobody Paid

**Niche:** [[niches/indie-game-studios/the-contract-collaborator/profile|The Contract Collaborator]]
**Industry:** [[industries/indie-game-studios|Indie Game Studios]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The contractor delivered, invoiced, and is now waiting on a studio whose runway ran out, with no recourse and a relationship to protect.
**Tags:** #worker-facing #quick-win #workflow-orchestration #automation #revenue-impact #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #compliance
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to make the freelance artist, composer and contractor who staffs most indie projects a predictable part of a three-year schedule rather than its largest variable — and whoever does it takes the account.

## The Problem
An artist delivers a set of assets, invoices, and hears nothing. The studio is not dishonest — its funding is late, or its own milestone payment slipped, or the person who approves invoices is the person shipping the game. The contractor cannot chase hard without damaging a relationship they depend on, cannot stop work without leaving the project broken, and absorbs the cash flow gap personally. This is routine rather than exceptional in the sector.

## Why It's Still Broken
The studio's runway problem is invisible to the contractor until it becomes a payment problem — a person who cannot see a cash constraint coming cannot plan around it, and finds out only when the money does not arrive. Invoicing is manual on both sides. Terms are informal and rarely enforced. And the power asymmetry means the contractor absorbs it quietly.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Make the money predictable and the constraint visible early. Tie payment to milestone acceptance with an automatic trigger, which is the fix and removes the discretionary step where invoices stall. Show the contractor the studio's payment history and current milestone status, since forewarning is worth more than recourse. Generate and send invoices automatically on acceptance rather than leaving it to the contractor to remember and chase. Escrow or stage funds at engagement where the studio can, which is how the better-funded studios already operate. Send reminders on the studio's behalf so chasing is not the contractor's social problem. Flag a slipping payment to the studio lead before it is late, as the approver is usually the person who is heads-down. Record terms plainly at engagement, because most disputes are about what was agreed. Support partial and staged payment for long deliverables, which suits how this work actually runs. Keep a payment reliability record per studio, which the community currently shares informally. And give the contractor a clean statement of what is owed and when, since they are running a business with no back office.

## Who Feels the Pain
Contract artists and composers absorbing cash flow gaps; studio leads who did not notice an invoice; projects that stall when a contractor stops; and the relationships that quietly end over money.

## Impact If Fixed
A person who cannot see a cash constraint coming cannot plan around it, and finds out only when the money does not arrive. Automatic payment on milestone acceptance removes the discretionary step where invoices stall.

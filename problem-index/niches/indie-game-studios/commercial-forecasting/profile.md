# Commercial Forecasting

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/indie-game-studios|Indie Game Studios]]
**Category:** 🔵 High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to tell a studio whether their game will sell while they can still change it — and the contest splits cleanly enough that it is not terminal.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.1B US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~28% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Very Low — a counter and folklore
**Target Buyer:** Studio leadership
**Automation Potential:** Very High — signals exist, comparison does not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A studio commits three years and learns the commercial answer in launch week. The signals that predicted it — wishlist velocity and its shape, demo retention curves, where playtesters stop — existed during development and were either not collected or not interpretable by anyone on the team. This is the industry's defining problem, it is the reason publishers can charge what they charge, and it is two problems: reading a studio's own signals, and obtaining the comparison set that makes reading them possible.

## Current Tools & Gaps
A wishlist counter, a store dashboard, forum advice and a publisher's opinion. The gaps: no forecast from own signals; no comparison against other games' trajectories; wishlist shape not interpreted; demo and playtest data not connected to outcomes; and the asymmetry monetised by publishers.

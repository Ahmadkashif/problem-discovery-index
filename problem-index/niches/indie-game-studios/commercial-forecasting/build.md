# An Answer Before the Money Runs Out

**Niche:** [[niches/indie-game-studios/commercial-forecasting/profile|Commercial Forecasting]]
**Industry:** [[industries/indie-game-studios|Indie Game Studios]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** The feedback loop is longer than the funding runway, and the signals that would shorten it are sitting uninterpreted.
**Tags:** #time-series-forecasting #gradient-boosting #survival-analysis #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #revenue-impact #k-nearest-neighbors #hypothesis-testing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to tell a studio whether their game will sell while they can still change it — and the contest splits cleanly enough that it is not terminal.

## The Problem
A two-person studio spends three years and finds out in launch week. Eighteen months earlier their wishlist curve had a shape, their demo had a retention profile, and their playtesters stopped in identifiable places — all of which are predictive of the outcome and none of which they could read, because reading them requires knowing what those curves look like for games that succeeded and games that did not. The publishers who do see many games' trajectories charge substantially for what is pattern recognition.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The comparison data sits across the platform layer and the aggregators and the studios hold none of it, so interpretation is impossible from inside a studio — an asymmetry where one side has the data and the other has the decision is stable until someone assembles the data for the other side. Publishers benefit from the asymmetry and are the parties best placed to end it. Studios are numerous, individually tiny and have no purchasing power. And nobody has connected development-stage signals to outcomes at scale.

## What to Build
Read the signals and assemble the comparison. Interpret a studio's own wishlist, demo and playtest signals against outcome-linked trajectories, which is one half and is what turns a counter into a forecast. Assemble the benchmark corpus across thousands of games, which is the other half and is the precondition for the first. Forecast in ranges with honest uncertainty, since a false precision on a three-year commitment would be worse than nothing. Deliver the forecast early enough to act on, as a prediction at launch is a postmortem and one at eighteen months is a decision. Say what would change the outcome rather than only predicting it, because a studio's realistic options are scope, positioning and timing. Identify the game that is not going to work early, since the kindest and most valuable finding is one that saves two years. Compare against similar games rather than against the market, as genre and scope dominate everything. Validate the forecasts prospectively and publish the accuracy, because the sector is full of confident advice and checkability is the differentiator. Price it for a studio of two people, which is the entire market. And make the interpretation the product rather than the data, since a studio needs a decision and not a dashboard.

## Target Customer
Studio leadership, funders and publishers who currently arbitrage the asymmetry, platform holders whose ecosystem depends on studio survival, and game analytics vendors.

## Impact If Built
An asymmetry where one side has the data and the other has the decision is stable until someone assembles the data for the other side. The development-stage signals are predictive and uninterpreted, and interpreting them is what publishers are actually paid for.

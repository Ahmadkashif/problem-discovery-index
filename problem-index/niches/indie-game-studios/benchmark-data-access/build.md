# The Corpus That Makes Reading Possible

**Niche:** [[niches/indie-game-studios/benchmark-data-access/profile|Benchmark Data Access]]
**Industry:** [[industries/indie-game-studios|Indie Game Studios]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Every interpretation a studio needs requires a comparison set nobody has assembled and several parties are paid not to.
**Tags:** #data-integration #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #compliance #revenue-impact #k-means-clustering #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to assemble the cross-studio corpus of trajectories and outcomes that makes any individual game's signals readable — and whoever builds it ends the asymmetry publishers currently charge for.

## The Problem
A wishlist curve means nothing without knowing what curves look like for games that sold and games that did not. A demo retention profile means nothing without the same. The trajectories and outcomes exist across thousands of games, spread between the platform holder, the aggregators, the publishers and the studios themselves, and nobody has assembled them into a corpus. Publishers, who see many trajectories through their own portfolios, charge substantially for what that visibility enables.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The parties holding the data benefit from holding it, so the corpus is not assembled by anyone with the ability to assemble it — an asymmetry that is monetised will not be removed by its beneficiary. Platform holders have no commercial reason to publish. Studios who could contribute are unorganised and worry about revealing performance. And the scraped public data is thin and outcome-poor.

## What to Build
Assemble it from the side that has an incentive. Build the corpus from studio contribution rather than from platform disclosure, since studios gain more from access than they lose from contributing and are the only party with an aligned incentive — this is the structural insight the whole thing rests on. Make contribution the price of access, which solves the free-rider problem and is the standard consortium design. Anonymise and aggregate so no studio's specifics are exposed, as that is the precondition for participation. Combine with public and scraped data to seed the corpus before contribution reaches scale. Structure it around outcomes, since trajectories without outcomes are uninterpretable and the outcome is the part studios hold. Segment by genre, scope, price and platform, because comparisons across those boundaries are misleading and are what folklore currently does. Publish aggregate findings openly, which builds the case for contributing and serves the sector. Approach the platform holders as partners, since their ecosystem depends on studio survival and the argument has never been made to them properly. Handle the contribution terms carefully, as a studio's commercial data is sensitive and trust is the whole asset. And keep it current, because the discovery environment changes yearly and a stale benchmark misleads.

## Target Customer
Studio leadership, platform holders whose ecosystems depend on studio viability, publishers and funders, and games data vendors.

## Impact If Built
An asymmetry that is monetised will not be removed by its beneficiary, so the corpus must be assembled from the side that gains. Studios gain more from access than they lose from contributing, which is the only aligned incentive in the system.

# Consortium Benchmarking From Other Industries

**Niche:** [[niches/indie-game-studios/benchmark-data-access/profile|Benchmark Data Access]]
**Industry:** [[industries/indie-game-studios|Indie Game Studios]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Industries full of small firms built contribution-for-access benchmarking consortia, and games has folklore and a paid publisher.
**Tags:** #data-integration #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #compliance #revenue-impact #automation #hypothesis-testing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to assemble the cross-studio corpus of trajectories and outcomes that makes any individual game's signals readable — and whoever builds it ends the asymmetry publishers currently charge for.

## The Problem
Sectors full of small firms that individually cannot see the market — agriculture, professional practices, restaurants, construction — built benchmarking consortia: you contribute your numbers, you receive the aggregate, the terms are governed, and everyone gains information none of them could buy. The design is well understood, including the free-rider problem, the anonymity requirements and the governance. The indie games sector has the same structure and no consortium.

## What Already Exists
Contribution-for-access benchmarking models; anonymisation and aggregation thresholds; governance structures for participant data; free-rider handling; and published aggregate reporting.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to firms with one product and a binary outcome. It requires: (1) contributors whose entire performance is one title, so contributing is revealing far more than a line of a profit and loss account — this is the substantive sensitivity and shapes the anonymity design; (2) the platform holder holding most of the relevant data, which no benchmarking consortium usually faces; (3) trajectories over time rather than periodic figures, which is richer and needs a different structure; (4) participants who are individuals rather than firms with an association to convene them; and (5) a publisher layer with a commercial interest in the asymmetry persisting.

## Target Customer
Studio leadership, platform holders, industry associations and events, and benchmarking data vendors.

## Impact If Solved
Small-firm sectors built consortia because individual members could not see their own market. The sensitivity here is higher because a contributor's whole performance is one title, which makes the anonymity design the critical piece rather than an afterthought.

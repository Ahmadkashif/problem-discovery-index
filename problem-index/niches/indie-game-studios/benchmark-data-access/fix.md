# Postmortems Nobody Can Compare

**Niche:** [[niches/indie-game-studios/benchmark-data-access/profile|Benchmark Data Access]]
**Industry:** [[industries/indie-game-studios|Indie Game Studios]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The sector's shared knowledge is a few dozen blog posts written by people who are no longer in business.
**Tags:** #quick-win #descriptive-statistics #data-integration #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #compliance #revenue-impact #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to assemble the cross-studio corpus of trajectories and outcomes that makes any individual game's signals readable — and whoever builds it ends the asymmetry publishers currently charge for.

## The Problem
Studios do share. They write postmortems, post sales numbers, speak at conferences and answer questions in forums. That material is generous, unstructured and unusable in aggregate — different metrics, different periods, different definitions, and a survivorship bias toward the games that did well enough to be worth writing about. The sector has a culture of sharing and no structure to make the sharing cumulative.

## Why It's Still Broken
Postmortems are written as narratives, so the numbers in them are illustrations rather than data — a form designed to tell a story does not produce a comparable record. Nobody defined a common set of figures. The failures, which are the most informative cases, are the least likely to be written up. And there is no place to put a structured contribution.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Give the existing generosity a structure. Define a short standard set of figures a studio can publish, which is the fix and turns a culture of sharing into a growing dataset. Provide a place to submit them, since the willingness exists and the venue does not. Structure the existing postmortems retrospectively where the numbers are present, as a considerable body already exists. Actively seek the quiet failures, because survivorship bias is what makes the current shared knowledge misleading and those studios will often share if asked privately. Publish the aggregate rather than individual figures, which lowers the barrier to contributing. Include the trajectory rather than only the outcome, since the shape is the informative part and postmortems usually give a total. Segment by genre and scope in any published finding, as blended figures are what produce the misleading rules of thumb. Credit contributors if they want it and anonymise if they do not. Make the aggregate freely available, because the sector's problem is information poverty and a paywall reproduces it. And refresh annually, since the discovery environment changes and last year's benchmark misleads.

## Who Feels the Pain
Studios planning against anecdotes from survivors; developers who generously shared numbers nobody could use; new entrants absorbing rules of thumb that were never true; and a sector whose collective knowledge does not accumulate.

## Impact If Fixed
A form designed to tell a story does not produce a comparable record, so a generous culture produces unusable data. A short standard figure set with somewhere to submit it turns the existing sharing into a corpus.

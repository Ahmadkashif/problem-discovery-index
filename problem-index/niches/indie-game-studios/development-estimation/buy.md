**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)

**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to tell a small team when their game will actually be finished — and whoever forecasts from recorded velocity rather than from optimism prevents the failure that kills more studios than bad games do.

## The Problem
Software delivery spent two decades learning that estimates are unreliable and measured throughput is not: forecasting from historical cycle time, probabilistic completion dates from Monte Carlo simulation over actual delivery rates, explicit scope tracking, and the abandonment of date-based commitment in favour of ranges. The practice is documented and effective. Game development, which is software development with a creative front end, uses the estimate.

## What Already Exists
Throughput and cycle time measurement; Monte Carlo completion forecasting; scope change tracking; probabilistic delivery communication; and the evidence base showing estimates underperform measured velocity.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to creative work with an undefined finish line. It requires: (1) a definition of done that is a judgement about whether the game is good rather than a specification satisfied — this is the fundamental difficulty and is why the practice was dismissed; (2) a unit of work that is heterogeneous, where a level, a mechanic and a shader are not comparable; (3) teams of one to three with no process capacity, so every measurement must be passive; (4) scope that grows because the game is being discovered rather than because requirements changed; and (5) funding from personal savings, which makes the runway forecast far more consequential than a corporate deadline.

## Target Customer
Studio leadership, publishers and funders, platform holders, and delivery forecasting vendors.

## Impact If Solved
Software learned that measured throughput beats estimates and games kept the estimates. The undefined finish line is the real difficulty, which is why the forecast should be about the runway rather than about the date.

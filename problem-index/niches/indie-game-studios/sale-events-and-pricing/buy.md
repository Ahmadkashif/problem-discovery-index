**One-liner:** Retail has modelled promotion depth, timing and cannibalisation for decades, and game studios pick a round number.

**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to tell a studio what a discount at a given depth and moment will actually earn over the title's remaining life — and whoever answers it takes the account.

## The Problem
Retail promotion optimisation is a mature discipline: elasticity estimation, depth and frequency selection, cannibalisation and halo modelling, calendar planning against seasonal events, and post-promotion evaluation separating lift from baseline. Grocery chains run it on thousands of items weekly. A game catalogue is a small, well-instrumented version of the same problem with perfect sales data and no inventory constraint, and none of the method has arrived.

## What Already Exists
Price elasticity estimation; promotion depth and frequency optimisation; cannibalisation and halo modelling; promotional calendar planning; and post-promotion lift measurement.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a durable good bought once, with a finite audience that depletes. It requires: (1) a single-purchase product rather than a repeat-purchase basket, so the demand curve depletes as the audience is exhausted — this is the substantive difference and it inverts much of the retail intuition; (2) platform sale events that supply their own traffic, which must be separated from the discount effect; (3) a long tail measured in years with a price expectation the studio is training; (4) a catalogue of one to five titles rather than thousands of SKUs, so the model must borrow strength from comparable titles; and (5) regional pricing against purchasing power, which retail handles by market and games handle by conversion default.

## Target Customer
Indie studios, publishers, platform partner teams, and retail pricing vendors seeking a digital goods vertical.

## Impact If Solved
Retail has run elasticity and cannibalisation modelling for decades on a harder version of this. A depleting single-purchase audience and platform events supplying their own traffic are the adaptations that make it a different model.

# Nothing Posted for Six Weeks

**Niche:** [[niches/indie-game-studios/the-developer-who-must-market/profile|The Developer Who Must Market]]
**Industry:** [[industries/indie-game-studios|Indie Game Studios]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The developer went heads-down on a hard feature, six weeks passed, and the audience they spent a year building went quiet.
**Tags:** #worker-facing #quick-win #workflow-orchestration #automation #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #confidence-intervals #large-language-models
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get eighteen months of consistent marketing out of someone who became a developer specifically because they did not want to do that — and whoever makes it survivable changes the commercial outcome of the whole sector.

## The Problem
Marketing consistency fails in the predictable way: the developer is deep in a difficult part of the project, posting stops, weeks pass, the audience's attention moves on, and restarting feels worse than continuing would have because there is now a gap to explain. The gap is not a failure of will — it is what happens when a discretionary activity competes with an urgent one, repeatedly, in the same person's week.

## Why It's Still Broken
Posting is the only task with no external deadline, so it loses every collision — an activity with no due date competes with one that has a build breaking and always loses. Nothing is scheduled in advance. The developer feels guilty rather than systematic about it. And nobody has told them how much the gap actually costs.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Build the buffer and lower the bar. Schedule posts in advance from a buffer of material, which is the fix and decouples posting from the week the developer is having. Harvest content continuously from the work — screenshots, clips, commits, notes — so the buffer fills without a separate activity. Set a low sustainable cadence and keep it rather than alternating between intensity and silence, since consistency outperforms volume here. Prompt with a specific thing to post rather than an empty box, because a blank prompt during a hard week produces nothing. Allow the honest development post, as a difficult week is legitimate content and developers think it is not. Report what a gap actually costs in wishlist velocity, which nobody has measured and which would either relieve or motivate. Batch the work into one session rather than spreading it, which suits how these people work. Warn before a long gap rather than after, since the recovery is harder than the prevention. Keep a small reserve of evergreen material for the worst weeks. And tell developers plainly that the gap is normal, because the guilt is itself part of why restarting is hard.

## Who Feels the Pain
Developers who lost an audience they built; studios whose launch underperforms for a reason unrelated to the game; communities who wondered whether the project died; and the person carrying guilt about the thing they are least suited to.

## Impact If Fixed
An activity with no due date competes with one that has a build breaking and always loses. A scheduled buffer harvested from the development work decouples posting from the week the developer is having.

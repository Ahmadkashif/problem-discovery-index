# Conversion Optimisation From Ecommerce

**Niche:** [[niches/indie-game-studios/store-presentation-and-discovery/profile|Store Presentation & Discovery]]
**Industry:** [[industries/indie-game-studios|Indie Game Studios]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Ecommerce tests product imagery and copy continuously against conversion, and a game's store page is set once by consensus.
**Tags:** #hypothesis-testing #gradient-boosting #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #causal-inference #contrastive-learning #revenue-impact #descriptive-statistics
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to make capsule art, a trailer and the first paragraph convert a browsing player into a wishlist — and whoever tests those choices rather than asking a Discord decides the launch a year in advance.

## The Problem
Ecommerce treats the product page as a conversion surface to be tested: imagery variants, copy, ordering, social proof, all measured against a purchase rate with a well-understood experimental apparatus. A game's store page is functionally the same artefact with a higher stake, since it feeds an algorithm that compounds the result, and it is produced once by a team that cannot see its conversion rate in a comparable form.

## What Already Exists
Product page testing frameworks; image and copy variant testing; conversion rate measurement and benchmarking; experimental design for small samples; and creative performance libraries by category.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a page the studio does not control the traffic to. It requires: (1) traffic allocated by the platform's algorithm rather than bought, so a studio cannot run its own split test on the live page — this is the fundamental constraint and forces testing to happen off-platform; (2) a conversion event that is a wishlist rather than a purchase, which is a weaker but earlier signal; (3) an outcome that compounds through the algorithm, making early conversion disproportionately valuable; (4) a single product rather than a catalogue, so the studio cannot learn across items and needs the cross-studio benchmark; and (5) a budget of essentially nothing.

## Target Customer
Studio and marketing leadership, publishers, platform holders, and conversion optimisation vendors.

## Impact If Solved
Ecommerce tests the page because it controls the traffic, and an indie studio controls neither. Testing off-platform with a representative panel is the adaptation, and the compounding through the algorithm is what makes it worth far more here.

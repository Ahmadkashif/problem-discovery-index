# Testing the Page Instead of Asking Friends

**Niche:** [[niches/indie-game-studios/store-presentation-and-discovery/profile|Store Presentation & Discovery]]
**Industry:** [[industries/indie-game-studios|Indie Game Studios]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** The most consequential image the studio will ever make is chosen by people who already know what the game is.
**Tags:** #contrastive-learning #cnns #gradient-boosting #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #hypothesis-testing #causal-inference #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to make capsule art, a trailer and the first paragraph convert a browsing player into a wishlist — and whoever tests those choices rather than asking a Discord decides the launch a year in advance.

## The Problem
A stranger scrolling a store sees a small image, a title and a genre tag, and decides in under a second whether to click. If they click, a trailer and a paragraph decide whether they wishlist. Those decisions accumulate into the wishlist total that drives the launch. The studio chooses the image by posting three options in a Discord full of people who have followed the game for a year, which is the least representative audience available and the only one they have.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The studio's own community is free and immediate, so it became the testing mechanism — an available audience displaces a representative one when there is no alternative. Testing with strangers requires a panel nobody provides at indie budgets. Store page conversion is not reported in a way that supports iteration. And nobody has published what actually converts.

## What to Build
Test with strangers and measure the conversion. Provide a testing panel of representative players who do not know the game, which is the core and is the single change that fixes the selection problem. Measure click-through from a browsing context rather than preference, since asking which image someone likes and observing which they click are different questions. Test the trailer's opening seconds specifically, because that is where the decision happens and studios optimise the whole thing. Test the first paragraph and the genre framing, as positioning is frequently the actual failure rather than the art. Compare against conversion for comparable pages, connecting to the benchmark corpus. Report the page's conversion rate over time, so a change can be evaluated rather than debated. Model what converts by genre from the corpus, which is the pattern recognition publishers sell. Test before the page is live, since the first impression on the store is also the algorithm's first impression. Handle the tagging and categorisation decision, as it determines which browsing contexts the game appears in and is chosen by intuition. And price it for a two-person studio, because that is the whole market.

## Target Customer
Studio and marketing leadership, publishers advising on positioning, platform holders whose discovery depends on page quality, and conversion testing vendors.

## Impact If Built
An available audience displaces a representative one when there is no alternative, so the page is tested on people who already love the game. A panel of strangers measuring click-through is the change that makes the most consequential artefact testable.

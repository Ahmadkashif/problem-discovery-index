# Cohort and Funnel Analysis From Product

**Niche:** [[niches/indie-game-studios/signal-interpretation/profile|Signal Interpretation]]
**Industry:** [[industries/indie-game-studios|Indie Game Studios]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Product analytics made funnel and cohort analysis routine for any software team, and game studios do not instrument their own demo.
**Tags:** #survival-analysis #gradient-boosting #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #descriptive-statistics #time-series-forecasting #cross-validation #k-nearest-neighbors
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn a studio's own wishlist shape, demo retention and playtest behaviour into a commercial forecast — and whoever reads those signals best tells a two-person team what three years of their life is worth while they can still change it.

## The Problem
Product analytics is a mature and accessible discipline: funnels, cohort retention, drop-off analysis, activation definition and a well-understood vocabulary. Any software team can instrument a product and read where users leave. A game demo is a product with a funnel and a retention curve, the analysis would be directly applicable, and most indie studios have never instrumented one.

## What Already Exists
Funnel and drop-off analysis; cohort retention curves; activation metric definition; session and event instrumentation; and the analytics tooling that makes it accessible.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a game with a commercial outcome months later. It requires: (1) the outcome being a launch-week sales result rather than in-product conversion, so retention must be related to a distant commercial event — this is the substantive difference and requires the benchmark to bridge it; (2) a demo that is a marketing artefact as well as a product, so its funnel means something different from a trial; (3) studios with no analytics practice and a resistance to instrumenting a creative work; (4) engine telemetry rather than web analytics, which is a different implementation path; and (5) very small sample sizes, so the analysis must work on hundreds of players rather than thousands.

## Target Customer
Studio and design leadership, publishers, and product analytics vendors with no games presence.

## Impact If Solved
Product analytics made funnel reading routine and games never adopted it for demos. Relating a retention curve to a launch-week outcome months later is the adaptation, and it is what makes the instrumentation worth doing.

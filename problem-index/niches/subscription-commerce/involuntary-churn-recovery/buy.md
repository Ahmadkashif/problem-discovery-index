# Payment Recovery and Collections Practice

**Niche:** [[niches/subscription-commerce/involuntary-churn-recovery/profile|Involuntary Churn Recovery]]
**Industry:** [[industries/subscription-commerce|Subscription Commerce]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Collections built contact strategy optimisation and channel testing over decades, and subscription dunning sends three emails on a fixed schedule.
**Tags:** #gradient-boosting #markov-chains #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #revenue-impact #hypothesis-testing #automation #survival-analysis
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to recover the subscriber whose card failed rather than the one who chose to leave — and whoever does that keeps the revenue, because a payment failure is a large, mechanical share of churn from customers who never decided anything.

## The Problem
Getting a payment that failed to succeed is what collections does, and the discipline optimises everything about it: which channel, at what time, with what message, in what sequence, for which segment, with propensity models deciding who gets what treatment and rigorous testing behind every step. Card network practice adds authorisation optimisation and network-level card updating. Subscription dunning typically sends three emails on a fixed schedule to everybody.

## What Already Exists
Collections contact strategy with channel and timing optimisation; propensity-to-pay modelling for treatment assignment; authorisation optimisation and retry logic tuned to decline codes; network card updater services; and rigorous champion-challenger testing infrastructure.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a customer who is not in arrears and is not at fault. It requires: (1) tone and framing appropriate to a service failure rather than to a debt, since the subscriber did nothing wrong and collections language damages a relationship the operator wants to keep — this is the most important difference and the one that makes borrowing the practice without adapting it counterproductive; (2) decline-reason-specific treatment, which authorisation optimisation does routinely and subscription dunning does not; (3) treatment assignment by expected lifetime value, so a long-standing subscriber gets more effort than a first-cycle one; (4) the subscription kept alive during recovery where the economics support it, which is a decision collections does not face and which materially changes the recovery rate; and (5) testing infrastructure, since every lever here is testable and almost nothing is tested.

## Target Customer
Subscription operators, billing and dunning vendors, and the collections discipline whose optimisation practice transfers with a change of tone.

## Impact If Solved
Collections optimises every lever and subscription dunning sends three emails to everybody. Adapting the tone to a service failure rather than a debt is the difference between borrowing the practice usefully and damaging the relationship the recovery exists to preserve.

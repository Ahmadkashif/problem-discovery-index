# Supply & Curation Buying

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/subscription-commerce|Subscription Commerce]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to buy the right quantity of the right things for a subscriber base whose size and composition at delivery is unknown — and whoever does that protects the margin, because the buy is committed months before the base that receives it is known.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$550M US in working capital and margin
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~9% of category operating spend
**Digital Adoption:** Very Low — committing inventory against a base that will churn
**Target Buyer:** Merchandising and procurement functions
**Automation Potential:** High — the forecast and the allocation are both modellable

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A subscription buy has an unusual structure: the quantity depends on how many subscribers there will be in three months, which depends on churn and acquisition nobody forecasts well, and the composition depends on what those subscribers will like, which depends on a preference model the company may not have. The buy is committed to suppliers with lead times, and both errors are expensive — short and subscribers receive substitutions that damage retention, long and the surplus has no channel because the box is the only route to market. Merchandisers make this decision on a subscriber count from last month and a judgement about appeal, and the curation and the buying are the same decision made by the same people with no feedback from what subscribers kept.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Spreadsheets, supplier lead time knowledge, and a subscriber count. The gaps: no subscriber forecast at the horizon the buy requires; churn is not projected into the buy quantity; preference data does not inform the buy; surplus has no planned disposition; and past buys are not evaluated against what subscribers actually kept.

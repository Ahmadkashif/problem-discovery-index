# Early Churn Diagnosis

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/subscription-commerce|Subscription Commerce]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to know why the first three deliveries fail — and whoever does that takes the economics, because that is where nearly all the churn happens and the reasons are specific and fixable.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.4B US attributable to early retention
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~24% of category operating spend
**Digital Adoption:** None — a monthly rate and more acquisition
**Target Buyer:** Every subscription operator, and the capital funding their acquisition
**Automation Potential:** High — the signals are present in the first cycles

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Most cancellations happen within the first three deliveries, and the reasons are concrete: the first box did not match what the sign-up implied, the cadence is wrong for how fast the customer actually uses the product, an item arrived damaged, the value was not obvious relative to the price, or the customer wanted to skip once and could not find how. Those are product and operations failures with identifiable causes, and the industry reports a monthly churn percentage, categorises retention as a marketing function, and responds by acquiring more subscribers to replace the ones leaving. The diagnosis is tractable because the business observes the same customer repeatedly against a stated expectation, which almost no other commerce model does.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Cohort retention charts, cancellation surveys, and a monthly churn rate. The gaps: churn is not decomposed by cycle number, so the front-loading is invisible in a monthly figure; cancellation reasons are collected in a dropdown at the worst moment and not validated against behaviour; first-delivery experience is not measured; the gap between what sign-up implied and what arrived is nobody's metric; and no intervention is triggered before the decision is made.

# Early Intervention Practice

**Industry:** [[industries/subscription-commerce|Subscription Commerce]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Customer success in software moved retention from the renewal conversation to continuous health monitoring a decade ago, and subscription commerce still staffs the cancel page.
**Tags:** #gradient-boosting #survival-analysis #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #worker-facing #causal-inference #revenue-impact #workflow-orchestration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to move the retention conversation earlier than the cancel page — and whoever does that takes the saves, because by the time the agent is involved the decision has been made and a discount is the only tool left.

## The Problem
Software subscription businesses learned that a renewal is decided long before the renewal date, and restructured around it: health scores from usage and engagement, proactive outreach triggered by declining signals, defined playbooks per risk pattern, and a function whose job is to prevent the problem rather than to argue at the end. The whole discipline of customer success exists because the renewal conversation was the wrong place to intervene. Subscription commerce, which has richer behavioural signals than most software products, intervenes at the cancel page.

## What Already Exists
Customer health scoring from behavioural signals; risk-triggered outreach playbooks; customer success operating models with defined intervention points; churn prediction with intervention targeting; and the measurement practice distinguishing prevented churn from deferred churn.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a consumer relationship with no account manager and physical service failures. It requires: (1) health signals that are operational rather than usage-based — delivery timing, condition, skips, accumulation, complaints — which are richer and more objective than software's engagement proxies and are not used at all; (2) intervention at consumer scale and cost, since a customer success manager per account is impossible and the playbook must be largely automated with human contact reserved for the highest-value cases; (3) remedies that are operational rather than commercial, which requires the retention function to be able to change the product experience and not only the price; (4) the intervention targeted by uplift rather than by risk, since contacting every at-risk subscriber is expensive and some would have stayed anyway; and (5) the feedback loop to the operational teams, since the health signals identify systemic failures as well as individual ones.

## Target Customer
Customer experience and retention organisations, subscription operators, and the customer success discipline whose model transfers with a consumer-scale adaptation.

## Impact If Solved
Software restructured retention around continuous health a decade ago because the renewal conversation was the wrong place, and this category has richer signals and still staffs the cancel page. Operational health signals are more objective than the engagement proxies software uses and are entirely unexploited.

# Handed a Decision Already Made

**Niche:** [[niches/subscription-commerce/the-retention-agent/profile|The Retention Agent]]
**Industry:** [[industries/subscription-commerce|Subscription Commerce]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Retention agents are handed customers at the moment they have already decided to leave, armed with discounts, and measured on saves they mostly cannot make.
**Tags:** #worker-facing #gradient-boosting #confidence-intervals #evaluation-metrics #causal-inference #revenue-impact #survival-analysis #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to move the retention conversation earlier than the cancel page — and whoever does that takes the saves, because by the time the agent is involved the decision has been made and a discount is the only tool left.

## The Problem
A subscriber has had two deliveries arrive late, one damaged, and has been accumulating product they cannot use. They decide to cancel. They reach the cancel page, an agent is triggered, and the agent — who can see a subscription record and a billing history and nothing about the late deliveries, the damage or the accumulation — offers twenty percent off. The customer, whose problem was never price, declines. The agent records a failed save. Their save rate is low, they are coached on their offers, and the three fixable problems that caused the cancellation are invisible to everyone including them.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The retention function was placed at the cancel page because that is where cancellation happens, which conflates the moment of the event with the moment of the cause. The agent's tooling came from support systems that show the account rather than the experience. Discounts are the only lever a support agent can pull without another team's cooperation. And the save rate metric makes the agent accountable for a process failure upstream.

## What to Build
Move the intervention earlier and arm the agent properly. Predict the cancellation from the experience signals weeks ahead and intervene then, when the problem is still fixable and the customer has not yet decided — which is the fix for the structural problem and changes what the retention function is. Give the agent the experience view: delivery history with timing and condition, skips, swaps, complaints, accumulation signals, and the predicted reason, so the conversation is about the actual problem rather than about price. Equip them with operational remedies — change the cadence, change the contents, credit the damaged delivery, skip the next one — which address what went wrong and which a discount does not. Let them fix rather than only offer, with the authority to make the operational change in the conversation. Route the cancellation reason back to the team that owns it, so the third late delivery becomes a logistics finding rather than a lost customer. Measure the agent on resolution and on twelve-month retention rather than on immediate saves. Staff the earlier intervention rather than only the cancel page, since a conversation at the moment of the second late delivery is worth several at the cancel page. And report how many cancellations arrived with a predictable, unaddressed problem, because that number is the case for the whole change.

## Target Customer
Customer experience organisations, the agents, and operators whose retention function is positioned at the end of the process it is meant to affect.

## Impact If Built
The decision was made weeks earlier in response to problems the agent cannot see, and the only tool they have addresses a price objection the customer did not have. Predicting and intervening at the moment of the second late delivery is worth several conversations at the cancel page.

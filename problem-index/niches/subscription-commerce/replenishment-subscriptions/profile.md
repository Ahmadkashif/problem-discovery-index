# Replenishment Subscriptions

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/subscription-commerce|Subscription Commerce]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this sub-niche is fighting to have the next delivery arrive as the last one runs out — and whoever does that keeps the subscriber, because the alternative is a customer who simply buys it when they need it and does not need a subscription at all.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$900M US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~15% of category operating spend
**Digital Adoption:** Moderate — cadence is configurable and unmanaged
**Target Buyer:** Operators of consumable and replenishment subscriptions
**Automation Potential:** Very High — consumption prediction is a well-posed problem

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A replenishment subscription delivers something the customer chose and wants again: coffee, razor blades, supplements, pet food, cleaning products. There is a right answer to when the next delivery should arrive, defined by when the customer runs out, and it is different for every household. Get it right and the subscription is effortlessly valuable; get it wrong in either direction and it is worse than buying ad hoc, which is exactly what the customer will go back to. The contest is consumption prediction, the signal exists, and most operators run a plan-level cadence chosen from a dropdown.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Configurable cadence per plan, skip and delay, and reminder emails before charge. The gaps: consumption is not estimated per household; the cadence does not adapt to observed behaviour; skips and early reorders are treated as billing events rather than as consumption evidence; no allowance for households of different sizes or usage; and the pre-charge reminder is the only intervention point.

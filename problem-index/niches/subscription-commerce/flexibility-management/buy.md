# Save Offers and Deflection Design

**Niche:** [[niches/subscription-commerce/flexibility-management/profile|Flexibility Management]]
**Industry:** [[industries/subscription-commerce|Subscription Commerce]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Telecoms and media subscriptions spent decades learning which save offers work and which merely delay the cancellation, and this category offers a discount.
**Tags:** #causal-inference #gradient-boosting #confidence-intervals #evaluation-metrics #revenue-impact #hypothesis-testing #survival-analysis #cross-validation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to make skip, pause and swap the obvious first response to a problem rather than a hidden setting — and whoever does that keeps the subscriber, because the customer who cannot find the skip cancels instead.

## The Problem
Retaining a subscriber who has decided to leave is a discipline telecoms and media built at scale: save offers matched to the stated reason, uplift-modelled targeting so offers go to customers whose behaviour they will change, downgrade paths as an alternative to cancellation, and rigorous measurement of whether a save is a genuine retention or a deferred cancellation with a discount attached. This category offers a percentage off and a survey.

## What Already Exists
Save offer libraries matched to cancellation reasons; uplift modelling for targeting offers at persuadable customers; downgrade and tier-change paths as cancellation alternatives; win-back programmes with tested timing; and post-save retention measurement distinguishing genuine saves from deferrals.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a subscription whose problems are physical rather than financial. It requires: (1) save actions that change the product experience — cadence, quantity, contents, timing — rather than the price, since the underlying problems here are usually about what arrives and when and a discount addresses none of them, which is the central difference from the telecoms playbook; (2) uplift targeting applied to flexibility offers rather than to discounts, so a skip is offered to the customer whose behaviour it will change; (3) the offer matched to an inferred reason rather than a stated one, since the stated reason at the cancel page is unreliable; (4) pause with a defined and attractive return, which the media world uses well and this category implements as an indefinite suspension; and (5) post-save measurement over the following year, since a saved subscriber who cancels two months later at a discount was not saved.

## Target Customer
Retention and product teams, subscription platform vendors, and the subscription retention practitioners in telecoms and media whose playbook transfers with a product-lever substitution.

## Impact If Solved
Decades of save-offer practice exists and this category offers a discount for problems that are about what arrives and when. Substituting product levers for price levers is the central adaptation, and post-save measurement over a year distinguishes a save from a deferral.

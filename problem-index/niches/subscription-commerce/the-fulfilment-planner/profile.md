# The Fulfilment Planner

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/subscription-commerce|Subscription Commerce]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to smooth a demand wave that the business itself created — and whoever does that takes the cost out, because the peak is self-inflicted and the planner absorbing it has no authority over the thing causing it.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$380M US in peak capacity and overtime
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~6% of category operating spend
**Digital Adoption:** None — absorbing a peak somebody created
**Target Buyer:** Operations and fulfilment leadership
**Automation Potential:** Very High — the smoothing is a scheduling decision

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Subscription fulfilment arrives as a wave — everything ships in the same few days — and the planner absorbs a peak they could have smoothed if anyone had let them. The wave exists because billing dates cluster, which is a choice made by whoever designed the sign-up flow and never revisited. The planner staffs for the peak, pays third-party logistics providers peak rates, runs overtime, and carries the risk of missing the window, while the lever that would fix it — spreading the billing and shipping dates across the month — belongs to a product team who have never been told it matters. The planner is accountable for a cost whose cause is outside their control and largely unexamined.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Warehouse management systems, labour scheduling, third-party logistics contracts, and a ship window. The gaps: no smoothing of the cycle across the month; peak cost is not attributed to the billing design that creates it; no capacity model connecting subscriber growth to fulfilment load; substitution and shortfall decisions are made under peak pressure; and the planner has no input into the sign-up and billing design.

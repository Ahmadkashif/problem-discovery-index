# Small Carriers Are Where the Coverage Isn't

**Niche:** [[niches/freight-tech-platforms/truckload-eta-accuracy/profile|Truckload — Coverage and ETA Accuracy]]
**Industry:** [[industries/freight-tech-platforms|Freight Tech Platforms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Visibility platforms report high coverage figures that are driven by large fleets, while the small carriers that hold most of the country's truckload capacity are the least connected — and the aggregate number conceals exactly the gap that matters.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #hypothesis-testing #confidence-intervals #data-integration #automation #worker-facing #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in truckload visibility is fighting to get location from a fragmented carrier base and turn it into an arrival time a dock will plan against — and whoever holds tracked-shipment share and ETA accuracy highest takes the account.

## The Problem
A shipper's visibility coverage is reported at a high percentage, computed across its shipments with the large contract carriers dominating. Its spot market freight, which moves on small carriers and which is the freight most likely to go wrong, is tracked poorly or not at all. The coverage number therefore describes the freight the shipper worries least about. When a spot load is late, the shipper is back to telephone calls — which is precisely the situation the platform was bought to eliminate.

## Why It's Still Broken
Connecting a small carrier requires that carrier to take an action with no benefit to itself: it must connect its telematics provider to a platform that serves its customer, giving a broker or shipper continuous visibility of its trucks, in exchange for nothing. Some carriers reasonably object to the surveillance implication. Platforms have pursued connection through brokers applying pressure, which works unevenly and creates resentment. And the aggregate coverage metric lets everyone avoid confronting the gap, since it reports well.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Measure coverage where it matters and make connection worth something to the carrier. Report coverage segmented by carrier size, by freight type and by contract versus spot, which immediately relocates the conversation to the freight that is actually uncovered. Then address the incentive honestly: a small carrier that connects should receive something concrete — automatic detention documentation as described above, faster payment triggered by verified delivery, reduced check calls, and a verified performance record it can take to other brokers. Give the carrier control over scope, so location is shared for the duration of a load rather than continuously, which removes the legitimate surveillance objection and costs the platform almost nothing. Publish connection rates by segment so that progress is visible rather than asserted.

## Who Feels the Pain
Shippers with a coverage number that does not describe their risky freight; small carriers asked to give continuous visibility for nothing; and drivers, for whom always-on tracking by a customer is a working condition nobody asked them about.

## Impact If Fixed
Load-scoped sharing with concrete carrier benefits is the only connection model that aligns the parties, and it addresses the objection that has kept small-carrier coverage low. Segmented coverage reporting is free and reframes the entire discussion from a flattering aggregate to the specific gap that leaves spot freight untracked.

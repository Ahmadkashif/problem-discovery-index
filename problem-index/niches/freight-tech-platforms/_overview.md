## Why These Niches

Freight's defining current problem is identity. A broker frequently cannot establish who is actually hauling a load, double brokering and authority takeover have escalated sharply, and the losses are large and growing. The vetting layer was built for an era when an authority number and an insurance certificate were sufficient, and the fraud is visible in the relationships between parties rather than in any single carrier's documents — which makes it a network problem and makes the platform, which sees the network, the only party positioned to solve it.

Visibility is the other large block and **failed the filter as one niche**. Truckload visibility is a coverage and ETA accuracy problem solved with telematics and prediction; ocean and intermodal visibility is a milestone reconciliation problem across carriers, terminals and customs with data that arrives late, inconsistently and from parties with no obligation to supply it. Different data, different competitors, different definitions of success. Decomposed below.

Dock scheduling and specialised freight are the two underdigitised operating areas, each with a specific cause: every facility bought a different appointment system and configured it its own way, and oversize freight runs on permits, route restrictions and phone calls. Small carriers and small shippers are the two underserved buyers — most US trucking capacity sits in carriers with fewer than twenty trucks, and most businesses that ship freight ship too little to have a rate, a TMS or any leverage at all.

The two automation niches are the industry's largest labour sinks: the accessorial billing rules that decide whether a detention charge gets paid, and the two hundred cold calls a carrier sales rep makes to cover loads the platform already has the data to match.

## Filter Notes

**Niche 2 failed the filter as stated and was decomposed.** "Freight visibility" describes two products sold under one word. In truckload, the location data exists — every truck has an electronic logging device — and the contest is coverage across a fragmented carrier base and ETA accuracy against a dock appointment. In ocean and intermodal, location is largely irrelevant and the contest is milestone reconciliation: gate-in, loaded, departed, arrived, discharged, customs released, available, picked up — reported by carriers, terminals and customs brokers at different times, in different formats, with different definitions of the same event. Both sub-niches are terminal and are written in full.

**Niches 1, 3–8 are terminal as stated.** Each names a testable capability: fraudulent-carrier detection rate before the load moves, appointment booking without a phone call, permitted route planning time, net-of-cost load valuation for a small carrier, a defensible rate for a shipper with no volume, accessorial first-pass payment rate, and tender acceptance rate on the first carrier contacted.

# Small Shipper Freight Buying

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/freight-tech-platforms|Freight Tech Platforms]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor selling freight to small shippers is fighting to give a business with no volume a rate it can trust and a way to check it — and whoever makes pricing legible to a shipper with no leverage takes the segment.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$410M US software and service spend across businesses shipping small volumes of freight
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~5% of freight technology revenue, against a large and fragmented shipper population
**Digital Adoption:** Low — a relationship with one broker, a telephone, and no visibility into whether the price is fair
**Target Buyer:** Operations, purchasing or finance staff at manufacturers, distributors and e-commerce businesses shipping a few loads a month
**Automation Potential:** High — quoting, mode selection and rate benchmarking are all automatable and are currently a phone call

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A business shipping four truckloads a month has no freight capability and no leverage. It does not have a transportation management system, a rate agreement, a benchmark, or anyone whose job is freight. It calls a broker it has used before, accepts the quoted rate, and has no way to tell whether that rate is reasonable — the market information that large shippers buy is priced for large shippers, and the load boards that carriers use are not built for or available to a business shipping occasionally. The same business frequently ships the wrong mode, moving as truckload what should be less-than-truckload or the reverse, because mode selection requires knowing a rate structure it has never seen. It is a market where one side has complete information and the other has none, and the software industry has served the informed side exclusively.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Digital freight platforms offer instant quotes to shippers of any size, which is genuine progress, and the parcel and LTL space has more self-serve tooling than truckload. Freight marketplaces and small-shipper-focused brokerages exist. Rate benchmarking products are sold to shippers with volume. The gaps for the small shipper: no accessible benchmark against which a quoted rate can be checked, no mode recommendation that accounts for the shipment's actual characteristics and the current rate environment, no assistance with the documentation and classification that determines whether an LTL shipment is reclassified and rebilled at a higher price, and no accumulation of the shipper's own history into anything resembling negotiating position.

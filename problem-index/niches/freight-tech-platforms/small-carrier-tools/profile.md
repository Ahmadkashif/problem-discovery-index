# Small Carrier Tools

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/freight-tech-platforms|Freight Tech Platforms]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor selling to small carriers is fighting to tell an owner what a load is actually worth to them, net of deadhead, fuel and the next load's prospects, before they accept it — and whoever answers that in one screen takes the segment.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$620M US software spend across carriers with 1-20 trucks
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~8% of freight technology revenue, against the majority of US truckload capacity
**Digital Adoption:** Low — load boards, a phone, a factoring company and a spreadsheet
**Target Buyer:** The carrier's owner, who is frequently also a driver and the dispatcher
**Automation Potential:** Very High — load valuation, backhaul planning and compliance are computable and are currently done by instinct

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Most US truckload capacity sits in carriers with fewer than twenty trucks, and a large share of it in carriers with one. These businesses make a decision several times a day that determines whether they survive — accept this load at this rate or wait for a better one — with almost no information. A rate that looks good may be terrible once deadhead to the pickup, fuel at current prices, tolls, and the difficulty of finding a load out of the destination are accounted for. The carrier knows this and estimates it mentally. Meanwhile their costs are opaque to them: many small carriers cannot state their own cost per mile with any precision, which makes every rate decision a guess against an unknown threshold. The software sold into this segment is load boards and factoring, both of which monetise the carrier's weakness rather than addressing it.

## Current Tools & Gaps
DAT and Truckstop dominate load discovery and rate reference; factoring companies provide cash flow at a cost; a fragmented set of small TMS products serve dispatch and compliance; ELD providers supply the mandated device. Rate benchmarking is available as a subscription and reports lane averages rather than what this carrier should accept. The gaps are fundamental: nothing computes the carrier's actual cost per mile from its own fuel, maintenance and payment records; nothing evaluates a load net of deadhead and destination market conditions; nothing plans a sequence of loads rather than one; and the compliance obligations — hours, maintenance, drug and alcohol programme, IFTA — are managed by the owner in the evening across several unconnected systems.

# Compliance Managed in the Evening Across Six Systems

**Niche:** [[niches/freight-tech-platforms/small-carrier-tools/profile|Small Carrier Tools]]
**Industry:** [[industries/freight-tech-platforms|Freight Tech Platforms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A small carrier's regulatory obligations — hours, vehicle inspections, maintenance intervals, drug and alcohol programme, IFTA, insurance and authority renewals — live in six unconnected systems and are managed by the owner after driving all day.
**Tags:** #workflow-orchestration #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #compliance #automation #worker-facing #quick-win #data-integration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor selling to small carriers is fighting to tell an owner what a load is actually worth to them, net of deadhead, fuel and the next load's prospects, before they accept it — and whoever answers that in one screen takes the segment.

## The Problem
An owner-operator finishes driving at eight. Waiting: the ELD portal showing a log discrepancy that needs certifying, a maintenance interval that came due on one truck, a quarterly IFTA filing, a drug and alcohol consortium notification, an insurance certificate that expires next month and a broker who needs a copy, and a biennial authority update. Each is in a different system with a different login. None of them talks to any other. A missed item can put the truck out of service, which for a one-truck carrier is the entire business stopped. This administrative load is one of the most consistently cited reasons small carriers exit, and none of it is difficult — it is simply scattered.

## Why It's Still Broken
Each obligation was digitised separately by a vendor serving that obligation, and none of them has an incentive to consolidate. The carrier is too small to demand integration and too price-sensitive to pay for a platform. And the failure mode is quiet — nothing happens when a deadline is approaching, only when it passes — so there is no forcing event until there is a serious one.

## What a Fix Looks Like
One list, in date order, with everything on it. Obligations are pulled from wherever they live — ELD, maintenance records, consortium, insurance, federal registration — and presented as a single sequence of what is due, when, and what happens if it is not. Where an action can be taken in place, take it: generate the IFTA filing from ELD miles and fuel card gallons, send the insurance certificate to the broker who asked, book the inspection. Where it cannot, link directly to the right screen in the right system rather than to a homepage. Alert early enough to act, and escalate as a deadline approaches, with the consequence stated plainly — out of service, authority revoked, load refused — because the severity is what makes an exhausted owner deal with it tonight rather than next week. None of this requires the vendors to cooperate; most of the data is retrievable with the carrier's own credentials.

## Who Feels the Pain
Owner-operators doing administration after twelve hours of driving; small fleet owners who are also dispatchers, mechanics and bookkeepers; and drivers put out of service by an expired item nobody was watching.

## Impact If Fixed
Consolidation removes the most common cause of unforced business interruption in the segment and returns evening hours to people who have very few. It is also the wedge into a population that has resisted software adoption, because unlike every other product sold to them it solves a problem they experience nightly rather than one they are told they have.

# Document & Accessorial Billing

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/freight-tech-platforms|Freight Tech Platforms]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in freight billing is fighting to get an accessorial charge paid on first submission against a specific shipper's evidence rules — and whoever holds first-pass payment rate highest takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$490M US spend across freight document management, audit and accessorial billing
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~6% of freight technology revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — capture is solved, the rules that decide payment are not encoded
**Target Buyer:** Billing operations leaders at brokerages and carriers; freight audit providers
**Automation Potential:** Very High — the rules are in contracts and the evidence is in systems, and neither is connected

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Document capture from proof of delivery paperwork is a solved commodity — extraction from a photographed bill of lading works well and every provider offers it. What determines whether a carrier or broker actually gets paid for detention, lumper fees, layover, truck ordered not used, or any other accessorial is a different thing entirely: the shipper's own evidence requirements, which are written into a contract or a routing guide and differ by shipper. One requires the detention start time to be signed by facility personnel. Another accepts a gate timestamp. A third requires notification within two hours of the free time expiring, in writing, to a specific address. Charges submitted without the right evidence in the right form within the right window are simply not paid, and a large share of legitimate accessorial revenue is written off for procedural reasons rather than substantive ones. Nobody has encoded the rules.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Document capture and optical extraction are mature and widely deployed. Freight audit and payment providers check invoices against contracted rates competently. Brokerage and carrier TMS platforms handle invoicing. The gaps sit exactly at the accessorial: shipper-specific evidence requirements are not encoded anywhere, so a billing clerk's knowledge of what each customer requires is tacit and inconsistent; notification deadlines are not tracked, so a chargeable event becomes uncollectable while everyone is busy; and nobody measures accessorial write-off by cause, so the industry complains about detention without knowing how much of its own loss is procedural. The evidence — geofence timestamps, photographs, driver messages — generally exists and is not assembled into the form the shipper requires.

# Freight Visibility Platforms

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/freight-tech-platforms|Freight Tech Platforms]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is that every competitor is fighting to tell a shipper where its freight is and when it will arrive — but in truckload that is a coverage and prediction problem and in ocean it is a milestone reconciliation problem, with different data and different competitors. Decomposed below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$2.2B US freight visibility and supply chain execution software
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~28% of freight technology revenue
**Digital Adoption:** High — visibility platforms cover most of the truckload market and a growing share of international freight
**Target Buyer:** Supply chain and logistics leaders at shippers; operations directors at brokerages reselling visibility
**Automation Potential:** High but split — prediction where location is known, reconciliation where events are reported

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Visibility became a category because shippers stopped accepting that they could not tell where their freight was, and it consolidated rapidly into a small number of large platforms. Its commercial promise is simple: know where the freight is, know when it will arrive, and act on the exception. Its technical reality divides sharply by mode, and the division is not cosmetic. Domestic truckload has location data from electronic logging devices on essentially every truck, which makes the problem coverage — persuading a fragmented carrier base to connect — and prediction, since a location is not an arrival time. International ocean has almost no useful continuous location and instead has a sequence of reported milestones from carriers, terminals, customs brokers and drayage providers, each with their own timing, formats and definitions of the same event. Building one product for both produces something that is adequate at neither, which is the observable state of the category.

## Current Tools & Gaps
project44 and FourKites dominate, with the mode-specific specialists and the carrier-side telematics providers filling the edges. Coverage in domestic truckload is genuinely good. The consistent gaps span both modes and appear in different forms: ETAs are presented as point estimates with no uncertainty, which makes them unusable for the decisions they should inform; exception management is an alert list rather than a prioritisation; and nobody publishes accuracy, so a shipper cannot compare platforms on the only thing that matters. The category sells the location and leaves the interpretation to the customer, which is the same gap that recurs throughout this vault.

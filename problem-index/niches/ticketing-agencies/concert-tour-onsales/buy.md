# Airline Revenue Management With a Fan-Price Ceiling

**Niche:** [[niches/ticketing-agencies/concert-tour-onsales/profile|Concert Tour Onsales & Allocation]]
**Industry:** [[industries/ticketing-agencies|Ticketing & Reservation Agencies]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Airlines have priced perishable seats against forecast demand for forty years; a tour has to do it in one morning with a ceiling the artist sets in public.
**Tags:** #time-series-forecasting #bayesian-inference #monte-carlo-methods #optimization-fundamentals #evaluation-metrics #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this sub-niche is fighting to put a tour's tickets into fans' hands at the price the artist chose, against bots and broker account farms, and whoever lets the smallest share of the house reach resale in the first 48 hours takes the account.

## The Problem
Dynamic and platinum pricing were bolted onto ticketing platforms to capture what brokers would otherwise take. They have produced public backlash when prices surged during queues, and the fan-price question is now part of an antitrust remedy.

## What Already Exists
Airline and hotel revenue management systems (PROS, Amadeus, IDeaS) forecast demand by fare class and set booking limits and prices. Cinema circuits face a gentler version of the same question, analysed as [[niches/movie-theaters/ticket-pricing-and-subscriptions/profile|Ticket Pricing & Subscriptions]] under Movie Theaters.

## The Customization Gap
A tour needs: demand forecasting from presale registrations and queue size rather than booking curves; a hard per-section price ceiling and a fan-fairness constraint set by the artist; allocation across presale groups rather than fare classes; a measure of broker capture as an explicit cost; compliance with the FTC all-in display rule and state resale caps; and a decision window of minutes, not weeks.

## Target Customer
Promoters' pricing teams and large artists' management.

## Impact If Solved
Prices set on forecast demand inside a ceiling the artist can defend, with fewer seats left to brokers and fewer surges in public.

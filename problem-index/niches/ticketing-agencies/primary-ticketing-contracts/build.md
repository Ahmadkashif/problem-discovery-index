# A Venue Yield Audit Across Ticketers

**Niche:** [[niches/ticketing-agencies/primary-ticketing-contracts/profile|Primary Ticketing for Venues, Tours & Teams]]
**Industry:** [[industries/ticketing-agencies|Ticketing & Reservation Agencies]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A venue choosing a ticketer cannot measure what its current one actually sells per seat, so contracts are compared on signing money rather than yield.
**Tags:** #causal-inference #gradient-boosting #survival-analysis #evaluation-metrics #data-integration #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is that every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to win a venue's or rights holder's exclusive primary ticketing contract, now that the DOJ settlement caps fees at 15% and opens venues to rival ticketers. A tour onsale and a team's season-ticket base are won on different evidence, by different buyers, on different clocks. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## The Problem
An arena's ticketing contract typically runs five to seven years (estimate). When it comes up, the venue's general manager compares bids on the signing payment, the share of fees paid back per ticket and the technology roadmap. What matters most is how much of the house each ticketer would sell, at what net price, with how much leakage to resale. Nobody can show that. The venue's sales history sits in the incumbent's reports, in the incumbent's format, and the rival's claims are pitch-deck case studies from other buildings.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Until the 2026 settlement, exclusivity and rebates made the comparison moot: the incumbent usually won, and rivals competed on cheques. Ticketers have no reason to build a tool that makes them comparable, and venues have rarely had the analytical staff to build it themselves.

## What to Build
A neutral audit that ingests the venue's own seat-level sales exports over several seasons and produces, per event type, the sell-through curve by section, the net yield per available seat after fees and rebates, unsold and comped inventory, and the resale premium observed on marketplaces. It adjusts for artist, date and market so a rival's results in other buildings can be translated into an expected yield for this one. The audit then becomes the scorecard written into the next contract.

## Target Customer
General managers and chief revenue officers at arenas, amphitheatres and large theatres, and the venue management companies (Legends Global, which absorbed ASM Global in 2024, and Oak View Group) that negotiate for dozens of buildings.

## Impact If Built
Contracts priced on evidence of yield rather than signing payments. Even one or two points of yield on a $50M-a-year building is worth more than most signing cheques (estimate).

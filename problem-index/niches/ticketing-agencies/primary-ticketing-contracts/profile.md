# Primary Ticketing for Venues, Tours & Teams

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/ticketing-agencies|Ticketing & Reservation Agencies]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is that every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to win a venue's or rights holder's exclusive primary ticketing contract, now that the DOJ settlement caps fees at 15% and opens venues to rival ticketers. A tour onsale and a team's season-ticket base are won on different evidence, by different buyers, on different clocks. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$5B of US service-fee revenue retained by primary ticketing companies (estimate; Ticketmaster's global Ticketing segment revenue was ~$3.1B in 2025 on $37.1B of fee-bearing transactions)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~20% of the industry's ~$25.0B receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** High. Every sale, scan and transfer is digital; the contract economics and the onsale decisions are not
**Target Buyer:** Venue general manager or chief revenue officer, promoter's head of ticketing, team's VP of ticketing
**Automation Potential:** High for setup, pricing support and reconciliation; low for the contract negotiation

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Primary ticketing is the selling of first-issue tickets as agent for the venue, promoter or team under multi-year exclusive contracts. Ticketers have historically won those contracts with signing payments and a share of the per-ticket fees paid back to the venue. The April 2026 jury verdict for 33 states and D.C., and the DOJ settlement's 15% fee cap and open-venue terms, put that model under review in the remedies phase running into 2027. Competitors are Ticketmaster, AXS, SeatGeek, Paciolan, Etix, Eventbrite and Tixr. Once fee rebates are capped, contracts will have to be won on what a ticketer actually sells. But "what it sells" means fan allocation against bots for a tour and season-ticket renewals for a team, which is why the niche splits.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Ticketers' own client portals and sales reports; venue accounting systems; consultants who run ticketing RFPs. Gaps: no neutral measure of yield per seat that venues can compare across ticketers; rebate and fee-split clauses reconciled by hand; and the venue's own sales history locked in the incumbent's reporting.

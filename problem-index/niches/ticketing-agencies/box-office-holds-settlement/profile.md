# Box Office Holds, Comps & Show Settlement

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/ticketing-agencies|Ticketing & Reservation Agencies]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to close a show's settlement with a hold, comp and kill record that the promoter, the artist's tour accountant and the venue all accept, and whoever produces a signed settlement fastest with the fewest disputed seats takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$600M of box office labour, settlement tools and ticketing-system box office modules at US venues (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~2.4% of industry receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low. Seat status is in the ticketing system, but who promised which held seat to whom is in spreadsheets, email and text messages
**Target Buyer:** Venue director of ticketing or box office manager; promoter's settlement and finance lead
**Automation Potential:** High for reconciliation and statement drafting; release decisions stay with the treasurer

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Every show at an arena, amphitheatre or theatre ends with a settlement: the box office statement of tickets sold by price level, comps by category, fees, taxes and facility charges, signed by the venue, the promoter and the artist's representative. It decides how the night's money is split. Behind it is a day of hold management. Artist, promoter, production, sponsor, ADA and presale holds are released, swapped or comped up to show time. Treasurers and ticket sellers, often unionised at large venues, do this work. The data trail is the weakest link in the industry.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Box office modules in Ticketmaster, AXS, Etix and Paciolan; promoter settlement software such as Prism.fm; tour accounting in Master Tour and spreadsheets. Gaps: no hold ledger with purpose, owner and release deadline; comp requests arriving by text; and settlement discrepancies traced seat by seat by hand.

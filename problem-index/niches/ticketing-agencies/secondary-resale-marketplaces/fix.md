# Speculative Listings Sold as Real Seats

**Niche:** [[niches/ticketing-agencies/secondary-resale-marketplaces/profile|Secondary Resale Marketplaces]]
**Industry:** [[industries/ticketing-agencies|Ticketing & Reservation Agencies]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Sellers list seats they do not yet hold before the onsale, and the buyer finds out when the seller cannot buy them.
**Tags:** #logistic-regression #large-language-models #evaluation-metrics #compliance #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to guarantee that every listed seat is real, deliverable and lawfully priced in the venue's state, and whoever settles the fewest failed orders, speculative-listing complaints and chargebacks per thousand tickets takes the account.

## The Problem
Before a big onsale, resale sites can show listings for seats the seller expects to buy. Some are labelled; many look like real tickets. When the seller fails to get them, the order is cancelled or filled with worse seats, and the buyer blames the marketplace.

## Why It's Still Broken
Speculative inventory is profitable supply in a sold-out market, policies differ by marketplace, and laws differ by state: Vermont bans it from July 2026, others allow it with disclosure. Without proof of possession, a marketplace cannot tell a speculative listing from a held seat.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Require proof of possession or a clear speculative label before an onsale; block speculative listings where the venue's state bans them; and flag listings that appear before the onsale or with seat details that do not match the venue map.

## Who Feels the Pain
Buyers, marketplace support teams and the honest brokers whose real inventory competes with phantom seats.

## Impact If Fixed
Fewer cancelled orders on the biggest shows, and compliance with state bans built into listing rather than policed after complaints.

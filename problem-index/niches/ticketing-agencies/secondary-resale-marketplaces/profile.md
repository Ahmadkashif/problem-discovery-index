# Secondary Resale Marketplaces

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/ticketing-agencies|Ticketing & Reservation Agencies]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to guarantee that every listed seat is real, deliverable and lawfully priced in the venue's state, and whoever settles the fewest failed orders, speculative-listing complaints and chargebacks per thousand tickets takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$2.8B of US resale marketplace take (estimate; StubHub reported ~$1.7B revenue on ~$9.2B of gross merchandise sales in 2025, Vivid Seats $571M on ~$2.7B)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~11% of industry receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Very high for listing and checkout; delivery and dispute handling still involve manual transfers and calls
**Target Buyer:** Marketplace chief operating officer or head of trust and safety; on the supply side, the owners of large broker operations
**Automation Potential:** High for listing checks, delivery tracking and dispute handling

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Resale marketplaces sell tickets they do not own on behalf of brokers and fans, backed by a buyer guarantee. StubHub, Vivid Seats, SeatGeek, TickPick, Gametime and Ticketmaster's own resale compete for the same buyers and the same broker inventory. Their growth has diverged: StubHub's sales grew 6% in 2025 while Vivid Seats' fell 31%. Rules are tightening around them. The FTC fees rule requires all-in prices on resale, Vermont caps resale at 110% of face from July 2026, North Carolina restricts speculative listings, and the FTC's 2025 complaint targets the account farms that feed resale supply. The buyer comes back to the marketplace that got them in the door; the large broker lists where payouts are reliable and penalties fair.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Marketplace listing systems and broker APIs, broker POS tools (SkyBox, TradeDesk, Automatiq), seller-risk and payment-fraud tools. Gaps: speculative listings hard to tell from held inventory, delivery risk not scored per listing, and state cap rules applied by policy page rather than per listing.

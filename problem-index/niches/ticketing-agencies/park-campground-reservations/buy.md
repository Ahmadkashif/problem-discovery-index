# Onsale Queue Defences for Campsite Release

**Niche:** [[niches/ticketing-agencies/park-campground-reservations/profile|Public Park & Campground Reservations]]
**Industry:** [[industries/ticketing-agencies|Ticketing & Reservation Agencies]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Concert ticketing already runs waiting rooms and bot defences for releases that sell out in minutes; a campground release has the same shape and far less protection.
**Tags:** #gradient-boosting #logistic-regression #evaluation-metrics #compliance #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to allocate scarce campsites, permits and timed entries so that people rather than bots and cancellation scrapers get them, and whoever can show the agency the lowest share of bookings captured by automated tools takes the account.

## The Problem
Daily release windows at popular parks behave like concert onsales: thousands of users and scripts arrive at the same second for a few hundred sites.

## What Already Exists
Virtual waiting rooms and bot-management services (Queue-it, Akamai, HUMAN, Cloudflare) randomise queue order, detect automation and enforce limits for ticketing and retail drops.

## The Customization Gap
Parks need: release windows every day rather than once per show; multi-night stays and site attributes in the booking logic; account rules that must satisfy public-sector accessibility and equity requirements; vendor contracts that pay per reservation and may not reward slowing bots; and reporting that agencies can publish.

## Target Customer
Agency reservation programme managers and the reservation-system vendors bidding for their contracts.

## Impact If Solved
Peak-site releases decided by a fair queue rather than by the fastest script.

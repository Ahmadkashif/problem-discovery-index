# Brokers Posing as Season-Ticket Holders

**Niche:** [[niches/ticketing-agencies/season-ticket-renewals/profile|Season-Ticket & Membership Ticketing]]
**Industry:** [[industries/ticketing-agencies|Ticketing & Reservation Agencies]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Teams count brokers who resell almost every game as loyal members, and price, seat and service them as fans.
**Tags:** #k-means-clustering #logistic-regression #feature-engineering #evaluation-metrics #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this sub-niche is fighting to tell a team which season-ticket members will not renew while there is still time to save them, and whoever's renewal forecast holds up at the renewal deadline takes the account.

## The Problem
Some season-ticket accounts are resale businesses holding several accounts under different names. Their seats rarely scan under the holder's name and appear on marketplaces within days of release. Teams set prices, upgrade priority and service effort as if these were fans. Some teams welcome brokers and others don't, but few know which accounts they are.

## Why It's Still Broken
The evidence is split. Scans and transfers are in the ticketing system and listings are on marketplaces, and nobody joins the two at account level. Some teams also tolerate brokers because they renew reliably.

## What a Fix Looks Like
An account-level behaviour profile (scan rate, transfer recipients, resale share and timing, linked accounts) that classifies each account as fan, sharer or reseller, so the team can decide its policy on purpose.

## Who Feels the Pain
Ticket service reps, pricing managers and the fans on upgrade waitlists behind broker accounts.

## Impact If Fixed
Teams know what share of their base is resale inventory, which changes how they price, allocate upgrades and forecast renewals.

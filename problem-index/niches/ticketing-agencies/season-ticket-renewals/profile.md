# Season-Ticket & Membership Ticketing

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/ticketing-agencies|Ticketing & Reservation Agencies]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this sub-niche is fighting to tell a team which season-ticket members will not renew while there is still time to save them, and whoever's renewal forecast holds up at the renewal deadline takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.2B of primary ticketing fees and ticketing-CRM spend at US professional teams and college athletics departments (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~5% of industry receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** High. Scans, transfers and resale are all digital; renewal work is still a sales team calling down a list
**Target Buyer:** VP of ticketing or chief revenue officer at a team; associate athletic director for ticketing at a university
**Automation Potential:** High for risk scoring and outreach timing; retention conversations stay human

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A team's ticket revenue rests on season-ticket and membership holders who commit before the season and renew each year against a deadline. Ticketers here sell to the team, not to promoters: Ticketmaster, SeatGeek (which holds several NFL and NBA contracts) and Paciolan, which powers 160+ college athletics programmes. CRM and analytics vendors such as KORE Software and StellarAlgo sit on top. The account is won by protecting the renewal base. A member who stops attending, transfers every game or resells most of their seats is either about to leave or is a broker. That shows in scan and resale data months before the deadline, but few teams act on it early enough.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Ticketing platform account managers, team CRMs (Salesforce, Microsoft Dynamics via KORE), renewal campaigns run by inside sales. Gaps: renewal risk scored late or by gut; brokers in the base unidentified; and resale behaviour visible on the marketplace but not joined to the account.

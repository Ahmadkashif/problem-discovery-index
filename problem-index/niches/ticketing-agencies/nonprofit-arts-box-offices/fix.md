# One Patron, Three Records

**Niche:** [[niches/ticketing-agencies/nonprofit-arts-box-offices/profile|Nonprofit Arts & Small-Venue Box Offices]]
**Industry:** [[industries/ticketing-agencies|Ticketing & Reservation Agencies]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The same patron appears as a ticket buyer, a donor and an email subscriber under three spellings, so the organisation thanks, asks and markets to them as strangers.
**Tags:** #k-nearest-neighbors #word-embeddings #evaluation-metrics #data-integration #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn a first-time single-ticket buyer into a returning subscriber or donor using only the box office's own records, and whoever lifts the second-visit rate for a box office of two people takes the account.

## The Problem
Tickets bought by one spouse, gifts made by the other, a box office walk-up recorded under a nickname, an old email address: small arts databases fill with duplicates. A loyal subscriber gets a "first-time visitor" email and a donor solicitation that ignores last month's gift.

## Why It's Still Broken
Merging records is manual and risky, and a two-person box office never has the hour a week it takes. Built-in duplicate tools match only exact fields.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Fuzzy matching across names, addresses, emails, phone numbers and household links, with confident merges applied automatically and uncertain ones queued for a one-click review.

## Who Feels the Pain
Box office and development staff, and the patrons who feel unrecognised.

## Impact If Fixed
A clean patron list makes every other tool, from second-visit offers to donor asks, work as intended.

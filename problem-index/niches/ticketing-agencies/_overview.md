# Niche Analysis — Ticketing & Reservation Agencies

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/ticketing-agencies|Ticketing & Reservation Agencies]]

## Niche Selection

A ticketing or reservation agency sells a perishable, numbered unit, such as a seat, a week or a campsite, on behalf of the venue, promoter, team, resort or park that owns it. Its core judgement is how to price, hold and release that inventory before the market opens, and the market grades the judgement within hours. Around that sit an onsale fought against bots and account farms, a resale market now capped and policed by state and federal rules, and the refunds and disputes that follow every cancellation. The eight niches below split the industry by what ticketers, marketplaces, reservation contractors and their tool vendors actually compete over. Market sizes are estimates of the fees retained or the spend that turns on each niche (staff, software, services and losses), not of ticket face value. The whole code reported ~$25.0B of receipts in 2022.

## Why These Niches

Primary ticketing and resale take the two high-share slots because between them they hold most of the industry's fee revenue. Ticketmaster alone sold 346M fee-bearing tickets in 2025, and StubHub and Vivid Seats together resold ~$11.9B. Both are being reshaped at once by the April 2026 antitrust verdict, the DOJ settlement's 15% fee cap and the FTC's all-in pricing rule. The two low-digitized niches are where spreadsheets and phones still carry the record. The box office reconciles holds and comps by hand at every show. Timeshare exchanges, named in the NAICS code, still serve an older membership by phone and value deposits by formulas members never see. The two underserved audiences are the small arts organisation, whose box office is also its donor office and whose problem is first-time buyers who never return, and the occasional camper competing with scripts for public campsites. The two automatable niches are the most mechanical work in the industry: resolving many buyer accounts into one broker at the onsale, and applying refund policy to every order when a show moves.

## Filter Notes

Seven of the eight level-1 niches are terminal. Primary Ticketing for Venues, Tours & Teams is not. Its partial sentence, winning the exclusive ticketing contract now that fee rebates are capped, hides two contests with different buyers and winners. For a promoter-controlled tour, the promoter and artist choose the ticketer, and the contest is getting tickets to fans at the artist's price before brokers relist them; the winner is whoever leaks the smallest share of the house to resale in the first 48 hours. For a team or college programme, the team chooses, and the contest is the season-ticket base; the winner is whoever's renewal forecast holds up at the renewal deadline. Different data, clocks and competitors, so it decomposes into **Concert Tour Onsales & Allocation** and **Season-Ticket & Membership Ticketing**.

Five terminal calls were close. **Secondary Resale Marketplaces** and **Onsale Bot & Purchase-Limit Enforcement** both deal with brokers. They are kept apart because the marketplace's contest is the buyer guarantee after the sale, while enforcement is an identity problem at the onsale, bought by the primary ticketer. **Box Office Holds, Comps & Show Settlement** borders Concert Tour Onsales, since holds are set at scaling. It is kept separate because the settlement contest is the night's reconciliation, bought by the venue's box office rather than the promoter. It also borders the queued Event Venues industry, which will own the venue's in-house box office staff; this niche covers the outsourced ticket offices the NAICS code names and the ticketing systems and settlement tools that compete for the box office's account. **Season-Ticket & Membership Ticketing** borders the queued Sports Teams & Clubs industry in the same way: the team's own sales staff belong there, and the ticketers and CRM vendors competing for the team's account belong here. **Nonprofit Arts & Small-Venue Box Offices** borders the queued Performing Arts Companies industry; it stays here because the contest is the ticketing and patron system's job, while programming and fundraising belong to that industry when it is built. **Public Park & Campground Reservations** borders the queued RV Parks & Campgrounds industry; private campground booking belongs there, and the public agencies' contracted reservation services belong here.

Six candidates were considered and rejected. **GDS and airline reservation services and hotel bedbanks** fall inside the code's reservation-services examples, but their work is distribution to travel agencies and tour operators. The GDSs' data business is logged as [[niches/travel-agencies/gds-marketing-data/profile|🔍 GDS Marketing Information Data]] (39) and the bedbanks as [[niches/tour-operators/accommodation-wholesalers-bedbanks/profile|🔍 Accommodation Wholesalers & Bedbanks]] (29), so neither is profiled again here. **Convention and group housing reservations** are in the code, but the housing companies are already logged as [[niches/visitors-bureaus/convention-housing-bureaus/profile|🔍 Convention Housing Bureaus]] (36, kill switch), and the pickup contest they serve is decided in [[niches/visitors-bureaus/citywide-convention-bids/profile|Citywide Convention Bids & Incentives]]; a niche here would duplicate both. **Restaurant reservation services** such as OpenTable, Resy and Tock are named in the code, but their contest, no-shows and table yield, belongs to the restaurant and is covered from that side by [[niches/independent-restaurants/reservation-heavy-upscale/profile|Reservation-Heavy Upscale Independents]]. **Motor-club road and travel services** (AAA clubs' travel desks) are in the code, but the travel desk does agency work covered by [[industries/travel-agencies|Travel Agencies]], and the membership's contest is roadside assistance, not reservations. **Air, bus and cruise ticket offices** not run by carriers settle through ARC and are analysed as [[niches/travel-agencies/ticketing-exchanges-settlement/profile|Air Ticketing, Exchanges & ARC Settlement]]. **Small independent brokers as an underserved audience** produced only a generic sentence ("better pricing tools"); their real contest, delivery and inventory sync across marketplaces, is covered by Secondary Resale Marketplaces and the industry's second low-impact problem.

## Why These Pockets

No pocket qualifies, and the operators fail first. The industry's central label is split between two kinds of company that each employ large data teams and sell neither the analysis nor the data. The primary ticketers hold the scaling and hold decisions and every seat's sale, but they sell as agent, so the records are shared with the venue, promoter or team (37, kill switch), and one firm dominates. The resale marketplaces hold the truest price of each seat and own it (37), but they sell a take rate, and there are a handful of them. That is the same shape as [[niches/tour-operators/experience-marketplace-data-science/profile|🔍 Experience Marketplace Data Science]] (37) and the movie ticketing aggregators [[niches/movie-theaters/online-ticketing-aggregators/profile|🔍 Online Ticketing Aggregators]] (30). Several pockets this industry touches are already logged elsewhere. The economists retained in the Live Nation case are the same firms as [[niches/investment-banking-boutiques/antitrust-economic-consulting/profile|🔍 Antitrust Economic Consulting]] (46, kill switch). Touring demand comes from [[niches/music-publishers/music-consumption-data-providers/profile|🔍 Music Consumption Data Providers]] (43), and event registration and sourcing data from [[niches/event-planning/event-technology-platform-data/profile|🔍 Event Technology Platform Data Teams]] (46).

The only indexed work that reaches this industry sits with investors. Panels that estimate ticket sell-through and resale volume ahead of Live Nation's, StubHub's and Vivid Seats' earnings belong to [[niches/hedge-funds/alt-data-kpi-research-providers/profile|🔍 Alternative Data KPI Research Providers]] (56) and [[niches/data-analytics-consultants/alternative-data-research-providers/profile|🔍 Alternative Data Research Providers]] (56). They qualify where nothing here does because a reported quarter grades them on a date nobody controls. Inside the industry, the data that would grade a ticketing director sits with intermediaries that are not allowed to pool it. The fan-data consultancies (36), broker point-of-sale vendors (34) and park reservation contractors (27) all work on records their clients own. The fraud and chargeback vendors (38) are the exception: merchants supply data on terms that give the vendor product rights, so no switch is live, but the invoice is the fraud decision rather than the analysis. The convention housing companies repeat the pattern under Convention & Visitors Bureaus as [[niches/visitors-bureaus/convention-housing-bureaus/profile|🔍 Convention Housing Bureaus]] (36, kill switch).

The best clean near-misses are the fraud and chargeback vendors (38) and the resale marketplaces' own data science (37). Both own deep data that is checked quickly against outcomes. Neither sells analysis as the invoice, and each is a handful of firms. Neither is a buyer for a research engine. The purest insight-as-invoice work, the independent pricing and ticketing-RFP advisors, is done by firms too small to pass the gate. The empty position is a graded record of onsale decisions. Nobody sells it, and the opening it leaves is software sold to the promoters, teams and venues that own the decision, described in the Pass 1 niches above, not an insight-engine sale.

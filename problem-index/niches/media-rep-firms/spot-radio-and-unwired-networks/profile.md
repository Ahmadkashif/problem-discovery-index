# Spot Radio & Unwired Network Sales

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/media-rep-firms|Media Representative Firms]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to package hundreds of small radio stations into one buy an agency can place, verify and pay as a single line item, and whoever makes a 200-station radio buy as easy as one network order takes the national radio dollar.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$100M in rep commission on national spot radio and rep-built radio networks (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~2% of NAICS 541840 receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — avails and confirmations by email and spreadsheet; affidavits assembled station by station
**Target Buyer:** President of the rep's radio group; network sales leadership
**Automation Potential:** High for packaging, clearance tracking and verification

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Katz represents more than 3,400 radio stations outside iHeartMedia, many in small markets with a few thousand listeners each. A national advertiser buying radio either buys spot market by market, which is slow, or buys an "unwired network": a rep-assembled bundle of stations sold as one product with a combined audience. The rep's job is to make many small, separately trafficked stations behave like one buyable unit. That means one proposal, one order, one proof of performance and one invoice. National spot radio demand has weakened; iHeart's filing ties its rep segment's decline partly to lower political revenue, and third-party commentary blames soft national spot radio (estimate).

## Current Tools & Gaps
Radio traffic systems (WideOrbit, Marketron, RCS), Nielsen Audio ratings, Mediaocean on the agency side, Hudson MX for local radio buying, and Media Monitors for airplay verification. The gaps: clearance confirmations collected by email from each station; affidavits that arrive late or in different formats; and makegoods for un-cleared spots negotiated per station after the flight.

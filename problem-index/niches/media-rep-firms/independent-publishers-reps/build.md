# A Pipeline Window Each Publisher Can Open

**Niche:** [[niches/media-rep-firms/independent-publishers-reps/profile|Independent Publishers' Representatives]]
**Industry:** [[industries/media-rep-firms|Media Representative Firms]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A publishers' rep sells for five titles from one inbox, and each publisher sees only the revenue that eventually arrives, so the rep is judged on results it can't contextualise.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #bert #logistic-regression #workflow-orchestration #data-integration #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to show each publisher a live, account-level pipeline for its titles (who was pitched, what is pending, what renewed and why not), and whoever makes a commission rep as visible as an in-house salesperson keeps the title when the publisher reviews its reps.

## The Problem
When a publisher's ad revenue in a territory dips, it asks the rep what happened. The answer is a phone call and a hand-built spreadsheet of accounts pitched and pending. The publisher compares that with its in-house team's CRM dashboard and draws conclusions about the rep that may be unfair.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Rep firms are too small to build software, and CRM vendors sell seats to one company's salespeople, not to a firm selling for several principals who must each see only their own accounts.

## What to Build
A rep-side pipeline built from the rep's email and calendar. A language model classifies threads by publisher, account and stage (pitched, proposal sent, verbal, IO signed, renewed, lost with reason) and asks for confirmation in one click. Each publisher gets a partitioned portal that shows only its titles. A renewal-risk score per account draws on prior years' insertion patterns.

## Target Customer
Principals of independent publishers' rep firms with three or more titles; B2B publishers who rely on outside reps in some territories.

## Impact If Built
The rep can show the work behind the number, and the publisher can see a territory's pipeline as clearly as an in-house team's. That is the contest at every rep review.

# Candidate Orders & Lowest-Unit-Charge Quoting

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/media-rep-firms|Media Representative Firms]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to quote every candidate order at each station's true lowest unit charge for the class, and turn the disclosure paperwork around in hours, inside the 45- and 60-day windows, and whoever does it without a rebate claim or a political-file gap gets the national political buyer's next order.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$40M in rep commission on candidate orders in an election year (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~1% averaged over the cycle (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — LUC is computed in spreadsheets from rate history pulled station by station
**Target Buyer:** Head of political sales; station group political compliance lead
**Automation Potential:** High for the calculation and paperwork, low for legal judgement calls

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Under 47 CFR 73.1942, a legally qualified candidate pays no more than the station's lowest unit charge for the same class and amount of time during the 45 days before a primary and the 60 days before a general election. Every class, value-enhancing practice and preemption likelihood must be disclosed, and the station must negotiate in good faith. The station is the licensee and carries the liability. The rep is the conduit for national candidate buys, quoting on the station's behalf from rate history it has to assemble. The contest is precision and speed under a statutory clock, not price.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Political modules in station traffic systems, communications counsel's guidance memos, disclosure forms and spreadsheets. The gaps: LUC is recomputed by hand as commercial rates move during the window; the calculation behind a quote is rarely saved; and paperwork status is tracked by email.

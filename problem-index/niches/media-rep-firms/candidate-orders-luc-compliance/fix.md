# The Rebate Claim Nobody Can Answer

**Niche:** [[niches/media-rep-firms/candidate-orders-luc-compliance/profile|Candidate Orders & Lowest-Unit-Charge Quoting]]
**Industry:** [[industries/media-rep-firms|Media Representative Firms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A campaign asks months after the election whether it got the lowest unit charge, and the rep's quote, the station's rate history and the cleared spots have to be pieced together from three places.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #compliance #workflow-orchestration #data-integration #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to quote every candidate order at each station's true lowest unit charge for the class, and turn the disclosure paperwork around in hours, inside the 45- and 60-day windows, and whoever does it without a rebate claim or a political-file gap gets the national political buyer's next order.

## The Problem
Campaign auditors and political buyers review what they paid after the election. A question about one station's rates in one week requires the quote the rep sent, the station's commercial clearances in that class and week, and the candidate's as-run log. Each sits in a different system or inbox.

## Why It's Still Broken
Quotes are emails, not records. Nothing ties a quote to the rate history it was computed from, and political staff turn over between cycles.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Save every candidate quote as a structured record with the rate snapshot behind it and link it to the as-run log, so any question resolves to one view. Even without a full LUC engine, a quote archive with its evidence answers most claims.

## Who Feels the Pain
Rep political coordinators fielding claims in the off-year; station business offices; counsel billing hours to reconstruct.

## Impact If Fixed
Claims close in minutes, and the record itself discourages speculative ones.

**One-liner:** Regulated utilities and telecoms already run tariff-compliance tools that prove every customer was billed the right regulated rate; the lowest-unit-charge rule is a tariff that changes daily.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #hypothesis-testing #compliance #data-integration #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to quote every candidate order at each station's true lowest unit charge for the class, and turn the disclosure paperwork around in hours, inside the 45- and 60-day windows, and whoever does it without a rebate claim or a political-file gap gets the national political buyer's next order.

## The Problem
A rep needs to show, after the election, that each candidate paid no more than the lowest unit charge for each class at each station. The evidence is spread across traffic systems and email.

## What Already Exists
Billing-assurance and tariff-compliance tools used by utilities and telecom carriers compare every invoice line to the applicable regulated rate, flag exceptions, and keep an audit trail for regulators.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs several changes. The "tariff" must be derived dynamically from commercial clearances by class and period rather than read from a filed schedule. It must handle window dates per race and per primary or general. Preemptible classes have to be accounted for, since their effective rate depends on whether spots cleared. And the output must take the form of a candidate-by-candidate rebate test across many stations.

## Target Customer
Political compliance leads at rep firms and station groups, and the communications counsel who advise them.

## Impact If Solved
An after-the-fact rebate audit becomes a running check, and disputes are answered with evidence rather than reconstruction.

# Political Advertising Sales

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/media-rep-firms|Media Representative Firms]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is that every competitor is fighting to capture national political dollars for its stations in the final weeks before an election. But candidate orders are a compliance contest, won by quoting each station's lowest unit charge correctly and moving disclosure paperwork in hours. Issue and PAC orders are a pricing contest over scarce inventory, won by forecasting demand. The two have different inputs, clocks and winners. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$100M in rep commission in an election year, a fraction of that in odd years (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~2% averaged over the two-year cycle (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — orders, disclosure forms and rate quotes move by email and PDF under deadline
**Target Buyer:** Head of political sales at the rep firm; station group political director
**Automation Potential:** High for paperwork and quoting, medium for pricing

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Political advertising reached a record $11.1B in the 2024 cycle, $5.3B of it on broadcast TV. National political media buyers place much of it through the reps' political units. Katz's segment revenue fell from $327.1M in 2024 to $272.5M in 2025, mostly for lack of an election. Two regimes run side by side. Legally qualified candidates are entitled to the lowest unit charge for the class during the 45 days before a primary and 60 days before a general election, with every class and rate disclosed. Issue groups and PACs pay what the market bears. Both press on the same scarce inventory in the same weeks.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Station traffic systems with political modules, NAB disclosure forms, the FCC's online political file, AdImpact spending data and spreadsheets for LUC calculations. The gaps differ by regime, which is why the niche decomposes: candidate orders lack a verifiable LUC calculation and a fast paperwork loop, and issue orders lack any demand forecast.

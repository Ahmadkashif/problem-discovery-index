# Issue & PAC Inventory Pricing

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/media-rep-firms|Media Representative Firms]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to price non-candidate issue and PAC spots as close as possible to what the market will clear in the final weeks before an election, while deciding which regular advertisers to displace, and whoever forecasts that demand best books the most political revenue per spot.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$60M in rep commission on issue and PAC orders in an election year (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~1% averaged over the cycle (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — rates are set by phone and email from the desk's sense of demand
**Target Buyer:** Head of political sales; station group revenue and pricing leads
**Automation Potential:** Medium — demand forecasting is tractable, the final call stays human

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Non-candidate buyers (super PACs, party committees' independent expenditures, issue advocacy groups and ballot-measure campaigns) are not entitled to the lowest unit charge. They pay what the market bears, and in a contested market in late October that can be many times the regular rate (estimate). The rep's political desk must decide, station by station and week by week, how high to price, how much inventory to hold back for late money, and which regular advertisers to bump. Its inputs are race competitiveness, fundraising, prior cycles and bookings to date, not rate history.

## Current Tools & Gaps
AdImpact's spending tracking and projections, station pacing reports, and the desk's experience of past cycles. The gaps: no station-week demand forecast, no record of what past issue rates cleared against what demand, and no link between pricing and displacement cost.

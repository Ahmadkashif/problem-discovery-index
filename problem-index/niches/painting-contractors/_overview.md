## Why These Niches

Painting contracting fragments along setting (interior vs. exterior vs. commercial vs. industrial), project complexity (residential repaint vs. protective coatings), business scale (solo painter vs. 20-person crew), and customer type (homeowners vs. property managers vs. facility managers). These 8 niches cover the two largest revenue segments (residential interior and exterior), the two most digitally neglected (commercial painting with after-hours scheduling complexity and specialty/industrial with coating specification requirements), the two most underserved by existing tools (solo painters who find PaintScout too complex and property management turn painters who need batch scheduling), and the two highest-ROI automation targets (surface area estimation from photos and AI color consultation). Excluded: automotive painting (distinct industry), marine/aerospace coatings (specialized manufacturing), and artistic/decorative painting (craft business, not contracting).

## Why These Pockets

One pocket qualified, and it is not in residential painting at all. Residential repainting — the largest segment and the one Pass 1 describes — has no insight layer above it worth the name: the lead marketplaces sell leads, the franchisors benchmark their own franchisees, and the manufacturers give technical advice away to win specifications. The two strongest data businesses touching the trade, construction cost publishers and project lead data, are already indexed under cleaning companies and electrical contractors respectively.

The qualifier sits in industrial and infrastructure coatings, where the asset being painted is a bridge or a water tank and the decision is when to spend millions recoating it. Protective coatings consultancies write the condition assessments and specifications those decisions run on, and they hold something genuinely singular: forty years of field observations of how real coating systems performed on real structures under known exposure. That record does not exist anywhere else — not at the manufacturers, whose durability data comes from accelerated laboratory chambers that correlate poorly with the field, and not at the owners, who see only their own assets. The industry quotes service life as a convention and the only party with the evidence to test it has never assembled it.

The defects underneath are the familiar three. Condition ratings — the numbers the whole recommendation rests on — are assigned by eye against coarse reference photographs, with inspector-to-inspector variance that is never measured and propagates straight into capital plans, while the survey photographs that could measure it are filed as evidence and used for nothing. And the remaining-life estimate, which is the actual deliverable, is never scored against what the next survey found, even on repeat-cycle clients where the firm holds both the prediction and the outcome.

Two pockets fail the gate outright, and one of them is instructive: painting takeoff and estimating bureaus are the same shape as the electrical estimating service bureaus that qualified at 50, but they are three-person shops. The same business at a tenth of the scale is not the same business.

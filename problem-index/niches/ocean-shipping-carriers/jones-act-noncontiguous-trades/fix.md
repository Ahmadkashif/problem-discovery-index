# Storm-Season Schedule Changes Announced by Email

**Niche:** [[niches/ocean-shipping-carriers/jones-act-noncontiguous-trades/profile|Jones Act Noncontiguous Liner Trades]]
**Industry:** [[industries/ocean-shipping-carriers|Ocean Shipping Carriers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** When a hurricane or winter storm moves a sailing, every customer gets the same generic notice and then calls to ask about their own cargo.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #worker-facing #automation #quick-win #workflow-orchestration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to deliver an island's weekly freight on the day the island plans around, and whoever lands the most containers on the scheduled arrival day takes the account.

## The Problem
A storm closes a port or diverts a ship. The carrier emails a blanket advisory; hundreds of customers call to ask whether their reefer, their store's replenishment or their vehicle is on that ship and when it will arrive.

## Why It's Still Broken
Advisories are written per voyage, not per customer, and the booking-level consequences are worked out by hand.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Translate each schedule change into a per-customer notice listing their containers, the new expected availability and any action needed, and answer follow-up questions from the same plan.

## Who Feels the Pain
Customer service teams during storm season and island shippers trying to protect perishable cargo.

## Impact If Fixed
In a storm week, per-customer notices could remove ~1,000–3,000 inbound calls at ~5 minutes each, ~80–250 agent hours (estimate). Customers also decide earlier and less cargo spoils.

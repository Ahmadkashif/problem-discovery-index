# Emissions Monitoring, Reporting & Verification

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/ocean-shipping-carriers|Ocean Shipping Carriers]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn a fleet's noon reports and fuel records into emissions figures a verifier accepts before 31 March, and whoever gets the most ships' reports verified with no corrections takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$600M global spend on emissions monitoring, reporting and verification software, verifier fees and fleet staff time for the EU MRV/ETS, FuelEU Maritime and IMO DCS regimes (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~3% of carrier shore-side spend (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — the source data is a daily noon report typed on board, bunker delivery notes on paper and fuel figures in spreadsheets
**Target Buyer:** Head of Decarbonisation / Fleet Performance Manager at an owner, operator or ship manager
**Automation Potential:** High — aggregation, reconciliation and report generation are rule-bound; monitoring-plan choices need people

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Emissions data now carries money and deadlines. Ships of 5,000 GT and above trading to EU ports must have emissions reports verified by 31 March and surrender EU ETS allowances by 30 September, covering 70% of 2025 emissions and 100% from 2026, with 100% of intra-EU and 50% of extra-EU voyages counted (European Commission). FuelEU Maritime added a GHG-intensity limit from 2025: 2% below a 91.16 gCO2e/MJ reference, with a €2,400 per tonne VLSFO-equivalent penalty, a 31 January report and a 30 June Document of Compliance (search summary). The IMO data collection system and annual CII ratings run in parallel (recalled). Every figure traces back to fuel consumed per voyage, so the contest is reporting that a verifier accepts first time.

## Current Tools & Gaps
ZeroNorth, DNV's emissions platforms, Siglar Carbon, Marasoft and Kongsberg aggregate noon and sensor data; class societies and independent verifiers such as Verifavia check the reports. Gaps: noon reports, bunker delivery notes and voyage records disagree; voyage splits between EU and non-EU legs are done by hand; and the charterer, owner and manager each hold part of the data while one company must surrender the allowances.

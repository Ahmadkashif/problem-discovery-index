# Carbon Accounting Software Adapted to Ship Monitoring Plans

**Niche:** [[niches/ocean-shipping-carriers/emissions-monitoring-reporting/profile|Emissions Monitoring, Reporting & Verification]]
**Industry:** [[industries/ocean-shipping-carriers|Ocean Shipping Carriers]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Corporate carbon-accounting platforms handle audited emissions inventories; ship emissions follow approved monitoring plans, voyage rules and fuel-batch factors those platforms do not know.
**Tags:** #feature-engineering #evaluation-metrics #compliance #data-integration #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn a fleet's noon reports and fuel records into emissions figures a verifier accepts before 31 March, and whoever gets the most ships' reports verified with no corrections takes the account.

## The Problem
Shipping groups already buy carbon-accounting software for corporate reporting, then run ship-level regulatory reporting separately in spreadsheets or a maritime tool.

## What Already Exists
Corporate GHG platforms (Persefoni, Watershed, Sphera and others) build audited Scope 1–3 inventories with evidence trails and auditor workflows.

## The Customization Gap
A ship's report follows its approved monitoring plan: method (bunker delivery note, tank soundings or flow meters), fuel-specific emission factors including methane slip for LNG engines, and voyage classification by port call that decides whether 100%, 50% or none of a leg counts. FuelEU adds well-to-wake intensity, pooling and banking, and the ETS adds the shipping company or delegated party that must surrender. Corporate platforms have none of these rules and no feed from the ship. The customisation is the maritime rules engine and the on-board data link.

## Target Customer
Shipping groups that want one emissions system for corporate disclosure and ship-level compliance.

## Impact If Solved
One audited emissions record from ship to annual report instead of two systems that disagree. For a 50-ship group that removes ~1–2 analyst FTEs of reconciliation, ~$100,000–200,000 a year (estimate).

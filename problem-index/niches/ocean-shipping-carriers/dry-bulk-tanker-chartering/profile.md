# Dry Bulk & Tanker Chartering

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/ocean-shipping-carriers|Ocean Shipping Carriers]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to fix each open ship on the cargo that earns the most once port time, bunkers and laytime are counted, and whoever fixes the fleet at the highest time-charter equivalent against the Baltic route average takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.2B global spend on chartering and voyage-management software, broker market data and chartering-desk time at tramp owners and operators (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~6% of carrier shore-side spend, and most of the world's seaborne tonnage, which moves in bulk carriers and tankers rather than container ships (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — fixtures are negotiated through brokers by email and phone, and voyage estimates live in spreadsheets or a voyage-management system
**Target Buyer:** Head of Chartering / Commercial Director at a tramp owner or operator
**Automation Potential:** High for position-and-cargo matching and estimate preparation; low for negotiation and counterparty judgement

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Tramp ships have no schedule. Each fixture is a negotiated contract — voyage charter, time charter or contract of affreightment — for one ship and one cargo, struck through brokers who circulate positions and cargo orders in thousands of emails a day. A chartering manager compares open ships against cargo orders, runs a voyage estimate (freight or hire, bunkers, port costs, canal tolls, laytime and demurrage) and negotiates terms on BIMCO forms such as GENCON and NYPE (recalled). The score is the time-charter equivalent earned, judged against the Baltic Exchange route assessments the market uses as its yardstick. Red Sea re-routings and EU ETS costs, now on 70% of 2025 emissions for EU voyages, change voyage economics fixture by fixture.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Veson IMOS for voyage estimation and management, with Shipfix (email parsing) and VesselsValue (values and positions), both bought by Veson in 2023; Signal Ocean and Sedna for email-driven position lists; Marcura for laytime and voyage costs; broker reports and Baltic indices for the market. Gaps: estimates are rarely compared with voyage results, broker emails are parsed but not ranked against the desk's own economics, and nobody grades fixtures against the index after the voyage.

# Container Liner Pricing & Allocation

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/ocean-shipping-carriers|Ocean Shipping Carriers]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to fill each sailing with the highest-paying cargo that actually turns up, and whoever earns the highest net revenue per available slot across a season of sailings takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.5B global spend on pricing, revenue-management and allocation staff, tools and rate data at container lines (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~8% of container-line shore-side spend (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — bookings and rates are digital; the allocation call is a weekly meeting and a spreadsheet
**Target Buyer:** Head of Trade / Chief Commercial Officer
**Automation Potential:** High for booking-level scoring and overbooking targets; low for key-account and contract negotiation

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A liner's capacity is fixed and perishable: a weekly loop sails on its proforma day whether full or not. Revenue depends on the mix of annual contract cargo, index-linked contracts, NVOCC allocations and spot bookings on each sailing, and on how far the desk overbooks against bookings that will not load. Maersk once put the global no-show rate at about 20% and 35–40% on some trades (2011 statement). The contest is decided on one number, net revenue per available slot over a season, which every carrier measures and few attribute to the decisions behind it. Rate swings make the stakes large. Drewry's World Container Index stood at $4,585 per 40ft box on 22 October 2026, and capacity is being released as 63% of Far East–Europe services return to the Red Sea (Alphaliner, October 2026).

## Current Tools & Gaps
Carriers' in-house booking and pricing systems, spot e-booking channels and rate-index data from Drewry, Xeneta and Freightos (FBX). Index-linked contracts settle against those benchmarks. The gaps: no booking-level probability of loading, overbooking set by instinct per desk, roll decisions made under deadline without a value ranking, and no record of why any of it was done.

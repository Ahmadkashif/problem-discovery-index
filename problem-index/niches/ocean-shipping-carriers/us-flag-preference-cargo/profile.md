# US-Flag Preference Cargo Carriers

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/ocean-shipping-carriers|Ocean Shipping Carriers]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to have a cargo-preference-eligible US-flag ship available where government-impelled cargo loads, and whoever has a compliant ship inside the most tenders' loading windows takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$150M spend on tender management, programme compliance and fleet systems by US-flag carriers in international trade (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~1–2% of US carrier receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — tenders arrive through agency portals and email; eligibility and rate justification are assembled by hand
**Target Buyer:** Head of Government Business / Commercial Director at a US-flag international carrier
**Automation Potential:** Medium — tender monitoring, eligibility checks and rate documentation automate; agency relationships do not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
US-flag ships in foreign trade cost more to crew and operate than foreign-flag competitors, so they depend on government-impelled cargo: defence cargo moved under USTRANSCOM contracts, food aid and other agency shipments that cargo-preference law reserves for US-flag ships where available at fair and reasonable rates (recalled). The Maritime Security Program adds $6.5M per enrolled ship in fiscal 2025–26 (46 U.S.C. 53106) in return for availability to the military. Operators include US-flag arms of global groups and independents (recalled names in the source log). Winning means having an eligible ship on the right route when each tender's loading window opens. Rate matters, but only among the eligible ships available in that window.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Agency tender portals, MARAD's cargo-preference monitoring, and carriers' own liner systems. Gaps: tenders tracked by email; eligibility, schedule fit and fair-and-reasonable rate evidence assembled per tender; no view of which tenders a ship could have served.

# A Tender Radar for Government-Impelled Cargo

**Niche:** [[niches/ocean-shipping-carriers/us-flag-preference-cargo/profile|US-Flag Preference Cargo Carriers]]
**Industry:** [[industries/ocean-shipping-carriers|Ocean Shipping Carriers]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Government cargo tenders appear across several agencies and portals, and a carrier learns which it could have served only after the window closes.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #optimization-fundamentals #time-series-forecasting #evaluation-metrics #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to have a cargo-preference-eligible US-flag ship available where government-impelled cargo loads, and whoever has a compliant ship inside the most tenders' loading windows takes the account.

## The Problem
Defence, food-aid and other agency shipments are tendered with load ports, quantities and laydays. A US-flag carrier's commercial team watches portals and inboxes, checks whether a ship can position in time, and bids. Tenders are missed or bid too late, and ships sail part-empty on legs that preference cargo could have filled.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
The buyer pool is a few US-flag operators, too small for government-contracting software vendors to specialise.

## What to Build
A service that reads every relevant tender as it is published, matches it against each eligible ship's projected position and capacity, flags the feasible ones with a positioning plan and a draft bid, and keeps a record of tenders won, lost and missed against ship availability.

## Target Customer
Government business and chartering leads at US-flag international carriers.

## Impact If Built
Each extra preference cargo carried on a positioning leg that would otherwise sail part-empty is worth ~$0.5–2M in revenue (estimate) to costly US-flag tonnage.

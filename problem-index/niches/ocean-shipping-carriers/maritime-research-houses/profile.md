# Maritime Research & Market Intelligence Houses

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/ocean-shipping-carriers|Ocean Shipping Carriers]]
**Category:** Insight Layer
**Value-Chain Position:** Data & benchmark vendors

## What They Do
Clarksons Research, Drewry, MSI, Lloyd's List Intelligence and the liner specialists Alphaliner, Linerlytica and Sea-Intelligence (recalled names) keep fleet, orderbook, demolition and trade databases and sell subscriptions, forecasts and consulting studies. Their buyers are owners, carriers, banks, shipyards, brokers, investors and governments. Their numbers are the industry's shared reference: Alphaliner's capacity rankings and Sea-Intelligence's monthly schedule reliability (49.9% in August 2026) are quoted across the trade press.

## Insight Function
**Size:** ~50–200 analysts and data researchers at Clarksons Research, ~50–100 at Drewry, 10–40 at the specialists (estimates, per firm)
**Output:** Fleet and orderbook registers, supply-demand forecasts, capacity and schedule-reliability reports, bespoke studies
**Proprietary data:** Decades of vessel, orderbook, demolition and fixture records, at Clarksons partly fed by its own broking; much raw fleet data also sits in S&P Global's ship register (recalled)
**Clock:** The houses' own weekly and monthly publication calendars
**Buyer:** Head of Research / Managing Director

**Verdict:** Logged — below threshold. The nearest analogue is [[niches/cold-chain-logistics/freight-rate-benchmark-platforms/profile|🔍 Freight Rate Benchmark Platforms]] (54, indexed), another freight-data vendor selling insight as the invoice. The gap is mainly Q3: 4 here against 5 there. Xeneta-style benchmarks rest on contract rates that shippers contribute and nobody publishes, while two or three research houses can rebuild the same fleet picture from registries, yards and brokers, and the forecasts are graded against public deliveries. Q2 is kept at 3, not 4. Clarksons Research's 50–200 staff do grow with fleet coverage, but the work that grows is register researchers updating vessel records, which is close to production; the forecasting and writing analysts are set by sector coverage, not volume. That is how [[niches/metal-fabrication/metals-market-intelligence/profile|🔍 Metals Market Intelligence]] (45, Q2 3) is scored; Q2 4 would give 45. The clock is the houses' own, scored 2 under this run's ruling for data vendors' calendars; the benchmark pocket's Q4 4 and Q5 4 predate the rulings (see the overview). Q5 is 3 for a field of a handful of houses selling to many buyers. The AIS sibling [[niches/commodity-brokers/ais-commodity-flow-intelligence/profile|🔍 AIS-Derived Commodity-Flow & Freight Intelligence]] (47) earns Q4 4 because the EIA weekly and customs releases grade its nowcasts on third-party dates, and shipping research has no official release that grades it on a fixed day.

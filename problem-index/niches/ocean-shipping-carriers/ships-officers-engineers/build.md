# Enter Once at Source, Report Everywhere

**Niche:** [[niches/ocean-shipping-carriers/ships-officers-engineers/profile|Ships' Officers & Engineers]]
**Industry:** [[industries/ocean-shipping-carriers|Ocean Shipping Carriers]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Officers retype the same daily figures into a dozen formats for different readers ashore, when one record could generate them all.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #gaussian-processes #feature-engineering #worker-facing #automation #compliance #data-integration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep every statutory and commercial record on board correct and complete with the least officer time, and whoever's ships come through port state control with the fewest deficiencies per inspection takes the account.

## The Problem
The noon report, the charterer's template, the owner's performance form, emissions monitoring records, the oil record book and the pre-arrival port forms all repeat the same position, fuel, cargo and crew data. Each is typed separately, often after a watch, and the copies drift apart.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Each report belongs to a different buyer — owner, charterer, manager, regulator — and each vendor serves one of them. Nobody owns the ship-side record that would feed them all.

## What to Build
A ship-side application that captures each figure once, from sensors where they exist and from officer confirmation where they do not. It works offline and syncs when bandwidth allows, generates every required format automatically, flags figures that disagree with the ship's own history before they are sent, and keeps the evidence an inspector or verifier will ask for.

## Target Customer
Fleet managers at third-party ship managers and owners who want officer time back and cleaner data ashore.

## Impact If Built
Returning ~5–10 hours a week to each of four senior officers is ~1,000–2,000 officer hours a year per ship (estimate). It also means fewer queries from ashore and records that hold up in port state control inspections.

# Ships' Officers & Engineers

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/ocean-shipping-carriers|Ocean Shipping Carriers]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep every statutory and commercial record on board correct and complete with the least officer time, and whoever's ships come through port state control with the fewest deficiencies per inspection takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$800M global spend on shipboard reporting, planned-maintenance, crew-compliance and inspection-readiness software used by officers (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~4% of carrier and ship-manager shore-and-ship software spend (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — many reports are spreadsheets and forms sent by email over satellite links; logbooks and record books are still largely paper (estimate)
**Target Buyer:** Fleet Manager / Marine Superintendent at an owner or third-party ship manager
**Automation Potential:** High for data capture and report generation; low for the watchkeeping and engineering judgement itself

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Masters, chief officers and chief engineers are licensed under STCW and carry personal responsibility for the ship's records. They report to the owner, the charterer, the manager, the flag state, the class society and the EU and IMO emissions regimes, and must keep oil record books, garbage records, rest-hour records under the Maritime Labour Convention and planned-maintenance logs. Port state control inspectors check those records in every port, and deficiencies can detain the ship (recalled). Software is bought by shore offices and built around their needs. The person who enters the data, at sea on a small screen with limited bandwidth, is rarely the design target. See [[problems/ocean-shipping-carriers/worker-life-1|The Chief Engineer's Reporting Night]].

## Current Tools & Gaps
Fleet-management and planned-maintenance suites (ShipNet, Danaos, Star IPS, Helm), performance platforms (ZeroNorth, Kongsberg, StormGeo), crewing and rest-hour tools, and owner- and charterer-specific report templates. Gaps: the same figure typed into many forms, offline-unfriendly tools, inspection preparation by checklist, and nothing that shows an officer which records an inspector is likely to question.

# Rest Hours Recorded to Pass, Not to Show

**Niche:** [[niches/ocean-shipping-carriers/ships-officers-engineers/profile|Ships' Officers & Engineers]]
**Industry:** [[industries/ocean-shipping-carriers|Ocean Shipping Carriers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Rest-hour records under the Maritime Labour Convention are often filled in to look compliant, hiding the fatigue that paperwork itself causes.
**Tags:** #worker-facing #compliance #evaluation-metrics #quick-win #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep every statutory and commercial record on board correct and complete with the least officer time, and whoever's ships come through port state control with the fewest deficiencies per inspection takes the account.

## The Problem
Officers record rest hours that meet the rules because a non-compliant record invites questions, even when port calls, cargo work and reporting cut into rest. Fatigue goes unmeasured.

## Why It's Still Broken
Rest-hour records are self-reported and separate from the activity that consumes rest, so nobody can see where time actually went.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Derive a draft rest-hour record from the ship's activity — watch schedules, port calls, cargo operations, reports submitted — and let the officer confirm it, so overtime from administrative work shows up and can be removed.

## Who Feels the Pain
Senior officers working through rest periods, and owners exposed to fatigue-related incidents.

## Impact If Fixed
Honest records would show the ~5–10 hours a week of administrative overtime per senior officer (estimate) that the paperwork causes. That is the evidence needed to cut it.

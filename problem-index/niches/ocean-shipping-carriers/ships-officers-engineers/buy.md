# Inspection Readiness Built From Deficiency Histories

**Niche:** [[niches/ocean-shipping-carriers/ships-officers-engineers/profile|Ships' Officers & Engineers]]
**Industry:** [[industries/ocean-shipping-carriers|Ocean Shipping Carriers]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Audit and inspection-management software exists for many industries; a ship's port state control exposure depends on the port, the regime and the ship's own history.
**Tags:** #gradient-boosting #evaluation-metrics #compliance #worker-facing #data-integration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep every statutory and commercial record on board correct and complete with the least officer time, and whoever's ships come through port state control with the fewest deficiencies per inspection takes the account.

## The Problem
Officers prepare for inspections with generic checklists, and are caught by the deficiencies a particular regime emphasises in that season.

## What Already Exists
General audit and inspection-management tools, ship managers' internal audit programmes, and the public inspection databases of the port state control regimes.

## The Customization Gap
Readiness needs the ship's type, age, flag and class, the regime's targeting rules and concentrated inspection campaigns, and the deficiency history of similar ships at the next port. The customisation is a ship-specific readiness list ranked by likely deficiency, tied to the records on board.

## Target Customer
Marine superintendents and fleet managers responsible for detention records.

## Impact If Solved
A detention costs ~$20,000–50,000 a day in lost hire and port costs (estimate). Avoiding a few a year across a 100-ship fleet saves ~$0.1–0.5M (estimate), and officers prepare where inspectors will look.

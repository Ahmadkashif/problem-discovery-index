# Liner Operations Software Adapted to US-Flag Rules

**Industry:** [[industries/ocean-shipping-carriers|Ocean Shipping Carriers]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** US-flag operators run the same liner and fleet software as foreign carriers, which knows nothing of Jones Act, cargo-preference or MSP obligations.

**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is that every serious competitor is fighting to keep US-built or US-flag, US-crewed ships earning despite costs well above foreign tonnage, but the noncontiguous domestic trades are won by landing cargo on the day an island plans around, and the international preference trades by having an eligible ship inside each government tender's loading window. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## The Problem
Programme obligations — MSP availability and reporting, cargo-preference documentation, domestic-trade eligibility — are handled outside the operational system, by hand.

## What Already Exists
Commercial liner, fleet-management and crewing systems used across the global industry, often inherited from a foreign parent.

## The Customization Gap
The additions are programme-specific: tracking which ships carry which eligibility, recording preference cargo per voyage for compliance reports, holding MSP operating-agreement obligations, and keeping US crew-credential and union rules. These are small rule layers on existing systems rather than a new platform.

## Target Customer
IT and compliance leads at US-flag subsidiaries of global carriers and at independent US operators.

## Impact If Solved
Programme reporting that takes ~1–2 staff per operator today (estimate) moves to exception review, with compliance generated from operational data rather than a parallel paper process.

# The Mariner Who Can't Sail Because a Credential Lapsed

**Niche:** [[niches/ocean-shipping-carriers/us-flag-jones-act-carriers/profile|US-Flag & Jones Act Carriers]]
**Industry:** [[industries/ocean-shipping-carriers|Ocean Shipping Carriers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** With a small pool of qualified US mariners, one expired endorsement can hold a ship or force a costly last-minute relief.

**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is that every serious competitor is fighting to keep US-built or US-flag, US-crewed ships earning despite costs well above foreign tonnage, but the noncontiguous domestic trades are won by landing cargo on the day an island plans around, and the international preference trades by having an eligible ship inside each government tender's loading window. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## The Problem
A relief engineer is dispatched and found at the gangway to hold an expired medical certificate or missing endorsement. The ship waits or sails short-handed while a replacement is found.

## Why It's Still Broken
Credential dates sit in the mariner's wallet, the union's records and the operator's spreadsheet, and nobody checks them against the rotation plan in advance.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Read each mariner's credentials once, track every expiry against planned rotations, and notify the mariner and the operator weeks ahead with the renewal steps.

## Who Feels the Pain
Mariners who lose a trip, crewing managers scrambling for reliefs, and masters sailing short.

## Impact If Fixed
Each avoided last-minute relief saves the operator ~$5,000–10,000 in travel and handover costs (estimate) and saves the mariner a lost trip.

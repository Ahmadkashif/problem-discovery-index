# Cargo Claims & P&I Handling

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/ocean-shipping-carriers|Ocean Shipping Carriers]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to resolve each cargo claim inside the one-year Hague-Visby time bar for the least money, and whoever settles claims at the lowest paid-to-claimed ratio takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$500M global spend on cargo-claims staff, P&I correspondents, surveyors and claims software at carriers, excluding the claims paid (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~3% of carrier shore-side spend (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — claims arrive as letters and emails with survey reports, photos and invoices attached, and are tracked in spreadsheets or a basic case tool
**Target Buyer:** Head of Claims / Insurance Manager at a carrier
**Automation Potential:** Medium — intake, time-bar tracking and liability analysis automate; negotiation and settlement judgement do not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
When cargo arrives damaged, short, wet or spoiled, the cargo interest or its insurer claims against the carrier under the bill of lading. Under the Hague-Visby Rules, or COGSA in US trades, the carrier is discharged unless suit is brought within one year of delivery, and liability is limited per package (and under Hague-Visby per kilo) (recalled). Each claim turns on evidence: the B/L clauses, reefer temperature logs, stowage position, weather, survey reports and whether the damage was pre-shipment. Carriers handle smaller claims themselves and pass larger ones to their P&I club. The twelve International Group clubs cover ~87% of world ocean-going tonnage, each retaining claims up to $10M before pooling (IG P&I). The handler's score is how little is paid against what was claimed, without losing in court.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Case-management modules in carrier systems, generic insurance claims platforms, P&I clubs' own systems and local correspondents, reefer monitoring data and surveyors' reports. Gaps: time bars tracked by hand, liability evidence pulled from five systems for each claim, and no record of which defences succeeded on which claim types.

# Buy: Professional Practice From Adjacent Roles

**Niche:** The Awareness Manager
**Industry:** [[industries/security-awareness-training|Security Awareness Training]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Internal communications, occupational psychology and research ethics administration are all established roles with codes and communities, and the awareness manager has a platform login.
**Tags:** #evaluation-metrics #compliance #worker-facing #confidence-intervals #workflow-orchestration
**Contested on:** Whether a role built on deceiving colleagues and then asking for their trust can be made coherent.

## The Problem

Several adjacent roles do parts of this job and have the professional infrastructure the awareness manager lacks.

Internal communications professionals run organisation-wide campaigns, manage tone, negotiate with HR and works councils, and have a body of practice about how to address a workforce without alienating it. Occupational psychologists design workplace interventions with an evidence base and an ethical code. Research ethics administrators run the review processes for studies involving human participants, including deception. Learning and development professionals design programmes measured on behaviour change and have decades of argument about completion metrics behind them.

Each has a professional body, a code of conduct, a qualification route and a peer community. The awareness manager has a vendor's customer success contact and whatever they can find in an online forum.

The job draws on all four disciplines and has the professional standing of none of them.

## What Already Exists

Internal communications: professional bodies, campaign practice, tone and channel guidance, and established relationships with HR and employee representation.

Occupational psychology: professional codes governing workplace interventions, evidence standards, and a research base on trust, monitoring and psychological safety.

Research ethics administration: the profession that runs review boards, with training, standards and a well-developed understanding of deception protocols.

Learning and development: the discipline described in [[industries/corporate-training|Corporate Training]], with its own critique of completion metrics and its own evidence base.

Security awareness: vendor certifications, vendor user communities, and a small number of practitioner conferences.

## The Customization Gap

**No professional body owns the role.** It sits between security, communications, HR and learning, and none of their professional bodies claims it — so there is no code, no standard and no qualification.

**The ethical dimension has no home.** The person making deception design decisions has no professional code governing them, where a psychologist or a research administrator doing the same thing would.

**Internal communications practice is directly applicable and unused.** How to address a whole workforce without alienating it is a developed skill, and awareness campaigns are frequently designed by someone with no exposure to it.

**The evidence base is not consulted.** Occupational psychology has relevant research on monitoring, trust and reporting behaviour, and it does not reach this role.

**Peer support is vendor-shaped.** The available communities are organised around vendor products, which means the practice discussed is the vendor's practice.

**Career path is undefined.** The role has no recognised progression, so people pass through it and the accumulated judgement is not retained anywhere.

## Target Customer

Professional bodies in security, communications or learning, one of which could claim this role and give it a standard — which is the single change that would most improve it.

Security leadership, who would benefit from the role being professionalised in the same way that other specialist functions in their organisation are.

Occupational psychology and internal communications practitioners, for whom advising on awareness programme design is an adjacent service that essentially nobody offers.

## Impact If Solved

A role drawing on four established disciplines would acquire the standards and community that each of those disciplines has, which is what would let the person in it make hard calls against something other than their own judgement.

Internal communications practice is the most directly applicable and the most absent — the skill of addressing a whole workforce without alienating it is exactly what these campaigns need and rarely have.

And a professional code covering the deception decisions would give the awareness manager a basis to decline a design they believe is harmful, which they currently have to do on personal authority alone.

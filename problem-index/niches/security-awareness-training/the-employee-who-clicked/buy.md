# Buy: Just Culture From Safety-Critical Industries

**Niche:** The Employee Who Clicked
**Industry:** [[industries/security-awareness-training|Security Awareness Training]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Aviation and healthcare learned that punishing error suppresses reporting and destroys the information the safety system depends on, and built just culture to replace it.
**Tags:** #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #compliance #worker-facing #hypothesis-testing #descriptive-statistics
**Contested on:** Whether being deceived by a professionally designed message is treated as information about the message or as a failing of the person.

## The Problem

Two safety-critical industries have already worked through exactly this problem, and reached a firm conclusion.

Aviation and healthcare both depend on people reporting errors, near-misses and things that looked wrong. Both discovered that punishing individuals for errors produced a predictable and catastrophic result: people stopped reporting, the organisation lost visibility of the hazards, and the accident rate did not improve.

The response was just culture. It distinguishes human error, which is inevitable and should never be punished, from at-risk behaviour, which warrants coaching, from reckless behaviour, which warrants consequence. It holds that a system producing errors is usually a system problem rather than a person problem. It protects reporting explicitly and unconditionally. And it separates the learning system from the disciplinary system entirely.

Security awareness programmes routinely do the opposite. Being deceived by a professionally designed message is human error under any reading, and it is met with a list, an assignment and sometimes escalation — while the organisation simultaneously depends on those same people reporting things that look wrong.

## What Already Exists

Just culture: the framework itself, widely adopted in aviation and healthcare, with published implementation guidance and an extensive evidence base on its effect on reporting rates.

Confidential reporting systems: aviation's confidential incident reporting schemes, which protect reporters explicitly and which produced a step change in the safety data available.

Human factors: the discipline of designing systems that account for how people actually behave under load, rather than expecting vigilance.

Healthcare safety: the incident reporting and learning systems built on the same principles, with their own substantial literature.

Security awareness: click lists, training assignment and repeat-clicker escalation.

## The Customization Gap

**The framework's categories map cleanly and are not used.** Clicking a well-designed lure is human error. Repeatedly ignoring a clear warning is at-risk behaviour. Deliberately bypassing a control is reckless. Security awareness treats all clicks identically, which is precisely what just culture exists to prevent.

**Reporting protection is absent.** Aviation protects reporters unconditionally because the data is worth more than the discipline. Security awareness has no equivalent guarantee, which is why the employee who clicked something real and is unsure stays quiet.

**The learning and disciplinary systems are not separated.** Just culture insists on the separation. Awareness programmes feed results into performance conversations in many organisations, which merges them.

**Human factors thinking is missing.** A person clicking a convincing message during a busy afternoon is a predictable outcome of load and design, not a character flaw. The programme's framing is individual rather than systemic.

**The intervention is deception, which has no analogue.** Aviation does not stage fake emergencies to test pilots' error rates. The nearest thing is simulator training, which is announced, and the difference matters.

**The evidence is strong and transferable.** The effect of punitive measurement on reporting is one of the better-established findings in safety science, and it applies directly.

## Target Customer

HR and employee relations, who would recognise just culture immediately — many organisations already apply it elsewhere — and who own the fairness question.

Security leadership, for whom the argument is instrumental rather than moral: the programme depends on reporting, and punitive measurement reduces reporting.

Organisations that already run just culture for safety, where extending the same framework to security is a consistency argument rather than a new idea.

## Impact If Solved

A well-evidenced framework for exactly this problem exists in two industries that learned it the hard way, and the finding it rests on — that punishing error suppresses the reporting a safety system depends on — applies directly.

Separating the learning system from the disciplinary system is the structural import, requires no new capability, and would change what the programme teaches.

And an unconditional protection for reporting is the single most valuable thing an organisation could offer, because the employee who clicked something real and says nothing is the outcome that costs the most.

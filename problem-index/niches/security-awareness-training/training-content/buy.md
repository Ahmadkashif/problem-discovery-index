# Buy: Instructional Design and Behaviour Change Practice

**Niche:** Training Content & Delivery
**Industry:** [[industries/security-awareness-training|Security Awareness Training]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Learning science and behaviour change have substantial evidence about spacing, retrieval and contextual prompting, and security awareness delivers an annual video.
**Tags:** #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #hypothesis-testing #causal-inference #worker-facing #transfer-learning
**Contested on:** Whether the training changes what people do, or produces a completion record.

## The Problem

How people learn and how behaviour changes are both well-studied, and the findings are consistent enough to act on.

Spacing beats massing: material distributed over time is retained far better than the same material in one block. Retrieval practice — being asked to recall rather than to re-read — produces durable learning where passive review does not. Timing matters: an intervention delivered close to the moment of decision changes behaviour where one delivered months earlier does not. Friction and defaults frequently outperform education entirely. And knowledge does not reliably produce behaviour, which is the most important finding of all and the one this category's model depends on being false.

Security awareness training delivers an annual block of passive content with an immediate quiz, months away from any relevant decision, and measures completion.

Every one of those choices is the opposite of what the evidence supports, and the evidence is not obscure.

## What Already Exists

Learning science: the spacing effect, retrieval practice, interleaving and the testing effect, with a large and replicated literature.

Behaviour change: the behavioural science apparatus of nudges, defaults, friction and timing; implementation intentions; and the substantial evidence that information alone rarely changes behaviour.

Corporate learning: the L&D discipline described in [[industries/corporate-training|Corporate Training]], with its own long-standing critique of completion-based measurement.

Public health communication: decades of evidence about what changes health behaviour, including the repeated finding that awareness campaigns alone produce little.

Micro-learning platforms: spaced, short-form delivery tooling, which exists commercially and is used in this category mainly as a format rather than as a spacing strategy.

## The Customization Gap

**The compliance requirement enforces the wrong design.** Annual training is mandated by frameworks, which locks in the massed schedule. Satisfying compliance with a completion record while running a spaced programme alongside is achievable and rarely done.

**Retrieval practice is absent.** Quizzes immediately after viewing measure recall from working memory. Spaced retrieval — asking someone weeks later — is what produces retention and is not how any of these platforms work.

**The moment-of-risk intervention is the biggest gap.** Behaviour change research points at contextual prompting, and this category delivers months in advance. Integration with email clients, payment systems and data-sharing tools is where the effect would be and is largely unbuilt.

**Friction and defaults are outside the category's frame.** The most effective security interventions are frequently technical — blocking, warning banners, approval steps — rather than educational. A vendor whose product is training has no incentive to point this out.

**Knowledge-behaviour gap is the category's blind spot.** Public health learned that awareness rarely changes behaviour. Security awareness is built on the assumption that it does.

**Micro-learning is used as a format, not as spacing.** Short modules delivered annually are still massed. The value of micro-learning is the distribution and it is mostly not exploited.

## Target Customer

Corporate learning and development functions, who already know this literature, are frequently critical of completion metrics, and are rarely consulted on the security awareness programme.

Vendors willing to redesign around the evidence, for whom spaced retrieval and contextual prompting are a genuinely different product rather than a content refresh.

Security leadership, who would recognise the knowledge-behaviour gap immediately and who are the ones paying for a programme built on the assumption it does not exist.

## Impact If Solved

A substantial and settled evidence base about how people learn and how behaviour changes applies to a category designed around a compliance schedule.

Spaced retrieval instead of annual massing is a delivery change rather than a content change and is the intervention with the strongest evidence behind it.

And contextual prompting at the moment of risk is where behaviour change research consistently finds the effect, and it is the capability this category is furthest from and would benefit from most.

# Fix: Completion Is Not Learning

**Niche:** Training Content & Delivery
**Industry:** [[industries/security-awareness-training|Security Awareness Training]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The metric is that everyone watched it, the programme is reported as complete, and completion has never been shown to correspond to anything.
**Tags:** #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #hypothesis-testing #compliance #worker-facing #descriptive-statistics
**Contested on:** Whether the training changes what people do, or produces a completion record.

## The Problem

Ninety-eight per cent completion. The annual programme is reported as successful, the compliance obligation is satisfied, and the evidence is filed for the auditor.

Completion means the video played and a quiz was passed. In practice a meaningful share of employees have the module open in a background tab, answer the quiz by elimination, and retain nothing — which everybody involved knows and nobody measures.

The metric persists because it satisfies the requirement. Compliance frameworks ask whether training was delivered, and a completion record answers that exactly. It is auditable, it is unambiguous, and it has the property of being achievable by the programme regardless of whether anyone learned anything.

So the programme optimises for completion. Reminders, escalation to managers, deadline enforcement — all directed at the number, none at whether the content worked. And employees experience an annual obligation to click through something, which teaches them that security training is a compliance ritual, which is exactly what it has become.

The alternative measures are available. Spaced retrieval scores months later. Subsequent behaviour in simulations at calibrated difficulty. Real reporting rates. Each is more informative and none is required by anything.

## Why It's Still Broken

**Compliance asks for delivery, not for effect.** The framework's requirement is training delivered, and completion is the direct evidence for it.

**Completion is unambiguous and effect is not.** An auditor can verify a completion record. Verifying behaviour change requires a measurement nobody has agreed on.

**The vendor's product is completion.** Assignment, reminders and tracking are what the platform does well, and effect measurement is a different product.

**Nobody is measured on effect.** The awareness manager reports completion, the compliance function records it, and nobody in the chain owns whether behaviour changed.

**Measuring effect risks the finding.** A programme that measured behaviour change might find very little, which is a difficult result for the person who runs it.

**Employees are not the customer.** The people experiencing the annual click-through have no voice in how the programme is designed or measured.

## What a Fix Looks Like

**Test retention months later, not immediately.** A handful of questions at three months, unannounced, measuring what survived. This is the cheapest available effect measure and it would immediately show how much of the completion number means anything.

**Report behaviour alongside completion.** Reporting rate and simulation performance at calibrated difficulty, presented next to the completion figure. Completion satisfies the auditor; the behavioural measures are what the programme is actually for.

**Keep the compliance record and stop treating it as the outcome.** These are two different artefacts. Conflating them is what makes the programme a ritual.

**Shorten the annual module drastically.** Most of the length exists to feel substantial. A short, sharp module plus spaced follow-up would be better learning and less resented, and would satisfy the same requirement.

**Stop escalating completion to managers.** Deadline escalation buys the last few per cent of a number that does not mean anything, at the cost of the programme's standing with the workforce.

**Ask employees what they retained.** A short survey after a programme asking what people remember and whether they changed anything is informal, cheap, and more informative than the completion figure.

**Run one experiment.** Assign a module to half a population and compare subsequent behaviour. One study, once, would tell an organisation more about its programme than a decade of completion reporting.

## Who Feels the Pain

The employee, spending an hour a year clicking through content they will not retain, having learned that this is a ritual rather than an education.

The awareness manager, reporting a number they know does not mean what it is taken to mean, with nothing better available.

The organisation, which believes it has trained its workforce and has evidence that its workforce sat through something.

And the programme's credibility, which is set by the annual click-through far more than by anything else it does.

## Impact If Fixed

An unannounced retention check at three months is cheap and would immediately reveal how much of a ninety-eight per cent completion figure corresponds to anything.

Separating the compliance record from the outcome measure is most of the fix, because conflating them is what allows a satisfied requirement to be presented as a working programme.

And running a single randomised comparison would tell an organisation more about whether its training works than every completion report it has ever produced.

# Niche Analysis — Security Awareness Training

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/security-awareness-training|Security Awareness Training]]

## Niche Selection

This category reports performance on tests it designs itself, improves reliably on that metric, and has never related it to whether anyone resists a real attack. The eight niches below start with the simulation that produces the number, then the content it triggers, then the two questions the category avoids, then the two people the programme is run on and by, then the mechanical layers.

## Why These Niches

Simulation produces the headline number and the number is partly a statement about the test. Content is the intervention triggered by it, delivered annually and identically to everyone. Outcome measurement and role-based targeting are the two things the category could do with data every customer already holds and does not. The two underserved people are the employee who clicked a well-crafted email on a busy afternoon and is now on a list, and the manager whose job is to deceive colleagues and then ask them to trust the security team. Report triage and programme operations are mechanical.

## Filter Notes

Seven of the eight are terminal — each names one contest every serious competitor is fighting over, and decomposing further would produce features rather than markets.

**Phishing simulation** is not, because two entirely different questions sit inside it. Difficulty calibration asks whether the test measures anything: the difficulty is set by the vendor and the customer, so a falling click rate is consistent with a workforce that learned and with simulations that became easier, more recognisable or more pre-announced. Making difficulty an objective, externally-anchored, comparable quantity is a measurement-validity problem with a mature adjacent discipline in test theory, it is answerable from the platform's own response data, and it makes the product more defensible. Simulation harm and consent asks what administering the test costs the people it is administered to: simulations imitating bonus announcements, redundancy notices and benefit changes have repeatedly caused real distress and public controversy, and punitive use of results shapes how a workforce regards the security function. That is a research-ethics and occupational-psychology problem with a different owner inside the customer, a different evidence base, and no measurement that would make it look better. A vendor can build rigorous calibration and never address harm — calibration strengthens the headline while harm reduction may weaken it, and the most psychologically effective lures are exactly the ones that cause the damage.

Two adjacent candidates were rejected as belonging elsewhere: **general workforce learning and development** is the subject of [[industries/corporate-training|Corporate Training]], and **the compliance requirement that mandates the annual module** belongs to [[industries/grc-compliance-platforms|GRC & Compliance Platforms]].

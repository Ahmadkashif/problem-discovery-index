## The Problem

Two mature disciplines address exactly the two halves of this problem, and neither has been applied.

Educational testing solved comparability. Item response theory estimates item difficulty and respondent ability jointly, so a score is comparable across different test forms. Equating procedures let different tests produce comparable scores. Item banks are calibrated and maintained. Test security practice manages item exposure, because an item everyone has seen no longer measures anything. All of this exists because high-stakes testing could not tolerate a score that depended on which form you sat.

Research ethics solved deception. Studies involving deception require justification that no non-deceptive method would work, minimisation of harm, ethical review by a body independent of the researcher, debriefing afterwards, and the right to withdraw. Deception research is permitted and is permitted under conditions.

Phishing simulation is a deceptive intervention producing a score from an uncalibrated instrument, with no ethical review, variable debriefing and no comparability. Both disciplines have the answer and neither is present.

## What Already Exists

Test theory: item response theory and Rasch modelling, test equating, item banking and calibration, differential item functioning analysis, and item exposure control — all mature, documented and implemented in open software.

Assessment platforms: the systems used in educational and professional testing, with calibrated item banks and ability estimation.

Research ethics: institutional review boards, deception study protocols, debriefing requirements, informed consent frameworks, and an extensive literature on the harms of deception research.

Occupational psychology: research on workplace trust, psychological safety and the effects of monitoring and punitive measurement on reporting behaviour.

Simulation platforms: large template libraries with vendor difficulty labels and click tracking.

## The Customization Gap

**Item calibration is directly applicable and unused.** A phishing template is an item, an employee is a respondent, and the response data is exactly what item response theory consumes. The transfer is nearly direct and nobody has made it.

**Exposure control has a direct analogue.** Testing manages how often an item is seen because exposure destroys its measurement value. A phishing template reused across an organisation has the same problem and no equivalent practice.

**Ability estimation would replace the click rate.** Testing reports ability separated from item difficulty. Awareness reports a raw pass rate on items of unknown difficulty, which is what testing abandoned decades ago.

**Ethical review is entirely absent.** A deception study on employees would require review at any university. The same intervention at industrial scale in a workplace faces no equivalent, and the controversies that have occurred are exactly the ones a review would have prevented.

**Debriefing is inconsistent.** Research ethics requires it. Awareness programmes land on a training assignment, which is not the same thing and can feel punitive rather than explanatory.

**Consent is structurally harder in employment.** Research participants consent freely; employees cannot decline meaningfully. That argues for stronger protections rather than weaker ones, and the category has neither.

## Target Customer

Vendors positioning on rigour, for whom item calibration is a direct import that produces a genuinely defensible metric no competitor has.

Assessment and psychometrics vendors, for whom this is an adjacent application of core capability into a market with no measurement discipline at all.

HR, works councils and employee representation bodies, who would recognise the research ethics framing immediately and are increasingly the parties asking about these programmes.

## Impact If Solved

Two mature disciplines address the two halves of this problem precisely, and the transfer is closer to direct than in almost any other adaptation in this vault.

Item response theory applied to the response data these platforms already hold would produce a calibrated difficulty scale and comparable ability estimates — the first real measurement the category has had.

And applying research ethics practice, including review and debriefing, would prevent the controversies that have recurred in this industry with a predictable shape, at essentially no cost to the programme's effectiveness.

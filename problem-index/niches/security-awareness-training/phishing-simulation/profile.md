## What Makes This a Distinct Niche

Simulated phishing is the industry's engine. Messages are sent to the whole workforce, clicks and submissions are recorded, training is assigned to those who fall for it, and a click rate is reported that improves over the course of a programme.

The improvement is real and its meaning is unclear. Difficulty is chosen by the vendor and the customer. A programme that starts with a crude lure and progresses to recognisable templates will show a falling click rate that says nothing about the workforce. One that pre-announces campaigns, or uses the same sender patterns repeatedly, or excludes the lures that actually work, will show the same trend for the same reason.

Alongside that sits a separate problem the category discusses less readily. The simulations that produce the strongest responses are the ones that exploit genuine anxiety — bonus announcements, redundancy notices, benefit changes — and these have repeatedly caused real distress and public controversy. And in many organisations the results are used punitively, which teaches people that the safest response to uncertainty is silence.

## Current Tools & Gaps

Simulation platforms with large template libraries, scheduling, randomisation, landing pages, click and submission tracking, and automatic training assignment. Template difficulty labels assigned by the vendor. Industry benchmark click rates published by the larger vendors from their own customer base.

The gaps sit on both halves. Difficulty is a vendor label rather than a measured property, so click rates are not comparable between campaigns, customers or vendors. Published benchmarks compare numbers produced by different tests. Nothing anchors simulated difficulty to the real phishing an organisation actually receives, though its gateway records it. Harm is unmeasured — no vendor reports distress, complaints or trust effects from its own campaigns. And consent and notification practice varies enormously with no standard, in a domain where the intervention is deliberate deception of employees.

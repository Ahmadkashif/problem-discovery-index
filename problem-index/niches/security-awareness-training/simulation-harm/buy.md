# Buy: Deception Research Ethics, Applied at Work

**Niche:** Simulation Harm & Consent
**Industry:** [[industries/security-awareness-training|Security Awareness Training]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Deceiving people to study their behaviour is permitted in research under conditions worked out over decades, and the same intervention at industrial scale in a workplace has none of them.
**Tags:** #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #compliance #worker-facing #hypothesis-testing #descriptive-statistics
**Contested on:** What deliberately deceiving employees costs them, and whether the resistance the programme produces can be obtained without it.

## The Problem

Research involving deception is not prohibited. It is permitted under a framework developed over decades, largely in response to studies that harmed participants.

The framework is specific. Deception must be justified — no non-deceptive method would answer the question. Harm must be minimised, with anticipated distress weighed against the value of the knowledge. An ethics body independent of the researcher must approve the protocol before it runs. Participants must be debriefed afterwards, told what was done and why, and given the opportunity to withdraw their data. And consent, while it cannot be fully informed in a deception study, must be obtained for participation in research that may involve deception.

Phishing simulation is a deceptive behavioural intervention conducted on people who cannot decline, at a scale no research study reaches, with no independent review, inconsistent debriefing, and no consent framework at all.

The argument that this is operational rather than research is real and does not resolve the harm question. The intervention is the same, the population is more captive, and the protections are absent.

## What Already Exists

Research ethics: institutional review board processes, deception study protocols, debriefing requirements, and the extensive literature on when deception is justified and how harm is minimised.

Professional codes: psychological association ethics codes with specific provisions on deception, which set out the conditions in detail.

Workplace research governance: the frameworks under which organisational research on employees is conducted, which sit between research ethics and employment practice.

Occupational psychology: validated measures of workplace trust, psychological safety and the effects of monitoring on reporting behaviour.

Works councils and employee representation: consultation requirements that apply in some jurisdictions and are the closest existing governance in practice.

## The Customization Gap

**Consent cannot be voluntary in employment and the framework assumes it can.** An employee cannot decline. The correct response is stronger protections elsewhere — review, harm limits, debriefing — rather than treating consent as satisfied by an employment contract.

**No independent review body exists.** The programme owner approves their own campaigns. Introducing a panel with HR and employee representation is the single structural import and requires no new discipline.

**Debriefing is a training assignment.** Research debriefing explains what was done and why, and restores the participant's understanding. A landing page followed by mandatory training reads as a penalty, which is close to the opposite.

**Scale changes the harm profile.** A study involves dozens or hundreds; a programme involves an entire workforce, repeatedly, for years. Cumulative exposure to deception by one's employer is a different thing from a single study.

**The operational framing is used to avoid the question.** Calling it security operations rather than research does not change what is being done to people, and the ethics framework's substance applies regardless of the label.

**Harm is not anticipated in advance.** Research protocols require anticipating distress. Campaign approval considers brand appropriateness and rarely anticipated harm.

## Target Customer

HR and employee relations functions, who own the workforce relationship, are increasingly the ones handling the fallout, and would recognise the research ethics framing immediately.

Works councils and employee representation bodies, particularly in jurisdictions with consultation requirements, who are the constituency most likely to force the change.

Vendors, for whom a governance framework built ahead of pressure is considerably cheaper than one imposed after a controversy — and is a differentiator in the meantime.

## Impact If Solved

A framework developed specifically for deceptive interventions applies almost directly to an intervention conducted at greater scale on a more captive population with none of the protections.

An independent review panel is the single structural import, requires no new discipline, and would have prevented essentially every public controversy this industry has had.

And proper debriefing — explaining rather than admonishing — would change what the programme teaches, because the current post-click experience communicates blame where it should communicate how the deception worked.

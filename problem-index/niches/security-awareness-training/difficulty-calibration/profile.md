# Difficulty Calibration

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/security-awareness-training|Security Awareness Training]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Whether a simulation's difficulty is a measured property, so that click rates can be compared across campaigns, organisations and years.

## Profile

**Market Size:** ~$540M
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~18%
**Digital Adoption:** Very low — difficulty is a label
**Target Buyer:** Vendors, security leadership, insurers, auditors
**Automation Potential:** Very high — the response data is already collected

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche

This is the measurement-validity half. A click rate means nothing unless the difficulty of the test is known, and difficulty is currently a word a vendor attaches to a template.

The consequence runs through everything. A campaign cannot be compared to the previous one. An organisation cannot be compared to a benchmark. A vendor's published industry rates compare numbers produced by different instruments. And a programme cannot distinguish a workforce that improved from a test that got easier.

The fix is a solved problem in an adjacent discipline. Item response theory estimates item difficulty and respondent ability jointly from response patterns, which is precisely what these platforms' data supports — millions of responses, across many templates, many organisations and many populations.

It is separable from its sibling in every respect. [[niches/security-awareness-training/simulation-harm/profile|🎯 Simulation Harm & Consent]] asks what the test costs the people taking it, which is an ethics question with a different owner, a different evidence base and no measurement that makes it look better. Calibration is a psychometrics problem answerable from data the vendor already holds, and it makes the product more defensible rather than less — which is why a vendor would build it and defer the other.

## Current Tools & Gaps

Template libraries with vendor-assigned difficulty labels, usually a three-point scale. Campaign scheduling and randomisation. Click, submission and report tracking. Industry benchmark click rates published by the larger vendors from their own customer data.

The gaps are the whole niche. Difficulty is a label rather than an estimated parameter, so campaigns are not comparable. Individual susceptibility is not separated from which templates a person received, so the employee identified as high-risk may simply have drawn harder items. Exposure is uncontrolled, so a template reused across an organisation loses its measurement value silently. Nothing anchors the scale to real received phishing. And no vendor publishes a calibrated difficulty scale, which is why benchmarks compare incomparable quantities.

# Library Licence Pricing

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/film-distributors|Film Distributors]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to price a library title or package for a specific buyer, territory and window close to what that buyer will actually pay, and whoever knows the clearing price best closes the deal without leaving money on the table.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$900M a year in US subscription, pay-TV, broadcast and airline licence fees earned on library titles by independent holders (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~22% of US independent distribution and library receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — offers, counters and comparables live in salespeople's email and memory
**Target Buyer:** Head of library sales or SVP of worldwide television distribution at a library holder
**Automation Potential:** Medium for comparables and price ranges; low for the negotiation

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A subscription platform wants twenty titles for an 18-month term in three territories; a pay-TV channel wants a package of thrillers; an airline wants a quarterly rotation. Each deal is priced by negotiation, with almost no public prices. Litwak reports indie streamer licences as low as $1,000–10,000 a title, while a title with a recent theatrical success or a star can command many times that. The seller who knows what this buyer paid last time, what comparable packages cleared, and what demand the titles carry in each territory prices better. Demand-data vendors now pitch their numbers as anchors for minimum guarantees and licence fees.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Rights grids for availability, Parrot Analytics and Luminate demand data, deal history in spreadsheets, and sales staff's memory. The gaps: no structured record of past offers and clearing prices by buyer; demand data not joined to the firm's own realised prices.

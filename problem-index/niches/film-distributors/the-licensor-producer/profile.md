# The Licensor Producer

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/film-distributors|Film Distributors]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to give producers a distribution statement they can verify line by line against the receipts behind it, and whoever is most verifiably transparent wins the next film from the filmmakers who were burned last time.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$100M a year spent by producers and their financiers on collection account management, statement review and participation audits of distribution receipts (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~2.5% of US independent distribution and library receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — statements arrive quarterly as PDFs, often months late, with no access to the underlying receipts
**Target Buyer:** Independent producer or production company CFO; collection account managers; on the distributor side, the head of royalties who wants fewer disputes
**Automation Potential:** High for statement reading and reconciliation; low for the audit claim and the negotiation that follows

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Every distribution tool is built for the distributor. The producer who licensed the film receives a statement listing gross receipts, the distribution fee, recoupable expenses (P&A, delivery, market costs, sometimes cross-collateralised across territories or titles) and, rarely, an overage. Producers distrust it. A 2015 Writers Guild East survey reported that ~60% of indie filmmakers had trouble getting paid (press summary). Financed films route receipts through collection account managers such as Fintage House and Freeway, while most small films have only the distributor's word. Distributors bidding for films now compete partly on reputation for paying.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Collection account management for financed films; dashboard portals from some digital aggregators; entertainment accountants and participation auditors for disputes. The gaps: no way to check a statement against the licences and receipts behind it; no benchmark for what fees and expenses are normal; and audits too expensive for small films.

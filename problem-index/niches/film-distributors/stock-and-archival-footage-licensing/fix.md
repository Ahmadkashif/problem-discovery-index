# Rights Restrictions Found After the Screener Is Sent

**Niche:** [[niches/film-distributors/stock-and-archival-footage-licensing/profile|Stock & Archival Footage Licensing]]
**Industry:** [[industries/film-distributors|Film Distributors]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A researcher sends the perfect clip, and the producer finds out days later that it carries a music cue, a celebrity or a news organisation's copyright the archive can't license.
**Tags:** #cnns #transformers #large-language-models #compliance #automation #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to put a clearable clip of exactly the shot a producer asked for in front of them within hours, and whoever answers the research request first with footage that clears takes the licence.

## The Problem
Rights restrictions are recorded at reel or collection level, if at all. A clip that looks clean may contain copyrighted music, an identifiable public figure needing a release, or footage the archive itself licensed in from a third party.

## Why It's Still Broken
Restriction data was never captured at shot level, and checking every candidate by hand is slower than sending it and hoping.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Automatic flags per shot for music on the soundtrack, recognisable faces, on-screen logos and third-party source markers. These are combined with catalogue restrictions and shown alongside every search result before the screener goes out.

## Who Feels the Pain
Archive researchers who lose credibility; documentary producers who cut a clip into the edit and then must replace it; clearance coordinators.

## Impact If Fixed
Producers receive only clips the archive can license, cutting days of rework on both sides.

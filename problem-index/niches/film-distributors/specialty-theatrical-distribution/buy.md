# Studio Release Forecasting at Specialty Scale

**Niche:** [[niches/film-distributors/specialty-theatrical-distribution/profile|Specialty Theatrical Distribution]]
**Industry:** [[industries/film-distributors|Film Distributors]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Studios and tracking vendors forecast wide releases from awareness surveys; platform releases live or die on per-screen averages in a handful of arthouse theatres that those models never see.
**Tags:** #gradient-boosting #time-series-forecasting #survival-analysis #bayesian-inference #feature-engineering #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to price a festival film against what its platform release, awards run and later windows will actually earn, and whoever prices closest to the outcome wins the films that pay back and avoids the ones that sink the slate.

## The Problem
A platform release opens in four to six theatres, and the distributor decides each week whether to expand, hold or pull back, and where. The decision drives P&A spend, which often exceeds the film's budget. It is made on the head of distribution's feel for which markets and theatres suit which films.

## What Already Exists
Pre-release tracking from NRG, Screen Engine/ASI and Greenlight Analytics measures awareness and interest for wide releases. Box office forecasting from vendors such as Gower Street Analytics works at market and title level. Comscore reports theatre-level grosses.

## The Customization Gap
Specialty needs theatre-level forecasts for theatres the film hasn't played yet, with partial pooling across the distributor's own past releases and arthouse circuits. The forecasts should be conditioned on early reviews and exit-poll scores rather than awareness surveys. Run-length curves are needed for holdover negotiations, and the planner has to know the Academy rules: a seven-day qualifying run in one of six metros, and a Best Picture expanded run in ten of the top 50 markets within 45 days. Settlement history by theatre should feed in, because a theatre that grosses well and settles badly is worth less than its gross.

## Target Customer
Presidents of distribution and heads of theatrical sales at specialty distributors and studio specialty labels.

## Impact If Solved
Expansion spend goes to the markets where the next week's gross justifies it. Awards-path films complete their qualifying runs without wasted screens. The bid model gets an honest theatrical assumption to start from.

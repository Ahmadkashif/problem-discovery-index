# A Bid Ledger Graded Against Every Window

**Niche:** [[niches/film-distributors/specialty-theatrical-distribution/profile|Specialty Theatrical Distribution]]
**Industry:** [[industries/film-distributors|Film Distributors]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Specialty distributors place their largest bets on one viewing and never compare what they assumed at the bid with what each window later paid.
**Tags:** #tacit-knowledge-ml #gradient-boosting #bayesian-inference #large-language-models #confidence-intervals #evaluation-metrics #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to price a festival film against what its platform release, awards run and later windows will actually earn, and whoever prices closest to the outcome wins the films that pay back and avoids the ones that sink the slate.

## The Problem
A specialty distributor buys 10 to 30 films a year (estimate), most at four festivals. Each bid rests on a release plan and a set of window assumptions: opening cities, expansion target, premium VOD date, the streamer licence the theatrical result should command, the international MGs if world rights are in play. Those assumptions sit in the acquisitions model, which is revised until signature and then abandoned. Revenue arrives over three years in statements from a dozen counterparties. Nobody lines the two up, so the firm cannot say whether it systematically overpays for festival-prize winners, underestimates genre films, or misprices the subscription window.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Distributors are small and the bid happens in a frantic week. Tracking and box office vendors sell data about the market, not memory of the firm's own decisions. The outcome data is spread across licensee statements in incompatible formats, and the films not bought, the essential counterfactual, are tracked by no one.

## What to Build
A ledger that drafts a record for every film screened and every bid from voice notes, emails and the acquisitions model: assumptions by window, comparables, the release plan and a stated range. The buyer confirms each record in one tap. The ledger ingests statements as they arrive and joins them to the record, pulls public box office and licensing news for films passed on, and produces a calibration report by film type and window. At the next festival it surfaces the buyer's closest past reads and their outcomes.

## Target Customer
Heads of acquisitions and CFOs at specialty distributors, studio specialty labels and the mid-sized library buyers that acquire at festivals.

## Impact If Built
A firm that knows where it over- and under-bids can move a few points of accuracy into every guarantee. On a slate spending tens of millions a year on acquisitions and P&A, that is the difference between a profitable year and a write-down.

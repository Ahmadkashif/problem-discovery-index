# Specialty Theatrical Distribution

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/film-distributors|Film Distributors]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to price a festival film against what its platform release, awards run and later windows will actually earn, and whoever prices closest to the outcome wins the films that pay back and avoids the ones that sink the slate.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$600M a year in film rent to specialty and independent distributors on ~$1.2B of US box office, before the downstream windows the theatrical run sets up (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~15% of US independent distribution and library receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — box office and tracking data are digital; the acquisition decision and its record are notes, emails and a spreadsheet model
**Target Buyer:** Head of acquisitions; president of distribution; CFO of a specialty distributor
**Automation Potential:** Low for the bid itself, medium for comparables, release planning and post-release grading

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
These are the distributors that buy finished films at festivals and markets and release them theatrically, typically opening in a few cities and expanding on reviews and per-screen averages: Neon, A24, Bleecker Street, Magnolia, IFC Films, Roadside Attractions, and the studio specialty labels. The theatrical run is both a revenue window and the marketing that prices every window after it. Bids are made in days under auction pressure, on comparables and the buyer's read of the film. Neon reportedly paid ~$17M for *Together* at Sundance 2025 and spent ~$18M on P&A and awards for *Anora* (Litwak). The Academy's qualifying and Best Picture expanded-run rules set hard dates for any film with an awards path.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Comscore box office, tracking and exit polls from NRG and Screen Engine/ASI, festival screening schedules, Excel P&L models per film, and in-house comparables lists. The gaps: no record of the bid's assumptions; no grading of past bids against window-level outcomes; and films passed on are never tracked as the counterfactual.

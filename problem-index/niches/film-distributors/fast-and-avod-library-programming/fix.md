# Ad Breaks Marked by Hand in Every Film

**Niche:** [[niches/film-distributors/fast-and-avod-library-programming/profile|FAST & AVOD Library Programming]]
**Industry:** [[industries/film-distributors|Film Distributors]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Every film delivered to an ad-supported platform needs ad-break markers, and a technician scrubs through each one to find scene breaks that won't cut a line of dialogue.
**Tags:** #cnns #transformers #change-point-detection #automation #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn thousands of low-value library titles into ad-supported viewing hours at a delivery and metadata cost per title below what each title earns, and whoever programmes and delivers the most titles profitably takes the platforms' channel slots and the rights holders' libraries.

## The Problem
Platforms require break points at natural pauses, with spacing rules. Badly placed breaks drive viewers away mid-scene and cost ad revenue. Marking them by hand takes a technician most of a film's runtime.

## Why It's Still Broken
Library films were cut for theatres without breaks, older masters have no scene metadata, and each platform has slightly different rules.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Automatic detection of candidate breaks from shot changes, silence, music cues and dialogue boundaries, scored against each platform's spacing rules, with the technician approving or nudging the top candidates.

## Who Feels the Pain
Servicing technicians; aggregators paying per-title servicing costs; viewers interrupted mid-sentence.

## Impact If Fixed
Break marking drops from most of a film's runtime to a few minutes of review, which matters at the scale of thousands of titles.

# Earnings Forecast Before Each Title Is Delivered

**Niche:** [[niches/film-distributors/fast-and-avod-library-programming/profile|FAST & AVOD Library Programming]]
**Industry:** [[industries/film-distributors|Film Distributors]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** An aggregator pays to caption, re-master and deliver each library title to each ad-supported platform before it knows whether the title will earn back that cost.
**Tags:** #gradient-boosting #transfer-learning #feature-engineering #evaluation-metrics #revenue-impact #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn thousands of low-value library titles into ad-supported viewing hours at a delivery and metadata cost per title below what each title earns, and whoever programmes and delivers the most titles profitably takes the platforms' channel slots and the rights holders' libraries.

## The Problem
Delivering a title to a platform can cost several hundred dollars in captions, artwork, metadata and QC fixes (estimate). Many titles earn less than that in their first year. Aggregators decide what to deliver by genre rules and gut feel, and learn the result months later.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Platforms share performance per title only with the rights holder, and each aggregator's history is small relative to the variety of titles. Platform recommendation systems decide exposure in ways outsiders can't see.

## What to Build
A forecast of each title's first-year earnings per platform and as a FAST channel slot. It is trained on the aggregator's own title performance and uses metadata, genre, cast, year, runtime, artwork features and the title's prior performance on other platforms. It is paired with each title's delivery cost and recommends where to deliver it, where to hold it, and which titles to acquire next.

## Target Customer
Heads of AVOD and FAST at library aggregators and independent distributors with thousands of titles.

## Impact If Built
Delivery budgets go to titles that pay back, and acquisition of library packages is priced on predicted ad-supported earnings rather than title counts.

# Broadcast Scheduling Optimisation for FAST Channels

**Niche:** [[niches/film-distributors/fast-and-avod-library-programming/profile|FAST & AVOD Library Programming]]
**Industry:** [[industries/film-distributors|Film Distributors]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Broadcasters have optimised linear schedules against ratings for decades; FAST channel programmers build schedules from library titles by hand with far better minute-level data.
**Tags:** #time-series-forecasting #markov-decision-processes #gradient-boosting #feature-engineering #revenue-impact #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn thousands of low-value library titles into ad-supported viewing hours at a delivery and metadata cost per title below what each title earns, and whoever programmes and delivers the most titles profitably takes the platforms' channel slots and the rights holders' libraries.

## The Problem
A FAST channel runs 24 hours a day from a pool of a few hundred titles. The programmer decides the order, repeats and dayparts, and revenue depends on how long viewers stay through ad breaks.

## What Already Exists
Broadcast scheduling and rights-aware programming systems used by TV networks, with audience-flow analysis; FAST playout platforms with basic scheduling.

## The Customization Gap
FAST needs:
- minute-level viewing and ad-impression data from each platform's reports as the objective;
- rights windows per title and platform as hard constraints;
- repeat-fatigue modelling on small title pools;
- automated rescheduling as performance arrives weekly;
- costs that work for a channel earning thousands rather than millions a month.

## Target Customer
Channel programming leads at FAST channel operators and library aggregators.

## Impact If Solved
More viewing minutes per channel from the same library, and programmer time moved from building schedules to choosing the titles that feed them.

# Non-Theatrical & Educational Licensing

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/film-distributors|Film Distributors]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to be the one place a school, library or community group can license a public screening of the title it wants, and whoever covers the most studios' titles with the simplest licence takes the venue's renewal.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$250M a year in US public performance, educational and institutional licensing of films (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~6% of US independent distribution and library receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low-Medium — catalogues and ordering are online; venues' compliance and screening records are paper and goodwill
**Target Buyer:** Head of non-theatrical sales at a booking agency or educational distributor; on the venue side, a school district media coordinator or library programming lead
**Automation Potential:** Medium — licensing and ordering automate well, while catalogue coverage depends on studio relationships

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Showing a film to an audience outside the home, whether a school assembly, a college film night, a library screening, a cruise ship, a prison or a park, needs a public performance licence, which a DVD or streaming subscription does not include. Booking agencies (a 512199 activity) license these screenings on behalf of the studios and independents. Swank Motion Pictures, founded in 1937, describes itself as the sole source of public performance licensing from all major studios in the K-12 market, and licenses colleges per movie, per day. Educational distributors license documentaries to universities with classroom and streaming rights. The venues are tens of thousands of small, budget-constrained institutions that the theatrical system ignores. The buyer side belongs to K-12 schools and to the queued libraries-and-archives industry.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Booking-agency catalogues and online ordering, annual site licences, educational streaming platforms, and library procurement systems. The gaps: venues can't tell which agency holds a title's rights; independent and foreign titles are scattered across small distributors; and unlicensed screenings are invisible.

# One Rights Lookup for Every Public Screening

**Niche:** [[niches/film-distributors/non-theatrical-and-educational-licensing/profile|Non-Theatrical & Educational Licensing]]
**Industry:** [[industries/film-distributors|Film Distributors]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A librarian who wants to screen a specific film has no single place to learn who controls its public performance rights in the US, so independent titles go unlicensed or unscreened.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #k-nearest-neighbors #word-embeddings #data-integration #automation #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to be the one place a school, library or community group can license a public screening of the title it wants, and whoever covers the most studios' titles with the simplest licence takes the venue's renewal.

## The Problem
Studio titles are licensed through one or two agencies. A foreign film, a documentary or an older independent title may be controlled by a small distributor, the producer, or nobody who answers email. The venue spends hours tracing rights, gives up, or screens without a licence.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Non-theatrical rights are a footnote in most distribution agreements. Small rights holders don't list them anywhere, and the booking agencies' catalogues cover only what they represent.

## What to Build
A rights registry for non-theatrical public performance. It is seeded from agency catalogues, distributor websites and rights grids, and resolves title variants and remakes. It shows who licenses each title for which venue type, with an instant licence where the holder has opted in and a routed request where they haven't. Search covers titles, themes and curriculum topics. Small distributors list their catalogues for a commission.

## Target Customer
Booking agencies and educational distributors that want independent catalogues added; small distributors whose titles earn nothing non-theatrically today.

## Impact If Built
Revenue for titles that are now unlicensable in practice, fewer infringing screenings, and hours saved per screening for venue staff.

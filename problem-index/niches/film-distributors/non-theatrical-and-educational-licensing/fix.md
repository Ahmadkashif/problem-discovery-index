# The Community Screening Licensed After the Fact or Not at All

**Niche:** [[niches/film-distributors/non-theatrical-and-educational-licensing/profile|Non-Theatrical & Educational Licensing]]
**Industry:** [[industries/film-distributors|Film Distributors]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Parks departments, churches and community groups announce film nights on social media without a licence, and the rights holder learns of it by accident if at all.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #bert #transformers #automation #compliance
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to be the one place a school, library or community group can license a public screening of the title it wants, and whoever covers the most studios' titles with the simplest licence takes the venue's renewal.

## The Problem
Many organisers don't know a licence is needed. Those who do find the process slow for a one-off event. The agency's enforcement is a letter after the event, which sours a relationship that could have been a sale.

## Why It's Still Broken
Screenings are announced in thousands of local listings, newsletters and posts that nobody monitors at scale, and agencies have little reason to automate outreach for small fees.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Monitor public event listings for film screenings, match titles to the rights holder, and contact the organiser before the event with a pre-filled licence at the appropriate venue rate. The same feed tells agencies where demand for a title is rising.

## Who Feels the Pain
Rights holders losing fees; organisers caught out after the fact; booking agency staff sending enforcement letters instead of licences.

## Impact If Fixed
Unlicensed screenings become sales before they happen, and venues meet the agency as a service rather than a collector.

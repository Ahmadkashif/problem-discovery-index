# Sales CRM With Territory and Window Logic

**Niche:** [[niches/film-distributors/international-sales-and-territory-licensing/profile|International Sales & Territory Licensing]]
**Industry:** [[industries/film-distributors|Film Distributors]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Sales agents run their buyer relationships in a generic CRM or a spreadsheet that doesn't know a film has thirty territories, each with its own deal, price and delivery status.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #feature-engineering #k-nearest-neighbors #data-integration #workflow-orchestration #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to issue territory sales estimates that buyers actually meet and lenders will finance against, and whoever's estimates land closest to the minimum guarantees finally closed gets the next package from producers and financiers.

## The Problem
At a market a salesperson holds forty meetings a day, pitching a slate to buyers they have known for years. Offers are noted on paper or in a phone, followed up by email, and turned into deal memos weeks later. Buyer preferences (genres, cast, price ceilings, who passed and why) are not recorded anywhere the next salesperson can use.

## What Already Exists
Salesforce, HubSpot and Pipedrive manage contacts and opportunities. Cinando and market-specific databases list buyers and companies. Rights platforms at the top end track avails and deals after signature.

## The Customization Gap
An agent needs:
- opportunities modelled as title × territory × rights bundle, not as single deals;
- offers and counter-offers logged against the estimate;
- buyer profiles built from deal and pass history;
- meeting notes captured by voice and turned into structured offers;
- similar-buyer suggestions for each new package;
- a hand-off from closed offer to deal memo and delivery schedule.

## Target Customer
Heads of sales and sales operations at international sales companies with 10 to 50 staff.

## Impact If Solved
Offers stop getting lost between the market and the memo, buyer knowledge survives staff turnover, and the estimate ledger fills itself.

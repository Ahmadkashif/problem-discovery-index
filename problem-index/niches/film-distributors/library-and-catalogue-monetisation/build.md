# Title-Level Profit for Every Library Title

**Niche:** [[niches/film-distributors/library-and-catalogue-monetisation/profile|Library & Catalogue Monetisation]]
**Industry:** [[industries/film-distributors|Film Distributors]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A library holder with thousands of titles knows its revenue per platform but not its profit per title, so it keeps paying to service titles that lose money and underprices the few that carry the catalogue.
**Tags:** #gradient-boosting #survival-analysis #feature-engineering #evaluation-metrics #data-integration #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is that every competitor is fighting to earn more from each library title than it costs to hold, service and deliver. Writing it out produces two contests with different winners: ad-supported volume, where the winner delivers and programmes the most titles at the lowest cost per title, and licence pricing, where the winner knows what a specific buyer will pay for a specific title, territory and window. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## The Problem
Revenue arrives per platform, per month, in reports keyed by each platform's own title identifiers. Costs are incurred per delivery, per caption file, per artwork set, per minimum guarantee recouped, and per producer share. Nobody joins them by title, so renewal and acquisition decisions are made on totals.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Platform reports use inconsistent identifiers and periods. Servicing costs sit in accounting by vendor. The library holders are mid-sized firms without data teams, and rights software tracks entitlements rather than economics.

## What to Build
A title ledger that resolves each platform's identifiers to the library's titles and joins revenue to servicing costs and producer shares. It forecasts each title's remaining earnings curve from its history and comparable titles, and recommends whether to renew, drop, re-deliver or reprice. It feeds both sub-niches: ad-supported programming and licence pricing.

## Target Customer
CFOs and heads of digital distribution at library aggregators and independent distributors with large catalogues.

## Impact If Built
Servicing spend moves from loss-making titles to earning ones, renewals are priced on forecast earnings, and the library's value can be stated from evidence rather than an appraiser's estimate.

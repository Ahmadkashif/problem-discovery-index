# Catalogue Analytics From Music Publishing

**Industry:** [[industries/film-distributors|Film Distributors]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Music catalogue owners already value and manage songs as long-lived earning assets from royalty statements; film libraries hold the same kind of asset and manage it with spreadsheets.
**Tags:** #survival-analysis #time-series-forecasting #feature-engineering #data-integration #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is that every competitor is fighting to earn more from each library title than it costs to hold, service and deliver. Writing it out produces two contests with different winners: ad-supported volume, where the winner delivers and programmes the most titles at the lowest cost per title, and licence pricing, where the winner knows what a specific buyer will pay for a specific title, territory and window. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## The Problem
Film library holders need to know which titles earn, how fast earnings decay, and what the catalogue is worth to a lender or buyer. They answer by hand at each refinancing or sale.

## What Already Exists
Music publishing and catalogue-investment tooling ingests royalty statements from many sources, normalises them to works, models earnings decay and supports catalogue valuations. Generic BI tools sit on top of accounting data.

## The Customization Gap
Film needs:
- ingestion of platform, broadcaster and sub-distributor statements rather than collecting-society statements;
- rights-window awareness, because a title's earnings depend on which windows are free and when;
- servicing-cost allocation per title;
- decay models that reflect licence-term cycles rather than streaming play counts;
- valuation outputs in the format lenders and auditors use for film libraries.

## Target Customer
Finance teams at film library holders and the lenders who finance library acquisitions.

## Impact If Solved
A library's earnings and value become a maintained number rather than a one-off appraisal, which lowers the cost of financing and of every sale.

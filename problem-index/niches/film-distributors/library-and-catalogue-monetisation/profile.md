# Library & Catalogue Monetisation

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/film-distributors|Film Distributors]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is that every competitor is fighting to earn more from each library title than it costs to hold, service and deliver. Writing it out produces two contests with different winners: ad-supported volume, where the winner delivers and programmes the most titles at the lowest cost per title, and licence pricing, where the winner knows what a specific buyer will pay for a specific title, territory and window. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.5B a year in US library licensing and ad-supported revenue earned by independent library holders and aggregators (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~37% of US independent distribution and library receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — delivery and ad revenue reporting are digital; title-level economics and licence pricing are spreadsheets and judgement
**Target Buyer:** Head of library sales or digital distribution at a library holder or aggregator
**Automation Potential:** High for ad-supported delivery, metadata and channel scheduling; medium to low for pricing licences

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
After its first windows, a film becomes a library title, earning small amounts for decades from subscription licences, television sales, ad-supported platforms and FAST channels. Libraries have grown large and cheap. Cineverse reports rights to over 71,000 titles and episodes, an appraisal put its digital library at ~$40M, and it carries a ~$2.5M book value. FilmRise described a staff of over 60 in 2019 and growing. The money is in volume at very low revenue per title, so automation of delivery, metadata and scheduling decides whether a title is worth holding. Pricing the occasional valuable licence is a different skill entirely.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Platform delivery portals, FAST channel playout and scheduling tools, ad-revenue reports per platform, rights spreadsheets, and demand data from vendors such as Parrot Analytics. The gaps: title-level profit after delivery and servicing costs is unknown; and licence prices are set by feel against a buyer's opening offer.

# The Library Valued at Book Instead of at Earnings

**Niche:** [[niches/film-distributors/library-and-catalogue-monetisation/profile|Library & Catalogue Monetisation]]
**Industry:** [[industries/film-distributors|Film Distributors]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Film libraries are carried at amortised cost that bears little relation to what they earn, so owners can't show lenders or buyers what the catalogue is worth without paying for a new appraisal.
**Tags:** #survival-analysis #feature-engineering #evaluation-metrics #data-integration #quick-win
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is that every competitor is fighting to earn more from each library title than it costs to hold, service and deliver. Writing it out produces two contests with different winners: ad-supported volume, where the winner delivers and programmes the most titles at the lowest cost per title, and licence pricing, where the winner knows what a specific buyer will pay for a specific title, territory and window. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## The Problem
Cineverse's FY2025 10-K shows the gap clearly: an appraisal put its digital library at ~$40M against a ~$2.5M book value. Most library holders have no appraisal at all, and an appraisal is a point estimate that ages as soon as it is delivered.

## Why It's Still Broken
Valuations need title-level earnings histories that the holder doesn't maintain, so each appraisal starts by reconstructing them from statements.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Maintain the earnings history per title continuously from incoming statements. Produce a standing earnings-based valuation range each quarter with its assumptions shown, so an appraiser verifies rather than rebuilds.

## Who Feels the Pain
CFOs refinancing or selling libraries; lenders sizing facilities; acquirers pricing a catalogue.

## Impact If Fixed
Faster, cheaper financing and sales, and a library value management can actually steer by.

# Rights, Avails & Contract Administration

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/film-distributors|Film Distributors]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to answer whether a title is free for a given territory, medium and window on a given date correctly and in minutes, from contracts written in prose, and whoever never grants the same right twice keeps both its licensors and its licensees.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$150M a year in rights administration labour, business-affairs time and rights software across US independent distributors, sales agents and library holders (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~4% of US independent distribution and library receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — contracts are PDFs and scans; the rights grid is a spreadsheet kept by one coordinator at most independents
**Target Buyer:** Head of business affairs; VP of rights and contracts administration
**Automation Potential:** High for extraction, avails and conflict checks; low for interpreting genuinely ambiguous grants

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A distributor sells rights it holds under one contract (from the producer or sales agent) through many others (to theatres, platforms, broadcasters and sub-distributors). Every licence must fit inside what was acquired and outside what was already granted. Media definitions written before ad-supported and FAST streaming existed have to be read against today's windows. Holdbacks key one window to another's start, and terms run from first exploitation. The record is the distributor's legal exposure: a double grant is a breach, and a missed reversion is a dispute with the producer.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Rightsline, which absorbed FilmTrack (nearly 200 entertainment customers) and RSG Media in 2024, serves studios and broadcasters well. Contract lifecycle tools store PDFs and dates. Independents run Excel. The gaps: the grid is keyed by hand when it exists; media and territory definitions aren't normalised across contracts; and no tool answers an avails question with the clause attached at a price an independent will pay.

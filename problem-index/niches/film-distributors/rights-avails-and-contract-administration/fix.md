# The Reversion Date Nobody Diarised

**Niche:** [[niches/film-distributors/rights-avails-and-contract-administration/profile|Rights, Avails & Contract Administration]]
**Industry:** [[industries/film-distributors|Film Distributors]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Rights revert to producers and licences expire on dates buried in contracts, and the distributor finds out when a producer's lawyer writes or a platform pulls the title.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #bert #compliance #automation #quick-win #workflow-orchestration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to answer whether a title is free for a given territory, medium and window on a given date correctly and in minutes, from contracts written in prose, and whoever never grants the same right twice keeps both its licensors and its licensees.

## The Problem
Acquisition terms often run ~10 to 25 years (estimate), sometimes with early reversion if the distributor fails to release, account or pay. Licences out run one to seven years. Dates depend on events (first release, delivery, a statement not sent), so they never make it into a calendar. A distributor keeps selling a title whose rights have reverted, or lets a renewable licence lapse.

## Why It's Still Broken
Event-triggered dates require reading the contract and knowing when the trigger happened. The trigger date usually sits in a different system (the release date, a payment record) from the contract.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Extract every term, reversion and renewal clause with its trigger. Resolve trigger dates from release and accounting records. Diarise the result with warnings 90 and 30 days out, sent to business affairs and sales.

## Who Feels the Pain
Business affairs staff facing reversion claims; salespeople who sold what the firm no longer owned; producers whose rights were exploited past term.

## Impact If Fixed
Disputes that start with a missed date stop happening. Renewal opportunities are raised before the licence lapses rather than after.

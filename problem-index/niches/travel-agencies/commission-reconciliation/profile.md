# Supplier Commission Reconciliation

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/travel-agencies|Travel Agencies]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to match every expected commission to a payment and chase the rest before the supplier stops answering, and whoever recovers the highest share of earned commission takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$400M back-office labour, reconciliation tools and contingency recovery fees on supplier commissions (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~1% of agency receipts (estimate), governing the collection of most leisure revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — bookings and many statements are electronic, matching and chasing are manual
**Target Buyer:** Host agency CFO or back-office manager
**Automation Potential:** Very high for matching, high for chasing

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A leisure agency's revenue arrives as commission from hundreds of suppliers, on different schedules and through different channels: hotel commission clearing houses, cruise-line statements, tour-operator remittances, insurer reports. Each payment must be matched to the booking, compared with what it should have been, split with the advisor and chased if short. ASTA keeps a watch list of hotels that fail to pay 45 days after a completed stay, and contingency recovery firms take 50% of what they collect from 180 days after checkout. Whoever recovers the most of what is owed, at the lowest cost per claim, wins the host's back office.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Trams and ClientBase back offices, Tern's financial hub, host-built systems, clearing-house remittance files, recovery services such as Commtrak, and ASTA's Report a Hotel tool. Gaps: fuzzy matching left to people; no forecast of which commissions will not arrive; claims sent late; and commissions paid to the wrong agency number when advisors move between hosts.

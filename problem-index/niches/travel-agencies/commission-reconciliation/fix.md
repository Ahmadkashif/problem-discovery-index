# The Commission Paid to the Advisor's Old Host

**Niche:** [[niches/travel-agencies/commission-reconciliation/profile|Supplier Commission Reconciliation]]
**Industry:** [[industries/travel-agencies|Travel Agencies]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** An advisor moves to a new host, and commissions on trips booked before the move keep arriving at the old host for months, where they are held, lost or argued over.
**Tags:** #k-nearest-neighbors #evaluation-metrics #data-integration #workflow-orchestration #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to match every expected commission to a payment and chase the rest before the supplier stops answering, and whoever recovers the highest share of earned commission takes the account.

## The Problem
Switching hosts takes 30-90 days by the host survey's account, and bookings made under the old host's accreditation pay to the old host until travel completes. The advisor must track which commissions are still due through the old host, and the old host must identify and forward them under the separation terms.

## Why It's Still Broken
Commissions are paid against accreditation numbers, not advisors; the old host has little incentive to prioritise a departed advisor; and neither side has a shared list of in-flight bookings.

## What a Fix Looks Like
At departure, generate a list of in-flight bookings with expected commissions and dates, shared by both hosts; match incoming payments at the old host against it automatically; forward or credit them on a schedule; and show the advisor the status.

## Who Feels the Pain
Advisors waiting on commissions earned months earlier; host back offices handling forwarded payments by hand; clients caught in disputes over who services the trip.

## Impact If Fixed
Switching hosts stops costing advisors a season of commissions, and hosts stop arguing over money neither can trace.

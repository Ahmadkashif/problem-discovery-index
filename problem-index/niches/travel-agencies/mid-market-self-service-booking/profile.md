# Mid-Market Self-Service Booking Adoption

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/travel-agencies|Travel Agencies]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get a growing company's employees to book and change their own trips in the tool without calling an agent, and whoever keeps the highest share of trips booked and serviced touchless takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.5B booking-tool and TMC fee revenue from US companies spending ~$1-20M a year on travel (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~5% of agency receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** High — self-service tools are the product; agents are the fallback
**Target Buyer:** CFO or finance director at a growing company, often with an office manager as the day-to-day owner
**Automation Potential:** Very high for booking, high for routine changes

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A company of a few hundred to a few thousand employees has no travel manager. Finance wants spend visible and policy applied without running a programme; employees want to book as easily as on a consumer site. The TMC's economics depend on adoption: an online booking costs a few dollars to serve and an agent-assisted one many times that (~$5-40 per transaction, estimate). Software-led platforms (Navan, Perk, formerly TravelPerk, and Spotnana-powered TMCs) compete with regional TMCs on exactly this, often bundled with expense and card products.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Navan, SAP Concur, Perk and TMC-branded booking tools, with expense integration. Gaps: changes and cancellations still push travellers to the phone; NDC fares booked online are hard to change online; travellers leave for consumer sites when the tool shows higher hotel rates; and the finance buyer sees adoption only as a monthly percentage, not as the specific trips that leaked.

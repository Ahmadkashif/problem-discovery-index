# The Client Who Books Direct the Second Time

**Niche:** [[niches/travel-agencies/luxury-leisure-consortia/profile|Luxury Leisure Advisors & Consortia]]
**Industry:** [[industries/travel-agencies|Travel Agencies]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A client loves the resort the advisor found, then books it directly the next year, because nothing reminded them what the advisor's booking got them.
**Tags:** #logistic-regression #survival-analysis #large-language-models #revenue-impact #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get the client something on arrival that a direct booker would not get — the upgrade, the credit, the recognition — and whoever has its amenities honoured at the front desk most reliably takes the account.

## The Problem
The advisor's value on a repeat booking is the amenity and recognition, but the client remembers the hotel, not the breakfast credit. When the next trip comes, the hotel's own marketing reaches them first.

## Why It's Still Broken
Advisors have no systematic record of what each client received on each trip, and no prompt to get in touch before the client starts planning. CRMs log bookings, not the value delivered.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Record the amenities and recognition delivered on every stay, show the client a short summary after the trip, and predict from travel history when they are likely to plan the next one, so the advisor reaches them first with what the advisor's booking will add.

## Who Feels the Pain
Luxury advisors losing repeat commission; agencies whose client lifetime value erodes; consortia whose preferred partners capture the relationship.

## Impact If Fixed
Holding a few more clients per hundred through their second and third trips (estimate) compounds into the largest single gain in an advisor's income.

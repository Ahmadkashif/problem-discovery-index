# Luxury Leisure Advisors & Consortia

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/travel-agencies|Travel Agencies]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get the client something on arrival that a direct booker would not get — the upgrade, the credit, the recognition — and whoever has its amenities honoured at the front desk most reliably takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$4B commissions, consortium fees and supplier marketing funds on US luxury leisure bookings (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~12% of agency receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — supplier portals and consortium platforms are digital; the client relationship and supplier follow-up run on phone, text and email
**Target Buyer:** Agency owner or managing partner; consortium membership and supplier-relations leads
**Automation Potential:** Medium — research and proposal assembly automate, the client relationship and the supplier escalation do not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Luxury agencies sell access as much as advice. A consortium negotiates with hotels, cruise lines and tour operators for amenities its members' clients receive — breakfast, a resort credit, an upgrade on arrival, VIP recognition — in return for steering volume to preferred partners, who also pay marketing fees. Virtuoso alone reports over 1,200 agency locations, more than 20,000 advisors and ~$35B in normalised annual sales; Signature, Internova's Travel Leaders Network and Ensemble compete for the same agencies, and hotel brands run their own preferred-agency programmes. The client judges all of it at check-in.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Consortium booking portals and amenity listings, hotel preferred-partner programmes, supplier portals, agency CRMs such as ClientBase and Travefy, and the advisor's own phone. Gaps: no record of whether an amenity was actually honoured, property by property; amenity notes that do not reach the front desk; advisor knowledge of which general manager delivers held privately; and consortium negotiations run on sales volume rather than on the experience members' clients received.

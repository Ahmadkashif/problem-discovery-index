# The Independent Hosted Advisor

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/travel-agencies|Travel Agencies]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get a new advisor to steady paid commissions before they give up, and whoever gets recruits through their first year of earnings fastest takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$2B commissions earned by hosted independent contractors, plus host membership and tool fees (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~6% of agency receipts (estimate); far larger by headcount, since most of the industry's new advisors enter this way
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — hosts provide portals and training, but advisors juggle a dozen supplier systems with consumer-grade tools
**Target Buyer:** Host agency owner or head of advisor success
**Automation Potential:** High for research, proposal assembly and admin; low for client relationships

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A host agency lends independent advisors its accreditation numbers, supplier relationships, commission levels, back office and training in return for a split of commission and, often, fees. The advisor is a 1099 contractor, frequently part-time, working from home, and paid only when clients travel — months after the work. The host survey reports an average full-time income of ~$68,310, and Fora, now valued at $1B, reports ~15,000 active advisors. Hosts compete for advisors on split, support and culture: of advisors who left a host, 49% cited culture and 35% lack of support, against 17% financials.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Host portals and back offices, consortium platforms, itinerary and CRM tools (Travefy, Axus, Tern, TravelJoy), supplier training academies, and Facebook groups for peer help. Gaps: no measure of which recruits are on track to become productive; training disconnected from what the advisor actually sells; and a first year spent on admin, supplier registrations and unpaid research before the first commission arrives.

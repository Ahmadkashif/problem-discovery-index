# The Accessible Room Given to Someone Else

**Niche:** [[niches/travel-agencies/accessible-travel-specialists/profile|Accessible Travel Specialists]]
**Industry:** [[industries/travel-agencies|Travel Agencies]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The advisor books an accessible room, the booking channel transmits a generic room type, and the traveller arrives to find the accessible rooms assigned to other guests.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #workflow-orchestration #automation #worker-facing #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to verify that a room, cabin, transfer or excursion is actually usable for a specific traveller's disability before departure, and whoever's verification holds up on arrival most often takes the account.

## The Problem
Many booking paths map an accessible room request to a standard room code with a note, and front desks assign rooms on the day. The traveller who needs a roll-in shower can arrive late at night to find none left.

## Why It's Still Broken
Accessible inventory is small, notes do not travel reliably between channel, property system and front desk, and nobody owns pre-arrival confirmation.

## What a Fix Looks Like
After booking, an automated confirmation workflow asks the property to assign and confirm the specific room number in writing, re-confirms 72 hours before arrival, escalates to the property manager if unconfirmed, and gives the traveller the confirmation to show at check-in.

## Who Feels the Pain
Disabled travellers stranded with an unusable room; specialist advisors making late-night calls; properties facing complaints they could have avoided.

## Impact If Fixed
A written, room-specific confirmation for every accessible booking turns the most common failure into a rare one.

# Accessible Travel Specialists

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/travel-agencies|Travel Agencies]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to verify that a room, cabin, transfer or excursion is actually usable for a specific traveller's disability before departure, and whoever's verification holds up on arrival most often takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$150M commission revenue for advisors specialising in travellers with disabilities, wheelchair users and medically complex travel (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** <1% of agency receipts (estimate), on a much larger underserved population (~1 in 4 US adults report a disability, estimate from recalled CDC figures)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — verification is done by phone calls to properties, emailed photos and tape measures
**Target Buyer:** Specialist advisor or agency owner
**Automation Potential:** Medium — data capture and matching automate, the judgement about a specific client's needs does not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
For a wheelchair user, "accessible room" can mean a roll-in shower or a tub with a grab bar, a bed at transfer height or not, a door wide enough or an inch too narrow. Cruise accessible cabins are few and assigned early; tenders, excursions and airport transfers each have their own barriers; airline wheelchair damage and assistance failures are common. Specialist advisors build private libraries of measured properties and trusted ground operators and sell the certainty that the trip will work. General advisors and booking sites rely on a supplier's accessibility checkbox.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Hotel and cruise accessibility descriptions, crowdsourced accessibility apps for venues, specialist forums and the advisor's own notes and photos. Gaps: no structured, measured accessibility data at room level; accessible room types that booking channels do not guarantee; and no shared record of which suppliers delivered what they promised.

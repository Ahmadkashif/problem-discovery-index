# Traveller Tracking & Duty of Care

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/travel-agencies|Travel Agencies]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to tell a company within minutes of an incident which of its people are in the affected area, including those who booked outside the programme, and whoever accounts for every traveller fastest takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$600M US spend on traveller tracking, risk alerts and assistance tied to managed travel (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~2% of agency receipts (estimate), bought alongside the TMC contract
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — tracking platforms exist, but the itinerary data that feeds them is incomplete
**Target Buyer:** Head of global security or travel risk manager, with HR and the travel manager
**Automation Potential:** High for locating and messaging, low for the response itself

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Employers owe a duty of care to employees they send abroad, and large multinationals are judged on it by their own boards and insurers. When an earthquake, coup, attack or hurricane hits, the first question is who is there, and the answer depends on itinerary data: TMC bookings, hotel stays booked outside the programme, expense records, and mobile check-ins. The contest is won by completeness and speed of the traveller list, not by the booking tool, which puts risk-intelligence and assistance providers alongside the TMC.

## Current Tools & Gaps
International SOS, Crisis24 and similar providers ingest TMC itinerary feeds and send alerts and check-in requests; TMCs offer their own tracking dashboards; expense and card platforms hold the out-of-programme stays. Gaps: hotels and ground travel booked outside the TMC; multi-city trips changed on the road; contractors and new hires missing from HR feeds; and check-in responses that never close the loop.

# The Debit Memo That Eats the Month's Margin

**Niche:** [[niches/travel-agencies/diaspora-vfr-agencies/profile|Diaspora & VFR Fare Agencies]]
**Industry:** [[industries/travel-agencies|Travel Agencies]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A single debit memo on a family's reissued ticket can cost more than the agency earned on a dozen bookings, and it arrives weeks after anyone can remember why.
**Tags:** #gradient-boosting #logistic-regression #evaluation-metrics #compliance #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to change or refund a family's net-fare ticket the day their plans change without triggering a debit memo, and whoever reissues changes fastest under the consolidator's rules takes the account.

## The Problem
Margins on net fares are thin — tens of dollars a ticket (estimate). An airline audit finds a reissue that ignored a penalty or a fare-combination rule and debits the consolidator, which passes it to the agency with a fee. The agency pays, because disputing is slow and the evidence is a chat thread.

## Why It's Still Broken
Nobody checks reissues against the rules before submission, the debit memo history sits with the consolidator, and the agency has no view of which kinds of change keep going wrong.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A pre-submission check on every reissue against the fare rules and the agency's own past debit memos, and a monthly summary from the consolidator's records showing which airlines, routes and change types generate memos, so the owner fixes the habit, not the incident.

## Who Feels the Pain
Agency owners whose month disappears in one memo; consolidators carrying the dispute workload; families charged more when margins are rebuilt.

## Impact If Fixed
Most avoidable memos are caught before ticketing, and the agency can price changes knowing its real risk.

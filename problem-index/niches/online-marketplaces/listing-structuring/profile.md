# Listing Structuring

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/online-marketplaces|Online Marketplaces]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn an amateur's photograph and paragraph into the structured attributes everything downstream depends on — and whoever does that takes the account, because search, pricing, matching and recommendation all rest on structure the seller never provided.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.7B US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~8% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Low — amateur text against somebody else's taxonomy
**Target Buyer:** Search, catalogue and category teams
**Automation Potential:** Very High — extraction is the capability that has most improved

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Search infrastructure and learned ranking are mature commodities, and marketplace search still fails because the inventory is described by amateurs against a taxonomy designed by someone else. The seller writes what they would say to a person in a shop, chooses a category from a tree built for a different purpose, and leaves most attribute fields blank because filling them is work. Everything downstream — retrieval, filtering, matching, valuation, recommendation — depends on structure that was never supplied. Extracting it from the photograph and the prose is now straightforwardly achievable and is the highest-leverage automation available in the category, because a single extraction improves five systems at once.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Required attribute fields, category suggestion, and some image tagging. The gaps: attributes are requested rather than extracted, so most stay empty; the taxonomy is designed by category managers and does not match how sellers or buyers describe things; free-text descriptions carry information that is indexed literally rather than interpreted; images carry attributes nobody reads; and there is no measure of how much structure the inventory actually has.

# Condition Described in Words Nobody Agreed

**Niche:** [[niches/online-marketplaces/catalogued-product-search/profile|Catalogued Product Search]]
**Industry:** [[industries/online-marketplaces|Online Marketplaces]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Used goods are sold by a condition label the seller chose from a list nobody defined, so excellent means different things to two sellers and something else again to the buyer.
**Tags:** #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #hypothesis-testing #confidence-intervals #object-detection #automation #quick-win #logistic-regression
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this sub-niche is fighting to put the offer a buyer will actually be happy with at the top for an item they already named — and whoever does that takes the transaction, because the same item is one tab away on another marketplace.

## The Problem
Two sellers list the same used item as excellent condition. One means unmarked and boxed; the other means working with visible wear. The buyer, comparing on a grid, takes the cheaper. It arrives scratched, they dispute, the seller believes they described it accurately, and both are right within their own reading of a word the platform never defined. Condition is the largest source of dissatisfaction in used-goods marketplaces, it is described by an undefined vocabulary, and the platform has the returns and dispute data that would let it calibrate the labels and does not.

## Why It's Still Broken
A short condition dropdown is simple and adding definitions makes listing harder, which is friction at the moment sellers are least tolerant of it. Enforcing a definition means adjudicating disagreements the platform would rather leave between the parties. The disputes are handled case by case and never aggregated into a finding about the vocabulary. And every marketplace uses different words, so there is no external standard to adopt.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Define the labels and calibrate them against outcomes. Publish precise, illustrated definitions for each condition level, with photographs of what each looks like, which is a content exercise and removes most of the honest disagreement immediately. Calibrate labels against dispute and return rates, so a level whose listings are disputed three times as often as another is either mis-defined or mis-used and can be corrected — this is the measurement that turns a vocabulary into a standard. Require the specific disclosures that matter per category, since a scratch matters on a watch and not on a tool, and a structured prompt gets it where free text does not. Use the listing photographs to verify the claim automatically, flagging clear mismatches before publication, which is now tractable and catches the worst cases at the cheapest point. Report each seller's condition accuracy from their own dispute history, and surface it to buyers, which lets a careful seller compete on something other than price. Rank on condition-adjusted value rather than on price against a label that means nothing. Feed dispute outcomes back into the definitions, so the vocabulary improves rather than remaining as first written. And do not compare offers across sellers on condition alone until the labels are calibrated, because the grid currently invites exactly that comparison.

## Who Feels the Pain
Buyers receiving something other than what they read; honest sellers undercut by a looser reading of the same word; and the platform absorbing disputes caused by its own vocabulary.

## Impact If Fixed
Illustrated definitions are a content exercise that removes most honest disagreement, and calibrating labels against dispute rates turns a vocabulary into a standard. Per-seller condition accuracy lets a careful seller compete on something other than price.

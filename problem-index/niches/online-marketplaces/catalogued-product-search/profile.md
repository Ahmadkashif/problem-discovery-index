# Catalogued Product Search

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/online-marketplaces|Online Marketplaces]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this sub-niche is fighting to put the offer a buyer will actually be happy with at the top for an item they already named — and whoever does that takes the transaction, because the same item is one tab away on another marketplace.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$3.1B US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~15% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** High
**Target Buyer:** Buyers who know what they want, and the sellers competing for them
**Automation Potential:** Very High

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
When a buyer names an identifiable product, the marketplace's job is to decide which of many similar offers to show. Everything needed is structured: the item's identity, its condition, the price, the shipping, the seller's history, the return terms. The contest is predicting which offer will produce a satisfied buyer rather than which will produce a click, and the competitor is the same product on another marketplace one tab away. This is a ranking problem with a rich feature set and abundant labels, which makes it the most technically tractable surface in the industry — and the one where a small ranking improvement is worth the most, because the traffic volume is here.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Learned ranking over price, seller rating, delivery and condition; product matching to a catalogue; faceted filters; and sponsored placement. The gaps: satisfaction is not predicted, only clicks and conversion; condition descriptions are unstandardised, which is the main source of buyer disappointment in used goods; total cost including shipping and returns is frequently not the ranking basis; sponsored placement's effect on buyer outcomes is unmeasured; and product matching errors put the wrong offers under the wrong item.

# Seller Onboarding

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/online-marketplaces|Online Marketplaces]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get a new seller to their first sale before they decide the marketplace does not work — and whoever does that takes the supply, because the first weeks decide whether a seller stays for years.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.9B US in supply acquisition and churn
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~9% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Low — polished flows, poor outcomes
**Target Buyer:** Seller growth and marketplace operations functions
**Automation Potential:** Very High — listing assistance is directly automatable

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Listing flows are polished and well tested, and new sellers produce their worst listings during the exact window in which they decide whether the marketplace works for them. A first listing has a poor photograph, a title written for a human rather than for retrieval, missing attributes and a guessed price. It therefore ranks badly, gets no impressions, does not sell, and the seller concludes there is no demand. Supply acquisition spends heavily to bring them in and the product spends nothing on whether their first three listings succeed. The whole marketplace's supply side rests on a window of a few weeks that nobody owns.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Listing wizards, photo guidelines, category suggestions, and onboarding emails. The gaps: no measurement of first-listing quality or its effect on outcomes; no assistance that actually improves a listing rather than validating its completeness; no early signal to the seller that a listing will not sell; no comparison to what works in the category; and time-to-first-sale is not tracked as the retention metric it is.

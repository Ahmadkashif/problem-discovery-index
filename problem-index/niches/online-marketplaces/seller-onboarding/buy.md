# Activation and Onboarding Practice

**Niche:** [[niches/online-marketplaces/seller-onboarding/profile|Seller Onboarding]]
**Industry:** [[industries/online-marketplaces|Online Marketplaces]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Consumer software developed activation metrics and onboarding practice around getting a user to their first moment of value, and marketplaces measure listings created.
**Tags:** #survival-analysis #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #confidence-intervals #revenue-impact #hypothesis-testing #gradient-boosting #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get a new seller to their first sale before they decide the marketplace does not work — and whoever does that takes the supply, because the first weeks decide whether a seller stays for years.

## The Problem
Identifying the moment at which a new user experiences the product's value, measuring how many reach it and how fast, and redesigning everything before it to get them there is the central practice of consumer product growth. It has a vocabulary, a measurement discipline and a substantial body of case knowledge. Marketplace seller onboarding measures registrations and listings created — both of which are steps before the moment of value rather than the moment itself.

## What Already Exists
Activation metric definition and measurement practice; cohort retention analysis with survival curves; onboarding flow experimentation; behavioural triggering for users stalling at a step; in-product guidance and progressive disclosure; and lifecycle messaging tied to behavioural state.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a moment of value that depends on a third party. It requires: (1) activation defined as the first sale rather than as a completed action, since the seller's moment of value requires a buyer and the platform's usual activation framing assumes the user can reach it alone — this dependence is what makes marketplace onboarding genuinely different; (2) the platform intervening on the demand side to produce that first sale, which is a lever consumer products do not have and which is legitimate here since exposure is the platform's to allocate; (3) listing quality as the intermediate metric, because it is the thing the seller controls and the thing that determines whether demand can find them; (4) cohort analysis by category, since time-to-first-sale varies enormously across inventory types and a platform average is useless for intervention; and (5) messaging triggered by listing performance rather than by elapsed time, which is the signal that actually predicts a seller giving up.

## Target Customer
Seller growth functions, marketplace operators, and the product growth community for whom a two-sided activation dependency is an under-examined case.

## Impact If Solved
Activation practice assumes the user can reach value alone and a seller cannot — they need a buyer. Allocating exposure to produce a first sale is a lever consumer products lack and the platform legitimately has, and it is the one that makes onboarding work here.

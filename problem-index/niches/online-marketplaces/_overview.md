## Why These Niches

A marketplace is a matching problem wearing a commerce interface. Liquidity — the probability that a listing finds a buyer and a buyer finds what they want — determines whether both sides stay, and it is hardest exactly where marketplaces are most valuable: unique, one-of-a-kind and long-tail inventory where no two listings are the same. Every serious operator competes on this and most measure it only through the transactions that completed, which is the one view that cannot see the failures. That makes liquidity the largest contested surface in the category.

Search and discovery **failed the filter as one niche**. Where many sellers offer the same identifiable product, the contest is ranking among substitutes — price, seller reliability, delivery — over a catalogue that exists, and the competitor is another marketplace offering the same item. Where every listing is one of a kind, there is no catalogue, the description was written by an amateur, and the contest is understanding what the thing is well enough to surface it to somebody who was not searching for it. The techniques, the data, the buyer's intent and the definition of a good result share nothing. Decomposed below.

The two underdigitised areas are both about the seller's first weeks and their prices. New sellers produce their worst listings during the exact window in which they decide whether the marketplace works, and the listing quality determines the discoverability that determines whether they stay. And for unique inventory there is no reference price, so a seller guesses, and the pricing tools that do exist are frequently better outside the platform than inside it.

The two underserved constituencies are the trust and safety reviewer making consequential judgement calls on cases that are ambiguous by construction while being measured on throughput, and the support agent handling an appeal from a seller whose income has stopped, who cannot see why the account was suspended.

The automation niches are structuring amateur listing text and images into the attributes everything downstream depends on, and the complete record of demand that went unmet — which most operators treat as absence rather than as the most actionable dataset a matching business could have.

## Filter Notes

Seven of the eight level-1 niches are terminal. **Search & Discovery** is not: it names a function performed over two kinds of inventory whose contests are unrelated. Catalogued product search is a ranking problem among substitutes, where the item is identified, the attributes are known, and the question is which seller's offer to show — solved with learned ranking over a rich feature set, competing against other marketplaces listing the same product. Unique item discovery has no catalogue, an amateur's description, a photograph that carries most of the information, and a buyer who frequently cannot name what they want — the contest is representation and surfacing rather than ranking, and the competitor is the buyer giving up. Decomposed into two contested sub-niches.

Two candidates were rejected. *Payments, escrow and dispute resolution* was rejected because the layer is mature and commoditised and its remaining contest belongs to the payment processing industry covered separately in this vault. *Shipping and fulfilment* was rejected because it belongs to the logistics industries covered separately, and a marketplace's role in it is integration rather than competition.

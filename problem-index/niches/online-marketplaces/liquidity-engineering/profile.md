# Liquidity Engineering

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/online-marketplaces|Online Marketplaces]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to raise the probability that a one-of-a-kind listing finds its buyer — and whoever does that takes the market, because both sides stay or leave on that number and it is worst exactly where marketplaces are most valuable.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$5.2B US attributable to matching rather than to transacting
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~25% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Low — measured through the transactions that happened
**Target Buyer:** Marketplace operators and the investors valuing their network effects
**Automation Potential:** High — match rate is measurable and the levers are known

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Matching works when a thousand sellers offer the same product, because the buyer names it and the market ranks the offers. It breaks when every listing is one of a kind — a vintage guitar, a piece of handmade furniture, a wholesale line from a small maker, a used camera in a particular condition — which is precisely the inventory that marketplaces exist to serve and that no general retailer can. The probability that such a listing finds a buyer, and that a buyer finds something they want, is the marketplace's whole value and is worst where its differentiation lives. Operators compete on it continuously and measure it almost entirely through completed transactions, which is the one view that cannot see a failure.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Conversion and gross merchandise value reporting, ranking and recommendation investment, category-level supply acquisition, and promotional demand generation. The gaps: no match rate measured per listing cohort; no measure of demand that went unmet; supply and demand imbalance is not diagnosed by category or attribute; no intervention targeted at listings that will not sell; and no account of how long inventory sits before a seller gives up.

# The Search That Returned Nothing

**Niche:** [[niches/online-marketplaces/unmet-demand-intelligence/profile|Unmet Demand Intelligence]]
**Industry:** [[industries/online-marketplaces|Online Marketplaces]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A zero-result search is logged as a search quality problem and fixed by loosening the match, which replaces an honest empty page with irrelevant results and discards the clearest demand signal the platform gets.
**Tags:** #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #word-embeddings #k-means-clustering #revenue-impact #hypothesis-testing #quick-win #confidence-intervals
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn every search that found nothing and every listing that expired into a map of where supply and demand fail to meet — and whoever does that takes the market, because that map is the operating instruction for a matching business.

## The Problem
A buyer searches for something specific the marketplace does not have. The search returns zero results, which is logged as a failure of search. The remedy applied is to broaden matching so that something always comes back — and now the buyer gets ten irrelevant items instead of an empty page, leaves anyway, and the zero-result metric looks better. The clearest demand signal the platform receives, a buyer stating exactly what they want in their own words at the moment they want it, has been converted into a click-through statistic on results nobody wanted, and the underlying supply gap is now invisible.

## Why It's Still Broken
Zero results look like a broken product and the instinct to always return something is strong. The metric that improves is real, and the buyer's outcome is unchanged or worse, which nobody measures. Search teams own the zero-result rate and supply teams own inventory, and the handover between them does not exist. And a query that found nothing is treated as a defect rather than as information.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Treat the empty result as a signal and handle the buyer honestly. Route every zero-result and no-click search into the demand analysis rather than only into the search quality report, which is a pipeline change and preserves the signal the broadening destroys. Tell the buyer honestly that nothing matches and offer something useful — an alert when it appears, a request posted to sellers, a genuinely close alternative labelled as such — which is a better experience than ten irrelevant items and creates a durable demand record. Build the want-list as a product, since a buyer who tells you what they want and is notified when it arrives is a matching success the platform currently forgoes entirely. Distinguish a query with no supply from a query the search failed to match, since both return nothing and the remedies are opposite — and conflating them is the mistake that broadening makes structurally. Report both rates separately. Measure the outcome of broadened results, because the belief that showing something beats showing nothing is testable and appears never to have been tested. Publish the top unmet queries to sellers, who will list against them. And measure how many unmet searches later convert once supply appears, which is the proof that the signal is worth capturing.

## Who Feels the Pain
Buyers shown irrelevant results in place of an honest answer; sellers who would have listed the item had anybody told them; and operators whose clearest demand signal is being optimised away.

## Impact If Fixed
Broadening the match converts the platform's clearest demand signal into a click-through statistic and hides the supply gap. Separating no-supply from no-match is what makes the remedy correct, and a want-list turns an abandoned search into a match the platform currently forgoes.

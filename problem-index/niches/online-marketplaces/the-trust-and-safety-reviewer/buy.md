# Adjudication and Quality Assurance Practice

**Niche:** [[niches/online-marketplaces/the-trust-and-safety-reviewer/profile|The Trust & Safety Reviewer]]
**Industry:** [[industries/online-marketplaces|Online Marketplaces]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Clinical review, claims adjudication and legal decision-making all built practice for consistent judgement on ambiguous cases, and marketplace review measures cases per hour.

**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to support a reviewer whose every case is genuinely ambiguous — and whoever does that takes the quality, because these are the decisions automation could not make and they are being timed like data entry.

## The Problem
Making consistent decisions on ambiguous cases at volume, and knowing whether you are, is a problem several professions have taken seriously. Claims adjudication has calibration, dual review on high-value cases and audited consistency. Clinical review has structured second opinions and adjudication committees. Legal decision-making has precedent as its organising principle. Marketplace trust and safety has a policy document, a queue and an hourly target.

## What Already Exists
Calibration sessions bringing reviewers to a shared standard; dual review and adjudication for high-stakes or disputed cases; inter-rater reliability measurement with appropriate statistics; precedent and case-law systems; structured decision rationale capture; and audited consistency programmes.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to volume far beyond what those professions handle and to decisions that affect livelihoods without a formal process. It requires: (1) calibration at scale, which means sampling and measuring rather than convening everybody, but which is measurable in a way the existing professions would recognise and which nobody here runs; (2) precedent retrieval by case similarity rather than by citation, since reviewers will not search a case database and need the relevant prior decisions surfaced automatically; (3) dual review targeted by consequence and ambiguity rather than applied uniformly, since the volume forbids reviewing everything twice and the complexity score identifies where it pays; (4) rationale capture that costs seconds rather than minutes, since it is the input to both precedent and policy improvement and anything expensive will not happen; and (5) a route from repeated ambiguity to policy change, which is what a precedent system does naturally and what this industry has no mechanism for.

## Target Customer
Trust and safety organisations, their outsourced review partners, and the adjudication and quality assurance professions.

## Impact If Solved
Several professions take consistent judgement on ambiguous cases seriously and this one counts cases per hour. Precedent surfaced by case similarity is what makes consistency achievable at this volume, and targeted dual review puts the second opinion where it pays.

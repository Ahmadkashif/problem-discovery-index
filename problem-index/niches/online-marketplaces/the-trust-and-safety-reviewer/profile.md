# The Trust & Safety Reviewer

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/online-marketplaces|Online Marketplaces]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to support a reviewer whose every case is genuinely ambiguous — and whoever does that takes the quality, because these are the decisions automation could not make and they are being timed like data entry.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.2B US in review operations
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~6% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** None — throughput metrics on ambiguous cases
**Target Buyer:** Trust and safety organisations and their leadership
**Automation Potential:** Medium — the support is automatable, the judgement is the job

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Automation handles the clear cases. What reaches a human reviewer is, by construction, the residue that automation could not decide — which means every case in their queue is genuinely ambiguous, sits on a policy edge, and requires a judgement that reasonable people could disagree about. They are then measured on cases per hour and on agreement with a quality sample, as though the work were classification at volume. The metrics treat interchangeable throughput as the goal on a queue that contains only the hard cases, which is the clearest instance in this industry of a measurement system built for the wrong work.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Review queues, policy documentation, escalation paths, and quality sampling. The gaps: no case complexity weighting, so a two-minute case and a twenty-minute one count the same; policy guidance does not cover the edges that reach review, which is the whole queue; no precedent access, so similar past decisions are invisible; no route for a reviewer to flag that the policy itself is wrong; and the quality metric penalises defensible disagreement.

# Nobody to Escalate To

**Niche:** [[niches/membership-community-platforms/the-volunteer-moderator/profile|The Volunteer Moderator]]
**Industry:** [[industries/membership-community-platforms|Membership & Community Platforms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A member posts something that suggests they are in danger, and the volunteer who sees it has a delete button and a rules page.
**Tags:** #compliance #quick-win #worker-facing #workflow-orchestration #automation #evaluation-metrics #large-language-models #descriptive-statistics
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to support people doing unpaid emotional labour with no tooling, no policy and nobody to escalate to — and whoever does it interrupts a burnout cycle that is currently treated as inevitable.

## The Problem
Occasionally something arrives that is beyond the role: a member in crisis, a credible threat, a disclosure of harm, a legal matter. The volunteer is a member of a hobby community who agreed to help keep things tidy. There is no escalation path, no guidance, no resource to point the person toward, and no one to tell. They improvise, carry it afterwards, and often stop volunteering — and the next person will face the same thing with the same nothing.

## Why It's Still Broken
Moderation tooling was designed around content removal, so the serious human cases have no place in the workflow — a system built to delete posts has no concept of a person who needs help. Platforms are wary of appearing to provide advice. The cases are rare enough to be unplanned for and serious enough to be formative. And nobody asks moderators what they have had to deal with.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Prepare the path before it is needed. Define an escalation path for the serious categories, which is the fix and is what turns an impossible moment into a procedure. Provide vetted resources to point a member toward, since a volunteer should not be inventing a crisis response. Detect crisis language and surface guidance immediately, as the recognition itself is part of the difficulty. Tell the moderator plainly what is and is not theirs to handle, because the absence of a boundary is why they carry everything. Give them a named person to contact, even if that is the founder, and make it a stated arrangement rather than an assumption. Log the case so the person is followed up rather than lost. Debrief afterwards, since carrying it alone is the lasting part. Prepare the guidance in advance rather than during, as nobody reads a policy for the first time in a crisis. Review the cases periodically so the guidance improves. And accept that some categories should go to the platform rather than to a volunteer, which requires the platform to be willing to receive them.

## Who Feels the Pain
Volunteers improvising through something serious; members in crisis receiving whatever the volunteer could manage; founders who find out afterwards; and communities that lose their most conscientious moderators to exactly these cases.

## Impact If Fixed
A system built to delete posts has no concept of a person who needs help, so the hardest cases have no workflow. A defined escalation path with vetted resources converts an impossible improvisation into a procedure prepared in advance.

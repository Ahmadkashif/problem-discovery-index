# Support for Unpaid Emotional Labour

**Niche:** [[niches/membership-community-platforms/the-volunteer-moderator/profile|The Volunteer Moderator]]
**Industry:** [[industries/membership-community-platforms|Membership & Community Platforms]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Someone volunteered to help with a community and is now handling a harassment report at eleven at night with no training and nobody to ask.
**Tags:** #worker-facing #workflow-orchestration #compliance #automation #evaluation-metrics #large-language-models #descriptive-statistics #confidence-intervals
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to support people doing unpaid emotional labour with no tooling, no policy and nobody to escalate to — and whoever does it interrupts a burnout cycle that is currently treated as inevitable.

## The Problem
Volunteer moderators absorb the parts of a community that are genuinely difficult: the harassment, the dispute that will not end, the member in distress, the person who has to be removed. They do it unpaid, alongside their actual lives, among people they know socially, with no preparation for any of it. The retaliation lands on them personally. The burnout is predictable, the replacement is another volunteer, and the cycle is regarded by everyone as how this works.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Volunteers are free, so their sustainability was never a cost to be managed — labour that does not appear in a budget does not get invested in. Moderator tooling investment goes to large platforms with paid teams. The founder, who could help, is often the conflicted party. And the burnout looks like an individual's choice to step back rather than a designed outcome.

## What to Build
Equip and protect them. Provide policy with worked examples so a decision has a basis rather than resting on personal judgement, which is the core and removes most of the isolation. Give them an escalation path for the serious categories, because handling a crisis alone is the heaviest thing in the role and there is currently nowhere to send it. Support the decision with precedent, a second opinion and suggested language, since the communication is often harder than the decision. Reduce exposure to harmful content with filtering and previews, as repeated exposure is the occupational harm and is partly avoidable. Rotate difficult cases rather than defaulting to whoever is online, which spreads a load currently borne by the most conscientious. Shield them from retaliation, since decisions currently carry a personal cost in a community where everyone knows everyone. Measure their load, because nobody does and the first sign of a problem is a resignation. Recognise the work visibly, as unrecognised unpaid labour ends predictably. Offer a structured break rather than treating absence as abandonment. And give the founder a view of the moderation load, since they usually do not know what their volunteers are absorbing.

## Target Customer
Trust and safety and product leadership, volunteer moderators, founders, and moderation tooling vendors.

## Impact If Built
Labour that does not appear in a budget does not get invested in, which is why the volunteers have a delete button. Policy with examples, an escalation path and case rotation address the three things that actually cause the burnout.

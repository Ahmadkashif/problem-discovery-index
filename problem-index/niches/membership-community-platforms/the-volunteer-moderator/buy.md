**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Large platforms learned that moderation harms moderators and built wellbeing practice, and volunteer moderators receive none of it.

**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to support people doing unpaid emotional labour with no tooling, no policy and nobody to escalate to — and whoever does it interrupts a burnout cycle that is currently treated as inevitable.

## The Problem
Large platforms, after considerable public pressure, developed moderator wellbeing practice: exposure limits, content blurring and preview controls, rotation away from the hardest queues, counselling access, and workload monitoring. It exists because the harm to moderators became undeniable and expensive. Volunteer moderators in small communities do structurally similar work, with the aggravating factor that they know the people involved, and receive none of it.

## What Already Exists
Exposure limits and rotation policies; content blurring and preview controls; counselling and support access; workload monitoring; and training and calibration programmes.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to unpaid moderators inside the community they moderate. It requires: (1) no employment relationship, so protections must be product features rather than workplace policy — this is the substantive difference and determines what is even possible; (2) moderators who know and will continue to interact with the people they sanction, which large platforms deliberately avoid and which is unavoidable here; (3) no budget for counselling or staffing, so support must be built in rather than provided; (4) much lower volume but far higher personal stakes per case; and (5) a founder who is often a party to the dispute, removing the neutral escalation large platforms have.

## Target Customer
Trust and safety leadership, volunteer moderators, founders, and moderation platform vendors.

## Impact If Solved
Large platforms built wellbeing practice because the harm became undeniable, and volunteers get none of it. The aggravating factor here — moderators who know the people they sanction — is precisely what large platforms designed out and small communities cannot.

# Moving Without Losing the Community

**Niche:** [[niches/membership-community-platforms/migration-and-portability/profile|Migration & Portability]]
**Industry:** [[industries/membership-community-platforms|Membership & Community Platforms]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** The operator moves platforms and arrives with a member list, no history and two thirds of the people.
**Tags:** #data-integration #graph-theory #workflow-orchestration #automation #evaluation-metrics #compliance #confidence-intervals #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to let an operator move a community without losing its history, its structure and half its members — and whoever makes the move survivable wins the operators every competitor is trying to take.

## The Problem
A community is years of threads, relationships, roles, subgroups, events and accumulated reference material, plus a set of active subscriptions. When it moves, most of that does not come. The threads become an unsearchable archive or nothing; the structure is rebuilt by hand; every member must re-register and re-enter payment details; and the ones who do not are gone. The operator knows this, which is why they stay on a platform they have outgrown, and the challenger knows it, which is why they cannot win.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Portability reduces switching costs, so incumbents have no reason to build it and challengers cannot build it alone — a capability that requires cooperation from the party it disadvantages does not get built. Content migration is genuinely hard because threads, permissions and relationships are structural rather than tabular. Subscriptions involve payment credentials that cannot simply be handed over. And nobody measures what a migration costs in members.

## What to Build
Make the move survivable from the receiving side. Import content with thread structure, authorship and dates preserved, which is the core and is the difference between a community with a memory and one starting over. Reconstruct roles, subgroups and permissions rather than requiring a rebuild, since that is days of work and is inferable from the export. Handle member re-registration with as little friction as possible, because every step loses people and the loss is where communities die. Solve the subscription transfer with the payment providers rather than asking members to re-enter details, as that single step is the largest loss point and is technically addressable. Sequence the migration so the community is not in two places at once, which is the other common failure. Measure the loss rate at each step, so the next migration is better and the operator can plan. Keep the old community readable during the transition rather than switching it off. Communicate to members on the operator's behalf, since the messaging determines how many follow. Support a trial period before full commitment, which reduces the operator's risk of a failed move. And publish the migration success rate, because an honest number is what would make operators willing to move at all.

## Target Customer
Challenger platform leadership, operators stuck on an outgrown platform, members who would be lost in a move, and data migration vendors.

## Impact If Built
A capability that requires cooperation from the party it disadvantages does not get built, so portability stalls. Building it from the receiving side — with thread structure, roles and subscriptions preserved — is what makes switching possible at all.

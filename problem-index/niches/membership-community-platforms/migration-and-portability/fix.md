# Everyone Has to Re-Enter Their Card

**Niche:** [[niches/membership-community-platforms/migration-and-portability/profile|Migration & Portability]]
**Industry:** [[industries/membership-community-platforms|Membership & Community Platforms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The community moved and a third of the members never completed the new subscription form.
**Tags:** #quick-win #workflow-orchestration #automation #revenue-impact #evaluation-metrics #compliance #data-integration #descriptive-statistics
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to let an operator move a community without losing its history, its structure and half its members — and whoever makes the move survivable wins the operators every competitor is trying to take.

## The Problem
The migration's largest single loss point is not the content or the structure. It is that every member must find an email, click a link, create an account and enter payment details again. Each step loses people, the payment step loses the most, and the members lost are not the disengaged ones — they are anyone who was busy that week. The operator discovers the scale of it afterwards, when the new community is a third smaller and there is no way back.

## Why It's Still Broken
Subscriptions are held by the incumbent's payment integration, so transferring them requires the incumbent's cooperation or the member's re-entry — a credential held by the party being left is not going to be handed over voluntarily. Nobody measured the step-by-step loss. Operators plan the content migration and assume the members will follow. And the loss is discovered only once.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Attack the payment step and measure every other one. Work with payment providers to transfer subscriptions where the rails allow, which is the fix and addresses the largest loss point directly. Reduce the re-registration to the minimum possible steps, since each one costs members measurably. Let members join first and pay later rather than gating entry on payment, because getting them into the new community is what preserves the relationship. Measure completion at each step, so the operator knows where the loss is rather than only that it happened. Sequence the communication with the founder's voice rather than a platform notification, as members respond to the person and not the product. Give members a long window and repeated prompts, since a single email during a busy week is how most are lost. Keep the old community accessible so a late arrival is still possible. Contact the non-movers individually, because the list is finite and the founder can work through it. Report the migration loss rate honestly, which lets operators plan and lets platforms improve. And offer the operator a rehearsal, since a migration is done once and cannot be learned from.

## Who Feels the Pain
Operators who lose a third of their revenue in a move; members who meant to follow and did not; challengers who cannot win because switching is too costly; and communities that do not survive their own migration.

## Impact If Fixed
A credential held by the party being left is not going to be handed over voluntarily, which is why the payment step is the biggest loss. Letting members join first and pay later, with step-by-step measurement, preserves the relationship while the payment is sorted out.

# Everything Runs Through One Person

**Niche:** [[niches/membership-community-platforms/community-health-and-structure/profile|Community Health & Structure]]
**Industry:** [[industries/membership-community-platforms|Membership & Community Platforms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The founder takes a week off and the community goes quiet, and nobody had noticed how much of it was them.
**Tags:** #quick-win #graph-theory #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #worker-facing #confidence-intervals #automation #change-point-detection
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to surface the structural failures that are plainly legible in the interaction graph — centralisation, closure, exclusion — before they become a shrinking community.

## The Problem
The founder answers every question, greets every newcomer, starts most discussions and replies to most posts. It works, which is why it continues, and it means the community's activity is largely their activity. When they are ill, busy or on holiday the community stops, members notice, and some of them conclude it has died. Nobody measured the dependency because the community looked healthy, and it looked healthy because of the dependency.

## Why It's Still Broken
The founder's participation is indistinguishable from community activity in every available metric, so the dependency is concealed by the thing that causes it — a measure that counts all activity equally cannot reveal that one person is producing it. Delegation feels like lowering standards. There is no prompt to build redundancy. And the failure only appears during an absence.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Measure the dependency and reduce it deliberately. Report the founder's share of replies, greetings and thread starts, which is the fix and will be a number that changes the conversation immediately. Report what activity looks like with the founder's contributions removed, since that is the community's actual independent health. Identify the members who could answer and are not asked, as most communities have several and they would usually be glad to. Route questions to those members rather than defaulting to the founder, which is a routing change with a structural effect. Recognise the members who do answer, because visible recognition is what converts a member into a regular contributor. Detect the topics only the founder answers, so the gap is specific and coverable. Simulate an absence deliberately for a short period, as a planned test is far better than an unplanned one. Track the dependency measure over time, since reducing it is a programme rather than an action. Warn when it rises, which it does gradually as the founder absorbs more. And plan holidays around it rather than discovering the problem during one.

## Who Feels the Pain
Founders who cannot take a break; members who experience a dead community during an absence; potential contributors who are never asked; and businesses whose continuity rests on one person's availability.

## Impact If Fixed
A measure that counts all activity equally cannot reveal that one person is producing it, so the dependency hides behind the health it creates. Reporting the founder's share of replies is one query and is the number that starts the delegation.

**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to reach the member who stopped showing up months before they cancel — and whoever detects the silence early enough to act recovers revenue that is currently written off as inevitable.

## The Problem
Subscription businesses distinguish several kinds of churn: voluntary cancellation, involuntary payment failure, and passive non-renewal by a customer who stopped using the product long before. They built different detection and different responses for each, because a save offer to someone who stopped using the product six months ago is pointless and a payment retry to a happy customer is trivial. Community platforms report a single churn rate.

## What Already Exists
Churn taxonomy separating voluntary, involuntary and passive; usage-based health scoring; proactive outreach on engagement decline; save flows differentiated by cause; and win-back segmentation.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a product whose usage depends on other people. It requires: (1) engagement that a member cannot restore alone, since returning to a community where nobody spoke to them changes nothing — the intervention must produce a connection rather than prompt a visit, which is the substantive difference; (2) a health signal built from a social graph rather than from feature usage; (3) a founder executing rather than a retention team, so the output must be a short list of people to speak to; (4) an emotional relationship where a discount is close to insulting; and (5) very small absolute revenue per member, which rules out expensive intervention and makes automation the only option.

## Target Customer
Product and revenue leadership, founders, members, and subscription retention vendors.

## Impact If Solved
The churn taxonomy is standard and community platforms use one number. The adaptation is that a disengaged member cannot re-engage alone, which means every intervention has to produce a connection rather than a visit.

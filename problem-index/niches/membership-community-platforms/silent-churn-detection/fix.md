# Learning About It at the Renewal

**Niche:** [[niches/membership-community-platforms/silent-churn-detection/profile|Silent Churn Detection]]
**Industry:** [[industries/membership-community-platforms|Membership & Community Platforms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A third of the paying members have not opened the app this month and nobody has looked at that list.
**Tags:** #quick-win #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #revenue-impact #automation #confidence-intervals #worker-facing #change-point-detection
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to reach the member who stopped showing up months before they cancel — and whoever detects the silence early enough to act recovers revenue that is currently written off as inevitable.

## The Problem
The list exists. Every community has a set of members who are paying and have not visited in thirty days, and in most it is a substantial fraction of the membership. Nobody produces the list, nobody looks at it, and nobody contacts anyone on it. It is simultaneously the community's cancellation pipeline, its revenue risk and — for a business selling belonging — a group of people paying for something they are not receiving.

## Why It's Still Broken
Dashboards report who was active, so the inactive are the absence of the number rather than a population — a metric that counts presence never produces the list of the missing. Contacting them feels like admitting something. Founders are occupied with the people who are present. And nobody sized the group.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Produce the list and use it. Report the members who are paying and have not visited in thirty, sixty and ninety days, which is the fix and is one query nobody has run. Size the revenue attached to it, since the number will be larger than expected and will prompt action on its own. Contact them personally rather than by campaign, because a message from a person is the only thing that works here and the list is short enough. Ask rather than encourage, as a genuine question about what happened gets answers and a nudge does not. Offer a pause instead of leaving them paying for nothing, which is honest, reduces resentment and frequently preserves the relationship. Reconnect them to a subgroup or a person rather than to the community generally, since the general invitation is what did not work the first time. Track the list weekly, as it is small enough to manage and grows quietly. Segment by tenure, because a member who drifted after two years and one who never started are different problems. Measure how many return after contact, which will show whether the effort is worth repeating. And report the paying-but-absent count as a headline number, since it is the clearest statement of the community's actual condition.

## Who Feels the Pain
Members paying for something they are not getting; founders surprised by cancellations; communities shrinking invisibly; and platforms whose retention problem has an obvious and unexamined leading indicator.

## Impact If Fixed
A metric that counts presence never produces the list of the missing, so the cancellation pipeline is invisible. One query produces it, and the list is short enough for a founder to work through personally.

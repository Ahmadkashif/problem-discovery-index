# Trust and Safety at Platform Scale

**Niche:** [[niches/membership-community-platforms/moderation-economics/profile|Moderation Economics]]
**Industry:** [[industries/membership-community-platforms|Membership & Community Platforms]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Large platforms built policy, classifiers, tiered enforcement and appeals, and none of it is available to a community with four volunteers.
**Tags:** #compliance #bert #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #workflow-orchestration #automation #large-language-models #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to give a community of four hundred people the moderation capability that is currently priced for platforms with trust and safety departments — and whoever does it protects the communities where the harm is most personal.

## The Problem
Trust and safety at scale is a developed discipline: written policy with worked examples, automated classifiers, tiered enforcement, appeals, transparency reporting, moderator wellbeing programmes and escalation to specialist teams for crisis and legal matters. The apparatus is real and expensive. The communities where a harassment incident affects a group of people who know each other have none of it.

## What Already Exists
Policy frameworks with enforcement tiers; content classifiers for common harm categories; appeals and review processes; moderator wellbeing and rotation practice; and crisis and legal escalation paths.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to an intimate community with no staff. It requires: (1) everything delivered as a product rather than as a team, since there is no department and never will be — this is the binding constraint and determines every design choice; (2) an intimate setting where the parties know each other, so removal and sanction have social consequences a large platform's enforcement does not; (3) moderators who are members with relationships to the people they moderate, which is a conflict large platforms specifically avoid; (4) a founder who set the community's norms personally, so policy must reflect their intent rather than a generic standard; and (5) volumes low enough that per-case cost matters more than throughput.

## Target Customer
Founders and volunteer moderators, platform trust and safety leadership, members, and trust and safety vendors.

## Impact If Solved
The discipline is mature and is delivered as a department. Delivering it as a product to an intimate community whose moderators know the people they are moderating is the adaptation, and the conflict of interest is a feature of the setting rather than a defect.

# Moderation for a Community of Four Hundred

**Industry:** [[industries/membership-community-platforms|Membership & Community Platforms]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** The tooling that would help costs more than the community earns, so the founder handles the harassment report personally.

**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to give a community of four hundred people the moderation capability that is currently priced for platforms with trust and safety departments — and whoever does it protects the communities where the harm is most personal.

## The Problem
A paid community of a few hundred people will, over a year, encounter harassment, a dispute that escalates, a member in distress, a scam attempt, and a handful of genuinely ambiguous cases. Each requires judgement, a policy, a decision and sometimes an escalation. The community has a founder, some volunteers, a rules page written in an afternoon and a delete button. Professional trust and safety tooling exists and is sold to organisations with departments.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Trust and safety products were built for large platforms because that is where the budget and the regulatory pressure are, so the category's pricing and complexity exclude everyone else — a market served only at the top leaves the bottom with nothing rather than with a simpler version. Small communities are numerous and individually tiny. The work is absorbed by unpaid people. And the harm is invisible outside the community.

## What to Build
Deliver the capability at small-community economics. Detect the common harms automatically — harassment, scams, doxxing, crisis language — which is the core and is where the volunteer's attention is most expensively spent. Provide policy templates that are specific rather than generic, since a founder writing a code of conduct from scratch produces something unusable and most do not want to write one. Support the decision with precedent from the community's own history, as consistency is what makes moderation defensible and it is currently absent. Provide an escalation path for the serious cases, because the absence of anywhere to send a crisis is the heaviest part of the volunteer's load. Keep a record of decisions and their reasons, which protects the moderator and makes appeals possible. Handle the appeal, since a member removed without recourse is both an injustice and a public dispute. Batch and triage rather than presenting everything as equally urgent. Protect the moderator from exposure where possible, as repeated exposure to abusive content is the occupational harm here. Price it for a community of four hundred, which is the entire point and is what the incumbents cannot do. And measure moderator load, since burnout is the failure mode and nobody tracks it.

## Target Customer
Trust and safety and product leadership, community founders and volunteer moderators, members experiencing harm, and trust and safety vendors serving large platforms.

## Impact If Built
A market served only at the top leaves the bottom with nothing rather than with a simpler version, so small communities have a delete button. Detection plus specific policy templates and an escalation path is the capability, and pricing it for four hundred members is the product.

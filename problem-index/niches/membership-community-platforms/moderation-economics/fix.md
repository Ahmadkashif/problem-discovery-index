# A Rules Page and a Delete Button

**Niche:** [[niches/membership-community-platforms/moderation-economics/profile|Moderation Economics]]
**Industry:** [[industries/membership-community-platforms|Membership & Community Platforms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The moderator has to decide whether this is harassment, has no precedent, no policy detail and nobody to ask.
**Tags:** #compliance #quick-win #worker-facing #workflow-orchestration #evaluation-metrics #automation #descriptive-statistics #confidence-intervals
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to give a community of four hundred people the moderation capability that is currently priced for platforms with trust and safety departments — and whoever does it protects the communities where the harm is most personal.

## The Problem
A report arrives. The rules page says be respectful. The moderator must decide whether this crosses a line, what to do, whether to explain, and whether the same thing was handled differently last time. There is no precedent record, no policy detail, no second opinion and no escalation. Whatever they decide affects two members who will both remember it, and the decision will be made alone, quickly, by someone who volunteered to help with a hobby.

## Why It's Still Broken
The rules page is written once at launch as a formality, so it is aspirational rather than operational — a document written to state values cannot decide a case. Precedent is not recorded. Volunteers have no training and no support. And the founder, who could help, is usually the person who would be conflicted.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Give the decision structure and a record. Record every moderation decision with its reason, which is the fix and creates the precedent that makes the next decision consistent. Expand the rules into specific worked examples, since "be respectful" decides nothing and three examples decide most cases. Surface similar past decisions when a report is opened, as consistency is what makes moderation feel fair and is currently impossible. Provide a second opinion mechanism, even informally between moderators, because deciding alone is the heaviest part. Define an escalation path for the serious categories, as a crisis or a legal matter should not rest on a volunteer's judgement. Give the moderator language for the message to both parties, since the communication is often harder than the decision. Allow an appeal, which protects everyone including the moderator. Rotate difficult cases rather than defaulting to whoever is available, as repeated exposure is how volunteers burn out. Report decision volume and type, so the founder can see the load nobody is tracking. And thank the moderators visibly, because unpaid emotional labour with no recognition ends predictably.

## Who Feels the Pain
Volunteers deciding alone on cases they are not equipped for; members treated inconsistently; founders discovering a dispute after it has spread; and communities that lose their moderators on a predictable cycle.

## Impact If Fixed
A document written to state values cannot decide a case, so the rules page is aspirational. Recording decisions with reasons builds the precedent that makes the next case consistent and takes the weight off the person deciding alone.

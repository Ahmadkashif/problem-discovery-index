## The Problem
Feed ranking is one of the most heavily engineered problems in consumer software: candidate generation, multi-objective ranking, real-time personalisation, diversity and freshness controls, and extensive experimentation infrastructure. All of it was built to maximise engagement for an advertising business. Community platforms adopted the mechanics, and with them the objective, into a business whose revenue depends on whether individual members feel included.

## What Already Exists
Candidate generation and ranking architectures; multi-objective optimisation with guardrails; real-time personalisation; diversity and freshness controls; and mature ranking experimentation practice.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a subscription whose product is inclusion. It requires: (1) an objective of reciprocal interaction rather than consumption, which changes what is ranked and what is suppressed — this is the substantive difference and most of the mechanics transfer once it is set; (2) small candidate pools, where a community may have dozens of items rather than millions, making the engineering far lighter; (3) an explicit obligation to surface the underexposed, since the community's health depends on the newcomer being seen and social ranking has no such duty; (4) a paying member who can leave, making retention the honest evaluation metric; and (5) a founder who can and should override the ranking, which social platforms do not permit.

## Target Customer
Product leadership, founders, members, and ranking infrastructure vendors.

## Impact If Solved
The ranking machinery is mature and was built for the wrong objective. Changing the objective to reciprocal interaction — and adding an obligation to surface the underexposed — makes most of the existing mechanics directly usable.

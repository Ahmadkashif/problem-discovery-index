# Nine People Registered, Three Came

**Niche:** [[niches/membership-community-platforms/events-and-programming/profile|Events & Programming]]
**Industry:** [[industries/membership-community-platforms|Membership & Community Platforms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The operator prepared for an hour, nine people said they would come, and three did.
**Tags:** #quick-win #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #automation #workflow-orchestration #worker-facing #confidence-intervals #gradient-boosting
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to give members a recurring reason to show up without the operator inventing one every week — and whoever automates the programming keeps the rhythm that holds a community together.

## The Problem
Registration numbers are not attendance numbers, and the gap is large, variable and demoralising. The operator prepares for the registered number, experiences the actual number, and cannot tell whether the format failed, the time was wrong, the promotion was poor or people simply forgot. The session with three attendees feels like a failure to everyone present, including the three, and the operator's willingness to run the next one erodes.

## Why It's Still Broken
Registration is a free action with no cost to the registrant, so it measures intention rather than commitment — a signal that costs nothing to give will be given freely and mean little. Attendance is not analysed against time, format or promotion. The variance is absorbed emotionally by one person. And nobody reports the gap as a number to be managed.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Predict attendance and manage the gap. Report the registration-to-attendance ratio over time, which is the fix and turns a disappointment into a planning number. Predict attendance from registrations, timing, format and member history, since the ratio is fairly stable per community and is entirely learnable. Send a reminder shortly before rather than only at registration, as the drop is largely forgetting and the fix is a notification. Schedule at times the data supports rather than at the operator's convenience, which is how the time is currently chosen. Ask registrants to confirm nearer the time, because a second small commitment materially improves attendance. Offer an asynchronous alternative, so the members who cannot attend still participate and the session is not measured only by live bodies. Tell the operator the expected number in advance, which reframes three attendees from a failure into the forecast. Report which formats and times retain members rather than which fill, since a small well-attended working session may be worth more than a large passive one. Reuse the session's output so a low-attendance live session still produces value. And make small sessions feel intentional rather than failed, because the operator's morale is the resource actually at stake.

## Who Feels the Pain
Operators demoralised by empty sessions; members who attended a session that felt like a failure; communities whose programming quietly stops; and platforms whose retention depends on a rhythm nobody supports.

## Impact If Fixed
A signal that costs nothing to give will be given freely and mean little, so registration is intention rather than commitment. Predicting attendance and telling the operator the expected number in advance changes the same three attendees from a failure into a forecast.

## The Problem
Membership associations, professional bodies and local chapters have run programming for generations: an annual calendar, volunteer committees, chapter chairs, recurring formats, speaker pipelines and member-led sessions. The structure distributes the work and produces a predictable rhythm without any one person carrying it. Online communities have the same need and the structure of a single operator improvising.

## What Already Exists
Annual programme calendars; volunteer committee and chapter structures; recurring event formats; speaker and host pipelines; and member-led programming traditions.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a distributed community with no local structure. It requires: (1) volunteers recruited and coordinated online with no geography to organise around, where associations use chapters — this is the substantive difference and is where the structure has to be reinvented; (2) time zones spanning the world, which makes any single live session exclude somebody; (3) a founder who is also the product, so delegating programming risks the thing members joined for; (4) no annual cycle or membership year, meaning the calendar has no natural shape; and (5) a small enough scale that a committee is too heavy and a single person is too light.

## Target Customer
Community operations leadership, founders, member volunteers, and association management vendors.

## Impact If Solved
Associations distribute programming through chapters and committees and produce rhythm without exhausting anyone. Reinventing that structure for a community with no geography, spanning every time zone, is the adaptation.

# The Two People They Should Know

**Niche:** [[niches/membership-community-platforms/first-fortnight-onboarding/profile|First-Fortnight Onboarding]]
**Industry:** [[industries/membership-community-platforms|Membership & Community Platforms]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A new member needs one conversation with the right person, and the platform offers them a checklist.
**Tags:** #graph-theory #k-nearest-neighbors #optimization-fundamentals #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #automation #gradient-boosting #word-embeddings
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to put a new member in front of the two people they should know within days of joining — and whoever makes that introduction reliably decides whether the membership survives its first fortnight.

## The Problem
A newcomer arrives, posts an introduction into a channel where introductions scroll past, receives two emoji reactions, and concludes the community is not for them. What would have worked is being introduced to two specific existing members with a shared interest, a shared problem or a shared background, with a reason to speak. The platform knows enough about both sides to make that introduction and instead provides a checklist of product actions.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Onboarding was designed as product activation, so it teaches the interface rather than making a connection — a flow built to get someone using features will get them using features. Matching requires understanding both the newcomer and the existing members, which requires reading the graph and the content. Introductions channels look like they solve this and do not. And nobody measures whether a connection formed.

## What to Build
Make the introduction, do not host it. Match each newcomer to a small number of existing members on shared interests, goals, location, stage or expertise, which is the core and is a standard matching problem with unusually rich profile and behavioural data on both sides. Make the introduction actively to both parties with a reason to talk, since an unprompted introduction with context works and a broadcast does not. Enlist willing members as welcomers, because most communities have people who would do this and are never asked. Detect the ignored first post within a day and ensure a reply, as that single event is the strongest available predictor and is entirely observable. Measure connection formed rather than checklist completed, which is the outcome and is what should be optimised. Handle the small community and the large one differently, since matching in a community of eighty is a different problem from one of eight thousand. Route the newcomer to the right subgroup rather than to the main room, as the main room is where newcomers are invisible. Give the founder a short list of introductions to make personally, which is what they do well and currently do at random. Follow up in week two, because that is when the decision is being made and nothing currently touches it. And test the interventions against renewal, which is a clean and available outcome.

## Target Customer
Product and community leadership, founders, newcomers who left in week two, and community platform vendors.

## Impact If Built
A flow built to get someone using features will get them using features, which is why onboarding teaches the interface. An active introduction to two specific members is the mechanism that works, and the platform holds everything needed to make it.

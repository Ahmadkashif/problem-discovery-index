# The Introduction Nobody Answered

**Niche:** [[niches/membership-community-platforms/first-fortnight-onboarding/profile|First-Fortnight Onboarding]]
**Industry:** [[industries/membership-community-platforms|Membership & Community Platforms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The new member's first post got two reactions and no reply, and that was the end of it.
**Tags:** #quick-win #graph-theory #evaluation-metrics #automation #descriptive-statistics #worker-facing #confidence-intervals #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to put a new member in front of the two people they should know within days of joining — and whoever makes that introduction reliably decides whether the membership survives its first fortnight.

## The Problem
A newcomer posts. It scrolls past during a busy hour, or lands at a quiet time, and receives nothing. They do not post again. Whether a first post received a reply is a single, unambiguous, immediately available fact that strongly predicts whether the person is still there in a month, and no platform surfaces it, alerts on it, or does anything about it.

## Why It's Still Broken
The feed treats every post identically, so a newcomer's first post competes with everything else — a system with no concept of a first post cannot prioritise one. Nobody computed the correlation between first-post replies and retention, though it is a one-query analysis. Founders cannot watch every post. And the consequence appears as a cancellation months later.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Notice the first post and make sure it is answered. Detect a member's first post and flag it, which is the fix and is a trivial query with a large effect. Alert the founder or a welcomer if it goes unanswered for a day, since one reply is usually all it takes. Give first posts prominence in the feed for a short window, as visibility is the proximate cause. Ask willing members to watch for them, because many would and nobody has asked. Report the proportion of first posts that received a reply, which is a health metric the category lacks and is computable today. Prompt the newcomer to post somewhere specific rather than into a general channel, since a smaller room answers and a large one does not. Time the prompt to when the community is active rather than when the member happens to join. Follow up with the newcomer whose post was ignored rather than letting it stand, as the recovery is possible and currently never attempted. Correlate first-post replies with ninety-day retention, which will make the case internally in an afternoon. And treat an unanswered first post as an incident, because in this business it is one.

## Who Feels the Pain
Newcomers who concluded nobody wanted them there; founders losing members for a reason they never see; communities that shrink invisibly; and platforms whose churn analysis cannot reach back to the cause.

## Impact If Fixed
A system with no concept of a first post cannot prioritise one, so the strongest available retention signal passes unnoticed. Flagging and answering the unanswered first post is a one-query intervention against the clearest predictor in the data.

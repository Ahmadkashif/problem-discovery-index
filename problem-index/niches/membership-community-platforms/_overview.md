# Niche Analysis — Membership & Community Platforms

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/membership-community-platforms|Membership & Community Platforms]]

## Niche Selection

These platforms provide posting, spaces, events, courses and payments, and what every one of the businesses on them actually sells is belonging — while the analytics report daily active users and post counts, which correlate weakly with whether anyone felt connected. Churn is almost entirely silent: a member stops opening the app in week three, stays subscribed out of inertia, and cancels four months later when the disengagement is already a quarter old. The signal that predicted it — whether their first post got a reply, whether anyone learned their name, whether they found a subgroup — is in the data and reported nowhere. Around it sit a structural pathology everyone knows about, moderation economics that put serious tooling out of reach, and a founder whose personal availability is indistinguishable from the community's health. The eight niches below split the category by what competitors actually fight over.

## Why These Niches

Member lifecycle takes the largest share because the whole business is recurring revenue and the decision to stay or go is made in a fortnight and executed four months later. Community health is second because the structural failures — a centralised reply graph, a closed subgroup, a newcomer nobody answered — are legible in data nobody reads. The two low-digitized niches are the ones where the work is human and the tooling is priced for somebody else: moderation, and the endless programming that gives people a reason to show up. The two underserved audiences are the founder whose availability is the product and the volunteer handling harassment reports with a delete button. The two automatable niches are what members see when they open the app and what happens to a community when it has to move.

## Filter Notes

Seven of the eight level-1 niches are terminal. Member Lifecycle is not: the label names a span of time rather than a contest, and writing the contested statement produces two sentences with different winners. First-fortnight onboarding asks how to make a new member's first two weeks produce a connection — a matching and introduction design problem operating in a narrow window, where the winner is whoever puts a newcomer in front of the right two people. Silent churn detection asks how to notice the member who disengaged months before they cancel — a prediction and intervention problem operating over a long, quiet interval, where the winner is whoever reaches someone while it is still recoverable. One happens in fourteen days and is about introductions; the other happens over a hundred and is about absence; they are built by different people and used at opposite ends of a membership. It therefore decomposes into **First-Fortnight Onboarding** and **Silent Churn Detection**.

Two candidates were considered and rejected. **Course delivery inside a community** is a common product combination but the contest over instruction belongs to [[industries/online-course-platforms|Online Course Platforms]]. **Large-scale social platforms** share the graph and moderation problems at a completely different scale and economics, and are the contest of [[industries/ugc-video-platforms|UGC Video Platforms]].

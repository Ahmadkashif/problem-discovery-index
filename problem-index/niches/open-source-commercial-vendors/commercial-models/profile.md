# Commercial Models

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/open-source-commercial-vendors|Open Source Commercial Vendors]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor here is fighting to convert use of freely available software into revenue — and that contest is fought against the free edition in one model and against a hyperscaler in the other, which is why this niche is not terminal and is decomposed below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$18B US revenue from commercially backed open source
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~60% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** High — every vendor operates one or both models
**Target Buyer:** Platform teams choosing to pay for features; separately, teams choosing not to operate anything
**Automation Potential:** High for the measurement that should inform the decisions; the decisions are strategic

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Converting the use of software anyone can obtain for nothing into revenue is the category's central commercial problem, and the answers are few: put some features behind a licence, operate it as a service, or sell support and assurance — usually in combination. The stakes are existential rather than optimisational: several well-known companies have changed their licences under exactly this pressure, and the phenomenon is now recognised enough to be treated as a phase in a company's life rather than as a surprise.

The filter fails here. "Commercial models" names the monetisation question rather than a contest. Open core is a contest against the free edition and against the possibility of a fork, decided by a platform team weighing paid features against the effort of doing without them, and won by drawing a line that customers accept and the community tolerates. Hosted service is a contest against a hyperscaler offering the same open-source project, decided by a team that has already chosen not to operate anything, and won on operational quality, price and integration. The two require different capabilities and different evidence. Decomposed below.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Open core with feature flags and licence enforcement, hosted services, support subscriptions, and increasingly restrictive source-available licences. The gaps: which features actually drive purchase has never been measured, so the open-core line is redrawn by argument; the effect of a licence change on adoption and on revenue is not measured either, though several natural experiments have now occurred; the hosted model's competitive position against a hyperscaler is asserted rather than analysed; and support subscriptions are priced on convention rather than on the value or cost of the support delivered.

## The Problem
Occupational health has validated instruments for measuring burnout, a substantial literature on its causes and predictors, and established interventions — in healthcare, in emergency services, in teaching. Open-source maintenance exhibits the recognised risk factors almost completely: unbounded demand, emotional labour, asymmetric obligation, invisible work and no boundary between the role and the person. It is discussed as a cultural problem and measured by nobody.

## What Already Exists
Validated burnout instruments with decades of use; the occupational health literature on demand-control-support models, which maps onto this situation closely; workload measurement methodology; intervention research from professions with comparable demand structures; and survival analysis for time-to-departure. All published and none of it applied here.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to unpaid, voluntary, publicly conducted work. It requires: (1) behavioural measurement rather than survey instruments as the primary signal, since maintainers will not complete questionnaires and their activity is entirely public — response latency, activity pattern, tone drift and engagement decline are observable and are leading indicators; (2) the absence of an employer, which removes every intervention the literature assumes and leaves only community, foundation and corporate mechanisms — this is the central constraint and is why the existing intervention research does not transfer directly; (3) voluntary exit as the outcome rather than sickness absence, which changes the survival analysis and makes departure the event to predict; (4) measurement that is not surveillance, since publishing individual burnout indicators about identifiable volunteers would be unacceptable — the analysis must be aggregate, or must be reported to the maintainer themselves rather than about them, which is the same posture this vault takes to people analytics throughout; and (5) interventions that are available in this context, which are load sharing, funding, abuse interception and the addition of maintainers rather than anything an employer could offer.

## Target Customer
Foundations, corporate open-source programmes, research groups studying open-source sustainability, and the platforms hosting these projects.

## Impact If Solved
A mature occupational health literature describes this situation precisely and has not been applied to it. Behavioural leading indicators are observable in public activity, and the reported-to-the-maintainer posture is what makes measuring it acceptable rather than intrusive.

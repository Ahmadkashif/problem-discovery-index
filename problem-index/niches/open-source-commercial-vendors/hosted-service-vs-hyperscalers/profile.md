# Hosted Service Against Hyperscalers

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/open-source-commercial-vendors|Open Source Commercial Vendors]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor here is fighting to operate their own project better than a hyperscaler offering the identical software — and whoever does that takes the hosted market, because the customer has already decided not to operate anything and is choosing purely on who runs it best.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$9.6B US hosted services built on open-source projects
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~32% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** High and growing — hosting is where the category's revenue has concentrated
**Target Buyer:** Teams who have chosen not to operate the software themselves
**Automation Potential:** High — operational quality is the product and much of it is automatable

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The hosted model puts the project's creators in direct competition with cloud providers offering the same software, which is the dynamic that has driven most of the category's licence changes. The contest is narrow and unforgiving: the customer has already decided not to operate the software, the code is identical, and the choice comes down to operational quality, price, integration with the rest of their estate, and how much of the vendor's expertise reaches them. The vendor's structural advantage is that they wrote it and understand it better than anyone; the hyperscaler's is distribution, bundled billing, network proximity and an existing relationship. Almost none of the vendor's advantage is currently converted into anything the customer can see, which is the central problem of this sub-niche.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Vendor-operated services, hyperscaler managed offerings of the same projects, marketplace listings, and partnership arrangements of varying warmth. The gaps: the vendor's deeper expertise is real and is not expressed in the product in any way a customer can evaluate; operational quality is asserted by both sides and compared by neither; the vendor's fleet knowledge across their own hosted customers is the analogue of the database fleet corpus and is equally unused; egress and network proximity disadvantage the vendor structurally and are rarely quantified for the customer; and migration between the vendor's service and a hyperscaler's is a project, which is the real lock-in on both sides.

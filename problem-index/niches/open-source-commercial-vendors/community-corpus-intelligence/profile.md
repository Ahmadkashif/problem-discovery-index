# Community Corpus Intelligence

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/open-source-commercial-vendors|Open Source Commercial Vendors]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor that gets here is fighting to turn the abundant, textual, scattered public record of a project's community into commercial and product intelligence — and whoever does that takes the analytical position, because the record is public and nobody reads it.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$690M US, largely latent
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~2% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** None — the corpus is read one thread at a time by whoever happens to be in it
**Target Buyer:** The vendors themselves; secondarily foundations and investors
**Automation Potential:** Very High — the corpus is public, textual and large

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Everything about an open-source project's community is public: issues, discussions, chat archives, forum threads, pull request conversations, conference talks, blog posts, job postings requiring the technology, and the public code that uses it. Collectively this is a large textual record of who uses the software, how, for what, with what difficulty, and how that is changing — which is the evidence base for adoption, for product direction, for competitive position and for community health. It is read one thread at a time by whoever happens to be in it, which means the loudest participants determine what the company believes. The corpus is public, which removes the governance obstacle that constrains every other corpus opportunity in this vault, and it is still unexploited — which makes this the cleanest instance of the pattern in the whole collection.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Community metrics dashboards counting contributors and activity, sentiment monitoring of a rudimentary kind, and manual reading by developer relations. The gaps: the corpus is fragmented across half a dozen platforms and nobody assembles it; product direction is set from issue volume, which measures loudness rather than importance; adoption evidence sits in job postings and public code and is not mined; competitive mentions are visible and unaggregated; community health is measured by contributor count rather than by anything predictive; and the corpus's relationship to commercial outcomes has never been established.

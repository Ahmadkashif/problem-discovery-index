## The Problem
Extracting topics, entities, sentiment and stance from large volumes of public text is a mature capability applied commercially to social media, news and reviews at enormous scale. The open-source community corpus is text of exactly this kind — opinionated, contextual, organisationally revealing — and is consumed by a developer relations team reading threads.

## What Already Exists
Topic modelling and clustering; named entity recognition and entity linking to company identifiers; sentiment and stance detection; language models capable of extracting structured facts from technical discussion; and the social listening product category, which does this commercially for consumer brands.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to technical discussion by identifiable practitioners. It requires: (1) technical entity extraction rather than consumer-brand mentions, since the entities are versions, configurations, competing projects and deployment patterns, and a general model will miss all of them; (2) organisational resolution from weak signals — an email domain, a mention of an employer, a profile, a repository ownership — which is noisy entity resolution and is the piece that turns community text into adoption evidence; (3) sentiment that means something in this register, since technical communities express dissatisfaction as detailed criticism and a consumer sentiment model reads a thorough bug report as extremely negative; (4) ethical care about individuals, since the corpus is public and is written by identifiable people who did not expect to be analysed — the analysis should be about the project and the aggregate rather than about the participants, which is a design constraint rather than a preference; and (5) platform terms compliance, because assembling this corpus touches several platforms whose terms differ and some of which restrict bulk use.

## Target Customer
Open-source companies, developer relations platforms, foundations, and the social listening vendors for whom technical communities are an unserved segment.

## Impact If Solved
Mature text mining meets a large public corpus that is read by hand, which is the clearest available mismatch in the category. Organisational resolution is the piece that converts community text into commercial evidence, and register-appropriate sentiment is what stops the analysis being systematically wrong.

# Dual-Audience Support

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/open-source-commercial-vendors|Open Source Commercial Vendors]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor that takes this seriously is fighting to let one answer serve both a paying customer and a public community without the support engineer holding the boundary personally — and whoever does that takes the support organisation, because that boundary is the job's defining discomfort.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$620M US attributable to support operations at open-source companies
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~2% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Low — the same answer is written twice, by hand
**Target Buyer:** Support leadership at open-source companies
**Automation Potential:** Very High — the answers are identical and the corpora are separate

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Support engineers at open-source companies answer the same question in a contracted ticket and in a public issue, and must maintain a distinction that is commercially necessary and feels indefensible to hold. The awkwardness is structural rather than a matter of policy: the software is identical, the problem is identical, the answer is identical, and one person is paying and the other is not. Engineers cope by answering both, which is generous and unsustainable, or by answering the paid one and feeling bad, or by answering the public one thinly. Meanwhile the two knowledge bases diverge — the paid one contains answers the community never sees and the public one contains discussions the support team does not read — which means the same investigation is performed in both places and neither is complete. This is a distinct contested surface because the remedy is mostly knowledge architecture rather than policy.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Ticketing systems for contracted support, public issue trackers and forums for the community, and separate knowledge bases. The gaps: the two corpora are disconnected, so a public issue resolved in detail does not reach the support team's knowledge base and vice versa; there is no articulated principle for what is and is not part of a support entitlement, so the boundary is held by individual judgement; the community's issues contain product signal the commercial organisation does not read; response expectations are unstated on the public side, which produces the frustration the maintainer niche describes; and the support engineer's discomfort is nobody's concern.

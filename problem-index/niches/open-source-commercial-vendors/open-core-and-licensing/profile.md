# Open Core & Licensing

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/open-source-commercial-vendors|Open Source Commercial Vendors]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor here is fighting to draw a line between free and paid that customers accept and the community tolerates — and whoever can locate that line from evidence takes the model, because it is currently redrawn every two years by argument.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$8.4B US open-core and licensed commercial editions
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~28% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** High — nearly every vendor operates some version of it
**Target Buyer:** Platform teams weighing paid features against doing without them
**Automation Potential:** High for the measurement; the boundary itself is a judgement informed by it

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Open core is a contest against the free edition of the same software. The customer's alternative is not a competitor's product but the vendor's own, which changes everything about the decision: the question is never whether the commercial feature is good but whether doing without it is harder than paying. That makes the boundary the entire product, and it is placed by argument — feature flagging and licence enforcement are entirely solved, and where the line should sit is redrawn every year or two because nobody can measure which features actually drive purchase. The competitive dynamics are unusual too: a badly placed boundary invites a community reimplementation of the paid feature, which is the specific failure mode of this model and which no vendor tracks systematically until it has already happened.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Feature flagging and licence key enforcement, tiered editions, entitlement management, and increasingly restrictive source-available licences. The gaps: purchase attribution to features is not collected, so the boundary is placed on intuition; the community's reimplementation of paid features is the model's characteristic risk and is monitored by nobody; licence enforcement in an open codebase is inherently soft and the extent of unlicensed commercial use is unknown; the customer's actual alternative — what they would do without the feature — is never assessed, although it determines willingness to pay entirely; and the boundary's effect on contribution is unmeasured.

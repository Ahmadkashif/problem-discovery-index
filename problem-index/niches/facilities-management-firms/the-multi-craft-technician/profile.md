# The Multi-Craft Building Technician

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/facilities-management-firms|Facilities Management Firms]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to put the building's own history in the technician's hand at the equipment, and whoever makes the next diagnosis faster than a phone call to the chief engineer takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$900M a year of technician-facing mobile, diagnostic, knowledge and training spend across US FM providers (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~1.5% of US integrated facility services revenue (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low-Medium — every technician carries a CMMS app; almost none of it helps them diagnose
**Target Buyer:** Heads of technical services and training at FM providers; chief engineers at large sites
**Automation Potential:** Medium — briefing and close-out are automatable; the repair is not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
General maintenance and repair workers are the largest trade occupation in the code (12,990 in OEWS May 2023, mean wage $55,850), alongside HVAC mechanics and electricians. They cover plumbing, electrical, HVAC, carpentry and controls across buildings they did not commission and often visit rarely. The software they carry was bought for their managers: it dispatches them, times them and collects their close-out. The knowledge that lets a technician fix something first time sits with the chief engineer or in their own memory. When either is missing, the technician is guessing in front of a client.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Mobile CMMS apps (Maximo Mobile, Corrigo, ServiceChannel Provider, Eptura), OEM manuals and PDFs, phone calls to senior engineers, and manufacturer training. Remote-assist video tools exist but are rarely used. The gaps: no asset-level history worth reading, no capture of the senior engineers' diagnoses, and close-out designed for the manager's report rather than the next technician.

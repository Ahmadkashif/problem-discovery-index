# Close-Out Typed After the Shift

**Niche:** [[niches/facilities-management-firms/the-multi-craft-technician/profile|The Multi-Craft Building Technician]]
**Industry:** [[industries/facilities-management-firms|Facilities Management Firms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The next call is already late, so the technician closes this one from the van or the kitchen table, from memory, with the fewest words the app will accept.
**Tags:** #worker-facing #large-language-models #workflow-orchestration #quick-win #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to put the building's own history in the technician's hand at the equipment, and whoever makes the next diagnosis faster than a phone call to the chief engineer takes the account.

## The Problem
SLA clocks reward arriving and finishing, and the close-out form is long. Technicians defer it, then batch it at the end of the day. Times are estimated, causes are generic and PM checklists are ticked in bulk, which the trade calls pencil whipping. The record that results misleads the KPI report and the next technician alike.

## Why It's Still Broken
The form exists to serve billing and KPI reporting, and each field was added by someone who needed it. Nobody owns the technician's total time on administration, and KPI pressure punishes the honest technician who stops to type.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Cut the form to what the job actually needs, capture timestamps automatically from the phone's arrival and departure, draft the rest from a spoken summary at the equipment, and require photos only where they prove something. Measure administration minutes per job as a KPI in its own right.

## Who Feels the Pain
Technicians losing evenings, facility managers reporting KPIs built on estimates, and clients paying for PMs that may not have happened.

## Impact If Fixed
Close-out at the point of work, truthful timestamps and an end to unpaid evening administration (estimate: 30–60 minutes a day per mobile technician).

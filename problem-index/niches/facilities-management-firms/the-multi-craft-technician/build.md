# An Asset Briefing at the Equipment

**Niche:** [[niches/facilities-management-firms/the-multi-craft-technician/profile|The Multi-Craft Building Technician]]
**Industry:** [[industries/facilities-management-firms|Facilities Management Firms]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A technician standing at a rooftop unit has a work order and a timer, and none of what the last five people learned about that machine.
**Tags:** #tacit-knowledge-ml #large-language-models #k-nearest-neighbors #word-embeddings #worker-facing #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to put the building's own history in the technician's hand at the equipment, and whoever makes the next diagnosis faster than a phone call to the chief engineer takes the account.

## The Problem
Asset histories in the CMMS are lists of tickets that say "completed" or "reset." The diagnosis that mattered, such as the capacitor that keeps failing or the damper linkage that slips in cold weather, was spoken on a phone call or remembered by one engineer. The next technician starts from zero, often in a building they visit once a quarter.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
CMMS products were designed around managers' reporting needs, and technicians never had the time or the interface to write useful notes. The asset knowledge worth capturing is tacit, and capturing it needs a surface easier than typing on a phone on a roof.

## What to Build
A briefing that opens when the technician scans an asset tag or arrives at the location. It shows a summary of what actually happened on this machine, recurring failure patterns across similar machines in the provider's book, the senior engineers' recorded notes, parts and filter sizes, warranty status and photos of the disconnect and access. Technicians add to it by speaking a sentence. Similar-case retrieval across the provider's fleet turns one site's lesson into every site's.

## Target Customer
Technical services leaders at IFM providers with mobile and multi-site workforces; chief engineers who are tired of being the only phone number.

## Impact If Built
Higher first-time-fix rates, fewer repeat visits and calls to senior staff, and a plant history that survives turnover (estimate: a few points of first-time fix on reactive HVAC work).

**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to put the building's own history in the technician's hand at the equipment, and whoever makes the next diagnosis faster than a phone call to the chief engineer takes the account.

## The Problem
A work-order close-out wants labour time, parts, failure mode, cause, action, checklist results and photos. Technicians type the minimum, usually after the shift, so the record is thin exactly where the next technician needs it to be rich.

## What Already Exists
Ambient clinical documentation tools listen to a clinician's conversation or dictation and draft a structured note in the medical record for review; general voice assistants and dictation on phones.

## The Customization Gap
The building version needs: trade vocabulary and equipment nomenclature across HVAC, electrical, plumbing and controls; mapping into each CMMS's own fields and failure-code lists, which differ by account; use in noisy plant rooms and on rooftops with gloves; offline capture where there is no signal; asset identification from the scanned tag; and prompting only for fields that are genuinely missing, so the review is seconds, not minutes.

## Target Customer
FM providers' technical services and CMMS owners; CMMS vendors who could embed it.

## Impact If Solved
Richer histories at lower effort, close-out done at the equipment instead of at home, and failure codes that are finally usable for analysis.

# The Asset List Nobody Verified Before the Price Was Locked

**Niche:** [[niches/facilities-management-firms/ifm-bid-solutioning/profile|IFM Bid Solutioning & Baseline Pricing]]
**Industry:** [[industries/facilities-management-firms|Facilities Management Firms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The price is built on the client's asset list, and the provider discovers in transition that the list was wrong.
**Tags:** #cnns #object-detection #descriptive-statistics #confidence-intervals #quick-win #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to price a five-year, multi-site contract from an asset list and spend history it knows to be wrong, and whoever comes nearest the cost the portfolio actually turns out to have, without pricing itself off the shortlist, takes the account.

## The Problem
RFP asset registers are exported from the incumbent's or client's CMMS, often years after the last full survey. Equipment has been replaced, added or decommissioned and never recorded. The bid team walks a handful of buildings and trusts the rest. In transition the provider's verification surveys find missing chillers, extra rooftop units and life-safety systems nobody listed, commonly a substantial share of the register (estimate), after the price is fixed.

## Why It's Still Broken
Full surveys before the bid are too expensive for an opportunity the provider may lose, and clients rarely pay for them. The site walks that do happen produce notes and photos, not a statistical basis for extrapolating to unvisited sites.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A sampled pre-bid survey designed to estimate register error. Sites are chosen to cover building types and ages, and nameplates and plant rooms are captured by phone camera with image recognition to count and classify equipment. The result is an estimate of register error with a confidence interval that goes into the price or into a contract clause reopening price after verification.

## Who Feels the Pain
Bid teams who own the loss, transition managers who discover it, and account directors who spend five years explaining it.

## Impact If Fixed
The largest single source of first-year margin surprise becomes a measured, priced risk rather than a discovery.

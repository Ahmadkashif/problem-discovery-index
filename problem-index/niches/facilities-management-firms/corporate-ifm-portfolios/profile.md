# Corporate Occupier IFM Portfolios

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/facilities-management-firms|Facilities Management Firms]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is that every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to run a corporate portfolio at a margin the contract price can bear. That splits into two contests with different winners: pricing a portfolio whose real maintenance load nobody has measured before the bid closes, and deciding, service by service and region by region, what to self-deliver and what to subcontract once the account is live. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$25B a year of US corporate-occupier IFM contract revenue, pass-through included (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~40% of US integrated facility services revenue, counting the IFM revenue reported outside NAICS 561210 (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — enterprise CMMS and national service desks at the providers, but bid models, account P&Ls and delivery decisions live in spreadsheets
**Target Buyer:** Provider account directors and regional operations leaders; on the client side, heads of real estate and workplace
**Automation Potential:** Medium — reporting and pricing analysis are automatable; the delivery decisions are judgement on poor data

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Corporate occupiers with campuses, offices, labs and plants in many locations outsource hard services (MEP maintenance, building engineering, minor projects) and often soft services under one IFM contract with an SLA schedule, KPIs and some fee at risk. CBRE, JLL, Cushman & Wakefield, ISS, Sodexo, Aramark and ABM compete for them through real estate procurement every three to five years. The contracts run open-book with a management fee, fixed price, or a hybrid, and the provider's margin comes from the gap between what was priced and what the portfolio actually costs to run, much of it subcontracted.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Providers run their own CMMS instances or the client's, plus proprietary reporting platforms; clients run IWMS tools for space and leases. The gaps: bid assumptions are never compared with delivered cost; account P&Ls show margin variance without explaining it by site, service or delivery route; and scope boundaries live in a contract appendix nobody checks when a request arrives.

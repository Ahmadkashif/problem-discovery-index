# The Client-Side IFM Contract Manager

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/facilities-management-firms|Facilities Management Firms]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to give the occupier an independent read of whether its IFM contract is delivering what it was priced to deliver, and whoever can reconcile the provider's own work orders and pass-through invoices to the SLA schedule without the provider's help takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$400M a year of client-side contract governance spend: third-party audits, benchmarking, vendor-management tools and in-house analyst time (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~0.7% of US integrated facility services revenue (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — the client sees the provider's dashboards and an invoice
**Target Buyer:** Head of real estate and workplace, or FM contract manager, at corporate occupiers, universities and public owners
**Automation Potential:** High for reconciliation and audit; the commercial judgement stays human

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Once a corporation has outsourced facilities, a small team, often one or two people, is left to govern a contract worth tens of millions a year. Every number they see is produced by the party they are supposed to be measuring: the provider's CMMS, the provider's KPI pack and the provider's pass-through invoices with markup. They usually lack the data access, time or skill to test it, and the quarterly review becomes a negotiation over the provider's slides. Benchmarks from the providers' own research groups, profiled at [[niches/cleaning-companies/facility-management-benchmarking/profile|🔍 Integrated Facility Management Benchmarking]], come from the same side of the table.

## Current Tools & Gaps
The provider's portals, IWMS for space and leases, procurement and spend tools, and occasional audits by consultants. The gaps: no independent access to work-order data in a usable form, no automated test of KPI calculations against contract definitions, and no audit of pass-through invoices against rate cards and markup caps.

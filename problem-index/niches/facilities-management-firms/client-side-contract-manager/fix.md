# The KPI Pack Written by the Party Being Measured

**Niche:** [[niches/facilities-management-firms/client-side-contract-manager/profile|The Client-Side IFM Contract Manager]]
**Industry:** [[industries/facilities-management-firms|Facilities Management Firms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The provider computes its own KPIs, chooses its own exclusions and presents the result, and the fee at risk depends on it.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #compliance #quick-win #workflow-orchestration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to give the occupier an independent read of whether its IFM contract is delivering what it was priced to deliver, and whoever can reconcile the provider's own work orders and pass-through invoices to the SLA schedule without the provider's help takes the account.

## The Problem
SLA definitions leave room: when the clock starts, which tickets count as priority 1, what is excluded as "awaiting client approval" or "parts on order." The provider applies those choices each month in its own favour, not necessarily dishonestly, and the client cannot see the effect.

## Why It's Still Broken
Contracts define KPIs in prose, not as calculations, and the client never receives the ticket-level data in a form it can recompute. Challenging the pack means a dispute the client's small team cannot staff.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Write every KPI as an executable definition agreed by both sides at contract start, run it on the raw ticket data each month, and publish the exclusions with their tickets. Any change to a definition becomes a visible contract amendment rather than a spreadsheet edit.

## Who Feels the Pain
Client contract managers, provider facility managers who would rather not argue over definitions, and finance teams on both sides settling fee-at-risk.

## Impact If Fixed
Monthly disputes over how numbers were calculated disappear, and the review spends its time on performance rather than arithmetic.

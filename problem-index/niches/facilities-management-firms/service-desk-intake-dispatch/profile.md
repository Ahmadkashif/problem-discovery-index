# Service Desk Intake & Dispatch

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/facilities-management-firms|Facilities Management Firms]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn a vague occupant request into a correctly classified, prioritised and dispatched work order inside the SLA response clock, and whoever gets dispatch right the first time without a human triager takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.2B a year of service-desk labour and intake tooling across US FM accounts (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~2% of US integrated facility services revenue (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — portals and CMMS intake exist, but most requests are still read and classified by a coordinator
**Target Buyer:** Head of service desk or help desk operations at IFM providers; corporate workplace teams running in-house help desks
**Automation Potential:** High — classification, prioritisation, deduplication and routing are repetitive and well labelled by history

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Large IFM providers run national service desks that receive requests from occupants by phone, email, portal and app, around the clock. Each request has to be turned into a work order with the right site, asset, trade and priority, because the priority starts the SLA clock: an emergency misread as routine is a safety and SLA failure, and a routine call raised to priority 1 burns a callout. Coordinators triage thousands of requests a day using contract-specific priority definitions, and repeat failures hide behind new ticket numbers.

## Current Tools & Gaps
CMMS intake modules and portals (Corrigo, ServiceChannel, Maximo, Eptura), IT service-management platforms used for workplace requests (ServiceNow), telephony and occupant apps. The gaps: classification by keyword or by hand, priority definitions held in coordinators' heads per contract, and no automatic check for a repeat failure on the same asset.

# IT Service Management Intake Rebuilt for Buildings

**Niche:** [[niches/facilities-management-firms/service-desk-intake-dispatch/profile|Service Desk Intake & Dispatch]]
**Industry:** [[industries/facilities-management-firms|Facilities Management Firms]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** IT service desks already classify, prioritise and route tickets with machine learning; building requests carry a location, a physical asset and a safety dimension IT tickets do not.
**Tags:** #bert #transformers #k-nearest-neighbors #automation #workflow-orchestration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn a vague occupant request into a correctly classified, prioritised and dispatched work order inside the SLA response clock, and whoever gets dispatch right the first time without a human triager takes the account.

## The Problem
Many corporate occupiers already route workplace requests through their IT service-management platform and hand them to the FM provider's CMMS. Classification quality on that handoff is poor, and duplicates multiply across the two systems.

## What Already Exists
IT service-management platforms such as ServiceNow, with predictive categorisation, assignment and similar-ticket detection, plus workplace service modules.

## The Customization Gap
Buildings need: a location and asset register as first-class fields, resolved from free text ("by the lifts on 4"); priority logic that includes life-safety and business-critical spaces (labs, data rooms, kitchens) defined per contract; trade and subcontractor routing rather than IT assignment groups; SLA clocks that follow the FM contract, not the IT one; and a reliable two-way handoff with the provider's CMMS so status and repeat detection work across both.

## Target Customer
Corporate workplace teams running intake on an ITSM platform, and IFM providers integrating with them.

## Impact If Solved
One intake for occupants, correct classification at the handoff, and no duplicate tickets between the client's system and the provider's.

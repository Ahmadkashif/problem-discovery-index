# Self-Delivery vs Subcontract Delivery Mix

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/facilities-management-firms|Facilities Management Firms]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to decide, service by service and region by region, whether its own technicians or a subcontractor will do the work more cheaply at the contracted SLA, and whoever builds enough self-delivery density to keep margin off the pass-through line takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$12B a year of subcontracted work on US corporate IFM accounts whose delivery route is a provider decision (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~20% of US integrated facility services revenue (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low-Medium — technician and subcontractor data both exist; the comparison between them is not made
**Target Buyer:** Regional operations directors and the head of self-delivery or technical services at IFM providers
**Automation Potential:** Medium — the cost model is automatable, the workforce decisions are not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Once an account is live, every service line in every region has two delivery routes. The provider's own technicians earn the provider labour margin but must be kept busy. Subcontractors cost a markup the contract may or may not allow and are paid only when used. The choice depends on route density: how many sites in a metro, across all the provider's accounts, need the same trade. More than half of CBRE's 2025 Building Operations & Experience revenue was pass-through cost, and its double-digit growth in Local Facilities Management is a density strategy, sharing technicians across clients in a metro. The winner is whoever has the density and knows where it is.

## Current Tools & Gaps
CMMS work orders, payroll and timekeeping, AP subcontract invoices and field-service scheduling tools, each in its own system. The gap: technician time is booked to accounts, not work orders, so the cost of self-delivered work is unknown at the level where the decision is made, and density across accounts is invisible to any single account team.

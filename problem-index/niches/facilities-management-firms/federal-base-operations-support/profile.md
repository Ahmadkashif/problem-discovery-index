# Federal Base Operations Support Contracts

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/facilities-management-firms|Facilities Management Firms]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to carry an Exceptional or Very Good CPARS record from the option years into the recompete, and whoever documents performance against the surveillance plan well enough that the government's evaluation cannot drift to Satisfactory takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$10B a year of federal base-operations and facility-support contract obligations (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~15% of US integrated facility services revenue (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — government-mandated systems for maintenance and reporting, but evidence of performance is assembled by hand
**Target Buyer:** Program managers and capture leads at BOS contractors
**Automation Potential:** High for evidence assembly and reporting; low for the field work

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Military installations and federal campuses buy base operations support: facility maintenance, utilities operation, grounds, custodial, fire protection, logistics and more, under multi-year contracts with option years. V2X (2025 revenue $4.48B, about 41% from the Army), Amentum, KBR and Fluor compete with small-business and joint-venture primes. Performance is surveilled against a quality assurance surveillance plan (QASP) and rated in CPARS, where the contractor has 14 days to respond before source selection officials can see the evaluation. That record decides the next award, so past performance is the currency. Every option year is repriced to the current Service Contract Act wage determination, with 30 days to notify the contracting officer. The backdrop is a DOD maintenance backlog GAO put at $285B for FY2025, in a department that does not consistently track work orders.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Government-specified maintenance systems and the contractor's own CMMS, QC inspection checklists, deliverable trackers and proposal libraries. The gap: work orders, QC results and corrective-action records are never mapped to the QASP's rating elements as they happen, so the evidence for a rating is reconstructed after the evaluation lands.

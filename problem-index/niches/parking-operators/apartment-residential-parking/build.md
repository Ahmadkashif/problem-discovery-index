# Assigned-Space Pricing and Waitlists for Every Building

**Niche:** [[niches/parking-operators/apartment-residential-parking/profile|Apartment & Residential Parking]]
**Industry:** [[industries/parking-operators|Parking Operators]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Apartment buildings price reserved, covered and EV-ready spaces with one flat fee set at lease-up, while waitlists for the best spaces run for months.
**Tags:** #gradient-boosting #survival-analysis #logistic-regression #revenue-impact #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn a building's resident and guest parking into assigned, billed spaces, and whoever collects the highest parking income per apartment unit takes the account.

## The Problem
A 300-unit building may have 360 spaces of four kinds: surface, covered, garage and EV-ready. They are priced at lease-up and rarely revisited. Covered spaces have a waitlist, surface spaces sit empty, and guest spaces fill with residents' second cars.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Parking is a minor line in a property manager's budget, and residents complain about increases. Assignment data lives in spreadsheets, and pricing tools built for rent do not price spaces.

## What to Build
A per-building model of demand for each space type, built from the waitlist, assignment churn, unit mix, car ownership on applications and nearby street-parking rules, recommending prices and releasing spaces from the waitlist in order. Resident guest-pass use is metered, and repeat abusers are offered a paid second space instead of a tow. Prices change at renewal so residents are not surprised mid-lease.

## Target Customer
Multifamily operators with 20+ communities; mixed-use and condo managers.

## Impact If Built
Raising parking income by ~$10–20 per unit per month (estimate) on a 300-unit building adds ~$36–72K a year of net operating income, which is worth several hundred thousand dollars of property value at typical capitalisation rates.

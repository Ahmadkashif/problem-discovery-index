# The Sticker Wall and the Midnight Tow

**Niche:** [[niches/parking-operators/apartment-residential-parking/profile|Apartment & Residential Parking]]
**Industry:** [[industries/parking-operators|Parking Operators]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Residents get towed because a sticker peeled off or a guest pass was not visible, and the property manager spends the next morning on the phone.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #data-integration #worker-facing #quick-win #compliance
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn a building's resident and guest parking into assigned, billed spaces, and whoever collects the highest parking income per apartment unit takes the account.

## The Problem
Patrols look for stickers and hang tags. A new car, a rental, a faded sticker or a guest pass on the dashboard face-down becomes a tow. The resident pays the impound fee and the manager handles the complaint, the refund demand and the review site.

## Why It's Still Broken
The patrol and the leasing office use different lists, and the tow company is paid per tow.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Register residents' and guests' plates in one list the patrol checks before any action. Send a warning text to a registered resident instead of a tow, and require photo evidence and a list check before a tow is authorised.

## Who Feels the Pain
Community managers, residents and their guests, and towing firms facing complaints and regulators.

## Impact If Fixed
If a third of tows at a 300-unit community are wrongful or disputed (estimate), checking a plate list first avoids ~10–20 tow disputes a year at ~$200–400 each in fees and refunds, plus the hours spent handling them.

# Owner Revenue Reconciliation & Reporting

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/parking-operators|Parking Operators]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to account for every dollar a managed facility collects, and whoever shows the smallest unreconciled gap between recorded parking sessions and deposited revenue at the owner's audit takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$700M a year in operator revenue-control and accounting staff, owner-side audits and close tooling for managed facilities (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~7% of operator receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — the inputs are digital, but the reconciliation is Excel and the statement is a PDF
**Target Buyer:** Operator controller or VP of revenue control; owner's asset manager and internal auditor
**Automation Potential:** High — matching, rules and variance narratives are mechanical; investigations are not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Under a management contract the revenue is the owner's, and the operator is its agent. Mobile Infrastructure Corporation's 10-K says its operators "act as agents collecting revenues on our behalf." Every month the operator must prove that sessions, payouts, billing and deposits agree, that expenses charged back follow the contract, and that the incentive fee is right. Owners audit on their own schedule, often through outside firms: RSM's May 2026 audit of Arlington County's SP+ contract covered ~$3.6M of 2025 revenue. This is the back office behind every account in [[niches/parking-operators/commercial-garage-management/profile|Commercial & Mixed-Use Garage Management]], won or lost on audit findings rather than on rates.

## Current Tools & Gaps
General ledgers (NetSuite, Sage Intacct), close tools (BlackLine, FloQast), PARCS revenue reports, marketplace payout files and BI dashboards. Gaps: no matching of sessions to money at transaction level; contract rules kept in PDFs and analysts' heads; variance explanations written by hand days later; leakage found by auditors rather than prevented.

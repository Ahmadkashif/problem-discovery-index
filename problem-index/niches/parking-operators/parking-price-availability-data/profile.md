# Parking Price & Availability Data Vendors

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/parking-operators|Parking Operators]]
**Category:** Insight Layer
**Value-Chain Position:** Data & benchmark vendors

## What They Do
Firms whose product is data about parking itself: posted prices, opening hours, space counts, payment methods and predicted availability for off-street facilities and on-street zones. Parkopedia, now part of Arrive (EasyPark Group's name since June 2025, search summary), and INRIX's parking data are the best-known examples. They license feeds and APIs to car makers' navigation systems, map and navigation apps, fleets and occasionally cities. Operators appear in the data as subjects, not usually as buyers.

## Insight Function
**Size:** ~15–50 data, survey and modelling staff per vendor (estimate); field surveyors who collect prices are production and are not counted
**Output:** Licensed price and availability feeds, occupancy predictions, coverage reports for automotive and navigation clients
**Proprietary data:** Years of surveyed and crowd-reported price and facility records across many countries, plus availability models; city open-data feeds such as Seattle's are public inputs, not a moat
**Clock:** Car makers' model-year integration programmes and contract renewals (client clocks); no third-party deadline
**Buyer:** Head of data products or VP of automotive partnerships

**Kill switches:** none on the owned half — the surveyed and crowd-reported price records are the vendor's own, and public city open data is not a switch. Availability predictions that draw on operators' occupancy feeds rest on data-sharing terms that were not read, so that half is undetermined. The owned half carries the verdict, and the undetermined half would not lift the score.
**Verdict:** Logged — below threshold (36). The data is the product, but it grows with crawlers and models rather than analysts, no outside deadline governs delivery, and the buyers are a few car makers and map platforms. It sits below the closest analogue, [[niches/outdoor-advertising-companies/roadway-mobility-data-suppliers/profile|Roadway Mobility & Traffic Data Suppliers]] (43). The gap is Q1 −3 (parking data is often bundled into navigation deals rather than invoiced alone, 4 against 5), Q2 −2 (smaller modelling teams, 2 against 3) and Q5 −2 (a dozen automotive buyers against DOTs, planners and retailers, 2 against 3).

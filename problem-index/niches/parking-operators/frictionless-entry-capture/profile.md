# Frictionless Entry & Paid-at-Exit Capture

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/parking-operators|Parking Operators]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to have every car pay before it leaves, and whoever collects the highest share of parking sessions as payment at or before exit, with no notice needed, takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$500M a year in camera-entry platforms, gateless conversions, pay-by-plate and member programmes at operator-run facilities (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~5% of operator receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** High at converted sites; most facilities still run ticket-and-gate PARCS
**Target Buyer:** Operator COO or VP of technology; owners deciding on gateless conversion
**Automation Potential:** High — recognition, session matching and charging are automated end to end

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The winner here is the platform that makes payment happen without a person or a notice. Metropolis's computer-vision platform charges enrolled members on exit; it reported nearly 20 million members and over $5B of annual transactions in November 2025. AirGarage runs unattended lots on cameras and app payment, and FLASH, Rekor and Genetec sell camera and PARCS stacks to other operators. The contest is measured before any notice exists: sessions paid at or before exit as a share of all sessions. Metropolis was hiring for exactly this work on 2026-10-28: a Senior Machine Learning Engineer, Computer Vision (Seattle, first posted 2026-05-20) and Senior Staff Software Engineers on its Recognition Platform (Los Angeles, New York and Seattle, 2026-08-26). Where states restrict plate recognition at public lots, as Maine's 2025 law does, the platform has to fall back on QR, app or ticket entry.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Camera recognition platforms, member apps, pay stations with plate entry, PARCS integrations. Gaps: misreads that turn paying members into "unpaid"; no prompt between a failed charge and a notice; state-by-state limits on where cameras may be used.

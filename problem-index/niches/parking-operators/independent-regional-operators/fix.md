# The Cash Box and the Clipboard

**Niche:** [[niches/parking-operators/independent-regional-operators/profile|Independent & Regional Operators]]
**Industry:** [[industries/parking-operators|Parking Operators]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** At small event and surface lots an attendant still takes cash and counts cars on a clipboard, and the owner has to trust the count.
**Tags:** #object-detection #cnns #compliance #quick-win #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep lot owners from handing their facilities to a national or tech-led operator, and whoever keeps the largest share of its owner contracts at each renewal takes the account.

## The Problem
On a game night an attendant waves cars into a gravel lot, takes $20 bills and keeps a tally. The operator reconciles the cash with the tally, and the owner gets a share of a number neither can verify.

## Why It's Still Broken
Hardware is expensive for a lot used a few nights a month, and attendants resist anything that looks like surveillance of them.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A phone or a single camera counting vehicles at the entrance, card and QR payment at the attendant's handheld, and a nightly reconciliation of count against payments sent to operator and owner. No plate is read, which keeps it outside the states that restrict plate recognition.

## Who Feels the Pain
Small operators answering owners' questions; owners paid on unverifiable counts; attendants accused of skimming.

## Impact If Fixed
If cash-lot leakage runs at ~3–5% of revenue (estimate), a lot taking $200K a year recovers ~$6–10K for operator and owner, and the owner gets a count it can trust.

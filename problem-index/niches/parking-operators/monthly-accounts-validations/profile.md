# Monthly Accounts, Access Cards & Validations

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/parking-operators|Parking Operators]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep a facility's monthly-contract capacity sold and paid, and whoever bills and collects the highest share of that capacity each month takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$500M a year in monthly-account billing staff, access credentials, validation programmes and collections at commercial garages (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~5% of operator receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — monthly accounts live in spreadsheets or a PARCS module, hang tags and access cards are issued by hand, and validations are stamps, stickers or paper codes
**Target Buyer:** Operator facility manager and monthly-accounts coordinator; office building property manager whose leases carry parking ratios
**Automation Potential:** High — billing, credential lifecycle and validation invoicing are rule-bound

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Monthly contracts are the steady base of an office or mixed-use garage. Tenant employers buy blocks of spaces under lease parking ratios, individuals buy cards, and retailers, restaurants, hotels and medical tenants validate their customers' parking. Mobile Infrastructure Corporation names the transient/contract mix as a revenue lever and runs an internal sales team for monthly contracts. The work is the billing and credential administration: who holds a card, who has paid, which lapsed cards still open the gate, how many cards a facility can sell because not every holder comes in each day, and which validations were redeemed but never invoiced. Office tenants' parking ratios also tie this niche to [[industries/commercial-real-estate|Commercial Real Estate]] leases.

## Current Tools & Gaps
PARCS monthly modules, access-card systems, validation programmes from PARCS vendors and apps, operator billing portals and spreadsheets. Gaps: no forecast of how many cards can be sold safely; lapsed credentials left active; validations not reconciled to invoices; tenant-employer invoices built by hand.

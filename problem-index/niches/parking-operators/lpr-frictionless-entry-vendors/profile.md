# Plate-Recognition & Frictionless-Entry Technology Vendors

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/parking-operators|Parking Operators]]
**Category:** Insight Layer
**Value-Chain Position:** Suppliers selling into the industry

## What They Do
Vendors whose cameras and models recognise vehicles at parking entrances and exits so drivers can pay without a ticket or gate interaction. Metropolis runs its own computer-vision platform across the facilities it operates (4,000+ locations since buying SP+, nearly 20 million members in November 2025). FLASH, Rekor, Genetec AutoVu and Asura sell recognition and gateless PARCS to other operators, and Asura's video analytics feed notice processing at LAZ-managed lots in the Crostarosa complaint. Operators and owners pay for hardware, software subscriptions or, at Metropolis, an operating contract.

## Insight Function
**Size:** ~15–100 computer-vision, ML and data engineers at the largest vendor, far fewer at most (estimate). Annotators and image reviewers are production and are not counted. Metropolis listed 30 engineering and data-science postings on 2026-10-28, including a Senior Machine Learning Engineer, Computer Vision (Seattle, 2026-05-20)
**Output:** Recognition models, session matching, gateless PARCS software, member payment flows
**Proprietary data:** Labelled plate and vehicle images and session histories, captured at operators' and owners' facilities
**Clock:** Product release cycles and clients' conversion dates (client clocks); no third-party deadline
**Buyer:** CTO or head of product at the vendor

**Kill switches:** live on the vendor half. The images that carry Q3 are captured at clients' facilities for the client's use under service agreements whose terms were not seen, and client data under service agreements carries the switch unless checked terms grant product rights. Metropolis's own member records sit under member terms that were not read (undetermined), and at the managed sites it operates the session revenue is the owner's. Separately (not a kill switch), state law now limits where plates may be read at all: Maine's 2025 chapter 350 applies the state's ALPR prohibition to lots open to the public.
**Verdict:** Logged — kill switch (32). The verdict follows the vendor half, because the imaging teams and the data moat being scored sit on client captures.

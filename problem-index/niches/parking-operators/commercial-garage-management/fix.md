# The Renewal Pitch With No Evidence

**Niche:** [[niches/parking-operators/commercial-garage-management/profile|Commercial & Mixed-Use Garage Management]]
**Industry:** [[industries/parking-operators|Parking Operators]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Every management-contract renewal is defended with a slide deck of promises because the operator never recorded which of its decisions earned the owner money.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #causal-inference #evaluation-metrics #revenue-impact #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to return the most parking income per space to the owner after fees and expenses, and whoever delivers the highest net parking income per space against the owner's budget takes the account.

## The Problem
Management contracts run 1–3 years and owners rebid them. The incumbent's renewal proposal looks like every challenger's: technology, service, a projected revenue uplift. A challenger promising gateless entry and 15% more revenue sounds as credible as an incumbent with three years of results, because the incumbent cannot show what it did.

## Why It's Still Broken
Rate changes, monthly-account campaigns and staffing changes are made in different systems by different people, and nobody records the decision with its expected effect. When the renewal comes, the account team rebuilds a story from memory.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Log every material decision at the facility (rate change, channel added, monthly campaign, staffing change, technology conversion) with its date and expected effect. Six weeks later, draft a one-paragraph result note comparing the outcome with the expectation and a comparable period. At renewal the account team exports the facility's decision history and results as the proposal's core.

## Who Feels the Pain
Operator account and regional managers who lose accounts they ran well; owners' asset managers who must choose between proposals they cannot test.

## Impact If Fixed
If documented results raise renewal retention by even 5 points on a regional book of 200 managed facilities (estimate), the operator keeps ~10 more contracts at each cycle, each worth tens of thousands of dollars a year in fees.

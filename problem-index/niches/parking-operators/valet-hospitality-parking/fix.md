# The Claim-Ticket Tick-Box Nobody Fills In

**Niche:** [[niches/parking-operators/valet-hospitality-parking/profile|Valet & Hospitality Parking]]
**Industry:** [[industries/parking-operators|Parking Operators]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The "existing damage" box on the valet ticket is the operator's only defence against a claim, and on a busy night nobody fills it in.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #cnns #worker-facing #quick-win #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to park and return every guest's car undamaged, and whoever has the lowest damage and lost-key claim cost per 1,000 cars parked takes the account.

## The Problem
Paper and digital valet tickets carry a damage diagram for the runner to mark. At 8 pm with ten cars waiting it stays blank, and a blank box reads as "no damage" when a claim arrives.

## Why It's Still Broken
Marking damage takes time the runner does not have and earns them nothing. Captains do not check the box, and claims arrive days later, when nobody remembers the car.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Replace the diagram with two phone photos (front three-quarter and rear three-quarter) captured by the same tap that issues the digital ticket. A small model checks the photo shows the whole car and asks for a retake if not. Any obvious damage is pre-marked for the runner to confirm. At claim time the photos are attached automatically.

## Who Feels the Pain
Valet runners blamed for damage they did not cause; captains who adjudicate claims on hearsay; hotel managers fielding guest complaints.

## Impact If Fixed
If two forced photos add ~10 seconds a car (estimate), a 400-car night costs about an hour of runner time in total, against claims that cost ~$500–2,500 each (estimate) and the runner write-ups that follow them.

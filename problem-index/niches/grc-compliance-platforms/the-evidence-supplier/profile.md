# The Engineer Supplying Evidence

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/grc-compliance-platforms|GRC & Compliance Platforms]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Whether the residual evidence work reaches engineers as a specific, contextualised, one-off task or as recurring interruption they experience as a tax.

## Profile

**Market Size:** ~$720M
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~8%
**Digital Adoption:** Low — a ticket and a deadline
**Target Buyer:** Engineering leadership, compliance leadership
**Automation Potential:** High for the requests, low for the judgement they need

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche

Automation collected most of the evidence. What remains is the part the integrations cannot reach, and it lands on engineers: a screenshot of a configuration in a system with no API, confirmation that a process was followed, a justification for an exception, remediation of a finding the platform surfaced, an explanation of why an alert fired.

It arrives as tickets, from someone outside the engineering organisation, with a deadline set by an audit, about systems the requester does not understand well enough to be specific. The engineer receives a request to provide evidence of change management for the payments service and has to work out what that means, what would satisfy it, and what format it should be in.

This is where the category's promise frays. The pitch is that automation removed the compliance burden; what happened is that it moved from a compliance team doing screenshots to engineers doing the residue, concentrated before audits, framed as a distraction from the work they are measured on. The attitude that compliance is theatre is formed here, and it makes everything downstream harder.

## Current Tools & Gaps

Evidence request tickets in the engineering tracker. Manual upload into the platform. Remediation findings surfaced by the platform and assigned to owners, sometimes accurately. Reminder emails as deadlines approach. Some platforms offer engineer-facing integrations into code hosting and ticketing.

The gaps are about specificity and timing. Requests are phrased in framework language rather than in terms of the system, so the engineer has to translate before they can act. Nothing tells them what would satisfy the requirement, so evidence is produced, rejected and reproduced. Requests cluster before audits rather than being spread. The same request recurs every cycle with no memory that the same person answered it last year. Ownership mapping is coarse, so requests reach the wrong team and are forwarded. And nothing explains why any of it is being asked, which is the omission that produces the resentment.

# Vendor & Third-Party Risk

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/grc-compliance-platforms|GRC & Compliance Platforms]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Whether supplier assessment is scaled to what each supplier actually touches, or applied near-uniformly across hundreds of them because the process is the deliverable.

## Profile

**Market Size:** ~$1.26B
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~14%
**Digital Adoption:** Moderate — workflow plus questionnaires
**Target Buyer:** Vendor risk teams, procurement, security leadership
**Automation Potential:** Very high — collection, tiering and monitoring are all mechanical

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche

This is the same apparatus pointed outward. An organisation assesses its suppliers the way its customers assess it: send a questionnaire, collect a certificate, record that an assessment was performed, repeat annually.

The scale is what makes it a distinct market. A mid-sized company has hundreds of suppliers and a large one has thousands, against a vendor risk function of a handful of people. The arithmetic forces a near-uniform, shallow process — a questionnaire, a certificate, a file — applied to the payroll processor with access to every employee record and to the design tool one team uses for mockups.

The contest is over tiering and continuity. A programme that concentrated its effort on the small number of suppliers with genuine access, and monitored them continuously rather than annually, would produce far better risk reduction from the same headcount. Almost every programme instead spreads effort evenly and assesses at a point in time, because that is what the process was built to produce and because the thing being measured is coverage of the supplier list.

## Current Tools & Gaps

Vendor risk platforms handling questionnaire distribution, response collection, certificate storage, review workflow and renewal scheduling. Security ratings services providing externally observable scores. Some continuous monitoring of supplier external posture. Shared assessment models with partial adoption. Contract and procurement integration of varying depth.

The gaps follow the arithmetic. Tiering is coarse or absent, so effort is uniform across a wildly non-uniform supplier population. Data access is often not recorded accurately, so the tiering input does not exist. Assessment is annual against relationships that change continuously — a supplier's access expands, they are acquired, they change subprocessors. Fourth-party exposure is largely invisible. Nothing verifies self-asserted answers. And nothing measures whether the programme's assessments predict supplier incidents, which is the same unasked question that runs through this whole industry.

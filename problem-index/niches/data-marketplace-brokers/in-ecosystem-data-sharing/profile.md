# In-Ecosystem Data Sharing

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/data-marketplace-brokers|Data Marketplace Brokers]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this sub-niche is fighting to make external data appear inside the buyer's own platform with governance and lineage intact and nothing copied — and whoever does that takes the account, because the buyer has already chosen the platform and is choosing between its marketplace and friction.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$950M US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~19% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** High
**Target Buyer:** Enterprises standardised on a cloud data platform
**Automation Potential:** High

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Inside a cloud data platform, a shared dataset can appear as a queryable object in the buyer's account without being copied, with access controlled by the platform's own governance and lineage recorded by the platform's own catalogue. That is a genuinely large improvement over file delivery, and it is why these marketplaces have grown quickly. The contest is the quality of that experience: how completely the shared object behaves like a native one, how governance and lineage flow across the boundary, what happens when the provider updates or withdraws, and whether the buyer's compliance obligations can be discharged against a platform's records. The competitor is the other cloud data platform, and the buyer's platform choice is usually already made — which means this is fought supplier-side, for the providers who decide which ecosystems to publish into.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Zero-copy sharing primitives, cross-account and cross-region sharing, governance integration, and usage-based billing. The gaps: shared objects behave differently from native ones in ways that surface late; provider-side updates and schema changes reach consumers without warning; lineage frequently stops at the share boundary; consumption cost is attributed to the consumer with no visibility for the provider; and cross-cloud sharing remains awkward enough to force duplication.

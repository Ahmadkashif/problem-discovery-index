**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to give the reviewer something better than a supplier's self-assessment to certify against — and whoever does that takes the account, because this signature is the gate every data purchase now passes through.

## The Problem
Assessing a supplier once and sharing the result with many buyers is the structural answer third-party risk management arrived at years ago: shared assessment utilities, standardised questionnaires, and independent audit reports that a supplier commissions and distributes. Financial services and healthcare run this at scale. Data supplier provenance review is conducted independently by every buyer, asking similar questions, receiving the same self-assessments, and reaching conclusions nobody shares.

## What Already Exists
Shared third-party risk assessment utilities with member-funded supplier examinations; standardised security and privacy questionnaires; independent audit and attestation reports commissioned by the supplier and distributed to customers; continuous vendor monitoring services; and supplier risk scoring with published methodologies.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a risk that concerns how a supplier obtained its inventory. It requires: (1) assessment scope covering collection practice and consent rather than security controls, which is a genuinely different examination and the one no existing questionnaire performs; (2) upstream chain assessment, since a data supplier's risk is largely inherited and third-party risk practice already has the concept of fourth-party risk to build on; (3) evidence standards defining what constitutes proof of consent, which does not exist and which an attestation regime would have to establish first; (4) a shared utility funded by buyers, since the duplication is the largest waste in the process and no supplier will fund an examination that might fail; and (5) continuous rather than point-in-time monitoring, because a supplier's practices and the regulatory environment both change and an annual certification ages badly here.

## Target Customer
Compliance and vendor risk functions, data suppliers tired of completing four hundred questionnaires, marketplaces, and the assurance and audit professions.

## Impact If Solved
Shared assessment is the structural answer third-party risk reached years ago and this market repeats every examination. Defining what constitutes evidence of consent is the prerequisite, and upstream chain assessment is where the inherited risk actually sits.

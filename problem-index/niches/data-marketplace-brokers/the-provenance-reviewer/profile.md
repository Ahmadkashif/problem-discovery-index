# The Provenance Reviewer

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/data-marketplace-brokers|Data Marketplace Brokers]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to give the reviewer something better than a supplier's self-assessment to certify against — and whoever does that takes the account, because this signature is the gate every data purchase now passes through.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$260M US in review cost and blocked transactions
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~5% of category revenue equivalent
**Digital Adoption:** None — certifying on a self-completed questionnaire
**Target Buyer:** Compliance, vendor risk and legal functions
**Automation Potential:** Medium — evidence gathering is automatable, judgement is not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A provenance reviewer is asked to certify that a supplier's data was lawfully collected with valid consent, on the basis of a questionnaire the supplier completed about itself. They cannot audit the supplier, cannot see the upstream chain, cannot verify the consent language, and cannot inspect the data before purchase. Their tools are the questionnaire, the supplier's public privacy policy, and their own judgement. They carry personal and organisational accountability for a conclusion they have no means of reaching, and the rational response — declining — blocks transactions the business wants. They are the most exposed individual in this market and are served by nothing.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Supplier questionnaires, privacy policy review, contractual warranties, and vendor risk platforms designed for software suppliers. The gaps: no evidence beyond self-assessment; no visibility into upstream sources, where the actual risk usually sits; no comparable basis across suppliers, so each review is bespoke; no record of what was certified on what basis when a regulator asks later; and no independent party to defer to.

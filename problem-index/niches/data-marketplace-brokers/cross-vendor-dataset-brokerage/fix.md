# The Requirement Nobody Could Fill

**Niche:** [[niches/data-marketplace-brokers/cross-vendor-dataset-brokerage/profile|Cross-Vendor Dataset Brokerage]]
**Industry:** [[industries/data-marketplace-brokers|Data Marketplace Brokers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Buyers ask for data that does not exist in the market, the enquiry ends, and nobody records what was asked for — discarding the only reliable signal of what the market should be supplying.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #k-means-clustering #evaluation-metrics #revenue-impact #data-integration #automation #quick-win #confidence-intervals
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this sub-niche is fighting to turn a buyer's vague requirement into a shortlist of suppliers that actually exist, including the ones with no digital shopfront — and whoever does that takes the account, because the buyer's alternative is a month of cold outreach.

## The Problem
Forty buyers over a year ask for the same thing — a particular attribute at a particular granularity for a particular sector — and no supplier offers it. Each enquiry ends with an apology and disappears. Nobody counts them, nobody notices the pattern, and nobody tells the three companies who hold that data as operational exhaust that forty enterprises would pay for it. The most valuable information in a marketplace is what buyers wanted and could not get, and the market's structure ensures it evaporates at the moment it is generated.

## Why It's Still Broken
Failed enquiries are not transactions, so no system records them. Brokers experience them as lost time rather than as data. Acting on the signal requires going out to create supply, which is a different and harder business than matching existing supply. And each individual failure looks idiosyncratic — the pattern only appears in aggregate, which requires someone to be aggregating.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Capture the failure and act on the pattern. Record every requirement in structured form at intake, including the ones that go nowhere, which is a small process change and creates the dataset this whole fix depends on. Cluster unmet requirements to find recurring demand, since forty scattered enquiries only become a business case when they are counted together. Report the aggregate demand signal to potential suppliers, which converts a broker from a matcher into a market maker and is a materially stronger position. Approach latent suppliers with the specific demand attached — this many buyers, this requirement, this willingness to pay — which is a far more compelling approach than a general invitation to list. Publish an anonymised demand index, since that alone attracts supply and costs nothing to produce. Tell buyers when their requirement is common but unmet, because several buyers with the same gap may fund its creation together and none of them can know that today. Support commissioned collection as a product where the demand justifies it. And track how often a recorded unmet requirement is later filled, which is the measure of whether the market is actually being made.

## Who Feels the Pain
Buyers whose requirement quietly went nowhere; companies sitting on saleable data nobody told them was wanted; and brokers whose business is limited to the supply that already exists.

## Impact If Fixed
Unmet demand is the market's most valuable signal and it evaporates at the moment it is generated. Clustering it turns forty scattered failures into a business case, and it converts a broker from a matcher into a market maker.

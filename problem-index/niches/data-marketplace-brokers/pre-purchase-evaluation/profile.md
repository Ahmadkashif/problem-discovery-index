# Pre-Purchase Evaluation

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/data-marketplace-brokers|Data Marketplace Brokers]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to let a buyer measure a dataset against their own requirement before paying for it — and whoever does that takes the market, because every purchase in it is currently made blind.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.3B US attributable to the evaluation problem
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~26% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** None — properties discoverable only after payment
**Target Buyer:** Every buyer in the market, and the providers with genuinely good data
**Automation Potential:** High — the measurements are mechanical given access

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The properties that determine whether a dataset is worth buying — how much of the buyer's own population it covers, how fresh it is, how accurate it is against a reference the buyer trusts, and whether the specific fields they need are actually populated rather than nominally present — are all discoverable only after purchase and integration. What is offered instead is a curated sample, self-reported statistics and a description. The result is a market where buyers price in the probability of a bad outcome, good providers cannot distinguish themselves, and a large share of transactions end in a dataset that is quietly abandoned. Solving it is the difference between a listing service and a market.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Sample files, self-reported coverage and volume figures, data dictionaries, and trial periods at some providers. The gaps: samples are curated and therefore unrepresentative by construction; no measurement against the buyer's own population, which is the only coverage figure that matters; field population rates are not reported, so a documented field that is ninety percent null is indistinguishable from one that is complete; no accuracy measurement against any reference; and no standard evaluation protocol, so nothing is comparable across providers.

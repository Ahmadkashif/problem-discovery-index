# The Fabric That Failed the Disinfectant

**Niche:** [[niches/interior-design-firms/healthcare-senior-living-interiors/profile|Healthcare & Senior Living Interiors]]
**Industry:** [[industries/interior-design-firms|Interior Design Firms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A fabric approved as cleanable cracks or fades within months because the facility switched to a disinfectant the manufacturer never tested.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #bert #feature-engineering #compliance #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to prove that every finish and furnishing in a care setting meets infection-control, fire, accessibility and resident-safety requirements and survives the facility's cleaning regime, and whoever makes that proof fastest wins the specification.

## The Problem
Manufacturers publish cleaning guidance against named disinfectant families; facilities change products after supply shortages or infection-control reviews. Nobody re-checks the installed fabrics.

## Why It's Still Broken
Compatibility is stated in PDFs in inconsistent terms, and the facility's disinfectant choice is made by environmental services, not by the designer.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Extract disinfectant compatibility from manufacturer documents into the finish record, ask the facility's current disinfectant list at specification, and alert both the designer and the facility when the list or the specification changes.

## Who Feels the Pain
Healthcare designers whose specifications are blamed; environmental services teams; residents and patients sitting on degraded surfaces that are harder to clean.

## Impact If Fixed
Incompatible combinations are caught before purchase, avoiding early replacement of upholstery across whole facilities.

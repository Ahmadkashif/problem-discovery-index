# Construction Job Costing Adapted to Design Resale

**Niche:** [[niches/interior-design-firms/residential-full-service-studios/profile|Residential Full-Service Studios]]
**Industry:** [[industries/interior-design-firms|Interior Design Firms]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Contractors have had job costing with committed costs and change orders for decades; design studios need the same discipline for a project made of resold product rather than labour and materials.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #feature-engineering #large-language-models #data-integration #automation #compliance
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to become the single record from proposal to client invoice — selections, client deposits, purchase orders and markup — and whoever holds that record holds the studio's margin and its books.

## The Problem
A residential project behaves like a small construction job financially: a budget agreed with the client, commitments made to vendors, change orders when the client changes their mind, deposits in both directions and a final reconciliation. Studios run this with a proposal module and a ledger that do not share the concept of committed cost.

## What Already Exists
Construction job-costing tools such as QuickBooks job costing, Buildertrend and Procore financials track budgets, committed costs, change orders and draws for [[industries/general-contractors|General Contractors]].

## The Customization Gap
Design resale needs item-level selling price and markup policy rather than cost codes; client deposits as liabilities matched to purchase order lines; vendor deposits as prepaid assets; sales tax by delivery state with resale-certificate handling; client-facing budget views that show the room, not the cost code; and change orders triggered by selection swaps rather than scope changes.

## Target Customer
Studios that have outgrown QuickBooks plus spreadsheets and run projects above ~$250,000 in product.

## Impact If Solved
Committed-cost visibility tells a principal mid-project whether the client's budget and the studio's margin still hold, instead of discovering it at the final invoice.

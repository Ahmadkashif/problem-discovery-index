# Generative Space Planning Tied to Local Fit-Out Costs

**Niche:** [[niches/interior-design-firms/test-fit-and-ti-budgeting/profile|Broker Test-Fits & TI Budgeting]]
**Industry:** [[industries/interior-design-firms|Interior Design Firms]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Generative layout tools fill a floor plate in minutes but know nothing about what the result will cost in this city or this building.
**Tags:** #optimization-fundamentals #linear-regression #feature-engineering #data-integration #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn a candidate building's floor plate and a tenant's headcount into a test fit and a credible fit-out budget within 48 hours of a broker's request, and whoever is fastest and closest on cost wins the design commission when the lease is signed.

## The Problem
Designers have begun using generative space-planning tools to produce early layouts. The layouts are useful; the budgets are still done separately with per-square-foot guides.

## What Already Exists
Generative and parametric space-planning tools for office layouts, and published fit-out cost guides from brokers such as JLL and Cushman & Wakefield.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs quantity take-off from each generated layout; cost libraries by city and building class drawn from local fit-out data rather than national averages; an existing-conditions input (second-generation space, demising, core upgrades); and an output formatted for brokers' building comparisons against tenant improvement allowances.

## Target Customer
Commercial interiors firms already using generative layout tools for test fits.

## Impact If Solved
A layout and a defensible budget leave the office together, which is what the broker actually needs.

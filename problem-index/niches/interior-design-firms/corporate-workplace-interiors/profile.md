# Corporate Workplace Interiors

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/interior-design-firms|Interior Design Firms]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is that every serious competitor is fighting to win the occupier's fit-out commission, but the contest splits in two: before a lease is signed, firms compete to produce a test fit and a credible budget for each candidate building fastest; after occupancy, they compete to prove with measured utilisation how much space and what mix the occupier needs. Different clocks, buyers and winners. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.5B US spend on workplace interiors design fees, workplace strategy, test-fit services and space-planning tools (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~20% of interior design revenue (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium-High — drawings in AutoCAD and Revit, furniture in CET, utilisation data increasingly from sensors and badge systems
**Target Buyer:** Occupier's head of real estate or workplace, with the tenant-rep broker as gatekeeper
**Automation Potential:** High for test fits and utilisation analysis, medium for design development

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Office interiors are bought around lease events. A tenant searching for space needs test fits and budgets for several buildings; a tenant renewing or consolidating needs to know how much space it really uses. Both are judged against fit-out costs that JLL puts at ~$280 per square foot on average in the US in 2025, and against tenant improvement allowances that Cushman & Wakefield finds are not keeping pace. Contract furniture, specified through dealers in Configura's CET Designer, is a large share of the budget and is often sold by a dealer alongside the design.

## Current Tools & Gaps
AutoCAD, Revit and CET for drawings and furniture; generative space-planning tools for early layouts; occupancy platforms such as Density, VergeSense and workplace-experience surveys for utilisation; broker cost guides for budgets. The gaps: existing furniture is rarely inventoried for reuse; the designer's drawings and the dealer's order drift apart; and fit-out project tools are built for contractors.

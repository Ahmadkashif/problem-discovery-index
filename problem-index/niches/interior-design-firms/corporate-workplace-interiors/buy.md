**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Construction project platforms run RFIs, submittals and punch lists for the builder; the interior designer on the same fit-out works the FF&E side of those processes in email.

**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is that every serious competitor is fighting to win the occupier's fit-out commission, but the contest splits in two: before a lease is signed, firms compete to produce a test fit and a credible budget for each candidate building fastest; after occupancy, they compete to prove with measured utilisation how much space and what mix the occupier needs. Different clocks, buyers and winners. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## The Problem
On a fit-out the designer answers contractor RFIs on finishes, reviews finish and furniture submittals, coordinates furniture delivery with construction, and walks the punch list. The contractor runs this in a construction platform; the designer joins as a guest or not at all.

## What Already Exists
Procore, Autodesk Construction Cloud and similar platforms used by [[industries/general-contractors|General Contractors]] for RFIs, submittals, schedules and punch lists.

## The Customization Gap
The designer's side needs finish and FF&E submittals checked against the specification attribute by attribute; furniture delivery and install windows tied to construction milestones; punch items for furniture and finishes separated from building defects and routed to the dealer or vendor; and a design-intent record that links each answered RFI to the drawing it changed.

## Target Customer
Commercial interiors practices delivering office fit-outs of 20,000 square feet and above.

## Impact If Solved
Designers stop re-keying construction-platform items into their own trackers, and furniture punch items reach the party who must fix them in days rather than weeks.

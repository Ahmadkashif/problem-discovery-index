## The Problem
When a company merges two floors into one or closes a regional office, the cheapest and lowest-carbon furniture is the furniture it already owns. Reusing it needs an inventory: manufacturer, model, dimensions, finish, condition and quantity. Today someone walks the floors with a clipboard, or the inventory is skipped and new furniture is ordered while the old goes to liquidation.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Furniture asset records are poor or absent at most occupiers; dealers know what they sold but not what remains or its condition; and an inventory has no clear payer until a project starts, by which point the schedule leaves no time for it.

## What to Build
A walk-through capture tool: phone video of each floor, object detection and product recognition against contract furniture catalogues, condition grading, and counts mapped to the floor plan. Output a reuse inventory the designer can drag into the new layout, with a comparison of reuse versus new cost and embodied carbon.

## Target Customer
Workplace interiors teams and contract furniture dealers running consolidations, and occupiers' real estate and sustainability leads.

## Impact If Built
A floor that takes a person two days to inventory is captured in an hour, and reuse becomes a default option in the design rather than an afterthought, cutting furniture budgets on consolidations by a meaningful share (estimate).

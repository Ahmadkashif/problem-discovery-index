# Solo & Two-Person Studios

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/interior-design-firms|Interior Design Firms]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to let a one-person studio run procurement, billing and client communication without its first hire, and whoever replaces that first assistant takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$150M US spend on software and outsourced admin by solo and two-person design studios (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** a majority of firms by count but ~20% of revenue (estimate; BLS counts 26% of interior designers as self-employed)
**Digital Adoption:** Low-Medium — a mix of free tools, a studio app at the cheapest tier, and spreadsheets
**Target Buyer:** The designer-owner, who is also the bookkeeper, expediter and salesperson
**Automation Potential:** High for administration

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A one-person studio does everything the larger firm does — design, procurement, billing, sales tax, client communication — with no staff. Its growth limit is the first hire: once the owner has more projects than evenings, the choice is between turning work away and taking on payroll. Tools are priced for this buyer (DesignFiles from $49 a month, Programa from $59 per user) but are designed as smaller versions of studio software, not as a substitute for an assistant.

## Current Tools & Gaps
DesignFiles, Mydoma (now part of Studio Designer), Houzz Pro, Programa, Canva, freelancer suites such as HoneyBook and Dubsado, and QuickBooks. Gaps: no tool does the work an assistant would do unprompted; time is not logged; and procurement still runs from the owner's inbox.

# To-the-Trade Procurement & Receiving

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/interior-design-firms|Interior Design Firms]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to know, without a phone call, the true status and landed date of every item on order and to catch a wrong acknowledgement before the item ships, and whoever closes the gap between vendor paperwork and the receiving dock takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.2B US spend on receiving warehouses, white-glove delivery, freight management and procurement services used by design firms (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** touches ~70% of firms, the ones that resell product (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — purchase orders in studio software, everything after the PO in email, phone, vendor portals and spreadsheets
**Target Buyer:** Studio principal or procurement coordinator; receiving-warehouse owners on the supply side
**Automation Potential:** High

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
After a purchase order leaves the studio, an item passes through the vendor's acknowledgement, production, a freight carrier, a receiving warehouse that inspects and stores it, and a white-glove delivery and installation crew. Each party has its own system or none, and the designer stitches them together. Errors in finish or dimensions, slipped lead times and freight damage all surface here. Receiving warehouses and third-party procurement services sell this coordination as a service, which makes the niche a buyer of tools as well as a pain point.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Purchase-order modules in Studio Designer, DesignManager and Houzz Pro; vendor portals; carrier tracking pages; receiving-warehouse email reports and photographs. Gaps: no shared status between vendor, receiver and designer; acknowledgements checked by reading; concealed damage found too late to claim.

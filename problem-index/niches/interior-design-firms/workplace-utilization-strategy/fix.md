# The Post-Occupancy Evaluation Nobody Runs

**Niche:** [[niches/interior-design-firms/workplace-utilization-strategy/profile|Workplace Utilisation & Space Strategy]]
**Industry:** [[industries/interior-design-firms|Interior Design Firms]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Firms recommend a setting mix, the office opens, and nobody checks whether the recommendation was right.
**Tags:** #hypothesis-testing #descriptive-statistics #causal-inference #evaluation-metrics #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to prove, with measured occupancy rather than surveys and opinion, how much space and what mix of settings an occupier actually needs, and whoever produces the most defensible right-sizing recommendation wins the redesign.

## The Problem
Post-occupancy evaluation is widely endorsed and rarely performed, because it falls after the fee ends. The firm's next recommendation is therefore based on judgement, not on how its last ten performed.

## Why It's Still Broken
No one pays for it, and the occupancy data that would answer it stays with the occupier.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Write a light evaluation into the engagement: six and twelve months after move-in, pull the occupier's occupancy data, compare actual use by setting against the predicted program, and record the error in the firm's own library.

## Who Feels the Pain
Workplace strategists defending recommendations without evidence; occupiers left with empty rooms or overflowing ones.

## Impact If Fixed
The firm builds the evidence base that makes its next recommendation more defensible than a competitor's.

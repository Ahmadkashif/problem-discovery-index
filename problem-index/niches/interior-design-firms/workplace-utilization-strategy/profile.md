# Workplace Utilisation & Space Strategy

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/interior-design-firms|Interior Design Firms]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to prove, with measured occupancy rather than surveys and opinion, how much space and what mix of settings an occupier actually needs, and whoever produces the most defensible right-sizing recommendation wins the redesign.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$900M US spend on workplace strategy consulting, occupancy sensing and workplace analytics tied to interiors decisions (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~5% of interior design revenue, with most spend going to sensor and analytics vendors (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** High for data collection, low for turning data into layouts
**Target Buyer:** Occupier's head of workplace or corporate real estate
**Automation Potential:** High for analysis, medium for recommendation

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Hybrid work has made most offices under-used on some days and over-full on others. Before redesigning or renewing, occupiers want to know how many desks, meeting rooms and focus spaces they really need. Evidence comes from badge swipes, Wi-Fi, desk booking, occupancy sensors and workplace-experience surveys. The designer who turns that evidence into a defensible program wins the redesign; the contest is evidence quality, not drawing speed.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Occupancy platforms such as Density, VergeSense and Basking; desk-booking systems; workplace-experience surveys such as the Leesman Index (owned by HqO since 2022). Gaps: dashboards are built for facility managers, not for design decisions; setting-level demand curves (how many four-person rooms at peak) are not produced; and post-occupancy evaluation is rarely done.

# Hospitality FF&E & PIP Renovation

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/interior-design-firms|Interior Design Firms]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn a brand's design standards and a property's PIP into a compliant, priced FF&E specification and bid package before the franchisor's deadline, and whoever does it fastest at the lowest defensible cost per key takes the renovation.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.5B US hospitality FF&E design, specification and procurement fees (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~10% of interior design revenue (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — spec books and FF&E schedules in Excel and PDF, PIPs issued as PDF reports, brand standards in portals
**Target Buyer:** Principal of a hospitality design firm; behind them the hotel owner's asset manager or capital projects lead
**Automation Potential:** High for scope and specification assembly, medium for design

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Branded hotels renovate on the franchisor's schedule. A property improvement plan is issued on franchise renewal, change of ownership or conversion, typically every 7–15 years, and lists what must be replaced or upgraded to current brand standards by a deadline. Select-service change-of-ownership PIPs are quoted at roughly $10,000–40,000 per key and full-service at $50,000–150,000 or more. Hospitality designers translate the PIP and the brand's standards into an FF&E package that purchasing agents bid and buy. Many properties of the same brand share near-identical scopes, which makes the work unusually repeatable for design.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Excel spec books, AutoCAD, brand standards portals, purchasing agents' procurement systems, and the paid HVS/Nehmer Hotel Cost Estimating Guide for budgets. Gaps: PIP line items are converted to scope by hand; specs are rebuilt per property; and cost-per-key history from the firm's own projects is not reused.

# Quick-Service Menu Optimisation for the Pre-Show Window

**Niche:** [[niches/movie-theaters/concessions-and-food-and-beverage/profile|Concessions & Food and Beverage]]
**Industry:** [[industries/movie-theaters|Movie Theaters]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Quick-service chains test menu boards, combos and upsell prompts with controlled experiments; theatres change combos on instinct and judge them on a busy weekend.
**Tags:** #hypothesis-testing #causal-inference #gradient-boosting #evaluation-metrics #revenue-impact #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to raise food and beverage spend per patron in the few minutes between ticket scan and trailers without letting the line run past showtime, and whoever sells the most per head at the counter, kiosk, app or seat while holding service time takes the account.

## The Problem
Circuits launch film-tied combos, souvenir buckets and new menu items and read the result from per-caps that move with every film slate, so they can't tell a good combo from a good weekend.

## What Already Exists
Quick-service chains use digital menu-board and upsell personalisation engines (Dynamic Yield is the best known) and in-house test-and-learn programmes that test prices, combos and prompts against matched control stores.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs: controls for the film slate and audience mix, since a PG opener and an R-rated opener produce different baskets; the ticket-to-concession link to compute per-capita spend by show; souvenir items tied to one release with a life of weeks; kiosk and in-app prompts timed to showtime rather than meal periods; and service-time impact measured alongside spend.

## Target Customer
F&B and marketing teams at circuits of 30+ theatres.

## Impact If Solved
Honest experiments let a circuit keep the combos that really raise per-caps and drop the ones that only rode a strong film.

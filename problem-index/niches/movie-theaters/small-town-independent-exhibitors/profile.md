# Small-Town & Independent Exhibitors

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/movie-theaters|Movie Theaters]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get a one- to four-screen house first-run films on opening weekend without run-length and screen commitments it cannot meet, and whoever wins day-and-date access on survivable terms takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.5B in receipts at theatres of one to seven screens (estimate), spending ~$60M a year on booking agents, ticketing and tools (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~10–12% of receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low-Medium — cloud ticketing is common; booking is by phone through an agent, and settlement is on paper
**Target Buyer:** Owner-operator; the booking agent who represents them
**Automation Potential:** Medium — forecasting and settlement automate; access to films is a relationship

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Cinema United reported that of its 196 members in twelve states, 130 operate fewer than ten screens and 30 are single screens. The Independent Cinema Alliance speaks for ~180 companies with ~5,000 screens. For a two-screen house the binding problem isn't scheduling or pricing; it's access. Distributors expect a wide release to hold its screen for a minimum run (often two to four weeks, estimate), so a small house that takes one blockbuster can't rotate in the next opener. A Montana independent told local press that films are "cheaper and easier" to get two weeks after opening, and that it pays through a booking agent. Since the Paramount decrees ended, there is no rule on how distributors treat a town's only screen.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Booking agents, Veezi and other cloud ticketing, the ICA's Marketplace with Fandango ClubBuy for purchasing, and Screendollars newsletters for market data. Gaps: no evidence base for negotiating access and run length, and no shared view of how each distributor treats small houses.

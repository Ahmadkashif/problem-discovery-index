# A Pooled Playability Record for Small Houses

**Niche:** [[niches/movie-theaters/small-town-independent-exhibitors/profile|Small-Town & Independent Exhibitors]]
**Industry:** [[industries/movie-theaters|Movie Theaters]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A distributor deciding whether a two-screen town gets a film on opening day sees that town's grosses; the owner arguing for it has no comparable evidence at all.
**Tags:** #gradient-boosting #bayesian-inference #time-series-forecasting #feature-engineering #revenue-impact #data-integration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get a one- to four-screen house first-run films on opening day without run-length and screen commitments it cannot meet, and whoever wins day-and-date access on survivable terms takes the account.

## The Problem
Small houses negotiate access and run length one at a time, through an agent, against distributors who hold the national theatre-level grosses. The owner can't show that films like this one earn as much per screen in towns like theirs in a two-week run as in a four-week run.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Each small theatre's history is too thin alone and sits in its own ticketing system. Pooling requires a neutral party, and the ICA and booking agencies have had neither the data tools nor the mandate.

## What to Build
A consented, pooled record of admissions by film, week and theatre across hundreds of small houses, held by the ICA, a booking agency or a ticketing vendor. It forecasts each film's playability in each small-town market and produces a one-page case for each booking: expected gross per screen-week, comparable towns, and the outcome of shorter runs elsewhere.

## Target Customer
Booking agencies representing independents; the Independent Cinema Alliance as a member service.

## Impact If Built
Evidence-backed requests for opening-week access and shorter minimum runs could move small houses' first-run share and their ability to rotate films, which decides whether a town keeps its theatre.

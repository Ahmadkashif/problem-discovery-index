# The Minimum Run That Blocks the Next Opener

**Niche:** [[niches/movie-theaters/small-town-independent-exhibitors/profile|Small-Town & Independent Exhibitors]]
**Industry:** [[industries/movie-theaters|Movie Theaters]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A two-screen house that accepts a blockbuster's minimum run can't take the next big opener, so it either waits two weeks for both or plays a fading film to empty seats.
**Tags:** #optimization-fundamentals #time-series-forecasting #evaluation-metrics #revenue-impact #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get a one- to four-screen house first-run films on opening day without run-length and screen commitments it cannot meet, and whoever wins day-and-date access on survivable terms takes the account.

## The Problem
Minimum-run and full-screen commitments were designed for multiplexes with screens to spare. For a small house each commitment locks up half its capacity, and back-to-back openers force a choice between waiting for cheaper later weeks and holding a film past its audience.

## Why It's Still Broken
Commitments are negotiated film by film, the owner rarely sees the calendar of upcoming commitments laid out against upcoming releases, and the agent negotiates for many theatres at once.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A twelve-week calendar per theatre showing every commitment against the release schedule, with forecast grosses for each option (take now with a three-week minimum, take in week two with a shorter run, split screens by daypart), so the owner and agent choose deliberately and ask for the terms that fit.

## Who Feels the Pain
Owners of small-town theatres; booking agents; audiences who wait weeks for a film the city got on opening day.

## Impact If Fixed
Deliberate sequencing of commitments lets a small house play more openers per year without breaking terms, using a calendar and a forecast it doesn't currently have.

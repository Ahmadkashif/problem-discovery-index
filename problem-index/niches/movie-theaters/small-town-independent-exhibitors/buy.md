# All-in-One Small Venue Software With Film Booking

**Niche:** [[niches/movie-theaters/small-town-independent-exhibitors/profile|Small-Town & Independent Exhibitors]]
**Industry:** [[industries/movie-theaters|Movie Theaters]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Small venues (bowling centres, escape rooms, live theatres) run on all-in-one booking, point-of-sale and reporting suites; a two-screen cinema stitches together ticketing, an agent's emails and a paper settlement.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #time-series-forecasting #automation #data-integration #workflow-orchestration #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get a one- to four-screen house first-run films on opening day without run-length and screen commitments it cannot meet, and whoever wins day-and-date access on survivable terms takes the account.

## The Problem
The owner is buyer, scheduler, accountant and manager. Booking requests, holdover decisions, terms and settlements happen in phone calls and emails outside the ticketing system, so the owner rebuilds each week from scratch.

## What Already Exists
All-in-one small venue suites combine booking, point of sale, inventory and reporting: ThunderTix for small live theatres, Square's retail and restaurant suites for counters, and Veezi on the cinema ticketing side.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs: a film booking workflow (request, confirmation, commitments) shared with the owner's booking agent; terms storage and settlement statements generated from grosses; distributor and Comscore reporting; run-length tracking with alerts before a commitment ends; and pricing low enough for a theatre grossing under $1M a year.

## Target Customer
Owners of one- to seven-screen theatres and the agents who book them.

## Impact If Solved
One system for the whole week saves the owner several hours of weekly admin (estimate) and gives the agent a shared record instead of an inbox.

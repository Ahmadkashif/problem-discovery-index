# Drive-In Theaters

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/movie-theaters|Movie Theaters]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to sell each weekend night's field in advance and staff the snack bar to it while the weather call is still open, and whoever lets a two-person owner sell, cancel and refund a night cleanly takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$100M in drive-in receipts (2022 Economic Census, 512132), of which ~$10M a year is spend on ticketing, projection service and tools (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~0.8% of receipts (Economic Census)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — many sell at the gate in cash, post showtimes on Facebook and count cars by hand
**Target Buyer:** Owner-operator, often a family
**Automation Potential:** Medium — online sales, weather-driven cancellation and snack bar prep automate; the field, the screen and the projector are physical

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
NAICS gives drive-ins their own code, 512132. UDITOA counted 283 in October 2024, down from 305 sites and 549 screens in 2019. County Business Patterns found 250 of 290 drive-in establishments with fewer than five employees in March 2023, before most opened. They book the same first-run films on the same sliding-scale terms as indoor theatres, usually as double features, and sell by the car or the head. The season is a few months of weekends in northern states, and each night is a weather bet. Sound goes out over a low-power FM transmitter to car radios. Digital conversion cost more than $70,000 a screen and closed many sites (AP).

## Current Tools & Gaps
Veezi and other cloud ticketing tools serve some drive-ins; many use a website plug-in, a card reader and cash. Gaps: no weather-aware selling and refund process, no forecast for snack bar prep by night, and no record of which films drew carloads in past seasons.

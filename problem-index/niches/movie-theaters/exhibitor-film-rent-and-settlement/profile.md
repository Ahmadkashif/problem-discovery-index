# Exhibitor Film Rent & Settlement

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/movie-theaters|Movie Theaters]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to hold an engagement-level record of the terms the theatre actually agreed, so that accruals are right every week and each distributor's invoice is checked before it is paid, and whoever catches the most overcharges and knows each sales office's settlement habits takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$100M a year in film accounting labour, outsourced bookkeeping and tools at exhibitors, checking ~$4.5–5B of film rent (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~0.8% of receipts in spend (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — grosses are digital; terms are in emails and settlements in PDFs and spreadsheets
**Target Buyer:** Film accountant or controller at a regional circuit; owner of an independent
**Automation Potential:** High for extraction, accrual and invoice matching; low for the settlement negotiation itself

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
This is the exhibitor's side of film rent; the distributor's side is [[niches/film-distributors/theatrical-booking-and-settlement/profile|Theatrical Booking & Settlement]] under film distribution. Exhibitors accrue rent on "estimates of the final settlement rate, which occurs at the conclusion of the film's run" (Cinemark), and their exposure is overpayment and misstated profit rather than collection. The winner is whoever keeps a terms record the theatre can audit, not whoever forecasts demand, which is why this splits from the allocation contest.

## Current Tools & Gaps
General ledgers (QuickBooks, Sage Intacct), ticketing film-hire reports, distributor PDFs and spreadsheets. Gaps: no structured terms; accruals that miss term changes; invoices paid without being re-derived; no settlement history by distributor.

# Restaurant Workforce Software Fed a Film Slate

**Niche:** [[niches/movie-theaters/crew-scheduling-to-attendance/profile|Crew Scheduling to Attendance]]
**Industry:** [[industries/movie-theaters|Movie Theaters]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Restaurant scheduling platforms already forecast covers, draft schedules and enforce labour law; they just don't know what's opening Friday.
**Tags:** #time-series-forecasting #gradient-boosting #compliance #data-integration #worker-facing #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to staff each daypart to the attendance actually coming (box office, counter and auditorium turns) within minor-labour hour limits, and whoever cuts labour hours without late starts or lobby lines takes the account.

## The Problem
Many theatres already pay for a restaurant-style workforce tool, which forecasts from sales history and misses the film-driven swings that dominate theatre demand.

## What Already Exists
7shifts, HotSchedules (Fourth), Deputy and When I Work forecast labour from point-of-sale sales, build schedules, handle shift swaps and flag overtime and minor-labour violations.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs: a feed of showtimes and presales from Vista, Veezi or RTS as the demand driver; auditorium let-outs and turn workload as a labour driver separate from sales; per-station staffing ratios for box office, counter, ushers and in-seat service; and state-by-state minor-hour rules for school nights during the school year versus summer release season.

## Target Customer
General managers and district managers already using a workforce app.

## Impact If Solved
Feeding the slate into an existing tool gets most of the forecasting gain without a new vendor, and staff keep the app they know.

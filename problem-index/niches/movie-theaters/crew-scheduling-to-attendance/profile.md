# Crew Scheduling to Attendance

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/movie-theaters|Movie Theaters]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to staff each daypart to the attendance actually coming (box office, counter and auditorium turns) within minor-labour hour limits, and whoever cuts labour hours without late starts or lobby lines takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$2.3B annual payroll governed by the schedule (County Business Patterns 2023), with ~$30M a year spent on scheduling tools (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~17% of receipts in payroll (Census-derived); tools ~0.2% (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — schedules are built in a workforce app or spreadsheet from the manager's sense of the week
**Target Buyer:** District manager; general manager; VP of theatre operations
**Automation Potential:** High — forecasting attendance by hour and drafting a compliant schedule are mechanical

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Labour is the second cost a theatre controls after film rent, and it is mostly part-time and young: AMC reports 30,380 part-time employees out of 33,311, and ushers and attendants earn a median $13.63 an hour (BLS). Attendance swings by a factor of ten between a Tuesday matinee and an opening Saturday and is known in advance through presales. Many crew members are minors whose hours are limited by federal and state child-labour rules on school nights. The schedule determines the auditorium turns described in the [[problems/movie-theaters/worker-life-1|usher's fifteen-minute turn]].

## Current Tools & Gaps
Restaurant and retail workforce tools (7shifts, HotSchedules, When I Work, Deputy) and ticketing labour reports. Gaps: no forecast built from presales and the film slate, no model of turn workload by auditorium, and minor-hour rules checked by memory.

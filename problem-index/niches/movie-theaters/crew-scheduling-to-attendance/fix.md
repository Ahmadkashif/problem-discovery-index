# Minor-Labour Hours Checked by Memory

**Niche:** [[niches/movie-theaters/crew-scheduling-to-attendance/profile|Crew Scheduling to Attendance]]
**Industry:** [[industries/movie-theaters|Movie Theaters]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Late shows end after the hour a fifteen-year-old may legally work on a school night, and the manager finds out when the closing crew is short.
**Tags:** #compliance #workflow-orchestration #worker-facing #quick-win #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to staff each daypart to the attendance actually coming (box office, counter and auditorium turns) within minor-labour hour limits, and whoever cuts labour hours without late starts or lobby lines takes the account.

## The Problem
Federal rules limit 14- and 15-year-olds to set hours and end times on school days, and states add their own limits for 16- and 17-year-olds. Theatres close late and rely on teenage crews, so the closing shift is where violations and shortfalls happen.

## Why It's Still Broken
Managers schedule by availability and remember the rules; workforce tools check hours, but rarely know the theatre's actual let-out times, which move when showtimes change mid-week.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Tie each minor's scheduled end time to the actual last let-out of the auditoriums they'll clean, flag conflicts when showtimes change, and suggest an adult swap before the shift.

## Who Feels the Pain
Teen crew members kept past legal hours or sent home early; managers left short at close; operators exposed to wage-and-hour penalties.

## Impact If Fixed
Fewer violations and fewer short-staffed closes, using data the schedule and the showtime grid already contain.

# Showtime & Screen Allocation

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/movie-theaters|Movie Theaters]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to forecast each film's next-week demand by theatre and daypart well enough to set screens and showtimes before the mid-week publishing deadline, and whoever gets the holdover calls right most often takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$120M a year in buyer and scheduler labour, booking agency fees and scheduling tools (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~1% of receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — schedules are built in Vista or Veezi from grosses exported to spreadsheets
**Target Buyer:** Head film buyer; director of programming at a regional circuit
**Automation Potential:** High for forecasting and grid-building; the final call stays with the buyer

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
This is the weekly forecasting and allocation contest: which films hold, on how many screens, in which auditoriums, at which times. Multiplexes wait for Friday and Saturday grosses before fixing most of the next week (Marketplace), so the work compresses into Sunday night to Tuesday. The winner is whoever reads each market's demand best, which is a tacit skill experienced buyers carry (the [[problems/movie-theaters/high-impact|high-impact problem]]). It isn't a negotiation contest: terms are handled in the sibling sub-niche.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Vista's holdover listings and scheduling, Veezi for independents, theatre management systems that push the grid to projectors, Comscore market grosses. Gaps: no local demand forecast by daypart, no record of the buyer's expectation, and no check of the grid against crew turn capacity.

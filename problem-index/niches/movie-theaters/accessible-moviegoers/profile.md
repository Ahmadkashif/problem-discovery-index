# Accessible Moviegoers

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/movie-theaters|Movie Theaters]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to make the captioning or description device a deaf, hard-of-hearing or blind patron picks up work for the whole show, every show, and whoever can prove device readiness show by show takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$40M a year in captioning and audio description devices, replacement units, maintenance and staff time (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~0.3% of receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — devices are digital, but readiness is checked by hand, if at all
**Target Buyer:** Director of operations or ADA compliance lead at a circuit; general manager at an independent
**Automation Potential:** Medium — readiness logging and failure prediction automate; handing over and fitting a device is in person

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The DOJ's 2016 ADA movie rule (28 CFR 36.303(g)) requires closed captioning and audio description in every digital auditorium, with set minimums of fully operational devices: 4 captioning devices for one auditorium up to 12 for sixteen or more, and one description device per two auditoriums. The patron's experience depends on a small device (a cupholder caption display, glasses or a headset) being charged, paired to the right auditorium and handed over by trained staff. In 2020 AMC settled with the US Attorney in Los Angeles after a hard-of-hearing patron at the Tyler Galleria 16 reported devices that kept failing; five of the theatre's 12 caption devices had trouble holding a charge. Sensory-friendly screenings for autistic patrons are a related, programming-side need.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Device makers' captioning and description systems (Sony Entertainment Access glasses, CaptiView-style cupholder displays, Fidelio and similar receivers) and the theatre's check-out sheet. Gaps: no record of device charge and failure history, no tie between check-outs and the show being watched, and no way for patrons to confirm a working device before buying.

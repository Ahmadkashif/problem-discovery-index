# The Caption Device That Dies Mid-Film

**Niche:** [[niches/movie-theaters/accessible-moviegoers/profile|Accessible Moviegoers]]
**Industry:** [[industries/movie-theaters|Movie Theaters]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A deaf patron walks out halfway through a film to swap a dead device, and the replacement fails too.
**Tags:** #survival-analysis #descriptive-statistics #compliance #worker-facing #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to make the captioning or description device a deaf, hard-of-hearing or blind patron picks up work for the whole show, every show, and whoever can prove device readiness show by show takes the account.

## The Problem
The complaint behind AMC's 2020 settlement is a common one: devices that don't hold a charge, staff who don't know how to pair them, and replacements no better than the first. The patron loses the film and often stops coming.

## Why It's Still Broken
Charging is a closing-shift chore with no check, failed units go back in the drawer, and turnover means few crew members are trained. Nothing records which unit failed.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Number every device, log each failure at return with a scan, retire any unit with two failures, and run a pre-shift test of each unit planned for use that day. The AMC agreement's requirement of a named staff member per shift fits on the same sheet.

## Who Feels the Pain
Deaf, hard-of-hearing and blind moviegoers; crew handling the complaint; operators facing DOJ settlements.

## Impact If Fixed
A numbered, logged and tested device pool removes most mid-film failures for the cost of a scanner and a habit.

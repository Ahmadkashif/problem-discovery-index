# Hospital Asset Tracking for Assistive Devices

**Niche:** [[niches/movie-theaters/accessible-moviegoers/profile|Accessible Moviegoers]]
**Industry:** [[industries/movie-theaters|Movie Theaters]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Hospitals track infusion pumps and wheelchairs by tag, charge and maintenance status; theatres track caption devices on a clipboard.
**Tags:** #survival-analysis #descriptive-statistics #compliance #automation #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to make the captioning or description device a deaf, hard-of-hearing or blind patron picks up work for the whole show, every show, and whoever can prove device readiness show by show takes the account.

## The Problem
A theatre with 16 auditoriums must keep at least 12 captioning and 8 description devices working, and knows little about any one of them.

## What Already Exists
Healthcare real-time location and asset-management systems (for example Securitas Healthcare, formerly STANLEY Healthcare, and simpler barcode asset trackers) record where each device is, its maintenance history and readiness.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs: per-show check-out tied to the auditorium and showtime from the ticketing system; device pairing to the auditorium's caption server; charge-cycle tracking for consumer-grade batteries; the ADA minimum counts per theatre as a live compliance gauge; patron feedback on return; and a price suited to a few dozen devices per site.

## Target Customer
Operations leads at circuits; integrators who install accessibility systems.

## Impact If Solved
Off-the-shelf asset tracking, taught the ADA minimums and showtimes, gives each theatre a live count of working devices.

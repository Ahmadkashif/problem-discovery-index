# Film Programming & Booking

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/movie-theaters|Movie Theaters]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is that every serious competitor is fighting to turn each week's screen time into the most theatre margin, but that produces two contests with different winners: the weekly allocation of screens and showtimes, won by whoever forecasts each theatre's demand best, and the terms and settlement negotiated with each distributor, won by whoever keeps the best record of what was agreed and how each sales office settles. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$250M a year in film buying and booking labour, booking agency fees and programming tools, steering ~$4.5–5B of film rent (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~2% of receipts in spend; film rent itself is ~35–40% of receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — grosses and schedules are digital, while decisions and terms live in phone calls, emails and the buyer's spreadsheet
**Target Buyer:** VP of film / head film buyer at a circuit; owner of an independent booking agency
**Automation Potential:** Medium — forecasting and settlement arithmetic automate; the negotiation with each distributor stays human

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Programming is the exhibitor's core decision and its largest cost. Films are licensed "on a film-by-film and theatre-by-theatre basis" (AMC), and most rent is a sliding scale set before the run (Cinemark), so every week's screen and showtime choices change both revenue and the share of it the theatre keeps. Since the Paramount consent decrees ended in August 2020, with a two-year sunset on the block-booking and circuit-dealing bans, the rulebook that shaped the practice for seventy years is gone, but the practice hasn't changed.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Vista and Veezi hold schedules and grosses, with holdover and film-hire reports. Comscore supplies market grosses. Buyers keep terms and commitments in spreadsheets and email. No tool joins the allocation decision, the agreed terms and the settled rent into one record, which is why this niche splits in two.

# Ticket Pricing & Subscriptions

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/movie-theaters|Movie Theaters]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to price each showtime, seat type and subscription so that discount nights and passes fill empty seats without cannibalising full-price weekend sales, and whoever measures that cannibalisation honestly takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$8.9B of annual box office priced through these decisions (AMC FY2025 10-K), with ~$50M a year spent on pricing, loyalty and subscription tooling and analysts (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~50–55% of receipts are admissions; tooling ~0.4% (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** High — prices, passes and loyalty run in ticketing and apps; the measurement of their effect is weak
**Target Buyer:** VP of pricing or revenue management; head of loyalty at a circuit
**Automation Potential:** High — price setting by show and seat, offer targeting and subscription analytics are mechanical

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Ticket prices now vary by day, daypart, format and seat, and subscriptions sell attendance in advance. AMC cut Tuesday prices and raised weekends, moving Tuesday from least-visited to second-most-visited day, and tested seat-location pricing (Sightline) in 2023. Cinemark's Movie Club has ~1.5M paid members driving ~30% of domestic box office, and AMC Stubs members account for ~51% of AMC's US attendance. About 73% of AMC's US tickets are bought online. Film rent is a percentage of the price, so every discount is shared with the distributor, and some distributors set floors on discounting. The open question every circuit faces is how much of a discount night's crowd would have paid full price on Saturday.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Vista and other ticketing engines for price tables; Movio and in-house loyalty platforms; circuit apps. Gaps: price changes and subscription perks evaluated without controls, no per-member incremental-visit measure, and distributor floor rules held in buyers' notes.

# Airline Revenue Management Scaled Down to a Showtime

**Niche:** [[niches/movie-theaters/ticket-pricing-and-subscriptions/profile|Ticket Pricing & Subscriptions]]
**Industry:** [[industries/movie-theaters|Movie Theaters]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Airlines and hotels price perishable inventory by forecast demand; a theatre seat is just as perishable and is priced from a fixed table.
**Tags:** #time-series-forecasting #bayesian-inference #optimization-fundamentals #evaluation-metrics #revenue-impact #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to price each showtime, seat type and subscription so that discount nights and passes fill empty seats without cannibalising full-price weekend sales, and whoever measures that cannibalisation honestly takes the account.

## The Problem
Most circuits price by a table of day, daypart and format, adjusted a few times a year. Seats in a Wednesday 9:30 p.m. show sell the same as seats in a sold-out Saturday premiere of the same film.

## What Already Exists
Revenue management systems for hotels and airlines (IDeaS, Duetto, PROS) forecast demand by date and product and set prices and restrictions to maximise revenue.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs: thousands of short-lived showtimes a week instead of nights or flights; film rent as a percentage of price, so revenue to the theatre is net of a sliding share; distributor floors on discounting; subscription members who pay nothing at the point of sale; concession spend as a co-product of attendance; and price stability, because moviegoers punished AMC's seat-pricing test with backlash (recalled).

## Target Customer
Pricing teams at circuits of 50+ theatres.

## Impact If Solved
Modest, forecast-driven price movement on the emptiest and fullest shows lifts revenue per seat without the visible surge pricing that patrons reject.

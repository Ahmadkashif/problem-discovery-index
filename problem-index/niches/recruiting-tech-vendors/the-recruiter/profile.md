# The Recruiter

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/recruiting-tech-vendors|Recruiting Tech Vendors]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Whether the person running the process is measured on anything connected to whether the hires were good.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$840M — 7% of US recruiting technology spend
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~7%
**Digital Adoption:** Low — a requisition list and a time-to-fill
**Target Buyer:** Recruiters; recruiting leadership
**Automation Potential:** High — the load and the outcome measurement are both derivable

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche

Hundreds of applications per role, hiring managers who will not give feedback, and a metric that measures how fast the requisition closed rather than whether the hire was right.

The recruiter sits between an unmanageable volume on one side and an unresponsive manager on the other, measured on speed, with no information about whether any of their hires worked out. They screen faster than they believe is right, chase managers who do not reply, coordinate panels that collapse, and are held accountable for a time-to-fill number that a manager's slow feedback determines more than they do.

The niche is distinct because the role has all the accountability and none of the instruments — a recruiter cannot see their own hire quality, cannot demonstrate that a requisition is failing for reasons outside their control, and cannot carry a record of their work anywhere.

## Current Tools & Gaps

Requisition dashboards with time to fill, pipeline counts and stage conversion. Activity metrics — outreach sent, screens completed. Offer acceptance rate. Source effectiveness reporting of variable quality. Workload is managed by requisition count, which treats a straightforward role and an impossible one identically.

The gaps are outcome, attribution and load. No recruiter knows how their hires performed, so the feedback loop that would make them better does not exist. Delay caused by manager unresponsiveness is invisible, so it lands in the recruiter's number. And requisition difficulty is unmodelled, so load is allocated by count.

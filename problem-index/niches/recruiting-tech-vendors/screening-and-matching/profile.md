# Screening & Matching

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/recruiting-tech-vendors|Recruiting Tech Vendors]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Whether the screen selects the people who would do the job, or the people recruiters have historically selected.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$3.24B — 27% of US applicant tracking, sourcing and recruiting automation
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~27%
**Digital Adoption:** High — ranking and filtering everywhere, validation nowhere
**Target Buyer:** Employers' talent acquisition leadership; ATS and matching vendors
**Automation Potential:** High, and the automation is the problem

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche

A rejected candidate produces no outcome data, so any model trained on this record learns which people recruiters chose rather than which people would have done the job.

That is the structural condition of the entire category. Every applicant tracking system records applications, screens, rejections and hires across millions of decisions, and grades none of them. The only observable label is what the recruiter did, so a matching model fitted to it reproduces recruiter behaviour — which is the well-documented failure the field already has a canonical example of in Amazon's abandoned resume-screening tool.

The niche is distinct because the missing quantity is a counterfactual rather than a measurement. It is not that nobody has computed the answer; it is that the data required to compute it is not generated by the process, and generating it requires either re-examining decisions or intervening in them.

## Current Tools & Gaps

Keyword and boolean filtering. Knockout questions. Resume parsing and structured field extraction. Learned ranking and match scores, trained on recruiter and hiring manager actions. Semantic search over resumes. Some vendors report fairness metrics; several have retreated from strong matching claims under scrutiny.

The gaps are validation and honesty about it. No vendor in this category can demonstrate that their ranking predicts job performance, because the evidence does not exist. Recruiter agreement is used as the proxy and reported as fit. And the false rejection rate — how often a screen discards someone who would have succeeded — is unmeasured everywhere.

# The Candidate in the Pipeline

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/recruiting-tech-vendors|Recruiting Tech Vendors]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Whether a person who applied is told anything at all.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.56B — 13% of US recruiting technology spend
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~13%
**Digital Adoption:** Very low — silence is the default behaviour of every system
**Target Buyer:** Candidates; employers competing on experience; regulators
**Automation Potential:** Very high — every status transition is already recorded

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche

Applications go into a system that never responds, status is unknowable, and the rejection either arrives months later or not at all.

This is the most universal and best-documented complaint about hiring, it is entirely a workflow choice, and it degrades the applicant pool for the employers who do it worst. Every status transition in the process is recorded in the applicant tracking system. Communicating them is a configuration.

The niche is distinct because the failure is purely one of not sending, in a system built to send. It is also self-reinforcing: silence trains candidates to apply more widely and less carefully, which raises volume, which makes recruiters screen faster and communicate less.

## Current Tools & Gaps

Every ATS supports automated candidate communication — acknowledgement, status change notifications, rejection templates — and the features are configured off, or configured on for the first stage only. Candidate portals with status views, deployed inconsistently. Some employers have made candidate experience a genuine priority and show what is possible.

The gaps are the defaults and the closure. Rejection notification at the end of a requisition is commonly not configured, so candidates who were not advanced are left indefinitely. Status is either not exposed or exposed as a meaningless internal stage name. And nobody measures the silence — how many candidates were never told anything — because it is an absence.

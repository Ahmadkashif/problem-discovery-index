# Build: Randomised Marginal Admission as a Standing Programme

**Niche:** [[niches/recruiting-tech-vendors/counterfactual-evidence/profile|Counterfactual Evidence]]
**Industry:** [[industries/recruiting-tech-vendors|Recruiting Tech Vendors]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Advance a random sample of candidates from just below the screening threshold, follow their outcomes, and produce the only genuine evidence about whether the screen works.
**Tags:** #causal-inference #hypothesis-testing #confidence-intervals #survival-analysis #evaluation-metrics #bayesian-inference #compliance #monte-carlo-methods
**Contested on:** Whether an employer will accept the cost and the ethical complexity of randomising at the margin.

## The Problem

Nobody knows whether screening works, and the reason is not that the analysis is hard. It is that the data does not exist and cannot be produced by observation, because the process never observes what a rejected candidate would have done.

Selective randomisation at the margin of a screening threshold would produce genuine evidence at a cost most employers could absorb. Among candidates who narrowly failed the screen, advance a small random share. Follow them through the process and, for those hired, into performance, tenure and progression. Compare to the marginally-passing group. The difference is the screen's value at the margin, which is the only place its value is in question.

This is a standard research design applied to a question the field has never asked of itself.

## Why Nobody Has Built This

It requires deliberately advancing candidates the process rejected, which no recruiting leader has the authority to approve and which sounds, described plainly, like lowering the bar.

The timeline defeats it too. A usable sample takes years — enough marginal candidates advanced, hired, and observed long enough for performance to be meaningful — and no recruiting leader's tenure or budget cycle accommodates that.

The ethics and the law are genuinely unsettled. Randomising who progresses in a hiring process is an intervention in people's employment prospects, and although it advantages the randomised group rather than disadvantaging them, the framework for doing it responsibly — consent, disclosure, governance, the treatment of those not selected — has not been written for this setting.

And the possible finding is that the screen adds nothing, which would be a large and expensive conclusion.

## What to Build

A standing programme, designed to be small, defensible and cumulative.

**Randomise only at the margin and only upward.** Candidates just below the threshold, a small proportion advanced. Nobody who would have passed is rejected; the intervention only advantages the randomised. That asymmetry is what makes it ethically defensible and it should be the design's central feature.

**Keep the proportion small and the cost bounded.** A few percent of marginal candidates in high-volume roles produces a usable sample over a few years at a marginal cost that is genuinely small relative to a recruiting budget — and quantifying that cost upfront is what gets the programme approved.

**Follow the whole path, not just hires.** Advanced marginal candidates who are then rejected at interview are informative about the screen too — if they consistently fail the next stage, the screen was right. Performance outcomes for those hired are the strongest evidence and the slowest.

**Use the full outcome set.** Performance ratings, tenure, progression, involuntary termination, and objective output where it exists. Ratings alone are noisy and biased; the pattern across measures is the finding.

**Analyse as a regression discontinuity as well.** The threshold itself creates a natural discontinuity that can be analysed without randomisation, on historical data, as a first result while the randomised programme accumulates. Weaker, available now, and a useful early indication.

**Write the governance first.** Disclosure to candidates, treatment of the non-randomised, review by an appropriate body, and the legal position — established before the first candidate is randomised, because doing it afterwards is how a defensible programme becomes an incident.

**Pool across employers.** A single employer's marginal sample is small. A consortium of employers running the same design, analysed by a neutral party, would settle the question for a whole role family in a few years, and it is the structure the field most obviously needs.

## Target Customer

Very large employers with research capability, a long-horizon people function and roles hired at high volume — a small set, and the only set where this is feasible. Also academic labour economists, for whom this is a significant research opportunity, and regulators, for whom employer-generated validity evidence is the thing their disclosure regimes cannot compel.

## Impact If Built

The field acquires, for the first time, genuine evidence about whether screening adds value at the margin. The threshold gets set from data rather than from convention. And the candidates in the randomised group — people the process would have discarded — get a chance that is, in expectation, the fairest thing in this industry.

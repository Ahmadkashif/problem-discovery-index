# Fix: The Job Description Is Not What They Want

**Niche:** [[niches/recruiting-tech-vendors/hiring-manager-calibration/profile|Hiring Manager Feedback & Calibration]]
**Industry:** [[industries/recruiting-tech-vendors|Recruiting Tech Vendors]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The requirements were copied from the last requisition, the manager has different criteria in their head, and the recruiter is screening against the wrong document.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #large-language-models #workflow-orchestration #confidence-intervals #quick-win #worker-facing #tacit-knowledge-ml
**Contested on:** Whether thirty minutes at requisition open will be spent establishing what the manager actually wants.

## The Problem

A job description is written by copying the last one for a similar role, adding a few lines, and pushing it live to open the requisition. It lists requirements nobody selected deliberately — a degree that is not needed, years of experience chosen by convention, a technology list that includes everything the team has touched.

The manager's real criteria are different and are in their head. They want someone who has done this specific thing at this specific scale, and they will recognise it when they see it.

So the recruiter screens against the document and sends candidates the manager rejects, for weeks, while the manager becomes frustrated and the recruiter becomes defensive, and the requisition ages. The divergence is the single most common cause of a failing requisition and is diagnosed as a pipeline problem.

## Why It's Still Broken

The intake meeting exists and is not an elicitation. It is a thirty-minute conversation covering the role, the team, the timeline and the process, in which the criteria are discussed in general terms that both parties believe they agree on.

Writing the job description is also treated as an administrative step to open the requisition rather than as the specification everything downstream depends on, so it gets the attention an administrative step gets.

And the divergence surfaces slowly — several rounds of rejections in — by which point both parties have an interpretation of the problem that is about the other person.

## What a Fix Looks Like

Make the intake an elicitation, and check it against reality early.

Use examples, not attributes. Ask the manager to look at four or five real profiles — from past applicants or a quick search — and say which they would interview and why. Twenty minutes, and it produces more usable criteria than any amount of discussion about the ideal candidate, because people are far better at judging examples than at specifying requirements.

Ask the three questions that matter. What would make you say yes immediately. What would make you say no immediately. What does the person need to have actually done. These are answerable and they are not what the intake template asks.

Separate must-have from nice-to-have explicitly, and ask the manager to defend each must-have. Most requirement lists lose half their must-haves under one round of "would you reject someone strong who lacked this?"

Rewrite the description from the elicitation rather than from the last one. If the elicited criteria and the copied document disagree, the document is wrong.

Check at candidate five, not at candidate fifty. A short calibration after the first handful of decisions, comparing what the manager did to what they said, catches the divergence while the requisition is young. This is the highest-value thirty minutes in the whole process and almost nobody schedules it.

And record the criteria as structured requirements rather than prose, so the screen, the audit and the next requisition for the same role all have something to work from.

## Who Feels the Pain

Recruiters, screening against the wrong specification for weeks and blamed for the pipeline. Hiring managers, reviewing candidates who do not match criteria they never articulated, and concluding that recruiting does not understand the role. Candidates, screened in or out against requirements nobody is applying. And the organisation, with requisitions ageing on a communication failure.

## Impact If Fixed

The criteria get elicited using examples rather than discussed in the abstract, which is how people actually reveal what they want. Half the must-haves fall away under a single question. And the divergence gets caught at candidate five instead of candidate fifty, which is the difference between a requisition that fills and one that ages.

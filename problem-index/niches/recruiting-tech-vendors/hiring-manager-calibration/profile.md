# Hiring Manager Feedback & Calibration

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/recruiting-tech-vendors|Recruiting Tech Vendors]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Whether the recruiter ever finds out what the hiring manager actually wants.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.32B — 11% of US recruiting technology spend
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~11%
**Digital Adoption:** Very low — a rejection reason field nobody fills in
**Target Buyer:** Recruiting leadership; talent operations
**Automation Potential:** High — the signal is in the decisions rather than in the feedback

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche

A recruiter screens against a job description and sends candidates. The hiring manager rejects them, with a thumbs down and no reason, or with a reason so general it conveys nothing. The recruiter adjusts by guessing, sends more, and the cycle repeats.

This is the most-cited source of recruiter frustration and it is a communication failure with a specific shape: the manager's actual criteria are tacit, the job description does not capture them, and the only channel for transmitting them — feedback on individual candidates — is the one part of the process managers consistently skip.

The niche is distinct because the criteria exist and are recoverable. A manager who rejects six candidates and advances one has revealed a great deal about what they want, and nothing extracts it.

## Current Tools & Gaps

Rejection reason dropdowns in every ATS, with a handful of generic options, completed inconsistently. Intake meetings at requisition open, which are a conversation rather than a structured elicitation. Interview scorecards where they are used. Calibration sessions, common for interview panels and rare for screening criteria.

The gaps are elicitation and inference. Nothing helps a manager articulate criteria they hold tacitly. Nothing infers criteria from the pattern of their decisions, though the pattern is the most reliable signal available. And nothing detects the common case where the manager's real criteria and the published job description have diverged completely.

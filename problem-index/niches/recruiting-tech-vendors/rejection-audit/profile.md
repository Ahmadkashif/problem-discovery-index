# Rejection Audit

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/recruiting-tech-vendors|Recruiting Tech Vendors]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** How often a rejection was wrong by the employer's own stated standard.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$1.94B — 60% of the screening and matching niche
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~16% of US recruiting technology spend
**Digital Adoption:** Essentially none — the sample is never re-reviewed
**Target Buyer:** Employers' talent acquisition leadership; ATS vendors
**Automation Potential:** High — the sampling, routing and analysis all automate

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche

This is the half that can be done now. Take a sample of rejected applications, strip the identifying details, and have them re-reviewed blind by different reviewers against the same stated requirements. Compare the verdicts. Where the re-review advances a candidate the original screen rejected, the rejection was wrong by the employer's own standard.

It does not answer whether the candidate would have performed, which is the counterfactual and unavailable. It answers something narrower and immediately useful: whether the screening process applies the employer's own criteria consistently. And inconsistency is large — the same application reviewed by two recruiters on two days frequently produces two outcomes.

Every party benefits. The employer is losing candidates it wanted. The recruiter is being asked to screen at a speed the evidence does not support and would like that established. The vendor gets a genuine quality signal on a product whose quality is currently unmeasurable. And it requires no permission, no experiment and no waiting.

## Current Tools & Gaps

Inter-rater reliability methodology from every adjacent field. Structured scorecards in some ATS products. Calibration sessions for interviewers, which exist and are rarely extended to screening. Rejected applicant records retained for compliance periods, sitting unexamined.

The gaps are that nobody does it. No ATS ships a rejection audit capability, no employer runs one as standard, and the false rejection rate — the most basic quality measure of a screening process — is unknown everywhere.

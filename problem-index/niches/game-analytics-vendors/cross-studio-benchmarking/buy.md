# Benchmarking Consortia From Industry Data

**Niche:** [[niches/game-analytics-vendors/cross-studio-benchmarking/profile|Cross-Studio Benchmarking]]
**Industry:** [[industries/game-analytics-vendors|Game Analytics Vendors]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Many industries run benchmarking consortia where competitors contribute data and receive comparisons, and games analytics keeps the corpus to itself.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #compliance #confidence-intervals #evaluation-metrics #data-integration #k-means-clustering #hypothesis-testing #time-series-forecasting
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to tell a studio whether its numbers are good, which requires seeing comparable games — and whoever turns that corpus into a product takes the account.

## The Problem
Benchmarking consortia are an established institutional form. Banks contribute loss data and receive operational risk benchmarks; retailers contribute sales data and receive category comparisons; healthcare providers contribute outcomes and receive quality comparisons. Trusted intermediaries hold the data, aggregation rules protect contributors, and competition law compliance is designed in. The model works across industries where participants are direct competitors. Games analytics has the corpus already and no such product.

## What Already Exists
Trusted intermediary data consortia; contribution-for-access models; aggregation and suppression rules protecting contributors; competition law compliant designs; and peer group definition methodologies.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a vendor who already holds the data rather than an intermediary who must collect it. It requires: (1) a corpus already in the vendor's possession under contracts that did not contemplate this use, so the consent and contractual work is retrospective — this is the substantive difference and is the actual blocker; (2) peer groups defined by genre, mechanics and monetisation rather than by standard industry classification; (3) metrics with no agreed definitions across the industry, unlike financial or clinical measures; (4) a vendor with a commercial interest in the benchmark rather than a neutral intermediary; and (5) title populations that turn over fast, so peer groups must be recomputed continuously.

## Target Customer
Game analytics vendors, studios and publishers, industry bodies, and benchmarking and consortium data providers.

## Impact If Solved
Benchmarking consortia work across industries where participants are direct competitors, with designed aggregation and compliance rules. A corpus already held under contracts that never contemplated this use is what makes the consent work retrospective and hard.

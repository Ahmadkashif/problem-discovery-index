# Is It Us or the Market

**Niche:** [[niches/game-analytics-vendors/cross-studio-benchmarking/profile|Cross-Studio Benchmarking]]
**Industry:** [[industries/game-analytics-vendors|Game Analytics Vendors]]
**Type:** Build (Greenfield Opportunity)
**One-liner:** The vendor can see whether the whole genre moved that week and sells the studio a chart of its own decline.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #confidence-intervals #time-series-forecasting #evaluation-metrics #causal-inference #compliance #k-means-clustering #hypothesis-testing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to tell a studio whether its numbers are good, which requires seeing comparable games — and whoever turns that corpus into a product takes the account.

## The Problem
The single most valuable question a studio has is whether a movement is theirs or the market's. It is unanswerable from inside one game and trivially answerable from the vendor's position, which spans thousands of titles across genres, platforms and seasons. The vendor uses that corpus to power each customer's own dashboard and shows none of it back. Studios consequently spend weeks investigating a decline that affected every comparable game that month.

## Why Nobody Has Built This
Using customer data to serve other customers is contractually delicate and commercially sensitive, and nobody has designed the arrangement that makes it acceptable. Defining a comparable set is a real modelling problem. Customers may object without understanding what is shared. And the vendor's current product sells without it.

## What to Build
Design the sharing arrangement first, then the comparisons. Establish an opt-in contribution model where a studio receives benchmarks in exchange for contributing aggregates, which is the core — the contractual design is the hard part and the analytics are comparatively easy. Define comparable sets by genre, monetisation model, platform and audience rather than by crude category, since a benchmark against the wrong comparables is worse than none. Publish distributions rather than averages, as a studio needs to know where it sits and not what the mean is. Provide seasonal and calendar baselines, which explain a large share of movements outright. Signal market-wide movements as they happen, which is the alerting product and is what studios would pay most for. Aggregate to a level that cannot identify any contributor, and be able to demonstrate that, because trust is the entire foundation. Show sample size and comparable count so a benchmark's weight is visible. Cover the metrics studios actually argue about rather than everything available. Offer benchmarks on the funnel stages as well as on headline retention. And set contribution as the default with a clear opt-out, since an opt-in that nobody finds produces no corpus.

## Target Customer
Game analytics vendors, studios and publishers, platform holders, and industry data and benchmarking providers.

## Impact If Built
The most valuable question a studio has is unanswerable from inside one game and trivial from the vendor's seat, and the vendor sells them their own chart instead. A contribution-for-benchmarks arrangement is the product the corpus has always supported.

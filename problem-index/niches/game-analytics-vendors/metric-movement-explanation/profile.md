# Metric Movement Explanation

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/game-analytics-vendors|Game Analytics Vendors]]
**Category:** 🔵 High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to say why a metric moved, when the dashboard can only say that it did — and whoever answers it takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$340M US
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~29% of category revenue
**Digital Adoption:** Very low — analyst guesses
**Target Buyer:** Product and data leadership
**Automation Potential:** High — attribution plus comparison

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A retention number moves and the studio needs to know whether it was the new build, a content update, an acquisition source mix change, a seasonal pattern, a competitor's launch or a genre-wide shift. The dashboard can segment the movement every way the schema permits and cannot separate those causes. The answer is produced by an analyst forming a hypothesis and checking whether the data contradicts it — which is skilled work, slow, and not what the customer thinks they are buying.

### Contested sub-niches
This niche is not terminal. It names a question whose two halves need different data:
- [[niches/game-analytics-vendors/internal-cause-attribution/profile|🎯 Internal Cause Attribution]] — which of our own actions moved it
- [[niches/game-analytics-vendors/cross-studio-benchmarking/profile|🎯 Cross-Studio Benchmarking]] — was it us or the market

## Current Tools & Gaps
Funnels, cohort retention charts, segment breakdowns and an analyst. The gaps: no join between metrics and build, config and content history; no external or market context; no causal method; no ranked candidate explanations; and no record of past explanations against what turned out to be true.

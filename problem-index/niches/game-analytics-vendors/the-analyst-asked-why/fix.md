# Answering the Same Question Every Month

**Niche:** [[niches/game-analytics-vendors/the-analyst-asked-why/profile|The Analyst Asked Why by Thursday]]
**Industry:** [[industries/game-analytics-vendors|Game Analytics Vendors]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The analyst has investigated this exact question four times and starts from an empty query window each time.
**Tags:** #worker-facing #quick-win #workflow-orchestration #automation #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #data-integration #confidence-intervals
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to let the person asked why a metric moved answer it properly within the deadline they were given — and whoever equips them takes the account.

## The Problem
A large share of the weekly questions are repeats. Why did retention dip after the update. Why is this platform underperforming. Why did revenue per player move. The analyst has answered each before, sometimes several times, and starts again from a blank query each time because the previous work lives in a personal notebook, a message thread or nowhere at all.

## Why It's Still Broken
Nothing captures the work — an investigation that ends in a slide and a deleted query leaves nothing for the next person or the next month, however many times the same question is asked. Notebooks are personal. Nobody catalogues recurring questions. And rewriting the query feels faster than finding the old one.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Keep the analyses and name the recurring questions. Maintain a shared library of saved analyses for recurring questions, which is the fix and is a repository rather than a product. Catalogue the questions that recur, since naming them is what makes the pattern visible. Turn the most frequent into scheduled reports so the question is answered before it is asked, which removes them from the queue entirely. Record the conclusion alongside the analysis so context survives. Keep the queries runnable rather than as screenshots in a deck. Share across the team rather than per analyst, as the duplication is usually between people. Note which past conclusions turned out to be right, which is how the library becomes trustworthy. Update saved analyses when the schema changes, otherwise the library rots and stops being used. Track how much analyst time goes on repeats, which is the case for investing in the instrumentation that would end them. And treat a question asked three times as a reporting requirement rather than an investigation.

## Who Feels the Pain
Analysts redoing work they have done before; teams waiting for answers that existed already; studios paying skilled people to repeat themselves; and the questions that never get asked because the analyst is busy.

## Impact If Fixed
An investigation that ends in a slide and a deleted query leaves nothing for the next month, however many times the question is asked. A shared analysis library plus scheduled reports removes the repeats from the queue.

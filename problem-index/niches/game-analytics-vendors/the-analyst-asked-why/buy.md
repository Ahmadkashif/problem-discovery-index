**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to let the person asked why a metric moved answer it properly within the deadline they were given — and whoever equips them takes the account.

## The Problem
Incident response turned unstructured investigation into a discipline. Runbooks provide standard first moves, timelines are assembled automatically, hypotheses and their disconfirmation are recorded as the investigation proceeds, post-incident reviews capture what was learned, and a searchable history means a recurring incident starts from the last one. Analysts investigating a metric movement do structurally identical work in an ad hoc notebook with no memory.

## What Already Exists
Runbooks for recurring investigation types; automatic timeline assembly; hypothesis and evidence capture during investigation; post-incident review practice; and searchable incident history.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation is to a slow-moving behavioural question rather than a live systems failure. It requires: (1) investigations measured in days with effects that unfold over weeks, so the urgency structure and the evidence standard are entirely different — this is the substantive difference; (2) causes that are frequently external and unobservable rather than internal and logged; (3) a conclusion that is a probabilistic attribution rather than a fixed root cause; (4) no incident trigger, since the question arrives as a request in a meeting; and (5) an outcome that informs a business decision rather than a remediation.

## Target Customer
Studio analytics teams, game analytics vendors, publishers, and investigation workflow tooling providers.

## Impact If Solved
Incident response turned unstructured investigation into a discipline with runbooks and searchable history. Effects that unfold over weeks, with causes that are often external and unobservable, is what makes the analytics version a different evidence standard.

# Ten Minutes Reconciling Before the Meeting Starts

**Niche:** [[niches/game-analytics-vendors/data-trust-and-definitions/profile|Data Trust & Metric Definitions]]
**Industry:** [[industries/game-analytics-vendors|Game Analytics Vendors]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Two people bring the same metric to the meeting, the numbers differ, and the first ten minutes go on establishing why.
**Tags:** #quick-win #data-integration #evaluation-metrics #descriptive-statistics #workflow-orchestration #automation #compliance #sets-and-logic
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to make one number mean one thing across every tool and every team, and whoever establishes that takes the account.

## The Problem
It happens in most review meetings at most studios. Someone presents retention from the analytics platform, someone else has a different figure from the warehouse, and the meeting stops while they work out which filter or boundary differs. Sometimes it is resolved, often it is deferred, and either way the decision is made on a number the room does not fully trust. The same reconciliation recurs monthly with the same two figures.

## Why It's Still Broken
Nobody wrote the definitions down — two systems computing a metric with unstated rules will differ, and the difference is rediscovered in every meeting because the resolution is never recorded. The reconciliation happens verbally. Nobody owns the definition. And the meeting moves on.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Write the definitions down and reconcile once, in writing. Document the precise definition each system uses for the disputed metrics, which is the fix and is an afternoon for the handful that actually matter. Reconcile them once and record the difference and its cause, so the next meeting starts from the answer. Nominate one system as authoritative for each metric, which is a decision rather than a project and is what ends the argument. Label every chart with the source and definition used, since an unlabelled number invites the whole conversation. Fix the differences that are simply errors, which some of them are. Accept and document the ones that are legitimate, such as finance recognising revenue differently. Publish the definitions where the whole organisation can see them. Start with the five metrics that appear in decisions rather than the whole set. Review when a system changes, because a silent definition change causes the same problem again. And make the reconciliation document the thing people cite rather than their own query.

## Who Feels the Pain
Everyone in the meeting; analysts defending numbers against other numbers; leaders deciding on figures the room does not trust; and the data team, whose credibility erodes with every disagreement.

## Impact If Fixed
Two systems computing a metric with unstated rules will differ, and the difference is rediscovered in every meeting because the resolution is never recorded. Documenting five definitions and reconciling once ends a recurring ten minutes.

# Niche Analysis — Postproduction Studios

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/postproduction-studios|Postproduction Studios]]

## Niche Selection

A postproduction studio is paid per project to turn footage into deliverables, and its money is decided in three places. The bid sets a fixed price per shot or per service before anyone knows how hard the work will be. The craft (editorial, colour, VFX, sound) is judged by the client's creative team. The delivery is judged by a platform's inspection that passes or fails the whole package. The eight niches below split the industry by what studios and vendors actually compete over. Market sizes are estimates of US fees and tooling spend within each niche, not of industry revenue as a whole. The audience and automatable niches overlap the service-line niches by design.

## Why These Niches

VFX and long-form finishing carry the most revenue. VFX is the largest and most volatile service line, sold on fixed bids against breakdowns. Finishing is where the master is made and where the colorist relationship wins the show. The two low-digitized niches are where physical film and paper still run the work: library reels inspected on a bench and cleaned frame by frame before a platform will license them, and the 80–200-item delivery schedule (estimate) whose cue sheets, licences and certificates are retyped and chased by email. The two underserved audiences are people the tools skip: the independent producer who learns what deliverables cost after the film sells, and the post supervisor who runs six vendors from a spreadsheet. The two automatable niches are the most rule-bound work in the industry: packaging and inspecting masters against platform specs, and producing timed text that a speech engine now drafts.

## Filter Notes

Seven of the eight level-1 niches are terminal. **Feature & Episodic VFX** is not: its contested sentence produced two contests with different winners. Tentpole feature packages run to thousands of shots over a year and are awarded to the handful of large vendors that can commit hundreds of artists under a trusted supervisor in the richest incentive jurisdiction; there the contest is capacity held without idling between late turnovers. Episodic and invisible effects run to hundreds of small shots per episode on a weekly schedule and are awarded to boutiques on turnaround and price per shot; there the contest is pricing each shot close to its real effort. Different buyers, competitors and failure modes, so it decomposes into **Tentpole Feature VFX Packages** and **Episodic & Invisible Effects**.

Two terminal calls were close. **Long-Form Picture Finishing** could split into colour and online/conform, but the conform only exists to feed the grade and the master, and the account is won or lost on whether the look holds across episodes and versions inside the delivery window, so one contest governs. **Deliverables QC & Mastering** overlaps finishing, because the facility that grades often masters. It is kept separate because the contest (first-submission acceptance against a platform's inspection) is judged by a machine rather than a cinematographer and is fought by specialist mastering and QC houses as well as finishing facilities. Its buyer-side mirror is the content-operations reviewer niche under [[industries/streaming-video-platforms|Streaming Video Platforms]].

Boundaries with neighbours. Shoots, dailies on set, and wedding, corporate and real-estate video stay with [[industries/video-production-smb|SMB Video Production]]. Still-image retouching stays with [[industries/commercial-photographers|Commercial Photographers]] and still design with [[industries/graphic-design-studios|Graphic Design Studios]]. Translation economics belong to [[industries/localization-services|Localization Services]]; this industry keeps same-language captioning, subtitle timing and conformance, which NAICS 512191 names. Distributors' rights, avails and library sales sit with [[industries/film-distributors|Film Distributors]]. The delivery schedule niche here is the delivering side of the same paperwork, where the post house or producer closes the distributor's acquisition schedule; the distributor's re-servicing of its own licensees is analysed there. Shipping DCPs and keys to theatres is [[niches/movie-theaters/digital-cinema-delivery-and-keys/profile|🔍 Digital Cinema Delivery & Key Management]] under movie theaters; making the DCP is mastering here. Music mixing and mastering for artists sits with recording-studios.

Three candidates were considered and rejected. **Sound editorial and re-recording mix** is a major service line, but as a niche its contest splits between booking scarce mix stages and keeping the mixers that showrunners follow (a generic capacity-and-talent contest), and its automatable slice (loudness and spec compliance) sits inside Deliverables QC & Mastering. **Advertising post** (agency cutdowns, versioning, finishing for commercials) was rejected because its craft contest folds into finishing and its versioning contest into mastering, and commercial production itself belongs to [[industries/video-production-smb|SMB Video Production]]. **Trailer and promo houses** were considered; they sit on the border with advertising agencies, and their defining contest (turning one approved campaign into hundreds of territory, rating and platform versions overnight) is a versioning problem already covered by mastering.

## Why These Pockets

No pocket qualifies, and the reason is structural. Postproduction is a work-for-hire service on content the client owns. Every frame, note and QC result belongs to a studio or streamer under NDA and MPA Trusted Partner Network security rules. So the richest datasets in this value chain sit with the buyers, who use them for cost control rather than selling them. Studio VFX departments (31) hold competing bids from several vendors for the same shots, joined to what the shows finally cost. Streamer post-technology teams (24) hold every inspection result from every vendor, the only cross-vendor record of where deliverables fail. Neither invoices anything. The suppliers that touch the same data fail the gate once they are counted per vendor and by analysts only: QC and supply-chain software vendors keep a few spec analysts each, and the rest of their staff build and support software. Their defect corpus would carry a live "data isn't theirs" kill switch anyway, because it describes customers' pre-release content.

The strongest clean pocket is the production tracking databases (43), led by Luminate Film & TV. The subscription is the data, so it takes Q1 5 like the vault's other subscription data products, and the project status history is the vendor's own. It sits two points below Luminate's film and TV demand data, profiled as [[niches/film-distributors/streaming-demand-title-valuation/profile|🔍 Streaming Demand & Title Valuation Data]] (45), because no third party sets its clock. The gate failures are the expected result for an operator-tier service industry. The rollups' analytics teams are small and internal. Offshore prep vendors employ thousands of artists, which is production headcount, not research. The associations, standards bodies and TPN run on volunteers and small staff. Independent clearance firms are small, and the large title-search operations that produce most film title reports are the trademark search firms, already profiled as [[niches/graphic-design-studios/trademark-clearance-search-providers/profile|🔍 Trademark Clearance & Design-Mark Search Providers]] (47).

Several pockets that serve postproduction are already profiled under other industries and get no second profile here. Production incentive advisers who prepare VFX and post credit claims are [[niches/video-production-smb/production-incentive-advisory/profile|🔍 Production Incentive & Tax Credit Advisory]]. The payroll companies that pay union editors and sound crews and calculate reuse are [[niches/video-production-smb/residuals-payroll-administration/profile|🔍 Production Payroll & Residuals Administration]] (48). Completion guarantors and E&O underwriters are [[niches/video-production-smb/production-insurance-underwriting/profile|🔍 Production Insurance & Completion Underwriting]] (37). Media asset management vendors are [[niches/video-production-smb/media-asset-management-analytics/profile|🔍 Media Asset Management Analytics]] (31, kill switch). Test-screening research firms, whose notes drive re-edits in the cutting room, are [[niches/film-distributors/audience-tracking-test-screening/profile|🔍 Audience Tracking & Test-Screening Research]] (43). Forensic watermarking and anti-piracy services, which protect screeners and dailies, are [[niches/film-distributors/content-protection-anti-piracy/profile|🔍 Content Protection & Anti-Piracy Services]] (47). Music fingerprinting and cue sheet data vendors are [[niches/music-publishers/broadcast-usage-monitoring/profile|🔍 Broadcast & Usage Monitoring Vendors]] (40), because their product feeds performing rights royalties rather than post. The nearest thing to a qualifier in this value chain would be a neutral aggregator of bid-versus-actual or QC-failure data across vendors. Client ownership of the underlying material is exactly what keeps anyone from building one.

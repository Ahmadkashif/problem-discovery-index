# The Rejection Notice Nobody Classifies

**Niche:** [[niches/postproduction-studios/deliverables-qc-and-mastering/profile|Deliverables QC & Mastering]]
**Industry:** [[industries/postproduction-studios|Postproduction Studios]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Platform rejections arrive as emails and portal notices, get fixed overnight and are never recorded in a form that would stop the next one.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #bert #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #compliance #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to pass each platform's inspection on the first submission, and whoever delivers the lowest redelivery rate per programme hour takes the studio's mastering work.

## The Problem
A rejection notice names a failed check, an error code and a timecode. The operator fixes it, redelivers, and moves on. Next month a different operator on a different show hits the same failure. Nobody can say which failures recur, which department causes them or which platform is hardest.

## Why It's Still Broken
Each rejection is an emergency, and once fixed it stops mattering. Notices come in different formats from each platform, and nobody owns the record across shows.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Parse every rejection notice into a structured log entry: platform, spec version, check, category and timecode, linked to the package, show and the department that produced the failing component. A monthly report lists the top recurring failures and their root causes.

## Who Feels the Pain
QC operators and online editors doing redeliveries overnight; heads of deliverables explaining late deliveries; clients whose premieres are put at risk.

## Impact If Fixed
The recurring failures become visible within a quarter, and each one fixed at its root removes a repeating overnight redelivery.

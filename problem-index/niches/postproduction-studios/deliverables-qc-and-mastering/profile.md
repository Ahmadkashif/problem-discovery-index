# Deliverables QC & Mastering

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/postproduction-studios|Postproduction Studios]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to pass each platform's inspection on the first submission, and whoever delivers the lowest redelivery rate per programme hour takes the studio's mastering work.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$500M in US mastering, IMF and DCP packaging, versioning and QC services (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~8% of US postproduction revenue (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** High — automated QC is standard; triage and root cause are manual
**Target Buyer:** Head of deliverables or mastering at a facility; studio and distributor delivery operations
**Automation Potential:** Very high — checks, packaging and versioning are rule-based

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Mastering turns a finished programme into each platform's deliverables: IMF packages with Dolby Vision HDR and SDR, broadcast masters with segment timings, DCPs, international versions with textless elements and M&E, and dozens of territory and edit versions. Each platform inspects and rejects, and Netflix rejects the whole IMF delivery if one track fails. The work is the most rule-governed in postproduction: a spec, a check, a pass or fail. The buyer-side mirror, the streamer's content-operations reviewer, is mapped under [[industries/streaming-video-platforms|Streaming Video Platforms]].

## Current Tools & Gaps
Automated QC from Interra BATON, Venera Pulsar and Telestream; IMF tools (Colorfront Transkoder, Resolve, Clipster); loudness metering; SDVI Rally or scripts for orchestration; human QC operators for real-time review. The gaps: rejections arrive unclassified; human QC time is spread evenly, not by risk; and specs change faster than templates are updated.

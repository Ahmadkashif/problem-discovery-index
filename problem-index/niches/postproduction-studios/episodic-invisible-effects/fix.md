# The Change Order Nobody Raised

**Niche:** [[niches/postproduction-studios/episodic-invisible-effects/profile|Episodic & Invisible Effects]]
**Industry:** [[industries/postproduction-studios|Postproduction Studios]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A note that says "can we also lose the car in the background" adds a day to a shot bid as a screen replacement, and on a weekly schedule nobody stops to price it.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #bert #evaluation-metrics #revenue-impact #workflow-orchestration #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this sub-niche is fighting to turn around hundreds of cleanup, screen-replacement and set-extension shots per episode inside a weekly post schedule at a known cost per shot, and whoever prices those shots closest to their real effort keeps the series.

## The Problem
Notes arrive daily in Frame.io and email, from the show's VFX supervisor, editor, showrunner and network. Many are fixes inside the awarded scope. Some add new work to a shot. On a weekly schedule, artists just do it; the producer finds out at wrap that the package ran 20% over and can't reconstruct which notes caused it.

## Why It's Still Broken
Raising a change order means stopping to compare the note with the awarded shot description, writing it up and risking friction with a client who will award next season. Nothing in the review tool marks a note as out of scope, so the default is silence.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Classify every incoming note against the awarded description for that shot: in-scope fix, creative change, added element, new shot. Batch the out-of-scope ones into a weekly change-order summary for the producer, with the note, the frame and the estimated effort attached. The producer decides what to raise; the record exists either way.

## Who Feels the Pain
VFX producers at boutiques; artists asked to do unpriced work overnight; owners discovering the overrun after the money has been spent.

## Impact If Fixed
Scope changes become visible while the client still remembers asking for them. Raising even a third of them would recover several percent of package value on a typical series (estimate).

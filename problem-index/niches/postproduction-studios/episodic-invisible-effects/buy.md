# Quote Configurators Adapted to Cleanup Shots

**Niche:** [[niches/postproduction-studios/episodic-invisible-effects/profile|Episodic & Invisible Effects]]
**Industry:** [[industries/postproduction-studios|Postproduction Studios]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Configure-price-quote software already turns a product's options into a price in seconds; episodic VFX bids are still a supervisor's spreadsheet.
**Tags:** #decision-trees #linear-regression #feature-engineering #evaluation-metrics #automation #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this sub-niche is fighting to turn around hundreds of cleanup, screen-replacement and set-extension shots per episode inside a weekly post schedule at a known cost per shot, and whoever prices those shots closest to their real effort keeps the series.

## The Problem
A boutique may bid several series a month, each with hundreds of lines. Bids are assembled by copying last show's spreadsheet, adjusting rates by feel and re-typing assumptions. Bid turnaround is a competitive factor, and errors in copied assumptions go unnoticed until delivery.

## What Already Exists
CPQ platforms (Salesforce CPQ, DealHub, PandaDoc's quoting) price configurable products from option rules, apply discounts and approvals, and produce quotes and contracts.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs: shot attributes as options (camera move, frame count, occlusion, number of screens or elements, resolution, HDR), each with an effort rule; rules fitted from the vendor's own actuals rather than set by hand; assumptions written into the quote, so a change order can point at them ("bid assumed locked-off camera"); client-specific note behaviour as a multiplier; and output in the studio's bid template. Generic CPQ assumes a catalogue; here every line is a new plate.

## Target Customer
VFX producers at boutiques bidding several episodic packages a month.

## Impact If Solved
Bid turnaround falls from days to hours, assumptions are stated on every line, and later change orders have a written baseline to point at.

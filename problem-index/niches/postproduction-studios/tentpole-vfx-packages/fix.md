# The Idle Weeks Nobody Can Bill

**Niche:** [[niches/postproduction-studios/tentpole-vfx-packages/profile|Tentpole Feature VFX Packages]]
**Industry:** [[industries/postproduction-studios|Postproduction Studios]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** When a studio turns over sequences late, the vendor's held team sits idle, and the cost is absorbed because nobody recorded it in a form a studio would accept.
**Tags:** #descriptive-statistics #time-series-forecasting #evaluation-metrics #revenue-impact #workflow-orchestration #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this sub-niche is fighting to commit hundreds of artists, in the jurisdiction with the richest VFX credit, to a studio's fixed release date under a supervisor the studio trusts, and whoever can guarantee that capacity without idling it between turnovers takes the award.

## The Problem
Award letters assume turnover dates. When they slip, artists booked for the show wait, or are moved to filler work at a loss. Some contracts allow holding costs; vendors rarely claim them, because the evidence (who was held, for how long, against which promised date) is scattered across resourcing sheets and emails.

## Why It's Still Broken
Vendors fear damaging the relationship with a studio that awards next year's work, and producers have no clean record to show. Turnover dates are agreed in email and revised informally, so there is no baseline to measure against.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Record every promised turnover date and every revision as a dated event against the award, and every artist-week booked against that sequence. Produce a monthly holding-cost statement automatically: promised date, actual date, artists held, cost. It works as evidence for negotiation, or simply as the basis for pricing the next bid with this studio honestly.

## Who Feels the Pain
VFX executive producers negotiating overages; CFOs watching utilisation; artists laid off between shows.

## Impact If Fixed
Even where holding costs are never billed, pricing the next bid with a studio's actual slippage record recovers a share of the absorbed cost. On a large package, idle weeks can run to several percent of the award (estimate).

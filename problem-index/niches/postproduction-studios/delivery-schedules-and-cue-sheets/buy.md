# Rights Platforms Extended to Delivery Evidence

**Niche:** [[niches/postproduction-studios/delivery-schedules-and-cue-sheets/profile|Delivery Schedules, Cue Sheets & Paper Deliverables]]
**Industry:** [[industries/postproduction-studios|Postproduction Studios]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Rights management platforms already hold licences, terms and territories; they don't know which delivery schedule item each licence satisfies.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #bert #evaluation-metrics #compliance #data-integration #workflow-orchestration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to close every item on a distributor's delivery schedule (cue sheet, licences, credits, chain of title, E&O certificate) before the payment holdback date, and whoever closes schedules with no item sent back takes the producer's next show.

## The Problem
Music licences, talent agreements and chain-of-title documents are negotiated by production legal and the music supervisor, then requested by the deliverables team months later. Terms that matter at delivery (territory, media, term, credit obligations) are buried in PDFs.

## What Already Exists
Rights and contract platforms (Rightsline, FilmTrack, Synchtank for music clearance) that store agreements, avails and terms; contract lifecycle tools.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs: each stored right mapped to the delivery schedule items it satisfies; checks that a licence's territory, media and term cover each distributor's grant, so a festival-only music licence is caught before a streamer delivery; credit obligations extracted from contracts and checked against the final credit roll; and a delivery-evidence export per distributor. The platforms are built for rights owners managing avails, not for a post team proving a schedule.

## Target Customer
Production companies and post facilities already using a rights or clearance platform for music and talent agreements.

## Impact If Solved
Licence gaps get caught before the distributor's legal review rather than after it, and contracts no longer have to be re-read for every new territory delivery.

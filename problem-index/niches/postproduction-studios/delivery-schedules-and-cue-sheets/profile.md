# Delivery Schedules, Cue Sheets & Paper Deliverables

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/postproduction-studios|Postproduction Studios]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to close every item on a distributor's delivery schedule (cue sheet, licences, credits, chain of title, E&O certificate) before the payment holdback date, and whoever closes schedules with no item sent back takes the producer's next show.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$150M in post-house deliverables-department fees and coordinator time spent on paper deliverables (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~2% of US postproduction revenue (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — PDF schedules, spreadsheets and emailed documents
**Target Buyer:** Head of deliverables at a post facility; post supervisor; producer's delivery coordinator
**Automation Potential:** High for checklist tracking and cue sheet drafting; low for chasing third parties

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Picture and sound masters are half of a delivery. The other half is paper. This niche is the delivering side: the post house or the producer closing a distributor's or network's acquisition delivery schedule, which runs to ~80–200 items (estimate), dozens of them documents: music cue sheets, composer and song licences, credit block approvals, talent and location releases, chain-of-title documents, the E&O insurance certificate, closed caption files and dialogue lists. Distributors hold back part of the payment until the schedule is complete, and a returned item restarts the clock. The work falls on post coordinators and deliverables departments who did not create most of the documents. The distributor's half of the same paperwork, re-servicing each outbound licensee's smaller schedule from materials already held and keeping the record of what licensees reject, is analysed under [[industries/film-distributors|Film Distributors]].

## Current Tools & Gaps
PDF schedules, Excel trackers, email, music reporting services and PRO cue sheet portals, and rights platforms such as Rightsline. The gaps: schedules are parsed by hand per distributor; cue sheets are rebuilt after every re-cut; and nothing links each schedule item to the evidence that satisfies it.

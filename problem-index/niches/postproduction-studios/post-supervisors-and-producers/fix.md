# The Post Schedule Nobody Trusts

**Niche:** [[niches/postproduction-studios/post-supervisors-and-producers/profile|Post Supervisors & Post Producers]]
**Industry:** [[industries/postproduction-studios|Postproduction Studios]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The post schedule is a spreadsheet updated after the Monday call, so by Wednesday every vendor is working from a different version of it.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #evaluation-metrics #workflow-orchestration #data-integration #quick-win #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to give the post supervisor one live view of every vendor's status, cost-to-complete and delivery risk across a show, without making each vendor re-key its data, and whoever does it takes the production.

## The Problem
The supervisor sends the updated schedule as an attachment. Vendors save their copy. Dates change in email threads ("can we push episode 4 mix to Thursday?") without the spreadsheet catching up. The colour house books a suite for a lock that moved, the sound house holds a stage for an episode that isn't ready, and both bill for it.

## Why It's Still Broken
The spreadsheet is the only shared format every vendor accepts, and changes are agreed conversationally. Nobody has time to reconcile email agreements back into the master.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A shared schedule link instead of an attachment, with date changes extracted from email threads and proposed as edits for the supervisor to accept. Each vendor gets a calendar feed of its own bookings, and every change is logged with who agreed it.

## Who Feels the Pain
Post supervisors chasing confirmations; vendor schedulers juggling bookings; producers paying for idle suite and stage days.

## Impact If Fixed
One schedule version replaces a dozen, and idle suite and stage days caused by stale dates largely disappear.

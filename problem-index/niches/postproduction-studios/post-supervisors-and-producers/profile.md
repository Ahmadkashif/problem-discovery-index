# Post Supervisors & Post Producers

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/postproduction-studios|Postproduction Studios]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to give the post supervisor one live view of every vendor's status, cost-to-complete and delivery risk across a show, without making each vendor re-key its data, and whoever does it takes the production.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$300M in post supervision fees and post-management tooling on US scripted and feature work (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~5% of US postproduction revenue (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — schedules in Excel or Google Sheets, status by email and calls
**Target Buyer:** Post supervisor or post producer (often freelance, hired per show); facility-side post producers coordinating clients
**Automation Potential:** High for status aggregation and schedule risk; low for vendor negotiation

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The post supervisor runs a show from wrap to delivery. They pick and book the vendors (editorial rentals, finishing house, VFX vendors, sound house, captioning), hold the post budget and own the delivery date. Each vendor tracks its own work in its own system. The supervisor sees it through weekly emails, calls and their own spreadsheet. They are the post industry's most important buyer and have almost no software built for them: production tools stop at wrap, and vendor tools stop at the vendor's door. Facility-side post producers face the mirror image, coordinating dozens of client shows.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Spreadsheets, Movie Magic Budgeting for the budget, Frame.io for review, vendor portals and email. The gaps: no cross-vendor schedule with dependencies; cost-to-complete assembled by phone; and delivery risk visible only when something slips.

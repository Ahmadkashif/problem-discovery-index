# Production Scheduling Software Adapted to Post Dependencies

**Niche:** [[niches/postproduction-studios/post-supervisors-and-producers/profile|Post Supervisors & Post Producers]]
**Industry:** [[industries/postproduction-studios|Postproduction Studios]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Production scheduling tools model shoot days, cast and locations; post runs on locks, turnovers and vendor queues they don't represent.
**Tags:** #optimization-fundamentals #monte-carlo-methods #evaluation-metrics #workflow-orchestration #data-integration #worker-facing
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to give the post supervisor one live view of every vendor's status, cost-to-complete and delivery risk across a show, without making each vendor re-key its data, and whoever does it takes the production.

## The Problem
A ten-episode series moves through post in overlapping waves: director's cut, producer's cut, network notes, lock, turnover, VFX, colour, mix, QC and delivery, each episode a week behind the last. A slip in one episode's lock ripples through every vendor's booking. Supervisors manage it in spreadsheets they rebuild weekly.

## What Already Exists
Movie Magic Scheduling, StudioBinder and Yamdu for production schedules; generic project tools (Asana, Monday.com, Smartsheet) with Gantt views.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs: a post template with the real dependency chain per episode; vendor capacity and booked dates (colour suite days, mix stage days) as constraints; lock slippage propagated through every downstream booking automatically; streamer delivery windows and premiere dates as hard deadlines; and a risk view that simulates likely slips from past shows. Shoot schedulers stop at wrap, and generic tools know nothing about locks or stage bookings.

## Target Customer
Post supervisors on series; facility post producers scheduling suites and stages across client shows.

## Impact If Solved
A lock slip shows its knock-on effect on every booking the same day, so the supervisor rebooks a mix stage a month ahead rather than paying for an idle one.

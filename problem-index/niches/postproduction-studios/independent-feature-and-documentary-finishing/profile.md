# Independent Feature & Documentary Finishing

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/postproduction-studios|Postproduction Studios]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to take a locked independent cut to a deliverables package a distributor or streamer will accept, inside a finishing budget set before anyone read the delivery schedule, and whoever quotes the full deliverables cost up front and holds it takes the filmmaker.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$400M in US finishing, sound and deliverables work for independent features and documentaries (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~6% of US postproduction revenue (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — filmmakers cut on Premiere, Resolve or Avid themselves; finishing and delivery knowledge is thin
**Target Buyer:** Independent producer or director-producer; documentary producer; post supervisor hired for a few weeks
**Automation Potential:** Medium — deliverables QC, DCP creation and archival tracking automate; creative finishing doesn't

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Independent features and documentaries are finished on budgets set at financing, often before a distributor exists. When the film sells at a festival, the distributor's delivery schedule arrives with requirements the budget never covered: Dolby Vision HDR, Atmos or 5.1 mixes, M&E tracks, textless elements, captions, E&O insurance and, for documentaries, archival licences cleared for every territory and medium. Producers often lack a post supervisor and learn the specs as they fail them. Post houses serve them with discounted "indie rates" and lose money on the deliverables tail.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Consumer-grade NLEs, DCP-creation tools (DCP-o-matic, Resolve), deliverables-only post houses, archival producers with spreadsheets of clips and licences. The gaps: no tool turns a delivery schedule into a cost before finishing starts; archival clips in the cut drift out of sync with their licences; and festival DCPs fail at the venue.

# Clearance Logs Adapted to Archival Documentaries

**Niche:** [[niches/postproduction-studios/independent-feature-and-documentary-finishing/profile|Independent Feature & Documentary Finishing]]
**Industry:** [[industries/postproduction-studios|Postproduction Studios]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Clearance and rights tools track licences; an archival documentary needs every clip in the current cut matched to the licence that covers it, for every territory being sold.
**Tags:** #cnns #feature-engineering #large-language-models #evaluation-metrics #compliance #data-integration
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to take a locked independent cut to a deliverables package a distributor or streamer will accept, inside a finishing budget set before anyone read the delivery schedule, and whoever quotes the full deliverables cost up front and holds it takes the filmmaker.

## The Problem
An archival documentary may use hundreds of clips from news archives, stock libraries, home movies and other films. Licences are negotiated per clip with varying terms (festival only, North America TV, worldwide all media), and the cut keeps changing. At delivery, the archival producer must prove that every second in the final cut is licensed for the buyer's grant, or covered by a fair-use opinion from counsel.

## What Already Exists
Clearance logs in spreadsheets, music and clearance platforms such as Synchtank, stock and archive licensing portals, and E&O insurers' clearance procedures.

## The Customization Gap
The adaptation needs: visual matching of each shot in the current cut to the source clip it came from, so recuts don't break the link; per-clip licence terms (territory, media, term, duration licensed versus duration used) checked against each distributor's grant; flags where on-screen duration exceeds the licensed seconds; and a delivery-ready clearance report in the form E&O underwriters and distributors expect.

## Target Customer
Archival producers and documentary production companies; post houses offering deliverables packages to documentaries.

## Impact If Solved
Archival overruns are caught in the edit, while a clip can still be replaced, rather than at delivery, when the only option is an expensive re-licence.

# Feature & Episodic VFX

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/postproduction-studios|Postproduction Studios]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is "every serious competitor is fighting to price shot packages on fixed bids accurately enough to win the award and still make money," but the winners differ: tentpole feature packages are won by whoever can commit hundreds of artists under a trusted supervisor in the richest incentive jurisdiction, while episodic and invisible-effects work is won by whoever turns around high shot counts at a known cost per shot inside a weekly schedule. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$2.2B in US-billed VFX services for film and scripted television (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~35% of US postproduction revenue (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** High — the craft is entirely digital and tracked shot by shot
**Target Buyer:** VFX executive producer and head of production at the vendor; studio VFX executive on the buying side
**Automation Potential:** High for prep, roto, matchmove and bidding support; low for creative supervision

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Visual effects is the largest and most volatile service line in postproduction. Work is awarded per show, usually as a fixed price per shot or per shot category, after a competitive bid against a breakdown. Margins are thin, revisions are absorbed, and studios move work between vendors and jurisdictions as incentives change. The UK's 39% VFX credit from 2025 and Australia's 30% PDV Offset are examples. The industry's large failures (Rhythm & Hues in 2013, Technicolor's MPC and The Mill in 2025) were VFX businesses.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Nuke, Houdini, Maya, Mocha and Silhouette for the work; Autodesk Flow Production Tracking or ftrack for shots, versions and time; Excel bid templates; Frame.io or in-house review. The gaps: shot identity breaks when the edit changes, so bids, versions and invoices drift apart; per-site incentive allocation is a spreadsheet; and no vendor can say how accurate its bids are by shot type.

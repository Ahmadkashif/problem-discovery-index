# The Inspection Report Written on Paper

**Niche:** [[niches/postproduction-studios/film-scanning-and-restoration/profile|Film Scanning, Restoration & Library Remastering]]
**Industry:** [[industries/postproduction-studios|Postproduction Studios]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Every reel's condition (shrinkage, splices, vinegar syndrome, torn perforations) is written by hand on an inspection sheet that nobody can search when the library plans next year's slate.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #descriptive-statistics #data-integration #evaluation-metrics #quick-win #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn a library title's surviving film elements into a 4K HDR master a platform will license, at a cost per title its licence fee can carry, and whoever cuts the hand clean-up hours per reel takes the library's slate.

## The Problem
Film technicians inspect each reel on a bench, measure shrinkage, count and repair splices, test for acetate decay and note damage by footage count. The results go on a paper or PDF sheet filed with the job. When the library asks which of its 600 titles have elements in good enough shape for a 4K remaster, the answer requires pulling paper files or re-inspecting.

## Why It's Still Broken
Inspection is bench work with gloves on and film in hand; a keyboard is in the way. Labs have never needed the data beyond the job in front of them, and libraries assumed the lab kept it.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Voice-dictated inspection, transcribed into a structured record per reel: element type, gauge, shrinkage, splice count, decay test results, damage by footage, recommended handling. Old paper sheets are digitised and extracted the same way. Records are shared with the library owner as part of the deliverable.

## Who Feels the Pain
Film technicians repeating inspections; archive directors planning remastering slates blind; labs quoting without history.

## Impact If Fixed
A searchable condition record across a library, built from work the technicians already do, and re-inspections avoided on titles already handled.

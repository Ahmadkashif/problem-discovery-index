# Film Scanning, Restoration & Library Remastering

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/postproduction-studios|Postproduction Studios]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn a library title's surviving film elements into a 4K HDR master a platform will license, at a cost per title its licence fee can carry, and whoever cuts the hand clean-up hours per reel takes the library's slate.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$250M in US film scanning, restoration and catalogue remastering (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~4% of US postproduction revenue (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — the inputs are physical reels, inspected by hand and recorded on paper
**Target Buyer:** Head of restoration at a lab; studio or independent library's archive or asset-management director
**Automation Potential:** Medium — dust-busting, stabilisation and scratch repair automate; damage triage and grain judgement do not

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Library owners remaster catalogue titles for streaming and 4K releases, and the work starts with physical film: original negatives, interpositives and prints, some shrunken, scratched or suffering vinegar syndrome. Technicians inspect each reel on a bench and repair splices. The reels are scanned on ARRISCAN, Scanity or Lasergraphics scanners, then cleaned digitally frame by frame and graded to an HDR master. Costs vary by an order of magnitude with element condition, from ~$30,000 to $300,000+ a feature (estimate), and the lab quotes before it knows what it will find. A shrinking pool of experienced film technicians holds the judgement. Film laboratories are classified under NAICS 512199 rather than 512191; this niche covers that film-lab slice, and the rest of 512199 sits with [[industries/film-distributors|Film Distributors]].

## Current Tools & Gaps
Film scanners, MTI DRS Nova, Phoenix and Diamant for digital restoration, Resolve or Baselight for grading. The gaps: inspection reports are handwritten and unsearchable; quotes are guesses until the scan; automated clean-up confuses grain and detail with dirt; and libraries cannot rank which titles are worth remastering on cost.

# Aircraft Maintenance Records & Logbook Audits

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/aircraft-mro-shops|Aircraft MRO & FBO Shops]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to prove an aircraft's history complete (every AD, inspection, major repair and alteration traced through its logbooks) before a buyer's or lessor's records auditor finds the gap, and whoever finds the gap first takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$300M a year in records research, scanning, audit and reconstruction work by MROs and records specialists (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~1% of North America's ~$31.8B MRO demand (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — business and general aviation history still lives in paper logbooks and binders, scanned at best into unsearchable PDFs; airline records are digital from the 2000s but older history is paper
**Target Buyer:** Records manager or director of maintenance at an MRO or FBO shop doing pre-buy inspections and records research
**Automation Potential:** High for reading, indexing and gap-finding; low for the final records certification

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
14 CFR 91.417 requires the owner to keep, and pass to the next owner, the aircraft's total time, the status of life-limited parts, inspection status and the status of every applicable AD with its method of compliance. It also requires copies of the Form 337 for every major repair and alteration. When a business jet or piston aircraft is sold, a pre-buy inspection at an MRO includes a records review, and a missing 337, an AD signed off without a method, or a gap in the logbook can cut the price or kill the deal. Airlines and lessors run the same exercise at redelivery, which sits with the queued aircraft and railcar lessors industry. The MRO does this work because its inspectors are qualified to judge what an entry means. This niche is the whole-aircraft history (logbooks, ADs, alterations, damage); the engine life-limited-part trace sits in [[niches/aircraft-mro-shops/llp-workscope-planning/profile|LLP & Workscope Planning]]. Bluetail launched crowdsourced on-site logbook scanning because so much of the record is still paper.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Bluetail and flydocs for scanning and indexing; tracking services (CAMP, Traxxall, Veryon) for current status; records consultancies for transaction audits. The gaps: scanned pages aren't read, so the AD history and alteration file are rebuilt by hand for each sale; missing documents are found late; and nobody keeps a standing "records health" score per aircraft.

# The Missing 337 Found at Closing

**Niche:** [[niches/aircraft-mro-shops/maintenance-records-traceability/profile|Aircraft Maintenance Records & Logbook Audits]]
**Industry:** [[industries/aircraft-mro-shops|Aircraft MRO & FBO Shops]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A buyer's records reviewer finds an avionics installation in the logbook with no Form 337 on file, and the sale stalls while the seller's shop tries to reconstruct paperwork from a job done twelve years ago.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #bert #compliance #workflow-orchestration #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to prove an aircraft's history complete (every AD, inspection, major repair and alteration traced through its logbooks) before a buyer's or lessor's records auditor finds the gap, and whoever finds the gap first takes the account.

## The Problem
Major repairs and alterations need a Form 337 (or an equivalent approved record) filed with the FAA and kept with the aircraft records under 91.417. Over decades, installations get logged without the 337 being kept with the records, or the 337 references data that can't be found. Reviewers find these gaps at sale or at an FAA inspection, when fixing them means a records search with the FAA and the original shop, or a field approval.

## Why It's Still Broken
Nobody owns the alteration file between sales. Shops record the work in the logbook and send the 337 off; owners keep binders; the FAA's copy has to be requested. The gap is discovered only when someone reads everything, which happens at sale time.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Each time a shop performs records work or an annual on an aircraft, a quick automated pass matches every logbook entry that looks like a major repair or alteration (avionics installs, STCs, structural repairs) against the 337s and approvals on file and lists the unmatched ones. The owner gets a short list to resolve now, with the FAA records request drafted.

## Who Feels the Pain
Aircraft owners and brokers whose deals stall; records reviewers at pre-buy shops; IAs who inherit unclear alteration histories at annual inspections.

## Impact If Fixed
Resolving alteration gaps between sales rather than at closing could save ~2–6 weeks of deal delay per affected sale (estimate) and avoid price cuts that buyers demand for unresolved paperwork.

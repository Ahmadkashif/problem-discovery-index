# Maintenance Entry & Non-Routine Card Drafting

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/aircraft-mro-shops|Aircraft MRO & FBO Shops]]
**Category:** Highly Automatable
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to turn the mechanic's own words into a record that passes QA and the customer's records review first time, and whoever has the fewest rejected entries takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$340M a year in mechanic, inspector and records-clerk time spent writing, correcting and re-keying maintenance entries and non-routine cards (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~3% of 488190's ~$11.2B 2024 wage bill (BLS QCEW; share is an estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium-low — e-task cards at airline bases; handwritten or typed entries in logbooks and work orders elsewhere, re-keyed into tracking systems by clerks
**Target Buyer:** Director of maintenance or VP operations at an MRO, airline maintenance base or FBO chain
**Automation Potential:** High — the drafting is language work; the mechanic still reviews and signs

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Every maintenance action produces a record under 14 CFR 43.9: what was done, against what approved data, when, by whom, with certificate number. Non-routine findings at heavy checks produce cards that must describe the defect precisely enough for an engineer to disposition and a customer to approve. The same content is often written two or three times (work order, logbook or tech log, tracking system) and corrected when QA or the customer's records team rejects it. With ~9,100 certificated mechanics short in North America in 2026 (ATEC/Oliver Wyman), every hour of rewriting is an hour of maintenance lost. This niche is the drafting and coding of the record at the source. The compliance check of the package against ADs is a separate contest, in [[niches/aircraft-mro-shops/ad-work-card-compliance/profile|AD & Work-Card Compliance Checking]].

## Current Tools & Gaps
E-task-card modules in AMOS, TRAX and IFS Maintenix; repair-station ERPs; ATA recoding tools (AIXI's AutoCoder inside AireXpert, ATP's ChronicX); and voice-first AI entrants: Zymbly (YC W26), Fleetcraft and Veryon's AIRE. The gaps: entries written from memory at the end of a shift, codes assigned inconsistently, and no learning from what QA rejects.

# Newly Certificated A&P Mechanics

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/aircraft-mro-shops|Aircraft MRO & FBO Shops]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get a newly certificated A&P signing off work alone on a type in months rather than years, and whoever shortens time to independent sign-off without raising QA findings takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$220M a year in type training, on-the-job instruction, mentoring time and learning tools spent on mechanics in their first three years (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~2% of 488190's ~$11.2B 2024 wage bill (BLS QCEW; share is an estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low-medium — Part 147 schools and OEM type courses are partly digital; on-the-job training is a paper task list signed by a senior mechanic
**Target Buyer:** Director of training or director of maintenance at an MRO, airline base or FBO chain
**Automation Potential:** Medium — high for knowledge retrieval and OJT evidence; low for the hands-on skill itself

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
The FAA issued a record 11,103 new mechanic certificates in 2025, and schools produced 7,496 new mechanics (ATEC/Oliver Wyman 2026, reported by AVweb). At the same time 41% of certificated mechanics are over 60 (ATEC 2025), and North America is projected ~9,100 certificated mechanics short in 2026. So the industry is replacing its most experienced people with its least experienced ones. A new A&P holds a certificate but not the judgement: how to troubleshoot an intermittent fault on a type they've never touched, when a borescope finding is borderline, what a senior inspector will reject in a write-up. Shops pair them with seniors whose time is the scarcest resource on the floor. Tools are built for the shop (tracking, ERP) or for the school (curricula), not for the mechanic in their first year on the job.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Part 147 curricula and A&P test prep; OEM and FlightSafety or CAE type courses (recalled); paper OJT task lists; shop knowledge in senior mechanics' heads; Veryon tracking now in Embry-Riddle's maintenance curriculum (May 2026). The gaps: no searchable record of how the shop's seniors actually fixed things; OJT sign-offs without evidence; and no measure of when a mechanic is ready to sign alone.

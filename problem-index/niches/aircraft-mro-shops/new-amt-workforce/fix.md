# OJT Signed Off Without Evidence

**Niche:** [[niches/aircraft-mro-shops/new-amt-workforce/profile|Newly Certificated A&P Mechanics]]
**Industry:** [[industries/aircraft-mro-shops|Aircraft MRO & FBO Shops]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** A senior signs twenty OJT tasks on a Friday afternoon to get a new mechanic onto the roster, and the record shows competence nobody observed.
**Tags:** #compliance #worker-facing #workflow-orchestration #quick-win #evaluation-metrics
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to get a newly certificated A&P signing off work alone on a type in months rather than years, and whoever shortens time to independent sign-off without raising QA findings takes the account.

## The Problem
OJT task lists are paper or spreadsheet checklists. Under staffing pressure, seniors sign them in batches from memory, sometimes for tasks the new mechanic watched rather than did. The station then issues authorisations on that basis, and the first sign that something was missed is a QA finding or an incident.

## Why It's Still Broken
The OJT record is disconnected from the work order where the task was actually done. Signing a checklist is quicker than finding the job, and nobody audits OJT against work history until something goes wrong.

## What a Fix Looks Like
Each OJT task completes only when linked to a work order the new mechanic performed, with the supervising senior's sign-off on that job. The checklist fills itself from work history, and seniors confirm rather than recall. Tasks with no matching job stay open and show up as training needs.

## Who Feels the Pain
Training coordinators and quality managers answerable for authorisations; seniors asked to vouch from memory; new mechanics given privileges before they are ready.

## Impact If Fixed
Tying OJT to work orders could cut QA findings attributed to newly authorised mechanics by a quarter or more (estimate), and it gives the station a defensible record if the FAA asks how a mechanic was qualified.

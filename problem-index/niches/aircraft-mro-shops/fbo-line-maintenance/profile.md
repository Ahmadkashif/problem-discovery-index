# FBO Line Maintenance & Transient AOG Support

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/aircraft-mro-shops|Aircraft MRO & FBO Shops]]
**Category:** Low Digitized
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to return a transient aircraft to service the same night it arrives with a fault, and whoever diagnoses, sources the part and signs it off before the crew's departure time takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$150M a year in line-maintenance work-order, troubleshooting, parts-sourcing and authorisation tools and the labour they would replace at FBO shops (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~0.6% of 488190's ~$23.3B receipts (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — FBO software runs fuel, ramp and invoicing; the maintenance side often runs on paper work orders, phone calls to the operator's maintenance controller and the mechanic's own contacts for parts
**Target Buyer:** Director of maintenance at an FBO chain or a regional FBO with a repair station
**Automation Potential:** Medium — high for triage, parts search and authorisation paperwork; low for the repair itself

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
FBOs serve business and general aviation at airports, selling fuel, hangar space and handling, and many hold a Part 145 certificate or employ A&Ps for line maintenance. Signature Aviation reports more than 200 locations and agreed in July 2026 to buy TAC Air's 16 FBOs; Atlantic Aviation reports more than 100 in North America. Their maintenance customer is often a transient aircraft: a jet that landed with a fault message, a crew that needs to depart at 7 a.m., and an operator's maintenance controller several states away who must authorise work and spend. The mechanic may not know the type well, the manuals are in the operator's subscription, and the part is somewhere in the country. The contest is a same-night return to service, which is different from scheduled base maintenance. Fuel, ramp and terminal operations stay with the queued airport operators industry where they belong to the airfield.

## Current Tools & Gaps
FBO management software for fuel and invoicing (FBO Manager, Total FBO and others, recalled), maintenance ERPs at larger shops (Corridor, Avsight), OEM AOG desks and Veryon's Guided Troubleshooting for some types, and parts searched by phone and on marketplaces. The gaps: no structured triage before landing, authorisation and purchase orders by phone at night, and no record of how the last transient fault on that type was fixed.

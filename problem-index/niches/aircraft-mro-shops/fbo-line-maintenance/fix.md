# Repair Authorisation by Phone at Midnight

**Niche:** [[niches/aircraft-mro-shops/fbo-line-maintenance/profile|FBO Line Maintenance & Transient AOG Support]]
**Industry:** [[industries/aircraft-mro-shops|Aircraft MRO & FBO Shops]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** The mechanic has found the fault, but can't order the part or start the work until someone at the operator answers the phone and agrees to pay.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #workflow-orchestration #automation #quick-win #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to return a transient aircraft to service the same night it arrives with a fault, and whoever diagnoses, sources the part and signs it off before the crew's departure time takes the account.

## The Problem
For a transient aircraft, the shop needs the operator's authorisation before doing work beyond the diagnosis, and often a purchase order before ordering parts. The crew can't usually authorise spend. The maintenance controller at a fractional operator, a management company or a flight department may be on call, in another time zone, or juggling several AOGs. Authorisation takes hours by phone and email, and the last overnight parts shipment cut-off passes.

## Why It's Still Broken
Each operator has its own authorisation limits, contacts and PO process, held in the FBO's memory and in email threads. The request itself is unstructured: a phone call describing the fault and a rough cost. Controllers ask for details the shop hasn't written down.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A structured authorisation request sent from the work order: the fault, the diagnosis with photos, the proposed repair with approved-data reference, a parts quote with availability and shipping cut-off, and a labour estimate. The operator's controller approves on a phone in one tap within pre-agreed limits, and the PO and approval are attached to the work order for billing.

## Who Feels the Pain
FBO line mechanics and night-shift leads waiting to proceed; operators' maintenance controllers fielding calls; crews waiting at the FBO.

## Impact If Fixed
Cutting authorisation time from ~2–3 hours to ~20 minutes (estimate) catches the overnight parts cut-off far more often, which can turn a two-day AOG into a next-morning departure. For a business jet that is worth thousands of dollars in charter replacement cost (estimate).

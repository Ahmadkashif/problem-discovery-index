# Engine Shop Visits & Overhaul

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/aircraft-mro-shops|Aircraft MRO & FBO Shops]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is that every engine shop is fighting to release a serviceable engine at the lowest cost per flight hour inside the date it quoted. Cost and date are won by different capabilities, though: the workscope and life-limited-part decisions are won on records and engineering prediction, while the date is won on slot capacity and control of parts and repair vendors. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$450M a year in shop-visit planning, records, inspection and engineering software, services and the labour they would replace (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~3% of North America's ~$14.8B 2026 engine MRO demand (Oliver Wyman; share is an estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — large shops run MRO systems and test-cell data capture; workscoping, LLP reconciliation and customer approvals still move through spreadsheets, PDFs and email
**Target Buyer:** VP Engine Services or shop general manager at an independent or airline engine shop
**Automation Potential:** Medium — high for records and planning, low for the inspection and engineering calls on hardware

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Engines are the largest MRO segment, at $14.8B of North America's $31.8B in 2026 and $70.1B of the world's $139.6B (Oliver Wyman). An engine shop visit is the most expensive single event in an aircraft's maintenance life: a narrowbody performance restoration runs to ~$3–7M (estimate), and a spare-engine lease costs ~$100,000–$200,000 a month (estimate) while it lasts. Bain found turnaround up 35% or more for legacy engines and over 150% for new-generation engines against pre-pandemic levels. IATA counted 648 GTF-powered aircraft grounded at the March 2025 peak. Independent shops such as StandardAero ($6.06B 2025 revenue, about 8,000 staff), AAR and VSE compete with OEM networks and airline shops. Factory-level overhaul can be coded to 336412 rather than 488190 (recalled scope note), but the work is MRO and the vault holds it here; parts manufacture and PMA design stay with [[industries/aerospace-parts-suppliers|Aerospace Parts Suppliers]].

## Current Tools & Gaps
MRO systems (AMOS, TRAX, IFS Maintenix, SAP at the largest shops), engine OEM fleet analytics, trend-monitoring services, borescope and NDT equipment, and records platforms. The gaps: the induction assessment that joins on-wing borescope findings, trend data and records into one view before teardown; workscope changes approved by email; and no shared view of which delays (parts, repair vendors, test cell) drive the release date.

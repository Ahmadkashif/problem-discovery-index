# Airframe Heavy Checks & Base Maintenance

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/aircraft-mro-shops|Aircraft MRO & FBO Shops]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to absorb a check's non-routine findings without moving the redelivery date, and whoever forecasts the non-routine load before the panels come off takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$250M a year in heavy-check planning, non-routine management, engineering disposition and customer-reporting tools and labour (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~4% of North America's ~$6.1B 2026 airframe MRO demand (Oliver Wyman; share is an estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — airline-grade MRO systems and electronic task cards at the large bases; non-routine cards, engineering queries and customer change orders still pass through paper, spreadsheets and email
**Target Buyer:** Base maintenance director or customer programme manager at a heavy-check MRO
**Automation Potential:** Medium — high for forecasting and reporting, low for inspection and structural repair

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A heavy check (C-check or D-check on an airliner, a major inspection on a business jet) takes an aircraft out of service for weeks. The routine package is known in advance; the non-routine work isn't. On ageing aircraft, findings of corrosion, cracks, worn fittings and chafed wiring can equal the routine hours (recalled industry range), and each may need an engineering disposition, an approved repair, a long-lead part or a scarce inspector. Hangar slots are sold months ahead into airlines' seasonal schedules and lessors' redelivery dates. Redelivery penalties and lost revenue make the date the thing the customer remembers. Bases such as AAR's and HAECO's, plus airlines' own, compete on slot availability, labour rate and delivery record. North America's ageing fleet (Oliver Wyman expects maintenance spend to outpace fleet growth there) keeps non-routine load high. Unlike engine shops, the whole aircraft is in the hangar, so the contest is labour and finding management, not repair-vendor loops.

## Current Tools & Gaps
MRO systems (AMOS, TRAX, IFS Maintenix, Ramco) for task cards and stores; mobile e-task cards at some bases; engineering query tools; spreadsheets for daily planning. The gaps: no non-routine forecast at quote time; card-level blocking reasons (parts, engineering, inspector) not structured; and change orders argued after the fact.

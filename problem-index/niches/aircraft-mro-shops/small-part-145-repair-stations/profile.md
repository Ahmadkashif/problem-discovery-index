# Small Part 145 Repair Stations

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/aircraft-mro-shops|Aircraft MRO & FBO Shops]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep a small repair station's certificate and its customers' approvals with a one-person quality department, and whoever leaves the fewest findings per FAA and customer audit takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$120M a year in quality-system software, consulting, audit preparation and quality-manager time at small stations (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~4% of the ~$3.1B receipts of 488190 firms under 20 employees (SUSB 2022; share is an estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — quality manuals in Word, training and calibration records in spreadsheets, work-order packages in binders
**Target Buyer:** Accountable manager or quality manager (often the same person) at a repair station of under ~20 staff
**Automation Potential:** High for evidence capture and audit packs; low for the quality decisions themselves

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
2,825 of 488190's 3,358 firms had fewer than 20 employees in 2022 (SUSB), and ARSA told Congress in 2018 that the average US repair station employs 46 people, pulled up by a few large ones. A small station (an avionics shop, a propeller shop, a component overhaul shop, a regional airframe shop) carries the same Part 145 obligations as a large one. It needs an FAA-acceptable quality control manual and system (145.211), an approved training programme with records (145.163), calibrated tools (145.109), current technical data, 96-hour defect reporting (145.221), two years of records (145.219), and often an EASA Part-145 approval for foreign customers. It also hosts audits from each airline and operator customer; GAO found some stations getting around 50 airline visits a year. The FAA has not extended safety management system rules to Part 145, which ARSA and AEA opposed, but customers increasingly ask for SMS-like evidence anyway. Tools are built for airline MROs or for manufacturing quality, not for a shop where the owner signs the QC manual.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Generic QMS suites (ETQ, MasterControl, Qualio), repair-station ERPs (Corridor, Avsight, EBIS, Quantum Control) with partial quality modules, consultants who write manuals, and spreadsheets. The gaps: evidence for each manual procedure isn't captured as work happens; customer audit questionnaires are answered from scratch each time; and training and calibration records sit apart from the work orders that depend on them.

# LLP & Workscope Planning

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/aircraft-mro-shops|Aircraft MRO & FBO Shops]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to build each engine to the cheapest workscope that still delivers the on-wing life the owner needs, with every life-limited part proven back to birth, and whoever predicts module findings and remaining part lives most accurately at induction takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$200M a year in workscope engineering, LLP records reconciliation and build-planning tools and labour (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~1.4% of North America's ~$14.8B engine MRO demand (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium-low — LLP status lives in tracking systems and spreadsheets, back-to-birth evidence in scanned PDFs, workscope logic in engineers' heads and OEM guides
**Target Buyer:** Director of powerplant engineering at an engine shop, or the technical manager specifying workscopes for an operator or lessor
**Automation Potential:** Medium-high for LLP reconciliation and build planning; low for the engineering acceptance of hardware

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A workscope decides how much of an engine gets opened and restored, and therefore its cost and how long it stays on wing afterwards. The binding constraint is usually the life-limited part with the fewest cycles remaining. Engineers "build to a stub life": they replace LLPs that would otherwise force another removal before the target interval, and choose new, repaired or used serviceable parts depending on price and remaining life. Each choice requires the part's cycles to be proven back to birth, because a part without an unbroken trace loses most of its value (recalled industry practice). After the AOG Technics forgeries (over 60,000 suspect parts, sentenced February 2026) owners check every certificate. The winner is the shop whose induction-time prediction of findings and lives is closest to what teardown shows, so the owner isn't surprised by cost or a short next interval. Buying and selling used serviceable material belongs to the queued aviation parts distributors industry; this sub-niche is the shop's decision to use it.

## Current Tools & Gaps
OEM workscope planning guides, LLP tracking in AMOS, TRAX and tracking services, spreadsheet build-standard models, records platforms (flydocs, Bluetail), and used-serviceable-material quotes from distributors. The gaps: back-to-birth proof assembled by hand per engine; no model of module findings from the shop's own history; and used-material offers compared on price without a remaining-life and trace comparison.

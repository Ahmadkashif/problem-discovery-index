# Financial Planning Software Adapted to Crop Marketing

**Niche:** [[niches/commodity-brokers/farmer-hedge-accounts/profile|Farmer Hedge Accounts & Marketing Advice]]
**Industry:** [[industries/commodity-brokers|Commodity Brokers]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Wealth advisers already show clients goal-based scenarios on a screen; farm brokers explain hedges with a pencil.
**Tags:** #monte-carlo-methods #probability-distributions #large-language-models #worker-facing #quick-win
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to keep a farmer's futures and options hedges matched to the bushels actually grown, insured and contracted, with a margin plan the farmer's lender has agreed in advance, and whoever keeps the most farmer hedges on through a margin spike takes the account.

## The Problem
Farmers make marketing decisions with brokers in short calls and kitchen-table meetings, mostly from memory of what was sold where. The trade-offs between selling cash, hedging with futures, buying puts and relying on revenue insurance are explained verbally.

## What Already Exists
Goal-based financial planning software used by wealth advisers, which runs Monte Carlo scenarios, visualises outcomes, and records the recommendation and the client's acceptance.

## The Customization Gap
Crop marketing needs production uncertainty (yield) alongside price, local basis, and instrument types the planning tools lack: futures with margin calls, options premiums, HTA and basis contracts, and revenue-protection insurance with harvest price options. It needs crop-year timelines with USDA report and insurance dates. And it needs a record of the recommendation that satisfies NFA's communications expectations rather than an investment adviser's.

## Target Customer
Ag brokers at introducing brokers and FCM farm desks; farm marketing advisers who pair advice with brokerage.

## Impact If Solved
Recommendations a farmer can see and remember, and a documented decision trail for the broker.

# Margin Financing Sized After the Rally Starts

**Niche:** [[niches/commodity-brokers/commercial-grain-hedge-clearing/profile|Commercial Grain Hedge Account Clearing]]
**Industry:** [[industries/commodity-brokers|Commodity Brokers]]
**Type:** Fix (Pain Point)
**One-liner:** Elevators and their FCMs discover how much margin financing they need in the week they need it.
**Tags:** #monte-carlo-methods #time-series-forecasting #evaluation-metrics #quick-win #revenue-impact
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to clear an elevator's or processor's hedge book against its cash-contract position every day, hedge-to-arrive and basis contracts included, and to fund its margin calls through a price spike without the lender pulling the line, and whoever keeps an elevator's hedges funded through the steepest rally takes the account.

## The Problem
When corn rose about $1 in two weeks in spring 2022, some elevators faced margin calls in the millions while CME raised margins repeatedly. Lines sized in calm markets ran short, and some banks hesitated to extend them.

## Why It's Still Broken
Nobody owns the stress test. The FCM sees the futures, the bank sees the line, and the elevator's CFO is busy buying grain.

## What a Fix Looks Like
A standing quarterly stress report from the FCM to each commercial hedger and, with consent, its lender. It shows margin calls under historical rallies applied to the current book, including margin rate increases, set against available financing, with a recommended line size.

## Who Feels the Pain
Elevator CFOs, FCM agricultural credit officers, and the farmers whose HTA contracts are renegotiated when an elevator runs short of cash.

## Impact If Fixed
Financing is arranged before the rally, not during it. That costs an afternoon a quarter and avoids the forced hedge liquidations that turn a price spike into a solvency event.

# Commercial Grain Hedge Account Clearing

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/commodity-brokers|Commodity Brokers]]
**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to clear an elevator's or processor's hedge book against its cash-contract position every day, hedge-to-arrive and basis contracts included, and to fund its margin calls through a price spike without the lender pulling the line, and whoever keeps an elevator's hedges funded through the steepest rally takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$250M a year in clearing, margin-financing and position-reporting services spend by commercial grain hedgers (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~7% of technology and services spend (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — futures clearing and elevator grain accounting are both digital, but they meet in a daily spreadsheet
**Target Buyer:** Head of agricultural clearing at an FCM / CFO or merchandising manager of the hedging business
**Automation Potential:** High for position reconciliation, medium for margin financing decisions

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Country and terminal elevators, feed mills, ethanol plants and processors hedge nearly every bushel they buy or sell. That makes their futures accounts large, constantly moving and, in a rally, enormously cash-hungry. In spring 2022 some elevators faced margin calls in the millions while CME raised margins at least six times in six weeks. The FCM's job is to clear those books, finance their calls (often alongside the elevator's bank) and judge the hedge against the cash position it offsets. The elevator's merchandising and grain accounting belong to the queued grain-elevators industry, and the desks' bid-setting analytics are logged as [[niches/crop-farming/grain-merchandising-analytics/profile|🔍 Grain Merchandising Analytics]] (36). The clearing and margin-financing relationship is the broker's. ADM Investor Services, StoneX and CHS Hedging are the specialists.

## Current Tools & Gaps
FCM back offices and portals; elevator grain-accounting systems with position reports; bank lines tracked by the elevator's CFO. The gap is a shared, daily, reconciled view of futures against the cash-contract book that the FCM, the elevator and its lender all trust.

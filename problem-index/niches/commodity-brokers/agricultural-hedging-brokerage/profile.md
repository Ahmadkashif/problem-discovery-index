# Agricultural Hedging Brokerage

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/commodity-brokers|Commodity Brokers]]
**Category:** High Market Share
**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is "every competitor is fighting to keep an agricultural hedger hedged through a price spike". But for a farmer the contest is advice and a margin plan that the farmer and the farmer's lender trust, won by brokers with farm relationships. For elevators, feed mills and processors it is clearing a large hedge book against cash-contract positions and financing its margin calls at scale, won by FCMs with balance sheets and grain-system integration. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$600M a year in brokerage technology, data, advisory tools and margin-financing services spend on agricultural hedge accounts (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~15–20% of technology and services spend (estimate); a much larger share of accounts than of customer funds
**Digital Adoption:** Medium — clearing is electronic; client positions, cash contracts and advice still move by phone, fax-era confirmations and spreadsheets
**Target Buyer:** Head of agricultural brokerage at an FCM / principal of an ag-focused introducing broker
**Automation Potential:** Medium — position assembly and reporting automate well; hedge advice and margin credit stay human

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
Agricultural hedging is the reason commodity futures exist, and it remains a large, distinctive book for non-bank FCMs. ADM Investor Services ($8.0B segregation requirement in August 2026), StoneX (now with R.J. O'Brien's ag network) and CHS Hedging carry it, alongside hundreds of farm-town IBs. CME's agricultural volume hit records in 2025 (~1.9M contracts a day), and the hedgers are unusual clients. Their futures losses are offset by gains on physical crops and cattle, so a margin call is a financing problem, not a loss, provided someone believes the hedge is real. The elevators' own buying, storing and merchandising of grain belong to the queued grain-elevators industry; this niche covers the brokerage and clearing of their hedges.

## Current Tools & Gaps
FCM back offices and portals, ag data from Barchart (cmdty) and DTN, elevator platforms such as Bushel for cash contracts, crop insurance software at agencies, and spreadsheets for everything that joins them. The gap is a single view of a hedger's futures, options, cash contracts and insurance, which no party holds.

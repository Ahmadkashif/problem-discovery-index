**Contested on:** *Not terminal as stated.* The honest partial sentence is "every competitor is fighting to keep an agricultural hedger hedged through a price spike". But for a farmer the contest is advice and a margin plan that the farmer and the farmer's lender trust, won by brokers with farm relationships. For elevators, feed mills and processors it is clearing a large hedge book against cash-contract positions and financing its margin calls at scale, won by FCMs with balance sheets and grain-system integration. Decomposed into contested sub-niches below.

## The Problem
Agricultural hedgers manage exposures that change with weather and planting decisions, priced off futures plus a local basis that moves independently. Their brokers track hedge ratios in spreadsheets.

## What Already Exists
Treasury and commodity risk management systems used by corporates and energy firms, which link physical exposures to derivative hedges, compute mark-to-market and value at risk, and document hedge relationships.

## The Customization Gap
Agricultural use needs exposures in bushels and head that change with planted acres, yield estimates and crop insurance guarantees. It needs local basis as a separate risk factor, cash-contract types (HTA, basis, minimum price, deferred pricing) as hedge instruments, and crop-year rather than fiscal-year horizons. It needs margin-call liquidity against a lender's hedge line, and a presentation a farmer reads on a phone.

## Target Customer
Ag brokerage desks at FCMs and larger ag IBs serving commercial farms and feedlots.

## Impact If Solved
A proven risk engine without a greenfield build, with hedge ratios and margin exposure computed consistently across a broker's whole book.

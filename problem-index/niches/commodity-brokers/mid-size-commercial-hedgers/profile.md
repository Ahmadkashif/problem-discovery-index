# Mid-Size Commercial Hedgers

**Parent Industry:** [[industries/commodity-brokers|Commodity Brokers]]
**Category:** Underserved Audience
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to give a company too small for a bank's OTC desk a hedging programme (exposure measurement, futures execution and hedge-accounting documentation) that one finance person can run, and whoever gets a programme running on the least finance-staff time and through a clean audit takes the account.

## Profile
**Market Size:** ~$250M a year in brokerage, advisory and hedge-accounting services spend by mid-size commercial end users of futures (estimate)
**Share of Parent Industry:** ~7% of technology and services spend (estimate)
**Digital Adoption:** Low — exposures are tracked in spreadsheets, orders are phoned to a broker, and hedge documentation is assembled at audit time
**Target Buyer:** CFO or controller of the hedging company; the FCM's commercial brokerage head sells to them
**Automation Potential:** Medium — exposure capture and documentation automate; hedge policy needs judgement

## What Makes This a Distinct Niche
A regional propane dealer, a bakery chain buying wheat, a trucking fleet buying diesel, a feed mill buying soybean meal or a plastics molder exposed to resin and natural gas all have commodity exposures large enough to hurt and too small for a bank's commodity derivatives desk. Many have no treasury staff. Bank FCMs, which hold most customer funds, focus on institutional clients. Retail platforms are built for speculators. These companies need an FCM or IB that measures their exposure, recommends a hedge ratio, executes futures or options, and produces the documentation their auditor needs to apply hedge accounting (ASC 815). Some of the hedgers' own industries, such as propane and heating oil dealers, are queued in the vault; this niche is the brokerage service to them.

## Current Tools & Gaps
Commercial desks at non-bank FCMs (StoneX, Marex, ADM Investor Services), independent hedge advisers, treasury systems priced for large corporates, and auditors' templates. The gaps: no affordable exposure-capture tool, hedge documentation written after the fact, and effectiveness testing done by the auditor rather than the company.

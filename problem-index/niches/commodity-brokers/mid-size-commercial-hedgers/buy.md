# Corporate Treasury Hedge Accounting Scaled Down

**Niche:** [[niches/commodity-brokers/mid-size-commercial-hedgers/profile|Mid-Size Commercial Hedgers]]
**Industry:** [[industries/commodity-brokers|Commodity Brokers]]
**Type:** Buy & Customize (Vertical Adaptation)
**One-liner:** Large corporates run hedge accounting in treasury systems; mid-size commodity hedgers cannot afford them and need the same outputs from far less input.
**Tags:** #linear-regression #hypothesis-testing #large-language-models #compliance #automation
**Contested on:** Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to give a company too small for a bank's OTC desk a hedging programme (exposure measurement, futures execution and hedge-accounting documentation) that one finance person can run, and whoever gets a programme running on the least finance-staff time and through a clean audit takes the account.

## The Problem
Without hedge accounting, futures gains and losses hit earnings in different periods from the purchases they hedge, so a sound hedge makes results look volatile. That discourages hedging at companies whose lenders read quarterly numbers.

## What Already Exists
Treasury management systems with hedge accounting modules used by large corporates for interest rate, FX and commodity hedges: designation, documentation, effectiveness testing and journal entries.

## The Customization Gap
Mid-size commodity hedgers need exchange-traded futures and options as the main instruments rather than OTC swaps. They need exposures derived from purchasing data rather than a treasury forecast, and basis between the futures and the delivered price (a Gulf Coast diesel contract against rack prices) handled in effectiveness tests. They need pricing and onboarding that fit a company with one finance person, and an FCM statement feed as the source of hedge transactions.

## Target Customer
Controllers at commercial hedging clients of non-bank FCMs and their auditors.

## Impact If Solved
Hedge accounting becomes routine rather than an annual scramble, removing a main reason mid-size companies avoid hedging.

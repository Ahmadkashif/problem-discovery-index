## Cases

**1. Fundamentals & Estimates Content Operations (56).** Thousands of collection analysts and methodologists produce the licensed numbers against filing and earnings deadlines. Their real corpus is decades of mapping and exclusion decisions, which they don't keep as decisions.

**2. Alternative Data KPI Research Providers (56).** YipitData, M Science, Consumer Edge, Earnest and Bloomberg Second Measure sell KPI estimates graded by the reported number on a known earnings date. They hold the full estimate archive and keep no honest accuracy record by ticker and metric; calibrated confidence per estimate is a feature competitors without the archive can't copy.

**3. Portfolio Valuation Advisory (55).** Lincoln, Houlihan Lokey, Kroll and Stout produce the quarterly ASC 820 fair-value mark on a clock set by fund reporting and audit; Lincoln alone marks 7,000+ companies a quarter. The quarter-by-quarter panel is mostly used to publish an index, routine marks are rebuilt by hand, and marks are rarely graded against eventual exit prices.

**4. Credit & Distressed Intelligence Services (53).** Octus, CreditSights, 9fin and Debtwire hold years of covenant reviews and restructuring coverage in separate products, never joined. Joining them would show which flagged loopholes borrowers actually used.

**5. Commercial Due Diligence Consultancies (53).** The report is the invoice, studies are staffed linearly and the exclusivity window is the clock. They study the same mid-market sectors repeatedly for different sponsors, but market evidence carries over only in partners' heads.

**6. Transcript Library Platforms (52).** AlphaSense/Tegus, Third Bridge Forum, In Practise and the networks' own libraries. The transcript is the subscription product, output grows with each analyst-hosted call, and the corpus of dated first-hand expert statements can't be rebuilt by a competitor. The core defect is that nobody checks those statements: many become publicly verifiable within months, yet no library measures which kinds of expert claims turn out right, so libraries compete on size.

**7. Private Markets Research & Collection Teams (52).** PitchBook, Preqin, MSCI/Burgiss and Dealroom chase data that must be requested rather than parsed. Nobody estimates how much of the universe is missing, and knowledge of which contacts respond leaves with the researchers.

**8. Quality of Earnings & Financial Due Diligence Providers (52).** Every boutique-run sale rests on an adjusted EBITDA that an accounting firm's transaction advisory team produces to the banker's timetable, and each adjustment is later tested by the other side's diligence and by the price bridge from LOI to close. The practice holds tens of thousands of proposed adjustments as its own working papers and has never queried which kinds survive.

**9. Analyst Estimates & Consensus Data Providers (52).** LSEG I/B/E/S/StarMine, FactSet Estimates, Visible Alpha and Bloomberg hold the only complete record of what each sell-side analyst forecast and when, broken out by line item and graded by public results every quarter. StarMine already weights EPS and revenue consensus by accuracy, but the line-item, guidance-regime and lead-lag structure of the record is barely modelled, and most buyers still see an average.

**10. ESG Ratings & Sustainability Research Providers (51).** MSCI ESG, Sustainalytics, ISS ESG and S&P run large analyst teams and own ratings histories, with the EU ESG Ratings Regulation (2024/3005) as a live clock (applies from 2 July 2026). Ratings of the same company diverge widely across providers, and no provider keeps a standing record of what its own ratings predict.

**11. Fixed-Income Evaluated Pricing (51).** Evaluators (ICE, BVAL, LSEG, S&P) apply tacit judgement before the daily NAV strike; every client price challenge and every later TRACE print grades that judgement, and none of it is used to learn.

**12. Event-Driven Deal & Antitrust Intelligence (51).** Dealreporter/PaRR, MLex, Capitol Forum and CTFN publish thousands of dated regulatory and completion-risk assessments; every deal later closes or breaks on a known date, and nobody grades the assessments.

**13. Distressed & Restructuring Credit Intelligence (51).** Octus, Debtwire, 9fin and CreditSights publish thousands of forward-looking covenant-capacity and liability-management calls on clocks set by courts, coupons and maturities. Their own archive records how each call resolved, and none of the calls is graded against it.

**14. Index Providers' Research & Methodology Teams (50).** Marginal pass. The custom, thematic and direct-indexing production line where methodology integrity is the brand; Q4 is 3 because providers set their own rebalance dates.

**15. Financial Reporting Valuation (PPA & Impairment) (50).** Every acquisition by a reporting company needs an ASC 805 purchase price allocation and later ASC 350 impairment tests, each assumption reviewed by the auditor. Firms set attrition, royalty and discount rates across thousands of engagements and defend each one from scratch. Borderline at exactly 50.

## The pattern across all seven

Every qualifier has the same shape: **a research team publishes dated, gradeable judgements, the answer key arrives later in public, and nobody joins the two.** Examples are KPI estimates against reported numbers, covenant calls against restructurings, marks against exit prices, evaluated prices against TRACE prints, and expert claims against later filings. This repeats the vault's existing *missing join* class, now inside finance research itself.

The firms at the centre (hedge funds, asset managers, PE sponsors, banks) mostly **fail Q1 or carry an MNPI or confidentiality kill switch**. The qualifying pockets are the firms that **sell research to them**.

- **Portfolio valuation:** qualified at 55 under `private-equity-firms`, but kill switch at 53 under `hedge-funds` (credit-fund valuation, borrower MNPI).

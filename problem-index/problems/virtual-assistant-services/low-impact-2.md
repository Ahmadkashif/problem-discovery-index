# Context Capture and Handover

**Industry:** [[virtual-assistant-services|Virtual Assistant Services]]
**Type:** Low Impact (Customisation Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Everything that makes an experienced assistant valuable lives in their head, and the agency model replaces assistants routinely.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #bert #graph-neural-networks #word-embeddings #k-nearest-neighbors #evaluation-metrics #workflow-orchestration #tacit-knowledge-ml

## The Problem
An assistant becomes useful through accumulated context: which meetings this executive will always move, who gets a same-day reply and who does not, which airline and which seat, how the expense policy is actually applied, which colleague to route a request to, what tone the executive uses with which counterparty, and the hundred small conventions of a particular working life.

That knowledge is built over months and stored nowhere. Standard operating procedures exist in most engagements and are written once at the start, cover the explicit processes, and miss the judgement entirely — which is what the context actually consists of.

When an assistant leaves, the context leaves. The replacement receives the procedure document and starts rebuilding, and the executive experiences a service that was working well and has suddenly regressed to its first week. Given the turnover in this workforce, that cycle repeats frequently.

The same problem affects coverage. An assistant on leave, ill or in a different timezone during a crisis cannot be substituted, because the substitute has none of the context.

## What Already Exists
Standard operating procedure documents and process wikis are the standard mechanism. Password managers handle credential transfer. Some agencies maintain client profiles with preferences. Shared inboxes and calendars preserve the record of what was done, which is the raw material of the context and is not organised as such. Handover checklists exist at better-run agencies. Generative assistants are beginning to absorb routine drafting, which makes the context — what to draft and for whom — more rather than less important.

## The Customisation Gap
The context is latent in the work record and nobody extracts it. Months of calendar decisions, email handling, travel bookings and expense processing contain the executive's revealed preferences — which meetings get moved, which senders get priority, which options are consistently chosen — and deriving a preference profile from that record is a far better artefact than a procedure document written on day one.

Decisions and their reasons are the second layer. When an assistant makes a judgement call — declining an invitation, routing a request, choosing between options — recording the decision and the reason turns a private accumulation into an organisational one, and it is exactly what a handover needs.

Handover as a generated artefact is the third. What the incoming assistant needs is a distilled account of how this executive works, the current open items, the relationships and the conventions — assembled from the record rather than written by a departing assistant in their last week, which is when it is currently attempted and least likely to be done well.

And the same artefact enables coverage. A substitute who can consult the preference profile and the decision history can hold the role for a week rather than merely receiving messages.

## Impact If Solved
Context is the entire value of an experienced assistant and this industry discards it on a routine schedule, which is why the service so rarely compounds and why turnover is so damaging. Derived preference profiles, recorded decisions with reasons and generated handovers convert private accumulation into a transferable asset — which benefits the client, makes coverage possible, and gives the assistant something to point to that is not just tenure.

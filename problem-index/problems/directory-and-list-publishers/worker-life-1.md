# The Tele-Researcher on a Sub-Minute Call

**Industry:** [[directory-and-list-publishers|Directory & List Publishers]]
**Type:** Worker Life Changing
**One-liner:** A tele-researcher spends eight hours on an auto-dialer verifying businesses in calls averaging under a minute, held to 99% accuracy and a production quota, and the hard calls that need their judgement are the ones that count against them.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #feature-engineering #evaluation-metrics #worker-facing #automation #workflow-orchestration

## The Problem
Data Axle's posting for the role describes it plainly: a short, scripted phone interview through an auto-dialer to verify and gather basic business information, an average call time under a minute, and a 99% accuracy rate while meeting production standards, often from home. A former employee describes calls arriving at a rapid pace with little time between them, eight hours seated and the pressure of the quota. At ~60,000 calls a day across 150+ specialists, each person handles hundreds of connections and attempts per shift.

Most calls are routine: confirm the address, the contact name, the hours. A minority are not. Someone answers with a different business name. An answering service picks up for what turns out to be a dozen different listings. The owner says "we sold last month, call the new people". A voicemail greeting names a person but not a business. The researcher has seconds to pick a status from a fixed list, and the call that needed thought is the one most likely to be audited as wrong. Caller ID labelling now marks unfamiliar outbound numbers as possible spam, so a growing share of attempts never connect at all.

## Why It Matters to the Worker
The work is measured on throughput and on agreement with a quality auditor, not on the insight the researcher brings. An experienced caller who recognises a lead-generation front from the greeting gets no credit for it unless a code happens to exist; if the auditor reads the call differently, it is an error. Hang-ups and abuse are part of the shift, and the dialer leaves no recovery time between them.

Pay is modest — the posting cites a third-party estimate of ~$44,500–$57,000 for its Nebraska location — and the skill that develops, reading a business from a few seconds of audio, has no visible ladder. It is exactly the skill a compiler most needs to keep, and the job is designed in a way that makes people leave.

## What a Solution Looks Like
A pre-call card that says why this record is being called and what changed — the filing status, the stale website, the second business at the address — so the researcher knows what to listen for. Live transcription that drafts the structured outcome and the one-line note while the call runs, leaving the researcher to confirm rather than type. Records that can be verified from registries, websites and carrier data without a call never reach the queue. Ambiguous outcomes go to a second review instead of an audit failure, and a researcher's good reads — the patterns they flag — are credited and shared.

## Impact If Solved
Fewer, more decisive calls per shift, less time typing, and audits that separate genuine errors from genuine ambiguity. A researcher who can see that their judgement is captured and valued has a reason to stay long enough to become the expert the compiler depends on.

# The Directory Consultant Selling a Shrinking Book

**Industry:** [[directory-and-list-publishers|Directory & List Publishers]]
**Type:** Worker Life Changing
**One-liner:** Every year a print directory consultant has to ask the same plumbers, lawyers and tow operators to renew an ad in a book fewer people open, with no evidence beyond the advertiser's own sense of whether the phone rings, and their income falls with every cancellation.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #linear-regression #evaluation-metrics #worker-facing #revenue-impact #workflow-orchestration

## The Problem
Print directories are sold on an annual canvass. Before each edition's close date the consultant visits or calls every advertiser in their territory, reviews last year's ad, proposes this year's size and placement, takes new copy, gets the proof approved and books the order. Many advertisers are long-standing, often older owners of trades and local services who have bought the same display ad for years. The consultant's commission and quota rest on renewals, and the book is shrinking: Thryv's Marketing Services segment, which still carries its print Yellow Pages, fell 32.6% in 2025 to $324.0M, after falling from $653.2M in 2023.

Every renewal conversation is an argument about evidence nobody has. The owner says calls have dropped; the consultant believes the book still works for this heading in this town but cannot show it. Some ads carry tracked numbers, most do not. When an ad prints with an error — a transposed digit in the phone number, a lapsed licence number, the wrong service area — the advertiser loses a year of calls from the book and the consultant usually loses the account, after a credit claim.

## Why It Matters to the Worker
The consultant's income is tied to a product in structural decline, and the decline arrives as one hard conversation after another with customers who are often loyal and often angry. Pushing a full renewal on a weak heading damages a relationship; recommending a smaller ad cuts their own commission. They carry the blame for proof errors made under a close-date rush, and the job offers no tool for telling an honest story about what the advertiser is getting.

## What a Solution Looks Like
An evidence pack for each advertiser before the visit: tracked calls by heading and month where they exist, call trends for comparable advertisers in the same book, and a recommended right-size — keep the listing, shrink the display, or move part of the spend to a digital product — with the reasoning stated. A proof checker that validates phone numbers, addresses and licence numbers in ad copy against the compiler's own record and the state licence board before the proof goes out. A simple renewal history that shows which recommendations kept advertisers and which lost them.

## Impact If Solved
Honest right-sizing keeps more advertisers in the book and in the publisher's digital products, which protects the consultant's base even when ad sizes fall. Catching copy errors before print removes the most painful class of credit claims, and the canvass becomes a conversation with evidence rather than a negotiation over memory.

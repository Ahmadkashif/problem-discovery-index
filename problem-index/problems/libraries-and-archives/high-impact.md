# The Selector's Bet Nobody Grades

**Industry:** [[libraries-and-archives|Libraries & Archives]]
**Type:** High Impact
**One-liner:** A library records every loan, hold and expired licence in perfect detail and never records why a selector bought, licensed, renewed or withdrew the title, so the judgement that shapes the collection is neither measured nor kept when the selector retires.
**Tags:** #tacit-knowledge-ml #gradient-boosting #survival-analysis #time-series-forecasting #causal-inference #feature-engineering #evaluation-metrics

## The Problem
A collection development librarian at a mid-size public library system spends a few hours every week in vendor carts. For print, the wholesaler's portal offers pre-publication titles with review snippets and a suggested quantity; for digital, OverDrive's Marketplace offers ebooks and audiobooks on licences that expire after a period or a number of loans, at prices well above what a reader pays. The selector decides, title by title, whether to buy, how many copies for how many branches, which format, and whether to re-license a title whose licence just ran out. Weeding runs the other way: the same person, or a branch manager, walks the shelves and decides which books leave, guided by the CREW method's MUSTIE criteria (misleading, ugly, superseded, trivial, irrelevant, elsewhere) and their own sense of the community.

Experienced selectors "just know" things they cannot write down. That a debut thriller with a particular blurb will build a holds queue in one branch and sit on the shelf in another. That a celebrity memoir needs twelve copies for six weeks and two copies forever after. That a local author's book will circulate steadily for years. That a dated-looking travel guide still earns its shelf space because recent immigrants use it. That an audiobook licence is worth renewing at full price and the ebook licence is not. This is tacit knowledge in the strict sense: pattern recognition built over thousands of titles and years of watching what the community borrows, which the selector cannot fully specify.

The library records the outcome of every one of these bets in detail. The integrated library system logs every checkout and renewal, OverDrive logs holds, wait times and expiries, and the shelf list shows what was withdrawn. What it never records is the decision itself — the alternatives considered, the expected demand, the reason for the quantity, why a licence was not renewed — so it cannot score the bet. Public libraries spent an estimated ~$1.3B on print and electronic materials in FY 2022 (derived from the IMLS survey) and academic libraries far more. When a selector retires, the library keeps the circulation history and loses the judgement that produced it.

## Why It's Unsolved
The data collection problem is that the decision has to be captured where it is made: inside the vendor's cart, in a few seconds per title, by someone choosing hundreds of titles a week. A rationale written afterwards is reconstruction; one written at the time is a tax on the selector with no visible payoff. Carts export orders, not reasons, and the decisions not taken — the titles passed over — are never logged at all.

The labelling problem is that circulation is a noisy and biased label for decision quality. A title circulates more because it was bought in quantity, displayed on an end-cap or promoted in a newsletter; titles never bought produce no loans to compare with. Holds queues depend on licence terms and on how many copies the library chose to license. Experts also disagree with themselves: the same selector weeds a shelf differently in a year when the budget is cut or a book challenge is in the news. Honest labels need demand adjusted for copies, placement and promotion, plus a sample of repeated judgements to measure each selector's own consistency. Patron privacy adds a constraint: most states protect library records by statute, and the profession's ethics rule out person-level modelling, so the work has to stay at title, branch and segment level.

The deployment problem is speed and politics. Any suggestion has to appear inside the cart and be faster than the selector's own glance, or it will be ignored. And since 2023 every purchase and withdrawal may have to be defended in public: ALA documented challenges to 4,235 unique titles in 2025. That cuts both ways: a recorded rationale is valuable evidence that a decision followed policy, and also a document a selector may fear writing.

## What a Solution Looks Like
A decision ledger first and models second. Every buy, quantity, licence, renewal and withdrawal decision is attached to a short structured record — expected demand band, intended audience, reason code tied to the collection policy, and the alternative considered — drafted automatically from the cart, review sources and the title's metadata, and confirmed by the selector in one click. Each record is graded later against copy-adjusted circulation, holds ratio and time to first and last loan, so selectors see which of their instincts hold up.

On top of the ledger, learn the experienced selector's reads. Train demand models on title metadata, pre-publication signals, branch circulation history and the selector's own past quantities, and survival models for how long a title keeps earning its shelf space or licence cost. Surface "copies to license now, and when to re-evaluate" in the cart, and "keep, move or withdraw, and why" in the weeding list, each with its track record attached. The goal is not to replace the selector but to make a veteran's judgement visible, testable and transferable to a new hire or a small library with no selector at all.

## Impact If Solved
A library system that can show which selection and licensing decisions pay off can move part of its materials budget from copies nobody borrows and licences that expire unused to shorter holds queues — at mid-size systems that is plausibly tens of thousands of dollars a year in licence spend recovered (estimate). The recorded rationale also gives directors and boards a policy-based defence of every purchase and withdrawal when a challenge arrives, and keeps a retiring selector's judgement in the building.

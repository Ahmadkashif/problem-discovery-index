# The Merchandiser and the Misfiled Pocket

**Industry:** [[greeting-card-publishers|Greeting Card Publishers]]
**Type:** Worker Life Changing
**One-liner:** A part-time card merchandiser spends most of each store visit hunting misfiled cards and decoding planograms, when what she needs is a phone that shows her what is wrong with the fixture.
**Tags:** #cnns #object-detection #transfer-learning #evaluation-metrics #worker-facing #automation #workflow-orchestration

## The Problem
Card departments are maintained by part-time merchandisers, either publisher employees or contractors. Job ads from publishers such as Designer Greetings describe independent contractors paid a flat monthly rate per store to straighten racks, stock, reorder and swap seasonal cards by planogram; others describe setting fixtures to planogram and processing credits for out-of-date product. A typical merchandiser covers several stores, each visited weekly or more often around holidays.

Shoppers pull cards out, read them and put them back in the wrong pocket. Envelopes separate from cards. A pocket empties and nobody notices until the next visit. Seasonal changeovers compress the work: Valentine's Day must be set in the days after Christmas, and the Christmas cards must be pulled, counted and credited at the same time. The planogram she works from may be a printed sheet several resets out of date, a PDF on a small phone screen, or a stock number on a pocket tag she has to match card by card.

## Why It Matters to the Worker
She is paid by the assignment, not by the hour, so every minute spent finding the home of a stray card or working out which version of the planogram applies is unpaid. The work is physical (bending, reaching to the bottom row, carrying cases) and the seasonal surges are relentless. She is judged on fixture condition and sales she cannot fully control, and when a store's numbers fall it is often her visit log that gets questioned. Many do this work around childcare or retirement, and turnover is high because the job feels like endless tidying with no sense of progress.

## What a Solution Looks Like
A phone app that photographs each fixture section and, in seconds, marks misfiled cards with the pocket each belongs in, empty and low pockets, cards out of season, and pockets that do not match the current planogram. It drafts the reorder from the empty and low pockets, generates the seasonal pull and credit list from the photos rather than a hand count, and logs the visit with before-and-after images that prove the work was done. The planogram is always the current one, shown over her own photo rather than on a separate sheet.

## Impact If Solved
Cutting the search-and-check part of a visit by half (estimate) gives back an hour or more per store per week. It turns the visit record from a disputed log into evidence, and lets her spend the time she is paid for on the stocking and seasonal work that actually moves sales.

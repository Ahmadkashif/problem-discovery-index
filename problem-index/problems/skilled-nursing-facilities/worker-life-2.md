# The 5 a.m. Call-Off Scramble

**Industry:** [[skilled-nursing-facilities|Skilled Nursing Facilities]]
**Type:** Worker Life Changing
**One-liner:** A staffing coordinator starts every day by phoning and texting through a list to fill the night's call-offs before the day shift begins, and ends up working the floor herself when nobody answers; she could get back her mornings and her weekends.
**Tags:** #time-series-forecasting #gradient-boosting #optimization-fundamentals #worker-facing #workflow-orchestration #automation

## The Problem
Most facilities have one staffing coordinator, often a former CNA or LPN, and a director of nursing who is the last line of cover. Call-offs arrive by text and voicemail from about 4 a.m.: a sick child, a car that will not start, a second job that ran over. Each hole must be filled before shift change or the floor runs short. The coordinator works down a mental list of who is close to overtime, who is reliable, who said no last week and who lives nearby. She sends group texts with bonus offers, calls the agency, posts the shift to ShiftKey or IntelyCare, and asks the night CNA to stay four more hours. If nothing works, the unit runs short, the DON or the coordinator takes the assignment, and the day's PBJ hours dip. A dip like that, repeated, can cost the facility its staffing star: only 11% of nursing homes hold a 5-star staffing rating (MedPAC), and 29% fall below the 3-star staffing threshold for the new risk-based survey (CMS QSO-26-14-NH).

## Why It Matters to the Worker
The coordinator's day has no end. She is on call every weekend because callers have her mobile number. She is blamed for both short shifts and agency spend, though agency labour, at 80% more an hour than employees for nurse aides in 2021 (ASPE), is often the only option at 5 a.m. Her knowledge of who can be counted on lives in her head and in old text threads, so she cannot hand the job to anyone for a day. Turnover in the role is high (estimate). When she leaves, the facility loses its best-informed person about its own workforce.

## What a Solution Looks Like
A staffing assistant that forecasts call-offs before they happen, from the facility's own history: day of week, shift, holidays, weather, pay period, individual attendance patterns and recent overtime. It pre-books standby cover for the shifts most at risk. When a call-off arrives it offers the shift in ranked order to the people most likely to accept, within overtime and fatigue limits and union rules where they apply, by text with one-tap acceptance. It goes to the facility's own float pool, then marketplaces, then agency, and it shows the cost and the effect on the day's hours per resident day before each step. The coordinator approves and handles the exceptions. Weekend calls go to the assistant first.

## Impact If Solved
Filling routine call-offs automatically would give the coordinator back her first two hours most mornings (estimate) and most of her weekends. It would cut last-minute agency bookings, the most expensive hours the facility buys, and keep reported staffing steady enough to protect the stars and VBP staffing scores that the whole building is judged on.

# The Disability Claims Examiner's Caseload

**Industry:** [[life-insurance-carriers|Life Insurance Carriers]]
**Type:** Worker Life Changing
**One-liner:** A long-term disability examiner carries well over a hundred open claims, each a growing file of medical records, and spends the week chasing paper and writing letters instead of talking to the people trying to get back to work.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #survival-analysis #gradient-boosting #worker-facing #workflow-orchestration #compliance #automation

## The Problem
Group and individual disability income is written by life carriers, and its claims run for months or years. An examiner at a group LTD carrier manages a caseload commonly above a hundred open claims (estimate). For each one they request and read attending physician statements and medical records, track the elimination period and the own-occupation to any-occupation change (typically at ~24 months), order independent medical exams and vocational assessments, and coordinate offsets for Social Security disability and workers' compensation. Every adverse decision must be written to ERISA claims-procedure standards, with deadlines for decisions and appeals. Records arrive by fax and portal in no order. Diaries tell the examiner which claim needs action today, but not which claimant is likely to recover with a little help and which file is about to become a lawsuit.

## Why It Matters to the Worker
The job is emotionally heavy. Examiners talk to people who are sick, frightened and often angry, and the system pushes them toward paperwork and away from those conversations. They work with constant exposure: an ERISA denial letter that misses a reason, or a decision that misses its deadline, ends up in litigation with the examiner's notes as evidence. Caseloads rise when colleagues leave, and experienced examiners, the ones who can tell from the first call whether a back injury will resolve, burn out and leave, taking that judgement with them.

## What a Solution Looks Like
A claim file that organises itself. Incoming records are classified, dated and summarised into a medical and functional timeline with page links. Restrictions and limitations are extracted and compared against the occupation's demands. Deadlines under the claims regulation are tracked automatically. Letters are drafted with the specific policy provisions and medical evidence cited, for the examiner to edit and sign. A return-to-work likelihood, trained on the carrier's own closed claims, sits beside each file, so the examiner calls the claimant who will benefit most from a call this week.

## Impact If Solved
Examiners recover an estimated 8–12 hours a week from reading and drafting, carry larger caseloads without the error rate rising, and spend that time on the conversations that actually change claim outcomes. Claimants get faster, better-documented decisions.

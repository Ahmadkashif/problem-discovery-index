# The Worker Whose Submission Was Rejected Without a Reason

**Industry:** [[crowdsourcing-platforms|Crowdsourcing Platforms]]
**Type:** Worker Life Changing
**One-liner:** The work was done, the payment is withheld, no reason is given, the approval rate drops, and better-paid work closes off — with no appeal to anyone.
**Tags:** #bayesian-inference #gradient-boosting #confidence-intervals #hypothesis-testing #evaluation-metrics #compliance #worker-facing #expectation-maximization

## The Problem
Rejection is the defining hazard of crowdwork. A worker completes a task, submits it, and the requester rejects it. The payment is not made for labour already performed, and on platforms where approval rate gates access to work, the rejection also reduces future earning capacity.

Reasons are frequently not given. The mechanism often permits bulk rejection, and a requester rejecting a batch for a systematic reason — a broken task, a changed specification, a quality threshold applied retroactively — may reject hundreds of submissions at once without individual review. Some rejections are for work that was correct.

Appeal is weak or absent. A worker can message the requester, who has no obligation to respond. Platform intervention varies and is generally limited, since the platform's position is that the requester judges quality. There is no independent adjudication and no requirement that a reason be given.

The approval-rate consequence is the part that compounds. A worker with a period of rejections loses access to the higher-paying qualified work, which leaves lower-paying tasks, which makes rebuilding the rate slow. A single bad requester interaction can affect earnings for months.

And the whole thing operates across jurisdictions on contractor terms, with a workforce that has essentially no collective capacity and no representation.

## Why It Matters to the Worker
This is the withholding of payment for labour already performed, decided unilaterally, frequently without reason, with no appeal. In an employment relationship that would be unlawful in most jurisdictions; here it is a platform term.

The disproportion is what makes it severe. The financial amount per task is small; the approval-rate consequence is not, and the two are bundled into one decision made by someone with no obligation to explain it.

The information asymmetry is total. The worker does not know why they were rejected, cannot tell whether it was their work or a broken task, and therefore cannot improve. Many rejections are batch-level events the worker had no part in causing.

And the population affected includes a meaningful number of people for whom this is primary income, frequently in countries where the earnings are locally significant, with no access to the platform's dispute processes in any practical sense.

## What a Solution Looks Like
Require a reason. A rejection without a specific stated reason should not be permitted to withhold payment or affect approval rate. This is a platform policy change rather than a technical one and it is the single most consequential fix available.

Detect batch rejections and treat them differently. A requester rejecting a large fraction of a batch at once is almost certainly acting on a systematic issue rather than on individual quality, and those events should trigger platform review before the consequences apply to workers.

Score requesters and surface it. Rejection rate, payment speed, responsiveness to messages and appeal outcomes are all observable, and showing them before a worker accepts work is the information workers currently maintain their own review sites to approximate.

Provide independent adjudication. An appeal reviewed by the platform rather than by the rejecting requester, with the submission and the task specification available, is the mechanism that makes the rest of the system legitimate.

And decouple the penalties. Payment for work performed and qualification standing are different questions and should not be decided by a single unexplained click.

## Impact If Solved
Rejection withholds earned payment and forecloses future earnings on an unexplained, unappealable decision — the most severe feature of this form of work. Requiring reasons, catching batch events before they damage workers, publishing requester behaviour and providing independent appeal are all platform decisions rather than technical problems, and together they would change the fundamental risk of participating in this market.

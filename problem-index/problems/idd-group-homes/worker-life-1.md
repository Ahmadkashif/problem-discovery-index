# The House Manager Who Is the Float List of Last Resort

**Industry:** [[idd-group-homes|IDD Group Homes]]
**Type:** Worker Life Changing
**One-liner:** When a DSP calls off and nobody answers the float texts, the house manager covers the shift herself, often the overnight after a full day, because the house cannot close and the residents cannot be left.
**Tags:** #time-series-forecasting #gradient-boosting #survival-analysis #optimization-fundamentals #worker-facing #workflow-orchestration #automation

## The Problem
A house manager runs one or two homes: the schedule, the DSPs, the medication reconciliation, the van, the groceries, the family calls, the licensing binder. She also carries the on-call phone. A group home must be staffed every hour of every day at the ratio in each resident's plan, with an awake staff member overnight in most houses and one-to-one staffing for some residents. When the 11 p.m. DSP calls off at 9:40, she starts texting the float list and the part-timers. If nobody answers she either mandates the DSP finishing the evening shift or drives in and works the overnight herself, then runs the house the next day. In many agencies covering open shifts is an unwritten part of the job (estimate).

Call-offs never stop because the workforce is thin and new. The average DSP turnover ratio was 37% in 2024, as high as 54% in some states. Nearly two in three leavers had been in the job less than a year, and 26% of agencies turned away referrals because they could not staff them (NCI State of the Workforce, 2024). Every departure leaves a hole on her schedule until a replacement clears background checks, pre-service training and person-specific training, which takes weeks (estimate).

## Why It Matters to the Worker
House managers are usually former DSPs promoted for being reliable. They are often salaried, so the overnight she covers is unpaid in practice (estimate). The phone can ring at any hour, seven days a week, and she cannot plan a weekend, a child's game or a night's sleep without a backup she may not have. Each forced double she hands a DSP costs her goodwill with the staff she depends on. Burnout in this role takes out the person who holds the house's routines, its residents' preferences and its staff together. A house that loses its manager often loses several DSPs in the months after (estimate).

## What a Solution Looks Like
A scheduling assistant that sees call-offs coming and fills them without her. It would forecast which shifts are likely to go short from the agency's own attendance history: Friday nights, the shift after payday, a new DSP's fourth week. It would pre-book voluntary standby cover from DSPs already trained for that house and those residents. When a call-off comes, it would text the shift to the qualified people most likely to accept, checking med certification, person-specific training, driver's licence, gender preference and weekly overtime, and escalate to the regional on-call rota rather than to her. She would see a morning summary of what was filled overnight, and a monthly count of the shifts she covered herself that her director also sees.

## Impact If Solved
If automated offers and standby fill even half the shifts she now covers or chases, a house manager gets back ~4–8 unplanned shifts and ~20–30 late-night phone calls a month (estimate). Agencies keep the managers who keep their DSPs, and the cost of that cover shows up in a report instead of being absorbed silently.

# The Arbitration Admin's Monday Queue

**Industry:** [[wholesale-auto-auctions|Wholesale Auto Auctions]]
**Type:** Worker Life Changing
**One-liner:** The arbitration admin spends the week after every sale taking calls from angry buyers and defensive sellers, rebuilding each case from photos, scan codes and a policy that changed in June, and gets back the hours to judge the cases that need judging.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #k-nearest-neighbors #worker-facing #workflow-orchestration #compliance #automation

## The Problem
Claims arrive in waves. Buyers who picked up cars at the weekend open them on Monday, find the transmission slipping or the camera dead, and file. A buyer of a digital sale at Manheim has 10 calendar days, or two days after delivery if transport ran longer. The arbitration admin, or case specialist, takes the claim by phone, portal or email and checks it against the announcement, the condition report and the NAAA Appendix I categories and thresholds. They request photos, videos or a repair estimate, which Manheim requires within 5 calendar days. They decide whether the car needs a post-sale inspection and tell the seller, who rarely agrees.

Every case means opening four systems and rereading the policy. Did the $50,000 car's defect clear 2%? Was "check engine on" a sufficient announcement after June? It no longer is. Is a lane-departure camera an ADAS component now covered? It is. The buyer wants an answer today, the seller wants proof, and the admin is the person both shout at. ACV's careers page on 2026-10-28 listed Arbitration Admin roles in Buffalo, including an 11 a.m. to 8 p.m. shift. OPENLANE was hiring a bilingual automotive technical specialist for claim resolutions in Canada.

## Why It Matters to the Worker
The job is emotional labour with a rulebook. The admin is not paid to be liked by either side, but their day is decided by how many people are angry at them and how long each case stays open. A case reversed on appeal, or a seller who escalates to their account manager, lands on the admin. The rules changed mid-year, and admins absorbed the change through memos rather than tools.

The skill that matters, telling a real undisclosed defect from buyer's remorse, gets the least time. Most of the hours go on gathering documents and repeating policy.

## What a Solution Looks Like
A case workspace that opens every claim pre-assembled: the announcement, the condition report and photographs, the scan codes at inspection, the guideline clause and threshold that apply, and the most similar past cases and how they were settled. The system drafts the request for evidence and the message to the seller, and suggests an adjustment from past settlements for that defect category and price band. The admin reviews, edits and decides. Claims that fail the threshold or a filing window are answered with the reason and the clause, ready for one click.

## Impact If Solved
If preparing a case drops from twenty minutes to five, an admin handling 30–40 claims a day gets back more than an hour of every shift (estimates). The time moves to the borderline cases and the phone calls that need a person. Consistent answers that cite the clause should also cut escalations to account managers, the part of a case that drags on longest.

# The Instructor Answering the Same Question for the Four Hundredth Time

**Industry:** [[online-course-platforms|Online Course Platforms]]
**Type:** Worker Life Changing
**One-liner:** A course Q&A accumulates thousands of questions, most of them the same twenty, and the instructor either answers them forever or lets the course look abandoned.
**Tags:** #bert #transformers #large-language-models #k-nearest-neighbors #word-embeddings #evaluation-metrics #worker-facing #automation

## The Problem
Every course has a question forum, and for a successful course it never stops. Thousands of questions accumulate over years, and the overwhelming majority are variations on a small set: a setup step that fails on a particular operating system, a version mismatch, a concept explained too quickly in lesson nine, a typo in provided code, a prerequisite the learner does not have.

The instructor faces a choice with no good option. Answer them all, which is unpaid ongoing labour proportional to the course's success — the better it sells, the more of your life it takes. Hire assistants, which requires revenue most creators do not have and introduces quality risk. Or let it go unanswered, which learners read as abandonment and which reviews punish, damaging the ranking that produces the income.

The deeper waste is that the forum is a perfect record of exactly where the course fails, and it is treated as a support burden rather than as feedback. Two hundred questions about lesson nine means lesson nine is broken. Nobody aggregates it, so the instructor answers two hundred questions instead of re-recording four minutes.

## Why It Matters to the Worker
Support load scales with success and never ends, which inverts the normal relationship between effort and reward. An instructor with five successful courses has five permanent forums, and many describe reaching a point where they cannot launch anything new because the existing support obligation consumes the available time.

It is also emotionally uneven work. Most questions are routine, some learners are frustrated and say so, and a few are hostile. Handling that solo, indefinitely, for an asset that may be earning very little per learner, wears people down in a way that is hard to justify to anyone.

And answering the same question repeatedly is corrosive in the specific way that repetitive work is when the person knows the fix exists. The instructor knows lesson nine is the problem. Answering individually is the available action; fixing it requires time the support load has consumed.

## What a Solution Looks Like
Cluster the questions and surface the diagnosis. Grouping a forum's history into recurring themes with volume and location attached turns thousands of tickets into a short list of course defects — the highest-value feedback an instructor could have and currently the most buried.

Answer the recurring ones automatically, grounded in the course. A retrieval-based responder that answers from the actual course material and the instructor's own prior answers handles the routine majority accurately, and should defer visibly rather than guess when the question is outside what it can support — a wrong answer in a teaching context is worse than no answer, because the learner cannot evaluate it.

Route what matters to the instructor. Genuinely novel questions, confusions that indicate a conceptual gap, and frustrated learners are what a person should see, and they are a small fraction of the volume.

Turn the forum into a maintenance queue. Question clusters located at a specific lesson and timestamp, prioritised by volume and by their correlation with abandonment, tell the instructor precisely what to re-record — which is the fix that removes the questions permanently instead of answering them forever.

## Impact If Solved
Unbounded support obligation is the main reason independent instructors stop producing, and it is concentrated on exactly the courses that work. Automating the routine majority, escalating what needs a person, and converting the forum into a prioritised list of course defects removes the obligation and simultaneously produces the feedback loop this category has never had — the one place where learners tell you precisely where the teaching failed.

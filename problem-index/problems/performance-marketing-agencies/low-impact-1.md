# Creative Production at Paid-Social Volume

**Industry:** [[performance-marketing-agencies|Performance Marketing Agencies]]
**Type:** Low Impact (Customisation Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Paid social now consumes creative faster than any team can produce it, and the tools that generate volume have no idea which concepts are worth producing.
**Tags:** #cnns #transformers #large-language-models #contrastive-learning #gradient-boosting #transfer-learning #evaluation-metrics #feature-engineering

## The Problem
Automated buying platforms consume creative. Advantage+ and Performance Max want dozens of assets per campaign and fatigue them within weeks; a client running always-on acquisition needs a continuous supply across formats, aspect ratios, hooks and lengths. The agency's creative team, built for campaigns, is now a production line.

Volume is no longer the hard part — generative tooling has cut the cost of producing a variant dramatically. What has not changed is knowing which concepts to produce. The decision of what to make is based on what performed last month, a strategist's judgement, and whatever the platform's automated placement chose to spend on, which is itself confounded with the outcome being measured. So teams produce a great many variants of a small number of ideas, and the platform's optimiser picks among them.

The deeper miss is that the learning does not accumulate. An agency running creative for eighty clients across five verticals generates thousands of creative outcomes a year and retains none of it in a usable form. The next brief starts from a moodboard.

## What Already Exists
Smartly, Celtra and Creatopy handle dynamic assembly and format adaptation at scale. Generative tools — Firefly, Runway, and a wave of ad-specific vendors — produce variants cheaply. Creative analytics from VidMob, Vidsy and Motion score assets against tagged attributes and are genuinely useful for post-hoc analysis. The buying platforms run their own creative selection internally and report which asset won without saying why. Brand asset management tools hold the approved library.

## The Customisation Gap
The generic tools optimise the production of assets; what an agency needs is a model of what works for *this client's* audience, product and category, and a way to carry learning across clients without carrying the brand. Those are different requirements pulling in opposite directions, which is why neither the per-client approach nor the universal benchmark works.

The transferable unit is the creative attribute in context — a hook structure, an opening three seconds, the position of the offer, whether a person appears and how — evaluated against the placement and audience it ran in. Attributes transfer across clients; assets do not. An agency is uniquely placed to learn attribute-level effects across its portfolio and apply them to a new client from day one, which is exactly the cold-start moment where creative decisions are currently worst informed.

The client-specific customisation on top is constraint: brand systems, claim substantiation, regulatory copy, and the things a particular brand will never do. Those live in a PDF and a creative director's head, are enforced by review after the fact, and are the main reason generative production at volume still requires a human bottleneck.

And the outcome link is missing at the concept level. Platforms report which asset spent and converted; the confound is that the platform chose the spend. Isolating creative effect requires either deliberate balanced rotation or explicit tests, and almost nobody runs them because the platform discourages it.

## Impact If Solved
Creative is consistently measured as the largest controllable driver of paid social performance and is the one part of the stack the platforms have not automated away from the agency. An attribute-level model learned across a portfolio is the agency's natural advantage and directly addresses the cold-start problem that wastes the first weeks of every new client's budget. It also turns creative from a cost centre consuming margin into the service the agency can defensibly charge for.

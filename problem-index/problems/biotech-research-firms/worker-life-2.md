# The Study Director Holding Fifteen Studies

**Industry:** [[biotech-research-firms|Biotech Research Firms]]
**Type:** Worker Life Changing
**One-liner:** At a preclinical CRO, each GLP study has one study director who is personally accountable for everything that happens in it, and a study director running a dozen or more studies at once spends evenings on deviations, sponsor emails and report drafts rather than on the science they sign for.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #survival-analysis #feature-engineering #compliance #worker-facing #workflow-orchestration

## The Problem
Under 21 CFR 58.33 the study director has overall responsibility for the technical conduct of a GLP study and is its single point of control: protocol and amendments, correct recording of data, documentation of every unforeseen event, GLP compliance, and archiving. At a large preclinical CRO a study director commonly carries ~10–20 concurrent studies at different stages (estimate). Each produces a stream of events. A dosing formulation is re-made outside its stability window. An animal is found dead overnight. A sponsor asks to add a satellite group. QA's in-life audit returns findings that need responses within a deadline. The pathologist's peer review changes a diagnosis that ripples through the report. At the end, the study director assembles a draft report of hundreds of pages from contributing scientists' sections, data tables and the SEND package, and must answer the sponsor's comments before the date the sponsor has promised its board for the IND.

## Why It Matters to the Worker
The accountability is personal and the workload is set by other people. A study director signs the GLP compliance statement and is the person an FDA inspector interviews. A deviation assessed hastily at 9 p.m. can resurface in an inspection years later. Sponsors are anxious: their financing tranches and pre-IND meeting dates depend on the report, so the study director absorbs their pressure while depending on vivarium, pathology and formulation colleagues they do not manage. Experienced study directors are scarce, and burnout drives them toward sponsor-side or regulatory jobs, which deepens the load on those who remain.

## What a Solution Looks Like
A study director's assistant that sits on the study's data and correspondence. It drafts deviation records with the protocol section, the GLP clause and an impact assessment for the study director to edit. It tracks QA findings and response deadlines across all studies, and summarises sponsor email threads into decisions and open questions. It assembles the draft report from validated data tables, checking that every number in the narrative matches its table and its SEND domain. A schedule-risk view flags which studies are likely to slip, so conversations with sponsors happen early rather than the week before the report is due.

## Impact If Solved
Cutting the drafting and tracking load could return an estimated ~5–8 hours a week to each study director (estimate). It would reduce the inconsistencies between report, tables and SEND that cost review cycles, and it lets an experienced study director carry a full portfolio without the evenings that push them out of the role.

# The Facility Manager Building the Monthly KPI Pack

**Industry:** [[facilities-management-firms|Facilities Management Firms]]
**Type:** Worker Life Changing
**One-liner:** The provider's site or account facility manager gets back several days a month, and stops defending numbers they did not choose, when the SLA report assembles itself from the work orders and explains its own misses.
**Tags:** #worker-facing #large-language-models #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #data-integration #workflow-orchestration #quick-win

## The Problem
Every IFM contract carries a schedule of key performance indicators: response and completion times by priority, PM completion rate, first-time-fix, customer satisfaction scores, statutory compliance, energy, and sometimes a gain-share or fee-at-risk tied to the results. Every month the provider's facility manager produces the pack that proves them. They export work orders from the CMMS, clean out duplicates and tickets logged against the wrong priority, chase technicians for missing completion times, pull subcontractor data from emails, reconcile the client's own survey results, build the charts in Excel or PowerPoint and write the commentary on every red cell.

The pack takes days. It is then argued over in a monthly meeting and a quarterly business review where the client's real estate lead questions the misses and part of the provider's fee can depend on the outcome. Most misses have a mundane cause: a work order raised as priority 1 that was really routine, a part on back-order, or a subcontractor who finished on time but closed the ticket late. Proving that means going back through the tickets one at a time.

## Why It Matters to the Worker
The facility manager was hired to run buildings and spends a week a month as a report clerk. The pack is a monthly exam, graded by someone looking for reasons to withhold fee, on data the manager did not enter and cannot fully trust. The stress falls hardest on site managers at accounts in transition or rebid, where a bad month can be quoted back at them in the next RFP. Time spent on the pack is time not spent walking the buildings, which is the work they were trained for and the work that actually moves the numbers.

## What a Solution Looks Like
A reporting assistant reads the CMMS, the subcontractor portals and the survey tool continuously rather than once a month. It flags data problems while they can still be fixed: tickets with no completion time, priorities that do not match the request text, duplicates. It computes every KPI against the contract's own definitions and drafts the commentary for each miss from the ticket history ("three of the four P1 misses were weekend roof leaks awaiting a roofer; average arrival 5.2 hours"). The manager reviews and edits rather than builds from scratch, and can answer a question in the meeting by asking it.

## Impact If Solved
Two to four days a month given back to each account facility manager (estimate), fewer disputed fee deductions, and a manager who walks into the review knowing where the numbers come from, not hoping nobody asks.

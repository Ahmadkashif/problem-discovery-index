# Brand PLM Built for One Brand, Not a Studio of Twelve

**Industry:** [[specialty-design-studios|Specialty Design Studios]]
**Type:** Low Impact (Customisation Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Product lifecycle management software solves tech packs, sample tracking and factory comments for a single brand, and a design studio serving a dozen brands ends up working inside a dozen client systems or back in Excel.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #feature-engineering #data-integration #workflow-orchestration #automation #quick-win

## The Problem
A small apparel development studio may carry ~8–15 active clients at once (estimate): a direct-to-consumer startup on Backbone, a mid-size brand on Centric, a retailer's private-label team on its own portal, and several founders with no system at all. Each client has its own size chart, its own points-of-measure naming, its own tolerance conventions, its own tech pack template and its own factories. The studio's technical designers re-key the same garment logic — a five-pocket jean, a raglan hoodie, a bias-cut slip — into each client's format, and carry the fit history of every style in their heads because it lives in a different system for every client.

The studio also owns things no single brand owns: its block library, its construction details, its knowledge of which factory handles which fabric, and its own utilisation. None of that has a home in a brand's PLM, and when the engagement ends the studio loses access to the record of the work it did.

## What Already Exists
Fashion PLM is a mature category. Centric PLM serves large multi-region brands; Backbone, Onbrand, Delogue, WFX and Techpacker target small brands and direct-to-consumer teams, with tech pack builders, BOM libraries, sample request tracking and factory collaboration portals. Generic project tools (Asana, Monday, Notion) and spreadsheets cover the rest. All of them assume the user is the brand: one size standard, one style numbering scheme, one set of factories, one owner of the data.

## The Customisation Gap
A studio needs a multi-tenant layer above its clients' systems: a client-partitioned workspace where each brand's data stays isolated and handed over on exit, beside a studio-owned library of blocks, construction details and grading rules that can be applied to any client's template. It needs translation between clients' point-of-measure vocabularies and tolerance conventions, so a studio block becomes a client tech pack without re-keying; export into each client's PLM format; a studio-wide view of every sample in flight across all clients and factories; and time capture per style per round for fixed-fee pricing. Contract terms matter as much as features: what the studio may retain (its own blocks and methods) and what it must hand over (the client's styles) has to be built into the data model, not left to an email at the end.

## Impact If Solved
A technical designer who stops re-keying the same garment into different templates recovers several hours a week, and the studio gains a single view of its sampling pipeline and a library that compounds across clients instead of evaporating at each handover — the difference between selling hours and selling a method.

# The Merchant Whose Shelf Was Sold

**Industry:** [[retail-media-networks|Retail Media Networks]]
**Type:** Worker Life Changing
**One-liner:** The category merchant is still accountable for category profit after the top of their search results was sold to the highest bidder, and nobody has given them a number for what it cost.
**Tags:** #causal-inference #gradient-boosting #hypothesis-testing #confidence-intervals #evaluation-metrics #revenue-impact #worker-facing #tacit-knowledge-ml

## The Problem
A category merchant owns assortment, pricing, promotion and profit for their categories. Their expertise is built over years: which items build baskets, which supplier promises are reliable, what a shopper in this category actually does. Then the retail media business sold the top of the shelf.

Now the first results a shopper sees in their category are determined by an auction the merchant does not run, cannot see the economics of, and is not consulted on. A competitor's sponsored placement sits above the merchant's chosen hero item. Private label, which the merchant is targeted on, is outranked by a brand paying for the slot. An item the merchant knows is a poor experience — thin margin, slow fulfilment, high return rate — is promoted because its supplier bid well.

The merchant remains accountable for the category's profit, is now measured partly against media revenue targets they do not control, and negotiates with suppliers who have discovered they can get placement by buying it rather than by earning it in the trade negotiation the merchant runs. Internally the media team and the merchandising team are separate organisations with separate P&Ls, separate systems and meetings that do not overlap.

## Why It Matters to the Worker
This is a loss of professional agency, which is a specific and corrosive thing. The merchant's judgement about what belongs at the front of their category was the substance of the role, and it has been partially transferred to a bidding process with no visibility back. When category profit misses, the merchant explains it; when the cause was ad load or a promoted item that under-converted, they cannot demonstrate that, because no system attributes category performance to media decisions.

The supplier relationship changes too. Trade negotiations that used to be about volume, promotion and shelf commitment now have a parallel track where placement is purchased, and the merchant's leverage in the negotiation they run is reduced by a channel they do not control. Suppliers notice this before merchants are told.

And the tacit knowledge is being stranded. A merchant who knows that a particular item's sales are almost entirely incremental, or that a category's shoppers abandon when the first two rows are sponsored, has no mechanism to put that into the system. It stays in their head, or it leaves with them.

## What a Solution Looks Like
Give the merchant the number. Category-level attribution of ad load to category outcomes — conversion, basket size, margin mix, return visits — is measurable with the same randomised variation that incrementality measurement requires, and reporting it by category is the single change that would make the merchant a participant rather than a bystander. Ad load becomes a setting with a known cost curve instead of a policy imposed from elsewhere.

Put merchandising economics into the ranking. Margin, availability, return rate and basket-building value are the merchant's variables; expressing them as explicit terms in the ranking objective is how the merchant's judgement re-enters the system, with weights they help set and can revisit by season.

Make the two planning processes one. Trade negotiation and media negotiation concern the same supplier, the same category and the same shelf, and running them in separate organisations against separate targets is how a supplier ends up paying twice for one thing or paying once for two. A joint planning view — what this supplier committed, what they bought, what the category got — is mostly a data integration problem and is not built anywhere.

## Impact If Solved
Merchants are the people who know why a category works, and the retail media build has quietly reduced their control while leaving their accountability intact — which is the standard recipe for losing experienced people. Giving them the cost curve and a seat in the ranking objective protects the category P&L that the media revenue is being extracted from, and answers the question the retailer's own leadership will eventually ask: what did we give up for this margin.

# The Consultant Billing Utilisation

**Industry:** [[saas-implementation-partners|SaaS Implementation Partners]]
**Type:** Worker Life Changing
**One-liner:** A consultant is measured on billable hours, staffed across two or three engagements at once, and spends the unbilled remainder rebuilding things the firm has already built four hundred times.
**Tags:** #gradient-boosting #large-language-models #k-nearest-neighbors #time-series-forecasting #evaluation-metrics #worker-facing #automation #tacit-knowledge-ml

## The Problem
Implementation consulting runs on utilisation. A consultant has a target percentage of hours billed to clients, it is reported weekly, and it is the primary number their performance is discussed against. Anything not billable — internal work, learning a new platform release, writing down what they learned on the last project — competes with it and loses.

The work itself is fragmented. Being staffed across two or three engagements means context-switching between different customers, different configurations and different stakeholders, with each client's workshop scheduled without reference to the others. Travel, where it still applies, sits on top.

And the content of the work is substantially repetition. The consultant runs a discovery workshop that resembles the last twelve, builds a configuration close to one they built in the spring, and writes documentation that mirrors documentation they have written repeatedly, all from scratch, because the firm's reuse assets are out of date and finding the previous version is harder than rebuilding it.

The certification treadmill sits underneath. Platforms release constantly, certifications expire, and keeping current is an expectation performed in unbilled time.

## Why It Matters to the Worker
Utilisation as the primary measure makes every improvement to the firm an act against one's own numbers. A consultant who spends a day writing a reusable asset that saves the firm fifty days has a bad week by the metric they are judged on. Firms say they value reuse and measure the opposite, and consultants respond rationally.

The repetition is the part that drives people out. The first five implementations are a genuine education; the fortieth is a rerun with different stakeholder names. Experienced consultants describe leaving not because the work is hard but because it has stopped teaching them anything, and the firm loses exactly the people whose accumulated judgement is most valuable.

Multi-project staffing produces a particular kind of exhaustion — never fully present in any engagement, always behind on one of them, with the cognitive cost of switching unpriced and unmeasured.

## What a Solution Looks Like
Make reuse the path of least resistance. Retrieval over the firm's own prior configurations, requirement documents and integration mappings, surfaced at the moment a consultant is about to build something, is more effective than any asset library — because the failure is not that the assets do not exist, it is that finding them costs more than rebuilding.

Draft the documents. Requirement summaries, configuration documentation, test scripts and training material follow known structures from the configuration itself, and generating them turns a writing task into a review task. That is a large share of the unbilled and low-value hours.

Measure the firm, not only the person. A firm that tracks reuse rate, discovery time trend and adoption outcomes alongside utilisation gives consultants a reason to invest in the asset base. The metric is the mechanism.

Respect the switching cost. Staffing that accounts for context-switching load, and workshop scheduling coordinated across a consultant's concurrent engagements rather than booked independently, is a scheduling problem with a real quality-of-life payoff.

## Impact If Solved
Repetition and a metric that punishes investment are why implementation firms lose experienced consultants at the point where their judgement becomes most valuable. Retrieval-first reuse and generated documentation cut the rebuilt work directly, and measuring reuse alongside utilisation removes the contradiction between what the firm asks for and what it rewards.

# Sustainability and Compliance Reporting

**Industry:** [[agtech-platforms|Agtech Platforms]]
**Type:** Low Impact (Customisation Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Report generation is a solved software problem and every carbon programme, grain buyer and conservation scheme wants different evidence in a different format, so growers enrol in one, decline the rest, and the data gets re-entered each time.

## The Problem
A grower now faces a growing set of programmes that pay for or require documented practice: carbon and ecosystem service markets, grain buyer sustainability schemes, conservation programme requirements, lender and insurer information requests, and in some regions nutrient management regulation.

Each wants evidence of practice — tillage, cover crops, nutrient rate and timing, residue management — over a defined historical baseline, in its own format, with its own verification requirements and its own definitions of what counts. A programme may require three years of history the grower has in a filing cabinet and a proprietary format.

So a grower enrols in one programme, because enrolling in three means doing the work three times, and the payments are individually small enough that the administrative burden is a genuine deterrent. The data required is largely the same across programmes and is largely already in the machine record.

## What Already Exists
Farm management systems produce field records and reports. Carbon programme platforms handle their own enrolment and verification. Some conservation reporting is supported by government systems. Remote sensing can verify practices like cover cropping and tillage independently and increasingly well. Nutrient management planning software exists in regulated regions.

## The Customisation Gap
Every programme is a separate integration and nobody maintains a canonical practice record that all of them can be generated from. The reporting problem is not report generation, it is that the underlying practice history is incomplete, inconsistent and unverified.

Practice inference from remote sensing is the piece that would change the economics. Whether a field was cover cropped, how it was tilled, and when residue was disturbed are observable from satellite imagery at reasonable accuracy, which means the baseline history a programme demands can be reconstructed rather than assembled from a filing cabinet. That is what makes multi-programme enrolment affordable.

Uncertainty deserves honest treatment here in a way the sector currently avoids. Soil carbon change is estimated from models with wide error bars, and programmes pay against point estimates. A platform that carried the uncertainty through to the grower's expected payment would be more trustworthy than the ones that do not, and would be a differentiator rather than a liability.

The third gap is programme comparison. Which programme is worth enrolling this field in, given its practices, its baseline and the payment terms, is a question no grower can currently answer and which is straightforward once the practice record exists.

## Impact If Solved
Sustainability programmes are moving real money into agriculture and the administrative burden is deterring exactly the growers whose practices would qualify. A canonical practice record with remote-sensed history makes enrolment a decision rather than a project.

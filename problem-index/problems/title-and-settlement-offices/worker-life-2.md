# The Post-Closer Chasing Releases

**Industry:** [[title-and-settlement-offices|Title & Settlement Offices]]
**Type:** Worker Life Changing
**One-liner:** The post-closing processor works a backlog with no end — releases not recorded, final policies not issued, recorded documents not returned — chasing servicers and counties by phone, while the cost of every forgotten item lands on someone years later.
**Tags:** #survival-analysis #large-language-models #bert #feature-engineering #worker-facing #workflow-orchestration #compliance #quick-win

## The Problem
After the closing celebration, a file still has work to do. The deed and mortgage must be recorded and the recorded copies returned; the old mortgage was paid off with a payoff check or wire, and its lender must now record a release, which some servicers do in days and others in months; a HELOC that was paid must be frozen and closed so the seller cannot draw on it again; the final owner's and lender's policies must be issued from the commitment, with requirements marked satisfied; and the premium must be remitted to the underwriter on its schedule. ALTA's time study counts final policy issuance as a task of its own in every file.

The post-closer, often a single person in a small office, holds a list of hundreds of open items across months of closings. They send release-demand letters on a schedule, call servicer release departments that have changed names twice, check county indexes for the satisfaction, and re-send the same payoff evidence. Nothing about the work is hard; the difficulty is that it never ends and nobody notices it until it fails.

## Why It Matters to the Worker
The work is invisible when done well and blamed when it fails. An unreleased mortgage surfaces when the property sells again, years later, as a curative headache for another examiner and a question about who dropped it. Policy backlogs surface at the underwriter's audit, and the post-closer is the person in the room. The role is often the entry-level seat in a title office, staffed by someone learning the trade, yet it is graded on the institution's memory.

It is also lonely work. The post-closer spends the day on hold with call centres that treat a title company's release request as low priority, with no tool that tells them which of the hundreds of open items is actually at risk.

## What a Solution Looks Like
An open-items queue built automatically from each closed file: every recording, release, freeze letter, policy and remittance due, with the date it is due under the state's release statute or the underwriter's agreement. Daily checks of county e-records that close an item when the satisfaction appears. A model of how long each servicer typically takes, so the queue escalates the items that are genuinely late rather than the oldest. Release demands drafted automatically and the final policy assembled from the commitment for review.

## Impact If Solved
The post-closer moves from re-checking hundreds of items to working the twenty that need a human, the office arrives at audit with no surprise backlog, and the next owner of the property does not inherit a release nobody chased.

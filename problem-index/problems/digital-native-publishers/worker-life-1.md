# The Reporter on a Traffic Target

**Industry:** [[digital-native-publishers|Digital Native Publishers]]
**Type:** Worker Life Changing
**One-liner:** Reporters are measured on a metric the business has already moved away from, producing volume against a referral supply that keeps shrinking.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #bert #word-embeddings #k-nearest-neighbors #gradient-boosting #evaluation-metrics #worker-facing #automation

## The Problem
The reporter has a quota, explicit or understood. Some number of stories a week, with traffic expectations attached, visible on a dashboard the whole newsroom can see.

Meeting it means volume, and volume means aggregation: rewriting what another outlet reported, covering the thing that is already trending, producing the service piece that ranks. Original reporting takes days and may produce less traffic than a post assembled in forty minutes.

The traffic itself has become unreliable in a way that is nobody's fault and everybody's problem. Search referrals fall when an algorithm changes or an AI summary answers the query. Social distribution is gone. A reporter's numbers can halve for reasons entirely external to their work, and the dashboard does not distinguish.

Layoffs have run through the industry repeatedly, and traffic numbers are widely understood to feature in those decisions, which changes how every reporter relates to the dashboard.

Meanwhile the work has expanded. Reporters are expected to promote on social, appear in video, write newsletters and engage with audiences, on top of reporting and writing.

And the beat knowledge — the sources, the context, the ability to recognise what matters — is the actual asset and is measured by nothing.

## Why It Matters to the Worker
Being measured on something outside your control is the core grievance. A reporter can write the best piece of their career and see it underperform because of a ranking change, and the number is what appears in the review.

The incentive conflicts with the craft. Everyone in the newsroom knows aggregation performs and original reporting matters, and the measurement rewards the first. That gap between what is valued and what is measured is corrosive over years.

Job insecurity is constant and the industry's contraction is not a rumour. Reporters make career decisions against a metric they distrust, at organisations that keep shrinking.

The expanded remit dilutes everything. Time spent on promotion and video is time not spent reporting, and the promotion is measured while the reporting is not.

And there is no recognition for the work that builds the audience. A reporter whose beat produces the loyal readers who subscribe is invisible in a pageview ranking.

## What a Solution Looks Like
Measure contribution to audience rather than traffic. Which reporters' work produces returning readers, newsletter signups, subscriptions and retention — this is computable from the reader record and would substantially reinvert the current ranking, which is precisely why it matters.

Separate external shifts from performance. A ranking change or a platform shift is detectable as a simultaneous break across many articles and outlets, and attributing a traffic decline to it rather than to the reporter is both accurate and humane.

Automate the research pass. Background assembly, prior coverage retrieval, document and filing search, and source material organisation are hours per story and are retrieval problems.

Handle the mechanical production. Headline variants, social copy, newsletter blurbs and metadata are generated work that currently consumes reporter time and produces no journalism.

Verification support. Fact checking against sources, quote verification and prior-correction cross-referencing reduce the error risk that reporters carry personally.

Make beat value visible. The audience a reporter's beat retains, the subscribers it holds, and the depth of engagement it produces are all measurable and none of them appear anywhere today.

## Impact If Solved
Reporters are measured on a collapsing external supply rather than on the audience they build, at organisations whose survival now depends on that audience. Measuring contribution to subscription and retention, attributing external shifts correctly and automating the research and production passes aligns the incentive with the business and returns hours to journalism.

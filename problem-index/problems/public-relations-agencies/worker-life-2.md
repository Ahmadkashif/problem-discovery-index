# The Crisis Night Shift

**Industry:** [[public-relations-agencies|Public Relations Agencies]]
**Type:** Worker Life Changing
**One-liner:** When a client is in crisis, a small agency team watches news and social feeds through the night, redrafts holding statements through legal review at 2 a.m., and has to decide alone whether a spike is the start of a story or noise.
**Tags:** #change-point-detection #time-series-forecasting #large-language-models #bert #worker-facing #workflow-orchestration #automation

## The Problem
A product recall, a data breach, a viral video of an employee, an executive's resignation, a short-seller report. The agency's crisis team (often a senior counsellor, an account director and two account executives) moves onto a rota. Someone watches the monitoring platform, the social listening dashboard and the major outlets every hour through the night and messages the senior counsellor when volume jumps or a national reporter calls. The holding statement is redrafted as facts change; each draft goes to the client's general counsel, who edits for liability, and back again. Reporter calls are logged by hand in a spreadsheet so the team knows who has been told what.

For a cyber incident at a listed client there is a statutory clock as well: once the company determines the incident is material, it has four business days to file a Form 8-K, and the media statement, customer notice and filing language must say the same thing.

## Why It Matters to the Worker
The person on the overnight watch carries a judgement nobody has trained them for: does this jump in mentions mean a national outlet is about to run the story, or is it a few accounts recycling yesterday's post? Waking the senior counsellor for noise costs credibility; failing to wake her for a real escalation costs the client. The decision is made tired, alone, from dashboards that show volume but not trajectory.

The rota also falls on top of normal client work, so the same people run a crisis by night and their other retainers by day. Crises cluster unpredictably, and the burnout figures that run through the industry are sharpest for those who are always on call.

## What a Solution Looks Like
A crisis desk that watches for the team: it tracks mention volume, the reach and type of accounts amplifying, reporter inquiries and pickup by outlets that historically lead to national coverage, and estimates the chance and timing of escalation with the reason attached. It wakes a human only when that estimate crosses a threshold the senior counsellor set. It keeps a single fact sheet that every draft statement is checked against, tracks which version legal approved, and logs every reporter interaction so the morning handover takes minutes.

## Impact If Solved
Removing most of the manual watching and reconciliation from a crisis night would let teams rotate one person rather than three through the overnight shift (estimate), cut false alarms to the senior counsellor, and make the escalation call on evidence rather than fatigue.

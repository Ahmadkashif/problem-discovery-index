# The Account Executive's Pitch Treadmill

**Industry:** [[public-relations-agencies|Public Relations Agencies]]
**Type:** Worker Life Changing
**One-liner:** A first- or second-year account executive spends most of the week building lists, sending pitches most reporters delete and chasing follow-ups, and gets almost no feedback on why any of it worked or failed.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #word-embeddings #k-nearest-neighbors #evaluation-metrics #worker-facing #automation #workflow-orchestration

## The Problem
An account executive at an independent agency typically works across four to eight client retainers (estimate). For each active announcement the routine is the same: pull a list from the media database, check that the reporters still cover the beat (many have moved, been laid off or gone freelance since the database last updated), write a pitch, personalise the opening line for the most important names, send, log it, follow up twice, and then search the monitoring feed each morning to see whether anything ran. Across clients that can be a hundred or more pitches in a busy week (estimate), most of them unanswered.

The rest of the week goes on the tasks around the pitch: entering hours in the timesheet against each client's retainer, building the weekly status report, pulling coverage for the monthly report, and drafting releases and social copy for review. Placements are what the account executive is judged on, but the inputs that decide them (the angle, the target, the timing) are mostly chosen above them.

## Why It Matters to the Worker
The job is high volume and low feedback. A pitch that is ignored teaches nothing: the account executive cannot tell whether the angle was weak, the reporter had moved on, the timing was wrong or the subject line never got opened. Senior colleagues who know the answers are busy and teach by occasional correction. Progress depends on luck in which accounts and mentors one gets.

The stress is measurable. PRSA cites research that 75% of agency PR staff rate their stress above the midpoint of a ten-point scale, higher than in-house teams, and Muck Rack's 2024 survey found half of PR professionals had considered leaving over burnout in the previous year. Junior staff absorb the volume and the rejection without the relationships that make senior work rewarding.

## What a Solution Looks Like
A pitching workspace that starts from the story, not the database: it proposes the ten reporters most likely to care, each with the evidence (what they wrote recently, how they responded to similar pitches, whether they are still on the beat) and the angle that fits them. It drafts personalised openings from each reporter's own recent work for the account executive to edit, tracks embargoes and follow-ups automatically, and closes the loop by showing which pitches led to coverage and what distinguished them. Coverage pulls and status reports draft themselves from the same record.

## Impact If Solved
Cutting list building, follow-up tracking and report assembly by half would return roughly a day a week to each account executive (estimate), and the feedback loop would shorten the years it takes to develop news judgement from accidental mentoring to deliberate practice.

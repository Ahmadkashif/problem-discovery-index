# Announcement Day Under Disclosure and Embargo Rules

**Industry:** [[public-relations-agencies|Public Relations Agencies]]
**Type:** Low Impact (Customisation Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Project tools and newswire schedulers already run launches; none of them knows the disclosure sequencing, embargo commitments and approval chain a public-company announcement has to follow to the minute.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #evaluation-metrics #compliance #workflow-orchestration #automation #data-integration

## The Problem
A listed client is announcing an acquisition, a leadership change, a product recall or quarterly results. The PR firm owns the communications run-of-show: the press release, the internal memo, customer and partner letters, social posts, executive talking points, media briefings under embargo, and the timing of every one of them relative to the wire. Each draft passes through legal, investor relations and the executive team, often in four or five rounds of tracked changes by email. Reporters given the story early have agreed embargo times that differ by outlet. The wire release must cross before any selective conversation with analysts or investors, and anything that leaks forces the whole sequence forward.

The work is handled with a shared spreadsheet, a Word document with a version number in its filename, and an account director who carries the sequence in her head. Errors are rare but expensive: an embargoed briefing that runs early, a social post scheduled before the release crosses, an outdated figure in the customer letter after legal changed it in the release.

## What Already Exists
PR Newswire, Business Wire and GlobeNewswire schedule distribution to the second and handle exchange-ready formatting. Asana, Monday and Workfront manage approval tasks. Muck Rack and Cision send embargoed pitches. Document collaboration in Word and Google Docs tracks changes. Disclosure management software used by legal and finance teams handles SEC filings.

## The Customisation Gap
What none of them carries is the announcement logic: which documents depend on which (a figure changed in the release must change everywhere it appears); which audiences may hear before others under the company's disclosure policy and Regulation FD; which reporter agreed to which embargo time and whether that still holds after a schedule slip; which exchange and wire deadlines apply to this market and time of day; and which approver has signed which version. For crisis disclosures the clock is statutory: a material cyber incident must be disclosed on Form 8-K within four business days of the materiality determination, and the media statement has to match the filing. The customisation is a dependency graph of documents, audiences and clocks, with consistency checks across drafts.

## Impact If Solved
A run-of-show that checks every draft against the latest approved facts and every send against the disclosure sequence would remove the most common announcement-day errors and the late-night reconciliation rounds that precede them, for agencies running dozens of listed-company announcements a year.

# The Care Agent and the Promo Credit That Fell Off

**Industry:** [[wireless-carriers|Wireless Carriers]]
**Type:** Worker Life Changing
**One-liner:** A carrier care agent spends much of each shift explaining why a customer's bill went up, usually a 36-month device credit that stopped after a plan or line change, and gets back clarity and a fair chance to help if the answer is assembled before they pick up.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #bert #evaluation-metrics #worker-facing #automation #workflow-orchestration

## The Problem
Carrier promotions now pay for phones through monthly bill credits over 24 to 36 months, conditional on keeping a qualifying plan, keeping the line active and sometimes trading in a specific device. Customers change plans, add and remove lines, move a phone to a new line or pay off a device early, and any of these can end a credit silently. The next bill is ~$20–30 higher (a typical monthly device credit, estimate), with prorated charges from a mid-cycle change on top.

The customer calls. The agent opens the billing system, the promotion engine, the device payment plan, the order history and the knowledge base, often five screens or more, and tries to work out which promotion the line had, under which version of the terms, which change broke eligibility, and whether policy allows a goodwill credit. A single call can run 15 to 20 minutes (estimate) and still end with the agent guessing. Meanwhile the agent is scored on handle time, first-call resolution, survey results and save rates.

## Why It Matters to the Worker
The agent absorbs the customer's anger at a decision made months ago by a promotion design they had no part in. When they can't find the reason, they choose between an unexplained credit that may be reversed in a quality review and a refusal that ends in a complaint or a port-out. Either way their metrics suffer for a problem they couldn't see.

The work is also hard to learn. Promotion terms change several times a year and old versions stay live on existing lines, so new agents rely on the few veterans who remember how last year's offer behaved. Turnover in carrier care is high, and every leaver takes some of that memory with them.

## What a Solution Looks Like
When the call routes, the agent's screen already shows a plain-language bill comparison: what changed between last month and this month, which promotion ended, which customer action ended it, quoting the exact term version that applied to the line, and what the customer would need to do to restore it if that is possible. It also shows what the policy allows, and the customer's churn risk and eligible offers if retention is the right move. The agent edits and explains rather than investigates.

## Impact If Solved
Cutting the investigation half of bill-change calls could take several minutes off each one (estimate) and lift first-call resolution. For the agent, the change is being able to give a correct answer with confidence, fewer escalations and reversed credits, and a shorter ramp for new hires. For the carrier, a customer who understands the bill is less likely to port out.

# The Representative Explaining a Bill the Customer Can't Pay

**Industry:** [[electric-utilities|Electric Distribution Utilities]]
**Type:** Worker Life Changing
**One-liner:** After a rate increase or a cold month, a utility customer service representative takes call after call from people angry or frightened about a high bill, with a handle-time target and no quick way to show why the bill rose or what help the caller qualifies for, and gets back the ability to actually help.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #gradient-boosting #feature-engineering #worker-facing #automation #compliance

## The Problem
Utilities asked commissions for record rate increases in 2025 (nearly $31B for electric and gas, per a PowerLines analysis), and the effect reaches the call centre a few months later. A representative takes a high-bill call. The customer insists their usage did not change. The representative opens the billing system, then the meter data system, then the rate schedule, trying to work out whether the cause was the new rate, the weather, an estimated read, a broken meter or real usage. Then comes the harder part: the customer is behind, a disconnection notice is coming, and the right answer depends on rules that vary by state and season. Those rules cover payment-arrangement options, budget billing, medical certificates, winter moratoria, energy-assistance programmes such as LIHEAP and the utility's own discount rate.

Each of these calls takes far longer than the average handle-time target, and the representative is measured on that target.

## Why It Matters to the Worker
This is emotional labour with little power. The representative takes the anger at a rate decision they had no part in, and hears from people choosing between electricity and food. They often cannot see what the customer is eligible for without transferring the call. Mistakes on disconnection rules expose the utility to commission complaints and the representative to discipline. Turnover in utility call centres is high (estimate), and new hires take months to learn the rules.

## What a Solution Looks Like
A bill-explanation and assistance assistant beside the representative. It reads the customer's interval meter data, weather and rate history and explains in plain language why this bill differs from last month's and last year's: rate change, weather-driven heating or cooling, estimated reads, or a usage change at specific hours. It flags a likely meter or read problem. It checks the account against payment-arrangement rules, assistance programmes and protections that apply in that state, and drafts the arrangement or application for the representative to confirm.

## Impact If Solved
High-bill calls resolved in one contact instead of two or three transfers and a callback. Fewer wrongful disconnections, and more eligible customers enrolled in assistance before arrears grow. Representatives would spend the call helping rather than searching.

# Tax-Credit Transfer Diligence Built From Paper Payrolls

**Industry:** [[renewable-power-producers|Renewable Power Producers]]
**Type:** Low Impact (Customisation Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Certified-payroll software already checks wages on public works, but selling a project's tax credits needs an apprenticeship-hours, begin-construction and foreign-content record that no payroll tool keeps, so each sale is diligenced from PDFs.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #feature-engineering #compliance #data-integration #workflow-orchestration #automation

## The Problem
A solar or storage project that sells its 48E or 45Y credits to a corporate buyer has to prove what the credit rests on. The full rate requires prevailing wages for every laborer and mechanic of every contractor and subcontractor during construction, and again for alteration and repair work after the project is placed in service. It requires registered apprentices for a set share of construction labour hours, with ratio checks and records of good-faith requests (15% for projects beginning construction after 2023, recalled). The begin-construction date now rests on physical work under Notice 2025-42, a domestic content bonus rests on cost percentages, and projects beginning construction from 2026 must pass the new foreign-entity material assistance test.

The buyer's counsel and tax insurer send a diligence list. The developer's tax director then spends weeks collecting weekly certified payrolls from 30 to 60 subcontractors (estimate) in PDF, Excel and scanned paper, matching each worker's craft classification to the county wage determination, totalling apprentice hours by facility, and tracing the physical-work evidence. Gaps surface late, when they cost correction payments, an escrow holdback or an exclusion on the tax insurance policy. Transfers reached $21B in the first half of 2026 (Crux), so this exercise now runs on hundreds of projects a year.

## What Already Exists
Certified payroll platforms built for Davis-Bacon public works, such as LCPtracker and Elations, collect weekly payrolls from subcontractors and check rates against wage determinations. Data rooms such as Datasite and Intralinks hold the documents. Big Four and boutique tax advisers run prevailing wage reviews for a fee, marketplaces such as Crux and Reunion standardise diligence request lists, and tax insurers price the remaining risk.

## The Customisation Gap
Davis-Bacon tools were built for a contracting officer checking wages on a federal job, not for a credit buyer testing a tax position. The credit-specific logic is: apprenticeship labour-hour percentages computed per facility across every contractor, with ratio tests and request logs from registered programmes; correction and penalty amounts computed for each shortfall; alteration and repair coverage after placed-in-service, which means O&M contractors' payrolls for years; begin-construction evidence and the four-year continuity window; domestic content cost shares for steel, iron and manufactured products; the foreign-entity cost ratio; and every result exported in the buyer's diligence format, tranche by tranche, with each figure traced to its source page.

## Impact If Solved
On a $40M credit sale, a 5% escrow held back for prevailing wage gaps ties up $2M for years (estimate). A record built during construction rather than reconstructed at sale could cut diligence from ~8 weeks to ~2 and shrink or remove that holdback.

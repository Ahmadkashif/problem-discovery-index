# The Portfolio Company CFO Answering the Sponsor

**Industry:** [[private-equity-firms|Private Equity Firms]]
**Type:** Worker Life Changing
**One-liner:** A portfolio company CFO gets back the week a month spent producing the sponsor's package, the lender's package and the board's ad hoc requests from the same ledger in three formats.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #feature-engineering #time-series-forecasting #evaluation-metrics #worker-facing #data-integration #automation

## The Problem
A sponsor-backed company's CFO, typically running a finance team of three to ten, owes monthly reporting to the sponsor, a covenant compliance certificate to the lender, a board deck each quarter, a 13-week cash flow when things are tight, and an unending series of ad hoc requests from deal partners and operating partners — "can you split gross margin by customer cohort", "what does the add-on contribute on a pro forma basis". Each consumer wants the same underlying numbers in a different definition and layout. Many of these companies were founder-owned two years earlier and their systems (QuickBooks, an older mid-market ERP, a stack of spreadsheets) were never built for this cadence.

## Why It Matters to the Worker
The CFO was hired to professionalise the business and spends the first week of every month as a reporting service bureau for the owner. The requests arrive without priority and with an implied threat — sponsor confidence is the CFO's job security, and PE-backed CFO turnover is notoriously high. Every definition mismatch between the sponsor's KPI and the company's own becomes a credibility question for the person producing it.

## What a Solution Looks Like
One mapped data model from the company's ledger and operational systems to each consumer's definition — sponsor KPI template, credit-agreement EBITDA, board deck — maintained once and refreshed automatically; ad hoc requests answered from that model with the definition and calculation shown, so the CFO reviews rather than builds; and a standing reconciliation between the three versions of EBITDA that explains every difference in one table.

## Impact If Solved
Returning several days a month to a portfolio CFO is time spent on cash, pricing and systems — the work that actually creates the value the sponsor is paying for — and removes one of the commonest sources of friction between sponsors and the executives they hired.

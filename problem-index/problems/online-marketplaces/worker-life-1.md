# Trust and Safety Reviewer

**Industry:** [[online-marketplaces|Online Marketplaces]]
**Type:** Worker Life Changing
**One-liner:** Trust and safety reviewers make consequential judgement calls at high volume on the cases automation could not decide, which means every case they see is genuinely ambiguous and the metrics treat them as interchangeable units of throughput.
**Tags:** #cnns #bert #large-language-models #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #hypothesis-testing #worker-facing #compliance

## The Problem
A marketplace's trust and safety operation removes counterfeits, prohibited items, fraudulent listings and abusive behaviour. Automation handles the clear cases, which is most of the volume, and everything ambiguous reaches a human.

That selection makes the queue uniquely difficult. Every case a reviewer sees is one the system could not decide. Is this a counterfeit or a genuine item photographed badly. Is this listing for a legitimate collectible or a prohibited item. Is this seller's behaviour a scam pattern or an unusual but legitimate business.

The reviewer has a policy document, the listing, some seller history and a target handle time measured in seconds or a couple of minutes. Their decision suspends someone's income or lets a harmful listing stand.

Policies do not cover the edges, which is why the case arrived. So reviewers develop informal norms that vary by team and by individual, and nothing measures the resulting inconsistency.

Certain queues carry additional weight: reviewers in some categories encounter content depicting harm, and marketplace operations have generally not built the protections that dedicated content moderation has begun to adopt.

## Why It Matters to the Worker
The work is consequential and measured as throughput. Reviewers are evaluated on cases per hour and accuracy against a quality sample, which pushes toward speed on decisions that deserve care, and the tension is unresolvable at the individual level.

The moral weight is real. A reviewer knows that suspending a seller may end a small business and that failing to remove a fraudulent listing will cost a buyer money. Both errors are theirs, at volume, all day.

Policy ambiguity is the daily frustration. Reviewers are held to a standard the policy does not clearly define, and when a decision is overturned on appeal, the feedback is that they were wrong rather than that the policy was unclear.

There is no path from the queue to the policy. Reviewers see the edges constantly and know exactly where the rules fail, and most operations have no channel for that observation.

## What a Solution Looks Like
Decision support rather than a verdict. Surfacing what a reviewer needs — comparable past decisions on similar cases, the seller's full history, the specific policy clauses that apply, what similar listings were decided and how — turns a judgement from memory into a judgement from evidence.

Consistency measurement, run deliberately. Sending the same case to multiple reviewers periodically produces a disagreement rate, and clusters of disagreement identify policy defects rather than reviewer defects.

Policy gap detection from the queue itself. Cases that generate disagreement, appeals or overturns cluster around specific policy language, and identifying that language is the highest-value output the queue produces.

Routing by difficulty and by reviewer strength, so genuinely hard cases go to experienced reviewers with more time, rather than distributing everything uniformly against a single handle-time target.

Exposure management for the queues that carry harmful content, including limits, rotation and support, adopted from content moderation practice rather than reinvented.

## Impact If Solved
Trust and safety decisions determine whether a marketplace is safe to buy on and whether sellers are treated fairly, and they are made under throughput pressure on cases automation deliberately declined. Decision support and policy gap detection improve consistency directly and turn the queue into the mechanism that fixes the rules rather than absorbing their failures.

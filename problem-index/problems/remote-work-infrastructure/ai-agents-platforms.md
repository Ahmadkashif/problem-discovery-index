## 1. Auditable Compliance Platform
#ai-platform #bert #large-language-models #change-point-detection #graph-neural-networks #confidence-intervals #compliance #evaluation-metrics

**Concept:** A platform that converts a compliance tick into evidence. Every determination carries the rule applied, its source, the date it was last verified and the facts it was applied to — which is what a client will actually need if an authority asks three years later. It monitors official legislative, regulatory and case law sources across jurisdictions continuously, detects material changes, and links each one to the rule entries, determinations, clients and payroll calculations it affects, turning a detected change into a remediation list rather than an update note. And it assesses classification on how an engagement actually operates rather than on what the contract says.

**Inputs:** Official publication sources per jurisdiction; the internal rule base with dependency structure and verification dates; structured engagement facts — control, exclusivity, integration, equipment, hours; client headcount and activity by country; historical dispute outcomes.

**Outputs / Actions:** Determinations with rule provenance, vintage and reasoning attached. Staleness made visible, so re-verification is prioritised by risk rather than by whoever asks. Change detection measured on recall, since a missed change becomes a liability years later while a false positive costs a specialist minutes. Continuously monitored permanent establishment exposure that updates as roles and headcount change, rather than a determination made once at onboarding. And classification risk positions that will sometimes return uncomfortable answers, which is the point of assessing facts rather than paperwork.

**Why now:** Distributed hiring has scaled faster than the compliance apparatus supporting it, the liability lands on clients years later, and multilingual legal source monitoring across heterogeneous formats has only recently become tractable.

**Market:** Employer-of-record and global employment platforms, the enterprises building distributed workforces on their assertions, and the tax and employment advisors who are called when a determination fails.

## 2. Payroll Integrity Platform
#ai-platform #gradient-boosting #change-point-detection #hypothesis-testing #confidence-intervals #compliance #automation #evaluation-metrics

**Concept:** A verification layer over multi-jurisdiction payroll. It recomputes payslips independently against the rule base, detects anomalies in individual components against the employee's own history and against peers in the same jurisdiction and salary band, and flags before payment rather than after — because a correction across borders is slow and a pay error is a direct personal harm rather than an accounting one. It automates retroactive recalculation when rules apply retrospectively, which is well-defined, laborious and entirely mechanical. And it separates benefit compliance from benefit competitiveness, which clients routinely conflate when making offers in unfamiliar markets.

**Inputs:** Payslip components and their derivation; the rule base with rates, bases, caps and thresholds; employee contracts and history; peer distributions across the platform's client base by jurisdiction and band; rule change effective dates including retrospective ones; local market compensation data.

**Outputs / Actions:** Pre-payment error detection with precision high enough that a team working to a hard cycle deadline does not bypass it. Automated retrospective recalculation. Statutory minimum and local market competitiveness reported as two distinct answers, since a package can be compliant and locally unattractive or unintentionally generous. Anomaly detection that is genuinely independent of the payroll engine, since reimplementing the same logic against the same interpretation reproduces the same errors.

**Why now:** The number of jurisdictions a typical company now pays into has grown substantially, rule change velocity has not fallen, and the errors are silent — a wrong contribution base produces a plausible number that surfaces as a liability or as an employee's shortfall.

**Market:** Employer-of-record and global payroll platforms, in-country payroll providers, and the finance functions carrying multi-jurisdiction payroll exposure.

## 3. Worker Entitlement and Work Visibility Agent
#ai-agent #large-language-models #bert #gradient-boosting #confidence-intervals #compliance #worker-facing #causal-inference

**Concept:** An agent serving the two people this industry currently serves least. For the worker employed through an entity they have never heard of, it derives their actual entitlement position — notice period, accrued severance, statutory leave, benefits held, contributions made — from their specific contract and local law, stated plainly in their own language, and answers their questions with grounding rather than generic portal guidance. It notifies them when a local rule change alters an entitlement, which the platform will know and they will not, and it documents a functional grievance route for the case where the problem is a manager who is not their employer. Separately it offers the alternative to activity monitoring: output-based measurement drawn from the systems where work actually lands.

**Inputs:** Individual contracts and engagement terms; jurisdictional employment law and its changes; contribution and leave records; the platform's determinations relevant to the worker; domain delivery systems — version control, ticketing, pipeline, delivery records; monitoring deployment variation across teams.

**Outputs / Actions:** A plain-language personal entitlement position that most workers in this arrangement currently cannot obtain. Grounded answers to statutory questions against a specific contract, with abstention where the answer requires legal advice. Change notifications on entitlements. Team-level output measures reported with uncertainty — never individual productivity scores derived from partial domain signals, which would repeat the proxy problem in a new form. And the study nobody runs: whether monitoring deployment improves delivery outcomes, compared across teams that have it and teams that do not.

**Why now:** A growing population is employed through arrangements whose entitlements they cannot see, and workforce monitoring has been deployed widely on an evidence base that a straightforward internal comparison would test — which is exactly why the comparison is not run.

**Market:** Employer-of-record platforms differentiating on worker experience, works councils and worker representatives in jurisdictions with consultation rights, and the employers who would rather measure delivered work than keystrokes.

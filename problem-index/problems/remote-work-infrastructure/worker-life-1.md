# The Employee Whose Employer Is a Company They Have Never Heard Of

**Industry:** [[remote-work-infrastructure|Remote Work Infrastructure]]
**Type:** Worker Life Changing
**One-liner:** Legally employed by a local entity, actually working for a company abroad, and unsure who to ask about notice periods, benefits or what happens if the contract ends.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #bert #gradient-boosting #confidence-intervals #evaluation-metrics #compliance #worker-facing #graph-neural-networks

## The Problem
Under an employer-of-record arrangement the worker's legal employer is a local entity belonging to the platform. Their actual working life — their manager, their team, their objectives, their culture — belongs to the client company abroad. The two are separate and the separation is where the worker's difficulties arise.

Questions have no obvious destination. What is my notice period. What happens to my statutory benefits if the client ends the contract. Am I accruing severance entitlement. Is my pension contribution being made. Who do I raise a grievance with if the problem is my manager at the client, who is not my employer. The platform's support handles payroll and administrative matters; the client's human resources function does not employ them.

Termination is where the arrangement is most exposed. If the client stops the engagement, the employment relationship with the local entity ends, and what the worker is entitled to depends on local law applied by an entity whose commercial relationship is with the client rather than with them. Whether that produces the protection local employment law intends is a real question and it varies.

Career progression is structurally awkward. The worker is not on the client's headcount, may be excluded from equity, promotion cycles and internal mobility, and is frequently treated as a category apart by systems and by colleagues.

And the contractor variant is worse in every respect: no employment protections at all, classification that may not survive examination, and payment terms set by a platform whose customer is the other party.

## Why It Matters to the Worker
This is employment with the entitlements intact on paper and the relationship fragmented in practice. The worker is protected by local law in principle and has to navigate it alone, across a language and institutional boundary, against parties whose interests are aligned with each other.

The information asymmetry is the daily form of it. Statutory entitlements are genuinely complex and the worker's sources are a platform portal written for administrators and a client that is not their employer. Many workers in this arrangement do not know their notice period or their severance position.

The exclusion is felt continuously. Being administratively distinct from the team you work with, missing from internal systems, and outside the progression mechanisms, shapes how people are treated in ways that accumulate.

And the precarity is real. An engagement that ends at the client's convenience terminates an employment relationship, and the worker's recourse runs through an entity that has no ongoing interest in them.

## What a Solution Looks Like
Give the worker their own entitlement position. What their notice period is, what severance they have accrued, what benefits they hold, what statutory leave they have, what contributions have been made — stated plainly in their own language, derived from their actual contract and local law rather than from generic guidance. This is computable and is not currently provided.

Answer their questions properly. Statutory entitlement questions in a specific jurisdiction against a specific contract are exactly what a well-grounded retrieval system can handle, and the current alternative is a portal article or nobody.

Make the grievance route explicit and functional. Who to raise what with, and what happens when the issue is with the client rather than the employer, should be documented and operational rather than a gap between two organisations.

Notify on change. When local law changes an entitlement — a statutory minimum, a leave provision, a notice requirement — the affected workers should be told, since the platform will know and the worker will not.

And address the exclusion. Inclusion in the client's internal systems, progression processes and equity where lawful is a client-side decision that platforms could make easy and mostly do not.

## Impact If Solved
A growing population is employed through arrangements they do not understand, with entitlements they cannot see, and no clear party responsible for answering them. A plain-language personal entitlement position and a grounded answer service are both straightforward to build from information the platform already holds — and making the grievance route functional addresses the gap between two organisations that currently falls entirely on the person in it.

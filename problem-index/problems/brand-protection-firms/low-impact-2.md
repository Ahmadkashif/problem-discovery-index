# Enforcement Routing and Notice Operations

**Industry:** [[brand-protection-firms|Brand Protection Firms]]
**Type:** Low Impact (Customisation Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Every platform has its own notice process, evidence standard and response time, and the choice of which lever to pull is made by whichever is easiest rather than by which works.

## The Problem
Once something is detected, enforcement means submitting a notice through the relevant platform's process. Each is different: its own form, evidence requirements, rights verification, response times, counter-notice handling and repeat-infringer policy. A brand protection firm operates dozens of these simultaneously.

The operational work is substantial and largely clerical — assembling evidence in the required format, submitting, tracking, escalating, handling counter-notices, and recording the outcome. It scales linearly with volume, which is why volume-priced contracts push toward the cheapest notice type.

The routing decision is where the value is and it is made by habit. Listing removal, seller suspension, domain action, payment processor complaint, logistics interdiction, app store removal and registrar action have different costs, different success rates and very different effects on whether the operator continues — and the choice is usually the one the platform process makes easy.

Evidence standards vary in ways that matter. Some platforms accept an assertion of rights; others require a test purchase, a comparison, or a legal declaration. Knowing what will succeed where, at what evidentiary cost, is operational knowledge held by experienced staff.

And counter-notices are where the process turns adversarial. A counter-notice from a legitimate seller should be resolved quickly and correctly, and the incentives of a volume-priced programme point toward defending the original notice.

## What Already Exists
Firms operate notice management systems tracking submissions and outcomes across platforms. Platform programmes provide APIs and bulk submission for enrolled brands. Legal templates and rights documentation are maintained per jurisdiction. Test purchase operations supply evidence for platforms that require it. Escalation relationships with platform teams exist for the larger programmes and are a genuine asset.

## The Customisation Gap
Routing should be learned from outcomes. Which enforcement type succeeds on which platform against which operator profile, at what cost and with what effect on operator survival, is estimable from the firm's own history — and would replace habit with a policy that targets the lever most likely to remove the operator rather than the listing.

Evidence assembly should be automatic and platform-specific. The evidence a given platform requires is known, and assembling it from the detection record in the required format is mechanical work that currently consumes the operations team.

Counter-notice handling needs to be treated as a quality signal rather than as an obstacle. A counter-notice rate by detection type is a direct measure of the programme's false positive rate, and a firm that tracked it would know something about its own accuracy that nobody currently publishes.

And repeat-infringer policies are the underused lever. Most platforms have one, most are triggered by accumulation, and a firm that sequenced its notices to trigger them — rather than spreading notices across accounts — would use the platform's own mechanism to remove operators rather than listings.

## Impact If Solved
Notice operations are the cost centre of a brand protection programme and the routing decision is where its effectiveness is actually determined. Outcome-learned routing, automated platform-specific evidence assembly and deliberate use of repeat-infringer policies would shift enforcement from the layer that is easy to the layer that works — and counter-notice tracking would give the industry its first internal measure of how often it is wrong.

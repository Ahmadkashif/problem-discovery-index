# The Brand Manager Handling the Reseller Who Was Wrongly Removed

**Industry:** [[brand-protection-firms|Brand Protection Firms]]
**Type:** Worker Life Changing
**One-liner:** The vendor's report says four thousand takedowns, an authorised reseller has been suspended by mistake, and the brand's own manager is the one who has to fix it and justify the spend.

## The Problem
An in-house brand protection manager owns the programme: the vendor relationship, the budget, the reporting to leadership, and the consequences. The vendor reports volume, and the manager must convert it into a statement about whether the brand is better off — which the data does not support.

The escalations are the daily reality. A legitimate reseller has been taken down, a repair business has had its listings removed, a retailer with grey-market stock has complained through the commercial team, and occasionally a critic or a parody account has been actioned, which is a reputational problem rather than a commercial one. Each arrives as an angry contact and the manager investigates, reverses where appropriate, and apologises.

The internal position is difficult. Finance wants to know what the spend achieves; the manager has takedown counts. Commercial teams resent enforcement that hits their own distribution partners. Legal wants the programme to be defensible. Nobody is satisfied by the available evidence.

And the manager knows the fundamental thing: the operators come back. They see the same images reappear under new accounts, month after month, and report a rising takedown number as progress.

## Why It Matters to the Worker
This is a role required to justify spending with evidence that does not exist, while absorbing the complaints generated by its own programme's errors. Both halves are structural and neither is the manager's fault.

The reseller and partner escalations are particularly corrosive because they damage relationships the brand depends on. A wrongly-actioned authorised reseller is a commercial relationship harmed by a programme meant to protect the brand, and the manager mediates that repeatedly.

The measurement gap makes the role professionally weak. Without an effect measure, every budget conversation is a defence of a number the manager privately distrusts, and there is no way to argue for a different enforcement strategy — payment disruption, supply-source action — because there is no evidence that the current one is failing.

And the parody and criticism cases carry a different kind of discomfort. Enforcement against legitimate speech is a reputational risk and an ethical one, and it happens through automation the brand commissioned.

## What a Solution Looks Like
Give the manager a supply measure. Standing infringing supply over time, operator counts and operator survival after enforcement are the numbers that describe the problem rather than the activity, and they let the manager make an argument about strategy rather than volume.

Protect the partner channel explicitly. Authorised resellers, distribution partners and known legitimate secondary sellers should be an allow-list maintained as a first-class part of the programme, with any action against them requiring explicit review — which removes the most damaging category of error outright.

Classify legitimate use deliberately. Resale, repair, compatible parts, parody and criticism are lawful and identifiable, and a programme configured to exclude them rather than to catch everything is a policy choice the brand can make and currently does not know it is not making.

Track and report the error rate. Counter-notices, appeals and reversals by category, reported by the vendor as a standard metric, is the accountability the contract currently lacks — and a vendor unwilling to report it is telling the brand something.

And align the contract to effect. Pricing on outcomes rather than notice volume is the structural fix, and it requires the supply measure to exist first.

## Impact If Solved
The brand-side manager is accountable for a programme whose effect is unmeasured and whose errors they personally absorb. A supply and operator-survival measure gives them an argument about strategy; an enforced partner allow-list and deliberate legitimate-use classification remove the most damaging errors; and a reported error rate makes the vendor accountable for precision rather than only for volume.

# The Adjuster's Read of Where a Liability Claim Is Headed

**Industry:** [[property-casualty-insurers|Property & Casualty Insurers]]
**Type:** High Impact
**One-liner:** An experienced bodily injury adjuster can tell within days which claims will draw an attorney, build up treatment and end in a policy-limits demand, but that read is recorded only as a reserve number, graded years later in aggregate, and lost when the adjuster leaves.
**Tags:** #tacit-knowledge-ml #large-language-models #transformers #gradient-boosting #survival-analysis #evaluation-metrics #worker-facing #revenue-impact

## The Problem
A commercial auto claim arrives: a box truck rear-ended a sedan at a light in a large metro county. The facts in the system are thin. There is a first notice from the driver, a police report, two photos and a phone number for the claimant. Within a week the assigned bodily injury adjuster has spoken to the claimant, seen whether a letter of representation arrived, noticed which clinic the claimant went to first and which law firm's name appears, and weighed the county the suit would be filed in. On that basis the adjuster sets a case reserve and decides how hard to push for an early settlement.

The best adjusters are right far more often than their files suggest. They recognise a soft-tissue claim treated at a clinic that refers to the same three firms. They notice a claimant who stops answering once an attorney is involved, and a lien pattern that predicts surgery. They know the venues where juries anchor on the trucking company rather than the injury. Asked how they know, they say they have seen this claim before. This is tacit knowledge in the strict sense: pattern recognition built over thousands of files that the adjuster cannot write down as rules, and that a claims manual reduces to "evaluate exposure promptly".

The read matters because the claim's path is set early. A claim that settles before an attorney is retained typically closes for a fraction of one that does (estimate). A claim mis-reserved low drifts for a year or more, then jumps when a demand letter arrives, and the jump appears as adverse development in the actuarial review. Liability lines carried that drift in 2025. Other liability occurrence and commercial auto liability still developed adversely by about $7.3B and $1.8B while personal lines released reserves (NAIC). Nuclear verdicts of $10M or more against corporate defendants rose 40.7% to nearly 200 (Marathon Strategies). Every carrier now says it wants to find the claims that will escalate. Most still rely on the same signal they always had: whichever adjuster happened to be assigned.

## Why It's Unsolved
The data collection problem is that the read is never captured as a read. The claim system records a reserve figure, a few file notes written in shorthand, and the payments. It does not record what the adjuster suspected, how confident they were, or which cue moved them. Asking adjusters to record that is a tax on people already carrying 100 to 175 open files (estimate). A note written after the claim escalates is hindsight, not a prediction.

The labelling problem has two layers. The outcome — attorney representation, suit, verdict, ultimate paid — arrives months to years later and is shaped by the handling itself. A claim the adjuster correctly flagged and settled early never shows its escalation, so the best interventions erase their own labels. Experts also disagree with themselves. The same adjuster reserves the same fact pattern differently after a recent bad verdict, and two senior adjusters given the same file will often set reserves far apart (estimate). Any model trained on reserves learns the department's habits, not the truth. It has to be trained on ultimates, with the handling decisions treated as interventions.

The deployment problem is speed and trust. The read is valuable in the first one to three weeks (estimate), before a letter of representation arrives, so a model that scores a claim after the demand is useless. Adjusters will not follow a score they cannot interrogate, and claims leaders worry that an algorithm steering settlement could look like an unfair claims practice. Defence-counsel communications enter the file once suit is filed and carry privilege, so the richest late-stage evidence cannot be pooled freely. A tool has to be faster than the adjuster's own instinct, show its reasons in the adjuster's vocabulary, and leave the decision with a licensed person.

## What a Solution Looks Like
A claim-trajectory record first, a model second. Every liability claim gets a short structured read at day three, day ten and day thirty. The system drafts it from the file — notes, recorded-statement transcript, police report, medical bills, correspondence — and the adjuster confirms or corrects it in seconds: likelihood of representation, expected treatment path, venue risk, the specific cue that worries them, and their confidence. Reads are graded later against what happened, adjusted for the handling the read triggered.

On top of that record, learn the reads of the adjusters whose early calls prove right. Pair file text and structured features with senior adjusters' annotations and the eventual outcome. Then surface to every adjuster the escalation cues the best ones respond to, with retrieval of similar closed claims ("the last eight claims from this clinic with this firm"), a calibrated probability of representation and of a limits demand, and a suggested reserve range with its reasons. Route the highest-risk claims to early-resolution specialists before the letter of representation arrives.

## Impact If Solved
In 2025 other liability occurrence and commercial auto liability developed adversely by ~$7.3B and ~$1.8B (NAIC), about $9.1B in a year when personal lines released reserves. If earlier identification moved even a tenth of that development into adequate initial reserves or early settlements (estimate), carriers would book ~$0.9B a year less adverse development on the lines where social inflation is concentrated, and cut severity on the claims settled before an attorney is retained. Keeping the read of a senior adjuster through a retirement turns the industry's most expensive knowledge loss into something the claims department keeps.

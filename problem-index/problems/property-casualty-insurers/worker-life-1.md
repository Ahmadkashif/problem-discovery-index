# The Premium Auditor in the Insured's Back Office

**Industry:** [[property-casualty-insurers|Property & Casualty Insurers]]
**Type:** Worker Life Changing
**One-liner:** A field premium auditor spends the day on the road and in small-business back offices, sorting payroll records into classification codes for an owner who knows the audit will end in a bill, and gets back time, fewer confrontations and calls they can defend.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #k-nearest-neighbors #feature-engineering #worker-facing #automation #compliance

## The Problem
Workers' compensation and general liability premiums are set at inception on the insured's estimate of payroll or sales, and settled after the policy expires by a premium audit. A carrier's field auditor visits the business, or chases it remotely, for payroll registers, quarterly tax returns, general ledgers, 1099s for subcontractors and certificates of insurance proving those subcontractors were covered. Then comes the judgement. Every employee's pay is assigned to a classification code: the roofer who also does office work, the restaurant cook who makes deliveries, the clerical exception the owner swears applies. Uninsured subcontractors are added to payroll, and overtime premium pay is excluded where the rules allow.

An auditor works a monthly quota of ~60–100 audits (estimate). Many involve a small-business owner with records in a box, a bookkeeper who is out that day, and a strong interest in the result coming out low. Carriers want audits completed within a set window after expiry (~60–90 days, estimate), because the additional premium cannot be billed until the audit is done.

## Why It Matters to the Worker
The job is lonely and confrontational. The auditor is the person who tells a contractor in a bad year that they owe thousands more in premium, and who has to stand behind a classification call when the insured disputes it with the carrier, the agent or the state rating bureau. Much of the day goes to driving and to re-keying numbers from paper and PDFs into the audit system, not to the classification judgement the job actually depends on.

The judgement is also exposed. Two auditors can classify the same employee differently. A disputed audit lands on the auditor's record, and the carrier's quality reviewers check the classification, not the hour spent finding the 1099s.

## What a Solution Looks Like
The insured uploads records to a secure portal before the visit or instead of it. Payroll registers, tax forms and ledgers are read automatically and reconciled to each other. Each employee and subcontractor gets a proposed classification, with the job description and similar past audits that support it. Certificates of insurance are checked against the subcontractor list, and variances from the policy estimate are flagged before the auditor arrives. The auditor reviews, adjusts and explains rather than transcribes, and the report carries the reasoning the dispute desk will need.

## Impact If Solved
Auditors spend fewer hours driving and keying and more of the day on the classification calls they are paid for. More audits can be done remotely, disputes can be settled from the documented reasoning, and carriers bill the earned premium weeks sooner (estimate).

# The Reserving Actuary Before 1 March

**Industry:** [[property-casualty-insurers|Property & Casualty Insurers]]
**Type:** Worker Life Changing
**One-liner:** From year-end close to the 1 March opinion, a P&C reserving actuary spends most of the hours rebuilding triangles and chasing claims for explanations, and the least on the judgement they will personally sign.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #descriptive-statistics #bayesian-inference #confidence-intervals #worker-facing #automation #data-integration

## The Problem
Every P&C carrier must file a Statement of Actuarial Opinion on its loss and loss-adjustment reserves with the annual statement by 1 March. The confidential Actuarial Opinion Summary, with the actuary's range and point estimate against the carried reserve, goes to the domiciliary state by 15 March. The appointed actuary signs personally. Before signing, the reserving team pulls year-end paid and incurred data and rebuilds the loss triangles for every line and accident year. They reconcile the triangles to the general ledger and Schedule P, and run chain-ladder, Bornhuetter–Ferguson and frequency–severity methods. Then they choose their selections.

The data is the bottleneck. Claims departments change case-reserving practice without telling anyone, so a strengthening in commercial auto looks like adverse development. A large claim reopens. A system migration moves claims between lines. The actuary emails claims managers asking why incurred losses on one accident year jumped. The answers come back as anecdotes in early February, when the selections have to be locked.

## Why It Matters to the Worker
The opinion carries the actuary's credential and name. A reserve that later proves deficient is reviewed by regulators, auditors and sometimes a court, and the appointed actuary must have documented why each selection was reasonable. The weeks before 1 March are long and repetitive: the same reconciliations as last year, the same data breaks to trace, the same memo sections to update. The interesting part is reading the claims environment correctly — knowing that this year's jump is a reserving-practice change, not a loss trend. It gets squeezed into the last days.

Junior actuaries spend their early careers on the data work. Many learn the judgement late, from the one senior colleague who knows the history of every line.

## What a Solution Looks Like
Triangles rebuild themselves at close and reconcile automatically to the ledger and Schedule P, with breaks traced to the claims that caused them. Large movements arrive annotated: the claims behind the jump, the adjusters' own notes on why reserves changed, and a flag when a change in case-reserve adequacy rather than a change in losses is the likely cause. The actuary starts February with diagnostics already written. They review a draft memo that records the history behind each selection, so that history no longer lives in one senior person's head.

## Impact If Solved
The reserving team gets back weeks of the year-end window (estimate) to spend on the selections the actuary signs. Juniors learn the judgement from documented reasoning rather than folklore, and opinions rest on a reconciled, explained data trail.

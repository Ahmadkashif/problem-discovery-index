# The Inside Sales Rep's Hundred Dials

**Industry:** [[sports-teams|Sports Teams & Clubs]]
**Type:** Worker Life Changing
**One-liner:** An entry-level ticket sales rep makes about a hundred calls a day to a list that is mostly cold, on low base pay and a quota, and would get back time, earnings and dignity from a list that knows who is likely to buy.
**Tags:** #gradient-boosting #logistic-regression #large-language-models #feature-engineering #worker-facing #revenue-impact

## The Problem
Most big-league clubs and many minor-league and college departments hire new sales reps into an inside-sales programme: a cohort of recent graduates on a base salary plus commission, selling mini-plans, group outings and new season-ticket memberships. The working day is a dial count. Reps make ~80–120 calls a day (estimate) from lists pulled from the CRM: single-game buyers, people who once filled in a contest form, lapsed members, businesses near the stadium. Most calls go to voicemail. Those that connect often reach someone who bought one ticket three years ago for a friend's birthday.

The data that would rank the list exists in pieces. The ticketing platform holds purchase and scan history. The CRM records email clicks and website visits, and the resale marketplace shows whether a buyer resells. Few clubs join them into a score the rep can act on. The managers set the order by recency or by territory.

## Why It Matters to the Worker
The job is the industry's most common entry point and one of its most draining. Base pay is low, the commission depends on conversions the rep cannot control, and the daily rejection rate is very high. Many reps leave within a year or two, and the industry's habit of treating that churn as normal hides what it costs the reps themselves. Reps who do well are often the ones handed the warmer lists, and the others conclude they are not good at the job.

Time is the other loss. Every hour spent dialling people with no intent to buy is an hour not spent with the buyers who would have taken a plan if called the week after they attended a game.

## What a Solution Looks Like
A ranked daily list, with the reason for each name. The system scores every contact on likelihood to buy a plan or group outing in the next 30 days, from attendance, scan and resale history, email and web engagement, and the club's schedule (a buyer who just attended a walk-off win is warmer). It drafts a two-line call note for each: "attended three Friday games this year, bought in the left-field reserved section, kids' club member". Reps see their conversion against a fair baseline for the list they were given, not raw dials. Season-ticket renewal risk, which a different team handles, stays in the renewal tools covered at [[niches/ticketing-agencies/season-ticket-renewals/profile|Season-Ticket & Membership Ticketing]].

## Impact If Solved
If ranked lists cut the dials needed per sale by a third (estimate), a rep reaches quota in fewer hours, earns more commission on the same effort and has time for the in-person seat visits that close larger plans. For a cohort of 20 reps that is thousands of hours a season moved from voicemail to selling.

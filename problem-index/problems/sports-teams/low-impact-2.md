# Sponsorship Entitlements Tracked in Spreadsheets

**Industry:** [[sports-teams|Sports Teams & Clubs]]
**Type:** Low Impact (Customisation Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A team's partnership contracts promise hundreds of specific deliverables, from LED rotation minutes to PA reads and suite nights, and the activation staff track them in spreadsheets and rebuild the proof by hand for every recap deck.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #object-detection #data-integration #workflow-orchestration #automation #revenue-impact

## The Problem
A big-league club carries ~80–150 corporate partners (estimate), each on a contract listing its entitlements. Contracts specify things like 30 seconds of LED ribbon rotation per half-inning, a logo on the concourse, two PA reads a game, four social posts a month, a suite for six games, a theme night and category exclusivity in the stadium. The partnership activation staff schedule those deliverables across 41 to 81 home games, capture photos and screenshots as proof, and at season end build a recap deck for each partner with impressions, media value and photos.

The work is spread across tools that do not talk to each other. The contract is a PDF and the entitlement list is a spreadsheet, while the LED schedule sits in the video board system and the posts in the social tool. Measured media value comes from a vendor report. When a deliverable is missed, a rainout or a rotated board, the makegood is negotiated by email, and the recap rarely shows it. Sponsors' finance teams now ask whether the money moved their business. Bain's 2026 survey of 1,397 marketing and finance executives found only 41% of marketers feel equipped to tie performance to business outcomes.

## What Already Exists
Sponsorship and fan-data platforms such as KORE (part of Two Circles since 2024) manage partner records and fulfilment workflows. SponsorUnited sells deal intelligence and a proposal evaluator, while Relo Metrics, Nielsen Sports and KORE's Hookit measure logo exposure and social media value. Salesforce and Dynamics hold the sales pipeline. Contract-management tools store the PDFs.

## The Customisation Gap
The generic tools record that a partner exists and what exposure its logo got. They do not hold the contract as a list of dated, countable obligations. The sports-specific logic is: extracting each entitlement from the contract language into a schedule across home dates, rainouts and doubleheaders; reconciling what the video board, PA script and social calendar actually ran against what was owed; counting makegoods; and joining delivered exposure to the partner's own outcome data when the partner agrees to share it. A recap that shows delivery against contract line by line, with exceptions and makegoods, is what a partner's procurement team can check. Outcome attribution beyond that needs the sponsor's data, which the team cannot see, the same boundary drawn at [[niches/esports-organizations/sponsor-outcome-attribution/profile|Sponsor Outcome Attribution]] for esports.

## Impact If Solved
Recap decks take ~10–20 hours per partner (estimate), or ~1,000–2,000 activation hours a season for a club with 100 partners. Cutting that by two-thirds and catching missed deliverables before the partner does protects renewals on a partnership book of ~$30–100M a year at a big-league club (estimate).

# The Aid Counsellor in Verification Season

**Industry:** [[colleges-and-universities|Colleges & Universities]]
**Type:** Worker Life Changing
**One-liner:** From January to June a financial aid counsellor spends most of each day chasing tax transcripts, resolving ISIR comment codes and answering the same family questions, while the students who need a real conversation wait.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #bert #worker-facing #automation #workflow-orchestration #compliance

## The Problem
When a student's FAFSA reaches the college as an ISIR (Institutional Student Information Record), the aid office cannot always award on it. The Department of Education selects files for verification. Comment codes and C-flags signal conflicting information, a missing signature or a citizenship match failure. The institution's own policy may require more documents. A counsellor with a caseload of several hundred files requests the documents, reads what arrives (tax returns, W-2s, verification worksheets, letters from employers, custody documents), compares them with the ISIR, submits corrections and waits for the reprocessed record. Between files they answer phone calls and emails from parents asking where the aid offer is, what the Student Aid Index means and why the number changed.

The 2024-25 FAFSA rollout made this much worse: ISIRs arrived months late and in batches, some with calculation errors that had to be reprocessed, and offices across the country sent award letters after families had to decide. The 2026-27 form opened early and on time, but the July 2026 loan changes, new Pell rules and the Repayment Assistance Plan mean every counsellor is relearning rules while explaining them to families.

## Why It Matters to the Worker
The job attracted people who wanted to help students afford college, and the season leaves them little time for that. The part that needs judgement is the conversation with a student about to walk away, and assembling a special-circumstance case well enough for the director to decide it; appeal and professional-judgment decisions themselves sit with the director and senior staff. That part gets the evenings. The repetitive work fills the day and carries risk: a verification error is a programme-review finding and a liability for the institution, and it is the counsellor's name on the file. Families' frustration lands on the counsellor personally. Offices lose experienced staff to burnout and better-paid jobs elsewhere in the university, which loads the rest further.

## What a Solution Looks Like
Document intake that reads each upload, extracts the values verification requires, compares them with the ISIR and drafts the correction or the follow-up request, with every extracted value linked to its source page and low-confidence values flagged. A comment-code assistant that explains each C-flag and proposes the resolution steps under current federal guidance. A family-facing assistant that answers status and policy questions from the student's actual file and hands off to a counsellor with the history attached. The counsellor reviews and signs; the system never certifies a file on its own.

## Impact If Solved
Taking document reading and status questions off the counsellor's day gives back an estimated 10–15 hours a week in peak season (estimate). That time goes to students at risk of not enrolling and to complete appeal files for the director, and fewer files reach a programme review with an error the counsellor had no time to catch.

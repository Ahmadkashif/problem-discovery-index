# Representative Data Entry

**Industry:** [[crm-platforms|CRM Platforms]]
**Type:** Worker Life Changing
**One-liner:** Salespeople stop spending several hours a week typing what happened into a system that pays them nothing for it, because the record writes itself from the calls, emails and meetings that already occurred.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #bert #word-embeddings #evaluation-metrics #automation #workflow-orchestration #worker-facing

## The Problem
A sales representative is compensated on closed revenue. Everything else is overhead, and the largest item of overhead is the CRM. Log the call. Write the meeting notes. Update the stage. Adjust the close date. Fill the required fields the operations team added last quarter. Create the follow-up task. Attach the deck that was sent.

Estimates of the time vary and are consistently unflattering — several hours a week, in a role where hours spent selling is the entire performance model. Representatives do it late, in batches, from memory, immediately before a pipeline review. What gets recorded is therefore both thin and retrospectively shaped, which is precisely why the data is unreliable.

The absurdity is that almost none of it is new information. The call happened on a recorded line or a video platform. The email is in the mail system. The meeting is on the calendar with its attendees. The document was sent from a tool that tracked it. The representative is transcribing events that were already captured, into fields, for someone else's report.

## Why It Matters to the Worker
Sales is a role where compensation depends on activity that administration displaces, so every hour in the CRM is felt as money. That makes the resentment rational rather than cultural, and explains why twenty-five years of adoption programmes, mandatory fields and gamification have not worked.

There is a second, sharper frustration: the data is entered for management's benefit and returned as scrutiny. A representative fills in fields, and the fields reappear in a pipeline review as questions about why a deal has not moved. Nothing they entered came back to them as help.

And the requirements grow. Every new operations initiative adds fields, and none are ever removed, so the burden ratchets upward while the value to the person entering it stays at zero.

## What a Solution Looks Like
The record assembled from what happened. Calls transcribed and summarised into the account's history with next steps extracted. Emails and meetings logged automatically with the right contacts associated. Stage movement proposed from what was actually discussed — a pricing conversation and a legal introduction are stage evidence — for the representative to confirm rather than assert.

Contacts created from the people who appeared on the thread, with titles resolved. Follow-up tasks drafted from commitments made in the conversation.

Crucially, the flow should reverse. If the system writes the record, it can also return something: what changed at this account since the last conversation, who has gone quiet, what similar deals needed at this point. Data entry that produces help is data entry people tolerate.

## Impact If Solved
Several hours a week per representative, in the most expensive headcount most companies carry, is the plainest arithmetic in enterprise software. The larger effect is on the data itself — a record captured from events rather than reconstructed from memory is the precondition for every analytical capability the category has been promising.

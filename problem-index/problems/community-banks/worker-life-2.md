# The CFO Who Runs the Exam

**Industry:** [[community-banks|Community Banks]]
**Type:** Worker Life Changing
**One-liner:** At a small community bank the CFO is also the Call Report preparer, the ALM modeller and the exam coordinator, and every 12 to 18 months a request letter of ~a hundred-plus items (estimate) turns their month into a document hunt across shared drives, inboxes and the core.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #worker-facing #workflow-orchestration #compliance #automation

## The Problem
A $400M community bank does not have a regulatory reporting department, a model risk team or an exam liaison office. It has a CFO, perhaps a controller, and a compliance officer who also handles the CRA file. The CFO prepares the Call Report every quarter against a 30-day deadline, runs or supervises the interest rate risk model and the CECL allowance, writes the ALCO package and the board's financial report, and coordinates the safety and soundness exam.

The exam begins weeks before examiners arrive. The pre-examination request letter, delivered through the regulator's secure portal, lists board minutes, committee packages, policies with approval dates, loan trial balances in a specified format, the watch list, the allowance calculation and its support, the interest rate risk model and its validation, vendor management files, audit reports and management responses, and the status of every prior finding. Each item lives somewhere different: the board portal, a shared drive organised by whoever set it up a decade ago, the core's report writer, the ALM vendor's portal, an auditor's PDF. The CFO and their team spend days finding, renaming and uploading files, then answer follow-up requests during the exam itself while still closing the month.

## Why It Matters to the Worker
The exam is the moment the CFO's work is judged by someone with authority over the bank, and the preparation is clerical. Late nights during exam month are routine, and an item that cannot be found looks like an item that does not exist. Many of the same documents were produced for the last exam, the external audit and the loan review, and are assembled from scratch each time. At small banks the CFO is often one of three or four officers who understand the bank's whole financial position; the exam scramble takes them away from the rate and liquidity decisions that matter more.

## What a Solution Looks Like
A standing evidence library that indexes the bank's documents as they are produced, tags each against the categories examiners request, tracks approval dates and policy review cycles, and knows which prior findings are open. When the request letter arrives, the system maps each item to what the bank already holds, flags the gaps and the stale documents, drafts the response index, and assembles the upload package for the CFO to review. Follow-up requests during the exam are answered by retrieval rather than search.

## Impact If Solved
Turning exam preparation from a multi-week document hunt into a few days of review gives the CFO and their team back roughly ~30–80 hours per exam (estimate: ~100–250 request items at ~15–20 minutes each to locate, check and upload), reduces findings that stem from documents nobody could locate, and lets the same library serve the audit and the loan review instead of three separate scrambles a year.

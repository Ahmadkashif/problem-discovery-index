# Deal Desk Signature Chasing

**Industry:** [[esignature-document-workflow|E-Signature & Document Workflow]]
**Type:** Worker Life Changing
**One-liner:** Deal desk and sales operations stop spending the last week of every quarter phoning people about unsigned documents, because stalls are diagnosed and escalated as they happen rather than discovered on the thirty-first.
**Tags:** #survival-analysis #gradient-boosting #large-language-models #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #automation #workflow-orchestration #worker-facing

## The Problem
Quarter end in any company with a sales organisation has a distinctive shape, and a large part of it is documents that have been sent and not signed.

The deal desk works a list. This one went to the wrong contact. This one is with the customer's legal team and nobody knows where. This one is waiting on an internal countersignature from an executive who is travelling. This one was never opened. This one was opened six times and not signed, which everybody agrees is meaningful and nobody can interpret.

The remedies available are phone calls, emails to account executives asking them to phone, and reminders from the platform. The work is entirely coordination, it happens under maximum time pressure, and it determines whether revenue lands in this quarter or the next — which makes it visible to everyone senior.

Then the quarter closes, the pressure releases, and the same envelopes that stalled will stall again next quarter for the same reasons, because nothing recorded why.

## Why It Matters to the Worker
Deal desk is a role for people with commercial and legal judgement — structuring non-standard deals, managing approval policy, protecting the company from bad terms. Quarter end converts it into a chase function for two weeks in every thirteen.

The pressure is disproportionate to the control. The deal desk is accountable for closing the paperwork and has no relationship with the customer's signer, no authority over the internal approver who is travelling, and no visibility into why the customer's legal team has gone quiet. They absorb executive urgency about outcomes they cannot influence.

It is also the same problem every quarter. Everyone in the function can list the recurring stall causes from memory. Nothing in the tooling captures them, so no fix accumulates and the same fire is fought four times a year.

## What a Solution Looks Like
Stall risk visible when the envelope is sent rather than at quarter end. Routing depth, unknown signer, unusual value, terms that historically trigger legal review — all knowable in advance, all fixable before sending, which is the only genuinely cheap moment.

Diagnosis from behaviour rather than from phoning. Never opened, opened and abandoned at a specific page, forwarded outside the organisation, stalled at an internal approver — each is a different situation with a different remedy, and the platform can distinguish them.

Escalation as an action rather than a reminder. Route to an alternate authorised signer, alert the internal owner directly, or surface the specific clause the recipient appears to have stopped at, so the account executive has something to call about.

And a record. Stall causes categorised and aggregated so that the recurring ones — a routing policy that always adds three days, an approval threshold set too low, a clause that customers consistently object to — become fixable rather than folkloric.

## Impact If Solved
Quarter-end signature chasing is a predictable, recurring organisational crisis conducted by phone over documents whose stall causes the platform can observe. Moving diagnosis upstream returns the deal desk to commercial work and converts a repeated fire into a set of process fixes.

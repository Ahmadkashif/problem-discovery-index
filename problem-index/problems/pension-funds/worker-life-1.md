# The Member Counselor on a 25-Minute Queue

**Industry:** [[pension-funds|Pension Funds]]
**Type:** Worker Life Changing
**One-liner:** A retirement system counselor answers call after call about irrevocable decisions, such as when to retire, which payment option to choose, and whether to buy service, while the queue behind them grows past twenty minutes and a third of callers give up.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #k-nearest-neighbors #evaluation-metrics #worker-facing #automation #workflow-orchestration

## The Problem
The counseling centre is where members meet the plan. In North Carolina, counselors handle eligibility, benefit estimates and calculations, retirement applications, refunds, disability benefits, service purchases, taxes and health coverage. The division describes these as matters that "can affect significant and sometimes irrevocable financial decisions." Average monthly call volume rose 17% from 2024 to 2025. In March 2026 the centre took 33,149 calls and 10,607 written inquiries. The average wait reached 25 minutes and 19 seconds, about 30% of callers hung up, and an outside review found a daily shortfall of five to eight staff out of 33 call-centre positions.

Each call is a lookup and a judgement under time pressure. The counselor opens the member's record in the pension administration system and reads years of service, purchased credit and salary history. They work out whether the member has hit the rule of 80, whether a leave of absence counts, and what a joint-and-survivor option would cost against a single-life annuity. All of this happens while the member waits on the line and the next caller waits in the queue. The rules span tiers, reciprocity agreements and decades of legislative changes.

## Why It Matters to the Worker
Training is long and the margin for error is small. North Carolina documents five to seven weeks of initial training, then two to five months on calls before a new counselor can handle most inquiries alone. A wrong answer about eligibility or an option election can change a member's income for life, and the counselor knows it. The calls are emotionally heavy: members facing a disability, a spouse's death or a retirement date they cannot move. The division's own memo names burnout and turnover as the risk, and every departure puts the training cost back on the people who stay.

## What a Solution Looks Like
A counselor's assistant that reads the member's record as the call connects. It summarises service, tier, eligibility dates and open items. It retrieves the plan rule and the precedent that applies to the member's situation, with citations to the statute or policy, and drafts the answer and the follow-up letter for the counselor to confirm. For estimates, it runs the calculation the counselor would run and shows its working. After the call, it logs the question type so that the systems can see which rules generate the most calls and fix the member communications that caused them.

## Impact If Solved
Cutting a few minutes from each of ~30,000 monthly calls (estimate based on North Carolina's volume) is the difference between a 25-minute and a 5-minute queue at the same headcount. New counselors reach independence in weeks rather than months, because the assistant carries the rules they have not yet memorised. Experienced counselors spend their attention on the member rather than on the screens.

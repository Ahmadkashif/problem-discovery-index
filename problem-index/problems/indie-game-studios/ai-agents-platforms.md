# AI Agents & Platform Opportunities — Indie Game Studios

**Industry:** [[indie-game-studios|Indie Game Studios]]

---

## 1. Commercial Signal Platform
#ai-platform #time-series-forecasting #gradient-boosting #bayesian-inference #confidence-intervals #cnns #evaluation-metrics #revenue-impact

**Concept:** A pooled benchmark and forecasting service for independent studios. Studios contribute their own wishlist curves, demo retention, festival results and realised sales; the platform returns the interpretive frame nobody currently has — where this title sits against the distribution for its genre, price band and launch window, and what launch revenue that implies as a distribution that updates through development. It also runs the store-asset side: attribute-level effects on wishlist conversion learned across many titles, which is the only way a low-traffic game gets evidence it could never generate itself.

**Inputs:** Contributed wishlist, demo, trailer and sales data; store assets and their coded attributes; genre, price, platform and launch window; public review and visibility signals as a seed where contribution is thin.

**Outputs / Actions:** A position against the genre distribution rather than a bare number. A launch revenue forecast with intervals that are honestly wide early and narrow late. Attribute-level store guidance validated prospectively rather than asserted as craft lore. Explicit refusal to answer the counterfactual — whether a different trailer would have helped is not in this data, and a product that implies otherwise is selling the thing studios most want and least can have.

**Why now:** Release volume has made pre-launch visibility decisive and has made the pattern recognition that publishers charge a large revenue share for the most valuable commodity in the sector. The pool is the hard part and it is a community-building problem more than a technical one — which is why it has not happened and also why it is achievable by someone trusted in the developer community rather than by a platform.

**Market:** Independent studios, publishers and funds who would pay for better signal on their own portfolio, and the festivals and showcases whose selection decisions currently rest on the same intuition.

## 2. Runway and Scope Agent
#ai-agent #time-series-forecasting #survival-analysis #bayesian-inference #confidence-intervals #gradient-boosting #worker-facing #automation

**Concept:** An agent that forecasts completion from what a team is actually doing rather than from what it planned, and presents it against the money. It reads the ordinary issue tracker, measures completion velocity and — the variable that dominates overrun and that nobody tracks — the rate at which new work is being discovered, and projects a ship date as a distribution that is genuinely wide in year one and narrows as the work becomes specified. It puts that curve next to the runway curve, monthly, so the decision about scope, funding or delay is taken while those are still choices.

**Inputs:** Issue tracker history including task creation, completion, reopening and estimates; task classification as exploratory or specified; team availability; burn rate and remaining funding; playtest telemetry showing which content players actually engage with.

**Outputs / Actions:** A completion distribution that updates weekly. A runway-versus-completion view, which is the actual question. A scope-cut candidate list grounded in engagement telemetry rather than in whose feature it is. An explicit warning when the discovery rate implies the current plan is not converging, which is the signal that currently arrives eighteen months late as an empty bank account.

**Why now:** Everything needed is already generated by any team using a tracker, and the failure it addresses is the leading identifiable cause of studio death. The reason it does not exist is that nobody has framed schedule overrun as a forecastable quantity rather than as a cultural inevitability.

**Market:** Independent studios of two to thirty people, the publishers and funds financing them who currently have no visibility into a project's real trajectory, and the grant bodies making the same bet with even less.

## 3. Campaign and Submission Agent
#ai-agent #large-language-models #bert #gradient-boosting #time-series-forecasting #compliance #worker-facing #workflow-orchestration

**Concept:** An agent for the two obligations developers are worst at and least willing to do. On marketing, it attributes wishlist movement to specific activities so the effort becomes legible, drafts development updates, press pitches, store copy and social posts from the studio's own build notes and footage, matches creators to the genre from public coverage history, and produces a dated cadence for the launch window so the obligation is scheduled and finite rather than an open competition with development every day. On certification, it checks builds against platform requirements engine-aware, and predicts which requirements this project is most likely to fail before the first submission rather than after.

**Inputs:** Build notes, screenshots and footage; wishlist and traffic data with timing; public creator coverage histories; genre campaign patterns and festival calendars; platform requirement documentation and build artefacts; the studio's prior submission outcomes.

**Outputs / Actions:** Attribution showing which marketing activities actually move wishlists, so the ones that do not can stop. Drafted updates, pitches and copy for editing rather than composition. A ranked creator outreach list. A launch cadence by genre. A prioritised pre-submission requirement list with the specific likely failures named, weighted toward recall because a missed requirement costs a week and a false alarm costs an hour.

**Why now:** Both obligations are performed badly by people who did not choose them, under deadline, with no feedback — and both are now largely mechanical given the studio's own materials and the platform documentation.

**Market:** Independent studios, particularly first-time console submitters; also the porting studios and marketing agencies serving them, for whom this is the tooling behind the service.

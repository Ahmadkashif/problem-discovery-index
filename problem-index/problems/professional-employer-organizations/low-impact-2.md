# ACA Reporting for Hundreds of Co-Employed Clients

**Industry:** [[professional-employer-organizations|Professional Employer Organizations]]
**Type:** Low Impact (Customisation Opportunity)
**One-liner:** ACA reporting tools are built for one employer measuring its own workforce; a PEO must determine large-employer status, offers and affordability client by client, including for clients whose controlled-group siblings sit outside the PEO.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #logistic-regression #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #compliance #automation #data-integration

## The Problem
Under the Affordable Care Act, the employer shared responsibility rules and Forms 1094-C and 1095-C apply to each applicable large employer: 50 or more full-time equivalents, counted across a controlled group. In a PEO the client remains that employer for ACA purposes, so the PEO must work out, for every client, whether it is an applicable large employer this year, which employees are full-time under the client's chosen measurement method, whether each was offered coverage that met minimum value and affordability, and which code goes on each line of each 1095-C. NAPEO's own onboarding best practice starts there: determine whether the new client has a coverage obligation, then review its payroll and benefits documentation to size it.

The hard cases are routine. A client joins in July with six months of hours on its previous payroll system. A client owner also owns a second company that is not a PEO client but counts toward the controlled group. Variable-hour employees need look-back measurement periods that began before the client joined. A client declined the master health plan and offers a carrier policy of its own that the PEO does not administer. Benefits staff collect the missing facts by email each winter, ahead of the early-March deadline for employee statements and the end-of-March electronic filing deadline (recalled dates, which shift year to year).

## What Already Exists
ACA reporting modules in payroll and HCM systems, PrismHR's reporting tools, and stand-alone ACA compliance services that track hours, determine full-time status, test affordability against the safe harbours and produce 1095-C files. They do the arithmetic well for a single employer whose full workforce and offers live in one system.

## The Customisation Gap
A PEO needs the determination run per client, not per PEO. It must ingest prior-provider hours for mid-year joiners, keep a register of each client's controlled-group and affiliated entities, including those outside the PEO, with the evidence for each, track each client's measurement-method elections and carrier offers outside the master plan, and route the open questions to the client owner in plain language. It also needs a defensible record of who supplied each fact, because the penalty letter (IRS Letter 226-J) goes to the client and the client will ask the PEO why.

## Impact If Solved
Clean ACA files for hundreds of clients without a winter of email chasing, fewer employer shared responsibility penalty letters reaching clients, and a stronger renewal conversation for the account team. For a mid-sized PEO, that is a few hundred staff hours each reporting season and a source of client churn removed (estimate).

# The Line Lead and the New Temp Crew

**Industry:** [[contract-packagers|Contract Packagers]]
**Type:** Worker Life Changing
**One-liner:** A line lead gives back an hour of every peak-season morning, and the strain of hitting a rate the crew cannot yet make, if new temps can learn the job from instructions they understand.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #cnns #object-detection #worker-facing #workflow-orchestration #quick-win

## The Problem
In peak season, the weeks before back-to-school, Halloween, the winter holidays and Easter, a co-packer's headcount can double, and much of the extra labour arrives from temp agencies, often on the same morning the job starts (estimate). The line lead meets a crew of ~8–20 people (estimate), some returning and some on their first day, some with limited English, and has to get them building a display or a variety pack correctly and at rate. The work instruction is usually a printed sheet with a few photos, written for the last job like it. The lead teaches by demonstration, station by station, then walks the line correcting folds, insert orientation, label placement and case counts. The rate the crew must hit came from a quote the lead did not write.

The lead is also the line's quality system. Wrong flavour in the variety pack, a carton upside down in the display, a short case — the lead is expected to catch these before QA does, while covering a station when someone takes a break, chasing components from the warehouse and reporting counts to the scheduler. If the crew misses rate, the lead hears about it. If a defect ships, the lead hears about it again.

## Why It Matters to the Worker
Line leads are usually promoted packers, paid hourly, carrying responsibility for output and quality without control over who is on the line or what the job was priced at. Repeating the same training every morning to a different crew is exhausting, and correcting people who do not share a language is harder still. The job is physical and fast. Repetitive hand work at speed carries strain risk for the crew, and the lead absorbs the pressure from both directions. Good leads are the people co-packers can least afford to lose, and peak season is when they burn out.

## What a Solution Looks Like
Work instructions generated from the job record and the approved sample: one short visual sequence per station, in the crew member's language, on a tablet or a screen at the station, with the common mistakes for this format shown. A camera at pack-out or a handheld check that flags the errors the lead currently hunts for by eye (wrong variant, missing insert, upside-down carton, short case) and shows the lead which station they came from. A live count by hour against the realistic ramp for a new crew, not the steady-state number from the quote, so the lead and the supervisor see the same expectation.

## Impact If Solved
New crew members reach rate faster and need fewer corrections, and the lead's first hour of every shift goes back to running the line instead of repeating the same training. Fewer defects reach QA or the customer. Above all, the lead's day stops being a fight between a quoted rate and a crew that was never set up to meet it, which is what keeps experienced leads through the next peak.

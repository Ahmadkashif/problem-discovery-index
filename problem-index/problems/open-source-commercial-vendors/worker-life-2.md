# Support Across the Paid and Free Boundary

**Industry:** [[open-source-commercial-vendors|Open Source Commercial Vendors]]
**Type:** Worker Life Changing
**One-liner:** Support engineers at open-source companies answer the same question in a contracted ticket and in a public issue, and must maintain a distinction that is commercially necessary and feels indefensible to hold.
**Tags:** #bert #word-embeddings #k-means-clustering #large-language-models #gradient-boosting #evaluation-metrics #automation #worker-facing

## The Problem
A commercially backed open-source company runs two support channels that look identical from the inside. Paying customers file tickets under a contract with response commitments. Community users file public issues and forum posts with no commitment at all.

The technical content is frequently the same. The same misconfiguration, the same version incompatibility, the same performance question. The support engineer answers it in a ticket for a customer and sees it again the next day in a public issue from someone who is not.

They must maintain the distinction and it is uncomfortable in both directions. Answering the community fully undermines the commercial proposition their colleagues sell. Not answering feels wrong when they know the answer and the person is stuck using software the company gave them.

The escalation path adds another edge. A customer issue that requires a code change goes to a maintainer who is also handling community work, and the paying customer's issue is prioritised — which is correct commercially and visible publicly, and periodically produces a community complaint that the project serves paying users first.

Meanwhile the knowledge is fragmented. The answer given in a ticket does not reach the public documentation, and the answer in a public issue does not reach the support knowledge base, so both are re-derived.

## Why It Matters to the Worker
The role sits on a boundary the company has not fully resolved, and the engineer holds it in every interaction. That is a low-grade ethical discomfort that accumulates.

It is also duplicated effort at a scale that is obvious to the person doing it. Answering the same question in two systems, neither of which learns from the other, is visibly wasteful and entirely within the company's power to fix.

And the public dimension changes the job. A support engineer at a proprietary vendor works in private. Here, a large part of the work is public, permanent and searchable, which raises the care required for every response and makes a mistake permanent.

## What a Solution Looks Like
A single knowledge base spanning both channels. An answer given anywhere should be available everywhere, with the commercial distinction expressed as response commitment and depth of engagement rather than as access to the answer at all.

Cross-channel duplicate detection, so an engineer answering a ticket sees that the same issue exists publicly, and vice versa — which converts duplicated effort into one investigation with two audiences.

Public answers turned into documentation automatically. A well-answered public issue is documentation that has not been filed, and the promotion is currently manual and therefore does not happen.

Community self-service built from the corpus of both channels, so the volume of straightforward questions falls before it reaches either queue.

And an explicit, published policy about what the community gets, so the engineer is applying a stated position rather than exercising personal judgement about a colleague's revenue in every interaction.

## Impact If Solved
Open-source support duplicates effort across two channels that do not learn from each other, and places the commercial boundary in the hands of individual engineers in every interaction. A unified knowledge layer removes the duplication, and an explicit policy removes the discomfort — neither of which requires resolving the harder strategic question underneath.

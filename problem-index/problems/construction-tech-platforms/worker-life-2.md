# Project Engineer RFI Chase

**Industry:** [[construction-tech-platforms|Construction Tech Platforms]]
**Type:** Worker Life Changing
**One-liner:** Project engineers stop spending their careers sending follow-up emails about unanswered RFIs and start doing the technical coordination the role is supposed to be.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #bert #survival-analysis #gradient-boosting #evaluation-metrics #workflow-orchestration #automation #worker-facing

## The Problem
The project engineer is the entry-level professional role on a construction project and the person who owns the paperwork that keeps it moving: submittals, RFIs, change order documentation, closeout. Of these, RFIs consume the most time, and almost none of that time is spent on the technical content.

An RFI is raised in the field. The project engineer writes it up, routes it, and then chases it. The architect has not responded. The structural engineer says it is with the architect. The owner's representative wants a cost impact before answering. Meanwhile the field is asking daily, because the work is stopped or is proceeding on an assumption.

The chase is email, phone calls and a spreadsheet. On a large project a project engineer may be tracking a hundred open RFIs, each with its own reviewer, age, and downstream consequence. Most of the day is triage: which of these matters most today, who do I call, what do I tell the superintendent.

## Why It Matters to the Worker
This is the industry's training ground. Project engineers become project managers become executives, and what they are supposed to be learning is how buildings go together and how to coordinate the people building them. Spending two years primarily on follow-up correspondence teaches persistence and not much else.

It is also structurally thankless. The project engineer has responsibility for RFI turnaround and no authority over any of the people who must respond. They absorb the field's frustration about delays caused by parties they cannot compel, and their performance is judged on a metric they do not control.

Attrition from the role is high, at a moment when the industry cannot find enough people entering it. The administrative shape of the job is a large part of why.

## What a Solution Looks Like
Prioritisation that reflects consequence rather than age. An RFI blocking a critical path activity next week is not the same as one affecting finishes in six months, and the platform holds the schedule linkage needed to tell them apart. The engineer should open a list ordered by what will actually hurt.

Predicted response time per reviewer, from the vendor's cross-project history — this reviewer type, on this kind of question, at this stage, typically takes eleven days — so escalation happens on evidence rather than on a fixed timer, and so the field can be told something true.

Automated, well-mannered follow-up that does not require the engineer to compose it, with escalation paths that fire on predicted rather than elapsed lateness.

And answer suggestion where the answer already exists: a large proportion of RFIs are questions already answered in the specification, in a prior RFI on the same project, or in a similar RFI on another project. Retrieving that before the RFI is routed removes a meaningful share of the volume entirely.

## Impact If Solved
RFI latency is a direct input to schedule slip, and the person managing it is the least empowered person on the project. Fixing the workflow improves the project's timeline and makes the industry's primary entry-level role into something that develops the professionals it is supposed to develop.

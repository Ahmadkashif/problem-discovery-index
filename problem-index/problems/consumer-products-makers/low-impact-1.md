# Retailer Deductions Written Off Below the Threshold

**Industry:** [[consumer-products-makers|Consumer Products Makers]]
**Type:** Low Impact (Customisation Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Every maker selling to big-box retail is paid net of deductions it never agreed to, and deduction software exists, but it is built for large finance teams and food brands, not for a candle or brush maker with one clerk, six retailer portals and windows that close in weeks.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #bert #gradient-boosting #feature-engineering #revenue-impact #automation #workflow-orchestration

## The Problem
A mid-sized candle, pen or cleaning-tool maker ships to Walmart, Target, Amazon, TJX, Home Depot or Lowe's, a dollar store chain and a few regional buyers. Each pays the invoice short. The remittance carries codes for promotional allowances, defective-merchandise allowances, shortages, damages, pricing differences, compliance fines for late or incomplete shipments, and labelling or packaging violations. Walmart charges 3% of the cost of goods on cases that miss its on-time or in-full targets (search summary). The Credit Research Foundation's 2023 deduction survey found median non-trade deductions of 1.1–2% of sales, up from a half to one percent in 2021 and a quarter to half a percent in 2018, with 17% of respondents above 5%; the same survey's median of 5.1–10% of deduction dollars judged invalid is reported in its results as compiled by Overdeduct. A vendor benchmark puts all deductions at 3–7% of gross revenue for mid-market makers (Finortal, vendor figure).

Disputing one means logging into the retailer's portal, pulling the claim detail, finding the bill of lading, proof of delivery, advance ship notice and the promotion agreement, and filing within the retailer's dispute window. The clerk does the large ones. CRF puts the median automatic write-off threshold at $50–$100, and its compiled results (via Overdeduct) show 91% of companies writing off small deductions automatically, which at a maker with thousands of small shortage and fine deductions a year is where most of the invalid money sits.

## What Already Exists
HighRadius and the ERP vendors sell deduction management to large finance teams, with remittance matching, workflow and reason-code analytics. A newer group sells to consumer goods brands specifically: Confido, Glimpse and SPS Revenue Recovery (SupplyPike, which SPS Commerce bought in 2024 for $205.8M, plus Carbon6 for Amazon) pull deductions from retailer portals, match backup and file disputes, and Vividly and Promomash join deductions to trade promotion plans. Contingency recovery firms work old deductions for a share of what they collect.

## The Customisation Gap
The CPG-specific tools grew up around food and beverage brands selling through grocery, distributors such as UNFI and KeHE and broker networks, so their reason-code maps, promotion logic and integrations follow that channel. A general-merchandise maker needs a different layer: home-centre and mass-merchant vendor agreements with defective-allowance and markdown terms; seasonal programmes with return-to-vendor and post-season markdown claims; Amazon Vendor Central shortage claims; retailer routing-guide fines tied to its own carriers and 3PL; and casket or brush makers whose few big accounts each run a different portal. It also needs to handle a maker with no promotion management system at all, where the only record of an agreed allowance is an email to the buyer.

## Impact If Solved
A maker with $50M in retail sales and deductions at a few percent of revenue carries several hundred thousand dollars a year of disputable claims, much of it under the write-off threshold (estimate). Tooling fitted to its retailers and its paperwork turns that from a written-off cost into recovered cash without hiring a second clerk.

# Legal Operations Queue Triage

**Industry:** [[contract-lifecycle-platforms|Contract Lifecycle Platforms]]
**Type:** Worker Life Changing
**One-liner:** Legal operations stops being a routing desk for a queue where a routine NDA and a genuinely dangerous agreement arrive looking identical.
**Tags:** #gradient-boosting #bert #large-language-models #word-embeddings #evaluation-metrics #workflow-orchestration #automation #worker-facing

## The Problem
Legal requests arrive constantly: an NDA before a meeting, a vendor agreement someone has already verbally committed to, a customer's redlines on the third round, a question about whether a clause permits something, a renewal that is due in a week.

Legal operations triages. Work out what this actually is, whether legal review is genuinely needed, which lawyer should see it, how urgent it truly is as opposed to how urgent the requester says, and whether it can be handled by a template or a self-service path.

The queue offers no help. A mutual NDA on the company's own template and a master services agreement with an uncapped indemnity arrive in the same inbox as attachments with a sentence of context. Distinguishing them requires opening and reading, which is the work.

Business stakeholders are also poor reporters of urgency. Everything is urgent, so nothing is, and legal operations negotiates priority with people who do not know that the contract in front of them is the routine one.

The volume grows with the company and the function does not, which means triage quality falls exactly as the stakes rise.

## Why It Matters to the Worker
Legal operations is a genuinely strategic function — process design, systems, metrics, vendor management, making a legal department scale — and triage consumes it. The people in these roles are usually the ones who could improve the process and are too busy running it.

The accountability is also asymmetric. Routing something to the wrong place or missing something dangerous is visible and serious; routing a thousand things correctly is invisible. That produces a defensive posture where everything gets escalated, which wastes counsel time and makes legal the bottleneck everyone complains about.

And the relationship cost is real. Legal operations spends its day telling people their request will take longer than they hoped, which makes the function unpopular for reasons entirely outside its control.

## What a Solution Looks Like
Classification at intake from the document itself rather than from the requester's description. Agreement type, counterparty, value, whether it is the company's paper or the counterparty's, and which terms are present or notably absent — all extractable before a human opens it.

Risk-based routing. Whether this needs senior counsel, junior review, or no review at all is predictable from the document and from which agreements senior counsel actually changed historically. That prediction is what converts a defensive escalate-everything posture into a defensible one.

Self-service paths for the genuinely routine, with the confidence to actually use them: a standard NDA on the company's own template with no modifications does not need a lawyer, and legal operations currently cannot prove that to anyone.

Duplicate and existing-agreement detection — a surprising share of requests concern a counterparty the company already has an agreement with, and nothing checks.

And a queue that reports its own composition, so the function can show what it is absorbing and make the case for resource with evidence rather than complaint.

## Impact If Solved
Legal operations is the function that makes a legal department scale, and it is consumed by triage that the document itself could largely perform. Risk-based routing also fixes the escalate-everything dynamic that makes legal a bottleneck, which is the complaint the whole business has about the function.

# The Reviewer

**Industry:** [[content-moderation-services|Content Moderation Services]]
**Type:** Worker Life Changing
**One-liner:** The people who see the worst material on the internet are employed by the vendor, measured on throughput, and protected by provisions that reporting has repeatedly found do not match the contract.
**Tags:** #transformers #cnns #large-language-models #gradient-boosting #confidence-intervals #evaluation-metrics #compliance #worker-facing

## The Problem
A content reviewer works a queue of material that classifiers escalated because it is severe or ambiguous. Over a shift that means graphic violence, child sexual abuse material in the specialist queues, terrorism, self-harm, and a long tail of content whose classification requires cultural knowledge and judgement.

The work is structured around throughput and agreement. A reviewer handles a target volume per shift with a quality score from sampled audits, and the combination of time pressure and distressing material is the precise condition occupational health research identifies as harmful.

The employment relationship compounds it. Most of this workforce is employed by outsourcing vendors rather than by the platforms whose content they review, frequently in lower-cost labour markets, on pay and conditions below those of platform staff doing adjacent work. Litigation in several jurisdictions has produced settlements and findings; reporting from delivery centres in several countries has described symptoms consistent with post-traumatic stress and working conditions that diverge from the wellness provisions in the client contract.

Those provisions are the recurring finding. Counselling that is nominally available and discouraged in practice by throughput targets; support staff without the relevant clinical training; break entitlements that exist on paper. The gap between the contract and the room is the thing multiple investigations have found.

And the exposure is higher than it needs to be. Reviewers routinely see material at full fidelity that could be presented reduced, review whole items when a segment was at issue, and receive severe categories in whatever order the queue delivers them.

## Why It Matters to the Worker
This is documented occupational injury, imposed on the workforce with the least bargaining power in the chain, in service of companies with the most. It is not a contested claim: the settlements, the clinical findings and the platforms' own vendor requirements all acknowledge the risk exists.

The structural position is the reason it persists. The vendor employs the people and carries the liability but does not control the exposure, which is determined by classifier thresholds, queue composition and presentation choices made in the platform's tooling. The party that could reduce the harm is not the party that pays for it.

The career structure offers little. Deep policy expertise accumulates in people on short contracts through vendors, and it leaves constantly — which is bad for them and degrades enforcement quality for everyone.

And the throughput-agreement metric actively discourages the care that makes the work bearable and correct. Taking longer on a hard case costs the reviewer twice.

## What a Solution Looks Like
Reduce exposure with the models already available. Pre-classification to determine what genuinely needs human eyes; presentation blurred, greyscale, muted or thumbnail-only by default with expansion on demand; segment isolation so a reviewer sees the nine seconds at issue rather than the whole item; text summaries permitting a decision without viewing where that suffices. Each is standard in the more mature parts of the field and absent from many deployments — and a vendor can build several of these on its own side rather than waiting for the client.

Manage cumulative load as a health metric. Exposure to the most severe categories tracked per person per shift, with enforced limits and rotation into lower-severity queues, is how every other profession with comparable documented risk operates.

Fix the metric. Quality audits that penalise contextual correctness teach reviewers to stop thinking, and time pressure on the hardest cases produces exactly the errors that surface publicly. Separate handling for ambiguous cases, with permission to escalate rather than decide, addresses both.

Make the provisions real and verifiable. Clinically trained support, genuinely accessible, decoupled from throughput measurement, on the same terms for vendor-employed staff as for direct employees — and audited by someone other than the party being measured, since the repeated finding is that the contract and the delivery differ.

## Impact If Solved
This is the clearest documented occupational harm in the digital economy and the one most reducible by technology already in the building. Every unit of exposure removed by better pre-classification is a direct reduction in injury; load management and metric reform address the conditions that amplify it; and independent verification of the wellness provisions addresses the gap that every investigation of this industry has found.

# Agency Service Rep Renewal Remarketing

**Industry:** [[insurtech-platforms|Insurtech Platforms]]
**Type:** Worker Life Changing
**One-liner:** Account service representatives stop rebuilding the same submission six times to shop a renewal, because the application data is already in the agency system and only needs to be routed.

## The Problem
Commercial policies renew annually, and when the incumbent carrier's renewal quote comes back with a significant increase, the account has to be remarketed — shopped to other carriers to find better terms.

The service representative assembles a submission: the current application data, updated exposures, loss runs from the expiring carrier, and any supplemental information each target carrier requires. Then they send it to six carriers, each of which wants it in a slightly different form, through a different portal or email address, with different supplemental questionnaires.

Then they wait, chase, collect quotes in different formats, build a comparison for the producer and the client, and place the business. The whole cycle runs weeks and much of it is transcription and follow-up.

The information is almost entirely already in the agency management system. It was keyed in at the last renewal. Very little has changed except the exposures the client updated and one year of additional loss experience.

## Why It Matters to the Worker
Account service is the backbone role in an independent agency and it is under permanent volume pressure. Renewals arrive in seasonal waves, remarketing is the most labour-intensive thing the role does, and it happens exactly when the representative is also handling ordinary service on every other account.

The work is repetitive without being simple. Each carrier's submission requirements are different enough that it cannot be done mechanically and similar enough that it feels like it should be. Representatives describe it as the reason for the hours, and agencies describe difficulty hiring for it.

There is a quieter frustration. Much of the remarketing effort produces nothing — carriers decline, or quote above the incumbent — and the representative cannot tell in advance which carriers are worth approaching. They are shopping blind, using their producer's relationships as a proxy for appetite.

## What a Solution Looks Like
Submissions assembled from the agency system rather than rebuilt. The data is there; what is needed is carrier-specific formatting, supplemental questionnaire population from existing answers, and routing.

Carrier appetite prediction is the change that matters most. The agency has years of history on which carriers quoted, which declined, and which won, by class, size and geography. That predicts which six carriers are worth approaching far better than relationship memory does, and it prevents the effort spent on carriers that were never going to write it.

Loss run collection should be automated where possible, since the delay in obtaining it from the expiring carrier is one of the most common causes of a remarketing running late into the renewal window.

Quote comparison should be structured automatically rather than assembled in a spreadsheet, including the coverage differences that matter and that a premium comparison hides.

## Impact If Solved
Remarketing is the most labour-intensive recurring task in agency service and much of it is duplication of data the agency already holds. Removing the reassembly and targeting the carriers likely to write the business converts weeks of chasing into days, at a moment when the client is deciding whether to stay with the agency at all.

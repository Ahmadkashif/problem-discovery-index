## 1. Verified Lockout Desk Agent
#ai-agent #large-language-models #gradient-boosting #compliance #workflow-orchestration #revenue-impact

**Concept:** An agent that answers a licensed locksmith's lockout line 24 hours a day under the firm's own name. It takes the caller's details, gives a binding price band from the job description, checks the name against the address or vehicle where lawful, and dispatches the nearest available technician. It then opens the work order with the jurisdiction's required fields already set. After the job it files the record, sends the receipt with the licence number, and drafts responses to review complaints and lead disputes on Google Local Services from the documented job.
**Inputs:** Inbound calls and texts, technician locations and schedules, the firm's price book, job history, state and city record rules, Google Local Services lead data.
**Outputs / Actions:** Quotes accepted in writing before dispatch; dispatched jobs with travel estimates; pre-filled work orders; flagged calls where the requester and the property don't match; lead-dispute submissions; after-hours summaries for the owner.
**Why now:** Voice agents can now hold a natural call and extract structured job details reliably. Google's Advanced Verification requirement for locksmiths has made "verified and licensed" a ranking factor rather than a slogan. And the bait-and-switch reputation means a written upfront price is a selling point.
**Market:** The ~3,900 employer locksmith firms and a larger number of sole proprietors (estimate), most of whom miss night calls today. Competes with [[answering-services|answering services]] and with lead networks that keep the customer relationship; differentiates by answering as the firm and keeping the record with it.

## 2. Opening Record and Door Compliance Platform
#ai-platform #cnns #object-detection #large-language-models #tacit-knowledge-ml #compliance

**Concept:** A platform that gives every commercial door a client's locksmith services a persistent record: photos, identified hardware, fire label, every fault, fix and callback. On top of that record it runs the parts-and-cause suggestions learned from senior technicians and an NFPA 80 and egress checklist that turns annual fire-door inspections into deficiency lists, quotes and closed-out reports for the authority having jurisdiction or the accreditation surveyor.
**Inputs:** Technician photos and voice notes, client tickets, invoices, hardware catalogues, door schedules from construction documents, fire-door inspection forms.
**Outputs / Actions:** Opening-level history; pre-dispatch parts lists; diagnosis suggestions graded by callbacks; fire-door inspection reports with deficiency tracking; repair quotes; client portal showing compliance status by building.
**Why now:** Phone cameras and vision models can identify hardware and wear well enough to be useful, and annual fire-door inspection, enforced in health facilities since 2017, gives facility clients a dated reason to pay for documentation.
**Market:** Commercial door-hardware specialists among the 101 locksmith firms with twenty or more employees and the larger shops below them, plus multi-site facility clients such as school districts, hospitals and retailers. The buyer category exists in fire alarm inspection software; for doors it is mostly paper and spreadsheets.

## 3. Key System Steward
#ai-agent #combinatorics-and-counting #graph-theory #large-language-models #compliance #data-integration

**Concept:** An agent that holds a client's master-key system as a live model rather than a binder. It digitises existing bitting charts and key-issue logs, keeps the key-holder ledger current from HR departures and work orders, and answers "this key is lost, what is exposed?" in seconds with a costed rekey plan. It also designs extensions without cross-keying, and coordinates with access-control credentials where mechanical keys and cards protect the same doors.
**Inputs:** Scanned bitting charts, pinning records, key-issue logs, door schedules, HR leaver lists, access-control cardholder exports, manufacturer keyway specifications.
**Outputs / Actions:** System model with cross-keying audit; exposure reports for lost keys; minimal rekey plans with parts and labour; extension designs; restricted-key duplicate authorisations checked against named signatories.
**Why now:** Language and vision models can turn handwritten charts and logs into structured data cheaply, and combinatorial checks that once needed a specialist's afternoon run instantly. Phone credentials under Aliro 1.0 will put mechanical and digital access side by side at many doors, which makes a joined record more valuable.
**Market:** Universities, hospitals, school districts, government campuses and property owners with large master-key systems, bought through their locksmith. The in-house lock shops at colleges (1,090 locksmiths in May 2023) are direct users. Competes with key cabinets and factory keying services, neither of which models exposure.

## 4. Automotive Job Feasibility Network
#ai-platform #gradient-boosting #survival-analysis #data-integration #worker-facing #automation

**Concept:** A platform where automotive locksmiths and tool vendors contribute anonymised job outcomes (vehicle pattern, tool, software version, procedure, success, minutes) and get back, at the moment of the call, the probability that a key-programming job will work with their own kit, the expected time, the known failure modes and a suggested price. It guides the NASTF authorisation steps and never stores security codes.
**Inputs:** VINs or vehicle details from the call; each locksmith's tool inventory and versions; contributed outcome logs; tool vendors' coverage releases.
**Outputs / Actions:** Go or no-go and price guidance before dispatch; procedure checklists with module-backup prompts; alerts when a manufacturer change breaks a previously working procedure; coverage-gap reports a tool vendor can act on.
**Why now:** Many current programmers connect online for updates and some charge per-job tokens, so outcomes can be logged automatically, and smart keys have made all-keys-lost jobs both the most lucrative and the riskiest work in the trade.
**Market:** Automotive locksmiths, dealer-service and auction key specialists, and the tool vendors themselves, who would pay to see where their coverage fails. No existing buyer category; forums and vendor coverage lists are the substitute.

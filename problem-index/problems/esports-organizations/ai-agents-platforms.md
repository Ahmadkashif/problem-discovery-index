## 1. Sponsorship Delivery Platform
#ai-platform #cnns #causal-inference #bayesian-inference #confidence-intervals #gradient-boosting #evaluation-metrics #revenue-impact

**Concept:** A platform that lets an esports organisation prove what it delivers. It aggregates attention across every surface — broadcast logo exposure with size and position, stream viewership, short-form clips including derivative uploads found by watermark and audio fingerprint, social — into a consistent unit weighted by prominence and likely notice, with the methodology published and the uncertainty stated. It reports delivery per placement rather than per deal, so a renewal conversation becomes a specific discussion about which elements to keep. And it captures sponsor-returned outcome signals where they can be negotiated, pooling them across deals into the first real evidence base about what this sponsorship does.

**Inputs:** Broadcast video with detection of logos and mentions; stream concurrency and session structure; clip performance and derivative tracking; social engagement; sponsor-returned codes, links and installs; audience composition data.

**Outputs / Actions:** An attention ledger that refuses to sum incomparable things and says so. Per-placement delivery reporting. Audience composition and engagement depth presented as the actual product, rather than raw reach measured against mass-media comparisons the sector will always lose. A smaller, defensible number with a published method — which is worth more to a sceptical sponsor than a larger one and is the opposite of the sector's instinct.

**Why now:** The contraction was driven substantially by an inability to answer a reasonable question about what sponsors received, and organisations closed while their audiences remained. The incentive to build this arrived exactly when the money to build it left, which is why it needs to be a product rather than each organisation's internal project.

**Market:** Esports organisations, leagues and tournament organisers, the agencies selling esports sponsorship, and the brands who want evidence before returning to the category.

## 2. Scouting and Roster Platform
#ai-platform #gradient-boosting #graph-neural-networks #bayesian-inference #confidence-intervals #transfer-learning #evaluation-metrics #causal-inference

**Concept:** A platform that evaluates players on what transfers rather than on what they produced in someone else's context. It adjusts individual statistics for role, teammates, patch and league strength; it measures the traits that persist across metas, including adaptation rate in the weeks following each patch, which is directly computable and essentially never computed; and it models fit — what this player would contribute to this team's structure — which is where roster money is actually lost. Everything is reported with intervals, because competitive samples are small and a confident rating on forty matches overstates the evidence.

**Inputs:** Event-level match data from publisher APIs and demo parsing; role and team structure; patch history with change content; opponent and league strength; the public record of historical transfers with before-and-after performance.

**Outputs / Actions:** Context-adjusted contribution rather than raw output. Patch-robust trait profiles including adaptation rate. A fit estimate against the signing team's existing structure. Post-transfer performance predictions validated on the public transfer record — which is the test the sector has never run on its own scouting and the one that would settle whether any of this beats reputation.

**Why now:** Roster spend is the largest budget line, contracts are multi-year, and the sector no longer has the capital to absorb the mistakes it used to. The label set — every historical transfer and what happened next — is public and unused.

**Market:** Esports organisations and their coaching staff, player agencies who need the same evidence from the other side, and the academy and tier-two ecosystems where undervalued players are found.

## 3. Team Operations Agent
#ai-agent #large-language-models #gradient-boosting #time-series-forecasting #evaluation-metrics #workflow-orchestration #worker-facing #compliance

**Concept:** An agent for the person who holds every operational risk in an esports organisation. It runs travel, accommodation, expenses and itineraries against the published competition calendar — standard corporate travel tooling this sector has never adopted. It turns visas from a recurring emergency into a tracked schedule: requirements by nationality and destination, processing time estimates, document checklists and deadline alerts derived from the calendar, assembled once from public information that is currently scattered. It runs the scrim market as a shared availability and matching system rather than as a weekly negotiation conducted in a dozen direct message threads. And it surfaces the welfare signals a manager is currently expected to notice personally while doing six other jobs.

**Inputs:** Competition calendars from publishers and organisers; roster nationalities and document status; visa requirement and processing data by corridor; travel and accommodation systems; scrim availability across participating organisations; practice schedules and player check-in signals.

**Outputs / Actions:** Booked and tracked travel with itineraries. A visa timeline with alerts early enough to act, which is the difference between attending an event and missing it. A two-sided scrim matching system with timezone and preference constraints — friction every manager in the sector experiences identically, which is why it would be adopted within a season. Welfare flags routed to an independent contact rather than to the person enforcing the practice schedule, which is the organisational separation the sector has not made.

**Why now:** The contraction fell hardest on operations staff, so the remaining managers carry more rosters and more titles with less support — and every one of these tasks is mechanical work performed by hand at every organisation independently.

**Market:** Esports organisations of all sizes, the leagues and tournament organisers who bear the cost when teams cannot travel, and the player associations who would use the welfare separation as a bargaining objective.

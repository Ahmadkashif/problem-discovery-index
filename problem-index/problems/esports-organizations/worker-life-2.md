# The Team Manager Who Is Also the Travel Agent

**Industry:** [[esports-organizations|Esports Organizations]]
**Type:** Worker Life Changing
**One-liner:** One person handles visas, flights, bootcamps, contracts, scrim scheduling, player welfare and the sponsor's content deadline, for a roster living in a house in another country.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #gradient-boosting #time-series-forecasting #evaluation-metrics #worker-facing #workflow-orchestration #automation #compliance

## The Problem
A team manager is the operational centre of an esports roster. The role covers travel and accommodation for international competition, visa applications for players of several nationalities, bootcamp logistics, scrim scheduling across timezones with other teams' managers, contract administration, expense management, the sponsor obligations attached to each player, and the daily welfare of a group of young people living away from home.

Visas are the recurring crisis. Competitive schedules are set by publishers and organisers with limited notice, rosters include players from many countries, and visa processing times do not accommodate that. Teams miss events because a player's appointment was not available, and the manager is the person who spent six weeks on it.

Scrim scheduling is a continuous negotiation conducted in direct messages across a dozen other managers and timezones, rebuilt weekly, with no shared system anywhere in the sector.

And the welfare part is unbounded. Young players living in team accommodation in a foreign country bring everything to the manager, who is usually not trained for it and is also the person enforcing practice schedules.

## Why It Matters to the Worker
The role concentrates every operational risk in an organisation into one undersupported person, and the failures are highly visible — a missed event, a late arrival, a contract issue — while the successes are invisible by definition.

The hours follow the team, which means competition weekends, international timezones and bootcamp periods with no separation. Managers live in the team house in many cases, which removes the boundary entirely in the same way it does for players.

The welfare responsibility is the heaviest part and the least acknowledged. Being the responsible adult for a group of teenagers and early-twenties players, far from home, under public scrutiny, without training or supervision, is a substantial duty that the sector has not resourced. Managers describe it as the reason they leave.

And the sector's financial contraction fell on exactly this layer. Operations staff were cut in the restructurings, which means the remaining managers absorbed more rosters and more titles.

## What a Solution Looks Like
Automate the logistics. Travel, accommodation, expense and itinerary management against a published competition calendar is standard corporate travel tooling that this sector has never adopted, and it would return a large share of the week.

Make visas a tracked process with lead time. Requirements by nationality and destination, processing time estimates, document checklists and deadline alerts derived from the competition calendar turn a recurring emergency into a schedule. The information is public and scattered; assembling it once is the entire work.

Build the scrim market. Scrim scheduling is a two-sided matching problem conducted in direct messages, and a shared system with availability, opponent preferences and timezone constraints would be adopted sector-wide within a season because everyone experiences the same friction.

Separate welfare from management. The person enforcing the practice schedule should not be the only person a struggling nineteen-year-old can talk to, and the fix is organisational — an independent welfare contact — supported by tooling that flags the signals a manager is currently expected to notice personally while doing six other jobs.

## Impact If Solved
Team managers are the operational load-bearing layer of esports organisations and were the layer most reduced in the contraction. Automating travel and visa processes, building the scrim market the sector improvises weekly, and separating welfare from enforcement address both the workload and the duty that makes people leave — and they make a smaller operations team viable, which is the constraint every organisation now works under.

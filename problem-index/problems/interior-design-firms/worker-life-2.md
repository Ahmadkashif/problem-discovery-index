# Approvals Scattered Across Every Channel

**Industry:** [[interior-design-firms|Interior Design Firms]]
**Type:** Worker Life Changing
**One-liner:** Clients approve, change and question selections by text at 10 p.m., by voice note, by Instagram message and by email, and the designer carries the job of remembering which version was actually agreed.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #worker-facing #workflow-orchestration #automation #quick-win

## The Problem
A residential client relationship is intimate and continuous. The client texts a photo of a lamp from a friend's house and asks whether it would work in the den. A spouse sends a voice note saying the dining chairs are too formal after all. An approval of the bedroom drapery arrives as a thumbs-up emoji on a photo in a messaging app. The formal proposal in the studio software says one thing; the latest decision lives in a text thread. Contractors, tradespeople and vendor reps add their own channels.

The designer becomes the single point where all of it is reconciled. Before placing an order they scroll back through weeks of messages to confirm what was approved and in which colourway. When the client later says they never agreed to the green velvet, the evidence is a thumbs-up somewhere in a thread. Clients expect quick replies at any hour because they are paying for personal attention, and many designers answer at night because the alternative feels like losing the client.

## Why It Matters to the Worker
The burden is cognitive and emotional rather than technical. The designer holds dozens of live decisions across several projects in their head and loses sleep over the one they might have forgotten. Disputes over what was approved are the most stressful conversations in the job, because they pit the designer's memory against the client's and put the relationship and the product margin at risk at once. The always-on expectation erodes evenings and weekends and is one of the reasons experienced designers cite for leaving firms or the profession.

## What a Solution Looks Like
A decision log that sits beneath every channel the client already uses. Messages, voice notes and emails are read as they arrive; anything that looks like an approval, a change or a question about a specific item is attached to that item with the original message and timestamp, summarised in one line, and confirmed back to the client in a single tap so that a thumbs-up becomes an explicit, dated approval. The designer sees, per project, what is decided, what is pending and what changed since the last proposal, and can set response hours with the system acknowledging out-of-hours messages and queuing them, so the client feels heard without the designer answering at midnight.

## Impact If Solved
The designer stops reconstructing decisions from message threads, which plausibly saves several hours a week across a full project load (estimate), and "I never approved that" disputes become a lookup instead of an argument. The larger gain is personal: evenings back and fewer decisions held in memory.

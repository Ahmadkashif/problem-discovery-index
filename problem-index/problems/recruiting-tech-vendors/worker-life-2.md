# The Candidate Who Never Hears Back

**Industry:** [[recruiting-tech-vendors|Recruiting Tech Vendors]]
**Type:** Worker Life Changing
**One-liner:** Applications go into a system that never responds, status is unknowable, and the rejection either arrives months later or not at all.
**Tags:** #gradient-boosting #large-language-models #time-series-forecasting #confidence-intervals #evaluation-metrics #compliance #worker-facing #bert

## The Problem
A job seeker applies and receives an automated acknowledgement. Then nothing. No status, no timeline, no indication of whether a human looked. Weeks later a rejection template may arrive, or the application may simply remain open indefinitely in a system nobody has closed.

The information asymmetry is complete. The employer knows the role was filled three weeks ago; the candidate does not. The employer knows the application was screened out in the first pass; the candidate is still hoping. The candidate cannot make rational decisions about where to direct effort because the system tells them nothing.

The volume makes it worse for everyone. Candidates apply more widely because response rates are low, which raises volume, which lowers response rates further. Generative tooling has accelerated this on both sides, and the result is a market where enormous unpaid effort is expended into silence.

The people most affected are the least advantaged. Someone unemployed, applying to dozens of roles, is spending real time on tailored applications with no feedback about whether anything is working. Assessment and interview stages add hours of unpaid time. And a candidate who invests in a multi-stage process and then hears nothing has been taken something from.

Rejection without reason removes the possibility of improvement. Over a hundred applications a candidate learns nothing about why they are not progressing, which is a striking failure in a process that shapes people's working lives.

## Why It Matters to the Worker
This is a person's livelihood, and the process treats their time as free and their need for information as unimportant. That is the near-universal experience of job seeking and it is almost entirely a set of workflow choices rather than a constraint.

The psychological cost is well documented. Prolonged job searching with no feedback is corrosive in a way that rejection with a reason is not — silence provides no interpretation, so people supply their own, usually unfavourable.

The time cost is concrete and compounds with disadvantage. Multi-stage processes with unpaid assessments select for people who can afford the hours, which is a bias operating before any evaluation happens.

And there is no recourse. No route to ask, no route to appeal an automated screening decision, and no way to know whether a human was ever involved — which is now the subject of emerging regulation precisely because the asymmetry is so complete.

## What a Solution Looks Like
Close every application. Automatic rejection when a role is filled or an application is screened out is a workflow rule, costs nothing, and eliminates the indefinite silence that is the single most-complained-about feature of the process.

Show status honestly. Where an application sits, how many are in the pipeline, when a decision is expected and whether the role is still open are all known to the system and would let candidates direct their effort rationally.

Give a reason where one exists. A screening rejection has a reason — a requirement not met, a stage not passed — and stating it costs nothing. Where the reason is volume rather than the candidate, saying so is more honest than implying a deficiency.

Bound the unpaid time and disclose it upfront. The full process, its stages and its expected time commitment, stated before someone begins, lets a candidate choose — and payment for assessment stages beyond a reasonable threshold would end the escalation.

And provide a route to contest an automated decision. Where a screening decision is made without human review, the candidate should be able to request one, which is both reasonable and the direction regulation is moving.

## Impact If Solved
This is the experience of a very large number of people at a vulnerable point in their lives, and almost every failure in it is a workflow choice rather than a technical constraint. Closing applications, showing status, giving reasons, bounding unpaid time and providing a route to human review are cheap, immediate and would change a process that currently treats applicants as an input to one that treats them as participants — which also improves the applicant pool for the employers who do it first.

# The Principal Investigator Writing Proposals at Midnight

**Industry:** [[contract-research-labs|Contract Research Labs]]
**Type:** Worker Life Changing
**One-liner:** A principal investigator at a small research firm writes most proposals in evenings and weekends before a portal closes, knowing most will lose, and gets back the research hours and the sleep if the assembly work stops being theirs.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #word-embeddings #k-nearest-neighbors #evaluation-metrics #worker-facing #automation

## The Problem
At a research firm of five to fifty people, the scientists who run projects are also the people who win them. A PI with three active contracts is expected to stay 70–85% billable to those contracts (estimate), so proposal writing happens in the hours that are left: evenings, weekends, and the last 72 hours before the agency portal closes. A DoD SBIR Phase I proposal needs a technical volume within a strict page limit, a cost volume and budget justification, a commercialisation plan, and a growing set of forms, including the foreign-affiliation disclosures that agencies now screen before award. A Phase II proposal is several times larger.

Much of it is reassembly rather than thinking. The PI hunts through old proposals for the team biographies, facilities description and the paragraph on a technique the firm has used ten times; reformats references; trims a page to hit the limit; re-derives a labour budget the firm's pricing spreadsheet already knows. With DoD selecting about one Phase I proposal in five (National Academies, FY2022), four out of five of these nights produce nothing, and the PI starts again at the next solicitation.

## Why It Matters to the Worker
These are people who took the job to do research and spend a large share of their discretionary time writing marketing for it. The work is unpaid in any direct sense — bid-and-proposal hours sit in overhead — and highly visible when it fails: a PI who goes a year without a win is soon a PI without a project to charge to. The deadline compression is relentless, especially at firms that bid across several agencies whose cycles overlap, and it lands on the same handful of senior staff every time.

It also blocks juniors. Early-career scientists rarely get to lead a proposal because the senior PI cannot spare the time to coach them through the assembly, so the firm's capacity to win never grows past a few people.

## What a Solution Looks Like
An assistant inside the firm's own environment that drafts the reusable parts of a proposal from the firm's library of past proposals, reports and CVs, matched to the topic's language; checks page limits, fonts, required sections and forms against the specific solicitation; drafts the budget justification from the pricing model; and keeps a living library of approved boilerplate so nothing is hunted for twice. The PI writes the technical idea and the approach — the part reviewers actually score — and reviews the rest.

## Impact If Solved
Halving assembly time on a Phase I proposal returns perhaps 20–30 hours per proposal to a PI (estimate), which across a dozen proposals a year is several weeks of research or rest, and lets the firm hand proposal leadership to juniors without drowning them.

# Partner Discovery and Recruitment

**Industry:** [[affiliate-networks|Affiliate Networks]]
**Type:** Low Impact (Customisation Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Finding the publishers who could sell a merchant's products is done by browsing a marketplace directory sorted by size, which is how every merchant ends up recruiting the same two hundred partners.
**Tags:** #word-embeddings #bert #k-nearest-neighbors #graph-neural-networks #gradient-boosting #contrastive-learning #evaluation-metrics #feature-engineering

## The Problem
A merchant joining a network gets access to a publisher marketplace: a searchable directory with categories, traffic estimates and self-described audiences. The affiliate manager filters by category, sorts by size, and sends recruitment messages to the top of the list. So does every other merchant in that category. The large publishers are saturated with offers and negotiate accordingly; the mid-tail — the sites and creators who would convert well for a specific product and are not already committed — is functionally invisible because nobody can find them at scale.

Matching is genuinely hard to do by eye. A merchant selling technical outdoor gear needs publishers whose audience buys technical outdoor gear, not publishers filed under Outdoors. Fit depends on content specificity, audience purchase behaviour, existing merchant relationships, commercial intent and whether the publisher's audience overlaps with the merchant's existing customers or extends beyond them — which is the difference between recruiting a partner who grows the business and one who takes credit for it.

## What Already Exists
Every network operates a marketplace with search and filtering; Impact and Partnerize have the most capable discovery tools. Third-party intelligence — Publisher Discovery, Similarweb, Ahrefs, Semrush — lets a manager work out who ranks for relevant terms and who a competitor is working with. Agencies maintain private lists built over years, which is the genuine competitive asset in the category. Creator-side platforms like LTK and ShopMy have their own matching, built around aesthetics and audience rather than search intent.

## The Customisation Gap
Directory search matches on stated category; fit is determined by demonstrated behaviour, and the network holds the demonstrated behaviour. It knows what each publisher's traffic actually converts on, at what basket size, with what return rate, across every merchant on the network — an outcome record of extraordinary specificity that is used to populate a leaderboard rather than to make matches.

The customisation each merchant needs is the direction of fit. A merchant with an established customer base wants partners whose audience they do not already reach; a new brand wants partners whose audience converts on adjacent products at all. Those are opposite queries and the same directory answers both identically. Neither is answerable without joining the merchant's own customer data to the network's publisher performance record, which nothing currently does.

Content-level matching is the second gap. A publisher is not one thing — a large site has sections that convert on kitchenware and sections that convert on nothing. Matching at page or content-cluster level rather than at domain level is what makes mid-tail discovery work, and it requires representing content and audience behaviour rather than reading a category label.

## Impact If Solved
Recruitment is the input to everything else in the channel and is currently a bottleneck that concentrates spend on the partners least likely to be incremental. Making the mid-tail findable expands the supply of genuine content partners, which is the side of the channel that has been shrinking, and it gives networks a reason for a merchant to stay beyond the switching cost of re-tagging their site.

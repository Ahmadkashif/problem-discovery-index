# The Showroom Consultant Chasing a Homeowner's Faucet

**Industry:** [[plumbing-and-hvac-distributors|Plumbing & HVAC Distributors]]
**Type:** Worker Life Changing
**One-liner:** The showroom consultant gets back evenings and credibility by no longer discovering, at the plumber's rough-in, that a valve doesn't fit its trim or a finish is three months out.
**Tags:** #worker-facing #graph-neural-networks #k-nearest-neighbors #large-language-models #data-integration #workflow-orchestration

## The Problem
A plumbing distributor's decorative showroom sells to homeowners and designers on behalf of the plumber or builder who will install. A consultant spends two hours with a couple choosing a shower system, a freestanding tub filler, three lavatory faucets, a kitchen faucet and accessories, across brands and finishes. The selection then has to become an order: each trim needs the right rough-in valve, which differs by brand, model and sometimes by year; a pressure-balance trim won't fit a thermostatic body; a tub filler needs a specific floor-mount rough; "brushed gold" from one brand isn't the same colour as another's; and a discontinued item or a twelve-week lead time can surface after the homeowner has signed off. The consultant tracks dozens of open projects at once in the ERP, a spreadsheet and a binder, answers homeowner calls about substitutions and calls from plumbers standing in a framed bathroom asking where the valve is.

## Why It Matters to the Worker
Showroom consultants are paid partly on sales and judged on whether jobs go smoothly, but most of the problems that reach them start upstream in manufacturers' catalogues and lead times. When a rough valve is wrong, the plumber has opened a finished wall or waits on site, the builder calls the branch manager, and the consultant spends a day reordering and apologising. The work is detailed and personal — they remember who wanted what — and it spills into evenings and weekends because homeowners shop then. Turnover is high, and a consultant who leaves takes the open projects' history with them.

## What a Solution Looks Like
A selection workspace that checks compatibility as the homeowner chooses: every trim linked to its required rough-in, valve type and connection; finishes matched across brands; lead times and discontinuations pulled live; and a complete bill of materials, including the rough parts the homeowner never sees, generated for the plumber's schedule. Changes are tracked with the homeowner's sign-off, and the rough-in package is released ahead of the finish package automatically.

## Impact If Solved
If wrong or missing rough-in parts affect ~5–10% of showroom projects today (estimate), catching them at selection saves the consultant several hours per incident and spares the plumber a return trip, while consultants can carry more projects without working weekends to keep them straight.

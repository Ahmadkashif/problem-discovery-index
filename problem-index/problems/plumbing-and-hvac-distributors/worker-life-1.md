# The Counter in a Heat Wave

**Industry:** [[plumbing-and-hvac-distributors|Plumbing & HVAC Distributors]]
**Type:** Worker Life Changing
**One-liner:** The counter salesperson gets back the ability to serve the technician in front of them without four phone lines, a text queue and a parts lookup across three manufacturers' sites pulling them apart at once.
**Tags:** #worker-facing #large-language-models #transformers #cnns #k-nearest-neighbors #automation #workflow-orchestration

## The Problem
In July, an HVAC branch counter opens at 6:30 or 7 a.m. to a line of technicians who need to be on a roof by eight. Each wants something different: a dual-run capacitor, a condenser fan motor, a contactor, a control board for a twelve-year-old furnace, a cylinder of R-454B, a line set, a coil that matches the condenser on the truck. Behind the counter, the phones ring with dealers asking whether a 3-ton A2L system is in stock at another branch, technicians text photos of rusted nameplates, and will-call orders placed overnight have to be pulled. The counter person looks up model numbers in the manufacturer's parts site, checks a cross-reference for a universal motor, confirms a refrigerant cylinder sale against the technician's EPA Section 608 card, and keys the sale into Eclipse — then does it again for the next person. On a 100°F day a branch may serve ~150–300 counter and phone transactions (estimate), and the questions get harder as A2L equipment brings new parts and rules.

## Why It Matters to the Worker
The counter person carries the technician's frustration. A wrong capacitor means a technician drives back across town and the homeowner waits another hour without cooling, and the counter takes the blame. The job demands knowledge that took years to build — which universal motor really fits, which board replaced the obsolete one — but most of the day goes to looking things up and keying them in while being interrupted. Summer means ten-hour days and Saturday openings, and the people who know the most get the most interruptions. Many good counter staff leave for the dealers they serve, where the hours are no better but the work is less frantic.

## What a Solution Looks Like
A counter assistant that takes whatever arrives — a model and serial number, a nameplate photo, a texted description — and returns the exact OEM part, approved universal alternatives, stock across branches and any warranty status, in seconds. Texted orders and photos are triaged before the counter person sees them, with the part identified and picked for will-call; refrigerant sales check the 608 certification automatically; and the sale writes itself into the ERP for one-tap confirmation. The counter person handles the exceptions and the conversation.

## Impact If Solved
If part lookups fall from a few minutes to seconds and half the texted orders arrive pre-identified (estimate), a counter person reclaims ~1–2 hours a peak day, wrong-part return trips fall, and the newest hire can answer what only the veteran could.

# The Quality Manager Who Is the Quality System

**Industry:** [[aerospace-parts-suppliers|Aerospace Parts Suppliers]]
**Type:** Worker Life Changing
**One-liner:** At a 40-to-150-person supplier, one quality manager answers every customer corrective-action request, hosts every audit and signs every first article, so their evenings and weekends belong to deadlines other companies set; a tool that drafts the response from the shop's own records would give those back.

## The Problem
The quality manager at a tier-2 or tier-3 supplier usually runs the AS9100 system with one or two engineers and a few inspectors. Their week is shaped by other people's clocks. A prime issues a supplier corrective action request (SCAR) after a rejected part, wanting containment within 24–48 hours and a root-cause and corrective-action response in about 30 days (estimate, varies by customer), filed in that customer's portal in that customer's format. Registrar surveillance audits, Nadcap audits of in-house special processes, and customer source inspections each take days of preparation. Every new purchase order brings a list of quality clauses to review. Every FAI needs their signature.

Much of the SCAR work is assembly: find the traveller, the inspection records, the operator training records, the last three similar nonconformances, and the corrective actions that were supposed to stop them; write the 8D; attach evidence; upload. The manager has often done the same analysis before for a different customer and cannot find it.

## Why It Matters to the Worker
It is personal accountability without control. A quality escape at a customer is traced to their system and often to their signature, and a run of SCARs can put the shop on a prime's probation list, which threatens jobs they know by name. The deadlines fall on top of the daily work, so responses get written at night. In small shops the role has no deputy, which means no real holidays during audit season, and the knowledge of why each corrective action exists lives in their head. Many come from the inspection bench and took the job for the problem-solving, and they spend their time on formatting.

## What a Solution Looks Like
A workspace that pulls everything related to a nonconformance (traveller, inspection data, operator and machine, material lot, prior similar cases and their corrective actions) the moment a SCAR arrives. It drafts the containment and 8D response in the requesting customer's format with the evidence attached, and shows which past corrective actions failed to prevent recurrence. It keeps one register of every open action and audit finding with its due date, across all customers, and does the audit prep from the same records.

## Impact If Solved
Cutting SCAR response assembly from ~6–12 hours to ~2 (estimate) and audit preparation by half returns roughly a day a week to a manager handling ~4–8 SCARs a month (estimate). It also cuts repeat findings, because the recurrence check runs on every response rather than when the manager remembers.

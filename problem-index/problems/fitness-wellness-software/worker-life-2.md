# Instructor Schedule Fragmentation

**Industry:** [[fitness-wellness-software|Fitness & Wellness Software]]
**Type:** Worker Life Changing
**One-liner:** Instructors teaching across several studios stop assembling their week from a group chat and a calendar app, because sub requests, availability and pay reach them in one place instead of five.
**Tags:** #optimization-fundamentals #gradient-boosting #large-language-models #evaluation-metrics #workflow-orchestration #automation #worker-facing

## The Problem
Most fitness instructors work for several studios. A full-time-equivalent income is assembled from classes at three or four locations, often on different platforms, frequently as independent contractors, plus private clients.

Nothing coordinates it. Each studio has its own system, its own schedule, its own sub request process — usually a group chat where an unfillable class is broadcast to everyone until someone volunteers. The instructor maintains a personal calendar that reconciles all of it, and the reconciliation is manual.

The consequences are ordinary and constant. Double bookings when two studios schedule the same slot. Classes accepted without accounting for travel time across a city. Sub requests missed because they went to a chat nobody was watching. Pay arriving from four sources with different rates, different structures and different timing, tracked in a personal spreadsheet if at all.

Studios experience the same problem from the other side: an unfilled class at short notice is a cancelled class, which is a bad experience for every member who booked it.

## Why It Matters to the Worker
Instructors are the product. Members choose a studio because of a specific person's class, and instructor departure is one of the strongest predictors of member churn. Yet the working conditions of the role are unusually poor: contractor status, no benefits, income that varies with class attendance, and an administrative burden the instructor carries entirely alone.

The fragmentation is the part that could most easily be fixed and is not, because each platform serves the studio and treats the instructor as a resource assigned to a class rather than as a person with a working week.

Burnout and attrition among instructors is high, and it takes members with it. The studios lose more from an instructor leaving than they save on every efficiency they have ever implemented.

## What a Solution Looks Like
A single view of the instructor's actual week across every studio they teach at, with travel time represented, so that a class accepted at one location cannot be silently double-booked against another.

Sub requests routed by fit rather than broadcast: instructors qualified for the class, available at that time, within reasonable travel, ranked by likelihood of accepting based on their own history. That fills classes faster and stops the group chat from being an interruption for everyone else.

Availability published once and respected everywhere, rather than communicated separately to each studio.

Earnings consolidated across studios, with the class-level detail behind them, so the instructor can see what they actually earn per hour including travel — a number most of them have never computed and would find informative.

## Impact If Solved
Instructor retention is upstream of member retention, and instructors are leaving a role whose administrative conditions nobody has taken responsibility for. Coordinating the week is a modest technical problem with a direct effect on the churn the whole sector complains about.

# MNPI Clearance of Research Inputs

**Industry:** [[hedge-funds|Hedge Funds]]
**Type:** Low Impact (Customisation Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Every expert call, channel check, dataset and management meeting has to be cleared for material non-public information, and the clearance is a compliance officer reading things one at a time.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #bert #transformers #evaluation-metrics #compliance #workflow-orchestration #automation

## The Problem
Research at a fundamental fund runs on conversations: expert-network calls with former employees, suppliers and customers; channel checks; meetings with company management; conferences; calls with sell-side analysts. Each carries the risk that someone passes on material non-public information, and the fund's compliance programme — required under the Investment Advisers Act's policies-and-procedures and insider-trading provisions — must pre-clear, chaperone or review a meaningful share of them.

In practice this means a compliance officer approving call requests against a restricted list, sometimes listening live, reviewing transcripts or notes afterwards, checking datasets for how they were collected, and maintaining wall-crossing records when the fund is brought over the wall on a deal. A busy analyst may request dozens of calls a month. The queue is the constraint on research speed, and the review is uneven because it depends on which officer reads which transcript.

## What Already Exists
Electronic communications surveillance from Global Relay, Smarsh and Behavox; compliance management platforms such as ComplySci and StarCompliance for personal trading and restricted lists; expert networks' own compliance screens and transcript libraries; and lexicon-based alerting.

## The Customisation Gap
Surveillance tools are built to find misconduct after the fact in communications; the research workflow needs a forward-looking clearance on research inputs. The fund-specific logic is: matching a requested expert's employment history and the topics to be discussed against current holdings, the restricted list, and recent corporate events; classifying transcript passages by MNPI risk type (non-public financial results, undisclosed transactions, confidential customer data) rather than keyword hits; linking a dataset's provenance documentation to the fund's own policy; and producing an audit trail an SEC examiner can follow. None of this is in a generic surveillance lexicon.

## Impact If Solved
Triaging research inputs by risk lets compliance spend its attention on the few calls and documents that warrant it and approve the rest in minutes, removing the most common wait in the research workflow while making the fund's MNPI controls more consistent, not less.

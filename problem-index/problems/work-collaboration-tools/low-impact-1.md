# Template and Workflow Libraries

**Industry:** [[work-collaboration-tools|Work Collaboration Tools]]
**Type:** Low Impact (Customisation Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Every platform ships hundreds of templates as an onboarding accelerant, and teams abandon them within a month because a generic marketing campaign template describes nobody's actual marketing campaign.
**Tags:** #bert #word-embeddings #k-means-clustering #large-language-models #dbscan #evaluation-metrics #transfer-learning

## The Problem
Templates exist to solve the empty-workspace problem. A new team opens the tool, sees nothing, and does not know how to structure their work. So every vendor ships a library: product launch, marketing campaign, sprint, onboarding, event planning, editorial calendar.

Teams use one for a week and drift off it. The generic template has phases that do not match the team's process, fields nobody fills, and gates that do not exist in this company. Adapting it takes as long as starting fresh, so most teams start fresh, and the workspace fills with ad hoc structures that nobody can compare, roll up or report across.

The result is that a company with four hundred projects has four hundred shapes, portfolio reporting is impossible, and the platform's own analytics have nothing consistent to analyse.

The template that would have worked is the one the team's best-run previous project actually used — which is in the same workspace, and which nothing offers back.

## What Already Exists
Template galleries are standard in Asana, Monday.com, Notion, Atlassian, Smartsheet and every competitor, with hundreds of options and community-contributed libraries. Custom template creation is supported everywhere. Automation rules and workflow builders are mature. Some platforms allow saving an existing project as a template.

## The Customisation Gap
Templates are authored top-down by a vendor content team and describe a generic process. What a team needs is its own successful process, generalised — and the platform holds every project that team has ever run, including which ones went well.

Inducing a template from history is a well-shaped problem: cluster completed projects by shape, identify the recurring phases, tasks, roles and dependencies, and propose the structure that the team's own successful projects share. Nobody does it, though the same platforms have been storing the projects for a decade.

Outcome linkage is the missing dimension that would make it valuable rather than merely convenient. Which structural choices are associated with projects that finished on time — a review gate here, a dependency there, a certain phase length — is estimable across the customer base and would turn a template gallery into evidence.

Drift detection is the third gap: a team that abandoned the structure mid-project is telling you the structure was wrong, and that is observable and never reported.

## Impact If Solved
Structural inconsistency across projects is why portfolio reporting in these platforms does not work, and it originates in templates that describe nobody's process. Inducing structure from a team's own successful projects makes standardisation attractive rather than imposed, using data the vendor has been accumulating without using.

# The Freelance Paralegal With Fifteen Supervising Attorneys

**Industry:** [[legal-support-services|Legal Support Services]]
**Type:** Worker Life Changing
**One-liner:** A freelance paralegal drafts for a dozen or more solo attorneys, each with their own forms, software login, court and habits, and spends the unbillable part of every day keeping those worlds — and the conflicts between them — apart.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #k-nearest-neighbors #evaluation-metrics #worker-facing #workflow-orchestration #compliance

## The Problem
Freelance and contract paralegals sell drafting to attorneys who cannot justify a full-time hire: discovery requests and responses, deposition summaries, medical chronologies, probate inventories, family-law financial disclosures, bankruptcy schedules. Each attorney sends instructions by email or text — often two lines — expects a draft back by a date set by a court deadline, and reviews and signs it. The paralegal works inside each client's practice-management seat or from their emailed files, in that attorney's own templates, citing that county's local rules.

The unbillable work multiplies with every client. Separate logins and folder conventions; separate style preferences remembered from past corrections; time tracking for invoices each attorney wants formatted differently; and a conflicts problem most freelancers manage in a spreadsheet: having drafted for the plaintiff's lawyer in one matter, they must not touch the defence side, and a two-attorney town can make that hard to see. The unauthorised-practice line sits over all of it. The paralegal may draft but not advise, may not deal with the attorney's client directly without supervision, and carries the reputational risk if an attorney signs without reading.

## Why It Matters to the Worker
Freelancers bill by the hour or the task at rates well below what the attorney bills for the same draft, and every minute spent re-learning a client's preferences, chasing missing documents or reformatting an invoice is a minute they cannot bill. Work arrives in bursts tied to other people's deadlines, so weeks of overtime alternate with gaps, without the benefits or paid leave of an in-house job.

The pressure is personal. A draft returned with the same correction a third time costs a client; a conflict missed costs a reputation that is the freelancer's only marketing.

## What a Solution Looks Like
A workspace built for one paralegal serving many attorneys rather than one firm serving many clients: a per-attorney profile that learns style and form preferences from each attorney's past edits; a matter register that runs a conflict check across every client before a new assignment is accepted; deadline tracking keyed to the court; a document request list sent to the attorney automatically when inputs are missing; and time capture that produces each attorney's invoice in their format. A supervision record shows that every deliverable went to an attorney for review before use.

## Impact If Solved
An estimated five to eight unbillable hours a week returned to a full-time freelancer (estimate), fewer repeat corrections, and a documented conflicts and supervision record that protects both the paralegal and the attorneys who rely on them.

## 1. Enforcement Transparency and Appeals Platform
#ai-platform #transformers #large-language-models #confidence-intervals #bayesian-inference #compliance #worker-facing #evaluation-metrics

**Concept:** A governance layer that turns enforcement from a category into a reasoned decision. Every action names the segment that produced it — which timespan, which visual or audio element, which transcript passage — and carries a calibrated confidence that routes both the consequence and the review path. Appeals are prioritised by expected error and by the size of the economic consequence rather than handled uniformly, which is what makes human review affordable where it changes outcomes. And it runs a standing sampled audit, independently adjudicated, producing a false-positive rate by policy category, by language and by content genre.

**Inputs:** Video, audio and transcript with timestamps; policy definitions and labelled examples; segment-level classifier outputs; historical decisions with appeal outcomes; creator economic exposure; language and genre metadata.

**Outputs / Actions:** Localised, actionable enforcement notices that end the defensive self-censorship the current opacity produces. Confidence-routed consequences, so a marginal advertiser-suitability judgement and an unambiguous violation are not treated identically. A prioritised appeal queue with the localised evidence in front of the reviewer. A published error rate broken out by language — the disparity that is largest, least examined and entirely invisible in aggregate reporting.

**Why now:** The Digital Services Act's statement-of-reasons and appeal requirements are forcing this from the outside, and the content understanding needed to localise a decision is already running on every upload for other purposes. Meeting the obligation in substance rather than in form is the difference between a compliance artefact and a system creators can actually use.

**Market:** Every UGC platform subject to the new regulatory regimes, the smaller platforms who need this capability and cannot build it, and the trust and safety vendors who could supply it.

## 2. Rights Claim Adjudication Platform
#ai-platform #contrastive-learning #graph-neural-networks #gradient-boosting #confidence-intervals #compliance #evaluation-metrics #revenue-impact

**Concept:** A layer between content matching and enforcement that asks the question matching never asks — whether this claim is a reasonable assertion of rights. It scores claimant behaviour from dispute and release history, breadth of claiming and patterns on public domain repertoire; it reads match context, distinguishing a three-second incidental background match from a full backing track from a critical review quoting a clip; and it routes accordingly rather than acting uniformly. It verifies reference library submissions in proportion to the enforcement power a reference confers, which is the entry point that makes the worst abuse possible.

**Inputs:** Claim histories with dispute outcomes; match duration, prominence and foreground-versus-background position; surrounding content type; reference submissions and their provenance; public domain and known-disputed repertoire.

**Outputs / Actions:** Claims routed by legitimacy and context instead of treated identically. Escrowed disputed revenue rather than payment to the claimant pending resolution — the single cheapest fix available and one that requires no model at all. Proportionate reference verification. Reporting that shows both revenue correctly retained by creators and genuine claims still enforced, together, since either measure alone can be gamed.

**Why now:** Years of dispute outcomes sit in these systems as a large labelled dataset that nobody has used to evaluate claimants, and the pattern of abuse is well enough documented that the current uniform treatment is hard to defend.

**Market:** UGC video and audio platforms operating content matching, the rights holders who are disadvantaged when the system's credibility erodes, and the creator organisations pressing for reform.

## 3. Moderator Protection Platform
#ai-platform #transformers #cnns #large-language-models #gradient-boosting #compliance #worker-facing #evaluation-metrics

**Concept:** A platform that applies the industry's own content understanding to reducing the harm its review layer absorbs. It determines what genuinely requires human viewing and at what fidelity, presenting material blurred, greyscale, muted, thumbnail-only or as a transcript summary by default and expanding only when the decision requires it. It isolates the segment at issue so a reviewer sees nine seconds rather than an hour. It tracks cumulative severe-category exposure per reviewer per shift as a managed health metric with enforced limits and rotation, the way professions with comparable documented risk have managed it for decades. And it separates speed targets from judgement quality in the audit.

**Inputs:** Content with segment-level severity estimates; the information reviewers actually used to reach past decisions; per-reviewer exposure history by category; audit outcomes and accuracy; queue composition and staffing.

**Outputs / Actions:** Reduced-fidelity review interfaces validated against full-fidelity decision accuracy, because exposure reduction that degrades decisions has traded one harm for another. Segment isolation. Enforced exposure limits with rotation into lower-severity queues. Audit criteria that reward contextual correctness rather than literal policy application — which improves both the work and the enforcement errors creators experience. Reporting on exposure removed as the outcome measure.

**Why now:** The occupational harm is documented in litigation, in reporting and in the platforms' own vendor requirements, and the tooling to reduce it is the same content understanding already running on every upload. This is the clearest case in the industry where capability and need sit in the same organisation and have not been connected.

**Market:** Platforms operating human review at scale, the outsourcing vendors who employ most of this workforce and carry the direct liability, and the regulators and litigants who have made this an accountable question rather than a discretionary one.

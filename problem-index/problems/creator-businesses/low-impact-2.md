# Multi-Platform Repurposing

**Industry:** [[creator-businesses|Creator Businesses]]
**Type:** Low Impact (Customisation Opportunity)
**One-liner:** One piece of content must become six, each cut for a different platform's format and audience, and the work scales linearly with platforms while the team does not.
**Tags:** #cnns #object-detection #large-language-models #bert #transfer-learning #evaluation-metrics #automation #workflow-orchestration

## The Problem
A long-form video becomes short vertical clips, a podcast episode, an audiogram, a set of stills, a newsletter section and a thread. Each platform has its own aspect ratio, length convention, captioning style, opening pattern and audience expectation.

Clipping is the labour. Identifying the moments that work standalone requires understanding both the content and each platform's norms, and it is done by a person scrubbing a timeline. Reframing to vertical requires tracking the subject. Captions need burning in with styling that differs per platform. Each clip needs its own hook, which is usually not the moment's original opening.

Volume expectations are brutal and asymmetric. Short-form platforms reward daily posting; the long-form content that generates the clips arrives weekly. So the clipping work is not a by-product, it is a second production line.

Performance differs unpredictably across platforms. The same clip can do nothing on one and travel widely on another, and there is no reliable way to predict which, so the strategy is to post everything everywhere and see.

And every platform's analytics live separately, so understanding what actually works across a creator's whole footprint means assembling data from five dashboards by hand.

## What Already Exists
Opus Clip, Vizard, Descript and CapCut automate clip identification and reframing with varying quality. Auto-captioning is mature. Scheduling tools handle multi-platform publishing. Some analytics aggregators pull cross-platform metrics. Subject tracking for vertical reframing is a solved computer vision problem.

## The Customisation Gap
Clip selection is generic rather than creator-specific. Existing tools select on transcript features and generic engagement heuristics; what actually works is specific to a creator's audience and format, and their own clip performance history is the training data that nobody uses.

Hook generation is the unaddressed half. A clip's opening seconds determine its performance and are usually inherited from wherever the cut started. Generating a hook — a restructured opening, an overlaid line, a reordered first beat — is where the value sits and where the tools stop.

Platform-specific adaptation is superficial. Beyond aspect ratio and captions, genuine adaptation means different pacing, different opening structure and different framing, and that is currently a human judgement applied clip by clip.

Cross-platform analytics are assembled manually, which means no creator has a reliable view of which content actually earns its production cost across their whole footprint.

## Impact If Solved
Repurposing is a second full production line staffed by the same people as the first, and it is the main reason creator teams burn out at the size they do. Creator-specific clip selection, generated hooks and genuine platform adaptation turn a linear cost into a marginal one, which is the difference between maintaining a multi-platform presence and being consumed by it.

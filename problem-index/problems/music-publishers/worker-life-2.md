# The Signed Writer Waiting on the Statement

**Industry:** [[music-publishers|Music Publishers]]
**Type:** Worker Life Changing
**One-liner:** A songwriter on a publishing deal lives on an advance and learns what their songs earned up to a year after the fact, in a statement they cannot check, and would get back the ability to plan their life and judge their deal.
**Tags:** #time-series-forecasting #bayesian-inference #confidence-intervals #large-language-models #worker-facing #data-integration

## The Problem
A staff writer signed to a publisher is paid an advance against future royalties, often in instalments tied to delivering a set number of songs. Until the advance recoups, the writer's share of income is kept by the publisher to pay it back. Statements usually come twice a year, around 60–90 days after each half closes (estimate). The money in them is older still, because it has already passed through PROs, the MLC and foreign societies, each with its own lag. A song released in spring may show its first meaningful income on a statement a year later (estimate).

So the writer works blind. They can see a co-write streaming well on Spotify and climbing in Brazil, but they cannot tell what that is worth, which share of it is theirs, whether it is being collected abroad, or when they will recoup. The statement, when it arrives, is a long list of codes, sources and deductions that few writers can read and fewer can verify. Many respond by taking the next advance, which pushes recoupment further away.

## Why It Matters to the Worker
For the writer, the problem is personal cash flow and dignity, not accounting. Rent is monthly, while statements arrive twice a year and are a year out of date. A writer who cannot see what they earn cannot judge whether their deal is fair or whether a rival publisher's offer is better. They cannot tell whether a collaborator's publisher has registered the song correctly, or whether a shortfall is a missed registration they could still fix. The information gap also sours the relationship with the creative manager, who usually cannot answer either.

## What a Solution Looks Like
A writer-facing view that estimates earnings as usage happens. It would start from streaming and airplay data, apply the writer's actual shares and deal terms, and project when each income stream will arrive and when the advance will recoup, with honest ranges rather than single numbers. Each estimate would be reconciled against the statement when it lands, and any gap would be explained: a registration missing in a territory, a conflict holding a share, a source not yet reporting. A plain-language assistant would answer questions about the statement from the writer's own lines. The goal is not to replace the statement but to make it unsurprising.

## Impact If Solved
A writer who can see a credible recoupment date can plan around it, chase their own missing registrations and negotiate their next deal from evidence. Publishers that offer this keep writers at option time, which is why transparency portals became a selling point in signing contests.

# The Quality Reviewer Judging Sessions by Rebooking

**Industry:** [[online-tutoring-platforms|Online Tutoring Platforms]]
**Type:** Worker Life Changing
**One-liner:** Someone at the platform is responsible for tutor quality and the only instruments they have are star ratings, rebooking rates and complaints.
**Tags:** #transformers #bert #large-language-models #gradient-boosting #confidence-intervals #evaluation-metrics #compliance #worker-facing

## The Problem
Tutor quality functions exist at every serious platform: vetting applicants, monitoring performance, handling complaints, and deciding who is removed. The instruments available are ratings, rebooking rates, cancellation and response metrics, and complaint volume.

None of them measure teaching. A tutor with excellent ratings may be doing the student's homework for them, which parents rate well and which produces no learning. A tutor with mediocre ratings may be insisting on difficult work that produces exactly the learning the family is paying for. The reviewer cannot distinguish these from the metrics and usually cannot watch enough sessions to find out.

Complaints are the main qualitative input and are unrepresentative — driven by personality clashes, scheduling problems and expectation mismatches as much as by teaching quality. A serious complaint about conduct requires a safeguarding response, and these platforms deal with minors, which makes the reviewer's judgement genuinely consequential.

Vetting at the front end is similarly weak. Subject knowledge can be tested; teaching ability largely cannot be, and the correlation between credentials and effectiveness is known to be modest. So the platform admits broadly and manages by outcome metrics that do not measure the outcome.

## Why It Matters to the Worker
This is a role accountable for the quality of a service the organisation has no instrument to measure. The reviewer knows the metrics are proxies, acts on them anyway because there is nothing else, and removes people's income sources on that basis.

The safeguarding dimension is heavy. Any complaint touching on conduct with a minor requires immediate, careful and consequential judgement, frequently on ambiguous information, with the reviewer aware that both over-reacting and under-reacting have serious consequences. Most platforms provide policy and few provide the training or the support that this work warrants.

And there is no feedback. A removed tutor does not generate evidence that the removal was right, and a retained one does not either, so the function cannot calibrate and its accuracy is unknown to itself — the same gap that runs through every platform enforcement role.

## What a Solution Looks Like
Build an instrument. Session analysis — with appropriate consent and safeguards, since these are recordings involving minors — can identify pedagogically meaningful behaviour: who is talking, whether the student is working problems or watching, whether questions probe understanding or supply answers, whether difficulty is appropriate. That is a far better quality signal than a star rating and it is derivable from recordings the platform already holds.

Separate satisfaction from effectiveness deliberately. Reporting both, and being explicit that they diverge, lets the platform reward what it actually wants rather than collapsing everything into a rating that measures warmth.

Triage complaints by severity with a real pathway. A safeguarding concern and a scheduling dispute should never share a queue, and the safeguarding path needs trained response, defined escalation and support for the reviewer — which is a resourcing decision the sector has largely not made.

Measure the removals. Sampling, audit and appeal tracking produce a false positive rate for a decision that ends somebody's income, and without it the thresholds are set blind.

And give tutors formative feedback rather than only enforcement. Session analysis that tells a tutor what would make them more effective is the version of this that improves the service, rather than one that only identifies who to remove.

## Impact If Solved
Tutor quality management currently removes people's income on the basis of proxies that measure whether a student enjoyed the session. Session-derived pedagogical signals give the function a real instrument, separating satisfaction from effectiveness aligns the platform's incentives with what families are buying, and proper safeguarding triage addresses the most serious responsibility this role carries with the least support.

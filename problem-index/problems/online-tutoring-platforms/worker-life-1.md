# The Tutor Working Unpaid Hours Around Paid Ones

**Industry:** [[online-tutoring-platforms|Online Tutoring Platforms]]
**Type:** Worker Life Changing
**One-liner:** Preparation, marking, parent messages and progress notes are what makes tutoring work and none of them are paid, so doing the job well costs the tutor money.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #gradient-boosting #transformers #time-series-forecasting #evaluation-metrics #worker-facing #automation #revenue-impact

## The Problem
Tutors are paid per session. The work that surrounds a session is not: reviewing the student's homework and identifying what went wrong, preparing materials for the topic, marking practice work between sessions, replying to parent messages, writing progress notes, and reading the syllabus or exam specification the student is working to.

That work is what distinguishes effective tutoring from an hour of conversation. A tutor who prepares properly for a session delivers substantially more and earns exactly the same, which means the per-hour return on doing the job well is negative. The rating system does not reliably reward it either, since a warm unprepared session frequently rates as highly as a rigorous prepared one.

Schedule fragmentation multiplies the cost. Tutoring demand concentrates in after-school hours and weekends, so a tutor's paid sessions are scattered across evenings with unpaid gaps between them that are too short to use for anything else. A tutor working fifteen paid hours may be occupied for thirty.

Commission takes a share of the paid portion only, so the platform's fee is levied on the visible hour while the invisible ones are entirely the tutor's.

And income is unstable. Demand is seasonal, students finish courses, exams end, and the platform's ranking can shift a tutor's bookings without explanation.

## Why It Matters to the Worker
This is a compensation structure that systematically underpays diligence. The people who prepare thoroughly — generally the more experienced and effective tutors — earn least per hour worked, which is a direct pressure toward doing less of what makes the service valuable.

The invisibility is the specific grievance. Neither the platform nor the family sees the preparation, so neither values it, and a tutor who raises it is describing work that nobody asked for as far as the fee structure is concerned.

The fragmentation is a quality-of-life problem distinct from the pay. Evenings and weekends broken into non-contiguous paid hours make it difficult to hold other work or to have a predictable life, which is why so much of this workforce is part-time and transient.

And the lock-in means there is no exit into an independent practice. The relationships that would let a tutor build their own business are the platform's, contractually, which removes the normal route by which a good freelancer graduates out of a marketplace.

## What a Solution Looks Like
Reduce the unpaid work rather than arguing about paying for it. Session preparation materials generated from the student's history and the topic, homework marking assistance, drafted progress notes produced from the session itself, and drafted parent communications address the bulk of the invisible hours directly.

Make the preparation visible. A record showing what a tutor prepared and what the student did between sessions gives families something to see and creates a basis on which thorough tutors can charge more — which is the market mechanism the current structure suppresses.

Improve the schedule. Clustering bookings into contiguous blocks where student availability allows, and showing a tutor which slots have high booking probability, converts scattered evenings into usable working periods.

Give tutors a portable record. Sessions delivered, subjects, student outcomes where measured, and preparation history, in a form the tutor owns, is the professional capital the lock-in otherwise denies them.

And be honest about the total. Reporting earnings per hour worked rather than per session, with preparation included, is uncomfortable for a platform and is the number the tutor is actually deciding against.

## Impact If Solved
A per-session fee structure on work that is substantially not sessions creates a direct financial penalty for doing the job well, applied to the tutors who do it best. Automating the surrounding work removes most of the unpaid burden, making preparation visible creates a route to being paid for it, and schedule clustering addresses the fragmentation that makes this work hard to sustain — which is why the workforce turns over as fast as it does.

# Diagnostic Assessment and Session Planning

**Industry:** [[online-tutoring-platforms|Online Tutoring Platforms]]
**Type:** Low Impact (Customisation Opportunity)
**One-liner:** A tutor's first session is spent working out what the student does not know, and the platform that has watched a million such sessions offers no help with it.
**Tags:** #hidden-markov-models #bayesian-inference #gradient-boosting #transformers #confidence-intervals #evaluation-metrics #k-nearest-neighbors #worker-facing

## The Problem
A new student arrives with a stated problem — struggling with algebra, preparing for an exam, behind in chemistry — and the tutor must work out what is actually wrong. That diagnosis is the highest-value thing a tutor does and it is done from scratch, in the first paid session, through conversation and trial problems.

The stated problem is frequently not the real one. A student failing at algebra may have a fractions gap from three years earlier; one struggling with essay writing may have a reading comprehension issue. Identifying the actual missing prerequisite is what distinguishes effective tutoring, and it takes an experienced tutor time that the family is paying for.

Session planning has the same shape. What to cover next, how much to review, when to move on — decisions made from the tutor's memory of the last session and their own judgement, with no record beyond whatever notes they keep personally.

And nothing accumulates. A student who works with a tutor for six months and then switches starts over: the new tutor receives no history, no map of what has been covered, and no record of the diagnosis that took three sessions to establish.

## What Already Exists
Some platforms provide diagnostic assessments, generally short and coarse. Curriculum-aligned content and practice problems are available on the larger platforms. Shared whiteboards and document tools support the session itself. Session notes exist as a free-text field most tutors use sparsely. Adaptive learning systems in the broader education technology market do sophisticated knowledge tracing and are mostly separate from human tutoring marketplaces.

## The Customisation Gap
The gap is that knowledge tracing — estimating what a student knows from their responses, a mature and well-studied technique — is standard in adaptive learning products and almost absent from human tutoring platforms, despite these platforms holding far richer evidence: full session recordings of a student working through problems with an expert probing their understanding.

A diagnostic that identifies likely prerequisite gaps from a short adaptive assessment, delivered before the first session, would let a tutor start with a hypothesis rather than an investigation, and would make the first paid hour productive rather than exploratory.

Session continuity is the second gap and is nearly free. A structured record of what was covered, what the student found difficult, what was mastered and what remains, maintained automatically from the session rather than typed by a tired tutor at nine in the evening, makes tutoring transferable between tutors and gives the family visibility.

And the platform could learn what works. Across millions of sessions, which explanatory approaches precede a student succeeding at a problem type is an answerable question and would give tutors something no individual's experience provides. That requires session analysis the platforms are not doing and must be approached carefully, since these are recordings of minors.

## Impact If Solved
Diagnosis and planning are where tutoring quality actually lives, and both are done from scratch by each tutor with no support and no accumulation. Pre-session adaptive diagnostics make the first paid hour productive, automatic session records make tutoring continuous across tutors and visible to families, and cross-session learning about what explanations work is the one form of accumulated craft knowledge this market could produce and currently discards.

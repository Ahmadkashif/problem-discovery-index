# The Researcher Who Was Six Hours Late

**Industry:** [[bug-bounty-platforms|Bug Bounty Platforms]]
**Type:** Worker Life Changing
**One-liner:** A week of work produces a genuine vulnerability, someone submitted the same thing that morning, and the payment is zero.
**Tags:** #gradient-boosting #k-nearest-neighbors #contrastive-learning #confidence-intervals #time-series-forecasting #evaluation-metrics #worker-facing #revenue-impact

## The Problem
Bug bounty income is contingent on several things a researcher cannot control. Being first to a finding, since duplicates pay nothing. The programme's severity assessment, which sets the bracket. Scope interpretation, decided after submission. And the programme's responsiveness, since a report can sit for weeks before anyone reads it.

The duplicate outcome is the sharpest. Substantial effort producing a real vulnerability, submitted hours after someone else, earns nothing — and the researcher frequently cannot verify the claim, because duplicate determinations are made by the programme and the original report is not shown.

The distribution of earnings is extremely concentrated. A small number of researchers earn substantially; most earn little, and the median participant is well below any reasonable hourly rate for the skill involved. The platforms' public communications emphasise the top of that distribution, which shapes how many people enter and what they expect.

Choosing where to spend time is the recurring strategic problem and is made blind. Which programmes are responsive, pay fairly, rate severity generously and have scope that is actually open is knowledge that circulates informally in the community and is not available in any structured form.

And the working relationship has no protections. No contract, no minimum, no recourse beyond a mediation process run by the platform whose revenue comes from the programmes.

## Why It Matters to the Worker
This is skilled technical work performed speculatively with no guarantee of payment, in an arrangement where the terms are interpreted after the work by the party paying. Most labour markets do not work this way and this one does because the alternative is not participating.

The uncertainty is corrosive over time. Researchers describe the duplicate outcome in particular as the thing that pushes people out — not the difficulty of the work, but the experience of doing it well and receiving nothing for reasons they cannot verify.

The concentration means most participants are effectively subsidising the model. Programmes receive attention from many researchers and pay only the first to each finding, which is efficient for the buyer and means the aggregate effort substantially exceeds the aggregate payment.

And the career path is unclear. Skills built here are genuinely valuable and the pathway into stable employment is informal, so many highly capable people remain in a lottery-shaped income for years.

## What a Solution Looks Like
Give researchers the information to choose. Programme responsiveness, historical severity ratings relative to the platform-wide reference, duplicate rates, scope breadth and payment timeliness are all measurable by the platform and would let a researcher allocate their time to programmes that treat people well — which also creates the competitive pressure that would improve the ones that do not.

Make duplicate determinations verifiable. A researcher told their finding is a duplicate should see enough of the original to confirm it, with appropriate redaction. This is a policy decision, it is the single most requested change in the community, and it directly addresses the most damaging experience in the model.

Reduce wasted effort at the source. Real-time indication that an area is already under active investigation, or that a class of finding in this scope has been repeatedly reported, would let a researcher redirect before spending the week rather than after.

Price partial credit. Programmes that pay something for a well-documented duplicate, or that operate collision windows, retain researcher goodwill at modest cost — and the platform is positioned to make that a norm rather than an exception.

And publish the honest distribution. Reporting what participants actually earn, not the top of it, lets people make an informed decision about entering.

## Impact If Solved
This model depends on a population of skilled people accepting speculative unpaid work under terms interpreted afterwards, and its attrition is driven by a small number of specific and fixable experiences. Verifiable duplicates, programme quality transparency and collision handling would change the participation calculus materially — and a larger, less demoralised researcher population is the thing every programme actually wants to buy.

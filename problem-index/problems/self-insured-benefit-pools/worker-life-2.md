# The Loss-Control Consultant With 150 Member Sites

**Industry:** [[self-insured-benefit-pools|Self-Insured Benefit & Risk Pools]]
**Type:** Worker Life Changing
**One-liner:** A pool's loss-control consultant gets back the evenings spent turning site-visit notes into narrative reports nobody reads, and the satisfaction of seeing their recommendations completed.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #cnns #object-detection #causal-inference #worker-facing #workflow-orchestration #quick-win

## The Problem
A public entity pool employs a few loss-control or risk-management consultants. Each covers a territory of perhaps 100 to 200 member entities (estimate): towns, fire districts, water authorities, small school districts. They drive to each one at least once a year. They walk the public works yard, the playgrounds, the pool, the jail or holding cells, the fleet garage and the council chambers, and check a list: fall hazards, playground surfacing, sidewalk trip edges, sprinklers and alarms, vehicle and driver records, use-of-force and pursuit policies, sexual-misconduct training for youth programmes. They photograph what they find and talk to whoever is responsible, often a clerk who also runs elections and payroll.

Then the writing. Back at the hotel or home, the consultant turns notes and photos into a report with recommendations. That means a page or two of narrative per site, written to a template, then entered into the pool's system. The recommendation letter goes to the member, and follow-up is an email in three months. The consultant rarely learns whether anything was fixed, and almost never whether a fix prevented a claim. When a claim does come in, a playground fall or a jail suicide, the first question from the claims department is whether loss control had flagged it.

## Why It Matters to the Worker
The job attracts people who care about preventing injuries: former firefighters, police officers, safety engineers. They spend a large share of their week on report writing and data entry rather than on the conversations that change a small member's behaviour. Their judgement about which hazards matter most is built over hundreds of site visits and is invisible to the pool. Burnout comes from the sense that recommendations disappear into members' inboxes, and from being second-guessed after a claim.

## What a Solution Looks Like
A field tool that drafts the report while the consultant walks the site: photos tagged to hazard types, voice notes turned into recommendations in the pool's standard language, and last year's open items pre-loaded so the visit starts with what remains undone. After the visit, the member receives a short, prioritised list with deadlines and examples. The pool can track completion by member and hazard type. Over time, the pool can see which recommendations, once completed, are followed by fewer claims, and the consultant can stop recommending things that do not matter.

## Impact If Solved
If report writing fell from hours to minutes per site, a consultant could visit more members or spend longer with the ones that need help. A pool that could show completion rates and claim effects would know which consultants' judgement to spread across the team, and the consultant would finally see their work land.

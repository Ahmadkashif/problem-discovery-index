**Industry:** [[stock-media-marketplaces|Stock Media Marketplaces]]

## 1. Contribution Accounting Platform
#ai-platform #diffusion-models #contrastive-learning #causal-inference #confidence-intervals #evaluation-metrics #revenue-impact #compliance

**Concept:** A platform that answers what a contributor's work is worth when it trains a model. It applies training data attribution to the marketplace's own corpus and model, publishes the method with its known failure modes, and reports per-asset influence rather than distributing by a formula unrelated to contribution. It computes output-time similarity between generated results and specific training assets, which is a weak but real signal and lets compensation follow use rather than inventory. It measures the substitution effect directly — where generated and library results compete in the same search result sets, the displacement is observable — so a contributor whose income fell can be told how much of it was generative competition rather than seasonality or ranking. And it makes every compensation input legible, which is the part that determines whether the arrangement feels negotiated or imposed.

**Inputs:** Training corpus with full provenance; model weights and configuration; generated outputs and prompts; corpus and output embeddings; search result composition; licensing revenue by asset and category over time; contributor catalogues and their characteristics.

**Outputs / Actions:** Per-asset influence estimates with stated uncertainty and method. Output-time attribution per generation. Substitution effect decomposition by category. Legible compensation statements showing every input. A genuine contributor opt-in decision on training use, with the compensation difference attached — which is what converts a unilateral arrangement into a market.

**Why now:** The question is being settled by litigation and by unilateral formula, and the marketplaces with clean provenance are the only parties who could demonstrate a principled attribution. That is also the only argument that distinguishes a licensed corpus from a scraped one on grounds other than legal exposure, which makes it commercially useful rather than merely fair.

**Market:** Stock media marketplaces, music and audio libraries, publishers and archives licensing to model developers, and collective licensing bodies. The buyer is the marketplace itself; the durable argument is that a demonstrated attribution is the thing that makes licensed data worth paying for.

## 2. Contributor Intelligence Agent
#ai-agent #contrastive-learning #bert #gradient-boosting #change-point-detection #time-series-forecasting #worker-facing #revenue-impact

**Concept:** An agent that gives contributors the information the marketplace already has. It reports demand gaps — what buyers searched for and did not find, clustered and ranked — which is the single most valuable thing a marketplace could tell a contributor and which nobody does. It decomposes income movements into catalogue growth, ranking changes, royalty rate mix, seasonality, demand shift and generative substitution, so a halved income becomes an explanation rather than a mystery. It diagnoses each asset as failing at surfacing, at the click, or at conversion, because those are three different problems with three different remedies. It suggests concept and use-case keywords learned from what buyers actually type rather than from what the pixels contain. And it reports ranking changes as dated events, so a contributor knows the algorithm changed rather than concluding their work stopped being good.

**Inputs:** Search query logs with results, views and licences; abandoned searches and reformulations; per-asset impressions, clicks and conversions; royalty rate mix and licensing terms; catalogue composition over time; ranking change events; generative substitution estimates.

**Outputs / Actions:** Ranked demand gap reports. Income decomposition with each term quantified. Per-asset failure diagnosis. Buyer-vocabulary keyword suggestions. Dated ranking change notifications. Revenue forecasts with intervals so normal variation is recognisable as such.

**Why now:** Contributors build catalogues over years and watch the income move for reasons they cannot see, which makes the work feel like a lottery rather than a profession. Everything required to explain it sits in the marketplace's own logs, and the effort misallocation — shooting against a download history that lags demand by a year — is a direct cost to the marketplace as well.

**Market:** Stock media marketplaces, music and sound libraries, font and design asset marketplaces, and print-on-demand platforms with the same structure. It sells as contributor retention and supply quality, which is the constraint every marketplace in this category actually faces.

## 3. Submission Pipeline Agent
#ai-agent #cnns #contrastive-learning #object-detection #k-nearest-neighbors #large-language-models #worker-facing #compliance

**Concept:** An agent that restructures review from asset-by-asset judgement into targeted decisions. It measures the objective technical properties — focus, noise, exposure, compression artefacts, aberration — removing a large share of decisions and, more importantly, removing the variance that makes contributors feel treated arbitrarily. It clusters near-duplicate batches so forty frames from one shoot become one decision with a best-of-cluster pre-selection. It computes catalogue saturation for the concept an asset occupies, which is the real reason behind many rejections and is currently hidden behind a technical-quality reason code. It detects and localises faces, logos, landmarks and protected designs, checks submitted releases correspond to the people actually visible, and applies encoded jurisdictional rules rather than relying on reviewer memory. And it writes rejection reasons a contributor can act on.

**Inputs:** Submitted assets and batches; catalogue embeddings and saturation by concept; release documents as submitted; jurisdictional rules on releases, editorial restrictions and trademarks; historical decisions, appeals and their outcomes; downstream licensing performance of accepted assets.

**Outputs / Actions:** Objective technical assessments. Batch clusters with best-of-cluster selection. Saturation figures shown at the point of decision. Localised rights detections with recognisability scored and the reasoning shown. Release correspondence checks. Specific, actionable rejection reasons naming the region, the logo or the saturation. Outcome feedback to reviewers on whether accepted assets licensed — the calibration signal the role has never had.

**Why now:** Submission volumes make careful per-asset assessment impossible, the reason codes cannot express what the reviewer saw, and the resulting appeals consume the capacity the pace was meant to create. Every component here is mature technology applied unevenly across an industry that needs it uniformly.

**Market:** Stock media marketplaces, print-on-demand and design asset platforms, user-generated content marketplaces and digital asset management vendors. The metric is assets reviewed per hour and appeal rate, with decision consistency being the change contributors will actually notice.

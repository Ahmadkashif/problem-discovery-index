# The Contributor Watching an Unexplained Income

**Industry:** [[stock-media-marketplaces|Stock Media Marketplaces]]
**Type:** Worker Life Changing
**One-liner:** Contributors build a catalogue over years and watch its income move for reasons they cannot see, decided by search ranking, licensing terms and now by models trained on their own work.
**Tags:** #gradient-boosting #time-series-forecasting #change-point-detection #contrastive-learning #confidence-intervals #evaluation-metrics #worker-facing #revenue-impact

## The Problem
A contributor's income is downloads times a royalty rate. Both move and neither is explained.

Downloads depend on search ranking, which depends on an algorithm the marketplace does not describe. A contributor whose income halves cannot tell whether the catalogue grew, their keywords aged, the ranking changed, demand shifted, or generated alternatives began appearing in the same result sets.

Royalty rates changed structurally across the industry as subscription licensing displaced single-image sales. A download under a subscription plan pays a fraction of what an individual licence paid, and the mix is determined by the marketplace's commercial decisions.

Generative models added a new and unexplained term. Contributor funds distribute money by formula, the formula's relationship to any individual's contribution is unstated, and the same models are producing alternatives that compete with the library.

The relationship is entirely asymmetric. Terms change unilaterally, rates change unilaterally, and the contributor's options are to accept or to withdraw a catalogue that took years to build and that is often distributed across several marketplaces under different terms.

And there is no feedback that would let them improve. Contributors do not know which searches their assets appear in, what buyers wanted and did not find, or why one image earns and a similar one does not.

## Why It Matters to the Worker
The income is real and often primary, and it moves without explanation, which makes planning impossible and makes the work feel like a lottery rather than a profession.

There is no leverage. Individual contributors have no bargaining position, the marketplaces are few, and collective organisation has been limited.

Years of accumulated work sit inside a platform under terms that can change, which is the specific insecurity of this profession and has no obvious mitigation.

The generative question is existential and is being decided without them. Whatever the outcome, contributors are observers of a negotiation about the value of their own work.

And the effort is misallocated for want of information. A contributor shoots what they believe sells, based on a download history that lags demand by a year and tells them nothing about what buyers searched for and did not find.

## What a Solution Looks Like
Explain income movements. Decomposing a change into catalogue growth, ranking changes, rate mix, seasonality, demand shift and generative substitution is a decomposition the marketplace can compute and currently does not report.

Show demand gaps. What buyers search for and do not find is the most valuable thing a marketplace could tell a contributor, it is sitting in the search logs, and it would redirect production toward genuine unmet demand.

Give per-asset diagnostics. Whether an asset fails at surfacing, at the click, or at conversion, and what would change it.

Report ranking changes as dated events. When the algorithm changes, contributors deserve to know that it did, rather than concluding their work has stopped being good.

Make generative compensation legible. Whatever formula is used, show the inputs; and report the substitution effect on the contributor's own categories, which is measurable and is currently the largest unexplained term in their income.

Forecast with intervals so that a contributor can plan and can recognise normal variation for what it is.

## Impact If Solved
This profession supplied the visual commons for two decades and now watches its economics change without explanation or participation. Decomposing income movements, publishing demand gaps and making generative compensation legible would not restore the leverage, and it would give contributors the information to make decisions — which is the minimum a marketplace dependent on their continued work owes them.

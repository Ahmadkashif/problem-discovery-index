# The Awareness Manager Everyone Resents

**Industry:** [[security-awareness-training|Security Awareness Training]]
**Type:** Worker Life Changing
**One-liner:** The role is to send colleagues fake emails designed to deceive them, assign them training when it works, and then ask them to trust the security team.
**Tags:** #causal-inference #gradient-boosting #confidence-intervals #large-language-models #evaluation-metrics #worker-facing #compliance #hypothesis-testing

## The Problem
A security awareness manager runs the programme: simulations, training assignment, completion chasing, reporting to leadership, and the internal communications that are supposed to make people care.

The position is structurally adversarial. The core activity is deceiving colleagues in order to test them, and the people deceived experience it as a trick regardless of the intent. Every simulation campaign generates complaints, and the ones that use emotionally significant lures — bonuses, benefits, job security — generate more than complaints. Those templates exist in the standard libraries and are used because they produce high click rates, which is the metric.

The manager is also the person who assigns remedial training to colleagues who failed, chases completion, and in some organisations supplies the list that goes to managers. Where results are used punitively, the awareness manager is the face of it even when the policy came from elsewhere.

And they are asked to demonstrate value with a click rate they know is partly a statement about their own template choices. Leadership wants the number to fall, the manager can make it fall by choosing easier simulations, and everyone involved is somewhat aware of this.

## Why It Matters to the Worker
This is a job that requires damaging the trust it also depends on. Security functions rely on people reporting mistakes and asking questions, and a programme built on deception and remediation makes people less likely to do either — which the manager can see happening.

The complaints are personal and frequent. Being the person who sent the fake redundancy notice is a position nobody wants, and the manager usually did not choose the template library or the policy on punitive use.

The metric problem is a professional integrity problem. Being asked to produce an improving number, knowing you control the difficulty, is a corrosive position — and arguing for harder simulations means arguing for a worse-looking result.

And the role has low status. It is frequently a junior position in the security function, with responsibility for organisation-wide behaviour and authority over nothing.

## What a Solution Looks Like
Fix the metric so the manager is not gaming it. Difficulty-controlled reporting makes an improving number mean something, and it removes the incentive to make simulations easier. A manager who can show resistance at a fixed difficulty level has a defensible result.

Change the primary objective to reporting. Reporting rate and speed are behaviours the programme can build without deceiving anyone punitively, they are the behaviours that limit harm, and optimising them puts the manager on the same side as their colleagues.

Remove the harmful templates and be able to justify it. Emotionally significant lures produce high click rates and measurable distress, and a manager who can show that the resistance benefit does not require them has an argument. Measuring the trust cost alongside the click rate is what makes that argument possible.

Separate the programme from discipline. Where results feed performance management, reporting collapses — and that is measurable. A manager able to demonstrate the effect has grounds to get the policy changed, which is currently a values argument they lose.

And automate the administration. Assignment, chasing, reporting and communications are most of the week and none of the value.

## Impact If Solved
This role damages the trust the security function depends on, using a metric its owner controls, and is frequently the most disliked function in an organisation for reasons that are structural. Difficulty control, a reporting-first objective, evidence about the trust cost of harmful templates and separation from discipline would let the manager run a programme that works without the adversarial dynamic that currently defines it.

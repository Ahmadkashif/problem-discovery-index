# Booking a Reporter Licensed for That Room

**Industry:** [[court-reporting-firms|Court Reporting Firms]]
**Type:** Low Impact (Customisation Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Scheduling software books people into time slots; a deposition needs an officer licensed in the venue's state, with the right certifications and add-ons, offered on the same terms to every party, often by tomorrow morning.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #gradient-boosting #time-series-forecasting #optimization-fundamentals #workflow-orchestration #automation #data-integration

## The Problem
A deposition notice arrives as a PDF in an email: case caption, witness, date, place or video link, method of recording, and sometimes a request for realtime, a videographer or an interpreter. A scheduler at a reporting firm keys it in, then works the phone and text messages to find a reporter who is free, licensed where the witness sits, certified for realtime if counsel wants a feed, and willing to take the page rate. Bookings move constantly. Depositions are continued, cancelled the night before, extended to a second day, or noticed for the next morning. A large share of the scheduler's day is re-matching jobs that have already been booked once.

## What Already Exists
General scheduling and dispatch tools (Calendly-style booking, field-service dispatch platforms, per diem shift marketplaces used in nursing and hourly staffing) and the national agencies' own client portals. CAT and production software tracks the job once it is assigned. Small firms run on a shared calendar, a spreadsheet and the owner's phone.

## The Customisation Gap
The deposition-specific layer is what generic tools lack. They need to parse notices into structured jobs, including the recording method the noticing party named under FRCP 30(b)(3)(A). They need licensing rules by venue: in California, Texas, Illinois and other certification states the officer must hold that state's licence or qualify by reciprocity, as 72 reporters did in California in the first months of 2025 through NVRA. They need certification matching (realtime, CART, legal video, interpreter language), with the remote-versus-in-person decision priced in. They need cancellation-fee and minimum-appearance rules per client, and a neutrality check, so that services offered to one party under an insurer's volume contract are offered to all. And they need a realistic forecast of which bookings will actually go ahead. A shift marketplace knows none of this, and a mismatch can cost more than a lost booking: a deposition taken before an unqualified officer can be challenged.

## Impact If Solved
A scheduler who can turn a notice into a confirmed, compliant booking in minutes, and see which tomorrow-morning jobs are likely to cancel, could cover more jobs with the same reporter pool. For a regional firm handling thousands of depositions a year (estimate), that means fewer dropped jobs and fewer apologetic calls to the lawyer whose witness flew in.

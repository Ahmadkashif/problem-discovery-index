# The Member Services Rep Explaining a Denial

**Industry:** [[health-insurance-carriers|Health Insurance Carriers]]
**Type:** Worker Life Changing
**One-liner:** Member services reps take frightened calls about denied claims and stalled authorizations with ten systems open and no clear answer in any of them. What they would get back is the ability to tell a sick person what happened and what to do next.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #k-nearest-neighbors #evaluation-metrics #worker-facing #automation #workflow-orchestration

## The Problem
Customer service representatives are the largest occupation at health insurance carriers: 70,860 of the 430,460 people employed in NAICS 524114 (BLS OEWS, May 2023). A large share of their calls are members asking why something was denied, why an authorization is still pending, why the explanation of benefits shows a balance, or whether a doctor is in network. Answering means finding the claim in the core platform, the authorization in the utilization management system, the denial reason code and its letter, the member's benefit configuration, and sometimes the provider's contract status. Each lives in a different application with its own search screen.

Volume peaks are brutal. Medicare plans face the Annual Enrollment Period from October to December and the January surge when new members discover their plan works differently. Marketplace plans faced a year in which premiums jumped and subsidised members switched to bronze plans with high deductibles (bronze rose from 30% to 40% of 2026 selections, CMS). Average handle time and first-call resolution are measured on every call.

## Why It Matters to the Worker
The rep is often the first human a member reaches after a denial letter, and the member may be in treatment, in pain or caring for someone who is. The rep is measured on speed, but explaining a denial correctly means reading a reason code written for a claims system, translating it, checking whether an appeal is possible and what it needs, and not promising anything the plan will not honour. Getting it wrong generates a grievance with the rep's name on it. Annual turnover in carrier call centres commonly runs ~30%–45% (estimate), and the job's emotional load is the reason most often given.

## What a Solution Looks Like
A single member view assembled for the call: the claim, the authorization, the reason code translated into plain language with the specific missing item or policy, the member's appeal rights and deadline, and the closest similar resolved cases. The rep sees a draft explanation and next steps, edits it and reads or sends it. Calls that are really grievances or appeals are classified and routed with the regulatory clock started, so the rep does not have to know every rule.

## Impact If Solved
Cutting the search across systems would cut handle time on denial calls by ~20%–30% (estimate) and raise first-call resolution on denial calls. For the rep, the larger change is being able to give a clear, correct answer and a next step, rather than a transfer, to someone who called because they were scared.

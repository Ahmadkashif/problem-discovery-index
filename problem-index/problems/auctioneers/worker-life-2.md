# Pickup Day and the Non-Paying Bidder

**Industry:** [[auctioneers|Auctioneers]]
**Type:** Worker Life Changing
**One-liner:** Every online close is followed by days of chasing failed payments, rescheduling pickups, quoting shipping and arguing about condition, and the owner and the office clerk absorb it at night and on weekends.
**Tags:** #worker-facing #logistic-regression #gradient-boosting #large-language-models #evaluation-metrics #workflow-orchestration #automation

## The Problem
An online timed auction closes on a Tuesday night with 700 lots sold to 300 bidders (illustrative). By Wednesday morning the office clerk is looking at declined cards, bidders who say they bid by mistake, winners in three other states asking for shipping quotes, and an inbox of questions about pickup times. Pickup is usually a ~2–3-hour window (estimate) at the house or the shop a few days later; bidders arrive late, send a friend without a receipt, bring a car for a dresser, or never come. Lots that are not paid for or not collected have to be offered to the under-bidder, relisted, or returned to the consignor, and each one changes the settlement.

Then come disputes. A bidder says the lamp was described as working and is not, or a tool was missing from a box lot. The auctioneer decides whether to refund, argue or absorb the cost, knowing that one bad review on the platform affects the next sale's bidding.

## Why It Matters to the Worker
In a firm of ~3–10 people (estimate) the post-sale work lands on the owner-auctioneer and one or two office staff, after a week that already included cataloguing and the sale itself. Much of it happens by phone and text, in the evening, with bidders who are angry or evasive. Non-paying bidders feel personal: the clerk has to tell a consignor their item will be resold, and the owner has to decide whether to ban a local regular. Pickup days mean standing in a driveway for hours, loading furniture into strangers' vehicles in the rain.

The stress is predictable and repeated weekly in busy seasons, and it is the part of the job auctioneers least expected when they went to auction school.

## What a Solution Looks Like
A post-sale assistant that sends invoices and payment reminders the moment the auction closes, retries cards, and scores each winning bidder's risk of not paying from their history on the firm's sales, so deposits or pre-authorisations can be required for risky registrants before the next sale. It books pickup slots, sends directions and reminders, generates shipping quotes from lot dimensions, drafts replies to routine questions and condition complaints from the catalogue text and photos for the clerk to approve, and offers unpaid lots to under-bidders automatically under the terms of sale.

## Impact If Solved
Automating reminders, scheduling and routine replies could return a working day per sale to a small office (estimate). Catching likely non-payers at registration cuts relisting and re-settlement work, and the owner gets evenings back during the busiest months.

# Consignor Settlement and Escrow Trust Accounting

**Industry:** [[auctioneers|Auctioneers]]
**Type:** Low Impact (Customisation Opportunity)
**One-liner:** General accounting software can hold a trust account; it cannot split one sale's proceeds across forty consignors on different commission schedules, net out unpaid bidders and sales tax, and meet a state's escrow deadline without a spreadsheet in between.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #descriptive-statistics #compliance #automation #workflow-orchestration #data-integration #quick-win

## The Problem
A consignment auction may sell goods for dozens of consignors in one night: a farm retirement with neighbours' equipment added, a monthly antiques sale, a municipal surplus sale with several departments. Each consignor has a contract with its own commission rate, often tiered, plus charges for advertising, trucking, cleaning or title work, and sometimes an advance already paid. Buyer's premium belongs to the auctioneer; sales tax belongs to the state, except where a dealer presented a resale certificate; a lot whose buyer never paid either goes back to the consignor or is resold. The clerk or the owner's spouse reconciles all of it to produce settlement statements and cheques, often on a weekend, while the state's rules say when the money must reach the escrow account and how often it must be reconciled.

## What Already Exists
QuickBooks and Xero handle trust and operating accounts; clerking packages (Auction Flex, Wavebid, AuctionMethod, Maxanet) produce consignor statements from sale results; payment processors built into the bidding platforms (ATG's atgPay processed 67% of U.S. LiveAuctioneers transaction value in FY25) collect from buyers. Real estate trust-accounting software exists for brokers.

## The Customisation Gap
The missing layer is the auction-specific rulebook joined across systems. Commission schedules with tiers, minimums and per-lot fees; buyer's premium and its sales-tax treatment, which varies by state; resale-certificate validation; unpaid-bidder reversals and second-chance offers; consignor advances netted at settlement; 1099 reporting where it applies; and the state's escrow clock. In Florida, for example, unsettled proceeds must be in an insured in-state escrow or trust account within two working days of the auction, with a separate ledger reconciled monthly and records kept two years; other licensing states set their own deadlines and bond or recovery-fund rules. The data sits in the bidding platform, the clerking package, the payment processor and the bank, and the reconciliation happens in a spreadsheet nobody audits until a consignor complains to the licensing board.

## Impact If Solved
Automated settlement that applies each contract's terms, reconciles platform payouts to the bank and flags late escrow deposits would cut settlement from a day of clerical work per sale to an hour of review (estimate), shorten the wait consignors complain about most, and give the firm a clean record when the licensing board or the recovery fund comes asking.

# The Servicing Coordinator Re-Delivering to Every Licensee

**Industry:** [[film-distributors|Film Distributors]]
**Type:** Worker Life Changing
**One-liner:** The coordinator who re-services each licensee's delivery requirements from the materials the distributor already holds gets back the evenings spent re-papering the same film for every buyer, and the dread of being the reason a licence fee is held.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #bert #worker-facing #automation #workflow-orchestration #data-integration

## The Problem
Delivery happens twice in distribution. First the producer, usually through a post house, closes the distributor's own delivery schedule of ~80–200 items (estimate). That delivering side is analysed under postproduction studios as [[niches/postproduction-studios/delivery-schedules-and-cue-sheets/profile|Delivery Schedules, Cue Sheets & Paper Deliverables]]. Then the distributor licenses the film onward, and each licensee sends a schedule of its own. A streamer, a pay-TV channel in Germany and an airline each want a different subset in a different form: their own master and caption specs, a music-and-effects track for dubbing, the cue sheet in their template, chain-of-title documents, and an errors-and-omissions certificate naming them as additional insured. Each outbound licence is ~40–120 items (estimate). That is fewer than the full schedule the producer delivered, because each licensee takes only its own territory, media and versions, but every item has to be re-checked against that licensee's terms.

The servicing coordinator holds this for every title and every licensee. They match each licensee's schedule against the materials on the shelf, order the versions that don't exist yet, and get the insurer to reissue the certificate. They check that each music licence actually covers the new territory and medium. When a licensee rejects the package, because a licence stops short of the territory, the certificate names the wrong entity or the captions came from an unapproved vendor, the coordinator starts that item again. Often that means emailing a producer who delivered two years ago and has moved on.

## Why It Matters to the Worker
The work is invisible when it goes right and personal when it goes wrong. A stalled delivery holds a licence fee the sales team has already announced, and finance asks the coordinator why. The coordinator is usually junior, carries dozens of open deliveries at once, and spends the day in spreadsheets and inboxes rather than learning the business they joined to learn. The knowledge of what each licensee actually rejects lives in their head and in old email threads. When they leave, the next coordinator relearns it one rejection at a time.

## What a Solution Looks Like
A servicing workspace per title. It holds the materials the distributor accepted from the producer, each licensee's schedule, and the distributor's history of what every licensee has rejected and why. For a new licence it maps the licensee's items onto materials already held, lists what must be made or re-papered, and checks documents against the new licence: territories and media in each music licence, named insureds and limits on the E&O certificate, approved caption vendors. It drafts the chase emails and assembles the package, flagging gaps before submission rather than after rejection.

## Impact If Solved
If first-time acceptance rises and each rejection cycle is avoided, the coordinator gets back several hours a week. Licence fees arrive weeks sooner. A junior role that is mostly chasing becomes one that learns the rights business, and the next hire inherits the rejection history instead of rediscovering it.

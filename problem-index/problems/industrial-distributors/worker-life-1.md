# The Outside Rep's Evenings

**Industry:** [[industrial-distributors|Industrial Distributors]]
**Type:** Worker Life Changing
**One-liner:** An outside sales rep gets back the evenings spent typing up plant visits, chasing quote status and preparing tomorrow's calls, and spends the day on the plant floor where the account is won.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #gradient-boosting #feature-engineering #worker-facing #automation #workflow-orchestration

## The Problem
An outside sales rep at an industrial distributor covers a territory of ~60–150 accounts (estimate), from a few large plants to many small shops, and spends the day driving between them. At each stop they walk the maintenance shop, talk to the planner and the storeroom attendant, look at the failed parts on the bench, note a gearbox that keeps failing or a competitor's vending machine that just appeared, and promise a quote or a visit from an application specialist. Very little of it gets recorded on site. The notes go into a phone, a notebook or memory.

The paperwork comes after hours. In the evening the rep types visit notes into the CRM because the sales manager reviews them, emails inside sales with quote requests built from photos and scribbles, checks which open quotes the customer hasn't answered, and looks up what each of tomorrow's accounts bought last quarter and what is on backorder for them. Each item is small; together they take ~1–2 hours a night (estimate). Skipped, the quote goes out late, the CRM shows nothing, and the next visit starts cold.

## Why It Matters to the Worker
The job sells itself as relationship selling and problem-solving on the plant floor, and the best reps are good at exactly that. The evening admin is what wears them down: unpaid in effect, invisible to customers, and the first thing to slip when the territory gets busy — after which the rep is blamed for thin CRM records and slow quotes. Reps with families feel it most, and the trade loses good ones to jobs with predictable hours.

It also shapes their pay. Many outside reps earn commission on gross margin, so a quote that goes out a day late, or a follow-up that never happens, is money out of their own pocket.

## What a Solution Looks Like
A visit assistant on the rep's phone. They talk for a minute in the truck after each stop; it writes the CRM note, drafts the quote request to inside sales with the photos attached and the likely parts identified, and creates follow-up tasks. Each morning it gives a brief per account: last orders, open quotes and their age, backorders, and anything the plant searched for on the website and didn't buy. Open quotes are chased by email automatically, and the rep is told only when a customer replies or goes quiet.

## Impact If Solved
If evening admin falls from ~1–2 hours a night to ~15 minutes (estimate), a rep gets back ~5–8 hours a week, quotes from visits go out the same day instead of the next, and the CRM finally holds what was said on the plant floor.

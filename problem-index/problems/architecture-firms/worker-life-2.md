# Thirteen Years to a Licence

**Industry:** [[architecture-firms|Architecture Firms]]
**Type:** Worker Life Changing
**One-liner:** An architecture graduate needs years of logged experience across six practice areas and six exam divisions to call themselves an architect, and the firm's staffing decides whether they ever see the work the licence requires.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #k-nearest-neighbors #feature-engineering #logistic-regression #worker-facing #compliance #workflow-orchestration

## The Problem
In 2024 it took an average of 12.9 years from starting school to earning a US architecture licence (12.3 years in 2025, per NCARB). A candidate must complete the Architectural Experience Program — ~3,740 hours across six practice areas (estimate) from practice management to construction and evaluation — and pass the six divisions of the Architect Registration Examination, whose pass rate fell to 55% in 2024. More than 39,000 people were working toward licensure in 2024, nearly all of them employed in firms while they did it.

The exams and the hours collide with how firms staff projects. Junior staff are most productive on construction documents, so that is where they stay. Hours in construction administration, project management and practice management — areas the candidate needs both for AXP and for the exam — go to licensed staff. Candidates log hours from memory against categories that don't match their timesheet codes, study at night for exam divisions that test site visits and contract administration they have never done, and pay for retakes out of pocket. Supervisors sign off hours without a clear view of what the candidate has actually done.

## Why It Matters to the Worker
Without a licence they cannot use the title, cannot stamp drawings, and in most firms cannot become an associate or principal; the pay gap between licensed and unlicensed staff widens through their thirties. Every failed division costs months and exam fees, and every year without the right experience extends the timeline. Many leave the profession before finishing, and the cost falls hardest on people who cannot afford to study unpaid or switch firms to get the exposure they need.

## What a Solution Looks Like
A licensure assistant connected to the firm's timesheet and project system. It maps actual tasks — the submittal reviews, the site visit, the fee proposal they helped draft — to AXP experience areas automatically, shows the candidate and supervisor exactly which areas are short, and recommends specific upcoming tasks on live projects that would fill them. For the exam, it generates practice scenarios from the firm's own projects (a real submittal log, a real change order) mapped to ARE objectives, and tracks which topics the candidate keeps getting wrong.

## Impact If Solved
If a candidate's experience gaps are visible and staffed against from their first year, the path to licensure could shorten by a year or more (estimate), retake costs fall, and firms keep the people they spent a decade training instead of losing them at the point they become most valuable.

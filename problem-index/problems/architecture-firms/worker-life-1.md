# The Construction Documents Crunch

**Industry:** [[architecture-firms|Architecture Firms]]
**Type:** Worker Life Changing
**One-liner:** In the weeks before a permit or bid set, junior architectural staff spend their nights on sheet setup, schedules, keynotes and redline pickup, and the design work they trained for gets the least of their time.
**Tags:** #transformers #large-language-models #cnns #object-detection #worker-facing #automation #workflow-orchestration

## The Problem
The last four to six weeks before a construction document deadline follow the same pattern in most firms. The model exists; now it has to become a set. Junior designers and job captains place views on sheets, number details, build door, window, finish and partition schedules, write keynotes and general notes, update cross-references so that every section mark points to the right sheet, overlay the consultants' latest structural and MEP backgrounds and hunt for conflicts, and then pick up the principal's QA/QC redlines one cloud at a time. Every consultant reissue and every owner change late in the phase restarts part of the cycle.

Much of this is clerical. A door schedule that disagrees with the plan tags, a detail callout pointing to a sheet that was renumbered, a keynote referencing a superseded spec section — these errors are made at 1 a.m. and found by a contractor's RFI months later. Overtime is routine: in the RIBA's 2024 survey of UK practice staff, about 90% regularly worked overtime and two-thirds of them weren't paid for it, and US staff describe 60- to 70-hour weeks in the final stretch (Monograph's 2021 survey found two-thirds of architects felt overworked).

## Why It Matters to the Worker
These are people who spent five or more years in architecture school to design buildings, and in the crunch they are doing data entry under deadline, often for salaries below those of peers in other professions. It shapes whether they stay in the profession at all. The mistakes are attributed to them in the red pen review, while the cause — a renumbered sheet set three days before issue — is structural.

It also costs them their path to licensure. Weeks spent on sheet cleanup count toward the construction documents experience area and nothing else, while the experience they need in design, project management and construction administration goes to someone more senior.

## What a Solution Looks Like
An assistant inside Revit and the markup tool that does the clerical half. It keeps schedules, tags and cross-references consistent as the model changes and flags every mismatch before issue. It reads the principal's redline clouds, classifies them (drafting fix, coordination conflict, design question), proposes the change for the drafting fixes and routes the design questions back to the project architect. It compares each consultant reissue against the last one and lists what moved. And it drafts general notes and keynotes from the project's specification so they cannot point to a section that no longer exists.

## Impact If Solved
Removing the clerical half of CD production could give each junior staff member back ten to twenty hours a week in the final month (estimate), cut the cross-reference and schedule errors that become RFIs, and move that time to the design and coordination work that builds a career.

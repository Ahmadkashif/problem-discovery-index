# Rights Clearance for Fonts, Stock and AI Elements at Handoff

**Industry:** [[graphic-design-studios|Graphic Design Studios]]
**Type:** Low Impact (Customisation Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Every delivered file carries fonts, images and increasingly AI-generated elements under licences that belong to the studio, not the client, and nobody produces the list of what transfers until a foundry's letter arrives.
**Tags:** #cnns #contrastive-learning #k-nearest-neighbors #large-language-models #compliance #data-integration #automation

## The Problem
A studio delivers an identity system: logo files, guidelines, templates, a packaging range. Inside those files are typefaces licensed to the studio's desktop seats, stock photographs licensed under the studio's account with print-run and usage limits, an illustration bought on a marketplace, and perhaps a texture or a hero image generated in Firefly or Midjourney. Desktop font licences generally don't transfer, don't cover web or app embedding, and don't cover the client's in-house team installing the font. Standard stock licences commonly prohibit use in logos or trademarks (estimate). And under the Copyright Office's January 2025 report, prompts alone don't make anyone the author of AI output, so an AI-generated element in a logo may be something the client cannot fully own or enforce.

The bill arrives months later and lands on the client: a font foundry's "continue, but pay" letter offering a retroactive licence, a stock agency's demand, or a trademark attorney asking what the client actually owns. A July 2026 Adobe community post describes exactly this — a packaging agency's client receiving a Monotype licence-violation email over a packshot built with Adobe Fonts. The studio's IP assignment clause promised everything; its files can't show what "everything" contains.

## What Already Exists
Font managers (Extensis Connect Fonts, Monotype Fonts) track which fonts are installed and licensed per seat. DAMs and brand portals (Bynder, Brandfolder, Frontify) have rights-metadata fields. Stock libraries keep licence histories per account. Adobe Content Credentials (C2PA) can attach provenance to Firefly output. Contract templates from Dribbble, Bonsai and the Graphic Artists Guild cover IP assignment in general terms.

## The Customisation Gap
None of these produces a per-deliverable rights bill of materials. The studio-specific version needs to:
- inventory every font (including glyphs converted to outlines or rasterised into images), image, illustration and generated layer inside each delivered file;
- map each to the licence it was used under, whose account holds it, and its scope — desktop, web, app, broadcast, ePub, logo or trademark use;
- generate the client's purchase list ("buy these four font licences for your team and your website") and the transfers the studio must make;
- flag hard conflicts: stock imagery inside a logo, a desktop-only font in a web template, an unprovenanced AI element in a mark the client intends to register;
- write the result into the handoff package and the IP assignment schedule.

## Impact If Solved
The studio closes a liability it currently carries silently on every project, sells licence procurement as a billable handoff service, and gives the client a document their counsel can rely on. One avoided retroactive licence claim, often in the thousands of dollars (estimate), pays for the process many times over.

# Usage Licence Estimating for Commissioned Shoots

**Industry:** [[commercial-photographers|Commercial Photographers]]
**Type:** Low Impact (Customisation Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Every commissioned shoot is priced as a creative fee plus a usage licence defined by media, territory, duration and exclusivity, and photographers still build that estimate in a spreadsheet or a dated desktop tool because generic quoting software has no idea what a licence is.
**Tags:** #gradient-boosting #feature-engineering #confidence-intervals #large-language-models #revenue-impact #automation #workflow-orchestration

## The Problem
An art buyer at an agency sends a brief: a beverage brand needs twelve images for a national campaign covering out-of-home, print, paid social and in-store, for two years in North America, with an option for Europe. The photographer or their producer has a day or two to send back a treatment and an estimate. The estimate has two halves. Production (crew, studio, stylists, props, casting, catering, retouching) is arithmetic. Usage is judgement: what twelve images for two years of national out-of-home and paid social are worth, whether exclusivity in the beverage category doubles it, and how to price the European option so it's accepted later without a fight. Above a threshold, agencies commonly triple-bid the job, and the estimate may be reviewed by a production cost consultant, so every line has to be defensible.

After the award, the terms written into the estimate become the licence. Nobody tracks them against the client's actual use, so renewals are missed and uses beyond scope go unbilled.

## What Already Exists
Generic proposal and invoicing tools (HoneyBook, Dubsado, QuickBooks estimates) handle line items, deposits and e-signature. Film production budgeting tools handle crew and equipment. Photography-specific estimating tools exist (Blinkbid for estimates and invoices, fotoQuote for stock and assignment licence pricing), as do the pricing calculators and consulting services offered by photo production firms and associations.

## The Customisation Gap
What the industry needs on top of a generic quote is a licence-aware estimate. Usage needs to be a structured object, not a text line: media categories, placements and impressions, territory, start and end dates, exclusivity scope, image count and options, priced against the photographer's own won-and-lost history. The output must be written in the standard licence vocabulary an art buyer and cost consultant expect. The estimate's usage assumptions should flow straight into the contract and into an expiry calendar, so renewals and option exercises turn into invoices. And when the client asks for "just add OOH in Canada", the add-on should be re-priced against the original terms rather than improvised. The dated desktop tools do part of this but don't connect estimate, licence and renewal, and the generic tools do none of it.

## Impact If Solved
For an advertising photographer whose usage fees make up half or more of the job, a defensible estimate in hours instead of a day, plus renewals that bill themselves, plausibly recovers ~5–15% of annual revenue (estimate) that is now discounted away under bid pressure or never invoiced after the licence lapses.

# The Proposal That Never Books

**Industry:** [[travel-agencies|Travel Agencies]]
**Type:** Worker Life Changing
**One-liner:** A home-based advisor spends evenings copying rates, photos and amenity terms from a dozen supplier portals into proposals, and many of them earn nothing because the client books elsewhere.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #word-embeddings #worker-facing #automation #workflow-orchestration #quick-win

## The Problem
A hosted advisor, often working part-time from home and paid only when a client travels, gets an inquiry for a ten-day family trip to Italy. Building a proper proposal means checking availability and rates in a hotel booking engine, the consortium's amenity listings, two tour operators' portals, a rail site and a transfer company; copying descriptions and photos into an itinerary tool; working out which rates are commissionable and refundable; and writing it up so it reads like a plan, not a price list. A complex leisure proposal commonly takes ~3-8 hours (estimate). Then the client asks for changes, the advisor rebuilds, and some share of clients — ~30-50% in advisor-forum accounts (estimate) — take the plan and book the hotels directly or on an online travel agency.

The work happens in the gaps of the advisor's other life: evenings, weekends, school pick-up queues. Rates expire while the advisor is still writing. Amenity terms (breakfast for two, a resort credit, an upgrade on arrival) must be copied exactly or the client is promised something the hotel won't give.

## Why It Matters to the Worker
For the advisor this is unpaid labour with a personal cost. The host survey's average full-time income of ~$68,310 hides a long tail of part-time advisors earning far less, and every proposal that doesn't book is a lost evening. Many advisors have started charging planning fees, but asking for one feels risky when the client can see the same hotel online, and new advisors rarely have the confidence to do it.

It also wears down the part of the job they chose it for. Advisors join to design trips and look after people, and the hours go into copying and reformatting rather than the conversation that decides whether the trip is right. The fear of losing the client to a cheaper channel makes them over-deliver early, which is exactly when they are least likely to be paid.

## What a Solution Looks Like
An assistant that drafts the proposal from the advisor's own shortlist: it pulls live availability, rates and amenity terms from connected supplier and consortium sources, marks which rates are commissionable and refundable, writes descriptions in the advisor's voice from the property's content and the advisor's past notes, and assembles a clean itinerary for review. Changes are re-run in minutes, not rebuilt. A simple planning-fee workflow — fee quoted with the first option, credited against the booking — makes charging for research routine rather than awkward.

## Impact If Solved
Cutting a typical proposal from ~5 hours to ~1 (estimate) gives a part-time advisor back several evenings a month and lets them answer inquiries the same day, which is when clients decide; a routine planning fee turns the proposals that never book from lost evenings into paid work.

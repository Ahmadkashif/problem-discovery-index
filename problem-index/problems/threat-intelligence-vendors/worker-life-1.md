# The Analyst Writing Reports Nobody Reads

**Industry:** [[threat-intelligence-vendors|Threat Intelligence Vendors]]
**Type:** Worker Life Changing
**One-liner:** An analyst spends a week producing a careful assessment, it is published to a portal, and nobody can tell them whether a single customer changed anything because of it.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #bert #transformers #gradient-boosting #evaluation-metrics #worker-facing #tacit-knowledge-ml #confidence-intervals

## The Problem
An intelligence analyst researches an adversary, a campaign or a technique, assesses what it means, and writes it up. The report is published to customers and the analyst's involvement ends there.

There is no feedback. Portal view counts, at best. Whether a customer read it, whether it changed a decision, whether anyone hunted for the activity described, whether they found anything — none of it comes back. An analyst can produce a year of work without knowing whether any of it was used.

The production cadence is relentless and partly disconnected from value. Publication volume is a commercial signal, so there is pressure to produce regularly regardless of whether something meaningful happened, which dilutes the significant work and consumes time that could go into deeper research.

Much of the writing is repetitive. Reports follow structures, a large share of the content is contextual background that exists in previous reports, and the analyst rewrites it because there is no mechanism to assemble it.

And the assessment language is a trap. Intelligence analysis uses calibrated expressions of confidence, readers interpret them inconsistently, and an analyst who is appropriately uncertain is frequently read as unhelpful — so the pressure runs toward more definite language than the evidence supports.

## Why It Matters to the Worker
This is skilled analytical work with no observable effect, which is demoralising in exactly the way it is for the user researcher and the penetration tester elsewhere in this vault. Craft improves with feedback and there is none.

The volume pressure is the daily grind. Producing to a publication schedule rather than to significance is the opposite of how analysis should work and everyone involved knows it.

The confidence-language problem is a genuine professional frustration. The discipline has developed careful conventions for expressing uncertainty and the commercial context rewards conviction, which puts the analyst's professional standards in conflict with what gets read.

And the expertise does not compound institutionally. An analyst's accumulated knowledge of an adversary lives in their head and in a series of published documents, and when they leave, the successor rebuilds it from the reports.

## What a Solution Looks Like
Close the loop with the customer. Whether a report was read, whether it triggered a hunt, and whether the hunt found anything is a chain customers can record and vendors can receive — and it converts finished intelligence from a publication into a measurable input, which is as good for the analyst as for the product.

Assemble the repeatable content. Background sections, adversary context, historical campaign summaries and technical appendices exist in the corpus and can be drafted from it, leaving the analyst the assessment and the judgement, which is the part worth their week.

Publish on significance, not on schedule. Measuring which reports produced action would let a vendor defend a lower volume of more significant output, which is the commercial argument the analyst cannot currently make.

Track how confidence language is interpreted. Testing how customers actually read the standard expressions, and reporting it, is a small study that would tell the field whether its conventions work — and would give an analyst grounds for keeping the honest formulation.

And build the institutional memory. A structured adversary knowledge base, maintained rather than re-narrated in each report, preserves what the analyst knows in a form the next one inherits.

## Impact If Solved
Intelligence analysis is careful work performed without any signal about its effect, on a publication cadence set commercially rather than by significance. Closing the read-hunt-find loop gives the analyst feedback and the vendor the outcome measure the whole category lacks; assembling the repeatable content returns the week to assessment; and a maintained knowledge base keeps the expertise when the analyst leaves.

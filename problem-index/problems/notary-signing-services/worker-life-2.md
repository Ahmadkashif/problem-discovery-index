# Questions the Agent Is Not Allowed to Answer

**Industry:** [[notary-signing-services|Notary & Signing Services]]
**Type:** Worker Life Changing
**One-liner:** At every signing a borrower asks why the rate, the cash to close or a fee is different from what they expected, and the signing agent — barred from giving legal or loan advice and often unable to reach the loan officer — has to hold the table together alone.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #bert #worker-facing #compliance #workflow-orchestration #quick-win

## The Problem
A signing agent's job is to identify the signer, present each document, collect signatures and notarise. They are not the lender, the escrow officer or a lawyer, and explaining what a document means legally, or advising whether to sign, can be unauthorised practice of law. But borrowers do not separate those roles. Halfway through the package someone notices the closing disclosure shows a higher cash-to-close than the estimate, or a prepayment rider they were not told about, or a name misspelled on the deed. The agent calls the title company and the loan officer from the table; at 7 p.m. nobody answers. Forum threads on unanswered calls from the signing table are long and angry. The borrower is anxious, sometimes hostile, sometimes in tears, and the agent can only say "that is a question for your lender" while trying to keep the signing alive and the courier deadline intact.

## Why It Matters to the Worker
The agent absorbs the borrower's frustration with a process they did not design, and they are blamed either way: by the borrower for not explaining, by the title company if the signing does not complete, and by the lender if they said too much. The line between describing a document ("this is the deed of trust, which secures the loan") and advising on it is not obvious to new agents, and stepping over it risks their commission. Long stalled signings also wreck the evening schedule and the next appointment.

## What a Solution Looks Like
A table-side assistant on the agent's phone that knows the package. It gives a neutral, pre-approved description of each document and section that agents may read out, keyed to the lender's own forms; drafts a short, factual escalation message to the loan officer and escrow officer with the page and the borrower's exact question; tracks whether anyone has replied; and records what was asked and what the agent said, so the agent can show later that they stayed within their role. For common disputes — cash-to-close changes, rate lock dates, rescission dates on a refinance — it explains where in the package the borrower can find the answer without the agent interpreting it.

## Impact If Solved
Fewer stalled or abandoned signings, shorter appointments, and an agent who can be helpful without fear of practising law. The intangible gain is dignity: the agent is no longer the only person in the room who cannot answer, with no one picking up the phone.

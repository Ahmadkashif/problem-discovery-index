# The $75 Signing That Takes Four Hours

**Industry:** [[notary-signing-services|Notary & Signing Services]]
**Type:** Worker Life Changing
**One-liner:** A loan signing agent is paid a flat fee for an order that, counting the calls, printing, driving, waiting, scanning and courier run, often takes half a working day — and the agent only learns the true cost after accepting it.
**Tags:** #gradient-boosting #linear-regression #time-series-forecasting #feature-engineering #worker-facing #revenue-impact #automation

## The Problem
An offer arrives on Snapdocs or by text from a signing service: a refinance tomorrow at 6 p.m., thirty miles away, $75-100 (forum reports). The agent has minutes to accept before another agent does. Then the work begins. They call the borrower to confirm the time and the ID, wait for documents that arrive late in the afternoon, print ~120 pages (estimate) on legal and letter trays, drive in rush hour, wait for a spouse, spend an hour at the table, scan back the required pages in a parking lot, and drive to the courier drop before the cutoff. If the documents are redrawn, they print again. If the borrower reschedules, the agent may get a cancellation fee or nothing. Payment from some signing services takes 30-90 days (estimate) and occasionally never comes, which is why forums keep lists of companies to avoid.

## Why It Matters to the Worker
Signing agents are independent contractors with no salary, no mileage reimbursement and no control over the fee, and many do this as their main income. A new agent on a forum asked whether $75-80 signings clear $20 an hour once travel and uploads are counted; experienced agents answer that it depends on things the offer does not show. The cost of a bad acceptance falls entirely on them: an evening lost, a tank of fuel, toner and paper, and the risk of a late-night drive to a stranger's house. Turning down too many offers lowers their ranking on the platform, so they feel pressure to take orders they suspect will lose money.

## What a Solution Looks Like
An offer evaluator on the agent's phone that, before acceptance, estimates the real time and cost of the order: drive time at the appointment hour from the agent's actual location, page count and print cost from the loan type and lender, likely wait from the signing service's history of late documents, redraw and cancellation probability, and the payer's actual days-to-pay from the agent's own records and pooled reports. It shows an effective hourly rate and suggests a counter-fee. After acceptance it handles confirmations, document-arrival alerts, print-queue setup, mileage logging and invoice chasing.

## Impact If Solved
Agents could stop accepting the orders that lose money and price the rest honestly, recovering several hours a week (estimate) and a meaningful share of their margin. Over time, pooled data on late documents and slow payers would reward the signing services that treat agents well.

# The Convention Services Manager Serving Forty Groups at Once

**Industry:** [[visitors-bureaus|Convention & Visitors Bureaus]]
**Type:** Worker Life Changing
**One-liner:** After sales hands over a booked meeting, one convention services manager supports dozens of groups at different stages at once, re-sending the same materials and chasing the same local vendors by email, then works the long days on site when each group arrives.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #word-embeddings #worker-facing #automation #workflow-orchestration #quick-win

## The Problem
Once a group signs, the sales manager moves on to the next lead and the meeting passes to convention services. At a mid-sized bureau one or two services managers carry the groups booked for the next 12-18 months, often 30-50 at a time (estimate), each at a different stage. A group meeting next spring needs attendance-building material now: destination photos, video, a promotional web page and a "why attend" email for the association to send its members. A group meeting next month needs a restaurant list, a vendor referral for a photographer or a tour, welcome signage at the airport, and a city official to speak at the opening session. A citywide meeting this week needs the manager on site at the convention centre, from early registration to the last evening event.

Most of this work is the same request answered again. Planners ask for the same photo library, the same dining guide and the same transport options. Vendor referrals go out by email to partner businesses, and the manager chases replies. Requests arrive by email, phone and the planner's own portal, and they live in the manager's inbox and a spreadsheet. On site the manager is the planner's first call for anything local: a shuttle that has not arrived, a restaurant that cannot seat 200, a lost attendee.

## Why It Matters to the Worker
The job is measured by planners' satisfaction and repeat bookings, but it is staffed as an afterthought, because services produces no room nights for the sales report. Services managers are often the most junior people in the bureau's group team, and they absorb every deadline the sales team promised. Their peak weeks are the ones when citywides are in town: twelve-hour days on their feet, evening events and weekend arrivals, while the inbox for every other group keeps filling.

The repetitive half of the work crowds out the part that keeps groups coming back: knowing a planner's attendees and fixing problems before they happen. When a services manager leaves, which in a junior role happens often (estimate), the next one inherits a spreadsheet and no memory of what each planner was promised.

## What a Solution Looks Like
A services workspace per booked group. It holds the group's profile and timeline from the moment sales hands over, assembles standard packages (attendance-building kit, dining and transport guides, welcome materials) from the bureau's own content library with the group's dates and venues filled in, and drafts replies to routine planner requests for the manager to approve. Vendor referrals go to matching partner businesses through one request, and their replies are collected in one place. On site, a shared issue log on the phone records each problem and who is handling it, and becomes the record of what was promised and delivered for the next time the group returns.

## Impact If Solved
Taking the repeated requests and referral chasing off the inbox returns ~5-8 hours a week to each services manager (estimate). That time goes to the on-site weeks and to the planners who decide whether the group comes back. The handover file means a new manager starts with the group's history instead of a blank spreadsheet.

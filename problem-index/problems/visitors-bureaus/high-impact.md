# The Contracted Block Nobody Checks

**Industry:** [[visitors-bureaus|Convention & Visitors Bureaus]]
**Type:** High Impact
**One-liner:** A bureau's convention sales director can tell which groups will choose the city and which will fill their hotel blocks, but the bureau reports the contracted block as its result and never records the director's read, or the pickup that followed, so neither the judgement nor the claim is ever graded.
**Tags:** #tacit-knowledge-ml #gradient-boosting #survival-analysis #logistic-regression #evaluation-metrics #feature-engineering #revenue-impact

## The Problem
A convention sales director at a mid-sized city bureau carries a book of a few hundred association and corporate accounts. For each, they hold a view: where the meeting rotates, who really decides, whether this year's RFP is a genuine search or a price check to pressure the incumbent city, how big the block should be, and whether the attendees will actually stay in it. On that view they decide which bids get a site visit and a familiarisation trip, which hotels to pull into a citywide package, and how much of the bureau's incentive fund to put on the table: rebates per room night, free convention-centre rent, shuttle subsidies.

The experienced director's read is the bureau's real asset, and very little of it is written down. They know that this medical society always contracts 2,200 peak rooms and has never picked up more than 1,700. They know that a planner who asks about the convention centre's loading docks in the first call is serious. They know that a regional association whose members can drive in will book around the block at cheaper hotels, and that a group meeting the week after a big national show in the next state will lose attendees to it. This is tacit knowledge in the plain sense: patterns built over hundreds of bids, which the director acts on and cannot fully specify.

The bureau's reporting throws this away twice. First, the CRM records the lead, its stage, the estimated room nights and the outcome (booked or lost, with a reason picked from a list), but not the director's confidence, the incentive logic or the expected pickup. Second, when a group books, the bureau reports the contracted room block as "definite room nights", the KPI in its contract with the city and its report to the hotel board. The industry's own performance-reporting handbook notes that post-event pickup "may not be obtainable", because attendees book outside the block. So the number on which the bureau is funded is the forecast, not the result. When the director retires or moves to a bigger city, which in convention sales happens every few years (estimate), the bureau keeps the lead history and loses the read.

## Why It's Unsolved
The data collection problem is that the director's read has to be captured at the moment of decision, by a busy person who gains nothing from writing it down. A confidence rating entered after a group books is hindsight. One entered at the tentative stage is a small tax that only pays off years later, when the meeting happens. Destination CRMs such as Simpleview CRM and Tempest's iDSS record each lead's requested rooms and stage. Any forecast entered beside them is filled in for the pipeline report rather than as an honest prediction, and it defaults to the planner's requested block.

The outcome is just as hard to get. True pickup is split across the housing company's registrations, the contracted hotels' in-block reservations and attendees who booked outside the block at the same hotels, at non-contracted hotels or on vacation rentals. The housing report covers the first. Hotels' guest folios cover the second and third only after a match against the registration list, which requires the group's cooperation. Many bureaus never ask, because an honest pickup figure lowers the room nights they reported.

The labelling problem is that experts disagree with themselves. The same director rates the same association differently after a recent loss. A booking won with a large incentive looks like a sales win, and is not one if the group would have come anyway. Groups that were turned away or not pursued produce no outcome at all, so any model trained on bookings alone learns from a selected sample. Meetings booked five years out also mean the label arrives slowly: a model trained on 2020-2025 outcomes learns from pandemic-distorted meetings.

The deployment problem is speed and politics. A pickup forecast is useful only before the incentive offer goes out and before the hotel blocks are committed. That is often within a week of the RFP deadline, so the forecast has to be faster than the director's own judgement. And it has to survive a sales culture whose bonuses and reputations rest on booked room nights: a tool that marks down the block will be resisted unless it is framed as protecting the group and the city from attrition penalties.

## What a Solution Looks Like
A bid ledger first, then models. Every citywide and large single-hotel bid gets a short structured record at the tentative stage: requested block, the director's expected pickup and confidence, the competing cities, the incentive offered and why. The record is drafted from the RFP, the CRM history and the director's notes, and confirmed in seconds. After the meeting, the record is closed with the housing report, hotel actuals matched to registrations where the group agrees, and attendance.

On the ledger, train two models. The first predicts whether a lead or tentative books, and when, using account history, rotation patterns, the competing destinations and the bureau's offer. The second predicts realised pickup as a share of the contracted block, from the group's history across cities (via meeting-history databases such as Knowland and MINT+), drive-in share, rate gap to nearby non-contracted hotels and the event calendar. Report both beside the director's own forecast, and over time show where the director beats the model and where the model beats the director.

## Impact If Solved
A bureau that knows its real pickup can price incentives against stays that will actually happen, size citywide blocks so groups avoid attrition penalties and come back, and report room nights its funders can verify. If a mid-sized bureau's contracted blocks overstate pickup by ~15-25% (estimate), correcting the bid mix and incentive spend is worth more than any marketing campaign it runs. It also turns the most expensive recurring loss in convention sales, the director's judgement leaving with the director, into something the bureau keeps.

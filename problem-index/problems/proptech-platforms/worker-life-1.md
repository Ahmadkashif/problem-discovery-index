# Site Manager Turnover Coordination

**Industry:** [[proptech-platforms|Proptech Platforms]]
**Type:** Worker Life Changing
**One-liner:** Site managers stop personally chaperoning every unit turn through a chain of vendors by phone, because the make-ready sequence schedules itself and tells them only what has actually gone wrong.
**Tags:** #time-series-forecasting #gradient-boosting #optimization-fundamentals #evaluation-metrics #workflow-orchestration #automation #worker-facing

## The Problem
When a resident moves out, the unit must be returned to leasable condition: inspection, cleaning, painting, flooring where needed, appliance and fixture repairs, a final walk, photographs, and the listing going live. Vacancy is pure loss, so every day matters, and in a portfolio with normal turnover a site manager is running several of these at once continuously.

Coordinating it is phone work. Call the cleaner, who cannot come until Thursday. Call the painter, who needs the cleaner done first. Discover on Thursday that the flooring is worse than the inspection noted, which means a different vendor and a four-day delay, which means the painter must be rescheduled, which means the cleaner comes back. Chase the invoice. Update the listing date, which was promised to three prospects.

Nothing in the software does any of this. The platform records the work orders and the invoices after the fact. The sequencing, the dependencies, the vendor availability and the re-planning live in the site manager's head and their call log.

## Why It Matters to the Worker
Site managers are the operational centre of a property and their day is already fully consumed by residents, leasing, deliveries, complaints and inspections. Turnover coordination is layered on top, it is deadline-driven, and it is the thing they get measured on — days vacant is a headline metric in every portfolio review.

The frustration is that almost none of it requires their judgement. It is sequencing and follow-up. When a turn goes badly it is usually because a vendor did not show or a scope surprise appeared, and neither is something the manager could have prevented by working harder — but they absorb the accountability for it.

Turnover in the site manager role itself is high, and portfolios run measurably worse during the gap, because the coordination knowledge is personal and undocumented.

## What a Solution Looks Like
A make-ready plan generated at notice-to-vacate rather than assembled by phone. The expected scope is predictable from the unit's age, its last renovation, the length of tenancy, the move-in and move-out inspection comparison, and the platform's own history of what units like this have needed. From that, a sequenced schedule with vendor bookings, dependencies respected and a realistic ready date.

When a scope surprise appears, the plan re-sequences and re-books rather than waiting for the manager to work the phone. Vendors get automated confirmations and reminders. The listing date updates from the plan, so leasing is not promising a date that maintenance cannot meet.

The manager's attention should be reserved for exceptions: this vendor did not show twice, this scope is materially larger than expected, this turn will miss its date and here is the cost.

## Impact If Solved
Days vacant is the metric every operator manages and it is currently produced by one person making phone calls between other duties. Automating the coordination shortens turns, makes ready dates truthful for leasing, and removes the most deadline-pressured part of a role with high and expensive turnover.

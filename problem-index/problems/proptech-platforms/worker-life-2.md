# Leasing Agent Lead Chase

**Industry:** [[proptech-platforms|Proptech Platforms]]
**Type:** Worker Life Changing
**One-liner:** Leasing agents stop spending their day returning calls to people who leased somewhere else last Tuesday, because the system responds instantly, qualifies, and books the tour before the prospect has moved on.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #gradient-boosting #logistic-regression #evaluation-metrics #automation #workflow-orchestration #worker-facing

## The Problem
Rental leads arrive constantly and decay fast. A prospect enquires on a listing site at nine at night, and if nobody responds until the following afternoon, they have already toured two other properties. Response speed is the single strongest predictor of whether a lead converts, and it is a function of whether a human happened to be free.

The leasing agent's day is structurally incompatible with fast response. They are showing units, processing applications, handling walk-ins, dealing with residents, and covering the office alone for stretches. Leads accumulate in a queue and get worked in batches, which is exactly the wrong shape.

The volume is also mostly noise. Many enquiries are unqualified — the income does not support the rent, the move date is four months out, the pet policy is a blocker, they are looking in the wrong price band entirely. The agent finds this out on the phone, one call at a time, and the qualification questions are always the same.

## Why It Matters to the Worker
Leasing compensation is frequently tied to occupancy or to leases signed, so a lost lead is a personal financial loss. Agents know that their conversion depends on speed and know they cannot be fast while also doing the rest of the job, which is a demoralising position to be in permanently.

The follow-up is the worst part of it. Agents are expected to make repeated contact attempts across a sequence — call, text, email, call again — for every lead, and most of those attempts reach nobody. It is repetitive, low-yield, and it fills the gaps between the parts of the job that people actually enjoy, which is showing homes and helping someone find one.

There is also a fairness issue that agents feel keenly: the leads they lose to slow response are counted against their performance identically to the ones they handled badly.

## What a Solution Looks Like
Instant response, at any hour, in the prospect's own channel. Answering the actual question — is this available, what is the rent, do you allow dogs, when could I see it — from the live listing data rather than a generic reply.

Qualification through conversation rather than interrogation: establishing move date, budget, occupancy and pets naturally, and routing accordingly. Prospects who are ready get a tour booked into the agent's real availability, or into self-guided access where the property supports it. Prospects who are early get a scheduled follow-up that fires at the right time rather than being chased weekly.

Follow-up sequences should run themselves, with the agent brought in when a prospect responds substantively or asks something the system should not answer.

The agent's day then contains tours and applications, which is the work, and not a call list.

## Impact If Solved
Speed to lead is the largest controllable factor in leasing conversion, and it is currently determined by whether someone was standing at the desk. Automating response and qualification raises occupancy, which is the number the whole business runs on, and gives the agent back the part of the job they were hired for.

# The Court Runner Paid by the Name

**Industry:** [[investigation-and-screening-firms|Investigation & Background Screening Firms]]
**Type:** Worker Life Changing
**One-liner:** The court runner drives between courthouses that allow searches only at a public terminal, types results by hand for a few dollars a name, and absorbs every re-pull, closed terminal and clerk limit as unpaid time.
**Tags:** #cnns #transformers #large-language-models #optimization-fundamentals #worker-facing #automation #workflow-orchestration

## The Problem
Many county courts still let the public search criminal records only at a terminal in the clerk's office, or only by handing a list of names to a clerk. Screening firms and retrievers cover those counties with court runners: usually independent contractors, often retirees, former clerks or part-timers, each covering a few courthouses. Every morning a list of names arrives by email or a portal. The runner drives to the courthouse, waits for a terminal, searches each name against the index, opens any possible matches, reads the docket for charges, dispositions and identifiers, and types the result into a portal or spreadsheet before the cut-off. Pay is per name searched, an estimated ~$3–10 depending on the county and the retriever.

The work goes wrong in ways the runner does not control. The terminal is down or reserved for attorneys. The clerk caps the number of names accepted per day. The index hides dates of birth, as California's courts have done since the *Hamrick* ruling in 2021, so a common name produces a page of possible matches that cannot be resolved. A result typed with a transposed case number comes back as a re-pull the next day, unpaid. Court holidays and lunch closures push names past the screener's deadline, and the runner hears about it from a coordinator.

## Why It Matters to the Worker
Per-name pay turns every delay into lost income: an hour waiting for a terminal or a morning of re-pulls is unpaid, and mileage between courthouses is often the runner's own cost. Runners are contractors without benefits, and their reputation with the retriever rests on error rates measured on typing, not on the judgement they exercise reading dockets.

That judgement is real and unrecorded. A good runner knows which clerk will pull a file, which docket codes mean a dismissal in this county, and when a "no record" really means "not yet indexed". None of it is asked for, and none of it raises their pay.

## What a Solution Looks Like
A mobile capture app built for the courthouse. The runner photographs the terminal screen or docket, and the app reads the charge, disposition, dates and identifiers into structured fields for confirmation instead of retyping. The day's names arrive batched by courthouse with the deadline for each, and known closures and clerk limits are already on the schedule. Every search carries a short "search conditions" note (index coverage, identifiers visible, backlog), and re-pulls caused by the court rather than the runner are flagged and paid.

## Impact If Solved
Cutting typing and re-pulls could add an estimated ~20–30% more names to a runner's day at the same hours (estimate), lift income for people paid by the name, and give screeners structured results with the conditions that limit them.

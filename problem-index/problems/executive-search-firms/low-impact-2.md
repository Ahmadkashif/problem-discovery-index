# Make-Whole and Offer Modelling for Executive Candidates

**Industry:** [[executive-search-firms|Executive Search Firms]]
**Type:** Low Impact (Customisation Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Proxy pay databases and equity-plan software are mature, but the question that decides whether a sitting executive moves — what the client must pay to replace the unvested equity, bonus and pension the candidate forfeits — is still worked out late, in a spreadsheet, by an associate.
**Tags:** #monte-carlo-methods #probability-distributions #large-language-models #feature-engineering #revenue-impact #data-integration #automation

## The Problem
The strongest candidates for senior roles are employed, successful and holding several years of unvested restricted stock, performance shares and options, plus a bonus year in progress and sometimes a deferred-compensation or pension balance that vests on tenure. Moving means forfeiting most of it. The hiring company therefore pays a "make-whole" — a sign-on equity grant and cash buyout calibrated to the forfeited value — and the size of that number decides more late-stage searches than any interview. Search firms bill on first-year compensation, and Heidrick's filings note that if actual pay exceeds the estimate, the firm may bill one-third of the excess.

The calculation is harder than it looks. Performance shares pay out between zero and a multiple of target depending on metrics measured years ahead; options are underwater or not; the forfeited bonus depends on the current year's results; and the candidate's own account of what they hold is optimistic. Too often the numbers arrive only at the offer stage, the client balks, and a search that took months collapses in a week — or the candidate accepts and then takes the counteroffer their employer constructs from the same arithmetic. Hunt Scanlon cites a CEB figure that half of people who accept a counteroffer leave within a year anyway.

## What Already Exists
Proxy and executive pay databases (Equilar, ISS-Corporate's compensation analytics, S&P's ExecuComp) hold named-executive grants, vesting and realised pay; the SEC's 2022 pay-versus-performance rule added five years of "compensation actually paid" to every proxy. Compensation consultants such as those in [[hr-consultants|HR Consultants]] model pay for boards, and equity-management platforms model grants for issuers.

## The Customisation Gap
Proxy data covers only named executive officers and arrives annually, while most searches target the level below. The search-specific layer is: extraction of a candidate's holdings from grant statements they share under confidentiality; valuation of each tranche with performance-share payout probabilities and option values; the forfeited-bonus estimate; tax and clawback considerations; the client's own equity-plan limits; and a comparison of make-whole structures (cash versus equity, vesting schedules) the client can approve. It also needs to happen at the shortlist stage, not the offer stage, so that a candidate who is unaffordable is known before the client falls for them. None of the pay databases or equity tools are built around a candidate's personal forfeiture.

## Impact If Solved
Moving the make-whole estimate from the offer stage to the shortlist removes the most common late collapse in senior searches. If ~1 in 10 searches that reach an offer stalls or collapses on compensation (estimate) and earlier modelling halves that, a firm completing ~1,000 searches a year saves ~50 searches from the brink — at Heidrick's 2024 average of $146,000 per search, roughly ~$7M of fees protected or not reworked (estimate). It also cuts the ~4–6 associate hours spent rebuilding each finalist's vesting table by hand (estimate), and gives the client a defensible number to take to its compensation committee rather than a candidate's assertion.

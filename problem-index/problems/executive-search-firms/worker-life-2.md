# The Engagement Manager Holding Every Search Together

**Industry:** [[executive-search-firms|Executive Search Firms]]
**Type:** Worker Life Changing
**One-liner:** The engagement manager who runs the day-to-day of several searches assembles every client update by hand, schedules confidential interviews across time zones, and chases partners for the debriefs that keep the record current.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #transformers #seq2seq #evaluation-metrics #worker-facing #workflow-orchestration #automation

## The Problem
Behind each search consultant sits an execution layer. Heidrick & Struggles reported 565 associates and engagement managers against 418 search consultants in its 2024 10-K, and that layer runs the mandate between the partner's client conversations. An engagement manager or search coordinator typically carries several live searches at once (estimate), and each brings the same recurring obligations: a weekly or fortnightly progress update to the client — how many approached, who is interested, who has interviewed, where each stands — and the logistics of every candidate in play.

The update is assembled by hand. The engagement manager pulls statuses from the CRM, which is never quite current because partners log their calls late or not at all, then chases the partner by message for what the candidate said last night, rewrites notes into client-safe prose, and builds the document or slides before the call. Scheduling is its own grind: a sitting executive can only talk from a personal phone, outside office hours, and cannot receive a calendar invitation at work, while the client's interview panel spans several time zones and moves twice. Psychometric assessments, reference calls, travel and confidentiality agreements all run through the same person.

## Why It Matters to the Worker
The job is coordination under permanent interruption, and almost none of it is visible. The client remembers the partner; the candidate remembers the partner; the engagement manager is the person who absorbs every rescheduled panel and every late debrief, often in the evening when candidates can speak freely. A wrong status in a client update is their mistake, even when the partner never logged the call.

It also limits their development. Engagement managers move up by learning to assess candidates and manage clients, and a week spent rebuilding status tables and rearranging calendars leaves little time to sit in on interviews or run first conversations.

## What a Solution Looks Like
Progress reports drafted automatically from CRM activity, email and call notes in each client's preferred format, with candidate statuses phrased safely and every line linked to its source, so the engagement manager edits rather than writes. A one-minute voice debrief the partner records after each candidate call, transcribed and structured into the CRM, so nobody has to chase. And scheduling that works through candidates' personal channels, proposes slots across the panel's time zones, and keeps candidate names out of client calendars until the candidate agrees.

## Impact If Solved
If report assembly and debrief chasing fall from ~2–3 hours to under an hour per mandate per week (estimate), an engagement manager carrying several searches gets back most of a working day each week, and the client receives updates that are accurate on the day they are sent.

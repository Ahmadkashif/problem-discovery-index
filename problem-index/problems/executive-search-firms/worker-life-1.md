# The Research Associate Rebuilding Every Map From Scratch

**Industry:** [[executive-search-firms|Executive Search Firms]]
**Type:** Worker Life Changing
**One-liner:** The researcher who builds every longlist starts each search with a blank spreadsheet, reconstructs org charts the firm already built last quarter, and gets credit for none of it when the placement closes.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #word-embeddings #k-nearest-neighbors #feature-engineering #worker-facing #automation #workflow-orchestration

## The Problem
A research associate at a search firm typically supports several consultants and a handful of live searches at once. For each, the routine is the same. Turn the partner's brief into a target company list. Work out who holds the role at each target, and the one or two people below them, from company websites, filings, press releases, conference speaker lists and professional networks. Decide whether a "Vice President, Finance" at one company is the same job as a "Head of FP&A" at another. Check each name against off-limits restrictions. Load the result into the CRM, write a one-line rationale for each, and start the first approaches — the cold calls and messages that most candidates never return.

Much of this was done before. The firm placed a CFO in the same sector eighteen months ago, and the researcher on that search built nearly the same map. But that map lives in a spreadsheet attached to a closed mandate, many of the names have moved, and nobody recorded who said what on the first call. So the new search starts again. Clockwork's 2023 survey found 68.9% of firms "very interested" in AI suggesting a target company list, which says how much of this work is still manual.

## Why It Matters to the Worker
The job is the training ground for the profession and the place where most of its drudgery sits. The researcher knows the market better than anyone on the team by the end of a search and is the least visible person on it: the client meets the partner, the candidate remembers the partner, and the placement is booked to the partner. When the map is wrong, the researcher hears about it; when it is right, the partner gets the credit. The repetitive reconstruction leaves little time for the work that would build their own judgement — calling candidates, sitting in on assessments, learning why one profile was preferred over another.

It also caps their career. Promotion from research to consultant depends on showing judgement, and a week spent levelling job titles in a spreadsheet does not show it.

## What a Solution Looks Like
A living market map the firm keeps between searches: target companies and their leadership structures, refreshed as people move, with every past approach and its response attached to the person. When a new search starts, the researcher begins from last quarter's map with the changes flagged, a proposed target list drawn from the firm's past searches in the sector, titles already levelled with the evidence shown, and off-limits conflicts cleared automatically. The researcher reviews, corrects and extends, and spends the saved days on first conversations.

## Impact If Solved
If the identification and levelling work falls from about a week to a day or two per search (estimate), a researcher supporting several searches gets back days a month to spend on candidate conversations and assessment — the work that builds a consultant — and the firm stops paying to rebuild the same map every quarter.

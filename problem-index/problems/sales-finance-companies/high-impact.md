# The Credit Buyer's Read of a Deal

**Industry:** [[sales-finance-companies|Sales Finance Companies]]
**Type:** High Impact
**One-liner:** The analyst who decides which dealer deals a finance company buys carries a pattern library of packed deals, drifting dealers and borrowers who will pay anyway, and the lender records the decision but never the read, so it cannot grade it, teach it or keep it.
**Tags:** #tacit-knowledge-ml #gradient-boosting #large-language-models #survival-analysis #causal-inference #evaluation-metrics #feature-engineering #revenue-impact

## The Problem
In indirect lending the credit analyst — called a buyer in most shops — sees a deal as a screen. The dealer's application arrives through Dealertrack or RouteOne with the bureau pull, the stated income and employer, the vehicle's VIN and options, the cash price, down payment, trade-in and payoff, the requested term and the add-ons. The decision engine has already scored it. For prime paper the engine decides alone. For everything near the edges — thin files, recent bankruptcies, high loan-to-value, self-employed income, a dealer with a mixed record — the file drops to a human queue, and the buyer has minutes to approve, decline or send back a counteroffer: less advance, a shorter term, more down, proof of income and residence as stipulations. The dealer has sent the same application to four other lenders, and the first workable answer usually wins.

What experienced buyers do in those minutes is the most valuable judgement in the business and the least recorded. They recognise that a stated income of $5,400 a month at a job title that pays half that is inflated, and that this dealer's incomes have crept up for six weeks. They see that the book value on the screen implies a trim level the VIN does not support. They notice that a co-borrower lives at a different address in a different state, the shape of a straw purchase. They also know the reverse: that a borrower with a 540 score, nine years at the same employer and a payment under 12% of income will pay, and that declining her loses the dealer. Job ads ask for exactly this — "a sound understanding of auto loans and the structure of deals in order to rapidly suggest alternatives" — and none of it is written in the credit policy.

The lender keeps the decision: approved, countered with these terms, declined with this reason code. It does not keep why. When the loan defaults in month four, the vintage report shows it as a scorecard miss; when the override performs, nobody learns that the buyer saw something the model did not. When a senior buyer retires or moves to a competitor, which in a market with a few dozen large non-prime lenders happens constantly, the read leaves with them and the new analyst learns it again from losses.

## Why It's Unsolved
The data collection problem is that the read happens in seconds, under a dealer's clock, and is expressed in an action rather than a sentence. A buyer who counters at 72 months instead of 84 has encoded a judgement about the borrower's stability and the vehicle's depreciation, but the system stores only the new term. Asking buyers to type a rationale for every decision slows the queue in exactly the market where speed wins the deal, so reason fields are filled with defaults. The cues they use — the dealer's recent behaviour, the plausibility of income for an occupation, the way a deal was restructured between submissions — are scattered across the origination network's deal history, the LOS, the bureau file and the buyer's memory of the dealer.

The labelling problem is that the outcome is slow, noisy and selected. Early payment default arrives within three to six months; charge-off and recovery take years. Declined deals have no outcome at all, because they were funded elsewhere or not at all, so the lender only ever observes the performance of deals it chose. Buyers also disagree with themselves: the same analyst is tighter after a bad month's early defaults and looser when volume targets slip, and two buyers reading the same file diverge most on exactly the marginal deals that matter. Some of what looks like judgement is also exposure — a buyer's read of "this kind of borrower" can be a proxy for a protected characteristic, which fair lending review must catch before any model learns it.

The deployment problem is speed and accountability. Any assistance must appear inside the queue, before the dealer's patience runs out, and must be faster than the buyer's own glance. Under ECOA and Regulation B, every decline or counteroffer needs a specific, accurate adverse-action reason, so a model that learns from buyers must explain itself in reasons a borrower can be told. And the dealer relationship punishes caution: a tool that slows or tightens decisions without demonstrably cutting losses will be switched off by the sales side within a quarter.

## What a Solution Looks Like
A decision record first and a model second. Every manual decision — approval, counter, decline, stipulation — is captured with the deal as it stood, the deal as it was resubmitted, the buyer's action and a one-click cue taxonomy drafted from the file ("income implausible for occupation", "book value unsupported by VIN", "dealer income drift", "address mismatch"), confirmed rather than typed. Each record is joined later to early payment default, first-year performance, fraud confirmations and dealer buybacks, with declined deals tracked through bureau data where a permissible purpose allows.

On top of that ledger, learn the buyers' reads. Models trained on the senior buyers' cue labels and the outcomes surface the cues for a junior analyst at decision time, flag deals where the read and the scorecard disagree, and score each dealer's paper for drift against its own history. Counteroffer suggestions are offered as structures a senior buyer would have proposed, with expected performance attached. Fair lending testing runs on every model version before release, and every suggestion carries a reason code that can go on an adverse-action notice.

## Impact If Solved
In a non-prime book losing 8–9% a year (S&P's 2025 subprime ABS average), a few tenths of a point of avoided early defaults and fraud on the manually decided slice pays for the programme many times over, and keeping a senior buyer's read through a departure removes one of the industry's most expensive recurring losses. The same record makes the lender's judgement auditable to its warehouse banks and examiners, which after Tricolor is becoming a condition of funding.

# The Stewardship Analyst in Proxy Season

**Industry:** [[asset-managers|Asset Managers]]
**Type:** Worker Life Changing
**One-liner:** The stewardship analyst gets back judgment time — currently consumed by thousands of routine ballot items between April and June — for the few dozen votes and engagements that actually matter.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #bert #k-nearest-neighbors #evaluation-metrics #worker-facing #compliance #workflow-orchestration

## The Problem
A large manager votes at thousands of company meetings a year, and most of them fall between April and June. Each ballot carries director elections, say-on-pay, auditor ratification, shareholder proposals on climate, governance and social topics, and the occasional contested meeting or merger. A stewardship team of a handful to a few dozen people receives proxy adviser research from ISS or Glass Lewis, applies the firm's own voting policy, consults portfolio managers on holdings that matter to them, records a rationale where the policy diverges, and casts the vote before the cut-off.

Then, after the season, the team writes the stewardship report, files Form N-PX — which since 2024 requires say-on-pay votes to be reported by managers and votes to be categorised in standard ways — and answers client and consultant questionnaires about how it voted and why.

## Why It Matters to the Worker
The analyst's expertise is in the hard cases: a contested board, a pay plan that is egregious relative to performance, a shareholder proposal that is material for this company and not for its peers. In season those cases compete for attention with thousands of routine items that still need a check and a record. Analysts work long hours for ten weeks, under public scrutiny from both sides of a polarised debate about ESG, and their rationales may be quoted back to the firm by clients, campaigners or politicians.

Engagement — the meetings with companies that are supposed to be the substance of stewardship — gets squeezed into the rest of the year and recorded inconsistently, so the analyst cannot easily show what an engagement achieved.

## What a Solution Looks Like
A triage layer that applies the firm's own policy to every ballot item, flags where it diverges from the proxy adviser recommendation and from the firm's own past votes on similar items, and routes only the genuinely contested items to a human with the relevant engagement history, the PM's position, and similar past decisions with their rationales. Rationales are drafted from the policy and the specific facts, for the analyst to edit. Engagements are logged with objectives and milestones so that, by report season, the outcome of each is a query rather than a memory.

## Impact If Solved
The routine majority of the season is handled consistently and documented automatically, the analyst's attention goes to the votes where judgment changes the outcome, and the stewardship report becomes an assembly of records rather than a reconstruction.

# The Treasurer's Midnight Settlement

**Industry:** [[ticketing-agencies|Ticketing & Reservation Agencies]]
**Type:** Worker Life Changing
**One-liner:** A venue box office treasurer spends show night reconciling holds, comps, kills and will-call against the ticketing system by hand, and gets the night back if the hold record keeps itself.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #feature-engineering #descriptive-statistics #evaluation-metrics #worker-facing #automation #workflow-orchestration

## The Problem
For a sold-out concert, the treasurer at a mid-sized arena or theatre manages a few thousand seats that were never on public sale (estimate). There are artist holds, promoter holds, production kills for the soundboard and camera positions, sponsor and suite allocations, card-presale leftovers, ADA seating and the comps the tour manager asks for at 4 p.m. Each group is tracked in a spreadsheet beside the ticketing system, because the system records seat status but not who promised what to whom. Through the day the treasurer releases unused holds back to sale, swaps seats when a production kill moves, prints or transfers comps, and works the will-call window as the doors open.

After the show comes settlement. The promoter's rep, the artist's tour accountant and the venue sit down with the box office statement: tickets sold by price level, comps by category, holds released and sold, fees, taxes and facility charges. Every seat must be accounted for. If the comp count disagrees with the tour's list, or a released hold sold at a different price level, the treasurer traces it seat by seat at midnight while everyone waits.

## Why It Matters to the Worker
The treasurer is personally accountable for the count, and at some venues for the cash. A discrepancy is a professional mark and sometimes a deduction, and the work happens at the end of a long show day, often several nights a week in season (estimate). The skill that makes a good treasurer, knowing which holds will come back, which tour managers over-ask for comps, which kills can be released, is invisible because the spreadsheet only shows the outcome.

The role is also a career dead end when it is all reconciliation. Experienced treasurers, many of them union members trained over years, spend their best hours copying seat numbers between screens.

## What a Solution Looks Like
A hold ledger inside the ticketing workflow: every hold created with its purpose, owner and release deadline; every comp request logged from the tour's email or text; every release and swap recorded with its reason. A settlement draft that assembles itself from the ledger and the sales data, with discrepancies flagged by seat before the meeting starts, and release suggestions during the day based on how each hold category has behaved at past shows.

## Impact If Solved
Settlement in fifteen minutes rather than an hour or more, fewer disputed seats on the box office statement, and a treasurer who goes home at a reasonable hour with a record that shows the judgement they exercised (estimates).

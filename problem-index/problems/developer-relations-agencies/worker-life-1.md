# The Advocate Who Is Also the Product

**Industry:** [[developer-relations-agencies|Developer Relations Agencies]]
**Type:** Worker Life Changing
**One-liner:** The job is being personally credible to developers, which means constant travel, public performance, an always-on presence and a reputation that is simultaneously your employer's asset and your own.

## The Problem
A developer advocate's effectiveness is inseparable from their personal credibility. They speak at conferences under their own name, publish under their own byline, and build relationships in communities as themselves. That is why the function works and it is also what makes the role difficult to sustain.

The travel is heavy — conference seasons with multiple events a month, in multiple time zones, each involving preparation, delivery and the social obligations around it. The presence is continuous, because community channels do not observe working hours and developers ask questions when they hit problems. The performance is public and permanent, and a talk that goes badly or a post that lands wrong is visible and archived.

Content production runs underneath it all: talks, posts, videos, samples, workshops, each requiring technical depth to be credible with an audience that detects superficiality immediately.

And the role is measured on activity, so the pressure is to do more of all of it.

## Why It Matters to the Worker
Burnout in this role is common enough to be a recurring public topic within the profession, and the causes are structural rather than individual: sustained travel, the emotional labour of being publicly available, and the impossibility of separating the professional persona from the person.

The credibility is portable, which cuts both ways. An advocate builds a reputation that follows them, which is genuine career capital and also means their employer's community relationship is personally held. Advocates feel the obligation of that — leaving means a community loses the person they trusted — and employers feel the risk, which introduces a quiet tension into a role already lacking clear metrics.

The lack of measurement makes it worse rather than better. Without outcome evidence, advocates cannot tell whether the exhausting thing they did was worth doing, cannot defend the slow relationship work that is probably the most valuable part, and cannot argue against the activity targets that drive the travel.

And the support load arrives regardless of the job description, because developers ask the person who seems approachable.

## What a Solution Looks Like
Make the content production sustainable. Turning one piece of work into many — a talk into a post, a workshop into a tutorial, a community answer into documentation — is mechanical adaptation that currently consumes the advocate's time and is a large share of the output volume.

Protect the presence. Triage of community questions so the advocate sees the ones genuinely needing them, with routine and already-answered questions handled, restores the ability to be absent without the community suffering.

Show the effect of the slow work. Even rough cohort-level measurement that indicates relationship-building in a community preceded adoption there gives an advocate something to point at when defending the work that activity metrics penalise.

Reduce the personal-dependency risk deliberately. Community relationships held by a team and a record rather than by one person's memory and rapport is better for everyone, and it requires the organisation to invest in continuity rather than relying on an individual's goodwill.

And measure the travel honestly. Event portfolios are chosen by habit and by invitation; evaluating which events actually produce anything — even crudely — would reduce the volume without reducing the effect.

## Impact If Solved
This is a role with well-documented burnout driven by activity metrics that reward exactly the behaviours that cause it. Content adaptation and question triage reduce the volume directly; cohort measurement defends the slow relationship work that is probably the highest-value part; and deliberate continuity investment addresses a succession risk that currently falls on individuals as a personal obligation.

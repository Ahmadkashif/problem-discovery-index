# Takedown and Copyright Reviewer

**Industry:** [[digital-goods-marketplaces|Digital Goods Marketplaces]]
**Type:** Worker Life Changing
**One-liner:** A reviewer decides copyright and originality questions at volume, between two creators who both claim the work, on evidence consisting of two files and two assertions.
**Tags:** #cnns #contrastive-learning #bert #large-language-models #evaluation-metrics #confidence-intervals #compliance #worker-facing

## The Problem
A creator files a claim that an item on the marketplace copies their work. The reviewer must decide.

Some cases are clear — an identical file uploaded under a different name. Most are not. Two templates in the same style may both derive from a common convention. A set of icons may be similar because the subject constrains the form. A font may share characteristics with another without being a copy. A course may cover the same material in the same order because that is the material's order.

The reviewer is applying copyright standards — substantial similarity, originality, whether the shared elements are protectable — that are genuinely contested and fact-specific. They do it in minutes, at volume, with no legal training.

Both parties are creators earning from the platform. Removing a legitimate original harms someone's income; leaving an infringing copy harms someone else's. The reviewer decides both ways all day.

Claims are also used tactically. Competing creators file against each other, and distinguishing a genuine claim from a competitive one is part of the job and is nowhere in the policy.

## Why It Matters to the Worker
Legal judgement at production pace is the structural problem. Copyright questions occupy courts and the reviewer has minutes, and the platform's liability and two creators' incomes rest on the outcome.

The asymmetry drives over-removal. Failing to remove risks liability from a rights holder; removing wrongly harms an individual creator with limited recourse. The rational individual response is to remove liberally, which is the documented pattern and is bad for the creators the platform depends on.

Both parties are sympathetic, which is emotionally harder than a queue with a clear villain. Reviewers hear from creators whose income has been removed and from creators whose work was copied, and both are frequently right about their own situation.

And the accumulated knowledge is discarded. A reviewer who has decided thousands of claims knows which patterns are genuine, which categories generate tactical claims, and where the policy is unclear, and there is usually no route from the queue to the policy.

## What a Solution Looks Like
Automated matching for the clear cases in both directions. Identical and near-identical files should be handled without human review, and demonstrably independent work should be cleared, concentrating reviewer time on the genuinely contested middle.

Evidence assembly for the rest. Upload timestamps, creation history, the creators' prior catalogues, similarity measured on the specific elements at issue, and comparable prior decisions turn a two-assertion standoff into a decision with grounding.

Common-convention detection. Where similarity is explained by a widely used convention in the category — measurable by comparing against the whole corpus — that is directly relevant and is currently a judgement from experience.

Tactical claim detection. Claim patterns between competing creators are visible in the data and identifying them protects the party being targeted.

Appeal outcome tracking as a first-class metric, since overturn rate is the only signal that the removal threshold is set wrong, and a real appeals process is what makes liberal removal correctable.

## Impact If Solved
Copyright review is legal judgement performed at production pace between two parties who both earn from the platform, with an asymmetry that drives over-removal. Automating the clear cases and grounding the contested ones in corpus evidence improves both accuracy and consistency, and appeal tracking is the only mechanism that keeps the threshold honest.

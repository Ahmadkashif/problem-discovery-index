# HR Generalist Answering the Same Question

**Industry:** [[hr-tech-platforms|HR Tech Platforms]]
**Type:** Worker Life Changing
**One-liner:** HR generalists stop spending their day on how-much-PTO-do-I-have and start doing the work that actually needs a person, because the routine questions are answered accurately from the employee's own record.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #bert #word-embeddings #transformers #evaluation-metrics #automation #workflow-orchestration #worker-facing

## The Problem
An HR generalist supports several hundred employees. Their inbox is dominated by a small set of questions asked repeatedly: how much leave do I have, how do I add a dependent, when does open enrolment close, what is the policy on remote work, how do I get a verification of employment, why did my paycheque change, what happens to my benefits if I go on leave.

Almost every answer is either in the handbook or in the employee's own record. The generalist looks it up and replies. Then the next person asks the same thing.

Self-service portals were built to solve exactly this and have not, because employees do not know which of forty policy documents applies to them, cannot navigate a benefits portal designed by a benefits administrator, and find asking a person faster than searching. Which it is.

Volume spikes predictably — open enrolment, performance cycle, bonus payment, any policy change — and the generalist absorbs it on top of the work that actually requires them.

## Why It Matters to the Worker
HR generalists are hired to handle the things that need judgement: an employee relations issue, a difficult accommodation, a manager who needs coaching, an investigation. That work is consequential, requires skill, and is what makes the role a career.

It gets displaced by lookups. The generalist is interrupted constantly, context-switches all day, and does the substantive work late or badly. The interruptions also arrive with emotional weight — a question about leave is often a question about a sick parent — so the volume is not merely administrative.

There is a quieter cost in accuracy. A generalist answering the same question for the fortieth time, under interruption, from memory rather than from the document, gets it wrong occasionally, and in employment matters a wrong answer given confidently creates real exposure.

## What a Solution Looks Like
Answers grounded in the individual's own record and the policies that actually apply to them — their jurisdiction, their employment class, their plan elections, their accrual balance — rather than a search across a policy library. The reason self-service failed is that it was generic, and the reason a generalist succeeds is that they apply the policy to the person.

It must cite the source. An employee will accept an answer about their leave entitlement if it shows the balance, the accrual rule and the policy paragraph. An unsourced assertion about employment terms is worse than no answer.

Escalation must be conservative and obvious. Anything touching an accommodation, a complaint, a medical circumstance or a termination goes to a person immediately, and the boundary should be drawn generously rather than optimised for deflection rate.

And the pattern should be reported back: forty people asking the same question in a week is a communication failure or a policy that is unclear, and it is currently invisible.

## Impact If Solved
The routine question volume is the largest single consumer of HR capacity in most organisations and the main reason the substantive work is done under interruption. Grounding answers in the individual's own record is what self-service was supposed to be and never was.

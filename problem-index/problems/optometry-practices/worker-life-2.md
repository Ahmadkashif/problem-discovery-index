# The Front Desk's Morning of Vision-Plan Portals

**Industry:** [[optometry-practices|Optometry Practices]]
**Type:** Worker Life Changing
**One-liner:** Front-desk staff spend the first hours of every day logging into vision and medical plan portals one patient at a time, then take the blame at checkout when a frame allowance or copay turns out different from what they said.
**Tags:** #worker-facing #large-language-models #transformers #automation #data-integration #quick-win

## The Problem
Tomorrow's schedule has forty patients. For each one, a receptionist or insurance coordinator checks eligibility before the visit, on whichever portal the patient's plan uses: Eyefinity for VSP, EyeMed's portal, Versant's for Davis and Superior, Spectera's, and the medical plan's portal or a clearinghouse for medical coverage. Each check means confirming the member is eligible today, when they last used their exam and materials benefits, the copays, the frame, lens and contact lens allowances, and whether an authorization number must be generated before the visit. Children on Medicaid and patients with two vision plans add more steps. There are 16,600 receptionists and information clerks in optometry offices (BLS OEWS 2023), and in a small practice the same person also answers the phone.

The work never finishes cleanly. Patients change employers and plans, give the wrong card, or used their benefits at a retailer last month. Then the patient chooses frames, and the allowance quoted in the morning does not match what the plan pays. The patient is upset at checkout, and the person they are upset with is whoever did the check.

## Why It Matters to the Worker
It is screen work done under pressure, with a phone ringing and patients arriving. It repeats daily with little variation, and the errors are public and personal. Plan rules change each year, and each plan describes the same benefit differently, so staff carry a mental translation table that no one else has. When they leave, the next person learns it by making the same mistakes in front of patients.

## What a Solution Looks Like
An overnight benefits check across every plan the practice accepts. It logs into portals or uses electronic eligibility where available, and turns each plan's wording into one standard benefits card per patient: exam, lenses, frames, contacts, copays, last-used dates and any authorization needed. Problems get flagged for a human by morning. At checkout it gives the optician and front desk a plain estimate for the frames and lenses the patient actually picks.

## Impact If Solved
At ~5 minutes a check across forty patients, staff would get back about three hours a day (~3.3 hours, estimate). Checkout disputes over allowances would drop sharply, and the job would turn from data entry into helping the patients in front of them.

# Personal Property and Condition Reports Built for No State

**Industry:** [[repossession-services|Repossession Agencies]]
**Type:** Low Impact (Customisation Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Every recovered car needs a photographed condition report and an inventory of the borrower's belongings handled to the state's clock, and agencies do it on generic inspection apps and paper forms that know nothing about either.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #cnns #object-detection #transformers #compliance #workflow-orchestration #automation #quick-win

## The Problem
When a car arrives on the lot the agent or a lot clerk walks around it with a phone: dents, glass, tyres, keys, mileage, warning lights, whether it was towed on dollies or drove. That record is the agency's defence when the borrower or the auction later claims the truck damaged the bumper. Then the clerk opens the doors and lists what is inside — a car seat, tools, a laptop, prescription bottles, a firearm, paperwork — bags it, labels it, and stores it.

The belongings carry the legal risk. California's guidance requires the inventory to reach the debtor within ~48 hours and the items to be kept for at least 60 days; Florida makes failing to safeguard and inventory property a prohibited act for licensed agencies; the CFPB has said holding property hostage to a fee is unfair. Lenders and forwarders add their own rules on photos, release appointments and fees. A missing item becomes a complaint against the lender and a mark against the agency on the forwarder's scorecard.

## What Already Exists
General field inspection apps such as SafetyCulture and CompanyCam capture photos and checklists; the wholesale auctions run their own condition reports at arrival, analysed under [[niches/auto-dealers-independent/auction-condition-report-ops/profile|Wholesale Auction Condition Report Operations]]; RDN's Cleardata module tracks personal property and debtor appointments for agencies on that platform; many agencies still use a carbon-copy inventory form and a camera roll.

## The Customisation Gap
The generic apps record what they are told and stop. The repossession version needs: a condition template matched to how the car was recovered and towed, so pre-existing damage is photographed before the hook as well as after; item recognition from interior photos that drafts the inventory and flags regulated items (firearms, medication, cash, documents with personal data) for special handling; the state's clock for the debtor notice, storage period and disposal written into each file, with the letter generated in the state's required form; the client's fee and release rules; and a signed release record when the borrower collects. One record should feed the lender update, the invoice and any later complaint file.

## Impact If Solved
A lot clerk who spends ~30–45 minutes per car on photos, forms and letters gets most of that back (estimate), the agency stops losing damage and missing-property disputes it cannot document, and the inventory deadline becomes something the system tracks rather than something a busy office remembers.

# Contract Obligations and Rights Administration

**Industry:** [[creator-talent-agencies|Creator Talent Agencies]]
**Type:** Low Impact (Customisation Opportunity)
**One-liner:** Every deal is a bespoke set of deliverables, windows and permissions written in prose, and the agency tracks them in a spreadsheet with a date column.
**Tags:** #large-language-models #bert #transformers #gradient-boosting #evaluation-metrics #compliance #workflow-orchestration #automation

## The Problem
A creator deal contains deliverables with due dates, a usage rights window with named placements and territories, an exclusivity period covering a defined category, whitelisting permissions, approval processes, disclosure obligations, and payment terms with milestones. All of it is expressed as contract prose, negotiated individually, and different in every deal.

An agency with forty creators has hundreds of these live at once. What it has to track them with is a spreadsheet somebody maintains, and the tracking is a summary of a negotiation whose details live in a signed PDF nobody rereads.

The failures follow directly. A brand keeps running a creator's asset as a paid advertisement after the usage window closed — extremely common, rarely detected, and worth real money when it is. An exclusivity clause blocks a deal nobody realised was blocked, or worse, a deal is signed that breaches one. A deliverable slips because nobody was tracking it until the brand chased. An approval round stalls and the launch date does not move.

## What Already Exists
Contract lifecycle management exists as a mature enterprise category and is priced and designed for corporate legal departments rather than for talent agencies. E-signature is universal. Influencer platforms track rights for the brands that use them, on the brand's side of the table. Some agencies use project management tools to track deliverables. Larger agencies within holding companies have access to business affairs systems built for traditional talent, which model film and television rights rather than social usage windows.

## The Customisation Gap
The gap is the extraction. Obligations are in prose and need to be structured — deliverables with dates, windows with placements and territories, exclusivity with a defined category and duration, approval steps with owners — and doing that by hand for hundreds of bespoke deals is why it is not done. Extracting them reliably and holding them as first-class records is the change that makes everything else possible, and it is precisely the task that recently became practical.

Enforcement is the second gap and it requires looking outward. A usage window has expired only matters if somebody checks whether the asset is still running, which means monitoring the brand's actual ad placements. Nobody does this, which is why the breach is so common and so rarely claimed. For an agency it is a direct revenue opportunity as well as a client protection.

Conflict checking across the roster is the third. Exclusivity clauses interact — a creator's category exclusivity, an agency-level arrangement, a competitor's pending deal — and answering "can this creator take this deal" requires querying obligations that currently exist only as prose in a folder.

## Impact If Solved
Rights breaches and missed deliverables are ordinary rather than exceptional in this business, and both are direct financial losses — the first to the creator, the second to the relationship. Structured obligations extracted from the contracts themselves, checked against what is actually running, turn a recurring quiet loss into a claimable one and remove a category of administrative failure that currently scales with the agency's success.

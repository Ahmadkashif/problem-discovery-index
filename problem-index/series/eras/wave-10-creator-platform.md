# Wave 10 — The Creator Platform (2012–2020)

**Trigger:** YouTube Partner Program 2007 (expanded Dec 10 2007); Twitch 2011, acquired by Amazon Aug 2014 for $970M; Patreon May 2013; OnlyFans Nov 2016; Douyin Sept 2016 → TikTok internationally Sept 2017, Musical.ly merged Aug 2018; Substack 2017
**What went to ~zero:** the cost of **reaching an audience**
**Failure class produced:** the asymmetric hold, applied to the self-employed

## What Was True The Day Before

Reaching an audience required an institution to select you. A label, a publisher, a studio, a network. The institution carried the distribution cost and the risk, and in exchange took ownership of the work and most of the money. Gatekeeping was not malice — it was the rational response to distribution being expensive.

## The Trigger

Platforms that would distribute anything to anyone and share revenue. The Partner Program was the pivotal move: the first time user-generated video had a revenue share attached, which converted a hobby into an occupation.

Patreon, Substack and OnlyFans then unbundled the model — direct payment from audience to creator, with the platform taking a fee rather than owning the work.

## The Competitive Fight

**Who owns the audience relationship.**

Platforms control discovery through opaque, frequently changed ranking systems. The creator has no ownership of the audience graph and **no contractual right to reach**. That is the whole contest, and Substack's pitch was an explicit reaction to it: an email list is creator-owned and portable, with no algorithm in between. Creators who had been burned by platform reach cuts moved to newsletters specifically to reclaim distribution.

Take rates in this fight, approximate and varying by programme and date: YouTube roughly 55/45 in the creator's favour on ad revenue; Twitch 50/50 on subscriptions as standard (70/30 only for Partner Plus up to $100k/yr, then reverting); Patreon 5–12%; Substack 10%. **Apple's 30% App Store cut applies on top** of platform fees for iOS in-app purchases, compounding the loss.

## What It Broke

**Creators bear business risk with no instrumentation.** Two documented cases, both with hard evidence:

**The adpocalypse, Feb–March 2017.** The Guardian pulled ads on 16–17 March after they ran beside extremist content; the UK government followed; by 23 March over 250 brands including McDonald's, AT&T, Walmart and Verizon had paused spend, with Nomura estimating up to $750M at risk. YouTube's automated response mass-demonetised creator videos — per Ethan Klein's 29 March account, with **no notification and no option to appeal.** A second wave followed in 2019 on child-safety and COPPA grounds.

**Facebook's pivot to video, 2015–16.** Publishers — BuzzFeed, Mashable, HuffPost — reallocated staff and budget on Facebook's video-engagement signal. Facebook later admitted, and was sued over, systematically **overstating average video view time by 60–80%** (settled for roughly $40M in 2019). The industry restructured itself around a metric its publisher was misreporting, with no independent way to check, and was then hit by the 2018 "Meaningful Social Interactions" change that cut publisher organic reach sharply.

That is the asymmetric hold exactly: the platform knew the true number and the parties whose businesses depended on it did not.

**And the distribution is brutally unequal.** Analyses from 2023–2025 find power-law exponents around 1.8–1.94 across YouTube, Instagram, Twitch and Facebook. The top 1% of creators captured roughly 21% of payment volume in 2025 (up from ~15% in 2023); the top 10%, roughly 62%. Only about **0.25% of 30M+ YouTube channels ever clear monetisation thresholds.** *(Point figures vary widely by source, year and platform definition — use ranges and treat the shape, not the number, as the finding.)*

*(Myth: Vine's 2016–17 shutdown is often folded into "platform killed creators" narratives. It was primarily Twitter's monetisation and strategic failure, not an algorithmic distribution change. Do not conflate it with the adpocalypse or pivot-to-video, which have documentary evidence.)*

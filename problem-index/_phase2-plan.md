## 1. Why this segment

The existing 118 industries are **operator tier**: US SMB service and trade businesses, 1–50 people, physically located. That population is fully swept — Pass 1 (944 niches) and Pass 2 (1,530 pockets, 118/118) are complete.

This segment is a different axis, not more of the same. The digital economy is **product, platform and infrastructure tier**: businesses whose deliverable is software, data, distribution or a digital service. They differ from the existing 118 in every way that matters to us:

- Research is a **build input**, not an overhead — roadmap, pricing, ranking, matching, moderation, underwriting.
- The proprietary data is **generated by the product itself**, continuously, at no marginal cost.
- The buyer is technical and already pays for tooling.
- The clock is a release cycle, not a season.

The eleven existing industries that touch this space (`software-dev-agencies`, `cybersecurity-mssp`, `it-managed-services`, `cloud-infrastructure-consultants`, `data-analytics-consultants`, `ecommerce-sellers`, `marketing-agencies-smb`, `podcasting-networks`, `video-production-smb`, `independent-publishers`, `news-media-local`) are all **operator tier — service firms selling hours**. Nothing in this segment duplicates them; where a subject is adjacent, this segment takes the **platform/vendor** side of it.

## 2. THE FILTER (standing, applies to everything from here)

> **Keep decomposing a niche until you can write one sentence: "Every serious competitor in this niche is fighting to solve X, and whoever solves X best takes the account."**

If that sentence cannot be written, the niche is still a *segment*, not a niche, and the work is not finished. This replaces "eight niches and stop" as the terminal condition.

**Mechanics — this breaks no invariant:**

- Every niche note gains a required **`**Contested on:**`** line stating that sentence, directly under the metadata block.
- Each industry still has exactly **8 level-1 niches × 4 files**. The 1:1:1 parity rule and the exactly-8 rule hold.
- Where a level-1 niche is **not** terminal under the filter, it gets **contested sub-niches** written as sibling directories (the same mechanism Pass 2 pockets already use), carrying `**Category:** Contested Sub-Niche` and 4 files each. `_overview.md` links them under the parent.
- A niche whose contested sentence is generic ("better UX", "lower price") has **failed the filter** — say so, and decompose or drop it. Do not manufacture a contested statement to fill a slot.

The same filter applies to Pass 2 pockets: a pocket is only worth a `build`/`buy`/`fix` if the contested sentence is writable.

## 4. Build order — layered, not per-industry

Reading the vault owner's instruction literally: *"take our count to 250 total industries by end of day … once we have a list, we then deep dive into niche and pocket enrichment."* That is **layer-by-layer**, and it is also the only ordering under which 250 today is real.

| Stage | What | Files | When |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | `industries/<slug>.md` + `problems/<slug>/` (7 files) × 132 | 1,056 | **today** — vault reaches 250 industries |
| **B** | Niche layer: `_overview.md` + 8 × 4 files, plus contested sub-niches under the filter | ~4,400 | next |
| **C** | Pass 2 insight-layer sweep across the 132 | ~1,600 | after B |

Full parity is still the destination — nothing is skipped, the order changes.

**Known temporary state during Stage A:** `industries/` ↔ `problems/` stays exactly 1:1 at every point. `niches/` lags by up to 132 slugs until Stage B closes. This is tracked here and in the cursor, and `_state/verify.sh` treats a slug present in `industries/`+`problems/` but absent from `niches/` as **Stage-A-pending**, not as a fault. Any other parity break is a real error.

**Cost at full build:** 132 × 41 parity files = 5,412, plus ~1,600 Pass 2 and ~800 contested sub-niches ≈ **7,800 files**. The vault holds 6,877 today, so this segment roughly doubles it. Stage A is ~1,056 of that.

## 5. Per-industry method

1. `industries/<slug>.md` — Profile, Key Pain Themes, Current Tech Landscape, Problems, Analysis.
2. `problems/<slug>/` — all 7 files.
3. `niches/<slug>/_overview.md` — four-axis table + rationale + niche links.
4. 8 niches × `profile.md` / `build.md` / `buy.md` / `fix.md`, **each carrying `**Contested on:**`**.
5. Apply the filter. Any non-terminal niche gets contested sub-niches as sibling directories.
6. Pass 2 insight-layer sweep over the industry (method in `_direction.md`), scoring into `_scorecard-index.md`; embedded-research reads into `_embedded-index.md`.
7. Update `_bookmark.md`, `niches/_bookmark.md`, and the cursor below.
8. Run `bash _state/verify.sh`. Fix before moving on.

Steps 7 and 8 run after **every industry**, not every batch.

## 6. Hard rules

- **Additive only.** Never edit the existing 118 industries, their `problems/`, or the 944 Pass 1 niches.
- Canonical tags only (`metadata/tags.md`). No deprecated tags. `profile.md` carries no Tags line.
- Prior deployments (agriculture, sports, private equity) carry **zero** scoring weight.
- Log negative results honestly. Never manufacture a contested statement to fill a slot.
- Do not parallelise across industries — shared files (`_scorecard-index.md`, bookmarks, this cursor) corrupt under concurrent writes.

**Batch 7 — Fintech ✅ COMPLETE.** All 11 industries: `neobanks`, `payment-processors`, `bnpl-providers`, `embedded-finance-platforms`, `crypto-exchanges`, `robo-advisors`, `lending-marketplaces`, `spend-management-platforms`, `ap-automation-vendors`, `payment-fraud-vendors`, `identity-verification-vendors` — 41 files each. The batch has one theme running through every industry in it: **the outcome of the decision is structurally unobservable to the party who made it, and the industry has built its entire metric set out of the half that returns.** Fraud vendors train and report on chargebacks, which exist only for approvals. Identity verification publishes a pass rate and never learns who the rejections were. Crypto exchanges freeze deposits and almost never discover whether the funds were criminal. Lending marketplaces rank on the click because lenders return nothing else. Robo-advisors record a stated risk tolerance and never check it against the behaviour they observe every day. Spend platforms store thousands of approve-or-deny judgements a month as audit trail. AP platforms record an exception's resolution as a status change. Neobanks, payment processors and BNPL each have the decision in one system and its consequence in another, owned by a different team, joined by nobody.

The second thread is what makes the first one durable: **the unobserved half is always the half where the cost falls on the customer rather than on the institution.** The false decline, the false freeze, the false reject, the wasted credit inquiry, the uncaptured early payment discount, the uncollected employer match, the deposit held for six weeks with no explanation — each is real money or real exclusion, borne by someone who is not in the room and does not appear in any report. The measurement gap and the harm distribution coincide exactly, which is why the gap has survived: closing it requires a party to pay a visible cost now to quantify a harm it currently does not carry. The fix named in these industries is correspondingly cheap and correspondingly unpopular — a small randomised approval allowance, a structured resolution reason, a follow-up on the rejected, a join between two systems that already exist.

## Stage A findings worth carrying into Stage B and C

Three distinct problem classes emerged across the twelve batches, and they are not the same opportunity:

1. **The missing join** (batches 1–5, and most of 9–10). The organisation observes the outcome of its own prediction and never joins the two — because the data sits in two systems, or two companies, or arrives too late to be connected. Fixing it is integration work with a clear owner.

2. **The declined join** (batches 6–8, and much of 11). The data is present, complete and in one place, and the measurement is deliberately not made because the honest number is smaller than the reported one. Retail media's closed loop reporting last-click ROAS; affiliate last-click; streaming royalty allocation; UGC enforcement that could localise a decision and does not; bug bounty programmes with no effect measure; brand protection counting takedowns. No new data or technique is required — only a party willing to compute and publish.

3. **The asymmetric hold** (batch 12, and the worker-life notes across 11). The party holding the measurement withholds it from the person whose livelihood it governs — gig offer composition, crowdsourcing rejection reasons, creator exchange rates, enforcement reasons, assessment validity. Several of these are policy decisions with a model in a supporting role, and the notes say so rather than engineering around them.

Classes 2 and 3 are candidate axes for cross-cutting Pass 2 pockets in Stage C.

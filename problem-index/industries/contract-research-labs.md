# Contract Research Labs

## Profile
**Category:** Professional Services
**NAICS 2022:** 541715, 541713 — Research and Development in the Physical, Engineering, and Life Sciences (except Nanotechnology and Biotechnology); Research and Development in Nanotechnology
**Market Size:** ~$268B in 2022 revenue for the wider NAICS 54171 group (Census Service Annual Survey), which also counts biotech R&D and captive corporate R&D establishments; $50.8B of it came from tax-exempt establishments, the nonprofit-institute model. NAICS 541715 had 12,929 establishments and 596,332 employees in 2023, and 541713 had 1,722 establishments (County Business Patterns, as reported). The research actually sold under contract or grant by independent institutes, SBIR-funded firms and spinouts is ~$40–60B (estimate). Federal agencies obligated $194.2B for R&D in FY2024, $69.9B of it to businesses and $16.4B to FFRDCs (NSF NCSES)
**Tech Maturity:** Medium — the instruments, simulation codes and test rigs are state of the art, while the business of research (choosing what to bid, estimating it, charging time to it, marking what is delivered and keeping the experimental record) runs on Word, Excel, a government-contract ERP or QuickBooks, and paper or half-used electronic notebooks.
**Workforce:** Principal investigators and senior research scientists and engineers (natural sciences managers alone number 41,540 across the 54171 group, OEWS May 2023), mechanical, electrical and aerospace engineers, chemists, physicists and materials scientists, research technicians, software developers, program and project managers, contracts and pricing staff, government-contract accountants, security and export-control officers, and technical writers

Nanotechnology R&D (541713) is the same business at a smaller scale: university spinouts and SBIR-funded firms developing nanomaterials, coatings, sensors and devices that live on contracts and grants until a product exists. They share the funding model, the proposal treadmill and the compliance clocks of 541715 firms, and add a characterisation-heavy bench (electron microscopy, scattering, surface analysis) and a dependence on shared fabrication facilities. Drug discovery and preclinical work belong to Biotech Research Firms (541714), a separate industry queued but not yet built. Engineering design delivered under a professional engineer's stamp belongs to [[engineering-consultants|Engineering Consultants]], and accredited testing against published standards to [[testing-laboratories|Testing Laboratories]]; this industry's product is research whose outcome is not known when it is bought.

## Key Pain Themes
A contract research lab sells a promise: a technical approach, a named team, a schedule and a price, written into a proposal and judged by a program manager or a corporate sponsor. Two calls made by a handful of senior people decide almost everything that follows: which solicitations to answer, and how many hours a technical approach will really take. Both are made from memory and never graded. At DoD, 18% of SBIR Phase I proposals were selected in FY2022 and 28% of Phase II proposals (National Academies), and every losing proposal is unbillable principal-investigator time recovered only through overhead. From FY2027 agencies will cap how many SBIR proposals a firm may submit under the April 2026 reauthorisation (P.L. 119-83), which turns topic selection from a volume game into a rationed decision.

The money arrives wrapped in clocks and rules that someone else sets. Solicitations open and close on fixed dates. Time must be charged daily to the right contract under FAR cost principles, indirect rates must survive a DCAA audit, and on cost-type contracts the incurred-cost submission is due six months after fiscal year end under FAR 52.216-7. Every deliverable must carry the right data-rights legend: unmarked SBIR data gives the government unlimited rights during the 20-year protection period, and the firm has six months to correct a marking. Defense work brings export control, controlled unclassified information and CMMC, whose Phase 1 self-assessments have applied at award since November 2025; the Phase 2 third-party certification due on 10 November 2026 was suspended in July 2026 pending review. The SBIR programme itself lapsed from 30 September 2025 until April 2026, and firms living on SBIR revenue saw no new solicitations for six months.

At the bench, the knowledge that makes a lab good sits in people and notebooks. The senior experimentalist who knows why a synthesis drifts, which instrument settings produce a trustworthy image and how to read a failed run rarely writes it down, and the postdocs and staff scientists who do the measuring turn over every few years. Routine data reduction, such as sizing hundreds of nanoparticles per sample from electron micrographs, is still done by hand in many labs.

## Current Tech Landscape
Government-contract ERPs such as Deltek Costpoint and Unanet GovCon handle DCAA timekeeping and indirect rates for mid-size and large performers; small SBIR firms run QuickBooks with timekeeping add-ons and spreadsheets for rates. Opportunity tracking runs on Deltek GovWin IQ, HigherGov, GovTribe and the agencies' own portals (SBIR.gov, DoD's DSIP, Grants.gov, Research.gov, eRA Commons). Proposals are written in Word from the last proposal; reports go out as Word and PDF with legends pasted by hand. Experimental records live in Benchling, LabArchives or paper notebooks, raw data on instrument PCs, and analysis in vendor software (Thermo Fisher Velox, Gatan DigitalMicrograph), ImageJ, MATLAB, COMSOL and Python. Each tool is competent inside its boundary. The record that matters most — what the lab proposed, what it estimated, what it actually spent, whether the milestone was met and whether the sponsor came back — spans all of them and is owned by none.

## Analysis
The industry's central unmeasured judgement is the research bet: the senior investigator's call that a topic is winnable, that a technical approach will reach its milestone and that it will take this many hours. Every bet is dated, priced and later resolved — the award is won or lost, the hours are booked in the ERP, the milestone is met or slips, the sponsor funds a next phase or does not — yet the records sit in four systems and nobody joins them, so a firm cannot say which of its PIs estimate well or which kinds of topic it actually converts. Three things block fixing it. The sample is small and every project is somewhat novel, so grading needs years of records. Defense data is export-controlled or controlled unclassified, and commercial results belong to the sponsor, which keeps firms from pooling. And senior PIs, whose standing rests on their judgement, are wary of having it scored. The strongest commercial opening is mid-size SBIR firms and independent institutes, where the FY2027 proposal caps make bid selection newly scarce and compliance clocks force spending; the long tail of one-to-five-person shops buys little. Under the Pass 2 rubric the operators themselves pass the gate but trip the kill switches, because sponsor-owned and export-controlled data cannot be reused. No pocket qualifies; the closest, at 49, sits beside them: the research security due diligence providers that agencies and universities now pay to screen applicants and researchers for foreign influence before each award, held back by a market of a handful of providers.

# Brand Protection Firms

## Profile
**Category:** Trust, Safety & Security
**Market Size:** ~$4B US in online brand protection — marketplace and social monitoring, counterfeit enforcement, domain and phishing takedown, and grey-market detection — from Corsearch, MarkMonitor, Red Points, BrandShield, OpSec and a wide specialist field
**Tech Maturity:** Industrial detection and enforcement against a metric nobody has validated. These firms scan marketplaces, social platforms, app stores and domain registrations at scale, identify apparent infringement and submit notices through each platform's process. Takedowns are counted, reported monthly and used to justify the contract. Whether the enforcement reduced counterfeit sales is not measured.
**Workforce:** Detection analysts reviewing listings, enforcement and notice operations staff, legal and IP counsel, customer success and reporting teams, data and platform engineers

## Key Pain Themes
Volume is the product. A brand protection contract is sold and renewed on listings removed, sites taken down and sellers actioned, and those numbers are reported without any accompanying estimate of effect. The pattern practitioners describe privately is that removed listings are replaced within days by the same operators under new accounts, so the count measures activity on both sides rather than progress on either.

The second theme is overreach and its cost to people who are not counterfeiters. Automated detection flags legitimate resale, parallel imports, repair services, parody, criticism and unrelated uses of similar names, and notice processes at most platforms favour the complainant — so a wrongly-actioned small seller loses income and faces an appeal process built for the party with counsel. This is a documented and recurring harm, and the firms' own error rates are not published.

The third is that enforcement is per-listing while the problem is per-operator. A single counterfeit operation runs hundreds of accounts across several platforms, and removing listings one at a time is the least effective available intervention — while the data to identify the operator behind them sits in the firm's own detection records.

## Current Tech Landscape
Detection combines marketplace and social scraping, image matching, text and seller-attribute analysis, and test purchasing for verification. Platform programmes — Amazon's Brand Registry, eBay's VeRO, and the equivalents at social and app stores — provide privileged notice channels for enrolled brands and are the main enforcement mechanism. Domain enforcement uses registrar processes and the UDRP. Payment and logistics disruption is used by the more sophisticated programmes and is far less common than listing removal. Test purchase and physical verification remain the ground truth for counterfeit determination.

## Analysis
This industry holds the longitudinal record of counterfeit operations across every surface it monitors — which accounts appeared when, sharing which images, shipping patterns, pricing behaviour and account characteristics, and what happened after each enforcement action. That record answers the two questions the category cannot currently answer: whether enforcement reduces infringing supply, and which operator is behind a cluster of accounts. It is used instead to generate a monthly takedown count. The measurement gap and the overreach problem share a cause — a business model priced on notice volume rewards more notices and is indifferent to whether they were right or whether they worked.

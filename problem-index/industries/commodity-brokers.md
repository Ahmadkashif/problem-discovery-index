# Commodity Brokers

## Profile
**Category:** Financial Services
**NAICS 2022:** 523160 — Commodity Contracts Intermediation
**Market Size:** ~$374B in futures customer funds that 75 registered FCMs were required to hold in segregation at end-August 2026 (CFTC Selected FCM Financial Data); BLS QCEW counted 3,516 private establishments, 25,925 employees and $10.2B in wages under NAICS 523160 in 2025, a count that also takes in commodity dealers acting as principals. Receipts for the 2022 code are not published.
**Tech Maturity:** Medium. Order routing, clearing and statements run on industrial platforms, and exchange connectivity is fully electronic. The judgements wrapped around them still run on phone calls, chat, PDF statements and spreadsheets: which under-margined client to chase, how a farmer should hedge, how an introducing broker pays its brokers.
**Workforce:** Associated persons (registered brokers holding the Series 3; NFA counted 37,051 associates in September 2026), FCM risk, margin and credit staff, segregation and treasury accountants, clearing operations clerks, compliance and AML officers, introducing-broker principals (NFA counted 900 IBs), and the agricultural hedging brokers who work from farm-town offices

## Key Pain Themes
A futures commission merchant guarantees its customers' trades to the clearing house. Each morning it pays the clearing house the variation margin its losing customers owe, then has to collect from those customers. The risk desk works through a list of accounts in margin deficit and decides, account by account, whether to call, extend or liquidate. Those calls lean on what the firm's brokers know about each client: who always wires late but always wires, whose hedge is real, who will stop answering the phone. The outcome of each decision is recorded exactly, as paid, deficit or bad debt. The decision itself is not. Shocks show what is at stake. On April 20, 2020 the May WTI contract settled at -$37.63, and Interactive Brokers absorbed about $88M of customer losses its systems had not been built to margin. In the spring 2022 grain rally CME raised margins at least six times in six weeks while elevators faced margin calls in the millions. And in 1988, banks pulled farmers' hedge lines and brokers liquidated short corn hedges near the top.

Around those decisions sit regulatory deadlines that don't move. Every business day an FCM must compute, currency by currency, the funds it holds for futures customers, foreign-futures customers and cleared-swap customers against what it owes them (Rules 1.32, 30.7 and 22.2), finish before noon the next business day, and report the result to the CFTC and its designated self-regulatory organisation. Its capital requirement scales with customer margin (8% of risk maintenance margin), so a busy market raises the requirement on the very day the firm is busiest. Oral communications that lead to trades must be recorded and kept for a year at every FCM, and at every IB with more than $5M of gross revenue over three years. Written communications are kept five years.

The structure squeezes the middle. Seven bank FCMs hold about two-thirds of all segregated customer funds. Among non-banks, StoneX's July 2025 purchase of R.J. O'Brien, with roughly 300 introducing brokers and 75,000 accounts, created the largest non-bank FCM. The 900 introducing brokers depend on their clearing FCM's statements to pay their own brokers and supervise them. Farm-belt offices are small: QCEW counted 58 establishments and 182 employees in Iowa against 8,020 employees in Illinois. Meanwhile retail apps, crypto venues and prediction markets (Robinhood, Webull, Coinbase Financial Markets, Kalshi Prime, FanDuel Prediction Markets) now appear on the CFTC's FCM list. One scope note: NAICS 523160 merges commodity dealing (the old 523130) with brokerage. This build covers only the intermediaries, the FCMs and introducing brokers that execute and clear for customers. Commodity dealing is out of scope: proprietary trading firms, energy and physical merchants' trading desks, and swap dealers acting as principals. QCEW counts them in the same code, so its establishment and wage figures overstate the brokerage half.

## Current Tech Landscape
Back offices run on FIS's Cleared Derivatives Suite (descended from SunGard's GMI) or ION's XTP, which Advantage Futures adopted in 2024. Front ends are CQG, Trading Technologies, Rithmic and the FCMs' own platforms such as StoneX One. Margin comes from CME's SPAN methodology and its SPAN 2 successor. Trade surveillance is sold by Nasdaq, NICE Actimize and Eventus, and voice and chat capture by Smarsh, Global Relay and NICE. Agricultural brokers add Barchart's cmdty data, DTN and the elevator platforms such as Bushel, and most brokers keep clients in a generic CRM or a spreadsheet. Each tool is competent within its boundary. No tool owns the client-level decision record: why a deficit was extended, what a farmer's hedge plan was meant to protect, which recommendation a broker gave and whether it worked.

## Analysis
The industry's central unmeasured judgement is credit: which customer to trust with an unpaid margin call, for how long, and when to cut them out. Every such decision has a perfect, dated outcome, and almost none is written down in a form that can be graded. Three things block fixing that. It is organisational, because the knowledge sits with brokers whose books are portable and who move between FCMs. It is regulatory, because the record must survive an NFA or DSRO examination, so firms prefer saying less. It is temporal, because the decision is made between the clearing house's settlement and the market open, when nobody has time to type. The strongest commercial opening is with the 75 FCMs, and particularly the ~two dozen (estimate) non-bank and agricultural FCMs between the bank giants and the retail apps. They carry real deficit risk, their own back offices and regulatory deadlines, and budgets for operations and risk tooling. Introducing brokers are numerous but poor buyers: small, commission-squeezed and bound to their clearing FCM's systems. For a research-insight engine the operators fail the gate. Their insight is given away to win brokerage, and the paying insight businesses around commodity futures (price reporting agencies and agricultural market intelligence) are already profiled under other industries. AIS-based commodity-flow intelligence is the nearest new pocket, and it falls short.

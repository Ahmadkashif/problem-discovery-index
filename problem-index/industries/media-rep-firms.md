# Media Representative Firms

## Profile
**Category:** Professional Services
**NAICS 2022:** 541840 — Media Representatives
**Market Size:** ~$6.2B in receipts in the 2022 Economic Census, across ~1,233 establishments and ~23,100 employees (County Business Patterns 2023). The Service Annual Survey reports ~$32B for 2022 after an unexplained reclassification that appears to sweep in large digital ad-sales entities (inference), so the narrower figure is used. The commission rides on a far larger flow: CoxReps alone describes over $3B in national sales.
**Tech Maturity:** Medium — order exchange between agency buying systems and station traffic systems exists, but WideOrbit itself said in 2020 that ~95% of local spot TV transactions still ran on manual workflows, and pricing, share analysis and makegoods still live in spreadsheets, email and the seller's memory.
**Workforce:** National account executives organised in teams by station group and agency office; sales assistants who pull avails and key orders and revisions; research analysts who post ratings and build market cases; political-desk specialists; digital and CTV sellers; and, at independent publishers' rep firms, a principal and a handful of commission salespeople covering a territory for several magazines or newspapers

## Key Pain Themes
A media rep sells time and space it does not own. Katz Media, the largest, represents more than 3,400 radio stations and over 400 TV stations outside iHeartMedia and is paid a commission on what airs, on contracts that run up to ten years. What a station buys from its rep is share: its share of the national dollars in its market divided by its share of the audience, the power ratio. The rep's core judgement is pricing. For every avails request an account executive decides what cost per point each station can hold with this buyer this quarter, and when to take the order instead. Experienced AEs make that call by feel. The proposal, counters and order end up split across the agency's buying system, the station's traffic system and email, and the share outcome arrives weeks later in pooled revenue reports such as Miller Kaplan's Market X-Ray. Nobody joins the decision to the share it produced, and when a senior AE leaves, their buyer-by-buyer reads leave too.

Under the judgement sits the plumbing. A national buy can repeat the same work across 210 markets. Avails requests arrive as emails and spreadsheets, proposals are keyed into the buyer's system, and orders and every later revision are keyed again into each station's traffic system. Buys are guaranteed on ratings. When a spot is preempted or a program under-delivers, the rep owes a makegood or an audience-deficiency unit, and in 2019 buyers were still waiting about 60 days for an invoice to find out. In January 2025 Nielsen switched local currency to Big Data + Panel in every market, and advised clients to keep posting 2024 buys on the old panel-only numbers, so one book of business was being settled against two measurement systems at the same time.

The clocks are set from outside. In even years political advertising dominates: the 2024 cycle reached a record $11.1B, $5.3B of it on broadcast TV. Candidates are entitled to the lowest unit charge for the class during the 45 days before a primary and the 60 days before a general election, and every class and rate must be disclosed to them. Katz's segment revenue fell from $327.1M in 2024 to $272.5M in 2025, largely because there was no election. Quarter-ends and the scatter market set the commercial cadence. Station groups review rep contracts, and some have taken national sales in-house. Trade press reports large groups negotiating TV rep commissions down to ~6% from a historical ~15% (estimate).

## Current Tech Landscape
Agencies buy spot through Mediaocean and FreeWheel's Strata. Stations run traffic and sales on WideOrbit, Imagine Communications and RCS. WideOrbit's Order Connect and Hudson MX carry avails, orders and revisions between the two sides with varying adoption. Ratings come from Nielsen, with Comscore and VideoAmp as alternative currencies. Guideline's SQADCosts supplies CPP and CPM benchmarks, and Miller Kaplan pools station revenue into market share reports. Vivvix and MediaRadar track who is advertising where, AdImpact tracks political spending, and Salesforce holds accounts. Pricing, posting and makegood tracking mostly run in Excel. Each tool is competent within its boundary. No tool owns the record that matters to the rep: the pricing decision, the order it produced, the delivery against the guarantee and the share outcome in the market.

## Analysis
The industry's central unmeasured judgement is the AE's pricing read: the rep is hired and fired on share, and share is driven by thousands of pricing calls a year that are never recorded with their reasoning or joined to the outcome. Three things block a fix. The data sits in systems owned by others (the agency's buying platform, the station's traffic system, a pooled revenue report). Rep firms represent competing stations in one market through separate divisions behind firewalls, so pooling pricing data across clients is fraught. And a commission business with falling rates and a shrinking national spot base has little appetite for long projects. The strongest commercial opening is tooling sold to rep firms and the larger station groups: avails-to-proposal automation, revision and makegood settlement, a political desk that quotes the lowest unit charge correctly, and share analytics that explain power ratio market by market. As buyers of a research-insight engine, the rep firms themselves are poor. TV representation is concentrated in two firms, their research supports selling rather than being sold, and the data belongs to the stations. Pass 2 finds no qualifier. The nearest pockets are pooled revenue reporting and media cost benchmarks, and the political ad tracking that matters most to the rep's even-year business is already indexed under local news media.

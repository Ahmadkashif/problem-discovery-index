# Sales Finance Companies

## Profile
**Category:** Financial Services
**NAICS 2022:** 522220 — Sales Financing
**Market Size:** ~$137B in annual receipts across 2,416 firms and ~96,000 employees (Census SUSB 2022), sharply concentrated: 160 firms with 500+ employees earn ~90% of the receipts while 1,982 firms have fewer than 20 people. They finance a large share of a $1.71T US auto loan book (New York Fed, Q2 2026) — captives alone wrote 43.4% of new-vehicle loans in Q1 2026 (Experian) — plus dealer- and vendor-sourced paper in a ~$1.3T equipment leasing and finance industry (ELFA)
**Tech Maturity:** Medium — automated at the front, manual in the middle and back. Decision engines answer prime applications from the dealer networks in seconds; funders still read every contract package and stipulation by eye, and repossession, refunds and warehouse reporting run on servicing-system letters, vendor portals and spreadsheets.
**Workforce:** Credit analysts ("buyers") who decision and structure dealer-submitted applications; funders who verify contracts, stipulations and titles before paying the dealer; dealer relationship managers; early- and late-stage collectors; repossession and remarketing coordinators; title and lien clerks; capital markets and securitization analysts; credit risk modellers; compliance and fair lending staff

## Key Pain Themes
A sales finance company never meets its borrower. A dealer's F&I manager keys a credit application once and sends it through Dealertrack or RouteOne to several finance sources at the same moment. Each returns an approval, a counteroffer or a decline in minutes, the dealer books the deal with whichever source makes it close, signs the customer on a retail installment contract, and assigns that contract to the lender, which pays the dealer the amount financed plus a dealer reserve. Everything the dealer got wrong becomes the lender's problem: an inflated income, a vehicle "power booked" with options it does not have, a straw buyer, add-ons packed into the amount financed. Point Predictive put auto lending fraud exposure at a record $10.4B in 2026, 69% of it first-party. The defence is the credit analyst's read of the deal and of the dealer who sent it — a judgement built over thousands of applications and written down nowhere.

After the decision comes a chain of document and recovery work. Funders check signatures, the truth-in-lending math, state fee caps, proof of income and residence, insurance and title before releasing money, while the dealer calls daily asking where the funding is. Once booked, the loan is serviced, collected and, for a growing share, repossessed: subprime auto ABS 60+ day delinquencies reached a record 6.18% in 2025 (S&P), and Cox Automotive estimated ~1.73 million repossessions in 2024, the most since 2009. Recovery is governed state by state — notices before and after sale, personal property, redemption rights, deficiency balances — and every unearned GAP or service contract fee on a terminated loan has to be refunded correctly.

The money and the clocks come from outside. Finance companies fund on bank warehouse lines and asset-backed securities (a record ~$127B of US auto loan ABS in 2025), so every month ends with borrowing base certificates, eligibility tests, servicer reports and, for public deals, 72 asset-level fields per loan on Form ABS-EE. The September 2025 collapse of Tricolor, whose executives were charged in December 2025 with falsifying loan data and pledging the same collateral to more than one lender, has made warehouse banks demand proof that each contract exists, is perfected and is pledged once. Regulation sets the rest: CFPB supervision of nonbank auto lenders with 10,000+ originations a year (a threshold the CFPB asked in 2025 about raising), and California's CARS Act, operative October 1, 2026, whose dealer violations reach the contract's holder through the FTC Holder Rule.

## Current Tech Landscape
Dealertrack (Cox Automotive) and RouteOne (owned by captive lenders) carry the applications and eContracts; defi SOLUTIONS, MeridianLink and Origence's CUDL run origination and decisioning; Experian, TransUnion and Equifax supply bureau files and auto scores; Point Predictive and Informed.IQ screen applications and verify stipulations; Open Lending insures near-prime decisions; Shaw Systems, Nortridge and LoanPro service the loans; RDN routes repossession assignments to agents and DRN's licence-plate cameras find vehicles; Manheim, ACV and OPENLANE remarket them; J.D. Power, Black Book and Kelley Blue Book value collateral and ALG sets lease residuals; Intex and dv01 model the securitisations. Each tool is competent inside its boundary. The record that joins a credit analyst's decision, the dealer's behaviour, the contract's defects and the loan's outcome belongs to none of them, so a lender cannot see which overrides, dealers or deal structures lose money until a vintage has seasoned.

## Analysis
The industry's central unmeasured judgement is the credit buyer's read: the experienced analyst who looks at a dealer-submitted deal, the dealer's recent paper and the structure on the screen and knows that this one is packed, that this dealer's stated incomes have drifted, or that this thin-file borrower will pay if the payment stays under a number. Outcomes arrive with a lag — early payment default within months, charge-off over years — and are attributed to scorecards and vintages, never to the override or the counteroffer that created them. Three things block fixing it. Fair lending law means any model trained on human judgement must survive adverse-action explanation and disparate-impact testing, and dealer markup is a standing enforcement theme. The dealer relationship punishes caution, because a lender that declines or slows deals loses its place on the dealer's screen. And the data is split across the origination network, the LOS, the servicing system, the repossession vendor and the auction. For workflow automation the strongest opening is in funding, refunds and collateral reporting, where rules are explicit and the post-Tricolor demand for collateral proof is new. Under the Pass 2 rubric, the finance companies are poor buyers of an insight engine: their research is monetised as interest income, borrower files sit under GLBA and FCRA, and the data businesses that sell into them most profitably — valuation guides, pricing benchmarks, skip-trace and registration data — are already indexed under other industries.

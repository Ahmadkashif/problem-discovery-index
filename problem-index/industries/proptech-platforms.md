# Proptech Platforms

## Profile
**Category:** Vertical SaaS
**Market Size:** ~$11B US property management and real estate operations software
**Tech Maturity:** High and consolidating — Yardi, RealPage, Entrata, AppFolio, Buildium and MRI run the operating systems of American rental housing, from ledger to leasing to maintenance. The category has more operational data about how people actually live in and move between homes than any government statistical agency, and publishes almost none of it.
**Workforce:** Implementation consultants, revenue management analysts, compliance content teams, integration engineers, screening operations staff, customer success managers

## Key Pain Themes
Rent setting is the category's defining problem and has become its defining legal exposure. Revenue management modules that recommend prices to competing landlords from pooled non-public data are the subject of active antitrust litigation, which has left the industry with an urgent and genuinely interesting question: how do you price a unit well using only what a single operator legitimately knows? Below that, maintenance is the operational centre of gravity — work orders arrive as a resident's free-text complaint, get triaged by a property manager, and get dispatched to vendors on relationships rather than on evidence about who fixes what well. Applicant screening carries a compliance burden that grows every year as more jurisdictions restrict criminal history use, mandate source-of-income protection, and cap fees, and the rules are configured per property by people who are not lawyers. Meanwhile the two roles that actually run a property — the site manager and the leasing agent — spend their days on coordination and follow-up that nothing in the stack does for them.

## Current Tech Landscape
Yardi and RealPage dominate institutional multifamily; Entrata competes hard on the resident experience; AppFolio and Buildium own the small and mid-size operator. Revenue management is a mature module category now under legal pressure. Screening is largely outsourced to consumer reporting agencies with platform integrations. Maintenance workflow is universal and thin. Resident payment, insurance and rewards have become significant ancillary revenue lines. Listing syndication is commodity. Smart-home and access control integration is growing and fragmented.

## Analysis
These platforms observe the American rental market at a resolution nobody else has: every enquiry, every application, every lease signed and at what price, every renewal offer and whether it was accepted, every move-out and its stated reason, every work order and how long it took. Housing statistics are published quarterly from surveys; this is a census updated continuously. The legal difficulty of the last few years is not that the data exists — it is that one specific use of it, coordinating prices between competitors, was the only use anyone monetised.

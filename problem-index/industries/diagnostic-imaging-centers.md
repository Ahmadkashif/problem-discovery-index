# Diagnostic Imaging Centers

## Profile
**Category:** Healthcare & Clinical
**NAICS 2022:** 621512 — Diagnostic Imaging Centers (freestanding and mobile centres that produce MRI, CT, PET, X-ray, ultrasound and mammography images on a physician's order, usually enrolled with Medicare as independent diagnostic testing facilities; hospital radiology departments sit with general hospitals, and the radiology groups that read for the centres are counted as physician offices but appear here because their reads run the centre's business)
**Market Size:** Diagnostic imaging centers took in $22.7B in 2022, up from $18.6B in 2018 (Census Service Annual Survey). The 2022 SUSB counts 4,651 firms and 6,904 establishments with 101,804 employees and $24.0B in receipts. 3,792 firms have fewer than 20 employees, while 206 firms with 500 or more own 1,587 sites and take 44% of receipts. The largest operators are RadNet (418 centres in nine states, $2.04B revenue in 2025) and Lumexa Imaging (192 centres at June 2026, 88 of them in health-system joint ventures; 14 states per its 10-Q, search summary)
**Tech Maturity:** Medium — every centre runs digital scanners, a RIS and a PACS, and radiologists read from anywhere, yet many orders still arrive by fax, authorizations are checked by portal and phone, the protocol for each scan is picked from memory, and MRI schedules are built on fixed slot lengths that ignore what the exam will actually take.
**Workforce:** 99,727 employed in 6,968 establishments (County Business Patterns, 2023). BLS staffing for NAICS 621500, which also counts clinical labs (May 2023): radiologic technologists (16,650), sonographers (9,570), MRI technologists (7,940), nuclear medicine technologists (1,140) and radiologists employed directly (4,110), with medical secretaries (11,290), managers (8,390), receptionists (5,460) and billing clerks (4,930). Most radiologists read under contract from their own groups or teleradiology firms. Only 400 data scientists and 2,100 software developers work across labs and imaging combined

## Key Pain Themes
The centre's most consequential judgement is made before the patient lies down. A one-line order arrives ("MRI lumbar spine, back pain"), and a radiologist or senior technologist decides what it should become. That can mean with or without contrast, which sequences to run, whether the indication fits the exam at all, or whether the physician meant a different study. Experienced protocollers "just know" that this history means an MR angiogram rather than a routine brain, or that a post-surgical spine needs contrast. A newer technologist runs the order as written. The patient is then called back for a second scan, or the study goes to the radiologist unable to answer the question. That judgement is recorded only as a protocol code in the RIS. Whether the study answered the clinical question shows up later in a report addendum, a repeat order or a referrer's complaint, if at all. The question of ordering appropriateness once had a federal clock. CMS paused the Appropriate Use Criteria programme and rescinded its regulations from 1 January 2024, with no date to resume. Radiology benefit managers now decide appropriateness at authorization, against their own guidelines rather than the ACR's 279 Appropriateness Criteria topics.

The money depends on matching paperwork and filling scanner hours. Every Medicare test must be ordered in writing by the treating physician (42 CFR 410.33). Commercial and Medicare Advantage plans authorize a specific CPT code, so an MRI approved without contrast and performed with it denies. Medicare Advantage plans alone made 52.8M prior authorization decisions in 2024 (KFF). Orders arrive by fax, and an order sent to several centres goes to whichever books the patient first (Capitol Imaging's CTO, search summary). Outpatient MRI no-shows ran at 19.3% in one published hospital series before a gradient-boosting model and phone calls cut them to 15.9% (AJR). Scanners are capped by people as much as by magnets: vacancy rates for CT and MRI technologists reached 19.4% and 17.4% in 2025 (ASRT), and supply of reading radiologists is projected to only keep pace with rising demand to 2055 (Neiman Health Policy Institute, search summary).

The clocks are federal. The CY2026 physician fee schedule cut radiology about 2% overall and applied a -2.5% efficiency adjustment to non-time-based codes, while shifting practice expense toward office-based, non-facility billing (ACR summary of CMS-1832-F). The CY2027 OPPS proposal would cut payment 60% for imaging without contrast in off-campus hospital departments. That would narrow the hospital price premium that has made freestanding centres the low-cost alternative (KFF). Mammography runs under MQSA, at 9,137 certified facilities and 45.1M procedures a year (FDA, October 2026). Since 10 September 2024, every patient letter must state breast density, and suspicious results must reach patient and physician within seven days. Medicare pays for MRI, CT and PET only from accredited suppliers (ACR, IAC, The Joint Commission or RadSite). IDTF enrolment must be updated within 30 days of any ownership, location or supervising-physician change, or billing privileges are at risk.

## Current Tech Landscape
RIS and PACS come from Merative (Merge), Fujifilm Synapse, Sectra, Intelerad, Novarad and AbbaDox. DeepHealth, RadNet's software arm, now sells RadNet's own RIS, PACS, viewer, reporting and AI to other providers as one Diagnostic Suite. Sirona Medical and New Lantern sell cloud reading platforms to radiology groups. Reports are dictated in Microsoft PowerScribe, and drafted increasingly by Rad AI and by Mosaic Reporting from Radiology Partners. Detection and triage AI from Aidoc, Annalise, Lunit, Qure.ai and Gleamer (bought by DeepHealth after year-end 2025, search summary) sits on top. HOPPR sells a medical-imaging foundation model to software builders. Roughly three in four of the FDA's ~1,500 AI-enabled devices are radiology tools (search summary). Authorizations go through radiology benefit manager portals (EviCore, Carelon, Evolent), Availity, and outsourced or automated checks from Experian Health, Waystar and Infinx. Scheduling and patient calls run through RIS modules, Luma Health and newer voice agents such as AbbaDox's (search summary). CareSelect Imaging (Optum) scores orders against the ACR criteria inside health-system EHRs. Each tool covers its step. None holds the record that joins the order, the protocol chosen, the images, the report and whether the study answered the referrer's question, and that join is where the centre's judgement could be graded.

## Analysis
The industry's central unmeasured judgement is whether the exam ordered, and the protocol run, will answer the referring physician's question. It decides repeat scans, contrast use, wasted scanner hours, authorization denials and the radiologist's ability to give a useful answer. It is recorded only as a protocol code. Three things block a fix. First, outcomes land with the referrer, so a centre rarely learns that a study was the wrong one. Second, appropriateness is decided by payers' proprietary guidelines, and the federal AUC programme that would have standardised it was rescinded. Third, the scarce radiologist is also the person who protocols, so any tool must save their minutes rather than spend them. The owner's MVP idea, an order-versus-ACR-criteria checker, needs reframing on three counts. With AUC gone no one is obliged to buy it. ACR's terms require permission to reuse the criteria. And the benefit managers' own imaging guidelines (EviCore, Carelon) are copyrighted too, so checking orders against them also needs a licence (recalled). The licence-free route is federal. From 1 January 2027, CMS-0057-F requires Medicare Advantage organisations, Medicaid and CHIP programmes and managed care plans, and QHP issuers on the federally facilitated exchanges to run a Prior Authorization API. The API lists the covered items and services that need authorization (drugs excluded) and can identify each one's documentation requirements (CMS). The sellable version reads those requirements for that share of volume, checks the order at intake, and attaches the protocol a senior radiologist would choose. The commercial opening is with vendors hiring engineers to deploy into centres and reading groups. On 24 October 2026, DeepHealth had three Forward Deployment Engineers plus a Senior Forward Engineer open (remote US), Prenuvo had a Senior Forward Deployed Engineer (remote US and Canada), and Rad AI and Sirona Medical were hiring engineers. HOPPR's FDE role appeared on Built In but not on its own pages (see the agents note). Under the Pass 2 rubric the operators fail: their insight is spent on throughput and margin, never invoiced, and every image is PHI. No pocket qualifies. The nearest are the clinical-trial imaging core labs (46) and the radiology registries (43), both behind kill switches. The cleanest is the de-identified imaging data licensors (35), whose provider terms are unread and which set their own calendars (see the niche overview).

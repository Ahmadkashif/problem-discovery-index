# Wholesale Auto Auctions

## Profile
**Category:** Wholesale & Distribution
**NAICS 2022:** 423110 — Automobile and Other Motor Vehicle Merchant Wholesalers (the dealer-only auction houses within it)
**Market Size:** More than 8 million used vehicles sold through NAAA member auctions in 2025 (7.2 million in-lane, 820,000 digital-only), with gross sales value above $100B (NAAA, via Auto Remarketing). The auctions earn fees, not vehicle values: ~$6–8B a year in auction, service and floorplan revenue (estimate), led by OPENLANE ($1.93B in 2025) and ACV ($0.76B), plus Cox Automotive's Manheim and Carvana's ADESA, which are not reported separately. The whole 423110 code had 4,521 firms, 128,083 employees and $758B of receipts in 2022 (Census SUSB), but 88% of those receipts went to 122 large firms, mostly new-vehicle distributors
**Tech Maturity:** Medium — half of in-lane sales go to an online bidder and digital-only auctions are growing, but condition grading still rests on an inspector's walkaround, and arbitration and title still run on phone calls, email and mailed paper.
**Workforce:** Vehicle condition inspectors, lot drivers and attendants, auctioneers and ringmen, arbitration admins and case specialists, title clerks, transport dispatchers, commercial account and remarketing managers, territory managers, floorplan representatives, reconditioning technicians at remarketing centres, and data, ML and software engineers at the digital players

## Key Pain Themes
A wholesale auction sells cars that most buyers never see. NAAA members offered 12.3 million vehicles in 2025 and sold 58.5% of those offered in-lane, and half of the in-lane sales went to someone bidding online. The buyer bids on a condition report: paint-meter readings, structural notes, trouble codes, photographs, a short drive and the inspector's grade. When the car arrives and does not match, the buyer files arbitration, and the seller usually pays. The NAAA's Arbitration Guidelines changed on 1 June 2026. The $800 per-defect threshold stays for cars under $50,000, but above that each defect must cost at least 2% of the price to repair. Generic "No Arb for [defect]" announcements are banned. Announcing a warning light no longer protects the seller from whatever caused it. ADAS components are now arbitrable. Manheim retired its own $75K high-value policy to match. Every seller announcement written before June now has to be rewritten as a specific disclosure, or the car sells as-is.

Supply is shifting towards commercial sellers. Cox Automotive expects ~11.8 million wholesale units in 2026: off-lease volume up 23%, rental up 5%, dealer-sourced units down 3%. Monthly off-lease returns rise from ~190,000 to ~220,000 in the second half, and EVs go from ~10% to ~16% of them. Commercial vehicles were already 51.4% of in-lane sales in 2025. The auction's commercial account managers win captives, lessors, rental fleets and lenders on one number: what the car nets against the Manheim Market Report. Values are soft. The Manheim index stood at 205.9 in September 2026, down 0.6% on a year earlier, with conversion at 55.2%, and Cox cut its year-end forecast to roughly flat.

Behind the lane run back-office clocks the auction does not set. A sale without a title must be cured within the auction's title window, or the buyer may return the car, and the NAAA title guarantee lapses after 90 days (search summary). Buyers pay through floorplan lines: OPENLANE's AFC alone has ~11,600 active dealers with average lines of ~$379,000. OPENLANE's 10-K says the auction sometimes releases a car or pays the seller before the buyer's money arrives and carries that risk. Ownership is consolidating across the salvage–clean-title line. Copart bought ACV for an equity value of ~$1.9B, and the deal closed on 16 October 2026. Carvana owns ADESA's physical auctions, and Cox owns Manheim, NextGear Capital and Central Dispatch.

## Current Tech Landscape
Manheim runs physical lanes with Simulcast, digital sales, the Manheim Market Report (MMR), DealShield return guarantees, NextGear floorplan and Central Dispatch transport. OPENLANE runs a digital marketplace, more than 40 private-label remarketing sites for most OEMs and captives, AFC floorplan and autoniq buyer tools. ACV, now part of Copart, runs 20-minute digital auctions, Virtual Lift undercarriage imaging, ML damage detection, True360 reports, ACV MAX, ClearCar, ACV Transportation, ACV Capital and its Go Green seller assurance. Independent auctions run on Auction Edge's management system and EDGE Pipeline (~206 auctions, a third-party count; search summary). Dealers gain admission through AuctionACCESS credentials and look up values in MMR, Black Book and J.D. Power, with vehicle history from Carfax and AutoCheck. NHTSA's vPIC decoder and recalls API are free. Each tool covers one step of the sale. No tool joins the condition the inspector reported, what the inspector suspected but did not write, the arbitration that followed and the price the car finally settled at.

## Analysis
The industry's central unmeasured judgement is the inspector's suspicion. An experienced inspector hears a lifter tick on cold start, feels a pull on the lot drive, or sees overspray on a weatherstrip, and decides whether it becomes a line on the report, an announcement, or nothing. The outcome is recorded precisely in arbitration claims, post-sale inspections, guarantee returns and the settled price. Nobody joins it to the suspicion, because the suspicion was never written down. Three things block the fix. The inspectors are a high-turnover production workforce paid by the car. Arbitration and condition data sit inside three or four firms that treat it as a competitive asset. And the NAAA, not the auctions, writes the rules that decide which suspicions matter. Under this vault's rubric the auctions are poor buyers for an insight product. Three to four firms dominate, and their condition and price data is bundled into fees. The strongest outside pockets, [[niches/auto-dealers-independent/vehicle-valuation-guides/profile|valuation guides]] (56) and [[niches/auto-dealers-independent/vehicle-history-report-providers/profile|vehicle history]] (52), are already indexed under [[industries/auto-dealers-independent|Independent Auto Dealers]]. Both scores predate this run's ruling that a vendor's own publication calendar is Q4 2: under it they would sit at ~52 and ~48–50, and they are logged for Stage E. The Pass 2 sweep here finds no qualifier. The commercial opening is on the operator side, where the digital players are hiring engineers now (source log, checked 2026-10-28). ACV lists a Software Engineer III, Vehicle Intelligence (Buffalo, NY, posted 2026-07-22), Machine Learning Engineers on its ML inspection team (Buffalo, Toronto, from 2026-07-02), a Software Engineer IV, Arbitrations (Chennai, 2026-08-27) and an Analytics Engineer III, Assurance working on arbitration risk (2026-08-19). OPENLANE lists an MLOps Engineer (Carmel, IN, 2026-10-20), an AI First Software Engineer (Carmel, 2026-09-29) and a Staff Software Engineer (remote, 2026-10-21). The clearest two-week MVP is an arbitration-risk checker that reads a condition report and seller announcement against the 2026 guideline thresholds, the NHTSA vPIC decode and the NHTSA recalls API.

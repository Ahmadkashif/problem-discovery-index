# Legal Support Services

## Profile
**Category:** Professional Services
**NAICS 2022:** 541199 — All Other Legal Services
**Market Size:** ~$5.99B in 2022 receipts at 4,860 US employer firms (5,083 establishments, 32,736 employees; 2022 Economic Census); County Business Patterns counts 5,776 employer establishments in 2023, 82% of them with fewer than five employees; sole-proprietor process servers, freelance paralegals and independent searchers likely add ~$1–2B more (estimate). Census also codes notary public services to 541199, but the vault treats mobile notaries and loan signing agents as their own industry, [[industries/notary-signing-services|Notary & Signing Services]], and this hub does not claim that work.
**Tech Maturity:** Low to Medium — national process-serving networks log every attempt with GPS and photos, and patent searchers work in sophisticated databases, but the typical operator is a one- to four-person shop running on a phone, a car, Word templates, county clerk portals and the owner's memory of which addresses, clerks and examiners behave how.
**Workforce:** Process servers (registered, licensed or court-certified depending on the state) and the dispatchers who route their papers; skip tracers; court runners and e-filing clerks at attorney-service companies; freelance and contract paralegals working under the supervision of solo and small-firm attorneys; legal document preparers and bankruptcy petition preparers who serve the public directly under state registration rules and 11 U.S.C. §110; patent search analysts and independent registered patent agents (the USPTO lists 14,873 active agents alongside 38,433 patent attorneys); and the trial and jury consultants the code also contains.

## Key Pain Themes
This is the part of the legal system that does legal work without practising law, and most of it is done to a clock somebody else set. A lawsuit is not a lawsuit until the defendant is served: in federal court the plaintiff has 90 days from filing (FRCP 4(m)) before the court may dismiss, state courts run their own windows, and a server who cannot find the defendant must document "reasonable diligence" before a judge will allow substituted service or publication. The affidavit of service that closes each job is signed under penalty of perjury and is the only evidence the court ever sees of what happened at the door. Where that evidence is false — "sewer service" — the defendant never learns of the case: in New York City only about 17% of 366,000 consumer-credit suits filed in 2019–2023 drew a response, courts entered roughly 152,000 default judgments against consumers in 2019–2024, and the city has required GPS devices and tamper-proof electronic logs since the 2010 reforms. Volume filers (debt buyers' law firms, landlords' attorneys) pay per serve and want the lowest price; litigators chasing an evasive defendant pay for whoever can find and serve them before the window closes. The same server often works both books.

The paralegal and document-preparation side runs on a different line: unauthorised practice. Freelance paralegals may draft almost anything as long as an attorney supervises and signs, so the business turns on how little supervision a draft needs. Document preparers who serve the public directly — California's legal document assistants and unlawful detainer assistants (registered with the county, $25,000 bond), Arizona's certified legal document preparers, bankruptcy petition preparers capped at ~$125–150 a case in several districts — may fill in forms but may not advise, and the person they serve is usually one of the self-represented parties who appear in about three-quarters of state civil cases.

Patent searching has the hardest clocks of all. An accused infringer has one year from service of the complaint to petition for inter partes review (35 U.S.C. §315(b)), so the invalidity search that decides whether a petition is worth filing starts the day the process server knocks. Courts set invalidity-contention deadlines; the Paris Convention sets a twelve-month priority year; the PCT gives the searching authority three months from receipt of the search copy. The analyst's craft is knowing where the closest art hides — which classification codes an examiner actually searches, which conference proceedings or foreign-language utility models carry the disclosure — and the market has just shifted under it: PTAB institution rates fell from 68% to 23% across the first three quarters of 2025 before rebounding to 41% in the fourth (RPX), and demand is moving to ex parte reexamination and district-court invalidity.

## Current Tech Landscape
Process serving runs on ServeManager (job management, GPS proof and affidavits for server firms), the national networks of ABC Legal and Proof (which raised a $7M Series A in 2022 and reports more than 3,500 law-firm and agency users) and California filing-and-service companies such as One Legal, plus skip-trace data from the identity-resolution vendors indexed under [[industries/collections-agencies|Collections Agencies]]. Court filing goes through each state's e-filing manager and certified e-filing service providers, with clerks still rejecting envelopes for wrong case types, fees or formatting. Freelance paralegals work in the supervising attorney's Clio or MyCase seat, or in email. Patent searchers use Derwent Innovation, Questel Orbit, PatSnap, LexisNexis TotalPatent and free sources (Espacenet, Google Patents, USPTO PPUBS), with AI semantic search now standard; registered agents such as CT Corporation and CSC receive and forward service of process for corporate defendants. Each tool owns its slice. The record that would grade the work — which attempts led to valid service, which affidavits were later traversed, which clerk rejections recur, which searched references an examiner or the PTAB later relied on — is split between the server, the court, the client and the patent office, and nobody joins it.

## Analysis
The industry's central unmeasured judgement is the veteran's read of a case before the work starts: the process server who knows from the address, the neighbourhood and the first attempt when the defendant will actually be home and whether the address is dead; the patent searcher who knows which corner of the literature the closest art is in. Both are exercised thousands of times a year and never graded, because the outcome lands in someone else's record — the court's traverse hearing or default docket, the examiner's office action, the PTAB's institution decision. Three things block a fix. Structurally, the operators are tiny: 4,733 of 5,776 employer establishments have fewer than five people (County Business Patterns 2023), and the attempt logs that would teach a model sit in phones and server-software accounts. Legally, the deliverable is someone else's — an affidavit filed in the client's case, a search report that is the litigator's work product — and paralegal and document-preparer work is fenced by unauthorised-practice rules that make automation of advice a liability. Economically, volume filers buy service on price, which rewards fewer attempts rather than better ones. The operators are therefore mostly poor buyers. The commercial openings sit with the national service networks, which already hold millions of GPS-stamped attempts (estimate), and with the larger patent search houses, which have the industry's only research functions of real size. Neither clears the insight-layer bar: the networks employ servers and dispatchers rather than analysts, and the search houses' archive of relevance calls, though partly testable against public PTAB decisions, is client work product they may not reuse, so they log behind a kill switch and the industry has no qualifying pocket. Transcripts and depositions belong to the queued court-reporting-firms industry; e-discovery, legal process outsourcing and court-data analytics are already analysed under [[industries/small-law-firms|Small Law Firms]] and [[industries/personal-injury-law|Personal Injury Law]].

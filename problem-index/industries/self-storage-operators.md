# Self-Storage Operators

## Profile
**Category:** Real Estate, Rental & Leasing
**NAICS 2022:** 531130 — Lessors of Miniwarehouses and Self-Storage Units (owner-operators and third-party managers of self-storage, including portable storage and vehicle, RV and boat parking run as self-storage)
**Market Size:** $20.8B of receipts across 8,813 employer firms and 18,286 establishments in 2022 (Census SUSB); the 22 firms with 500+ employees took ~52% and the 8,619 under 20 employees ~33%. TractIQ counts 68,243 facilities with 2.48B net rentable sq ft (2026-09-30). Public Storage closed its purchase of National Storage Affiliates on 2026-07-22 and holds interests in 4,347 facilities. Five storage REITs remain listed: Public Storage, Extra Space, CubeSmart, SmartStop (NYSE: SMA) and Global Self Storage (Nasdaq: SELF)
**Tech Maturity:** Medium — the REITs set every store's web rates daily in their own systems and open gates from a phone app, but most of the industry's thousands of small owners run one or two facilities on Sitelink or Storable Edge, with rates set by checking competitors' websites and increase letters chosen by the owner.
**Workforce:** Store managers (often one per facility, working alone), relief and district managers, revenue managers and pricing analysts at the REITs and third-party managers, call-centre agents, collections and lien clerks, maintenance technicians and owner-reporting accountants. Public Storage's ~5,770 employees include 4,920 in customer-facing property and call-centre roles

## Key Pain Themes
Self-storage is priced in two halves. New customers see a web rate and a promotion, often a first month for $1, chosen daily against competitors a few miles away. Existing customers get the real price later: Public Storage raises long-term tenants' rents "every six to twelve months", sizing each increase by "evaluating the additional revenue from the increase against the negative impact of incremental move-outs", and Extra Space says existing tenants receive increases "at least annually". Operators quoted by Slate in January 2025 described first increases of 20% to 100%. Whether a tenant pays or leaves is recorded to the day; the judgement behind each increase's size is not recorded anywhere. That judgement is now regulated. California requires 30 days' notice to amend a rental agreement, and Los Angeles County capped increases at 10% under Penal Code 396 until 2026-01-07. New York City sued Extra Space over increases after move-in and settled on 2026-07-09 for $1.7M; every storage facility there has needed a licence since 2026-08-25.

When a tenant stops paying, the operator becomes a lien creditor on a fifty-state clock. In California the owner may send a preliminary notice once rent is 14 days unpaid. The lien attaches 14 days after that notice, and the sale needs a further notice at least 14 days ahead. Excess proceeds are held for a year. Since 2026-01-01 an email notice there counts only with evidence the renter opened or acknowledged it (AB 498). Each state sets its own mailing, advertising and online-sale rules, and a sale that skips a step becomes a wrongful-sale claim. A California Senate analysis cites an estimate of ~155,000 units auctioned a year, each listed by a store manager on StorageTreasures, Lockerfox or Bid13.

The third pressure is staffing. A 500-unit facility is often run by one manager, and many stores now have nobody on site: kiosks, smart locks and phone apps let tenants rent and enter alone, and remote agents answer the gate intercom. Someone still has to walk every door to check locks against the ledger, because a locked vacant unit or an open overlocked one is lost rent or a theft claim.

## Current Tech Landscape
Storable dominates: Sitelink, Storable Edge and Storable Easy run "more than 33,000 facilities", and it also owns the SpareFoot marketplace and the StorSmart and Bader tenant-insurance programmes (search summary), and sells collections, auctions, access control and a call-centre add-on. OpenUnit (YC W20) gives management software away free, and Stora sells a self-storage operating platform from Belfast. Revenue and ECRI tools come from Prorize, Monument, Cubby and Storeganise, and the REITs build their own. Market data comes from TractIQ, Yardi Matrix, Radius+ and StorTrack. Janus International's Nokē Smart Entry and OpenTech Alliance's INSOMNIAC kiosks run unstaffed doors and gates, and OpenTech also sells the StorageTreasures auction site. Each tool records one piece of a tenancy. None joins the increase that was sent, why that size was chosen, the tenant's response and the owner's net rent a year later.

## Analysis
The industry's central unmeasured judgement is how much each existing tenant will bear. Move-outs are recorded perfectly, but the reasoning behind each increase is not. Neither is the counterfactual: what a smaller increase would have kept. Three things block the fix. Regulation now reaches the increase itself, so an optimiser must show it stayed within notice rules and caps. The best data sits with the largest REITs, and at the 1,856 stores Extra Space manages for owners the rent roll belongs to the owner. The 8,619 small firms cannot fund a pricing team. Operators are therefore weak direct buyers. The Pass 2 sweep finds no qualifier: the nearest clean pocket is the independent revenue-management and ECRI software vendors (32), and storage market data is already profiled under moving companies at [[niches/moving-companies/storage-facility-market-data/profile|Self-Storage Market Data]] (42; ~40 under the current clock ruling, pending Stage E). The commercial opening is on the platform side. On 2026-10-29 Storable was hiring in Seattle: a Senior Software Engineer, AI Agents and a Senior Software Engineer, Analytics Platform (both first posted 2026-09-23) and a Principal Software Development Engineer (2026-10-20). OpenUnit lists remote engineering roles, including a Ruby on Rails Engineer open to remote US candidates (no posting dates shown), and Stora was hiring engineers in Belfast (2026-08-28 and 2026-09-24). Neighbor's only engineering posting is an evergreen listing from 2020. The clearest two-week MVP is an ECRI move-out-risk and increase-size optimiser on a synthetic tenant ledger and publicly posted street rates, with each state's notice rule applied before any increase is recommended.

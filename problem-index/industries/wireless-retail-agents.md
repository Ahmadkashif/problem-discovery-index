# Wireless Retail Agents

## Profile
**Category:** Media, Telecom & Information
**NAICS 2022:** 517122 — Agents for wireless telecommunications services (authorised carrier dealers and prepaid agents paid on commission)
**Market Size:** ~$20.4B in 2022 receipts across 4,191 firms, 13,804 establishments and 83,434 employees (2022 Economic Census); most receipts are carrier commissions, bonuses and residuals rather than merchandise margin. The largest operator, Victra, reports 1,800+ Verizon stores and ~$3B in annual revenue (company figures).
**Tech Maturity:** Medium — the large exclusive chains run iQmetrix RQ or similar point-of-sale systems and carrier activation portals, but the money flow that decides profit (carrier commission statements, chargeback files, monthly comp plans and spiff bulletins) is reconciled in spreadsheets, and prepaid owner-operators often work it out from PDFs and portal screens.
**Workforce:** Wireless sales consultants, assistant and store managers, district and regional managers, back-office commission and reconciliation analysts, inventory and loss-prevention staff, trainers, and owner-operators of one to fifty prepaid doors. The 2022 census averages about six employees and ~$1.5M in receipts per establishment.

## Key Pain Themes
An authorised dealer does not set its own margin. The carrier does, through a compensation plan that pays per activation, upgrade and attached feature, changes every month or quarter, and can be terminated on short notice. GameStop's 10-K made the dependence explicit: Spring Mobile, then 1,329 AT&T stores, booked commissions "net of an allowance for chargebacks from AT&T for estimated customer cancellations", and wrote its AT&T dealer agreements down by $328.8M after AT&T changed the compensation structure in 2017. Every commission is provisional until the line survives a chargeback window, commonly 90 to 180 days (estimate), that reverses the payment if the customer cancels, never pays, never recharges or turns out to be fraud. The store manager who can look at a customer and a deal and sense that this one will come back is making the most valuable judgement in the business, and nobody records it.

The second pain is the month-end. Carrier statements arrive as files and PDFs in each carrier's format, and someone has to match every line to the activation that earned it, find what was short-paid, dispute it before the carrier's window closes, and pass every chargeback down to the rep whose advance it reverses. Store and district managers run against carrier scorecards (activation mix, protection and accessory attach, early deactivations, survey scores and audit results) that set bonus tiers and decide whether the carrier approves the next door. Prepaid dealers face the same arithmetic across several brand portals, with spiffs that can vanish on a week's notice and per-transaction 911 fees to collect and remit on each state's schedule.

The third pain is the door count itself. In 2026 T-Mobile began closing dealer and corporate stores, recording a $108M charge in the second quarter, while Verizon moved the other way, selling 274 company stores to about six authorised retailers effective August 16, 2026. Wave7 Research counts just under 17,000 postpaid carrier outlets and more than 16,000 prepaid brand stores. Whether a door is worth keeping is a forecast of carrier compensation, chargebacks and lease cost that dealers make with little more than last year's P&L.

## Current Tech Landscape
iQmetrix RQ is the dominant wireless retail management system (the company says it powered over 20,000 US and Canadian wireless stores by 2019), with ReconcilePro matching POS sales to carrier commission files. Carrier portals handle credit checks, activations and ports. Commission tools range from telecom-specific services (Modero, Commission Tracker, outsourced reconciliation shops) to general incentive-compensation software (Xactly, Spiff, QCommission). Prepaid dealers use multi-carrier activation and bill-pay platforms such as T-CETRA, and payroll, scheduling and loss-prevention come from general retail vendors. Each tool handles its own step. Nothing keeps one record per activation that runs from the customer at the counter to the commission paid, the chargeback and its reason, and the rep's net pay, so nobody can learn which deals come back.

## Analysis
The industry's central unmeasured judgement is whether an activation will stick. Dealers are paid on gross activations and clawed back on the ones that fail, and the deal-level read that separates a durable customer from a promotion-hopper, a non-payer or a fraud ring lives with veteran managers. Three things block fixing it. Customer data is the carrier's CPNI, held under dealer agreements that limit what the dealer may do with it. The outcome arrives months later, in a carrier file nobody joins back to the counter. And any rule that refuses or steps up a customer risks discrimination claims and the carrier's displeasure, so it has to work on the deal, not the person. Beyond that read, the commercial opening is in the back office: reconciliation, chargeback netting, comp-plan ingestion and scorecard projection, sold to a dozen or so exclusive chains per carrier and to thousands of prepaid owner-operators through the POS, master-agent and activation platforms they already use. As a buyer of a research-insight engine the dealer fails the Pass 2 gate. Its analysts serve its own stores, and the firms that study wireless retail are either too small, like Wave7, or carry a live kill switch, like the mystery-shop firms whose findings belong to the carrier that commissioned them.

# Land Developers

## Profile
**Category:** Construction & Trades
**NAICS 2022:** 237210 — Land Subdivision
**Market Size:** ~$16.8B of receipts in 2022 across 4,505 firms (Census SUSB 2022). Receipts are mostly lot sales, so they carry the land's value as well as the work. 29 enterprises with 500+ employees took ~33% of receipts, and the ~95% of firms with fewer than 20 employees took ~34%. Forestar, the largest pure lot developer, sold 14,240 lots in fiscal 2025 at an average of $108,400 a lot.
**Tech Maturity:** Low — land teams screen tracts in GIS viewers and spreadsheets, entitlement runs on consultants, attorneys and memory, and the horizontal work is subcontracted and tracked on pay applications.
**Workforce:** Land acquisition managers, entitlement and planning managers, horizontal development managers and field superintendents, and finance staff who run takedown billing and district reimbursements. The design and field work is bought in: civil engineers, surveyors, land-use attorneys, environmental consultants, and grading, utility and paving subcontractors. The code employed 22,909 people in 2022 (SUSB); QCEW counted 36,695 in 2024 at an average pay of $114,955.

## Key Pain Themes
A land developer's business is one judgement repeated at every tract: what can this land become, how long will it take to get there, and what will it cost per lot? The land acquisition manager walks a raw tract, reads the zoning, the floodplain, the creeks, the slope and the neighbours, and within a 30–120 day feasibility period (estimate) has to tell a land committee how many lots it yields, whether the rezoning will pass and when, and what grading, utilities and paving will cost per lot. That read sets the price paid for the land and the lot price quoted to builders. It is made mostly from experience. It is rarely written down in a form that can be checked against what happened, and the land department's best predictor of a city council is a person.

After the purchase contract come three external clocks. The entitlement clock runs through staff review, resubmittals, planning commission and council hearings that get continued, wetlands permits (the 2026 Nationwide Permits took effect on 15 March 2026 and run to 2031, with a 45-day default on complete pre-construction notices), and plat approval. The builder clock is the takedown schedule. Most production builders now buy finished lots on option rather than holding land: Lennar spun off $5.5B of land and $1.0B of cash into Millrose Properties, a homesite option platform, in February 2025, and D.R. Horton buys most of Forestar's output under a supply agreement. A developer who delivers lots late misses the takedown, and a builder whose sales slow can walk away from a deposit. The finance clock is reimbursement. In Texas and Florida, municipal utility districts and community development districts repay the developer's infrastructure from bonds sold years after the money was spent. Florida had 1,067 CDDs in August 2025, and its development-style districts had grown by more than half since 2020.

The work itself is coordination. Entitlement managers spend nights at council meetings and weeks answering staff comments. Development superintendents chase grading, utility and paving subs, municipal inspectors and the electric, gas and fibre companies whose "dry utility" installs set the real delivery date. NAHB's 2026 study put the cost of regulation imposed during lot development, embedded in the finished lot price, at $46,795 a home.

## Current Tech Landscape
Parcel and land data come from Regrid, LightBox's LandVision, Acres and Land id. New-home and lot supply data come from Zonda, now part of CoStar Group, and John Burns Research and Consulting. Site feasibility and conceptual layout run in TestFit and TraceAir's layout generator, with design in Autodesk Civil 3D at the civil engineer. TraceAir and Propeller turn drone scans into earthwork quantities and grade checks. Development budgets live in spreadsheets, Northspyre or construction accounting. Public-meeting monitoring is a new category (Hamlet), and AI permitting vendors such as PermitFlow serve the builder's vertical permits once lots exist. Each tool is competent within its boundary. The record no tool owns is the developer's own prediction for each tract (lots, approval date, cost per lot, takedown pace) joined to what actually happened.

## Analysis
The industry's central unmeasured judgement is the price of entitlement risk: how likely a tract is to get the zoning, utility capacity and permits it needs, how long that takes, and how many lots survive at what cost. Every input is partly public. Planning commission and council agendas, staff reports and votes sit in Legistar and similar systems (Phoenix's and Austin's open Legistar APIs return current rezoning cases without a key, and San Antonio's, whose records stop in 2021, shows "continued from" status in case titles), and floodplain, wetlands inventory, lidar and soils layers are free. The outcome is known within months to a few years. What is missing is the joined record, and the obstacles are structural. Three firms in four have fewer than five employees, the land manager's reasoning lives in his head and his deal spreadsheets, and the consultants who hold the detail (civil engineers, land-use attorneys) work per project for clients who own the files. The operators are poor buyers for an insight product. Their analysis is a cost inside a lot margin, and the largest of them sell most of their lots to one affiliated builder. The commercial opening sits with the buyers of entitlement risk: national builders' land teams, land-banking platforms and lot bankers, and the data vendors moving into land (Zonda has announced "Zonda Land AI"). For an engineer, the clearest two-week MVP is an approval-odds and timeline predictor built on public hearing records. No land-entitlement vendor checked was hiring US engineers on 2026-10-24. Zonda's land-data engineering roles were in Glasgow and Toronto, and its one US engineering role was an Austin technical lead for builder visualisation. PermitFlow, Paces, Symbium and Polimorphic were hiring engineers for adjacent work: builders' building permits, energy and data-center siting, residential permit compliance and municipal front offices. Symbium's posting is undated. None had a forward deployed title.

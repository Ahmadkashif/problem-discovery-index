# Direct Mail Agencies

## Profile
**Category:** Professional Services
**NAICS 2022:** 541860, 541870 — Direct Mail Advertising; Advertising Material Distribution Services
**Market Size:** ~$16.7B in 2022 employer-firm revenue across both codes ($11.7B for direct mail advertising and $5.0B for advertising material distribution, Census Service Annual Survey); US marketers spent ~$35.8B on direct mail in 2025 including postage and production (Winterberry Group), and USPS Marketing Mail carried 56.8B pieces for $15.7B in FY2025
**Tech Maturity:** Medium — postal data processing is rigorously digital because USPS prices depend on it (CASS, NCOALink, Intelligent Mail barcodes, Mail.dat), while the decisions that drive results — which lists, how deep, which offer, which routes — still run on a strategist's spreadsheet and memory, and door-to-door distribution runs on crew leaders and paper maps.
**Workforce:** Campaign strategists and account managers, list brokers and database marketing analysts, data processing specialists (merge/purge, address hygiene, presort), copywriters and designers, production and postal logistics coordinators, lettershop and inkjet operators, and for door-to-door work, crew leaders and part-time walkers and sampling crews. BLS counted ~32,200 employees in 2,587 direct mail establishments and ~17,200 in 4,310 distribution establishments in 2024.

The two codes are one business seen from two delivery channels. A 541860 firm designs a campaign, selects the audience from lists or carrier routes, prepares the pieces and pays postage for USPS to deliver them. A 541870 firm sells the same neighbourhood-targeted campaign — door hangers, flyers, product samples, polybagged packs — but delivers it by hand, because federal law (18 U.S.C. § 1725) forbids putting unstamped material in a mailbox. Geography selection, drop timing and response tracking are the same work; what differs is whether the last mile is a postage statement or a walker's GPS trace, and many agencies sell both to the same local clients.

## Key Pain Themes
The decision that makes or loses money is the circulation plan: which rented, compiled, co-op modelled and house lists to mail, how deep into each, with which offer and version, and when to stop. Experienced strategists and list brokers make it by feel — "this list is tired", "the co-op model will beat the rental at this depth", "drop a week earlier for this segment" — and read the result two or three months later from a matchback report that credits mail for every buyer on the file, including those who would have bought anyway. Holdout tests are rare because clients resent paying for names not mailed, so the agency's archive of campaigns is large and its knowledge of what actually caused response is thin.

Around that decision runs a production line with hard clocks set by others. Lists are due at the data processor on a fixed date before merge/purge; every address must be Move-Update compliant within 95 days of mailing; the file must be presorted, barcoded and documented in Mail.dat for Full-Service and Seamless Acceptance, where errors over threshold become automatic postage assessments; and the drop must hit an in-home window set by the client's sale, the nonprofit's year-end, or for political mail, the date ballots arrive. Postage is the largest cost line and moves on USPS's schedule: the Postal Regulatory Commission approved another increase effective 12 July 2026. Prescreened credit offers carry FCRA opt-out notices, political pieces carry FEC or state disclaimers, and nonprofit mail must satisfy the USPS cooperative mail rule to keep nonprofit prices.

Door-to-door distribution has its own failure mode: the client pays per door, the walker is paid per piece, and the commonest fraud — dumped bundles — is invisible until response collapses. Proof of distribution, GPS traces and photo verification are the product, and coverage gaps around apartment complexes, gated communities and no-solicitation ordinances eat into every route.

## Current Tech Landscape
Mail preparation runs on presort and address software (BCC Software's Mail Manager and Bulk Mailer, AccuZIP, Window Book) feeding USPS PostalOne!, with tracking through USPS Informed Visibility and resellers such as GrayHair Software. Audience data comes from compilers (Data Axle, Experian), cooperative databases (Epsilon Abacus, Wiland, Path2Response, Alliant) and list brokers; API print-and-mail platforms (Lob, PostGrid, Postalytics) serve triggered and small-batch mail. Large integrated mailers — RRD (which absorbed Vericast's shared mail in 2024), Quad, IWCO Direct and Taylor — combine data, print and postal logistics. Door-to-door crews use GPS proof-of-delivery apps. Each tool is competent at its step. The joined campaign record — list and segment source, offer, version, in-home date, response, downstream value and holdout result, across years of campaigns — belongs to none of them and lives in the strategist's spreadsheets.

## Analysis
Direct mail is a measurement business that rarely measures the thing it sells. Every piece carries a unique barcode, every response can be matched to a name and address, and every campaign is a dated, sized bet on a list, an offer and a drop date — yet the industry's central judgement, how deep to mail which audience, is graded by matchback reports that cannot separate mail's effect from buyers who would have arrived anyway. The obstacles are commercial (clients will not pay for holdout names), structural (the house file belongs to the client, the co-op data to the co-op, the delivery scans to USPS, so no one holds the joined record) and human (the strategist's feel for a list is never written down and leaves with them). The strongest commercial opening sits with the agencies and integrated mailers that run hundreds of campaigns a year and could keep a graded circulation ledger, and with door-to-door distributors whose entire value is verifiable proof of coverage. Operators themselves are weak buyers for a research engine — small, thin-margin and working on client-owned data — and the insight layer around them is a short list of data cooperatives, competitive mail panels and targeting vendors, none of which clears the bar for this vault's insight index.

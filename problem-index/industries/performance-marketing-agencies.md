# Performance Marketing Agencies

## Profile
**Category:** Adtech & Martech
**Market Size:** ~$25B US in fees for paid media management, conversion optimisation and performance measurement; independents like Tinuiti, Wpromote, Brainlabs, Power Digital and Jellyfish sit between the holding companies and a long tail of boutiques
**Tech Maturity:** Skilled operators on other people's automation. The agencies are sophisticated users of platform tooling and largely build only reporting on top of it. The buying craft that justified their fee has been absorbed into Performance Max, Advantage+ and their equivalents, and the remaining differentiators — measurement, creative and allocation — are the parts they are least structured to charge for.
**Workforce:** Media buyers and channel specialists, performance and account directors, analytics and measurement leads, creative strategists and production teams, new business and pitch teams

## Key Pain Themes
An agency's core deliverable is allocation: given a budget, decide how much goes to each platform and each campaign. That decision is made against numbers each platform reports about itself, using its own attribution window, its own identity graph and its own view of what it caused. Summed across platforms, reported conversions routinely exceed the number of orders the client actually took — a discrepancy every practitioner knows about and every weekly report reproduces.

The second theme is that automation ate the job description. Performance Max and Advantage+ removed most of the manual levers that buying expertise used to operate, and did so while reducing visibility into where budget goes. The agency retains full accountability for results and has lost the controls, which is a difficult position to be paid a percentage of spend for.

The third is the fee model itself. Most agencies charge a share of media spend, which pays them more for recommending more spend and pays them nothing for the finding that a channel is not incremental. Clients have noticed. Retention cycles have shortened, pitches are frequent and expensive, and the work that would most help a client is the work the contract is least designed to reward.

## Current Tech Landscape
The buying platforms — Google Ads, Meta, Amazon, TikTok, The Trade Desk — are the substrate and increasingly the automation. Reporting runs on Supermetrics, Funnel, Fivetran and Looker or on the agency's own warehouse. Measurement has fractured into three camps: platform-reported attribution, server-side conversion APIs and identity workarounds, and a revival of marketing mix modelling with open-source implementations from Meta and Google. Geo-experiment tooling exists and is underused. Creative production tooling from Smartly, Celtra and a wave of generative vendors has cut asset cost without changing how creative decisions are made.

## Analysis
Performance agencies sit on the one vantage point nobody else has: the same measurement question asked across hundreds of advertisers, dozens of verticals and every major platform, with the client's actual revenue visible on the other side of the engagement. That is the dataset from which cross-platform incrementality could actually be learned — priors on how much each platform overstates, by vertical and purchase cycle, calibrated against geo experiments run across a whole client base. Instead the corpus is discarded at the end of each engagement and the next client's plan starts from the platform's own numbers again. The agency that turns its portfolio into a measurement prior stops selling hours against a spend percentage and starts selling the only thing in this market that cannot be copied from a platform's documentation.

# Life Insurance Carriers

## Profile
**Category:** Insurance
**NAICS 2022:** 524113 — Direct Life Insurance Carriers (life insurance, annuities, disability income and accidental death and dismemberment written on the carrier's own paper)
**Market Size:** ~$1.29T in aggregate 2024 income, of which $824.7B was premiums and annuity considerations ($173.7B life, $432.5B annuity, $218.5B health and disability) and $361.9B investment income, on $9.3T of assets across 711 US life insurers (ACLI Life Insurers Fact Book 2025, from NAIC data); US retail annuity sales reached a record $464.1B in 2025 (LIMRA)
**Tech Maturity:** Medium — the investment and hedging side runs on sophisticated models, while underwriting still reads faxed physician records, policies issued in the 1980s sit on mainframe admin systems, and quarter-close reserves move through spreadsheets between projection runs.
**Workforce:** ~309,300 life insurance home-office staff (BLS, via ACLI): underwriters and case managers, pricing, valuation and experience-study actuaries, investment and ALM teams, new-business and policy-service processors, death and disability claims examiners, annuity suitability reviewers, compliance and product filing staff, and medical directors

## Key Pain Themes
A life carrier sells a promise that will be tested decades after it is priced. The mortality, lapse and interest assumptions set at issue are checked only as the block ages, and by then the underwriter, the pricing actuary and often the administration system are gone. The judgement that matters most is spread thin and hard to keep. Senior underwriters read a ~300-page attending physician statement and assign a risk class in about an hour (estimate). Valuation actuaries know which assumption moved the reserve before the attribution run finishes. Claims examiners can tell from the first call whether a disability claimant will return to work. Accelerated underwriting programmes now issue most simple cases without fluids or exams, so the hard cases that reach a human underwriter are harder, and the mortality cost of a misclassified accelerated case shows up only years later in actual-to-expected studies. The SOA's 2024 monitoring of 33,000+ lives across 30 programmes found about one audited case in five placed in a different risk class.

The external clocks are statutory and fixed. Annual statements and actuarial opinions follow the NAIC calendar, and quarterly statements are due ~45 days after quarter end. Principle-based reserving (VM-20) has been an accreditation standard since 2020. VM-22 extended it to non-variable annuities from January 2026, mandatory for new issues by January 2029, and Conning's GOES replaced the Academy interest rate generator at the same date. GAAP long-duration targeted improvements (LDTI) require cash-flow assumptions to be reviewed at least annually, and every assumption change flows through the income statement. On the sales side, 49 jurisdictions have adopted the NAIC best-interest revisions to the annuity suitability model, so every annuity sale, and especially every replacement, needs a documented review before issue. New York's Circular Letter No. 7 and Colorado's life insurer governance regulation require carriers to test the external data and models used in underwriting for unfair discrimination, and to stop relying on the vendor's word.

The in-force book is the third theme. Policies written forty years ago on systems nobody can still maintain are being converted, reinsured or sold to in-force block acquirers such as Fortitude Re and Talcott. Lincoln's 2023 and 2026 transactions alone moved billions of statutory reserves. Every conversion turns on reconciling decades of policy values to the penny, and every block sale turns on an experience study the buyer does not trust.

## Current Tech Landscape
New business runs on underwriting workbenches and rules engines (Swiss Re Magnum, RGA AURA, Munich Re's ALLFINANZ, Hannover Re's hr | ReFlex) fed by MIB checks, Milliman IntelliScript and ExamOne prescription and medical-claims histories, LexisNexis and TransUnion mortality scores, labs, and APS documents retrieved by fax and portal. Actuarial projection runs on Moody's AXIS, FIS Prophet, WTW RiskAgility and Milliman's Integrate and MG-ALFA, with experience studies in SAS, Python and Excel. Administration sits on legacy platforms (CyberLife, LifePRO, Vantage, home-grown COBOL) or newer ones (Equisoft, Sapiens, FAST, EIS), often run by TPAs such as Infosys McCamish, DXC, Andesa or SE2. Illustrations come from vendor engines, and annuity suitability review runs in carrier queues fed by e-application platforms (iPipeline, Hexure, DTCC). Each tool works within its boundary, but none owns the record that joins an underwriting decision, its stated reasoning and the policy's eventual mortality, or the one that joins an assumption change to the experience that justified it.

## Analysis
The industry's central unmeasured judgement is the risk class: the decision that a 52-year-old with controlled hypertension and a treated thyroid nodule is preferred, standard or table 2. That class sets the premium for decades, and its accuracy is known only through mortality experience that takes a decade or more to become credible. Carriers measure mortality at block level with great care through actual-to-expected studies and VM-51 experience reporting. They almost never join that outcome back to the underwriter, the evidence or the reasoning that produced the class, so they cannot say which underwriting judgements carry information. Three things block a fix. The label is slow and confounded by selection. Medical evidence arrives as unstructured faxes under HIPAA authorisations and FCRA rules. And the cross-carrier mortality data that would make the question answerable sits with reinsurers, MIB and consultants under cedent and client confidentiality. The carriers are good buyers of underwriting and actuarial tooling, because new-business economics, reserve calendars and regulators' data-testing rules all put a date on the spend. They are poor buyers of a research-insight engine under the Pass 2 rubric, because their insight is priced into premiums and reserves rather than invoiced, and most of the firms that do invoice for life-insurance insight work on someone else's data. No pocket qualifies in this industry's sweep (see the niche overview).

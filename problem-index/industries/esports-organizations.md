# Esports Organizations

## Profile
**Category:** Gaming & Interactive
**Market Size:** ~$1.5B US in esports organisation revenue — sponsorship, content, merchandise and league distributions — after a sharp contraction from the valuations of the early 2020s
**Tech Maturity:** Sophisticated in-game analytics, primitive business instrumentation. Competitive teams run genuine performance analysis with coaching staff and analysts; the commercial side, which is where essentially all revenue comes from, is run on screenshots of viewership charts and sponsorship decks assembled by hand.
**Workforce:** Professional players and coaching staff, team managers and operations, content creators and video teams, sponsorship sales and partnership managers, social and community staff

## Key Pain Themes
Prize money is a rounding error. Organisation revenue is sponsorship, content, merchandise and, where they exist, league distributions — which means the product being sold is audience attention, and the organisations cannot measure it. Attention is spread across a tournament broadcast, a dozen player streams, short-form clips on several platforms, and social accounts, with each surface reporting incompatible metrics and none of them attributable to a sponsor's outcome. Sponsors have grown considerably more demanding since the contraction and the sector has no answer.

The second theme is that the largest controllable cost — the roster — is allocated on evaluation that does not transfer. In-game statistics are heavily dependent on patch, meta, role, teammates and league strength, so a player's numbers on one team in one season are a poor guide to their contribution on another. Multi-year contracts are signed on that basis and buyouts are paid to correct them.

The third is that the business has been restructuring under financial pressure for several years. Organisations have closed, exited titles, cut rosters and pivoted to content, and the people affected — players with short careers and staff on thin contracts — have absorbed most of that adjustment.

## Current Tech Landscape
Publisher-run leagues and third-party tournament organisers structure competition. Performance analysis uses publisher APIs, demo parsing and bespoke tools, and varies enormously in sophistication by title. Streaming runs on Twitch, YouTube and Kick with each platform's own analytics. Sponsorship measurement uses logo-detection vendors for broadcast exposure, which measures visibility rather than effect. Content operations run on standard social tooling. Player scouting is substantially relationship-driven, supplemented by public statistics sites of variable quality.

## Analysis
An esports organisation is a media business that believes it is a sports business, and the confusion is expensive. Its costs are structured like a team — rosters, coaching, bootcamps, travel — and its revenue is structured like a creator network, earned from attention across surfaces it does not own and cannot measure. The sector spent its growth years selling a story about audience scale and its contraction years being asked for evidence it never built the instrumentation to produce. The measurement problem is genuinely hard, because attention is fragmented across platforms with incompatible reporting and no outcome data returns from the sponsor. It is also the entire business, and the organisations that survive the next few years will be the ones that can demonstrate what they deliver.

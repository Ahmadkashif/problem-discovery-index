# Credit Bureaus

## Profile
**Category:** Business Support & Waste Services
**NAICS 2022:** 561450 — Credit Bureaus
**Market Size:** ~$16.5B in receipts across 268 firms in 2022, of which the 17 firms with 500+ employees took ~$14.9B (~91%) and 134 firms with under five employees took ~$49M (Census SUSB 2022); Equifax alone reported $6.07B of 2025 revenue, $2.58B of it from employment and income verification, and TransUnion reported $3.58B from its US markets. Many tenant screeners and some verification and commercial credit firms are coded outside 561450, so the code understates the activity.
**Tech Maturity:** High at the data layer, manual at the edges — the nationwide bureaus run some of the largest consumer databases in the economy and answer lender inquiries in milliseconds, while furnisher onboarding, monthly file review, dispute investigation and the small specialty bureaus still run on format edits, reject reports, form letters and a few people who know each data source's quirks.
**Workforce:** Furnisher-relations and data-acquisition staff, data-reporting and data-quality analysts, dispute agents (many in outsourced or offshore centres), statisticians and data scientists building scores and attributes, product and sales teams selling to lenders, landlords, employers and agencies, and compliance and legal staff working under the Fair Credit Reporting Act; BLS counted ~22,100 employees in 1,292 private establishments in 2024, at an average pay of ~$185,000.

## Key Pain Themes
A credit file is assembled, not observed. Every month thousands of furnishers — card issuers, mortgage servicers, auto lenders, student loan servicers, collection agencies, credit unions, landlords — send the bureaus a Metro 2 file describing each account's balance, status and payment history. The bureau's load edits reject what is malformed. What is well-formed but wrong — a servicer whose system conversion reset every account's date of first delinquency, a collection agency reporting an account the original creditor also reports, a status code shifted by one column — passes the edits and lands in millions of consumer files at once. The people who catch these loads are data-reporting analysts who know what each furnisher's file normally looks like, and their judgement is the industry's real accuracy control. The FTC's 2012 study found one in five consumers had an error on at least one of their three reports.

The errors come back as disputes, and the dispute system is now a flood. Under FCRA §611 a bureau has 30 days to reinvestigate (45 if the consumer adds information), must notify the furnisher within five business days, and does so through e-OSCAR as an ACDV the furnisher must answer. In 2025 the CFPB received 6.6 million complaints, ~5.8 million (88%) of them about credit or consumer reporting, and it blamed part of the 182% rise on credit repair organisations and on language models and AI agents submitting duplicative and spurious complaints. Each dispute still needs an investigation a court would call reasonable, so the bureau and the furnisher both face more volume, more templated text and the same clock.

The rules around the file are moving at the same time. FHFA and HUD opened a limited rollout of VantageScore 4.0 for agency mortgages on 22 April 2026, with FICO 10T to follow and the tri-merge report kept; FICO now licenses mortgage scores directly to resellers at $4.95 per score plus a $33 funded-loan fee, or $10 per score. The Homebuyers Privacy Protection Act banned most mortgage trigger leads from 5 March 2026. The CFPB's medical debt rule was vacated in July 2025, leaving a patchwork of state laws. And from 1 January 2027 states must verify Medicaid work hours, a new market for employment data that was built for lenders.

## Current Tech Landscape
Furnishers report in the Metro 2 format maintained through the CDIA's Credit Reporting Resource Guide, and disputes move through e-OSCAR, which the nationwide bureaus built and own. Scores come from FICO and VantageScore and the bureaus' own attribute libraries; employment and income verification from Equifax's The Work Number (~209 million active records at end-2025), Experian Verify and consumer-permissioned payroll connectors such as Truv and Argyle; tenant screening from TransUnion SmartMove, RealPage, Yardi and SafeRent; mortgage merged reports from resellers such as Xactus and Factual Data; commercial files from Dun & Bradstreet, Experian Business, Equifax Commercial and Creditsafe. Each system is competent inside its own boundary. The record that links a furnisher's monthly file to the dispute it causes months later, and to what was actually wrong, is owned by none of them.

## Analysis
The industry's central unmeasured judgement is whether a record is right. Bureaus measure accuracy almost entirely through disputes, a sample biased toward consumers who look at their reports and toward errors that hurt them, and the FCRA's structure lets a bureau close most disputes on the furnisher's confirmation rather than its own evidence. The obstacles are structural (the consumer is the subject of the file, not the customer), legal (furnished data arrives under furnishing and membership agreements and leaves only for a permissible purpose) and organisational (the analysts who know each furnisher's normal are few and are not asked to write it down). The strongest commercial opening is load-time data quality and dispute triage sold both to the bureaus and to the thousands of furnishers who answer ACDVs. As a buyer for a research-insight engine the industry is already mapped: its qualifying pockets sit in the vault under other industries — [[niches/independent-retailers/commercial-credit-bureaus/profile|commercial credit bureaus]] (56), [[niches/property-management/tenant-screening-data/profile|tenant screening]] (54) and [[niches/collections-agencies/identity-skip-trace-data-providers/profile|identity graphs]] (54) — while the consumer credit file itself carries a live "data isn't theirs" kill switch for any outside engine, so the new Pass 2 sweep adds no qualifier.

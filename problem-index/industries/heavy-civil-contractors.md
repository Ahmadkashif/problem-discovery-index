# Heavy Civil Contractors

## Profile
**Category:** Construction & Trades
**NAICS 2022:** 237990 — Other Heavy and Civil Engineering Construction
**Market Size:** ~$37.0B in 2022 receipts across 4,533 firms and 4,673 establishments (Census SUSB 2022). 59 enterprises with 500+ employees took ~$14.4B (~39%); 3,799 firms under 20 employees (~84% of firms) took ~$5.9B (~16%). The code covers marine and port construction, dredging, dams, locks and levees, railroad and transit track, tunnels and microtunnelling, and sports fields, golf courses and parks. The federal dredging bid market alone averaged ~$2.3B a year in 2023–2025 (Great Lakes Dredge & Dock 10-K). For scale, highway and bridge contractors (237310) took ~$156B the same year.
**Tech Maturity:** Medium — mid-size and large firms estimate in HCSS HeavyBid, Trimble's B2W or Kiewit's InEight and capture field hours by cost code in HeavyJob-type apps, and GPS machine control is common on big equipment. But unit prices still rest on the chief estimator's production-rate judgement, schedules are P6 files built to each owner's spec, and change and delay records are put together after the fact.
**Workforce:** Estimators and chief estimators, project engineers, schedulers, superintendents and foremen, operating engineers, pile drivers and dock builders, dredge crews (levermen, deckhands, tenders, tug operators), hydrographic surveyors, railroad track crews, laborers, equipment managers and mechanics, payroll and compliance staff. BLS QCEW counted ~136,800 private employees in the code in 2024 at ~$97,600 average pay.

## Key Pain Themes
A heavy civil contractor wins work by writing a number next to each line of a public bid schedule: cubic yards of channel dredging, linear feet of sheet pile, track feet of new siding, cubic yards of mass concrete in a lock wall. Each unit price is a production rate in disguise. The chief estimator reads the plans, the geotechnical borings and the site, decides what a crew and its equipment will actually do per shift in this material and these conditions, and spreads mobilisation, risk and margin across the items. Get the rate wrong by ~15% (illustrative) on the item that dominates the bid and the job loses money for two years. The judgement sits with a few senior people per firm. The evidence that could check it exists but is never joined: the firm's own field time cards record real production by cost code, and every public owner publishes who bid what after each letting.

The work then runs on clocks the owner sets. On Corps of Engineers jobs a delay must be justified within 10 days of occurring, with a time impact analysis on the owner's Primavera schedule spec, and a differing site condition must be reported "promptly, and before the conditions are disturbed" or the claim is lost. Dredging is paid on volumes computed from before-and-after hydrographic surveys, with allowable and unpaid overdepth, so the contractor's margin depends on survey arithmetic and on the leverman's twelve-hour watch at the controls. Federal work brings Miller Act bonds above $150,000, Davis-Bacon certified payroll, often several wage determinations on one job (dredging is classed as heavy work, not highway, per a California wage determination; search summary), and, on DOT-funded transit and rail work, DBE goals. USDOT's October 2025 interim rule made every DBE re-certify and froze contract goals until each state finished.

The money is federal and lumpy. The Corps' FY2026 civil works budget was a record ~$10.4B. IIJA's five years of advance appropriations ended on 30 September 2026, and Congress extended surface programmes only to 11 December 2026. Great Lakes Dredge & Dock, the largest dredger, was taken private by Saltchuk in April 2026 for ~$1.5B (search summary). Its dredging backlog had fallen 36% in 2025, and Orion's marine backlog fell to $480M while it reported a $19.4B pipeline (earnings call, search summary), so who wins the next lettings is a live question.

## Current Tech Landscape
HCSS (HeavyBid, HeavyJob; "thousands of contractors"), Trimble (B2W since 2022, search summary; plus machine control and Business Center), InEight and Oracle Primavera P6 hold estimates, field cost codes and schedules. Hydrographic surveys run on HYPACK-type packages, Corps dredges report sensor data to the Dredging Quality Management office daily (search summary), and mixed fleets report hours and fuel through AEMP 2.0 (ISO 15143-3) feeds to tools such as Tenna. Bid tab data is sold by Oman Systems, whose Bid Tabs Professional several DOTs use (search summary), and Edgevanta pitches a bid-tab agent over 20+ states (startup directory, search summary). Trunk Tools (TrunkBid, TrunkChange; product names from its careers page, TrunkBid's beta launch via search summary) and Rudus (concrete takeoff that exports to HeavyBid and B2W) bring AI to bids and documents, and the earthwork quantity reconciliation contest is mapped at [[niches/construction-tech-platforms/earthwork-quantity-reconciliation/profile|Earthwork & Sitework — Quantity Reconciliation]]. Hiring checked on 2026-10-24: Rudus (forward deployed engineer, founding engineer, computer vision research scientist), Trunk Tools (forward deployed engineer, ML engineer, integrations and platform engineers), Bedrock Robotics and Teleo (autonomy, ML and software engineers), Tenna (engineering manager, principal data engineer) and HCSS (DevOps only) had engineering roles open. No heavy-civil-specific bidding or claims vendor had a confirmed AI role. No tool owns the record that matters most: the estimator's assumed production rate on each item set beside what the crew actually did, and beside what every competitor bid.

## Analysis
The central unmeasured judgement in heavy civil construction is the production rate behind each unit price: how much a given crew and spread will place, drive, dredge or lay per shift in this material, at this site, under this owner. It decides who wins the letting and whether the job makes money. Three things block fixing it. First, the knowledge is tacit and concentrated: a few chief estimators per firm carry it, and they adjust rates by feel for soil, water, access and owner. Second, the label exists but is never joined. Foremen record actual hours and quantities by cost code every day, and public owners post bid results after every letting (bidder totals against the government estimate at minimum, line-item unit prices where the owner publishes the full tabulation), yet almost no firm compares its bid rates with its field actuals and the market's prices item by item. Third, the work is lumpy and federal, so a firm sees few comparable jobs a year and the funding clock (the Corps' budget, IIJA's lapse) moves volume more than skill does. The operators are capable but margin-thin buyers, and as insight buyers they fail the Pass 2 gate. The strongest commercial opening is a vendor that joins three records the industry keeps apart: public bid tabs, the contractor's own estimate, and its field production. That is the owner's MVP shape: unit-price outliers from Corps, port, transit and railroad-owner bid results plus DOT marine, structures and rail items. DOT road-and-bridge unit-price intelligence belongs to the queued highway contractors build, where Oman Systems and Edgevanta (search summary) already sit at the data edge, while the confirmed forward deployed roles are at Rudus and Trunk Tools, one step to the side.

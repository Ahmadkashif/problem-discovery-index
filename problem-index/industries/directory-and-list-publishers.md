# Directory & List Publishers

## Profile
**Category:** Media, Telecom & Information
**NAICS 2022:** 513140 — Directory and Mailing List Publishers
**Market Size:** ~$4.6B in 2022 employer-firm revenue ($4,582M, Census Service Annual Survey), down from $18.5B in 2007 as the printed phone book collapsed; of that, $1.85B came from subscriptions and sales of directories and databases and $117M from renting or selling mailing lists, with directory advertising space last reported at $1.78B in 2020. The largest business-data sellers (Dun & Bradstreet, ZoomInfo, Experian) are partly classified elsewhere, so buyer spend on compiled business and contact data runs well above the Census figure.
**Tech Maturity:** Medium — compilation pipelines are industrial (thousands of sources, address hygiene, entity matching, increasingly model-ranked call queues), but the decision of whether a record is true is still made by a caller on a sub-minute phone interview or an analyst reading conflicting sources, and print directory sales still run on an annual canvass, proof sheets and a book close date.
**Workforce:** Tele-research associates who verify records by phone, compilation and data operations analysts (source ingestion, industry coding, de-duplication), data scientists who model employee size and sales volume, list sales and count-and-quote reps, data licensing account managers, print directory ad consultants and proof staff, and privacy staff who run suppression and deletion. BLS counted 1,375 private establishments and 14,671 employees in 2024, down from 1,697 and 19,016 in 2022.

## Key Pain Themes
The product is a claim that a record is true: this business exists at this address, answers this phone, employs ~20 people, sells ~$3M a year (an illustrative record) and belongs under plumbing and heating. Every part of that claim decays — B2B contact data loses an estimated ~22–30% of its accuracy a year in vendor estimates — and compilers fight decay with sources and with calls. Data Axle blends 4,000+ sources, from carrier subscriber listings that federal law (47 U.S.C. § 222(e)) obliges phone companies to supply to directory publishers, to state business filings and web crawls, and runs 150+ data specialists making ~60,000 calls a day, about 25M a year, at an average call time under a minute. The verdict on each record — open, moved, closed, never there — is made in that minute, and what survives of the reasoning is a status code. Accuracy is sold as a warranty and settled as a credit when a customer's emails bounce; no compiler can say how accurate a given field is in a given segment.

Selling happens by count and by subscription. A buyer specifies selects — geography, industry code, employee band, contacts with verified email — and a rep quotes a count and a price per thousand; subscription platforms sell seats with export credits. Buyers compare counts across compilers and favour the bigger universe, which rewards keeping marginal records and punishes the compiler that purges dead ones. Print directories sell advertising on an annual canvass against a shrinking book: Thryv's Marketing Services segment, which still includes The Real Yellow Pages, fell 32.6% to $324.0M in 2025.

Outside clocks are mostly legal. California's Delete Act sends registered data brokers — over 600 of them — deletion requests through the state's DROP platform, to be processed on a rolling 45-day basis from 1 August 2026. Phone lists carry Do Not Call and TCPA exposure, and the FCC's February 2024 ruling put AI-generated voices under the TCPA's artificial-voice rules. USPS licence terms forbid building new-mover lists from NCOALink. And the 2021 Epsilon deferred prosecution agreement — $150M after it admitted selling data on more than 30 million consumers to fraudulent mail schemes — made vetting who buys a list a criminal-exposure question, not a sales formality.

## Current Tech Landscape
Data Axle (business and consumer files, self-serve Salesgenie, Reference Solutions for libraries), Dun & Bradstreet (taken private by Clearlake in August 2025 for ~$7.7B including debt), ZoomInfo, Lusha, Cognism and Apollo sell compiled business and contact data; Experian and Acxiom sell consumer files; specialist directories include Thomasnet (bought by Xometry for $300M in 2021), Martindale-Hubbell, Chambers and Partners, Leadership Connect and Grey House; Thryv and independent publishers print local books. Underneath sit entity-matching engines (in-house, Senzing, AWS Entity Resolution), CASS and NCOALink address hygiene through USPS licensees, email verification services, reassigned-number checks for phones, and, since 2023, Overture Maps' open places layer (59M+ places at launch) as a free competitor for location data. Each tool is competent within its step. The verification ledger — every source assertion, call outcome, customer bounce, credit and later correction, per record and per field over time — belongs to none of them, so no compiler can measure its own accuracy.

## Analysis
The industry's central unmeasured judgement is whether a record is true, and therefore how true a file is in the segment a buyer selects. Compilers hold the raw material to answer it — decades of call outcomes, source histories and customer complaints — and do not join it, for three reasons. Commercially, buyers reward bigger counts, so honest purging looks like lost coverage, and accuracy guarantees are refund mechanisms rather than measurements. Structurally, ground truth arrives scattered: bounces land at customers, undeliverable mail at mailers, corrections at map platforms that license the data, and only the calls land at the compiler. Legally, deletion mandates shrink the file on someone else's schedule while TCPA rules and carrier call-labelling throttle the phone verification that produces the truth. The strongest commercial opening is a verification ledger and call triage for the mid-size and national compilers, where a measured accuracy figure becomes a sales asset and fewer calls verify more change. The operators themselves are a thin market — 1,375 establishments, mostly small — and the insight layer around them is dominated by verification production rather than analysis; the two pockets of this code that clear the bar, commercial credit bureaus and drug compendia, are already profiled under other industries.

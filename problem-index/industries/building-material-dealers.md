# Building Material Dealers

## Profile
**Category:** Wholesale & Distribution
**NAICS 2022:** 423310, 423320, 423330, 423390, 444180 — Lumber, Plywood, Millwork and Wood Panel Merchant Wholesalers; Brick, Stone and Related Construction Material Merchant Wholesalers; Roofing, Siding and Insulation Material Merchant Wholesalers; Other Construction Material Merchant Wholesalers; Other Building Material Dealers
**Market Size:** ~$475B in 2022 receipts across ~30,800 firm counts and ~45,000 establishments (Census SUSB 2022: 423310 had 4,552 firms and $171.3B; 423330 had 879 firms and $75.0B, ~84% of it at 46 enterprises with 500+ employees; 444190, the 2017 code that became 444180, had 20,291 firms and $150.5B). Enterprises with 500+ employees took ~$242.6B (~51%), while ~25,100 firm counts under 20 employees took ~$72.0B. Builders FirstSource, the largest dealer, sold $15.2B in 2025. The codes also hold masonry yards, wallboard and insulation distributors, fencing and cabinet showrooms, treated here as edges.
**Tech Maturity:** Low-Medium — the large dealers run Epicor BisTrack, DMSi Agility or ECI Spruce, builder PO portals and digital ordering, and Builders FirstSource is building AI order entry for its myBLDR platform, but most lumber packages are still taken off a plan set by an estimator, special-order windows are re-keyed from manufacturer configurators, and delivery runs off a whiteboard and a dispatcher's phone.
**Workforce:** Estimators and takeoff specialists, outside salespeople calling on builders and remodelers, inside and pro-desk sales, special-order window, door and millwork specialists, truss and wall-panel designers and plant crews, yard workers and forklift operators, boom-truck and flatbed drivers, dispatchers, credit managers and collectors, and commodity lumber buyers. BLS QCEW counted ~515,000 private employees in the codes in 2024 (264,855 in 4233 at ~$87,100 average pay; 250,158 in 444180 at ~$71,600).

## Key Pain Themes
A lumberyard sells a house before the house exists. A custom builder or remodeler emails a plan set — elevations, floor plans, a framing note or two, often no structural sheets — and asks for a price on the framing package. Somebody at the dealer has to turn those drawings into a material list: studs, plates and headers by size and length, sheathing, engineered beams and joists, trusses or rafters, hardware, wrap and fasteners, with a waste factor, priced against a lumber market that moves weekly. The experienced estimator knows what the drawings leave out, how this framer builds, and what the builder will change in month two; the newer one prices what is drawn. Errors show up later as add-on loads and returns, which the dealer's ERP records job by job and nobody joins back to the original takeoff. Higharc's June 2026 deal to bring AI estimating to US LBM, and Builders FirstSource's in-house Paradigm business, are the first serious attempts to automate the step.

Most of the remaining work is physical and on account. Pro dealers sell on 30-day terms to contractors with thin balance sheets, so the credit manager watches every job and the statutory notice clocks that keep the dealer's lien rights alive (California's preliminary notice is due within 20 days of first furnishing, Civil Code §8204). Deliveries leave at dawn on boom trucks for job sites with no address and a framer who wants the load in a particular spot. Special-order windows and doors are configured in each manufacturer's software, re-keyed into the dealer's ERP and checked by eye, so a wrong rough opening or handing surfaces weeks later on site. Dealer-owned truss and wall-panel plants turn the same plans into component layouts through designers who redraw them every time the builder revises.

Prices and tariffs set the margin. Framing lumber is priced off weekly assessments such as the Random Lengths composites, CME's lumber futures are thin, and Canadian softwood carries anti-dumping and countervailing duties (35.16% combined for most producers until Commerce's seventh-review final rates of 24.75% take effect, announced 14 October 2026) on top of a 10% Section 232 tariff from 14 October 2025. Housing sets the volume: 1,358,700 starts in 2025, down 0.6% (Census), and Builders FirstSource's 2025 sales fell 7.4%. Consolidation is rapid — QXO bought Beacon (~$11B, April 2025) and Kodiak (~$2.25B, April 2026), Home Depot's SRS bought GMS (~$5.5B enterprise value, September 2025), and Lowe's bought Foundation Building Materials (~$8.8B, October 2025) — while ~17,000 small dealers (firms under 20 employees in the retail code) buy through co-ops such as LBM Advantage and Do it Best.

## Current Tech Landscape
LBM ERPs (Epicor BisTrack, DMSi Agility, ECI Spruce) run pricing, inventory, job accounts and dispatch; production builders send purchase orders through builder-supplier portals such as Hyphen's SupplyPro. Takeoff runs on Paradigm Estimate (a service owned by Builders FirstSource), STACK and other on-screen tools, and Higharc now sells AI estimating to distributors. Truss and panel design runs on MiTek and Alpine software; window and door quotes come from manufacturers' configurators, bridged into ERPs by integrators such as Saberis. Credit runs on applications and notice services (Nuvo, Procore's Levelset), and material financing on platforms such as Billd. The AI quoting and order-entry vendors that sell across distribution — Avent, Mercura, Comena, Canals, Proton.ai and peers — are profiled once, under [[niches/industrial-distributors/rfq-and-order-intake-automation/profile|RFQ & Order Intake Automation]], and take the emailed material lists and builder POs. On 2026-10-24, Higharc had a Forward Deployed Engineer role open (remote, New York, embedded with a large production homebuilder) plus machine learning, research and structural software engineers (remote US); DMSi had data platform and automation engineers in Omaha; Avent had a founding FDE and a founding engineer in San Francisco; Mercura and Comena had FDE and engineering roles in Munich only; Canals had senior software engineers across the Americas (including San Francisco and Toronto) and an ML engineer in Bogotá; and Proton.ai's engineering roles were remote in Europe and Latin America, none in the US. Paradigm listed only an AI product manager. Each tool does its job. The record no tool owns is the takeoff decision: what the estimator added, assumed and wasted, and what the job actually consumed.

## Analysis
The central unmeasured judgement in building-material dealing is the takeoff: turning an incomplete plan set into a lumber package that is complete, priced to win and close to what the framer will really use. It decides the bid, the margin and the number of trucks a job takes. Three things block fixing it. The knowledge is tacit and sits with a few estimators per yard. The outcome is recorded but never joined: every add-on order, return and short-ship sits in the ERP under the job, so the label exists, yet no dealer compares it with the original takeoff. And the inputs are poor — residential plan sets rarely carry framing plans — so a model has to infer what the estimator infers. The dealers are capable but margin-bound buyers, and as insight buyers the operators fail the Pass 2 gate. The strongest commercial opening is the vendor field selling into them: plan-to-package takeoff, where Higharc and Builders FirstSource's Paradigm are moving and Higharc is hiring, and emailed list and PO intake, which belongs to the cross-distribution field profiled under [[niches/industrial-distributors/rfq-and-order-intake-automation/profile|Industrial Distributors]]. Whoever captures the takeoff decision together with the job's delivered net quantities will hold the dataset this trade has never had.

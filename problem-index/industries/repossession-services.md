# Repossession Agencies

## Profile
**Category:** Business Support & Waste Services
**NAICS 2022:** 561491 — Repossession Services
**Market Size:** ~$1.15B in receipts at the 575 employer firms coded to repossession services in 2022 (Census SUSB), and an estimated ~$1.5–2.5B of recovery-agent revenue once agencies filed under towing and owner-operators without employees are counted (estimate); Cox Automotive put 2024 repossessions at ~1.73M, the most since 2009
**Tech Maturity:** Medium at the edges, manual in the middle — assignments arrive digitally through the Recovery Database Network and licence-plate cameras feed hits in real time, but the decision of where to look, when to attempt and whether to walk away, and the paperwork after the hook, still run on the agent's memory, phone calls and portal re-keying.
**Workforce:** Recovery agents driving wheel-lift and flatbed trucks (often the owner), camera-car drivers who scan plates at night, skip tracers, office staff who post status updates and invoices to lender and forwarder portals, personal property and lot clerks, and a compliance manager at larger agencies; ~7,300 W-2 employees in the coded firms (BLS QCEW 2024, average pay ~$56,000) plus many contract drivers

## Key Pain Themes
An assignment arrives through RDN or a forwarder with a borrower's last known addresses, a vehicle description and a fee paid only if the car comes back, typically a flat ~$350–450 for an involuntary auto recovery (estimate). Most never do: platform data analysed by CURepossession put recoveries at ~29% of assignments in 2025, down from a CFPB-reported 41% for 2018–2021. The agency's whole economics turn on choosing which of hundreds of open files to work tonight, which address to sit on and at what hour, and which licence-plate hits are worth a drive across town. Camera cars now find the easy cars first, so the files left for the truck are the borrowers who moved, park in a garage, or rotate the car between a girlfriend's apartment and a workplace lot — and the fee for finding them has barely moved since 2000.

The recovery itself is a legal edge case performed in the dark. Under UCC 9-609 a lender may repossess without a court only if it proceeds without breach of the peace, and in many states a debtor's objection is enough to end the attempt. Agents were shot during recoveries in Mississippi, Texas and Wisconsin in 2025 alone. The agent must also be sure the order is still live: the CFPB found in 2022 that servicers had repossessed vehicles "even after consumers took intentional actions to prevent repossessions", and Wells Fargo's 2022 order paid a base $4,000 to each borrower whose car was wrongly taken. California's guidance requires a call to local police within an hour of the recovery and written notice within a business day.

After the hook comes a stack of clocks. A condition report and photographs protect the agency against damage claims; personal property must be inventoried, stored and offered back under each state's rules (California's guidance: an inventory to the debtor within ~48 hours and storage for at least 60 days; in Florida, failing to safeguard and inventory property is a prohibited act); and the lender or forwarder wants a status update on its own cadence, an invoice with proof attached, and the car delivered to auction. A missed step can mean an unpaid fee, a complaint that removes the agency from a forwarder's panel, or a licence action.

## Current Tech Landscape
The Recovery Database Network, owned by OPENLANE, connects 600+ lenders with 2,500+ service providers and is the assignment backbone; its Clearplan field app replaces paper run-lists, Cleardata handles personal property and transports, and an On-Hook Status flag tells agents to proceed, hold or cancel. Digital Recognition Network, owned by Motorola Solutions since 2019, sells camera kits at ~$13,000–16,000 and runs what it describes as the largest plate-scan database in the US; MVTRAC and subscription services such as RecoveryNet ($299 a month for 500 plates) compete beneath it. Skip tracing runs on identity-graph searches profiled as [[niches/collections-agencies/identity-skip-trace-data-providers/profile|Identity Resolution & Skip Trace Data Providers]]. Forwarders — Resolvion, PAR North America, Primeritus and agency-owned cooperatives — sit between national lenders and local agencies. RISC's CARS course is the de facto training standard. Each tool records its own slice. The attempt history — which addresses were worked at what hour, which hits were confirmed or rejected, what the agent saw at the scene and why they left — is owned by none of them and kept by most agencies in notes fields and memory.

## Analysis
The industry's central unmeasured judgement is the experienced agent's read of where a car will be at a given hour and whether this attempt can be completed without a confrontation. It decides recovery rate, safety and legal exposure, and it lives in the heads of owner-operators: 339 of the 575 employer firms have fewer than five staff. Three things block fixing it. Structurally, agencies are paid a flat fee on success, so they cannot price a hard file and have little margin to invest; the account data belongs to the lender, the scan data to DRN, and the agency keeps only its own attempt notes. Legally, every attempt sits under breach-of-peace case law, state licensing, and state limits on private plate readers. Organisationally, the assignment and camera layers are concentrated in two corporate owners, OPENLANE and Motorola Solutions, whose tools set the workflow. The strongest commercial opening is field and post-recovery tooling for agencies — attempt planning, hit confirmation, property and condition paperwork — sold through integration with RDN and DRN, and a recoverability score that lets an agency price skips rather than absorb them. The agencies themselves are poor buyers for a research-insight engine; the Pass 2 sweep finds no qualifier here, because the value chain's qualifiers were found first from neighbouring industries — identity resolution and skip-trace data under [[industries/collections-agencies|Collections Agencies]] and vehicle valuation guides under [[industries/auto-dealers-independent|Independent Auto Dealers]] — and the lender and towing sides are profiled under [[industries/sales-finance-companies|Sales Finance Companies]] and [[industries/towing-companies|Towing Companies]].

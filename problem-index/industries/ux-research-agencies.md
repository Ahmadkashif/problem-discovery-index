# UX Research Agencies

## Profile
**Category:** Digital Professional Services
**Market Size:** ~$4B US in user research services and the research operations tooling around them, spanning specialist consultancies, the research arms of design firms, and freelance practitioners
**Tech Maturity:** Good instruments, no memory. Recruitment panels, session recording, unmoderated testing platforms and research repositories are mature and widely used. The discipline's own central question — whether a finding was correct, and whether acting on it helped — is asked almost nowhere, because the agency hands over a report and the product decision happens afterwards without them.
**Workforce:** User researchers, research operations specialists, participant recruiters and panel managers, quantitative researchers and survey methodologists, project leads

## Key Pain Themes
A research engagement produces a deliverable — a findings report, a set of recommendations, a highlight reel — and ends. Whether the product team acted on it, whether the change worked, and whether the finding was right in the first place are all determined later, in systems the agency has no access to. The discipline has essentially no track record.

That absence has a compounding effect on credibility. Research is the first budget line cut in a downturn, and the reason given is always some version of not being able to demonstrate return. Researchers argue the point vigorously and cannot settle it, because settling it would require exactly the outcome data nobody collects.

The second theme is that findings rest on small samples and are reported as if they did not. Five participants is the standard heuristic for a qualitative study and it is genuinely useful for finding severe usability problems; it is not a basis for the confident generalisations that appear in report summaries, and the gap between what the method supports and what the slide says is the discipline's quiet methodological problem.

The third is the participant supply. Recruitment is slow, expensive and increasingly contaminated by professional participants and outright fraud, and screening is done by self-report — which is the weakest possible instrument against people whose income depends on passing screeners.

## Current Tech Landscape
Recruitment runs on User Interviews, Respondent, Prolific and Userlytics, with panel providers behind them. Unmoderated testing comes from UserTesting, Maze, Lyssna and Optimal Workshop. Session recording and behavioural analytics — FullStory, Hotjar, Amplitude — sit on the client side. Repositories from Dovetail, Condens and Marvin hold transcripts, tags and findings, and are widely described by their own users as write-only archives. Transcription and increasingly first-pass synthesis are automated. Survey methodology tooling is mature and separate.

## Analysis
This is a discipline built to reduce uncertainty about user behaviour that has never measured its own accuracy. Every study makes predictions — users will struggle here, this concept will not be understood, this feature will go unused — and every one of those predictions is testable against what happens after release. The agency is gone by then and the client rarely looks back. The result is a field whose practitioners hold genuine expertise, defended entirely by method and argument rather than by any record of having been right, and whose budget is therefore permanently contingent on a stakeholder's belief. The corpus that would fix this is a few years of studies matched to subsequent product outcomes, which nobody has assembled anywhere.

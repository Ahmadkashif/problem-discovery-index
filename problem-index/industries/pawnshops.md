# Pawnshops

## Profile
**Category:** Financial Services
**NAICS 2022:** 522299 — International, Secondary Market, and All Other Nondepository Credit Intermediation. The queue built this residual code around licensed pawnbrokers, its largest operator population; the code's secondary-market lenders (the GSEs), international trade and export finance and its other residual lenders are not analysed here.
**Market Size:** ~$6–8B a year in US pawn loan fees, merchandise and scrap sales (estimate), anchored by the two public chains: FirstCash's US pawn segment took $1.75B of revenue in 2025 on $450.5M of loans outstanding, and EZCORP's US Pawn segment earned $351.5M of pawn service charges on $233.8M of loans (year to September 2025). Census counted 9,726 establishments in the predecessor code 522298 in 2022, the best public proxy for ~10,000 US pawnshops (estimate)
**Tech Maturity:** Low at the counter, digital at the edges — the ticket, the police upload and the online listing run on software, while the decision that matters, what an item is worth and how much to lend against it, is made in a few minutes by a clerk with an acid kit, a scale, a phone and memory.
**Workforce:** Owner-pawnbrokers, pawn clerks who appraise, write loans, buy outright and sell, jewellery buyers working the testing bench, firearms counter staff under the store's federal firearms licence, store and district managers at the chains; FirstCash alone employed ~8,300 people in the US at end-2025

## Key Pain Themes
A pawn loan is a credit decision made without a credit check. The customer brings a gold chain, a drill kit, a pistol or a games console; the clerk identifies and tests it, estimates its resale value in this store and the odds this customer returns, and lends a fraction of resale (~25–60% by common account, estimate). There is no recourse: about a third of loans forfeit (33.4% in Oregon's 2025 pawnbroker report) and the item is the only repayment. Loans are small — $312 on average at FirstCash's US stores — so the appraisal takes minutes, and errors cut both ways: lend too much and the forfeit sells below the loan, too little and the customer walks across the street. The experienced pawnbroker's read — the heft of real gold against plated tungsten, which tool brands move in this town, which story is off — is the business, and it is recorded nowhere except as a loan amount.

External clocks bind every ticket. Florida's Pawnbroking Act is typical in shape: a transaction form with the customer's thumbprint, ID and a full description including serials and metal content, delivered to police by the end of each business day; goods held unaltered for 30 days; a 30-day maturity, a 30-day grace period and automatic forfeiture; a charge capped at 25% of the amount financed per 30 days. Other states and cities set different fields, holds and caps, and a police hold on one item can end in seizure of the item and the loan money with it. Every redemption of a pawned gun needs a Form 4473 and a NICS check, and a denied customer cannot have their own gun back. The Military Lending Act caps loans to covered service members at a 36% MAPR, which a standard ticket breaches.

Then forfeits must become cash. Gold goes to retail or to the refiner at spot, which hit a record close of $5,318 an ounce in January 2026 and sat ~22% lower in early October; EZCORP's US scrap sales rose 58% in fiscal 2025. Everything else sells in store or online, one used item at a time, and shelf time eats margin: EZCORP's US inventory turned 2.2 times in fiscal 2025 against 2.6 a year earlier.

## Current Tech Landscape
Many independents run Bravo Store Systems' pawn POS or its older PawnMaster Classic; the chains run their own systems. Police reporting goes to LeadsOnline, which lets agencies search 1.2+ billion transactions from pawnshops, secondhand dealers, scrap yards and kiosks, or to regional systems such as BWI's RAPID. Valuation leans on acid kits, electronic testers and, at chains, XRF analysers for gold; the spot price; the Rapaport Price List for diamonds, which sells a $1,000-a-year pawnbroker plan; eBay sold listings, PriceCharting and gun value guides for general merchandise. Firearms records run in electronic bound books such as FastBound or on paper. Online resale goes through eBay, GunBroker and the stores' own sites. Each tool covers its step. The record that joins the clerk's valuation at intake to the loan, the redemption or forfeit, and the price the item finally fetched sits in the POS and is used by no one.

## Analysis
The pawnshop's central unmeasured judgement is the counter appraisal: a valuation and a credit decision rolled into one number, whose outcome — redeemed, renewed, or forfeited and sold at a known price after a known number of days — is recorded in every POS. That makes it an unusually clean tacit-knowledge problem: the label arrives within months, in dollars. What blocks it is structural. The valuation is never written down separately from the loan amount, which mixes value with negotiation and the read of the customer; the realised price exists only for the forfeited third; and ~80% of shops are independents (estimate) with one store, no analyst and a vendor POS whose data they own. Police reporting data, the one cross-shop dataset, is confidential by statute and usable only for law enforcement. The strongest commercial opening is therefore the two public chains and the larger regional groups, which hold millions of graded outcomes and the store count to train on them, followed by POS vendors that could offer the same to independents. As a buyer of an insight engine the industry is weak: its insight is spent on lending, not invoiced, and the firms above it — price-guide publishers, precious-metals researchers, the police reporting databases — are small, concentrated or barred from reusing the data they hold.

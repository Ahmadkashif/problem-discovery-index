# Retail POS Platforms

## Profile
**Category:** Vertical SaaS
**Market Size:** ~$10B US retail point of sale and store operations software
**Tech Maturity:** High at the transaction layer, thin above it — Square, Shopify POS, Lightspeed, Clover, Heartland and Toast's retail cousins have made card acceptance, catalogue and basic inventory a commodity. Merchandising decisions, which are what actually determine whether an independent retailer survives, are made in spreadsheets alongside the software.
**Workforce:** Onboarding and catalogue specialists, integration engineers, payments and risk analysts, hardware support technicians, merchant success managers, retail advisory staff

## Key Pain Themes
Independent and specialty retailers make two decisions that determine their outcome, and their software helps with neither. What to buy, months ahead, against a budget — the open-to-buy problem, still run on a spreadsheet at the overwhelming majority of independents. And when to mark down, and by how much, which is where a season's margin is won or lost and which is currently decided by looking at the rack and feeling nervous. Beneath those sit two data problems the category has never fully solved: product catalogue quality, where specialty goods have attributes no UPC database carries and every merchant re-enters them; and multi-channel inventory, where the store, the website and the marketplaces disagree about what is in stock and the reconciliation is manual. The staff pay for it in cycle counts and shrink investigations that consume evenings and rarely conclude.

## Current Tech Landscape
Square and Shopify dominate the small end and increasingly the mid-market, with payments as the real business model and software as the wedge. Lightspeed serves specialty verticals with deeper inventory and vendor management. Clover and Heartland reach merchants through bank and ISO channels. Retail-specific ERP (NetSuite, Brightpearl, Cin7) handles the larger independents. Demand planning and markdown optimisation exist as a mature enterprise category and have never been packaged for anyone below it. Marketplace and channel integration is a competitive, fragmented layer.

## Analysis
Enterprise retailers have had markdown optimisation and demand planning for twenty years, staffed by planning teams and priced accordingly. The capability was never a technical secret; it was an organisational one, requiring analysts no independent retailer can employ. The POS platforms now hold, across hundreds of thousands of independent merchants, exactly the transaction and inventory history those systems consume — and they deliver a sales summary. The gap between what the category could compute and what it displays is the widest in vertical SaaS.

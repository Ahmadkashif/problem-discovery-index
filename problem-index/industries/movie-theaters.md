# Movie Theaters

## Profile
**Category:** Media, Telecom & Information
**NAICS 2022:** 512131, 512132 — Motion Picture Theaters (except Drive-Ins); Drive-In Motion Picture Theaters
**Market Size:** ~$13.1B in 2022 receipts across 2,076 firms and 4,459 establishments (2022 Economic Census: $12.98B for indoor theatres under 512131 and $100M for drive-ins under 512132). US/Canada box office was ~$8.9B in 2025, up ~1.5% (AMC FY2025 10-K)
**Tech Maturity:** Medium — ticketing, point of sale and digital projection are fully computerised at every circuit, while the decisions that set the week (which films, how many screens, which showtimes, how much popcorn and how many crew) are made from last weekend's grosses, a buyer's memory and a manager's feel
**Workforce:** Ushers, lobby attendants and ticket takers (~58,000 of ~122,500 workers), concession and counter staff, servers and bartenders at dine-in sites, shift leads and general managers, a shrinking corps of projectionists (~1,700), film buyers and bookers at circuit head offices or independent booking agencies, and marketing, pricing and loyalty teams at the large circuits

Both codes do the same job: license a film for a run, show it to a paying audience and sell food around it. 512131 is the indoor multiplex, single-screen and dine-in cinema. 512132 adds about 280 drive-ins (UDITOA counted 283 in October 2024), almost all small, seasonal and owner-run: County Business Patterns found 250 of 290 drive-in establishments with fewer than five employees in March 2023, before most had opened for the season. Drive-ins book the same films from the same distributors on the same sliding-scale terms, usually as double features, and earn a larger share of their margin at the snack bar. The industry is a few large circuits and a long tail. AMC alone runs 7,072 US screens and Cinemark 4,241, while the Independent Cinema Alliance speaks for about 180 member companies with about 5,000 screens.

## Key Pain Themes
The central decision is the weekly booking. Each Monday the film buyer sees the weekend grosses and decides, theatre by theatre, which films hold, which lose screens or showtimes, and what opens Friday on how many screens. Terms are set "on a film-by-film and theatre-by-theatre basis" (AMC), and "the majority of film rental rates are negotiated on a sliding scale formula" (Cinemark), so the distributor's share is highest in the opening weeks and falls as the run ages. A buyer who holds a fading film too long pays rent on empty seats, and one who drops it early loses the cheap late weeks. Experienced buyers know their markets: this suburb turns out for faith films, that college town ignores the second weekend of a horror sequel. A CMX executive called scheduling "an art, honestly." None of that judgement is recorded against the outcome.

Film rent is the largest cost (~49% of admissions at AMC and ~57% at Cinemark including advertising, both derived from filings), and it is accrued on estimates and settled at the end of each run by negotiation with each distributor. The margin is made in the lobby. Concession supplies cost about 20% of concession revenue at both public circuits, and Cinemark's US patrons spent $8.30 each on food in 2025 against a $10.52 ticket. That margin depends on prepping and staffing to attendance that swings tenfold between a Tuesday matinee and an opening Saturday. The crew is young, part-time and paid near the minimum wage: AMC reports 30,380 part-time employees out of 33,311. Ushers describe 15-minute turns between shows as "nearly impossible."

External clocks govern the work. Distributors set the Friday release dates and the KDM decryption keys that unlock each film's files for a set window. The exclusive window has fallen from about four months to about 45 days or less. The ADA movie rule (28 CFR 36.303(g)) requires working captioning and audio description devices in set numbers per theatre. FDA menu labelling applies to concession stands at chains of 20 or more locations. Drive-ins open and close on the weather.

## Current Tech Landscape
Vista dominates circuit ticketing and cinema management, and its cloud product Veezi serves single sites, art houses and drive-ins. RTS and Omniterm hold parts of the independent market. Movio, another Vista brand, runs loyalty marketing for circuits. Theatre management systems from Hollywood Software and GDC push showtimes to the projectors. Comscore collects every theatre's daily grosses and reports them to distributors, analysed under [[industries/film-distributors|Film Distributors]]. Fandango and the circuits' own apps sell the tickets, and National CineMedia sells the pre-show to advertisers across 17,621 screens. Back offices run on general ledgers, spreadsheets and email: film rent accruals, settlement worksheets, concession orders and crew schedules. Each tool records its own transactions well. No tool joins the booking decision to the attendance, concession spend and film rent it produced.

## Analysis
The industry's central unmeasured judgement is the booking call: how many screens and showtimes each film gets in each theatre each week, made by a few buyers from weekend grosses and local memory, and never graded against what the seats, concessions and film rent would have been under a different call. The data to grade it exists in every circuit's point-of-sale history. Three things block the fix. Terms are private and negotiated film by film, so the cost side of a booking sits in settlement emails rather than a database. Buyers' knowledge is tacit and personal, and the independents buy through agents who carry it for dozens of clients. And margins are thin enough that most operators can't fund analytics. The large circuits are capable buyers of programming and pricing tools, but there are only a handful of them. The independents are many but poor, so the commercial opening runs through the platforms that already sit in their lobbies: ticketing and point-of-sale vendors, booking agencies and the buying co-op. For a research-insight engine the theatre is a poor buyer. Its insight is monetised through its own seats, and the data businesses around it are covered in [[niches/movie-theaters/_overview|the niche analysis]]: advertising networks, ticketing aggregators and loyalty platforms. None of them clears the Pass 2 threshold.

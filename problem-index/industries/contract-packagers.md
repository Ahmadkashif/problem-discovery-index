# Contract Packagers

## Profile
**Category:** Business Support & Waste Services
**NAICS 2022:** 561910 — Packaging and Labeling Services
**Market Size:** ~$10.7B in annual receipts across 1,629 employer firms and 1,853 establishments (Census SUSB 2022), of which 43 enterprises with 500+ employees took ~$4.1B (~38%) and 1,231 firms with fewer than 20 employees took ~$1.75B; the code undercounts the activity, because Census files warehouses that also package under warehousing, processors that change the product under manufacturing, and many pharmaceutical packagers inside drug manufacturing
**Tech Maturity:** Low to Medium — pharmaceutical packagers run serialisation, vision inspection and electronic batch records under FDA cGMP, while most consumer co-packers quote in spreadsheets, schedule on whiteboards, hand paper work instructions to temporary crews and count client-owned components on tally sheets.
**Workforce:** Hand packers and kitting assemblers (many supplied by temp agencies in peak season), packaging machine operators, line leads, maintenance mechanics, QA inspectors and line-clearance checkers, schedulers, estimators and project managers, and account managers who face the brand owner; ~63,800 employees in the code at ~$59,500 average annual pay (BLS QCEW 2024), down 1.4% on 2023

## Key Pain Themes
A contract packager sells labour, lines and floor space against someone else's product. The brand owner ships in finished goods and components — cartons, labels, display corrugate, blister cards, inserts, shrink film — and pays a per-unit price to have them turned into retail packs, club packs, displays, kits or relabelled goods. PMMI's 2024 outsourcing study found brand owners buy the service mostly for formats they lack in-house (59%), product or packaging tests (48%), short or customised runs (48%) and surge capacity (43%); 11% use a contract provider as their whole production operation. That mix means short runs, frequent changeovers and constant new SKUs. The price is set before the job has ever run. One confectionery co-packer publishes $0.75–1.25 per club pack and $30–50 per display build, "fully loaded" and held for six months, so the margin depends on a units-per-hour estimate made from a sample and a spec sheet. When the shrink film wrinkles, a tray needs a fold jig or the temp crew takes three days to reach rate, the packager absorbs the gap.

The work is timed by other people's calendars and filled with other people's materials. Seasonal programmes are planned four to six months ahead (Valentine's Day displays in October, Easter in December) and must ship inside a retailer's window. Walmart charges suppliers ~3% of cost of goods on cases that miss its on-time, in-full rules, and Walmart, Costco and Target require food safety certification before they approve a co-packer. Components arrive late, short or misprinted from the brand's other suppliers, and every carton and label must be reconciled back to the client at the end of the job. Of the 87% of contract packagers planning equipment purchases within three years, 53% cite faster changeover as a reason.

In regulated work, a label error causes a recall. Under 21 CFR 211.130 a drug packager must inspect the line immediately before each run to prove the previous product and labelling are gone, and record that in the batch record. Since FDA's exemption for manufacturers and repackagers ended on 27 May 2025, every saleable pack must also carry a DSCSA serial number tied to an electronic record. In food, allergens drove 27.1% of FDA product recall records between FY2013 and FY2025 and 38.6% of USDA FSIS recall events, and the wrong film or label on a line is one way they happen. Imported goods that arrive without a legal country-of-origin mark owe a 10% marking duty unless re-marked under Customs supervision before liquidation, which sends relabelling work to packagers on deadlines set by Customs and Border Protection (CBP).

## Current Tech Landscape
Nulogy is the best-known software built for the industry itself. Its Shop Floor product runs production, labour and inventory workflows at what Nulogy calls hundreds of contract packaging sites, and its supplier collaboration connects co-packers with brand owners. Smaller packagers adapt food or process ERPs such as Deacom, NutraSoft, Business Central add-ons and MRPeasy, most of which keep lots in the plant's own inventory rather than in separate client-owned pools. Pharmaceutical packagers connect line-level serialisation from Antares Vision, Systech and Optel to networks such as TraceLink, which reports ~280,000 companies with DSCSA data access. Label verification and print inspection come from Cognex and Keyence cameras, and artwork comparison from GlobalVision and Esko. Brand owners send programmes through EDI and retailer portals. The platforms are consolidating: Bain Capital invested in PCI Pharma Services in July 2025 at a reported ~$10B valuation, Alcami agreed in April 2026 to buy Tjoapack (~400 staff, 23 lines), and Nonantum Capital bought MSI Express in 2025. Each tool works inside its own boundary, and no tool owns the quote's assumptions joined to the run's actual labour, changeover, scrap and component count.

## Analysis
The industry's central unmeasured judgement is the line rate: how many units an hour a crew will make on a job that has never run, judged by an estimator or production manager handling a sample and reading a spec sheet. That number sets the per-unit price, the crew size, the schedule and the margin. It is rarely written down with its assumptions, and it is almost never compared with what the run produced, because the quote lives in a spreadsheet, the run in labour sheets and line counts, and the component reconciliation in a third place. Fixing it is hard for structural reasons. Every job is a new combination of product, components and crew. Temp-heavy crews add noise to every outcome. Most packagers are too small to keep an estimating function: 76% of firms in the code have fewer than 20 employees. The commercial opening is in the operators themselves, as a quoting-and-costing record sold to the ~400 firms with 20+ employees, and in the regulated segments, where line clearance, label verification and serialisation already create the data and a compliance budget pays for it. Under the Pass 2 rubric nothing around the industry qualifies. The richest datasets — serialisation events, clinical supply plans, line telemetry, EPR packaging data — belong to clients, and the firms that own their data publish too little research to pass.

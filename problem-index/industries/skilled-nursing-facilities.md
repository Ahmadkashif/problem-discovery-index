# Skilled Nursing Facilities

## Profile
**Category:** Healthcare & Clinical
**NAICS 2022:** 623110 — Nursing Care Facilities (Skilled Nursing Facilities): Medicare- and Medicaid-certified nursing homes giving 24-hour nursing care, post-hospital rehabilitation paid under PDPM, and long-stay custodial care, assessed on the MDS and rated by CMS Five-Star
**Market Size:** ~$219.9B of 2024 spending at freestanding nursing care facilities and continuing care retirement communities, up 7.3% (CMS National Health Expenditures; the line includes CCRCs). 14,742 certified facilities held ~1.24M residents in July 2025, with Medicaid the primary payer for 63% of them and Medicare for 14% (KFF). Fee-for-service Medicare and its beneficiaries spent $31B on ~1.5M covered stays at ~14,400 freestanding SNFs in 2024 (MedPAC)
**Tech Maturity:** Medium — nearly every facility runs an EHR (PointClickCare, MatrixCare) and transmits MDS and PBJ files to CMS electronically, while hospital referrals still arrive by fax and portal, call-offs are filled by phone and text at 5 a.m., CNAs chart ADLs at a kiosk after the shift, and survey readiness lives in binders.
**Workforce:** 1,379,430 employed in NAICS 6231 (BLS OEWS, May 2023): nursing assistants (458,590, mean $18.62/hr), LPNs (171,290), RNs (124,340), medical and health services managers (28,100: administrators, directors of nursing), physical, occupational and speech therapists (28,240 combined, many more on contract), medical records specialists (9,530), plus MDS coordinators, admissions liaisons, staffing coordinators, housekeeping, dietary and activities staff

## Key Pain Themes
A skilled nursing facility is two businesses under one roof. A short-stay rehabilitation unit takes patients after a hospital stay and is paid well by Medicare: freestanding SNFs earned a 24% fee-for-service Medicare margin in 2024. A long-stay nursing home cares for frail and mostly Medicaid residents for months or years at rates near cost, so the all-payer margin was 2.1% (MedPAC). In the median freestanding facility, Medicare fee-for-service covers 8% of days and Medicaid 64%, and fee-for-service Medicare brings in 13% of facility revenue. Both are paid on the MDS resident assessment: PDPM sets the Medicare rate from the diagnoses, functional scores and care needs coded on it, and since the Optional State Assessment ended on 30 September 2025 most state Medicaid case-mix systems use PDPM groupings too. CMS's FY 2027 rule paid a 2.4% update, but it also opened a Request for Information on PDPM "case-mix upcoding", and MedPAC has asked Congress for a 4% cut.

Staffing is the largest cost and the most watched number. Total nurse staffing averaged 3.85 hours per resident day in July 2025, down 7% from 2015, and RN hours fell 19% (KFF). The 2024 federal minimum-staffing rule (3.48 total HPRD, 0.55 RN, a 24/7 RN) never took effect. Courts vacated its core, Public Law 119-21 barred enforcement until 2034, and CMS repealed it by interim final rule effective 2 February 2026. Hours reported through the quarterly Payroll-Based Journal, due 45 days after quarter end, still set the Five-Star staffing rating, two SNF VBP measures and eligibility for the new risk-based survey. Agency labour peaked at 10.5% of nursing hours in Q3 2022 and fell to 5.3% by Q4 2025 (LTCCC on PBJ data). In 2021 an agency nurse aide cost 80% more an hour than an employed one (ASPE). On the floor, the judgement that matters most is the experienced CNA's or charge nurse's sense that a resident is "not right" a day before the vitals change, which decides whether the resident goes back to hospital.

The clocks are external. State surveyors must inspect every 15 months. In June 2026, 1,410 facilities had gone more than 18 months without one, and the average facility draws 9.5 deficiencies a cycle, with 27% cited for harm or immediate jeopardy (CMS QSO-26-14-NH; KFF). Managed care, mostly Medicare Advantage, accounted for 42% of admissions in FY 2025 (CLA), each needing an authorisation and weekly continued-stay reviews. OIG found in June 2026 that the three largest MA insurers denied 12% of SNF requests, and nearly all appealed denials were overturned.

## Current Tech Landscape
PointClickCare dominates the clinical record, with 30,000+ provider organisations and 400+ marketplace partners (vendor-reported). MatrixCare (ResMed), Netsmart and Yardi hold most of the rest. MDS and PDPM review runs on EHR worksheets, Simple's compliance tools and consultants such as Zimmet and Harmony. Workforce management comes from SmartLinx, UKG and OnShift, and per-diem shifts from ShiftKey, IntelyCare and Clipboard Health. Hospital referrals come through CarePort (WellSky), naviHealth and fax, with Aline's referral CRM on the operator side. A new layer of AI vendors is hiring engineers: ExaCare (admissions and reimbursement agents, $30M Series A), Skypoint (agentic apps sold with forward deployed engineers to 1,100+ locations, though the only FDE role on its own job board is in Bengaluru and no US role is confirmed), Sunbound (revenue operations) and PointClickCare's own applied AI team. Falls technology from SafelyYou, Teton.ai and Inspiren grew up in memory care. The INTERACT "Stop and Watch" slip a CNA hands a nurse is still paper. No tool joins what the CNA saw, how the nurse assessed it, what the MDS coded, who was on shift that day and what followed: a hospital transfer, a survey tag or an MA denial.

## Analysis
The industry's central unmeasured judgement is the floor's early read of decline. A CNA who has bathed a resident for six months notices the change in appetite, gait or mood. A charge nurse decides whether that change warrants a call to the physician. Both decisions go mostly unrecorded, yet they determine readmissions, falls and much of the MDS coding that sets revenue. Structure, not technology, blocks the fix: CNA documentation is entered after the fact, turnover runs near half the nursing staff a year (~46–53%, estimate), every observation is protected health information, and operators run on a 2–3% all-payer margin. Operators are poor buyers for anything that does not pay back in revenue (PDPM capture, MA days), stars or survey outcomes within a year. The strongest openings are two. One is the PointClickCare partner ecosystem and the AI vendors now hiring into it. The other is products built on public CMS data (PBJ daily staffing, Care Compare, CMS-2567 inspection text and cost reports) sold to regional chains, REITs, lenders, MA plans and hospital networks without touching PHI. The Pass 2 sweep finds no qualifying insight pocket. The industry's analytical labour is large but fenced by HIPAA and facility ownership of the record, the same result as [[industries/home-health-agencies|Home Health Agencies]].

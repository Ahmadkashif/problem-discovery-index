# Physician Practices

## Profile
**Category:** Healthcare & Clinical
**NAICS 2022:** 621111, 621491 — Offices of Physicians (except Mental Health Specialists); HMO Medical Centers (primary-care and specialty physician offices billing E/M visits and procedures, including the office side of hospital-owned and private-equity-backed groups; urgent care, behavioural health and the software vendors sit in their own industries)
**Market Size:** Physician and clinical services were $1.1T of US health spending in 2024, up 8.1% and 21% of the total, with prices up only 1.8% (CMS National Health Expenditure). Offices of physicians (621111) took in $590.9B in 2022 (Census Service Annual Survey), and HMO medical centres add ~$40B–$60B (estimate). Only 42.2% of physicians still worked in private practice in 2024, down from 60.1% in 2012 (AMA Physician Practice Benchmark Survey), and 82% were employed by hospitals or corporate entities by January 2026 (PAI–Avalere)
**Tech Maturity:** Medium — every practice runs an EHR and bills electronically, yet practices handle 39 prior authorizations per physician per week at ~13 hours of physician and staff time (AMA, 2024), specialists receive referrals by fax, and the front desk still runs on the phone.
**Workforce:** 2.84M employed in NAICS 621100 (BLS OEWS, May 2023, which also counts psychiatrists' offices): medical assistants (430,490), physicians (381,060), medical secretaries (241,900), registered nurses (220,070), receptionists (174,460), nurse practitioners (137,030), physician assistants (80,610), LPNs (74,850), billing and posting clerks (69,840) and practice managers (68,370); only 1,070 data scientists and 1,200 software developers across the whole industry

## Key Pain Themes
The practice's most consequential judgement is made in a fifteen-minute visit, and almost none of it is recorded as judgement. An experienced primary care physician sees a patient with vague fatigue or a headache and "just knows" this one is different. They order the scan, book a one-week recheck, or tell the patient exactly when to go to the emergency department. A newer clinician with the same chart sends the patient home. About 5% of US adults, roughly 12 million a year, experience an outpatient diagnostic error, and about half of those errors could cause harm (Singh, BMJ Quality & Safety, 2014). The signal is hard to learn because the outcome leaves the building: the missed diagnosis shows up weeks later in a hospital emergency department the practice never hears from. It also walks out with the physician. Owners fell from 53.2% to 35.4% of physicians between 2012 and 2024 (AMA), and every move between groups breaks a panel.

Around the visit sits paperwork set by payers. Practices report 39 prior authorizations per physician per week, about 13 hours of physician and staff time, and 93% of physicians say it delays care (AMA 2024 survey). Medicare Advantage plans alone made 52.8M authorization decisions in 2024, denied 7.7%, and saw 80.7% of appealed denials overturned (KFF). Since 1 January 2026, CMS-0057-F has held impacted payers to 72-hour expedited and seven-day standard decisions. Their Prior Authorization APIs are due on 1 January 2027, and clinicians start attesting to electronic prior authorization in MIPS for 2027. Gold-card laws in at least ten states exempt high-approval clinicians, but only ~3% of Texas providers qualified under its 2021 law.

The money is reset every year in federal rulemaking. The CY2026 fee schedule lifted the conversion factor 3.26% to $33.40, but that includes a one-time 2.5% bump. It also cut work RVUs 2.5% on non-time-based services, which hits procedures and spares E/M visits (CMS-1832-F). MIPS holds its 75-point threshold through 2028. The Shared Savings Program has 511 ACOs and 12.6M assigned beneficiaries in 2026, and earned $4.1B in shared savings for 2024 (CMS Fast Facts).

## Current Tech Landscape
athenahealth, eClinicalWorks, NextGen, ModMed, Elation, Tebra and Epic (through Community Connect for affiliated groups) run charts, schedules and claims. athenahealth now includes its own ambient scribe, athenaAmbient, at no extra subscription cost for 170,000+ clinicians (HIT Consultant, reporting athenahealth's releases), which pressures Abridge, Ambience, Microsoft Dragon Copilot, Commure, Suki and Freed. Prior authorization runs through CoverMyMeds for drugs, payer and radiology benefit manager portals (EviCore, Carelon) for services, and newer automation from Commure (Athelas), Cair Health, Candid Health, SuperDial and Infinitus. Assort Health, Hello Patient and Hippocratic AI sell voice agents for the front desk. Tennr reads faxed referrals. Aledade, Privia, agilon and Pearl Health run value-based contracts for primary care groups, and CAQH, Medallion and CertifyOS handle credentialing. Each tool covers its step. None holds the record that joins what the physician saw and decided in the visit to the outcome that appears weeks later in another organisation's system.

## Analysis
The industry's central unmeasured judgement is the outpatient physician's sense of who is sicker than they look, and the follow-up decisions that come from it. It drives missed diagnoses, emergency visits and the cost line of every value-based contract, yet it is recorded only as side effects: an order, a short recheck interval, a sentence of safety-netting advice. Three things block a fix. Outcomes land in other organisations' records, so a practice cannot see its own misses. A physician who acts on a hunch prevents the outcome that would have proved it right. And most practices are too small to buy clinical AI, while hospital owners route it through slow committees. Ambient scribes add a new risk, because templated notes may wash out the hedged language that carried the signal. The commercial opening is therefore with the vendors that sell to practices, management services organisations and private-equity platforms and are hiring engineers to deploy. On 24 October 2026, Commure, Assort Health, Tennr, Cair Health, Candid Health, Hippocratic AI and Healthie all had forward deployed engineering roles open on their own boards or YC job pages, and Aledade, Ambience, Abridge and Hello Patient were hiring engineers (see the agents note). Under the Pass 2 rubric the operators fail: their insight is spent on care and margin, never invoiced, and every record is PHI. No pocket qualifies. The closest are the office-based specialty registries (43), which hold the deepest office clinical corpus and submit MIPS data against a federal deadline but work on identified records (HIPAA kill switch), and the drug coverage policy databases (40), which are clean but answer to no outside clock (see the niche overview).

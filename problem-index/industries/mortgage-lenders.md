# Mortgage Lenders

## Profile
**Category:** Financial Services
**NAICS 2022:** 522292 — Real Estate Credit (residential mortgage bankers and independent mortgage banks that fund loans in their own name, the scope of this industry; the code also covers commercial and multifamily mortgage bankers, such as agency multifamily lenders and CMBS conduit lenders, which are out of scope here; deposit-taking lenders sit in [[industries/community-banks|Community Banks]] and [[industries/credit-unions|Credit Unions]], and brokers who originate for them in [[industries/mortgage-brokers|Mortgage Brokers]])
**Market Size:** ~$2.1T in US single-family originations projected for 2026 across ~5.3M loans (MBA forecast, October 2026); state-licensed mortgage companies originated ~$1T, 59% of US originations, in 2024 (NMLS figures reported by the Virginia SCC); ~4,898 institutions of all types filed HMDA data for 2024 (CFPB)
**Tech Maturity:** Medium — the loan file lives in an origination system (Encompass holds roughly half the market by a STRATMOR estimate), Fannie Mae's and Freddie Mac's automated underwriting decides most credit, and pricing engines quote every lock, but income analysis, condition review, pipeline hedging, post-closing QC and repurchase rebuttals still run on underwriters' and secondary marketers' judgement in spreadsheets, PDFs and email.
**Workforce:** Loan officers in retail and consumer-direct channels, processors, underwriters (including senior and manual underwriters and condo reviewers), closers and funders, post-closers and shipping staff who chase trailing documents, lock desk and secondary marketing analysts, a capital markets head who runs the hedge with an outside advisor, QC auditors (often outsourced), compliance and HMDA staff, a treasurer managing several warehouse lines, and, where servicing is retained, a servicing or subservicer-oversight team

## Key Pain Themes
A mortgage lender borrows short to lend long and sells within weeks. It funds each loan on a warehouse line that advances 90–95% of the loan amount (FSOC), sells it to Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac, a Ginnie Mae pool or an aggregator, and earns par only if the file meets the buyer's guide. The judgement that decides whether it does is the underwriter's read of income. Fannie Mae's Selling Guide tells the lender to analyse "the stability of the borrower's income" and "the ability of the business to continue generating and distributing sufficient income"; it does not say how much decline is acceptable, and the answer is the approving underwriter's call. Income and employment were the largest category of critical defects in ACES's QC data, 27.24% of them in Q3 2025, against an overall critical defect rate of ~1.50% for the year. A Reggora and STRATMOR study put the average buyback at ~$32,288, with appraisal and income issues behind 57% of repurchase requests. The lender holds every half of that label (QC findings, agency defect notices, early payment defaults) and has never joined it to the underwriter decisions that produced it.

The work runs against clocks the lender does not set. A rate lock expires in 30–60 days (estimate), and the secondary desk must hedge the share of locks it expects to fund before it knows which will. Warehouse interest and haircuts accrue until an investor purchases the loan, so every missing trailing document or purchase exception costs money by the day. Fannie Mae's post-closing QC must select at least a 10% sample every month and finish selection, review, rebuttal and reporting within 90 days. A Notice of Potential Defect gives the lender 30 days to cure or respond. The HMDA register is due March 1, quarterly for large filers, and the NMLS Mortgage Call Report's loan activity section 45 days after each quarter.

The economics leave little room. Independent mortgage banks and bank mortgage subsidiaries earned $785 per loan in 2025 on $11,879 of revenue against $11,094 of cost, after $443 in 2024 and a $28 loss in the first quarter of 2025 (MBA). MBA expects 30-year rates above 7% through 2027 and volume falling from ~$2.1T in 2026 to just under $2T. Nonbanks now originate about two-thirds of mortgages and handle 66% of Agency servicing (FSOC), so the industry's capacity, and its staff, expand and contract with every rate cycle. Underwriters and post-closers hired in a refinance boom are laid off when it ends, and the judgement they built leaves with them.

## Current Tech Landscape
ICE's Encompass runs most loan files, with Dark Matter Technologies' Empower (sold to Constellation Software under the FTC-ordered divestiture) and MeridianLink behind it, and Blend and ICE's consumer apps at the front. Fannie Mae's Desktop Underwriter and Freddie Mac's Loan Product Advisor issue the credit decision; the DU validation service grants rep-and-warrant relief on income, employment and assets verified from Equifax's The Work Number, Truv, Argyle or bank-data providers, and Fannie says validating all four Day 1 Certainty components cuts repurchase risk by 64%. Ocrolus and LoanBeam extract and calculate income from documents. Pricing comes from Optimal Blue, Polly, ICE PPE and LoanPASS, and the hedge from advisors such as MCT, MIAC and Optimal Blue's CompassPoint. Wolters Kluwer, Docutech, DocMagic and IDS generate disclosures and closing packages; ACES Audit Technology runs QC workflows; ICE MSP and Sagent run servicing, often through subservicers. Each tool is competent within its boundary. The record no tool owns is the underwriter's reasoning: what income they accepted, what they doubted, what condition they set to resolve it, and what the loan then did.

## Analysis
The mortgage lender's central unmeasured judgement is the underwriter's decision that a documented income will continue: a read of tax returns, paystubs, deposits and the borrower's business, recorded as a single qualifying figure with the doubt stripped out. Automated underwriting decides whether that figure qualifies; it does not decide what the figure is, and the defect and repurchase data say that is where the losses sit. The lender holds both halves of the label, every income worksheet and condition in the origination system and every QC finding, defect notice and early default afterwards, and keeps them in different departments that never join them. What blocks a fix is structural (most lenders see too few repurchases a year to learn from alone, at a 0.49% buyback rate), organisational (operations staff turn over with every rate cycle, and senior underwriters resist being graded on calls the agencies later second-guess) and legal (borrower files are customer data under the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act and credit data under FCRA, so pooling them across lenders needs contracts most vendors do not have). The operator is a reasonable but cyclical buyer: a mid-size independent mortgage bank spends real money on technology in a boom and cuts it in a bust, and buys what its origination system vendor and the agencies' own tools already integrate. The strongest commercial openings sit beside it, and none clears the Pass 2 bar. The pipeline hedge advisors that already hold dozens of lenders' lock outcomes come closest, at 49, and the firms that review loan files for others (QC outsourcers and securitisation due diligence firms) score well but carry the borrower-data kill switch.

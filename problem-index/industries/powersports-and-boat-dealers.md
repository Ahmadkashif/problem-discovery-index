# Powersports & Boat Dealers

## Profile
**Category:** Retail & E-Commerce
**NAICS 2022:** 441222, 441227 — Boat Dealers; Motorcycle, ATV, and All Other Motor Vehicle Dealers
**Market Size:** ~$65.9B in 2022 receipts across 9,508 firms and 11,071 establishments: boat dealers took $24.2B (3,549 firms, 4,320 establishments), and motorcycle, ATV and all other motor vehicle dealers $41.7B (5,965 firms; the code also holds utility-trailer, golf-car and snowmobile dealers, and SUSB still tabulates it under its 2017 number) (Census SUSB 2022). New-unit demand fell in 2025: 215,237 new boats retailed, down 8.8% (NMMA), while new motorcycle and scooter sales fell 7.6% and ATVs 3.5% (MIC, via Powersports Business).
**Tech Maturity:** Medium-Low — every store runs a dealer management system (Lightspeed claims more than 4,500 dealers across five industries; DX1, Blackpurl, IDS and the marine system Dockmaster serve the rest) and logs into a separate portal for each OEM it carries, but the trade appraisal, the stocking order, the rigging checklist and the warranty write-up still live on paper, in phone photos and in the manager's head.
**Workforce:** 111,828 employees in 2022, about 10 per establishment (SUSB): dealer principals and general managers, sales managers and salespeople, F&I managers (at larger stores only), used-unit managers who appraise trades, service managers and service writers, flat-rate marine and powersports technicians, riggers and set-up technicians, parts and accessories counter staff, warranty administrators, title clerks and office managers, detail and lot staff

## Key Pain Themes
A powersports or boat dealer is a seasonal retailer carrying a year's inventory on borrowed money. New units are ordered months ahead of the season, often at the OEM's dealer meeting, and paid for by floorplan credit the day they ship: Brunswick Acceptance Company (49% Brunswick, 51% a Wells Fargo subsidiary) finances Brunswick's boat and engine dealers, Polaris Acceptance (a Polaris–Wells Fargo joint venture) most Polaris dealers, Harley-Davidson Financial Services every US and Canadian Harley dealer, and Northpoint, Huntington and others the rest. OEMs carry the interest for a while — MasterCraft reimburses dealer floorplan interest "generally ranging up to nine months" — and then the clock turns on the dealer: interest, then curtailments. OneWater Marine's 10-K states it plainly: "defined curtailments under the Inventory Financing Facility reduce the allowable advance rate as our inventory ages." When a season disappoints, as 2025 did, unsold units roll into a non-current model year that must be discounted, dealer-traded or carried. Polaris ended 2025 with North American off-road dealer inventory down about 9% and snowmobile dealer inventory down about 43% (Polaris 10-K).

Most of the gross profit sits outside the new-unit sale. At OneWater Marine (95 dealerships in 17 states), non-boat sales were 18.7% of revenue but 41.7% of gross profit in fiscal 2025. That money comes from a service department squeezed into a few weeks of spring commissioning and autumn winterization, staffed by technicians dealers can't find: 62% of powersports dealers surveyed in 2023 were short-staffed, 71% for technicians (Powersports Business). New boats need rigging, mating a hull from one OEM to an outboard from another, plus a trailer and electronics, before delivery. Warranty claims go into a different portal for each OEM line, and the parts counter looks up the same impeller across several catalogs with different part numbers.

Used units and finance close the loop. A trade-in boat's value turns on engine hours, compression, hull moisture and stringer condition that a walkaround can miss, and the used-unit manager prices it from memory and a guide. Financing runs long: Medallion Bank's average recreation loan at origination in 2025 was about $29,000 over about 15 years. Small stores often can't do F&I at all. Half of Medallion's recreation volume arrives through outside F&I service providers working for small dealers "that do not have the desire or ability to provide F&I services themselves". Each deal then needs titles and registrations, sometimes three per boat (hull, trailer and, in some states, the motor; recalled), inside each state's deadline.

## Current Tech Landscape
Lightspeed (sales, F&I, parts, service and accounting for powersports, marine, RV, trailer and golf dealers; launched an AI Command Center in March 2026 that scans dealer data for aging inventory and margin erosion), DX1, Blackpurl (cloud DMS relaunched as Blackpurl 2 in July 2025, search summary), IDS and Dockmaster run the store. Dealer Spike builds websites and sells PartSmart parts lookup, and claims 6,800+ dealers and 70+ OEM partnerships. Boat Trader, YachtWorld and boats.com (Boats Group) and CycleTrader carry listings. Octane (Roadrunner Financial), Sheffield, Synchrony, Medallion and the OEM captives finance retail deals; Ekho (raised $17.3M in July 2025) sells digital retail, DMV paperwork and an AI sales agent now used by National Powersports Distributors; Kenect sells texting; BoatBot, launched at MRAA Dealer Week in December 2024, targets marine service communication. J.D. Power's marine and powersports guides, NPA's auction-based Value Guide and BUC value used units; Statistical Surveys and Info-Link sell registration-based market share. On 25 October 2026 Octane had three software engineering roles open (Senior Software Engineer – Core Products, Irving TX, New York or remote, open since March 2026; Senior Software Engineer – Infrastructure, remote; Software Engineer II – Captive, remote), Ekho a Full-stack Engineer in New York City, and Dealer Spike five engineering roles, all in Mexico (remote) or Belmopan, Belize. Lightspeed had no software engineering openings (a Director of Security in South Jordan, Utah, and a database administrator in Montevideo, Uruguay), and Kenect none. No vendor had a forward deployed engineering role. Each tool holds one record. None holds the decision record: what the appraiser saw and what the unit later brought, what the manager ordered and what aged, how the warranty claim was written and why it was rejected.

## Analysis
The industry's central unmeasured judgement is the trade appraisal: what a used boat, bike or side-by-side is worth after the hours, the hull and the owner's habits are counted. The outcome is recorded precisely in the DMS (reconditioning cost, days to sale, final price) and is never joined back to what the appraiser saw. Three things block the fix. Condition is physical and the evidence (moisture readings, compression numbers, an engine-hour download, how the stringers sound under a hammer) is rarely written down. The data is split between each dealer's DMS, the floorplan lender's audits and the OEM portals. And the operators are small: 3,141 of 3,549 boat dealer firms and 4,834 of 5,965 motorcycle and other dealer firms have fewer than 20 employees. The tractable openings are mechanical and billed in dollars a dealer can count: warranty dollars paid on first submission, curtailments avoided, parts ordered right first time, finance income earned at stores with no F&I office. For the owner's placement goal the evidence is thin but real. Octane and Ekho were hiring US engineers to build digital retail and lending for these dealers, and Dealer Spike was hiring in Mexico and Belize. The largest DMS was not hiring engineers, and nobody had a forward deployed role, so the desk score's E = 3 holds only narrowly. For a research-insight engine the dealers are not buyers, and the Pass 2 sweep finds no qualifier around them.

# Sports Teams & Clubs

## Profile
**Category:** Arts, Entertainment & Recreation
**NAICS 2022:** 711211 — Sports Teams and Clubs (professional and semiprofessional teams earning from tickets, sponsorship and media rights)
**Market Size:** $44.4B of receipts across 1,285 firms and 1,372 establishments with 79,231 employees in 2022 (Census SUSB). The 74 firms with 500+ employees took ~60% of receipts and the 90 firms with 100–499 employees ~37%, so the big-league clubs are almost the whole industry; the 917 firms under 20 employees, mostly independent-league and semipro clubs, took ~1.4%. Payroll was $26.8B, ~60% of receipts, because player salaries sit inside it. The Service Annual Survey puts employer revenue at $41.6B for 2022, up from $26.6B in pandemic-hit 2020. League-level revenue across the NFL, MLB, NBA, NHL and MLS is ~$55–60B a year (estimate); the NFL's national revenue alone was ~$14.5B in 2025 (search summary)
**Tech Maturity:** Medium — big-league clubs run optical tracking, wearables, data warehouses and in-house R&D groups, but the commercial side runs on a CRM that sellers fill in late, sponsorship entitlements in spreadsheets, suite contracts in PDFs, and minor-league front offices that price seventy home dates once a season
**Workforce:** Players and coaches; general managers, scouts, analysts and video coordinators in baseball or basketball operations; athletic trainers and performance staff; ticket, premium and group sales reps, service reps and ticket operations managers; partnership sales, activation and fulfilment staff; marketing, content and broadcast staff; game-day operations crews, and, at college athletic departments run as businesses, revenue-share and NIL compliance officers

## Key Pain Themes
A club's most expensive decisions are about people it has not yet seen play at its own level. An area scout watches a high-school pitcher four times, writes grades on a 20–80 scale and a paragraph on "makeup" and "projection", and the club spends a signing bonus on that read. The outcome arrives four to seven years later, after the scout may have been let go, and nobody joins the report back to it. Tracking data from Hawk-Eye, TrackMan and charting vendors now measures what the body does; it does not record what the scout saw that the numbers missed. Teamworks bought PFF's enterprise business in March 2026, adding a platform it says is used by every NFL team and 240+ Division I programmes, which shows where vendors think the next decision layer sits.

The commercial side is under two clocks at once. Regional sports networks collapsed: Diamond Sports filed for bankruptcy in 2023 with rights to 42 teams, its successor stopped paying several MLB clubs, and by February 2026 MLB was producing local broadcasts for at least 13 clubs. Manfred says local media is "more than 20% of industry revenue", and each club now has to rebuild it as streaming and over-the-air reach. Sponsorship, the other big controllable line, is sold on exposure and renewed on faith. Sponsors' finance teams ask for proof that the jersey patch or naming right moved their business. Bain's 2026 survey of 1,397 executives found only 41% of marketers feel equipped to tie performance to business outcomes, and teams hold none of the sponsor's outcome data.

College athletics joined the business in July 2025. Under the House settlement, departments can pay athletes directly up to ~$20M a school in 2025-26, rising to $32.9M by 2034-35, and every third-party NIL deal over $600 goes through Deloitte's NIL Go. The College Sports Commission, with 29 staff, cleared more than $227M of deals in July and August 2026 alone. Athletic directors now run a capped payroll, a deal clearinghouse and the transfer portal at the same time.

## Current Tech Landscape
Ticketing comes from Ticketmaster, SeatGeek and, in college, Paciolan, with resale on StubHub and SeatGeek; the fan CRM and sponsorship layer from KORE (part of Two Circles since October 2024, with 118 of 154 big-five-league US teams as clients) and StellarAlgo on Salesforce or Dynamics. Sponsorship deal data comes from SponsorUnited, which says it has $45B of transacted deals behind its estimates, and exposure measurement from Relo Metrics, Nielsen Sports and KORE's Hookit. On the field, Hawk-Eye and Genius Sports' Second Spectrum supply tracking under league contracts, Catapult and Kinexon supply wearables (Catapult's revenue was US$140.7M for the year to March 2026), Hudl and Catapult supply video, and Teamworks ties scheduling, compliance, NIL and player personnel into one operating system. Each tool owns one slice. None of them keeps a scout's projection, a sponsor's renewal case or a game's price decision next to the outcome that later grades it.

Vendors selling to teams were hiring engineers on 2026-10-29: Teamworks (Software Engineer II, Frontend, remote US, posted 2026-08-14; Data Scientist II, Soccer, remote US, 2026-09-22), SeatGeek (Director of Engineering, Enterprise Product, New York or remote, 2026-09-11; Senior Machine Learning Engineer, first published 2025-09-12), whose roles span concerts as well as sports, and Genius Sports (Staff Software Engineer, Fan Engagement, New York, 2026-07-22). About 70% of Genius Sports' FY2025 revenue came from betting, so only its fan-engagement role counts here. KORE (Two Circles) and Relo Metrics showed no US engineering openings on their own boards, and StellarAlgo could not be checked (bot wall).

## Analysis
The industry's central unmeasured judgement is projection: the scout's read of what an amateur will become, and, on the business side, the ticket manager's and partnership seller's read of what a game or a sponsorship is worth. Outcomes are recorded in great detail (wins above replacement, scans, renewals, rights fees), but the reasoning that produced each bet is not kept in a form that can be graded. Three things block the fix. Labels arrive years late and through a development system that confounds them. The richest data is held under other parties' terms: tracking feeds under league contracts, athlete health data under collective bargaining agreements, and sponsor outcomes inside the sponsor's own business. And the decision-makers, general managers and scouting directors, are paid for their judgement and wary of scoring it. The teams themselves are poor buyers for an insight engine under this vault's rubric: they monetise insight through wins and ticket sales, not invoices, and the Pass 2 sweep finds no qualifier around them. The strongest commercial openings are software sold to clubs, in scouting memory, per-game pricing for the ~120 affiliated minor-league clubs, and sponsorship proof. The vendors that serve these clubs, Teamworks, SeatGeek and Genius Sports' fan-engagement arm, were the ones hiring.

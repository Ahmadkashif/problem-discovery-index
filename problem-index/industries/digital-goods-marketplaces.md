# Digital Goods Marketplaces

## Profile
**Category:** Digital Commerce
**Market Size:** ~$9B US creator-sold digital products — templates, assets, fonts, plugins, courses, presets and game content
**Tech Maturity:** Delivery solved, economics undefended — Gumroad, Envato, Creative Market, the Unity Asset Store, Epic's Fab, itch.io and Ko-fi handle payment, licensing and file delivery competently. The product can be copied perfectly at zero cost the moment it is sold, and no platform has a working answer to that.
**Workforce:** Content and copyright reviewers, creator support agents, payout and fraud operations staff, catalogue and discovery engineers, licensing and legal staff

## Key Pain Themes
A digital good is sold once and can then be redistributed infinitely at no cost, which means unauthorised sharing is not a leak but a direct substitution for sales. Platforms respond with takedown processes that fire after the fact and address the sites they know about. Licence terms are the second unsolved layer: an asset sold under a personal-use licence appears in a commercial product, a font licensed for one project is used across ten, and nothing in the delivery path expresses or checks the terms. Discovery is the third: creative asset search is genuinely hard because the query is aesthetic rather than lexical, and a creator whose work is not found does not earn. Behind all of it sit two roles absorbing the consequences — takedown reviewers adjudicating copyright claims at volume, and creator support agents explaining payout holds to people whose income has stopped.

## Current Tech Landscape
Payment, entitlement and file delivery are commoditised. Licence terms are expressed as prose in a purchase agreement and occasionally as a tier name, with no machine-readable representation and no enforcement in the delivery path. Digital watermarking and fingerprinting are mature in music and video and rarely applied to templates, fonts and design assets. Takedown processes follow the statutory notice-and-takedown pattern and are reactive by design. Discovery relies on creator-supplied tags and increasingly on visual embedding search, which is a genuine improvement. Generative tools have added a new and unresolved question about what may be sold and under what provenance.

## Analysis
These platforms sit between creators whose entire income depends on a good that costs nothing to copy and buyers who mostly comply. The unresolved question is not detection technology — fingerprinting works — but who bears the cost of enforcement, and the answer has been the individual creator, who is least equipped to bear it. The platforms hold the asset corpus, the sales record and the licence terms, which is everything an enforcement and verification layer would need, and have largely built takedown forms instead.

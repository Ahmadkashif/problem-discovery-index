# Psychiatric Hospitals

## Profile
**Category:** Healthcare & Clinical
**NAICS 2022:** 622210 — Psychiatric and Substance Abuse Hospitals (freestanding psychiatric hospitals owned by chains such as Acadia and UHS or run as joint ventures with health systems, inpatient detox and addiction hospitals, and privately operated forensic units, all paid under the Medicare IPF PPS and staffed around the clock by nurses; distinct psychiatric units inside general hospitals are a boundary case, named here but classed with general medical and surgical hospitals, and state-run psychiatric hospitals are context only)
**Market Size:** The 2022 Statistics of US Businesses counts 419 firms with 804 establishments, 245,003 employees and $32.0B of receipts in NAICS 622210, and the 195 firms with 500+ employees took ~81% of it. The Service Annual Survey puts 2022 revenue at $16.0B for private employer firms and $14.3B for government-owned hospitals, and the Quarterly Services Survey had the industry at $9.6B in Q2 2026 (seasonally adjusted). The two series count different populations. Medicare fee-for-service paid ~$2.4B to ~1,430 inpatient psychiatric facilities (IPFs, freestanding hospitals plus hospital units) in 2024 (MedPAC)
**Tech Maturity:** Low-Medium — every hospital runs an EHR and submits IPFQR measures to CMS, yet the 15-minute observation rounds are ticked on paper, continued-stay arguments are read to payer reviewers over the phone from notes, and involuntary-hold petitions are typed into court forms.
**Workforce:** Registered nurses, mental health technicians, psychiatrists (at Acadia generally independent contractors rather than employees), social workers and therapists, utilization review coordinators, intake staff and security. Acadia alone reported ~25,000 employees at 277 facilities with over 12,500 beds at the end of 2025; UHS ran 182 US inpatient behavioral facilities in February 2026 (10-Ks)

## Key Pain Themes
The decision that matters most on a psychiatric unit happens before anything goes wrong. An experienced charge nurse walks the hall at the start of a shift and knows which patient will need to be held down by midnight: the man pacing near the nurses' station, the woman who refused her evening dose and asked twice when her court date is, the teenager whose mother did not visit. She raises an observation level, moves a bed, offers medication early or asks a technician to sit with someone. When she reads it right nothing happens, and nothing is recorded. When she reads it wrong, CMS's public data records the hours of physical restraint and seclusion. The median facility reported 0.05 restraint hours per 1,000 patient hours in 2024, but the 90th percentile reported 0.63 and the worst 24.5 (IPFQR, 1,410 facilities). In 2017–2020 psychiatric and substance abuse hospitals recorded more than 100 violence injuries with days away from work per 10,000 workers, against 7–17 for the rest of the hospital subsector (AHRQ, using BLS data; search summary). The read lives in the nurse's head and leaves with her.

The money depends on proving, day by day, that the patient still needs a locked bed. Medicare pays a per diem ($912.40 base in FY2027) that falls from day 1 to day 10, and a physician must recertify the stay by day 12 and at least every 30 days after (42 CFR 424.14). Commercial plans and Medicaid managed care plans review each stay every one to three days by phone (estimate); Medicaid paid 57.7% of Acadia's 2025 revenue and commercial payers 24.6% (10-K). A utilization review coordinator reads the night's notes, finds the evidence of danger or failed medication trials, and argues for the next three days. The same days carry the opposite risk. Acadia disclosed a DOJ Criminal Division grand jury investigation, opened in 2024, into its acute service line's admissions, length of stay and billing practices, after settling an earlier OIG/DOJ investigation on medical necessity and length of stay for $19.9M (10-K). A stay cut short shows up in the 30-day readmission rate, whose facility median was 19.1% (IPFQR).

External clocks govern everything. State law sets the involuntary hold, commonly 72 hours, after which the hospital must petition a court or release the patient (estimate; it differs by state). A restrained patient must be seen face to face within one hour, adult orders expire after four hours, and a death in restraint must be reported to CMS by close of business the next business day (42 CFR 482.13). The FY2027 IPF PPS final rule, issued on 29 July 2026, adds a standardised IPF Patient Assessment Instrument (IPF-PAI), submitted through CMS's PARIT tool or a FHIR API: voluntary from 1 October 2027 and mandatory from 1 July 2028, when at least 50% of a facility's assessments must be complete, rising to 70% from 2030. For IPFs with 50 or more stays, it limits outlier payments to 20% of total IPF PPS payments for discharges in cost reporting periods beginning on or after 1 October 2027; CMS expects that to touch about 0.8% of providers in a typical year. It keeps the 2-percentage-point cut for missing IPFQR data (Federal Register 2026-15588).

## Current Tech Landscape
Freestanding hospitals run behavioral EHRs such as Netsmart and Qualifacts, detox hospitals often run Kipu, and hospital-based units use the parent's Epic or Oracle Health (named from recall). Payers apply InterQual or MCG behavioral criteria and, in some states, LOCUS or ASAM, and hospital UR coordinators assemble reviews by hand. Observation is still largely paper rounding sheets; AvaSure (search summary) and Oxehealth sell video observation, and Oxehealth's first US behavioral site opened in 2024 (search summary). Twenty-five states run crisis bed registries on their own software or on Bamboo Health, Carematch, CommCare or BH Link (NRI, January 2026). Quality vendors such as Medisolv abstract IPFQR measures (search summary). Revenue cycle AI vendors (AKASA, Candid Health, Trellis AI) sell to hospitals broadly, and Eleos Health sells documentation AI to behavioral providers. Each tool covers its own record. None joins the nurse's read of who will escalate, the observation level she set, the restraint that followed, the argument made to the payer for that day, and whether the patient came back within 30 days.

## Analysis
The industry's central unmeasured judgement is the unit's read of who is about to become dangerous, and it is tied to a second judgement that regulators and prosecutors now examine: whether each further day in a locked bed is medically necessary. Both are made by nurses and psychiatrists from patterns they cannot fully state, and neither is recorded as a prediction that can be checked. Three things block a fix. A correct read prevents the event that would have proved it. Every record is behavioral PHI, and detox records also fall under 42 CFR Part 2. And the record of a day's necessity is now evidence in investigations, so operators are wary of anything that scores it. The commercial opening is with the vendors hiring engineers to sell into hospitals, not the hospitals. AKASA and Candid Health, already described in [[problems/general-hospitals/ai-agents-platforms|🤖 General Hospitals AI Agents & Platforms]] and [[problems/community-health-centers/ai-agents-platforms|🤖 Community Health Centers AI Agents & Platforms]], still had forward deployed and AI engineering roles open on 2026-10-29 (AKASA in San Francisco and New York, published 3 August 2026; Candid in San Francisco, Denver and New York). Both sell revenue cycle work to hospitals in general, and Trellis AI, also hiring in San Francisco, works on medication access. Eleos Health's engineering roles were all in Tel Aviv, and Kipu and Perspectives Health showed no confirmable openings (see the agents note). The two-week MVP is a continued-stay review packet drafter on synthetic notes, paired with an IPFQR benchmark built from CMS's Provider Data Catalog (low-impact notes 1 and 2). Under the Pass 2 rubric the hospitals fail the gate: their insight is spent on care and payer arguments, never invoiced. No pocket qualifies, and only three pockets clear the gate, all behind kill switches; the highest is the chains' own corporate analytics (31), fenced by HIPAA and Part 2. The nearest clean pocket is already profiled: [[niches/health-insurance-carriers/medicare-health-policy-advisory/profile|🔍 Medicare & Health Policy Advisory Firms]] (46, below threshold), whose firm-wide teams do the IPF rule work that fails the gate as a psychiatric pocket of its own (see the niche overview).

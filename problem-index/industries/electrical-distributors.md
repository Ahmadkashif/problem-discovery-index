# Electrical Distributors

## Profile
**Category:** Wholesale & Distribution
**NAICS 2022:** 423610 — Electrical Apparatus and Equipment, Wiring Supplies, and Related Equipment Merchant Wholesalers
**Market Size:** ~$222.8B in 2022 receipts across 7,244 firms and 13,462 establishments (Census SUSB 2022); 192 enterprises with 500+ employees took ~$137.7B (~62%), while 5,881 firms under 20 employees (~81% of firms) took ~$23.4B. Electrical Wholesaling's narrower estimate of sales through electrical distributors is ~$151.2B, of which ~$87B goes to electrical contractors and ~$33.9B to industrial customers (2026 Market Planning Guide, search summary). EW estimates the top 10 distributors hold ~56% of sales (search summary).
**Tech Maturity:** Medium — the chains run Epicor Eclipse or SAP, IDEA product-data feeds, EDI with manufacturers and growing digital channels (Rexel North America's digital sales reached 27% of sales in the first half of 2026), but project quotes are still built by hand from plans and specs, SPA claims are reconciled in spreadsheets, and gear ship dates are chased across a dozen manufacturer portals.
**Workforce:** Counter salespeople, inside sales and quotation specialists, lighting and gear specialists, project managers and expediters, outside salespeople calling on contractors and plants, pricing and SPA analysts, purchasing agents, warehouse staff who cut wire from reels, and delivery drivers. BLS QCEW counted 199,357 private employees in 19,179 establishments in 2024, at ~$108,200 average pay.

## Key Pain Themes
An electrical distributor wins most of its money one project at a time. A contractor bidding a hospital wing or a data hall sends the plans, the Division 26 specification, a lighting fixture schedule and often a partial material list, days before the bid. Several distributors quote the same contractor, and the same distributor quotes several contractors chasing the same job. The quotation specialist has to turn that pile into a complete bill of materials — switchgear and panelboards, transformers, wire and cable, conduit and fittings, devices, and a lighting package priced through the manufacturer's agent — pick manufacturers the engineer will accept, request project pricing (an SPA) from each manufacturer so the number can win, and get it to the contractor before the bid closes. What the veteran adds that the contractor's list left out, and which substitution will survive the submittal, is learned over years and recorded nowhere. Win or loss is rarely recorded either: contractors seldom say.

Winning the quote starts a second clock. After award come submittals, release schedules and long-lead gear. Wood Mackenzie's Q2 2025 data put power transformers at an average of 128 weeks, and its August 2025 analysis estimated a 6% supply deficit for distribution transformers, whose unit costs had risen 78–95% since 2019; IDEA's ProjectNexus page says promised ship dates keep moving per order and that ~40% of inside-sales and project staff time goes to chasing dates (IDEA's own figure). The project manager in the middle relays every slip to a contractor whose schedule assumed the first date; on 13 May 2026 NAED picked an IDEA and Pull Logic joint venture to build ProjectNexus, an industry "Where's My Stuff?" platform. Margin depends on paper of two kinds. Manufacturers' special pricing agreements let the distributor sell a named job below its own cost and claim the difference back through ship-and-debit, the EDI 844 claim and 849 response, inside each agreement's window. And wire is priced off copper: the 50% Section 232 tariff on semi-finished copper and derivatives such as wire and cable took effect on 1 August 2025, and COMEX copper set a record settlement of ~$6.9/lb in September 2026 (search summary), so a wire quote held for a week can lose its margin. Graybar's gross margin fell from 20.1% to 19.3% in 2025 on competitive pricing.

The trade is concentrated at the top and personal at the counter. Wesco sold $23.5B in 2025, Graybar $12.9B and Rexel North America ~$10.4B (search summary), and Sonepar (€33.6B worldwide) has 571+ U.S. locations; on the other side, AD's Independent Electrical Supply Division, formed when IMARK Electrical's 725-odd members merged into AD in 2024, groups 700+ independents with $44B+ in sales (search summary). At 6:30 a.m. every branch counter faces a line of electricians who need the right breaker for a 40-year-old panel, and the counter veteran knows which classified breaker fits which loadcenter.

## Current Tech Landscape
Epicor Eclipse is the ERP built for electrical and plumbing distribution; Prophet 21, Infor CSD and SAP run elsewhere. IDEA, created by NEMA and NAED in 1998, runs the Industry Data Warehouse that feeds product and price data from hundreds of manufacturers (1,500+ brands) to 3,000+ distributor locations (search summary), and its new ProjectNexus targets order status. Ship-and-debit runs on EDI 844/849 and manufacturer portals, with rebate platforms such as Enable on the programme side. Contractor-side takeoff runs on Trimble Accubid, ConEst and newer AI takeoff tools. Parspec sells AI project quoting, BOM cross-referencing and submittal packages to electrical and lighting distributors, and said when it raised $20M in July 2025 that four of the five largest U.S. electrical distributors use it (company claim). The AI quoting and order-entry vendors that sell across distribution — Avent, Mercura, Comena, Hexa, Augment, Canals and Proton.ai — are profiled once, under [[niches/industrial-distributors/rfq-and-order-intake-automation/profile|RFQ & Order Intake Automation]]. On 2026-10-24, Avent had founding FDE and engineer roles open in San Francisco, Mercura and Comena had FDE and engineering roles open in Munich only, Canals had software and ML engineers open across the Americas (San Francisco, Toronto and Latin America), and Proton.ai's engineering roles were remote in Europe and Latin America only; Parspec, Augment and Prokeep listed no engineering roles. The chains are building their own: Sonepar announced €500M for AI in October 2026, and Border States uses AI for lead-time prediction. Each tool does its job. The record no tool owns is the quote decision: what the specialist added, substituted and priced, which SPA was requested, and whether the job was won.

## Analysis
The central unmeasured judgement in electrical distribution is the project quote: whether a bill of materials is complete, whether a substitution will pass the engineer's review, and what number wins. It decides most of the industry's revenue, and no distributor can say which of its quoters' habits carry information, because losses are rarely recorded and the reasoning lives in the quoter's head. Three things block fixing it. The knowledge is tacit and concentrated in a few specialists per region. The outcome is structurally hidden: the contractor, not the distributor, learns who won, and the manufacturer's SPA desk, which sees every distributor's request on the same job, uses that view only to approve prices. And the price itself is partly set upstream, by the manufacturer's SPA and the day's copper. The distributors are capable but margin-bound buyers, and as insight buyers the operators fail the Pass 2 gate. The strongest commercial opening is the vendor field selling into them, where quoting, submittal and order-entry tools compete on time-to-quote and where several firms are hiring engineers now. Whoever also captures the quote decision — additions, substitutions, SPA requests and inferred wins — as a by-product of quoting will hold the dataset this industry has never had.

# Document Preparation Services

## Profile
**Category:** Business Support & Waste Services
**NAICS 2022:** 561410 — Document Preparation Services
**Market Size:** ~$3.51B in 2022 receipts at 3,139 US employer firms (4,926 establishments, 39,631 employees, ~$1.17B payroll; 2022 Economic Census). County Business Patterns counts 4,375 employer establishments in 2023, 73% of them with fewer than five employees. Home-based freelance transcriptionists, resume writers and editors likely add ~$1–2B more (estimate). Transcripts of court proceedings sit in a different code (561492, ~$3.07B in 2022) and belong to [[industries/court-reporting-firms|Court Reporting Firms]]; this hub does not claim them.
**Tech Maturity:** Medium — speech recognition now drafts most transcripts and people correct them, but the typical shop still runs on a foot pedal, Express Scribe, Word templates, email and a quality-assurance sample checked by hand. Resume writers work from a phone interview and a Word file, and thesis formatters work from a university manual and a list of last term's rejections.
**Workforce:** Medical transcriptionists, now called healthcare documentation specialists and working mostly as speech-recognition editors (BLS counts about 42,000 jobs in 2025, 30% of them in administrative and support services, median pay $40,410, projected to shrink 4% by 2035); QA editors who score their work; general, legal and research transcriptionists, mostly home-based contractors paid per audio minute through platforms; evidentiary transcriptionists working on police, insurance and investigative recordings; resume writers and career-document specialists (some holding CPRW or NCRW credentials); academic editors and dissertation formatters; and the typists, word processors and desktop publishers who remain.

## Key Pain Themes
This industry sells accurate text, priced per 65-character line, per audio minute, per page or per document, and a machine now writes the first draft. In medical transcription a health system pays roughly $0.09–0.18 a line for transcription (vendor price guides), editors of speech-recognition drafts are paid ~3–5 cents a line (salary guides; one large vendor cut its rate from 4 to 3 cents in 2018), and quality is graded against AHDI's 2025 best practices. Those set 98.0 as the minimum score, give a critical error minus three points, and fail a report outright for one critical error under the common scoring method. The work left for transcription vendors is the dictation that front-end speech recognition and ambient scribes don't handle. Microsoft folded Dragon Medical One and DAX into Dragon Copilot in March 2025, and Nuance had already sold its transcription business to DeliverHealth in 2021 for about $29.8M. What remains is the hardest audio, the back-end edit and the quality score that decides both the vendor's service credits and the editor's pay.

Outside medicine the same squeeze arrives by the minute. Rev prices human-verified transcripts at $1.99 an audio minute against $0.25 for machine output, and trained its own recogniser on 200,000 hours of transcripts its human workforce produced. Every customer now asks which files actually need the human pass, and nobody can tell them before the file is edited. Evidentiary work is different in kind. Body-worn camera audio, interrogations, jail calls, 911 recordings and insurance recorded statements have to be transcribed verbatim with speakers attributed, often certified, before a discovery deadline or trial date. California's SB 524, in force since January 2026, now requires a disclosure and a preserved first draft whenever generative AI drafts a police report, which raises the value of a transcript whose uncertainty is marked honestly. The transcriptionists doing this work listen to violence for hours, and a grounded-theory study of transcriptionists found distress and no safeguarding protocol.

The document side runs to other clocks. Resume writers turn a phone interview into a resume, cover letter and LinkedIn profile for a flat fee. Since 27 September 2025 applicants for most federal jobs have had to fit their experience into OPM's two-page USAJOBS limit. Academic editors and formatters serve the candidates behind the 58,131 research doctorates US universities awarded in 2024 (temporary visa holders earned 38% of those in science and engineering), working against a graduate school's deposit deadline and its limits on how much third-party editing is allowed. Public universities must also bring web content, recorded lectures included, up to WCAG 2.1 AA by DOJ's Title II date, now 26 April 2027 for large entities.

## Current Tech Landscape
Medical work runs on front-end recognisers (Microsoft Dragon Copilot and Dragon Medical One, Solventum Fluency Direct), back-end speech-recognition editing platforms (eScription, now at DeliverHealth) and vendors' own QA tools, with the AHDI score sheet kept in spreadsheets. General transcription runs on platforms (Rev, GoTranscript, TranscribeMe, Verbit, 3Play Media), speech-to-text APIs (Deepgram, AssemblyAI, OpenAI Whisper, Rev's Reverb) and the transcripts built into Zoom, Teams and Otter, with Express Scribe and a foot pedal for the human pass. Resume writers use Word, questionnaire forms, Jobscan-style keyword matchers and builders such as Resume.io, and send drafts by email. Academic editors use Word tracked changes, PerfectIt, Zotero and EndNote, LaTeX templates and writing tools such as Paperpal and Writefull, while graduate schools run iThenticate before deposit. Each tool does its own step well. The record no tool owns is the edit itself: what the machine or the client wrote, what the expert changed, why, how severe the error was, and whether the finished document then worked — whether the report passed audit, the transcript held up in court, the resume won interviews or the thesis cleared format review the first time.

## Analysis
The industry's central unmeasured judgement is the correction: an experienced editor hears what a speaker meant where the machine heard something else, decides which errors matter, and flags what can't be known. Every correction is captured as a diff between draft and final, which makes this one of the few trades where the expert's judgement is logged automatically. The industry still throws the record away, scoring quality on a small random sample (~2–5% of reports, estimate) read by a second person who often disagrees with the first. Three things block fixing it. The economics are piece-rate, so nobody is paid to explain an edit. The audio belongs to the client, held under HIPAA business-associate agreements in medicine and under chain-of-custody rules for evidence, which limits pooling across accounts. And most operators are tiny: three establishments in four employ fewer than five people. The strongest commercial opening is selling routing, editing and severity-scored QA tools to the platforms and medical documentation vendors that hold the edit history at volume. The operators are poor buyers for a research-insight engine, though: they sell text, not insight, and the Pass 2 sweep finds no qualifying pocket around them. The nearest insight businesses are labour-market data vendors already indexed elsewhere in the vault and healthcare IT research firms that rate the recognisers replacing transcription.

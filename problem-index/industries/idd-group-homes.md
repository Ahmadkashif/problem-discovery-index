# IDD Group Homes

## Profile
**Category:** Healthcare & Clinical
**NAICS 2022:** 623210 — Residential Intellectual and Developmental Disability Facilities: Medicaid-certified Intermediate Care Facilities for Individuals with Intellectual Disabilities (ICF/IID) and the HCBS-waiver group homes that replaced most of them, where direct support professionals support two to fifteen residents around the clock
**Market Size:** ~$38B a year of facility revenue (estimate, derived from payroll and Medicaid spending below). In 2025, 26,070 establishments employed 496,846 people: 24,971 private sites with 420,745 employees paid an average $44,879 a year, plus 888 state-run and 211 local-government sites (BLS QCEW, NAICS 623210). Medicaid paid an average $159,520 for each of 60,264 people living in ICFs/IID in 2022, and $55,812 for each of 963,273 people with IDD on HCBS waivers in every setting (RISP). That year 301,155 people with IDD lived in group settings: 90,417 in homes of one to three people, 126,818 in homes of four to six, 49,311 in settings of seven to fifteen and 34,609 in settings of sixteen or more (RISP). Census put 2022 revenue at $59.2B for the wider NAICS 6232, which adds mental health and substance use facilities
**Tech Maturity:** Low–Medium — most agencies run an IDD record system for notes, incident reports and billing (Therap alone says it supports 15,000+ agencies in all 50 states), but inside the house the work still runs on paper behaviour-data sheets, pharmacy blister packs checked against a printed or tablet MAR, a whiteboard schedule and group texts for call-offs. Federal electronic visit verification excludes 24-hour congregate settings, so no system records who supported whom, when.
**Workforce:** 496,846 employed in NAICS 623210 in 2025 (BLS QCEW). Direct support professionals (DSPs) have no occupation code of their own; in the wider NAICS 6232, 293,550 home health and personal care aides, the code most DSPs fall under, earned a mean $16.83 an hour in May 2023, alongside 27,900 social and human service assistants, 21,260 residential advisors, 17,020 RNs, 12,140 LPNs and 12,170 social and community service managers (BLS OEWS). In the house: DSPs (many certified to pass medications), a house manager, the Qualified Intellectual Disabilities Professional (QIDP) or programme coordinator who writes and monitors each person's plan, a delegating nurse, and a behaviour analyst or psychologist, often contracted, who writes behaviour support plans

## Key Pain Themes
A group home is a household paid by the day. Typically four to six adults with intellectual and developmental disabilities share a house, the most common size in RISP's 2022 count, many non-verbal, many with epilepsy, swallowing difficulties or behaviour that can hurt themselves or others. Two or three DSPs cover each shift, and at least one is awake overnight (estimate). Medicaid pays a daily residential habilitation rate, tiered by each person's assessed support needs, and every billed day must be backed by documentation of the outcomes in that person's individual service plan. ICFs/IID add federal conditions of participation and active treatment, and must be surveyed at least every 15 months with a statewide average of 12 (42 CFR 442.109). The work never stops, so the binding constraint is labour. Across 3,936 agencies and 344,179 DSPs in 26 states and DC, the median DSP wage was $18.39 an hour in 2024 and the average turnover ratio 37%, ranging from 17% to 54% by state. Nearly two in three DSPs who left had worked there less than a year, and 26% of agencies turned away new referrals because they could not staff them (NCI State of the Workforce, 2024). A Sevita caregiver posting in south-central Pennsylvania offered $14.50–15.50 an hour (checked 2026-10-24). Vacancies are filled with mandatory overtime and double shifts.

The judgement that matters most is the long-tenured DSP's read of a resident who cannot say what is wrong. She notices that a man is pacing the way he did before his last assault on a housemate, or that a woman has eaten less, coughed after meals and slept through her favourite show. These are early signs of the conditions IDD services call the "Fatal Five": aspiration, dehydration, constipation, seizures and sepsis (Relias and ANCOR training material, search summary). That read lives in people who leave within a year, and it is rarely written down in a form anyone can learn from. When it fails, the cost is an emergency room visit, a restraint or a death. HHS OIG audits found that up to 99% of critical incidents in group homes went unreported to law enforcement or state agencies as required (OIG group homes page).

The external clocks are tightening. The 2024 Ensuring Access to Medicaid Services rule requires every state, from 9 July 2027, to define critical incidents (abuse, neglect, exploitation, misuse of restrictive interventions, medication errors that reach a poison centre or hospital, unexplained deaths) and to start, finish and correct at least 90% of investigations within state timeframes. From the same date states must reassess functional need and review the person-centred plan every 12 months for at least 90% of waiver participants. From 9 July 2028 they must report what share of habilitation payments reaches direct care workers, with facility-based services reported separately. The 80% pass-through minimum, effective 9 July 2030, covers homemaker, home health aide and personal care services, not residential habilitation (89 FR 40542). No proposed rule revising these provisions appears in the Federal Register as of 24 October 2026. Trade press reports a CMS proposal under OMB review that may rescind or revise the 80/20 requirement (search summary). Demand is rationed: about 649,900 people sat on Medicaid HCBS waiting or interest lists in 2026, 72% of them people with IDD, who waited 38 months on average (KFF, October 2026). The provider market is consolidating. Sevita closed its $835M purchase of ResCare Community Living from BrightSpring on 31 March 2026, after the FTC required 128 ICFs to be sold to Dungarvin (Behavioral Health Business; Home Health Care News reports 126).

## Current Tech Landscape
Agency record systems split into IDD specialists and human-services generalists. Therap is the IDD specialist: notes, general event reports, MAR, billing, and EVV for states including Alaska, Delaware, Nebraska and the Dakotas. MediSked, majority-owned by CaseWorthy (search summary), SETWorks and iCareManager compete with it, and newer AI-first entrants include Kibu (480+ organisations in 48 states, by its own count) and Statewise (150+ providers in 38 states, vendor claim). Generalists include Netsmart myEvolv and Foothold AWARDS. EVV aggregators Sandata and HHAeXchange matter only for an agency's in-home and supported-living services. DSP training comes from Relias and the College of Direct Support, and long-term care pharmacies publish DSP guides. Remote-support vendors (Sengistix, SimplyHome) replace some awake overnight staff with sensors and a remote monitor. Kibu and iCareManager are the IDD vendors hiring engineers this month, and Netsmart is hiring at scale in Overland Park and Bengaluru (see the source log). No tool joins five records: what the DSP noticed on shift, what the behaviour plan predicted, who was working, the incident or ER visit that followed, and the investigation's finding.

## Analysis
The industry's central unmeasured judgement is the DSP's read of a person who cannot report pain, fear or illness. It decides whether a behavioural crisis is defused, whether a bowel obstruction or aspiration pneumonia is caught a day early, and whether a restraint is ever needed. The structure blocks the fix more than the technology does. Two in three leavers go within a year, so the experienced readers are always outnumbered by new ones. Documentation is written at the end of a double shift to satisfy billing, not to capture observation. Every record is protected health information held by the agency, and often by the state where it runs the system. Rates are fixed by states, so agencies buy only what pays back in billable days kept, overtime cut, or citations and recoupments avoided. Operators are therefore poor buyers for anything speculative. The strongest openings are three. One is documentation that turns a DSP's spoken shift report into ISP-mapped notes, the work Kibu and iCareManager are hiring engineers to build. Another is incident management sold to providers and to state agencies facing the July 2027 Access Rule deadlines. The third is staffing tools that cut mandated overtime. The Pass 2 sweep finds no qualifying insight pocket. The analysis around the industry is commissioned by states on data they own, held as clients' records by vendors and reviewers, or done inside providers that invoice nothing, much as in [[industries/home-health-agencies|Home Health Agencies]] and [[industries/skilled-nursing-facilities|Skilled Nursing Facilities]].

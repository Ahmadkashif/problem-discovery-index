# Health Insurance Carriers

## Profile
**Category:** Insurance
**NAICS 2022:** 524114 — Direct Health and Medical Insurance Carriers (commercial group and individual medical, Medicare Advantage and Part D, Medicaid managed care, dental and other health plans written on the carrier's own paper)
**Market Size:** ~$1.37T of 2025 direct written premium across 1,156 insurers filing the NAIC health statement, with net earnings of just $6B (0.4% margin) on a 90.3% loss ratio (NAIC 2025 annual health industry report). Medicare is 37% of premium and Medicaid 26%; 35.2M people (55% of those eligible) are in Medicare Advantage in 2026 (KFF), 66.5M Medicaid enrollees were in risk-based MCOs in July 2024 (KFF), and 23.1M chose Marketplace plans for 2026 (CMS)
**Tech Maturity:** Medium — claims auto-adjudicate at high rates on core platforms and actuarial models are sophisticated, while prior authorization still arrives by fax and portal upload, provider rosters move as emailed spreadsheets, and HEDIS chart review is a seasonal army of nurses reading PDFs.
**Workforce:** 430,460 employed in NAICS 524114 (BLS OEWS, May 2023): member services representatives (70,860), utilization management and care management nurses (29,240 registered nurses), claims processors and examiners (45,350 combined), provider network and contracting staff, risk adjustment coders, medical directors, actuaries (2,730), data scientists (5,310), compliance officers (6,310), and Stars, quality and appeals teams

## Key Pain Themes
A health plan's margin is the gap between a premium set a year in advance and claims it cannot see until they arrive. That gap was $35 per member per month across the industry in 2025, and utilization ran above every plan's pricing for the second year in a row. Most of the judgement that protects it is clinical and rarely recorded. A utilization management nurse reads a 40-page faxed packet and knows in two minutes whether the knee replacement is approvable or whether the post-acute stay can safely end on Friday (estimate). Medicare Advantage insurers made 52.8M prior authorization determinations in 2024 and denied 7.7%. Only 11.5% of denials were appealed, but 80.7% of those appeals succeeded (KFF). Plans therefore cannot tell which reviewers, criteria and denial reasons hold up, and which are simply rarely challenged.

The clocks are federal and immovable. CMS-0057-F cut decision times to 72 hours (expedited) and seven calendar days (standard) from January 2026, required public prior authorization metrics from March 2026, and requires a prior authorization API from January 2027. MA bids are due the first Monday in June. Star Ratings, released each October (the 2027 ratings on 8 October 2026), move MA quality bonus payments worth ~$10B+ a year (estimate). HEDIS medical record review runs from January to the June submission. Risk adjustment is now audited at scale: CMS said in May 2025 it would audit every eligible MA contract each year and grow its coding staff from ~40 to ~2,000. In the ACA market, enhanced premium tax credits expired at the end of 2025, median exchange premiums rose ~20%, and plan selections fell 5% to 23.1M.

The third theme is data the plan does not control. Directories must match what provider offices say on the phone, but providers change locations faster than they update rosters. Medicaid MCOs answer to dozens of different state contracts, and their encounter data and quality results are reviewed by state-contracted reviewers due each 30 April. And appeals, grievances and enrollment discrepancies are worked case by case against CMS clocks that a program audit will test.

## Current Tech Landscape
Core administration runs on HealthEdge, Cognizant TriZetto (Facets, QNXT) or home-grown platforms, with Medicaid states on Gainwell and other MMIS systems. Claim editing runs on Lyric (the former ClaimsXten), Optum and Cotiviti content. Utilization management uses MCG and InterQual criteria inside care management platforms such as GuidingCare and Jiva, with specialty benefit managers (Evolent, Carelon, EviCore) and newer prior authorization vendors (Cohere, Availity). Risk adjustment relies on chart retrieval and coding vendors (Datavant, Episource, Reveleer, Cotiviti) and in-home assessment programs. HEDIS engines from Inovalon and Cotiviti calculate measures that NCQA-licensed auditors validate. Provider data sits in CAQH ProView, Quest Analytics network adequacy tools and plan MDM systems. Every tool works within its boundary. None owns the record that joins a clinical determination to its reasoning, its appeal outcome and what happened to the member afterwards.

## Analysis
The industry's central unmeasured judgement is the medical-necessity determination: the decision that this MRI, this inpatient day or this skilled nursing stay is covered. It is made tens of millions of times a year by nurses and medical directors applying licensed criteria to unstructured records. The outcome that would grade it, whether an appeal overturned it and whether the member was harmed or readmitted, sits in other systems and is rarely joined back. Three things block a fix. Regulators and courts are hostile to automated denials: CMS requires individualised decisions, and California's SB 1120 bars algorithm-only denials. The label is sparse, because almost nine in ten denials are never appealed. And every record is protected health information inside the carrier's walls. The opening is on the approval side. A model that approves faster what an experienced reviewer would approve on sight, and grades denials against appeal outcomes, meets the new clocks without automating refusal. Carriers are strong buyers of utilization, risk adjustment, quality and interoperability tooling, because CMS dates every one of those budgets. They are poor buyers of a research-insight engine under the Pass 2 rubric. Their insight is priced into premiums and bids, never invoiced, and HIPAA governs every byte of it. The firms that do invoice for payer insight mostly work on the carriers' own claims. No pocket qualifies in this industry's sweep (see the niche overview).

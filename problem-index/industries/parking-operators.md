# Parking Operators

## Profile
**Category:** Specialty Services
**NAICS 2022:** 812930 — Parking Lots and Garages (operators running lots, garages and valet under management or lease contracts)
**Market Size:** $10.7B of receipts across 2,777 firms and 11,944 establishments with 121,135 employees in 2022 (Census SUSB). The 43 firms with 500+ employees took ~58% of receipts, and the 2,235 firms under 20 employees ~13%. Receipts undercount the money the industry handles. At a managed facility the operator books only its fee: SP+ reported $883.2M of net services revenue for 2023 plus $899.1M of reimbursed management revenue, and its gross collections for owners are not reported at all. Gross parking revenue collected at operator-run facilities is ~$25–35B a year (estimate)
**Tech Maturity:** Medium — the nationals are moving to gateless camera entry, app payment and marketplace channels, but most facilities still run on PARCS gate equipment, monthly-parker spreadsheets, hang tags, validation stamps and an area manager who sets the rate board by walking past it.
**Workforce:** Cashiers, booth and lot attendants, valet runners and captains, facility and area managers, revenue-control and accounting staff who produce owner statements, monthly-account and permit coordinators, enforcement and notice-processing staff, remote intercom and call-centre agents, maintenance porters, and, at the tech-led operators, computer-vision, data and software engineers

## Key Pain Themes
A parking operator sells the same space many times a day at prices it can change hourly, and mostly sets those prices by feel. The area manager knows the office garage fills by 8:40 on Tuesdays and empties by 4, that a Thursday home game pulls cars from three blocks away, and that the surface lot across the street dropped its event rate to $25. The manager adjusts the board, posts an event rate and moves monthly parkers to the roof. None of that reasoning is written down, and the outcome sits in PARCS entry and exit counts nobody joins back to it. Owners have started measuring the result. Mobile Infrastructure Corporation, a listed owner of 36 garages, reports revenue per available stall (RevPAS) and converted its leases to management contracts in 2024 to "align the incentives" with operators. Its 10-K tells the operators to "actively manage parking rates based on local insights."

Most facilities run under management contracts, about 88% of SP+'s commercial locations in 2023. Under those contracts the operator collects the owner's money, pays expenses from it and earns a fee, so the owner's question every month is whether every dollar arrived. The owner's statement is assembled from PARCS transactions, app and marketplace payouts, monthly-account billing, validations, credit-card settlements and the payroll that was charged back. Owners audit it on their own schedule. Arlington County's 2026 audit of its SP+ garage contract, on ~$3.6M of 2025 revenue, found living-wage documents missing since Q3 2023.

Enforcement is now camera-based, and that has brought the law in. When a car leaves without paying, a plate-reader system matches entry and exit, pulls the registered owner from state motor-vehicle records and mails a notice. Federal Driver's Privacy Protection Act suits have followed, including Crostarosa v. LAZ Parking, Asura and PRRS in Colorado, where a January 2026 order sent the claim against the notice processor to arbitration but kept LAZ and Asura in court. Maine's 2025 chapter 350 requires posted reasonable fees, a written notice with at least 10 business days to pay, and treble damages for violations. It also applies the state statute that bars automated plate recognition at lots open to the public, subject to that statute's exceptions. Each state's notice rules now decide whether an unpaid session can be recovered at all.

## Current Tech Landscape
PARCS gate equipment and cloud platforms come from FLASH, Amano McGann, SKIDATA, TIBA and HUB. Metropolis runs a computer-vision "checkout-free" platform across the 4,000+ locations it acquired with SP+ and reports nearly 20 million members. AirGarage runs unattended lots on cameras and app payment. Reservation and event demand comes through SpotHero, ParkWhiz and Arrive's ParkMobile, and Parkopedia supplies price and availability data to car makers. Permits and enforcement run on T2, Passport and, for apartments and mixed-use buildings, Parkade. Notice processing is sold by specialists such as PRRS, and EV charging is added by networks under site-host deals. Cities publish open meter data: Seattle's paid-occupancy feed carried 13.4 million rows for the last 30 days on 2026-10-28, and SFMTA moves meter rates $0.25 a quarter on an 80%/60% occupancy rule. Each tool records one part of a parking session. No tool joins the rate the manager chose, what the manager expected to happen, the cars that came and the revenue the owner received.

## Analysis
The industry's central unmeasured judgement is the experienced manager's read of demand: how fast a garage will fill, how far an event rate can rise before cars go elsewhere, and which monthly accounts can be oversold. Rates are posted, cars are counted at the gate and revenue is settled to the cent, but the reasoning that linked them is never recorded, so it leaves with the manager. Three things block the fix. At the 88% of sites under management contracts the transaction data is the owner's revenue, which limits how an operator can pool it. Plate data is now regulated state by state, through Maine's 2025 law, California's ALPR statute and a run of DPPA suits. And most of the 2,235 small operators cannot pay for the camera and PARCS stack that would capture the data. Under this vault's rubric the insight layer around parking is thin. The Pass 2 sweep finds no qualifier. The nearest pockets are price-data vendors and the operators' own analytics teams, and parking's yield-pricing problem is already scored in its closest analogues, [[niches/hotels-boutique/centralized-revenue-management-services/profile|hotel centralized revenue management]] (50, kill switch) and [[niches/property-management/revenue-management-rent-pricing/profile|rent pricing systems]] (47). Both predate this run's clock rulings: the hotel pocket takes Q4 5 on daily pricing (~44–46 under the ruling) and rent pricing Q4 3 on self-run daily pricing (~45), logged for Stage E. The commercial opening is on the operator side, where three firms were hiring engineers on 2026-10-28 (source log). Metropolis lists Senior AI Engineers (Los Angeles, Seattle, New York; first posted 2026-04-21), a Senior Machine Learning Engineer, Computer Vision (Seattle, 2026-05-20), Senior Staff Software Engineers on its Recognition Platform (2026-08-26) and a Senior Software Engineer, AI Agentic Platform (Seattle, 2026-10-07). SpotHero lists a Senior Data Engineer I (Chicago, 2026-09-18). Parkade lists a Staff Front End Software Engineer (remote, San Francisco interviews, 2026-10-15). AirGarage and FLASH were not confirmed. The clearest two-week MVP is an hourly occupancy forecast and rate recommender trained on Seattle's paid-occupancy feed and SFMTA's quarterly rate changes, paired with an open-source plate-reader demo, run on consented or synthetic images with plates deleted after matching, that turns entry and exit images into parking sessions.

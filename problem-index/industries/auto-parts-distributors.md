# Auto Parts Distributors & Stores

## Profile
**Category:** Wholesale & Distribution
**NAICS 2022:** 423120, 423130, 441330 — Motor Vehicle Supplies and New Parts Merchant Wholesalers; Tire and Tube Merchant Wholesalers; Automotive Parts and Accessories Retailers
**Market Size:** ~$371.7B in 2022 receipts across 23,723 firm counts and 53,664 establishments (Census SUSB 2022: 423120 had 7,440 firms and $226.0B; 423130 had 1,130 firms and $58.1B; 441330, still tabulated under its 2017 code 441310, had 15,153 firms and $87.6B). Enterprises with 500+ employees took ~$269.7B (~73%), a class that includes vehicle makers' parts-distribution arms and the chains' distribution centres; ~20,700 firm counts under 20 employees took ~$36.2B. For scale, the Auto Care Factbook put U.S. light-duty aftermarket sales at $413.7B in 2024 (search summary), of which parts distribution is one channel.
**Tech Maturity:** Medium — the national chains run proprietary electronic catalogs, hub-and-spoke inventory and shop ordering sites at scale, while the typical jobber store still takes most shop orders by phone, resolves fitment in a counter person's head, and reconciles cores and warranty credits on paper.
**Workforce:** Counter professionals (parts salespersons; ~273,300 nationally in 2025, BLS, search summary), commercial delivery drivers, outside sales reps calling on repair shops, store, hub and branch managers, warehouse pickers, returns and core clerks, and catalog, pricing and category analysts at the larger houses. BLS QCEW counted ~610,500 private employees in the three codes in 2024 (178,871 in 423120 at ~$75,300 average pay; 34,437 in 423130 at ~$72,900; 397,189 in 441330 at ~$40,600).

## Key Pain Themes
The industry lives on one phone call. A technician has a 2014 pickup on the lift with the wheels off; the service writer calls, texts or clicks the parts store for "front pads and rotors." The counter professional decodes the VIN or asks year, make, model and engine, and the catalog returns three rotors: with and without the heavy-duty brake package, a one-piece and a two-piece design, and a production split partway through the model year. The experienced counter pro knows which one, from the shop's history, a look at the build sheet or a memory of last month's return. The part leaves on a hot-shot run inside ~30 minutes (estimate). If it is wrong, the car sits on the lift, a second run goes out and the shop's first call drifts to whoever gets it right next time. The chains fight that contest with inventory near the bay: AutoZone ended fiscal 2026 with 172 mega hubs carrying ~80,000–110,000 SKUs, and O'Reilly runs 399 hubs averaging 63,000 SKUs behind 6,447 U.S. stores.

Behind the counter sit three paper flows. Cores (starters, alternators, calipers, steering racks, batteries) carry a deposit that must be matched back to a returned unit and then credited by the supplier; warranty returns and supplier credits follow the same path, and Advance took a $28M charge in 2025 on receivables from an aftermarket supplier that filed Chapter 11, a reminder that credits owed by suppliers are real money at risk. Assortment is the second flow: with the average light vehicle a record 12.8 years old across 289 million vehicles (S&P Global Mobility), each store must pick ~20,000–25,000 SKUs from hundreds of thousands to match the vehicles around it. Catalog data is the third: every application is coded against the Auto Care Association's licensed VCdb (ACES 5.0 and PIES 8.0 were released on 2 April 2026), and conflicting supplier files land on the distributor to reconcile.

External clocks press in. Section 232 duties of 25% on non-USMCA covered parts took effect in May 2025 (search summary), with Commerce opening inclusion windows each January, April, July and October to add more parts; the Supreme Court invalidated IEEPA tariffs in February 2026, and AutoZone booked $96.0M of refunds. The federal REPAIR Act was narrowed in committee to codify the 2014 data-access agreement (search summary). Structure is shifting: Advance closed 522 stores in 2025, Genuine Parts bought 50+ businesses (mostly independent NAPA stores) and will separate its industrial arm in the first quarter of 2027, and American Tire Distributors went through a second Chapter 11 and lost Michelin as a supplier (search summary). In the AAPEX 2026 survey, attracting skilled talent was among the top three challenges, named by 36% of respondents, second only to uncertainty (45%); of the 21% already implementing enterprise AI, 60% named customer service as an investment area (respondents could pick several).

## Current Tech Landscape
Fitment runs on the Auto Care databases (VCdb licences cost $3,150–$12,075 a year), supplier catalog files and content from Epicor and MOTOR; the chains add proprietary catalogs such as AutoZone's Z-net and O'Reilly's point-of-sale catalog. Independent distributors run business systems from Epicor, MAM and Autologue. Shops order through e-catalog networks: WHI's Nexpart (370,000 active buyers and 43,000 seller locations by its own description; search summary), PartsTech, an OEC company connecting shops to 30,000+ parts and tire distributors, and OEC's RepairLink for dealer OEM parts. AI is arriving from two directions. Partly, an auto-specific parts-identification vendor headquartered in Austin, claims its Interpreter model is trained on 1B+ parts and was hiring a Staff/Senior Staff Software Engineer (posted 2026-08-11) and a Principal Product Engineer (2026-10-20) in Austin. Three other AI vendors near the order desk were hiring on their own boards on 2026-10-28, none of them auto-specific: Endeavor, which builds custom AI for manufacturing, distribution and supply-chain clients and names no auto customer (Forward Deployed Engineer, Early Career, Chicago, 2026-07-07); SalesPatriot, an adjacent quoting and procurement platform for aerospace, electronics, industrial and defence supply chains (Forward Deployed Engineer, 2026-05-25, and Lead Forward Deployed Engineer, 2026-10-10, both San Francisco); and Paragon, an industrials AI start-up whose only open engineering role is a Founding Engineer (San Francisco, undated; the forward deployed engineer title is gone). The general distributor order-intake field is profiled once under [[niches/industrial-distributors/rfq-and-order-intake-automation/profile|RFQ & Order Intake Automation]]. Each tool does its job. The record no tool owns is the fitment resolution: what the shop asked for, which candidates the catalog returned, which part went out, and whether it stayed on the car.

## Analysis
The central unmeasured judgement in auto parts distribution is fitment resolution: choosing the one part that fits when the catalog returns several, and knowing when the catalog itself is wrong. It decides whether the shop calls back, and it is answered millions of times a day without a record that joins the lookup to the outcome. Three things block fixing it. The reference data is licensed and fragmented: the VCdb is sold by subscription, supplier files disagree, and VIN decoders such as NHTSA's vPIC stop short of the option-level attributes that split candidates. Outcome data is split: returns are coded loosely and rarely joined to the lookup that produced them. And the richest lookup logs sit inside four national chains that treat them as competitive assets. As insight buyers the operators fail the Pass 2 gate: the chains' analytics serve their own pricing and assortment, and the independents are too small to staff any. The strongest commercial opening is the vendor field selling into the counter, where order-intake and parts-identification vendors compete on how many shop orders they match to the fitting part. Of the vendors hiring engineers now, only Partly (Austin) is auto-specific; Endeavor is distributor-wide with no auto customer named, SalesPatriot serves aerospace and defence, and Paragon is hiring a founding engineer only. Whoever captures the fitment-return record as a by-product of order intake will hold the dataset the trade has never kept.

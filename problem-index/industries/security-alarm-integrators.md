# Security Alarm Integrators

## Profile
**Category:** Business Support & Waste Services
**NAICS 2022:** 561621 — Security Systems Services (except Locksmiths): alarm dealers that sell, install and service intrusion, fire, access-control and video systems, and the central stations that monitor them
**Market Size:** $29.67B in 2022 receipts across 5,761 firms and 7,042 establishments employing 125,873 people (Census SUSB 2022); the 65 firms with 500+ employees take 57% of receipts, while 4,980 firms (86%) have fewer than 20 employees. ADT alone reported $5.1B of 2025 revenue and $359M of recurring monthly revenue (RMR)
**Tech Maturity:** Medium — panels, communicators and interactive platforms are thoroughly connected (Alarm.com counts 170 million devices), but the judgements that decide money and police response, such as dispatch-or-cancel, attrition risk and whether a fault is fixed, still sit in operators' and technicians' heads
**Workforce:** Security and fire alarm technicians and installers (83,540 nationally in May 2023, 52,480 of them in investigation and security services, a BLS group that also counts guard, investigation and locksmith firms), central-station operators working UL 827 shifts, NICET-certified fire alarm inspectors, door-to-door and inside residential sales reps, commercial project managers and system designers, account managers handling RMR retention, and dealer-programme and acquisition staff who buy accounts

## Key Pain Themes
The industry sells a promise that someone will act when a sensor trips, and most of the time nobody needed to. Between 94% and 98% of dispatched burglar alarms are false, by the Justice Department's COPS Office count. Cities have answered with false-alarm fines, permit registration and, increasingly, verified response. Seattle stopped sending officers to unverified alarms on 1 October 2024, after Salt Lake City had cut its alarm dispatches by about 95% two decades earlier. The central-station operator sits in the middle. Each signal gets a few seconds of judgement: a door contact at 3 a.m. on a windy night, an entry-delay trip followed by interior motion, a customer who always fumbles the code at 6:10. The operator places the enhanced-call-verification calls, reads any video clip, and decides whether to dispatch. TMA's ANSI-accredited AVS-01 standard now asks stations to stamp that judgement as a score from 0 to 4 and send it to the 911 centre. More than 150 cities receive alarms over ASAP, the station-to-dispatch link, which carries the score. The scoring is the most consequential decision in the business, and today it rests on an operator who may have been hired six weeks ago.

The money is recurring monthly revenue, and it leaks. ADT's gross revenue attrition was 13.1% in 2025, and the industry values an account at a multiple of its RMR, roughly 30–55× by advisers' estimates. That makes every move-out, non-pay and competitor takeover a capital loss. Dealer programmes and rollups are the buyers. ADT spent $596M on dealer-generated accounts and bulk purchases in 2025, and Pye-Barker bought 57 fire and security companies in the same year. Each underwrites portfolios whose attrition it cannot see until chargeback windows close. On the commercial side, integrators win access-control and video projects against consultants' specifications. They then fight to keep the service agreement and the hosted subscription, which is where the margin is.

Fire alarm work adds a hard external clock. NFPA 72 sets inspection, testing and maintenance frequencies device by device, and fire marshals increasingly require the reports through third-party portals such as Brycer's The Compliance Engine. A missed annual test or an unrepaired deficiency is a code violation the AHJ can cite. Meanwhile copper POTS retirement and cellular network sunsets force communicator swaps across whole account bases, each one a truck roll and a retention risk.

## Current Tech Landscape
Central stations run automation platforms such as Bold Technologies' Manitou, Dice, SIMS and MASterMind, with video handled in Immix or vendor portals. Residential and small-commercial dealers resell interactive services from Alarm.com, Resideo's Total Connect and Johnson Controls' Qolsys, and run billing and service on SedonaOffice, Bold's or Alarm.com's dealer tools. Commercial integrators install Lenel, Genetec, Software House, Brivo and Verkada access control, and Avigilon, Axis and Hanwha cameras. Fire alarm ITM runs on Inspect Point, BuildingReports or field-service suites. Each holds its piece well: the signal log, the device telemetry, the job ticket, the inspection report. No system holds the joined record of signal, operator disposition, verified outcome, technician fix and account lifetime. That record is the one that would say which alarms are real, which accounts will leave and which repairs actually worked.

## Analysis
The industry's central unmeasured judgement is whether an alarm is real. Every station makes that call millions of times a year, and the outcome almost never comes back. Police dispositions return rarely and late. Customer cancellations are recorded but seldom audited. And the operator who made a good call never learns it was good. Verified-response ordinances and AVS-01 scoring are turning that private judgement into a public, auditable score that decides whether police come at all. That is a strong reason to capture and grade it now. What blocks the fix is structural. Station data is spread across roughly a thousand monitoring centres on a handful of automation platforms. Dealers own the customer relationship while wholesale stations own the signal log. Video belongs to the subscriber. The commercial opening is therefore with the stations and platforms that see the most signals and can be held to a measured false-dispatch rate. Large dealers and acquirers are a second opening, for attrition and portfolio diligence, because RMR valuation turns better forecasts directly into capital. The 4,980 small dealers are poor direct buyers of anything analytical. They buy through their wholesale station or their platform, or not at all.

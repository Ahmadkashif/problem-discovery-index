# Trust Companies

## Profile
**Category:** Financial Services
**NAICS 2022:** 523991 — Trust, Fiduciary, and Custody Activities (bank trust offices, non-depository trust companies, securities custodians, escrow and fiduciary agencies except real estate). Most bank trust departments and the custody banks file their establishments under commercial banking, so this hub covers the fiduciary and custody activity wherever it is chartered; the lending and deposit side of the same banks sits in [[industries/community-banks|Community Banks]].
**Market Size:** ~$55.6B of fiduciary activities income in 2025 at FDIC-insured institutions on ~$45.7T of fiduciary and related assets (FDIC Call Report data, Schedule RC-T, December 2025); custody runs larger still, with BNY at $59.3T, State Street at ~$53.8T and Northern Trust at ~$18.7T in assets under custody or administration at end-2025; outside deposit insurance, South Dakota alone charters 115 trust companies holding nearly $815B (2024). The NAICS code itself counts only 2,982 private establishments and 21,735 employees (BLS QCEW, 2024), because most of the work is filed under bank establishments.
**Tech Maturity:** Medium — positions, income, settlement and statements run on mature trust accounting and custody platforms, while the fiduciary decisions on top of them (whether to make a discretionary distribution, how to read a forty-year-old instrument, what a farm or family company held in trust is worth, how an indenture treats a default) are still made from scanned documents, memos and the memory of senior officers.
**Workforce:** Personal trust officers and trust administrators who own beneficiary relationships, investment officers and portfolio managers, fiduciary tax specialists, special asset managers for real estate, farms, closely held businesses and mineral interests, trust operations staff posting income, corporate actions and distributions, custody and asset-servicing operations at the custody banks, corporate trust relationship managers and default administrators, account acceptance and KYC staff, fiduciary compliance and audit, and the trust or fiduciary committee of senior officers that approves exceptions and discretionary decisions

## Key Pain Themes
A trust company sells a promise to exercise someone else's judgement for decades. The instrument was drafted by a lawyer for a grantor who may be long dead; it tells the trustee to distribute for a beneficiary's "health, education, maintenance and support," or in its "sole discretion," and leaves the rest to the officer. FDIC-insured institutions alone held ~553,600 managed personal trust and agency accounts worth ~$996B at the end of 2025. Every one of them generates requests — tuition, a house down payment, a medical bill, a business, a car for a grandchild — that an officer must weigh against the instrument, the beneficiary's other resources, the remainder beneficiaries' interests and the family's history, then write up for a distribution committee. The officers who are good at this recognise patterns they cannot specify: the escalating cadence of requests that signals a beneficiary in trouble, the sibling dynamic that will produce an objection, the "education" request that is really support. Whether the call was right shows up years later, in a complaint, a petition to remove the trustee, a surcharge, or the next generation moving the assets when the trust ends.

Around that judgement sit clocks the trustee does not set. A national bank must review a prospective account before accepting it, review its assets promptly afterwards, and review every discretionary account's assets at least once each calendar year under 12 CFR 9.6. Calendar-year trusts file Form 1041 and beneficiaries' K-1s by April 15, with a 5½-month extension, while corrected tax information from funds keeps arriving through tax season. In corporate trust, the same banks act as trustee on ~424,000 bond and municipal issues with ~$30.5T outstanding, and 5,203 of those issues, ~$310B, were in default at year-end 2025; each default runs on the indenture's notice and direction deadlines. Custody banks settle and service assets against the client's own NAV cutoffs and the shortened US settlement cycle.

The industry is split between scale and craft. Custody is an oligopoly of a few global banks. Personal trust is spread across national banks, independent trust companies chartered in states such as South Dakota, Nevada, Delaware and Wyoming, and a long tail of 727 insured institutions with under $1B in fiduciary assets, where the median earned ~$0.3M of fiduciary income in 2025 and administers ~30 managed personal accounts. New charters keep arriving: in December 2025 the OCC conditionally approved five digital-asset firms for national trust bank charters. The attorneys who draft the instruments sit in [[industries/estate-planning|Estate Planning]], and the advisers who refer trusts and manage the assets of directed trusts sit in [[industries/wealth-management-rias|Wealth Management RIAs]]; this hub covers the trustee and custodian between them.

## Current Tech Landscape
Trust accounting runs on a few platforms: FIS (Global Plus and AddVantage), SEI's wealth platform, SS&C Innovest's InnoTrust and Fi-Tek's GWES, which hold positions, income and principal ledgers, fee schedules, distributions and statements. The custody banks run proprietary asset-servicing platforms on top of depository and SWIFT messaging, with reconciliation tools such as Duco and SS&C's matching products. Fiduciary tax is prepared on CCH and Thomson Reuters fiduciary modules or outsourced. Trust instruments sit as scanned PDFs in imaging systems; account reviews are tracked by ticklers; corporate trust waterfalls are coded deal by deal; special assets are tracked in spreadsheets kept by the one officer who knows the farm. Each tool is competent within its boundary. The record that no tool owns is the fiduciary decision itself: how the instrument was read, why a distribution was approved or declined, what the officer knew about the family, and what happened afterwards.

## Analysis
The trust company's central unmeasured judgement is the discretionary decision: whether to pay, how much, and why, made by an officer on an instrument written for another era, approved by a committee, and graded only when a beneficiary sues or leaves. The company holds both halves of the label — every request, memo and committee decision, and every later complaint, petition and asset departure — and has never joined them. What blocks a fix is legal (a trustee must exercise its discretion itself, so a model can inform the decision but never make it, and beneficiary files are customer information under the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act), structural (litigation is rare and slow, so outcome labels are thin at any single firm) and organisational (senior officers resist having their judgement scored, and the officers who carry the best reads are retiring). The operators are real buyers for decision support, review automation and tax tooling, but small trust departments buy through their trust accounting vendor and ask first whether the examiner will accept the output. Above the operators the Pass 2 sweep finds no qualifier: the firms that see across many trust companies and custodians either work on client-owned holdings and transaction data or sell to a handful of buyers, and the custodian analytics pockets already logged under other industries carry the same client-data kill switch.

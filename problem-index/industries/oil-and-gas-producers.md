# Oil & Gas Producers

## Profile
**Category:** Mining & Extraction
**NAICS 2022:** 211120, 211130 — Crude Petroleum Extraction; Natural Gas Extraction (E&P operators that hold leases, choose locations and run wells, plus the non-operating working-interest owners filed under the same codes; drilling, completion and well services are [[industries/oil-gas-field-services|Oil & Gas Field Services]])
**Market Size:** Statistics of US Businesses counts 4,213 oil and gas extraction firms running 5,276 establishments with $516.4B of receipts in 2022: 3,427 crude petroleum firms with $331.8B and 842 natural gas firms with $184.6B. The 76 firms with 500 or more employees took $425.7B (82%), while 3,761 firms with fewer than 20 employees took $9.6B (1.9%) (Census SUSB 2022, a high-price year). US crude output averaged a record 13.6 million b/d in 2025, up from 13.2 million in 2024, with the Permian at about 48%, even as WTI fell from $77/b to $65/b (EIA). More than 918,000 wells produced in 2024, and 78% of them made 15 BOE/d or less (EIA)
**Tech Maturity:** Medium — large operators run Petrel and DecisionSpace for the subsurface, ARIES, PHDwin or ComboCurve for forecasts and reserves, Enverus or Novi Labs for offset data, and real-time drilling centres. Yet the choice of where and how close to drill is still argued in slide decks, mineral title is still built from county deed books, and a small operator's whole back office is often one accounting package and a spreadsheet of lease dates.
**Workforce:** 84,770 employees in NAICS 211 (Census SUSB 2022): reservoir, completions and production engineers, geologists and geophysicists, landmen and lease analysts, division order analysts, revenue, production and joint-interest-billing accountants, regulatory analysts, EHS and emissions staff, A&D and planning analysts, and pumpers and lease operators (many of them contract). Contract field landmen, title attorneys, pumpers and service crews do much of the work outside the payroll.

## Key Pain Themes
An E&P company makes one decision that matters more than all the others: where to drill the next well, how close to its neighbours, and with what completion. The asset team argues it location by location. A geologist maps the landing zone, and a reservoir engineer picks offset wells as analogs and adjusts their production for spacing, stagger, timing and completion size into a type curve. The type curve sets the well's economics, the drilling budget and, in aggregate, the inventory that acquirers pay for. Experienced engineers carry judgement nobody wrote down: which offsets to trust, how much a child well loses to an older parent next door, when a type curve is flattering. The answer arrives roughly 12 to 24 months later (estimate) as production. But the analog choice and the spacing haircut are rarely recorded beside the curve, so nobody can grade the call.

Around the decision sits an ownership web that runs on paper and phone calls. Every unit has royalty, overriding royalty and working-interest owners, often hundreds, whose decimals come from title opinions built on runsheets from county deed books. Leases carry primary terms, shut-in clauses, continuous-development obligations and depth limits that a lease analyst tracks in spreadsheets. Division order analysts maintain the decimals, revenue accountants pay owners every month, and joint-interest-billing clerks bill partners. NARO puts US oil and gas royalty owners at 8 to 12 million (search summary). Texas presumes oil and gas proceeds abandoned after three years unpaid and takes them as unclaimed property (search summary).

External clocks set the rest. State commissions want monthly production reports, completion filings and plugging. Texas had 10,029 orphaned wells in August 2025, up 1,682 in the year, and plugged 775 of them with state funds (RRC, search summary). Banks redetermine reserve-based borrowing bases twice a year, and the SEC wants year-end reserves. EPA's 2024 methane rules for new and existing sites (OOOOb/c) were partly deferred: an interim final rule published 31 July 2025, and finalised in November 2025, moved several compliance dates, and the states' plan deadline of 9 March 2026, to 22 January 2027 (EPA). Congress pushed the methane Waste Emissions Charge back to 2034 emissions (search summary). In the Permian, Texas tightened disposal-well permits from 1 June 2025, with pressure and volume caps (RRC, search summary), and B3 Insight puts produced water at about four barrels per barrel of oil (search summary).

## Current Tech Landscape
Offset well, production and land data come from Enverus (whose data also underlies EIA's well report), S&P Global, Novi Labs, Wood Mackenzie and Rystad. Subsurface interpretation runs on SLB Petrel and Halliburton DecisionSpace, frac and spacing simulation on ResFrac, and forecasts and reserves on ARIES, PHDwin, ComboCurve and Enverus Value Navigator. Drilling and completion monitoring come from Corva and Pason, and industrial data platforms from Cognite. Accounting, revenue distribution, division orders and joint interest billing run on Quorum, W Energy, PakEnergy (WolfePak), P2 (BOLO) and Excalibur, and land on Quorum Land, Pandell and Enverus Courthouse, which added a Runsheet Assistant in October 2026 (search summary). Methane detection and records come from Project Canary, Bridger Photonics, Insight M, Qube, Validere, Highwood and GHGSat. Each is competent within its boundary. None of them owns the location record: which analogs were chosen, what spacing haircut was applied and why, and how the well then compared with the curve that funded it.

## Analysis
The producer's central unmeasured judgement is the location call: which undrilled spot, how close to its neighbours, and against which analogs. Each call is a dated, quantified prediction whose outcome the operator then measures every month, yet the reasoning lives in slides and in the engineer's head. Three things block fixing it. Organisationally, asset teams are reshuffled by every merger and their engineers move between operators. Structurally, the label is noisy and slow: a child well's shortfall mixes geology, spacing, timing and completion, and shows over two years. Contractually, much of the best evidence (partner wells, data-room curves, completion diagnostics) is held under joint operating agreements, confidentiality agreements or the operator's own ownership of well data. The commercial opening is with vendors more than operators. The 76 large firms buy on long enterprise cycles, and the 3,761 small ones buy one accounting package. Desk-scored at E = 4 for placement, the industry checks at about 3: on 25 October 2026 no land, royalty or production-accounting vendor had a forward deployed role open. Cognite, an industrial data platform that sells across industries, had a pre-sales/forward deployed Senior Field Engineer role in Houston or remote US (updated 9 October 2026). Novi Labs had platform and software-delivery director roles in Austin or remote, and Enverus had software engineers in Spokane, Pittsburgh and remote US, both in undated postings. Under the Pass 2 rubric the producers are poor buyers for a research-insight engine: their analysis is spent on their own drilling, and the strongest upstream insight pockets are already indexed under field services.

# Biotech Research Firms

## Profile
**Category:** Professional Services
**NAICS 2022:** 541714 — Research and Development in Biotechnology (except Nanobiotechnology)
**Market Size:** ~$91B in 2022 receipts across 4,593 firms (Economic Census, as reported), with 5,670 establishments and ~195,000 employees in 2023 (County Business Patterns, as reported); receipts understate the sector because most discovery-stage biotechs are pre-revenue and funded by equity. The largest preclinical CRO, Charles River, booked $2.40B of Discovery and Safety Assessment revenue in 2025.
**Tech Maturity:** Medium, and split — discovery teams run cheminformatics, structure-based design and generative models, while the regulated half (GLP toxicology, pathology, archiving) runs on validated legacy systems, glass slides and paper cage cards, and the decision record of both halves lives in slide decks and spreadsheets.
**Workforce:** Medicinal and synthetic chemists, discovery biologists and research associates, DMPK and in vivo pharmacology scientists, vivarium and animal care technicians, GLP study directors, toxicologists, veterinary pathologists and histotechnicians, quality assurance auditors, SEND and data managers, programme and alliance managers, and a thin layer of chief scientific officers who make the advance-or-kill calls.

## Key Pain Themes
A drug-discovery biotech is a sequence of expensive bets made on incomplete data. In discovery, medicinal chemists run the design–make–test–analyse cycle: they choose which molecules to make next, a chemistry CRO (often in China) synthesises them, biologists assay them, and the chemists read the results and choose again. A small-molecule programme may make ~1,000–5,000 compounds between hit and candidate (estimate), and each cycle takes ~4–8 weeks when chemistry is outsourced (estimate). The judgement that steers this — which molecule is worth making — sits with a few senior chemists who cannot fully say why they prefer one structure to another. Generative models now propose thousands of designs a week, which makes that judgement the bottleneck rather than the supply of ideas. Above it sits the advance-or-kill call on a whole programme, made knowing that only 7.9% of drugs entering Phase I reach approval (BIO/Informa/QLS, 2011–2020).

The second half of the industry is the preclinical CRO, which runs the IND-enabling package under FDA's Good Laboratory Practice rules (21 CFR Part 58). The study director is the single point of control for each study, signing for its conduct and data. Pathologists read hundreds to thousands of tissue slides per study (estimate), mostly on glass. Nonclinical data must be submitted in the CDISC SEND format for commercial IND studies started after 17 December 2017, and FDA rejects packages that fail its technical checks. Non-human primates for biologics toxicology became scarce after Charles River halted Cambodian imports in 2023. Analysts projected prices of $33,000 an animal against $2,500 in 2019 (as reported). FDA's April 2025 plan to phase out animal testing requirements for monoclonal antibodies is now redrawing which studies are needed at all.

External clocks govern both halves. An IND takes effect 30 days after FDA receives it unless FDA places a clinical hold. A pre-IND meeting package must reach FDA at least 30 days before the meeting or the meeting is cancelled. Financing tranches and partner milestones are written against IND dates and candidate criteria. Capital is scarce: global biopharma venture funding fell to $26B across 435 rounds in 2025 (DealForma), and layoff rounds rose 16% (Fierce Biotech), several of them eliminating whole discovery research teams. And the BIOSECURE Act, enacted in December 2025, requires a federal list of biotechnology companies of concern by 18 December 2026. That forces sponsors that depend on China-based discovery chemistry to plan alternatives.

## Current Tech Landscape
Benchling dominates the biotech electronic lab notebook and registry market. Dotmatics (bought by Siemens for $5.1B in July 2025) and CDD Vault cover chemistry registration and assay data. Genedata Screener handles high-throughput screening analysis, and Schrödinger and a crowd of generative chemistry startups supply design tools. Certara's Simcyp and Phoenix run PK and PBPK modelling. On the regulated side, Instem's Provantis and Xybion's Pristima capture GLP study data, Instem and Pinnacle 21 (Certara) tools produce and validate SEND, and Leica, Visiopharm and Indica Labs supply digital pathology scanning and image analysis. CAS SciFinder and Reaxys serve literature and patents, and Citeline and Evaluate supply competitive pipeline intelligence. Each tool records what it was built to record. None of them records why a compound was made, why a programme advanced, or what the pathologist saw and weighed. That decision record is what a partner's diligence team, an acquirer and the next chemist all need.

## Analysis
The industry's central unmeasured judgement is the experienced drug hunter's choice of what to make and what to advance. Every assay result is recorded; the reasoning that selected the compound or kept the programme alive is not, so no biotech can say which of its chemists' instincts carry information. Three things block fixing it. Experts disagree with each other and with themselves: in Novartis's own study, chemists agreed with one another at κ 0.32–0.40 and with themselves at only κ ~0.6. Data is scattered across CRO PDFs, notebooks and slide decks. And the regulated half sits inside a validated-system regime in which every study belongs to the sponsor. The operators are poor buyers. Discovery biotechs are cash-constrained and cutting research staff, and preclinical CROs sell capacity and GLP compliance, not insight, inside a perimeter where tooling procurement takes quarters. The strongest commercial openings sit around them, in the firms whose invoice is the analysis: regulatory consultancies that tell a sponsor what FDA will require before the pre-IND package is due (the one Pass 2 qualifier, at 50), and pipeline intelligence vendors whose records of every competing programme are graded by the events that follow (49, held back by a market of three or four providers).

# Renewable Power Producers

## Profile
**Category:** Utilities & Energy
**NAICS 2022:** 221114, 221115, 221118 — Solar Electric Power Generation; Wind Electric Power Generation; Other Electric Power Generation
**Market Size:** ~$20.3B of 2022 employer receipts across the three codes (Census SUSB: 725 firms and 17,184 employees, a sum of code counts), which undercounts the industry because most plants sit in project companies with no employees or inside utilities; wind and utility-scale solar generated 760 TWh in 2025, a record 17% of US utility-scale generation (EIA), worth ~$34B a year in energy sales alone (estimate)
**Tech Maturity:** Medium — every plant streams SCADA data to a remote operations centre and settles in ISO markets electronically, but queue strategy, tax-credit evidence and PPA settlement still run on spreadsheets, consultant reports and the judgement of a few senior people.
**Workforce:** Development and interconnection managers, transmission planning consultants, asset managers and performance engineers, remote operations centre operators, wind turbine and solar technicians (often employed by OEMs or independent service providers), commercial and settlement analysts, storage traders, and tax and project finance staff

## Key Pain Themes
A renewable project's fate is mostly decided years before it produces a megawatt-hour. Developers file interconnection requests with grid operators and utilities, wait for cluster studies to assign network upgrade costs, and decide at each stage whether to pay the next deposit or walk away. At the end of 2025 about 2,061 GW sat in US queues (1,312 GW of generation and 749 GW of storage), over 750 GW withdrew during the year, and the median project built in 2025 took 61 months from request to commercial operation, against 36 months in 2015. Only 13% of the capacity requested from 2000 to 2020 had reached operation by the end of 2025. FERC Order No. 2023 made the bet more expensive: study deposits of $55,000–$250,000, readiness deposits of twice that, then 5%, 10% and 20% of assigned network upgrade costs, with withdrawal penalties tied to them. The interconnection director's call on which positions to enter and which to drop before the deposits step up is the most valuable judgement in the firm, and nobody records it.

The clocks are set by others. Under the 2025 budget law (OBBBA), wind and solar projects lose the 45Y and 48E credits if placed in service after 31 December 2027, unless construction began by 4 July 2026. Treasury Notice 2025-42 removed the 5% cost safe harbor for all but small solar, so beginning of construction now rests on evidence of physical work, with a four-year continuity window. Credits are monetised by sale: transfers reached $21B in the first half of 2026 (Crux), and every buyer's diligence asks for prevailing wage, apprenticeship, domestic content and foreign-entity records gathered from dozens of subcontractors. Day-ahead bids close every morning, curtailment is rising (CAISO curtailed a record 919 GWh of wind and solar in March 2025), and battery revenues in ERCOT halved in 2025 as ancillary markets saturated (search summary).

Operations run remotely and break physically. About 11,800 wind turbine technicians (BLS) climb towers often over 200 feet to clear faults a remote operator could not reset, and a main bearing replacement with a crane costs $225,000–$400,000 (search summary). Settlement analysts reconcile every virtual PPA by hand each month, and the infrastructure funds that own much of the fleet read monthly reports they cannot check against the plant data.

## Current Tech Landscape
Siting and queue screening run on Paces, Enverus (which absorbed Pearl Street's interconnection modelling), Interconnection.fyi, Grid Status and the free LBNL Queued Up dataset; market data and price forecasts come from Yes Energy, Ascend Analytics, Aurora Energy Research and Wood Mackenzie. Operating fleets use asset performance software (Power Factors, Bazefield, Clir Renewables), condition monitoring (ONYX Insight and the turbine OEMs' own centres) and drone blade inspection (SkySpecs). Batteries are bid by Tesla Autobidder, Fluence Mosaic, Ascend's SmartBidder, Habitat Energy or in-house desks, and Tyba models storage and hybrid revenue. Offtake runs through LevelTen's marketplace and price index, credits through Crux and Reunion, settlement through ETRM systems such as Allegro and ION, and certificates through M-RETS, PJM-GATS and WREGIS. Each tool is competent within its boundary. No tool holds the joined record of a project position: what the developer expected at queue entry, what the studies and the market then delivered, and why the position was kept or dropped.

## Analysis
The industry's central unmeasured judgement is the price of a queue position: how likely a request at a given point of interconnection is to survive its cluster studies, what upgrade cost it will be assigned, and when it will reach operation. Every input is partly public. ISO queue files, the LBNL dataset and EIA-860 record status and dates, and the outcome arrives within about five years. What is missing is the developer's own expectation at each decision, and the obstacles are structural: outcomes are slow, transmission providers' network models are utility property, positions are traded between developers, and the senior people who "know which substations are full" move between firms. The operators are poor buyers for an insight product under this vault's rubric. They sell electrons and credits, not analysis, and the strongest clean outside pockets, renewable market intelligence (43) and power price and curtailment forecasters (42), stay below threshold. The commercial opening is on the operator side: vendors selling into development and storage are hiring engineers now. Paces lists AI, full-stack, geospatial and head-of-engineering roles in Brooklyn (posted April–June 2026); Tyba a senior software engineer (SF Bay Area, Chicago, LA or remote; posted 2026-06-08); Nira Energy only a CPTO (remote US; May 2026); and Redwood Materials' storage business site-controller software roles in San Francisco (2026-06-23), though it runs data-centre storage rather than selling to IPPs (source log, checked 2026-10-28). The clearest two-week MVP is a withdrawal-odds and months-to-operation model built on Queued Up, ISO queue files pulled with the open-source gridstatus library, and EIA-860.

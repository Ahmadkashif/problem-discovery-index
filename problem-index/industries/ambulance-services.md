# Ambulance Services

## Profile
**Category:** Healthcare & Clinical
**NAICS 2022:** 621910 — Ambulance Services (private, nonprofit and hospital-based operators of 911 and interfacility ground ambulances at basic life support, advanced life support and specialty or critical care levels, plus rotor and fixed-wing air ambulances, all billed under the Medicare Ambulance Fee Schedule; municipal fire-based EMS is the largest competitor and customer context, named here but counted as government; wheelchair vans and Medicaid NEMT brokers belong to [[industries/non-emergency-medical-transport|Non-Emergency Medical Transport]])
**Market Size:** The 2022 Statistics of US Businesses counts 2,913 private firms with 5,644 establishments, 178,532 employees and $20.8B of receipts in NAICS 621910; the 100 firms with 500+ employees took ~59% of it. Medicare fee-for-service paid $5.3B for 11.3 million ground transports by ~10,600 organizations in 2024 (MedPAC, June 2026). In Medicare's ground cost survey, government organizations were 54.6% of organizations and 38.5% of transports, and for-profits 18.7% and 42.1% (GADCS, 2022–2023)
**Tech Maturity:** Medium — every crew charts on an ePCR that exports NEMSIS v3.5 records to the state and every call runs through CAD, yet the physician certification statement still arrives by fax, the case for medical necessity is a crew's free-text narrative, and the level of service is coded later by a biller reading that narrative.
**Workforce:** EMTs and paramedics (BLS medians of $44,470 and $60,600 in May 2025, search summary), critical care nurses and flight crews, dispatchers and system status controllers, billers, coders and PCS specialists (often at outsourced billing firms), QA coordinators and fleet mechanics. Full-time EMT turnover reached 36% and paramedic turnover 27% in 2022 (AAA, search summary)

## Key Pain Themes
The decision that matters most in an ambulance happens in the first minutes with the patient. An experienced paramedic walks into a living room and knows the 70-year-old with "weakness" is septic, or that the man with indigestion is having a heart attack the monitor has not shown yet. That read sets the treatment, whether to run lights and sirens, and whether to bypass the nearest hospital for a stroke or cardiac centre. It also sets the level of service on the claim. Advanced life support emergency (A0427) pays $551.48 against $290.25 for basic non-emergency (A0428) in the CY2026 fee schedule (urban rates at a GPCI of 1.000, including the 2% add-on). The outcome of the read lives in a hospital record that seldom comes back to the crew, so nobody learns which reads were right. The documentation that justifies the call is the weakest link. Ambulance claims had a 10.4% improper payment rate in Medicare's FY2025 error-rate sample, $452.6M projected, and 46.7% of those errors were insufficient documentation and 24.9% medical necessity (CMS CERT).

Scheduled transports carry a second clock. Medicare's prior authorization model for repetitive, scheduled non-emergent ambulance transport (RSNAT), nationwide since the 2021–2022 waves, covers patients who ride three or more round trips in 10 days or weekly for three weeks, mostly to dialysis. If no request has been made by the fourth round trip in a 30-day period, claims go to prepayment review. Since 9 January 2025 the MAC has 7 calendar days to decide, and the PCS must be signed by the attending physician and dated within 60 days (CMS). In FY2024, 72.4% of 28,836 completed requests were provisionally affirmed, and 11,953 of 14,268 claim lines denied in prepayment review were denied because no documentation arrived (CMS). The PCS has to be chased from a discharge planner or dialysis social worker who does not work for the ambulance company.

The money is thin and uneven. Mean cost per transport was $2,878 in the lowest-volume quartile of ground organizations and $918 in the highest, and $1,626 at government-owned services against $563 at for-profits (MedPAC, June 2026). Ground ambulances sit outside the No Surprises Act. A federal advisory committee reported on balance billing on 28 August 2024, and about 22 states now protect patients from surprise ground bills (Commonwealth Fund, search summary). Air ambulances sit inside the Act: air operators won 89% of 12,263 federal IDR determinations in Q2 2025, and three firms (GMR, PHI and Air Methods) brought 82% of the disputes (CMS). County 911 contracts add a response-time standard, commonly 90% of priority-1 calls within about 8–9 minutes in urban zones, with penalties for late calls (estimate).

## Current Tech Landscape
ePCRs come from ESO, ImageTrend and ZOLL (emsCharts and RescueNet), and ImageTrend also sells state-level EMS data systems (named from recall). Dispatch and deployment run on CAD and system status software from ZOLL, Logis, Traumasoft and others (named from recall). Billing is often outsourced to Digitech (a Sarnova company), Ambulance Information Management (AIM), Intermedix (R1 RCM), Emergicon and regional billers, or run in-house on billing modules. RSNAT requests go to the MAC by fax, portal or esMD. Research data comes from NEMSIS (63.6 million activations from 14,801 agencies in the 2025 public release) and the ESO Data Collaborative (17.31 million records behind the 2026 ESO EMS Index). New AI vendors target the chart and the claim: CareSwift's ambulance-report scribe and Overdrive Health's ambulance revenue cycle, both early-stage Y Combinator companies. Each tool covers its own record. None joins the medic's impression, the level of service billed, the PCS behind a scheduled run, and what happened to the patient at the hospital.

## Analysis
The industry's central unmeasured judgement is the paramedic's read of how sick the patient is, and the same read becomes the level of service Medicare and auditors later question. Three things block a fix. The outcome sits in hospital records that few agencies receive. Every record is PHI. And the narrative is both the clinical record and the claim, so operators are wary of anything that scores it. The operators are thin-margin buyers: over half of ground organizations are government-owned, and the private side is led by a few platforms such as GMR (over five million patients a year, maturities pushed to October 2028 in its May 2024 refinancing) and DocGo, whose revenue fell from $616.6M in 2024 to $322.2M in 2025 as government contracts wound down (10-K). The commercial opening is with vendors hiring engineers to sell into them. On 2026-10-29 ImageTrend had a Technical Manager in software engineering (posted 2026-10-26) and a Database Developer (2026-10-07), both remote from Eagan, Minnesota. Candid Health, already described in [[problems/community-health-centers/ai-agents-platforms|🤖 Community Health Centers AI Agents & Platforms]] and [[problems/physician-practices/ai-agents-platforms|🤖 Physician Practices AI Agents & Platforms]], still had its forward deployed engineer role open, and Substrate, another shared revenue cycle vendor, had an undated forward deployed engineer listing in San Francisco. ESO's only engineering openings were a remote-US network engineer and roles in Canada and Prague. CareSwift was hiring an SDR only, and Overdrive Health had no openings (see the agents note). The two-week MVP is a PCS and medical-necessity checker on synthetic NEMSIS v3.5 ePCRs. It assigns A0426–A0429 and prices each run from the CMS Ambulance Fee Schedule PUF ([[problems/ambulance-services/low-impact-2|low-impact note 2]] and ML entry 2). Under the Pass 2 rubric no pocket qualifies. The operators and their billers spend their insight on claims, never invoice it, and hold it as PHI; the highest pocket is the platforms' own corporate analytics (31, kill switch).

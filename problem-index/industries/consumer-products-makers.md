# Consumer Products Makers

## Profile
**Category:** Manufacturing & Industrial
**NAICS 2022:** 339999, 339940, 339994, 339995 — All other miscellaneous manufacturing (candles, artificial flowers and trees, wigs, lighters, umbrellas and similar goods); office supplies except paper (pens, markers, crayons, art materials); brooms, brushes and mops (including paint brushes and rollers); burial caskets
**Market Size:** ~$21.4B in 2022 receipts across ~3,600 employer firms and ~65,300 employees (Census SUSB 2022: 339999 $14.2B and 3,042 firms; 339940 $3.4B and 356 firms; 339994 $3.0B and 138 firms; 339995 $0.7B and 71 firms). US retail candle sales alone were put at ~$3.14B a year in a 2013 Mintel estimate the National Candle Association still cites.
**Tech Maturity:** Low to Medium — the large makers run an ERP, EDI and retailer portals, but formulation, burn testing, finishing and brush-setting live in the plant's experienced hands, and most of the industry is small: about 85% of 339999 firms have under 20 employees.
**Workforce:** Production workers are ~69% of employment (2022 Economic Census): pour-line and wicking operators, machine tenders on pen and marker assembly, brush setters and trimmers, casket welders, finishers and upholsterers. Above them sit a formulator or lab technician, a quality and compliance coordinator, a demand planner, an accounts-receivable or deductions clerk, and an account manager who owns the big-box relationship.

## Key Pain Themes
These are physical products sold mostly through somebody else's shelf. A candle maker's year is decided between August and November, when it ships holiday programmes to Walmart, Target, TJX, Bath & Body Works and the gift trade; the National Candle Association puts about 35% of candle sales in the holiday season. Each new fragrance has to be matched to a wax blend, a dye, a vessel and a wick, then burned for hours at a time until a technician judges that the melt pool is full, the flame is steady and the glass stays cool enough. That judgement is the craft, it is not written down, and when it fails the result is a recall: CPSC recalled about 653,000 Trader Joe's scented candles in August 2024 after high-flame reports (search summary). Home candle fires still averaged 7,610 a year and 81 deaths in NFPA's 2014–2018 data, and ASTM F2417 sets the voluntary fire-safety bar that retailers test against.

The second theme is the retailer's ledger. Makers ship to programmes governed by routing guides, EDI rules and vendor agreements, and get paid net of whatever the retailer deducts: promotional allowances, damage and shortage claims, compliance fines and late-delivery penalties. Walmart charges 3% of the cost of goods on cases that miss its on-time or in-full targets (search summary). The Credit Research Foundation's 2023 deduction survey put median non-trade deductions at 1.1–2% of sales and the median automatic write-off threshold at $50–$100; the same survey's median 5.1–10% of deduction dollars invalid, and the 91% of companies that write off small deductions automatically, come from its results as compiled by Overdeduct. A vendor benchmark puts total deductions at 3–7% of gross revenue for mid-market consumer goods makers (Finortal, vendor figure). Each retailer has its own portal, codes and dispute window, and a small maker's one AR clerk cannot work them all before the windows close.

The third theme is rules that arrive from outside. From 8 July 2026 importers must eFile certificate data for every CPSC-regulated product at entry, which covers candles with metal-cored wicks under the 2003 lead-wick ban, child-resistant lighters and art materials under the hazardous art materials labelling law. California's Prop 65 produced 1,535 settlements and about $66M in payments in 2025, most of it attorney's fees. Imported candles have faced an antidumping order on Chinese petroleum-wax candles since 1986, and the Supreme Court's February 2026 ruling against tariffs imposed under the emergency-powers law (search summary) left importers chasing refunds while other duties stay. Caskets sit apart: a concentrated trade, led by Batesville (sold to LongRange Capital in 2023 for $761.5M) and Matthews, selling through funeral homes as NFDA projects burial falling from 31.6% of dispositions in 2025 to 13.0% by 2045.

## Current Tech Landscape
Large makers run SAP, Oracle, Infor or Epicor; mid-sized ones run NetSuite, Acumatica or QuickBooks with an EDI provider such as SPS Commerce or TrueCommerce. Deductions are worked in retailer portals and spreadsheets or in dedicated tools: HighRadius for large finance teams, and newer CPG-specific platforms Confido, Glimpse and SPS Revenue Recovery (SupplyPike plus Carbon6). Trade promotions are planned in Excel or in Vividly, Promomash or enterprise suites. Demand planners pull retailer POS from Walmart's Scintilla (formerly Luminate), Target's partner portal and Amazon Vendor Central, sometimes through Alloy.ai or Crisp. Compliance runs through third-party labs, UL's WERCSmart registrations, Prop 65 counsel and regulatory-content services such as Enhesa and 3E. The burn-test record, the casket finish standard and the brush-trim setting are on paper or in one technician's notebook, and no tool owns them.

## Analysis
The industry's central unmeasured judgement is the formulator's read of a burning candle: which wick, at what fragrance load, in which vessel, will pass a burn test and keep passing at the end of the candle's life. It decides how many new scents reach the holiday shelf and whether one of them comes back as a recall, and it lives in a few technicians at each maker. What blocks fixing it is structural. Burn tests are slow, physical and recorded on paper, and the outcomes that matter most (incidents in homes) arrive months later through retailer and CPSC channels the maker cannot join to its own lab notes. The strongest commercial opening is not the lab, though. It is the retailer ledger: deductions, chargebacks and compliance fines are paid by every maker that sells to big-box retail, the money is countable, the dispute windows are hard, and funded vendors (Confido and Glimpse in New York, both hiring engineers and forward deployed engineers on 2026-10-24) are already selling into it, mostly to food and beverage brands so far. The makers themselves are mostly small and are poor buyers of research; the insight-layer sweep finds no new qualifier, because the analytical functions around the industry either sit with retailers and measurement firms already indexed elsewhere or work on data that belongs to their clients.

# Product Design Studios

## Profile
**Category:** Digital Professional Services
**Market Size:** ~$9B US in digital product and experience design fees, split between independent studios, the design arms of consultancies, and a long tail of small practices
**Tech Maturity:** Excellent craft tooling, no measurement layer. Figma, prototyping tools, design systems and handoff workflows are mature and genuinely good. What no studio has is any record of whether the work they shipped did what they said it would, because the outcome lands in the client's analytics and the engagement ended at delivery.
**Workforce:** Product and interaction designers, design system specialists, UX writers, design technologists, producers and delivery leads, new business and strategy staff

## Key Pain Themes
A studio's core claim is that better design produces better business outcomes, and a studio running twenty engagements a year holds evidence for none of them. The redesign ships, the contract closes, and the conversion rate, retention curve and support volume that would grade the work sit inside the client's warehouse. What comes back is a testimonial or silence.

The consequence is that design arguments are settled by seniority and articulacy rather than by evidence. Every project relitigates the same questions — how many steps in the flow, how much onboarding, progressive disclosure or not — from first principles, because the studio's actual accumulated knowledge is a portfolio of screenshots and a set of strongly held opinions.

The second theme is that the deliverable decays the moment it is handed over. A design system shipped as a Figma library and a documentation site drifts from the client's production code within months, and the studio is not there to see it happen. Clients experience this as the redesign slowly reverting, which is also the most common reason a studio's work is judged to have failed.

The third is commercial: fixed-price scoping against a discovery process that by design does not know what it will find, and a pitch process that requires substantial unpaid work to win.

## Current Tech Landscape
Figma is the centre of gravity, with Storybook, Zeroheight and Supernova handling design system documentation and code linkage. Prototyping runs in Figma, Framer or ProtoPie. Research and analytics tools live on the client side — Amplitude, Mixpanel, FullStory, Hotjar — and studios usually see them only during an engagement, if at all. Project and utilisation management runs on Harvest, Float, Productive or a spreadsheet. Generative tooling has begun to compress early-stage visual exploration without touching the parts that are actually expensive.

## Analysis
A design studio is a firm whose product is a causal claim — this arrangement of an interface will produce that behaviour — sold to clients who can verify it and to a firm that never asks. Twenty engagements a year across a decade is a substantial natural experiment in interface design, running continuously, with the results discarded at each handover. The studios that have tried to close this loop have done it anecdotally, by staying close to a few clients. The systematic version is a contractual and instrumentation problem rather than a technical one, and the studio that solves it stops selling taste and starts selling evidence, which is a different business with a different margin.

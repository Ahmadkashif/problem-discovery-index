# Ecommerce Aggregators

## Profile
**Category:** Digital Commerce
**Market Size:** ~$8B US acquired brand revenue under aggregator ownership, sharply down from the 2021 peak
**Tech Maturity:** Financially engineered, operationally thin — Thrasio, Perch, Branded, SellerX and their peers raised enormous capital to buy Amazon third-party brands and applied playbooks that assumed acquired revenue would persist. Much of it did not, and the sector's contraction was fundamentally a failure of underwriting rather than of operations.
**Workforce:** Acquisition and diligence analysts, brand managers running portfolios, supply chain and sourcing staff, marketplace advertising specialists, catalogue and listing operations, integration teams

## Key Pain Themes
The category's defining failure is underwriting: predicting whether an acquired brand's revenue survives the change of ownership. Aggregators paid multiples on trailing earnings for businesses whose performance depended on things that did not transfer — a seller's supplier relationship, their ranking momentum, their review velocity, their responsiveness — and discovered it afterwards. Below that sit two operational problems that determine whether the thesis works at all: post-acquisition migration, where transferring a brand onto the aggregator's accounts and supply chain reliably disrupts the ranking that made it valuable; and supply chain consolidation, the main claimed synergy, which is genuinely hard across brands with different suppliers, specifications and minimum order quantities. Brand managers run portfolios far too large to manage attentively, and diligence analysts assess businesses through a marketplace's own reporting with all its known gaps.

## Current Tech Landscape
Amazon's Seller Central is the primary data source for both diligence and operations, and its reporting is designed for sellers rather than for portfolio analysis. Advertising is managed through Amazon's advertising console with a mature third-party tooling layer for bid management and keyword research. Listing optimisation tools are abundant. Supply chain runs on relationships and spreadsheets at most aggregators, with enterprise systems arriving only at the largest. Portfolio-level analytics are typically built in-house and are the main technical differentiator between operators, which is a low bar. Marketplace policy changes are a permanent operating risk that no tooling addresses well.

## Analysis
An aggregator that has bought dozens of brands holds something no individual seller and no marketplace analyst has: the same businesses observed before and after acquisition, with the operational changes made and the revenue consequences measured. That is a natural experiment on what actually drives marketplace performance, repeated dozens of times, and it is the empirical basis for both better underwriting and better operations. The sector's contraction happened while sitting on it.

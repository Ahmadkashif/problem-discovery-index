# Property & Casualty Insurers

## Profile
**Category:** Insurance
**NAICS 2022:** 524126, 524128 — Direct Property and Casualty Insurance Carriers; Other Direct Insurance (except Life, Health, and Medical) Carriers (mortgage guaranty, credit, warranty and other specialty direct lines)
**Market Size:** ~$1.11T in 2025 direct premiums written (NAIC market share data, 98% of filings in), $976.8B net written, a 92.9% combined ratio, $150.6B net income, $1.27T of policyholders' surplus and $1.03T of loss and loss-adjustment reserves (NAIC 2025 full-year report); surplus lines carriers wrote a record $143.3B, 12.9% of all P&C premium (AM Best)
**Tech Maturity:** Medium — personal auto pricing is among the most model-driven work in finance, and core policy and claims systems are moving to the cloud, while commercial underwriting still runs on emailed submissions and PDF loss runs, case reserves are set by an adjuster's judgement, and subrogation, loss control and regulatory data calls live in spreadsheets.
**Workforce:** ~699,300 people at P/C direct insurers in 2025 (BLS, preliminary, via Triple-I): field, desk and bodily injury claims adjusters and examiners, special investigation unit staff, personal and commercial underwriters and their assistants, pricing and reserving actuaries, data scientists, catastrophe modellers, loss control engineers, premium auditors, subrogation specialists, product and rate filing staff, and compliance officers

## Key Pain Themes
A P&C carrier sells a promise whose cost is unknown when the premium is set, and in the liability lines it stays unknown for years. The most consequential judgement in the business is made early and by one person. A bodily injury adjuster reads a new claim, the police report, the claimant's first call and the venue, and sets a case reserve. Experienced adjusters say they can tell within days which claims will draw an attorney, sit through a long treatment build-up and end in a demand for the policy limits. That read is written down as a reserve number and a few file notes, and it is graded only when the claim closes, often ~3–7 years later (estimate). Social inflation has made the read worth more and harder to make. Nearly 200 corporate verdicts of $10M or more were returned in 2025, up 40.7% on 2024 and about $25.6B in total (Marathon Strategies). In 2025 the industry released $15.9B of prior-year reserves in personal lines, while other liability occurrence and commercial auto liability still developed adversely by about $7.3B and $1.8B (NAIC).

The work runs on clocks set by others. Rates must be filed and, in prior-approval states, approved before they can be charged, and regulators send objection letters with response deadlines. The appointed actuary's Statement of Actuarial Opinion is due with the annual statement on 1 March, and the confidential Actuarial Opinion Summary by 15 March. Statistical data goes to the licensed statistical agents on their plans' schedules. Regulators add data calls on top: the NAIC's 2024 property market intelligence call asked more than 400 insurers for ZIP-code-level data within 90 days. Claims carry their own deadlines, from prompt-payment statutes and time-limited policy-limits demands to limitation periods for subrogation. Florida's HB 837 (March 2023) cut the negligence limitation period from four years to two.

The property side has a different problem. Catastrophe losses and reinsurance costs made homeowners unprofitable in the states where cover is most needed, and carriers responded by non-renewing. Regulators are now reopening the pricing toolkit. California completed its first review of a wildfire catastrophe model for ratemaking in July 2025, and carriers that use one must commit to writing more policies in distressed areas. Every model and data source a carrier adds must also survive unfair-discrimination scrutiny under the NAIC's December 2023 AI model bulletin and the states that adopted it.

## Current Tech Landscape
Policy, billing and claims run on Guidewire, Duck Creek, Sapiens, Majesco and Insurity, or on mainframe systems at older carriers. Advisory loss costs, forms and statistical reporting come from Verisk's ISO, AAIS and, for workers' compensation, NCCI and the state bureaus. Claims history and fraud signals come from ISO ClaimSearch and NICB, and consumer reports and credit-based insurance scores from LexisNexis and TransUnion. Auto physical damage is estimated on CCC, Mitchell and Audatex, and property damage on Xactimate. Catastrophe models come from Verisk, Moody's RMS and KCC, property condition scores from Cotality, ZestyAI and CAPE (now Moody's), and telematics from Cambridge Mobile Telematics, Arity and LexisNexis. Reserving runs on triangles in Excel, WTW ResQ or Milliman Arius, and litigation spend runs through bill review platforms. Each tool is competent within its boundary. None of them owns the record that joins the adjuster's early read and reserve to what the claim finally cost, or the underwriter's reasons for accepting a risk to that risk's losses.

## Analysis
The industry's central unmeasured judgement is the early read of a claim's ultimate cost. Carriers measure the outcome with great care in aggregate: loss triangles, reserve development by accident year, Schedule P. They almost never join it back to the adjuster who set the first reserve, the facts that adjuster saw, or the moment the claim turned. So they cannot say whose reads carry information or teach the next adjuster what the best ones see. Three things block a fix. The label is slow, because liability claims take years to close and the closing value is shaped by the handling itself. The evidence is unstructured: file notes, recorded statements and demand packages, some of it under attorney–client privilege once defence counsel is involved. And adjusters turn over faster than claims close. Regulators add a constraint of their own, because any model that touches pricing or claims must survive unfair-discrimination testing. The carriers are strong buyers of claims, pricing and catastrophe tooling, because loss ratios, filing calendars and the 1 March opinion put a date on the spend. They are poor buyers of a research-insight engine under the Pass 2 rubric, because their insight is priced into premiums and reserves rather than invoiced. The firms that do invoice for P&C insight already make up the vault's densest cluster of indexed pockets: bureaus, catastrophe modellers and estimating databases, all profiled under other industries (see the niche overview).

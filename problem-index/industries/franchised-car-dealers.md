# Franchised Car Dealers

## Profile
**Category:** Retail & E-Commerce
**NAICS 2022:** 441110 — New Car Dealers
**Market Size:** ~$1.3T in 2025 dealership sales across 16,990 franchised light-vehicle dealers, who sold 16.2 million new light vehicles and wrote more than 276 million repair orders worth over $164B in service and parts (NADA Data 2025)
**Tech Maturity:** Medium — every store runs on a dealer management system (CDK Global, Reynolds & Reynolds, Cox's Dealertrack DMS or the challenger Tekion) plus a CRM and dozens of OEM portals, but the judgements that make the money (what to order from the allocation, what to recommend in the service drive, how to code a warranty claim) still live in managers' heads, and the June 2024 CDK outage showed how much of the store stops when one system does.
**Workforce:** About 1.13 million people in 2024, ~65 per store (NADA, via CBT News): dealer principals and general managers, new- and used-car managers ("the desk"), sales consultants, business development centre (BDC) agents, F&I (business) managers, service advisors, flat-rate technicians, warranty administrators, parts counter staff, controllers and office staff, porters and lot attendants

## Key Pain Themes
A franchised dealer is a licensee of a manufacturer, and almost every clock in the store is set by the OEM or by the state law that limits the OEM. New vehicles arrive through an allocation formula that rewards recent sales and days supply ("turn-and-earn"), are paid for with floorplan credit from the day they land, and earn their margin partly through incentive programmes: consumer cash, dealer cash, subvented rates, and stair-step volume bonuses that pay per unit only if a monthly target is hit. At the September 2026 average transaction price of $50,562, incentive spending ran at 6.6% of ATP, about $3,359 a vehicle (Kelley Blue Book). The new-car manager decides each order cycle which trims, colours and packages to take, decline or trade, and an aged unit costs ~$8–10 a day in floorplan interest (estimate) while it waits. Those decisions are made in OEM portals from memory and feel, and nobody records why.

Fixed operations, the service and parts business, carries an estimated ~45–50% of a store's gross profit (estimate) and runs on a service drive that cannot answer its own phone. Car Wars tracked roughly 53 million inbound service calls across its dealer network in 2025; about 19 million were missed opportunities, more than half going to voicemail and 29% of callers hanging up on hold, and only about 4 million appointments were booked (Car Wars CEO, CBT News, March 2026). J.D. Power's 2025 Customer Service Index study (55,210 owners) found satisfaction holding while appointment waits stayed longer than in 2018–2022 and about 12% of repairs were not fixed right on the first visit (search summary). Behind the drive, a warranty administrator turns each covered repair into a claim against the OEM's policy manual, and the OEM can reject it or charge it back in a later audit. State franchise laws set the terms: Virginia, for example, requires warranty pay at the dealer's retail rates, payment within 30 days and chargebacks only within six months of the claim.

The F&I office has a moving rulebook. The FTC's CARS Rule was vacated by the Fifth Circuit on 27 January 2025 on procedural grounds, but California's CARS Act took effect on 1 October 2026 with total-price disclosure in the first written communication, add-ons disclosed as optional in 12-point type, a ban on "valueless" add-ons, a three-business-day cancellation right on used vehicles at or under $50,000 and two years of records. And the store itself is an asset that trades: Kerrigan Advisors counted a record 462 dealership transactions (724 franchises) in the twelve months to mid-2026, each needing OEM approval under the dealer agreement and state law.

## Current Tech Landscape
The DMS (CDK, Reynolds & Reynolds, Dealertrack DMS, Tekion; the three incumbents are put at ~80% of rooftops by secondary market-research estimates, search summary) holds deals, repair orders, parts and accounting. CDK's June 2024 ransomware attack took about 15,000 dealer locations offline for up to two weeks, and Anderson Economic Group put dealer losses above $1 billion. CRMs (VinSolutions, DealerSocket, Elead, DriveCentric, Fullpath) run sales leads; Xtime (Cox), myKaarma and Dealer-FX run service scheduling, video inspections and payments; Car Wars and CallRevu track phone performance; RouteOne and Dealertrack carry credit applications and e-contracts; KPA and ComplyAuto sell compliance programmes; OEM portals carry orders, warranty claims and incentive claims. A new layer of AI agents answers service and sales calls and texts. On 2026-10-24, BizzyCar had an Engineering Lead and a Senior Full Stack Engineer open (remote, United States); Numa a Senior Machine Learning Developer for its voice agents (Montreal or Toronto, Canada); Impel a mobile software engineer (Tbilisi, Georgia, or remote Australia); CDK staff, senior and associate software engineers in Austin, Texas, and engineering managers in Hyderabad, India; Tekion about 48 engineering and AI roles, almost all in Bangalore and Chennai, India; myKaarma engineers in Noida, India; and Fullpath backend engineers in Israel. Toma's forward deployed engineer listing appeared only on job aggregators and its YC page showed no open roles. Each tool holds one record. No tool holds the decision record: why the manager ordered this mix, why the advisor wrote the concern this way, why the claim was coded as it was, and what happened next.

## Analysis
The franchised dealer's central unmeasured judgement is the new-vehicle order: which configurations to take from an allocation the OEM controls, against incentives the OEM changes monthly, in a market only the manager has watched. The outcome is recorded precisely, in days to sale, front gross and incentive earned, and it is never joined to the decision that caused it. Three things block the fix. The data is split between the OEM's portals and the dealer's DMS, and the DMS vendors' rights to dealer data have been fought over in court. The manager's reasons are tacit. And the OEM owns most of the measurement around the store (CSI surveys, sales-effectiveness ratings, warranty analytics), which is why the insight businesses here keep hitting "data isn't theirs". The commercial opening is the service drive and the claims back office: high-volume, rule-bound, billed in dollars the dealer can count (booked appointments, warranty dollars kept, incentive dollars claimed). That is where the AI vendors that are hiring engineers are concentrated. The dealer is a fast, reachable buyer for those tools; for a research-insight engine it is not a buyer, and the Pass 2 sweep finds no qualifier around it.

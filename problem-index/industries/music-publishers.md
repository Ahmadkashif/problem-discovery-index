# Music Publishers

## Profile
**Category:** Media, Telecom & Information
**NAICS 2022:** 512230 — Music Publishers
**Market Size:** ~$7.3B in US music publishing revenue in 2025 (NMPA: $7.29B, of which performance 52%, synchronisation 24%, mechanical 19%, other 5%); the Census Service Annual Survey puts 512230 employer-firm revenue at $7.12B for 2022, and County Business Patterns counted 833 establishments employing 5,355 people in 2023. Worldwide, collecting societies took in €12.59B for music in 2024 (CISAC)
**Tech Maturity:** Medium — work registration and royalty processing run on enterprise rights systems and society portals, while the creative and capital decisions (which writer to sign, which song to pitch, what a catalogue is worth) run on email, spreadsheets and the memory of a few senior people.
**Workforce:** Creative managers and A&R, sync and licensing reps, copyright and administration coordinators, royalty accountants and analysts, business and legal affairs, catalogue acquisition and finance teams, and the signed songwriters themselves, who are paid advances as independent contractors rather than employed

## Key Pain Themes
A publisher owns or administers song copyrights, meaning the composition (melody and lyrics) rather than the recording, and is paid through three channels that each work differently. Performance royalties arrive from performing rights organisations: ASCAP, BMI, SESAC and GMR in the US and around 228 societies abroad. Mechanical royalties for US digital use arrive from the Mechanical Licensing Collective (the MLC), which the Copyright Office designated in 2019 and which began operating on 1 January 2021. Sync fees are negotiated directly with whoever puts a song in a film, series, game or ad. Every channel depends on a registration: the work, its writers, their shares and identifiers, delivered to each society. Where two parties' claims on a work add up to more than 100%, the money sits in suspense until someone proves their share. The clocks are external and hard. The MLC will start sharing out unclaimed money by market share in April 2027, beginning with January 2021 usage, from a database snapshot taken around 10 February 2027. EU societies must treat money still unclaimed three years after the collection year as non-distributable. Section 203 of the Copyright Act lets a writer serve notice to recapture a grant 35 years on, inside a window the statute fixes.

The creative side runs on a different kind of clock. A sync brief from an ad agency typically wants songs within 24–48 hours, sometimes three. The rep answering it searches a catalogue of tens of thousands of songs mostly from memory: which song fits the brief's mood, which supervisor buys what, which co-publisher will approve, and what fee the client will bear. Most pitches never land, and supervisors rarely say why. The record of pitch, shortlist, licence and fee sits in email and is never used to make the next pitch better.

Above both sits a capital market that treats songs as financial assets. Citrin Cooperman alone priced 566 catalogues worth nearly $13B in 2025, with older publishing catalogues trading around 15 times annual earnings. KBRA has rated about $8B of music royalty asset-backed securities since 2020. Every price rests on a forecast of how fast a song's earnings decay. That forecast is rebuilt by hand for each deal, and the Hipgnosis dispute showed what an unchecked one costs. A sale of non-core songs came in 14.2% below the independent valuer's mark for them, and the valuer resigned.

## Current Tech Landscape
Vistex's Counterpoint Suite, with Music Maestro and iMaestro for publishers, and Curve Royalty Systems handle the song database, CWR registration to societies and royalty statements. Statement-import tools advertise parsers for more than 150 society, DSP and sub-publisher formats. Societies run member portals, and the MLC adds a Matching Tool and an Overclaims Tool. Kobalt, Songtrust and Sentric sell administration as a platform to writers and smaller publishers. On the creative side, Synchtank's SyncUp manages pitches, quotes and licences, DISCO shares and tags catalogues for pitching, and Luminate, Chartmetric and Soundcharts sell consumption data. Each tool is competent within its own boundary. Two records belong to none of them. One links a song's usage in every territory to the money that should have arrived and the money that did. The other links a brief to the pitch, the licence and the fee.

## Analysis
The industry's central unmeasured judgement is what a song will earn next. Whether a publisher is pitching it, signing the writer or buying the catalogue, the decision rests on that forecast. Publishers hold decades of song-level earnings by territory and source, and every licence they ever quoted. Yet the sync rep's match is never recorded as data, and catalogue forecasts are rebuilt per deal by a handful of valuers. Three things block a fix. The data is split across societies, sub-publishers and statement formats that share no reliable key. Creative staff resist having their taste scored. And catalogue data moves under non-disclosure agreements. The operators are mostly small, about six employees per establishment, and the large owners keep their analysis in-house. Under the Pass 2 rubric the operators fail the gate. One pocket around them qualifies: the collecting societies (51). Their distribution work matches usage to the right member's share, against statutory deadlines in most of the world, from the deepest works-and-usage record in the industry. Catalogue valuation specialists (47) are the strongest near-miss. For tooling sold to publishers themselves, the strongest commercial opening is income-gap recovery against hard society deadlines, together with catalogue underwriting. Publishers and catalogue funds pay for both, because both are measured in recovered or correctly priced dollars.

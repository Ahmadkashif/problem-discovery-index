# Defense Systems Manufacturers

## Profile
**Category:** Manufacturing & Industrial
**NAICS 2022:** 336414, 336415, 336419, 332993, 336992 — Guided Missile and Space Vehicle Manufacturing; Guided Missile and Space Vehicle Propulsion Unit and Propulsion Unit Parts Manufacturing; Other Guided Missile and Space Vehicle Parts and Auxiliary Equipment Manufacturing; Ammunition (except Small Arms) Manufacturing; Military Armored Vehicle, Tank, and Tank Component Manufacturing
**Market Size:** ~$52.9B in 2022 receipts across 187 firm counts and 258 establishments (Census SUSB 2022: missiles and space vehicles $19.5B across 32 firms; propulsion units $19.9B across 17; other missile and space parts $2.5B across 34; ammunition except small arms $4.0B across 47; armored vehicles $7.1B across 57). 47 enterprise counts with 500+ employees took ~$51.0B (~96%), and 140 firm counts under 500 employees shared ~$1.9B. For scale, Lockheed Martin's Missiles and Fire Control segment alone reported $14.45B of 2025 sales and a $46.65B year-end backlog (its 2025 10-K), and General Dynamics' Combat Systems segment (including its European land systems unit) $9.25B, of which $4.97B was military vehicles and $3.10B weapon systems and munitions.
**Tech Maturity:** Medium — the primes run PLM, ERP, validated earned value systems and instrumented test ranges, while the work that decides whether a programme delivers (a propellant batch called good, a variance explained, an estimate at completion signed, a test anomaly found) runs on travellers, spreadsheets, narrative reports and the memory of a few senior people, often on air-gapped networks where modern software can't go.
**Workforce:** Energetics and propellant operators, explosive-fill and load-assemble-pack crews, machinists and welders on projectile and hull lines, assemblers and harness technicians, NDT and radiographic inspectors, manufacturing, test and systems engineers, control account managers and programme planners, cost estimators and pricers, government property and contracts staff, and the security officers who run classified areas. BLS QCEW counted ~136,600 private employees in the five codes in 2024 at ~$137,200 average pay; 336414 alone had 81,311 at ~$154,700, with California (24,415) and Florida (13,716) the largest disclosed states. Census SUSB, which classifies establishments differently, counted 105,697 employees for 2022.

## Key Pain Themes
The industry's defining problem in 2026 is rate. The Army made about 36,000 155mm rounds a month in March 2026 against a goal of 100,000 by October 2025, and the DoD Inspector General named projectile metal parts as the limit. A $469M General Dynamics line in Mesquite, Texas, opened in 2024 and had not produced a single projectile metal part meeting contract specifications (Defense News on the IG's July 2026 evaluation). Missiles face the same wall. CNAS counts $49.5B requested in FY2027 for ten offensive and interceptor munitions, about 8.5 times the FY2016–26 annual average, with 82% of it riding on reconciliation; it notes THAAD's production books cap output at 96 a year against an FY2027 order of 857. Solid rocket motors are the shared choke point. Two incumbents (Northrop Grumman and L3Harris's Aerojet Rocketdyne) face new entrants (Anduril, Ursa Major, X-Bow, General Dynamics), at least one key motor ingredient has a single US source, and an October 2025 explosion at Accurate Energetic Systems in Tennessee, an energetics supplier to the motor makers, killed 16 people (Breaking Defense, January 2026). New lines don't fail for want of drawings. They fail because the process knowledge that makes a qualified line hit specification lives in the people who run the old one.

The second theme is programme control under cost-type contracts. About 40% of Lockheed Martin's 2025 sales were cost-reimbursable, and a single estimate change can be large: its missiles segment recognised reach-forward losses of about $1.4B on a classified programme in 2024. Since 1 February 2026, DoD's Part 234 class deviation (2026-O0011) requires an EIA-748-compliant earned value system on cost and incentive contracts of $50M or more, a government-accepted system from $100M, and an integrated baseline review within 180 days of award. Every month each control account manager explains variances and re-signs an estimate at completion that DCMA can test. GAO's 2026 weapon systems assessment found 46 of 72 programmes reporting $122B of cost growth and average delivery times above 12 years (as reported by Defense News). Pricing runs on the same clock: the FY2026 NDAA raised the certified cost or pricing data threshold to $10M for contracts after 30 June 2026, and DCAA's FY2025 report counted 2,480 audit reports on $788B of contract costs (advisory-firm summary).

The third theme is evidence: test, security and data. Developmental and lot acceptance tests produce telemetry that engineers reduce by hand before the next event, and space programmes miss launch slots on late subsystems: SDA's first operational tranche slipped about six months on optical terminals and encryption devices (Air & Space Forces, November 2024). All of it sits behind ITAR and classification. CMMC Phase 2 certification was suspended on 13 July 2026, but Phase 1 self-assessments and DFARS 252.204-7012 still apply. Public award data moved from FPDS into SAM.gov on 24 February 2026.

## Current Tech Landscape
Programme control runs on Deltek Cobra and Costpoint, Oracle Primavera P6 and Microsoft Project, with earned value analytics from Empower and Excel. Pricing runs on ProPricer, Excel and parametric models such as SEER and TruePlanning. Engineering and production sit on Siemens Teamcenter, Dassault 3DEXPERIENCE or PTC Windchill, SAP or Oracle ERP, and MES such as iBASEt Solumina at the primes; First Resonance's ION serves newer hardware makers. Test data platforms (Nominal among the newest) are replacing in-house scripts. Exiger and Air (Govini renamed itself Air in June 2026) map sub-tier supply risk, and SalesPatriot automates quoting and procurement for defence suppliers and distributors. Proposal tools (GovDash, Deltek GovWin IQ, Unanet) help with capture. Each does its job. Two records belong to no tool: the process call on the line (why a propellant batch or a shell forging was accepted, adjusted or scrapped) and the control account manager's reasoning behind an estimate at completion.

## Analysis
The central unmeasured judgement in defence systems manufacturing is process acceptance on a qualified line: whether a propellant batch, a cast motor, a forged projectile body or a weld will pass, and what to adjust when it won't. It decides how fast the industry can grow output, and it is recorded as a stamp on a traveller rather than as the sensor history, the image and the reasoning. A second judgement, the estimate at completion, decides programme profit and is recorded as a number without its reasons. Three things block fixing either. Process changes on qualified munitions need requalification and government acceptance, so tools can only assist the qualified operator and inspector. Nearly all the data is ITAR-controlled, CUI or classified, which keeps cloud software out and puts a live kill switch on most of the insight businesses around the industry. And the buyers are concentrated: a handful of primes and one customer. As a buyer of insight the operator fails the Pass 2 gate, and the analytical functions with the deepest data (DCAA, DCMA, CAPE's cost analysts, the operational testers) belong to the government and cannot sell. The commercial opening is Pass 1's. Vendors selling into these manufacturers are hiring engineers now: Exiger and Air (supply chain), First Resonance (factory software), Nominal (test data) and SalesPatriot (procurement), alongside in-house teams at Anduril, Hadrian and Apex. Most require US-person status under ITAR, and many want clearance eligibility.

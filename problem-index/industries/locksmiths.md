# Locksmiths

## Profile
**Category:** Business Support & Waste Services
**NAICS 2022:** 561622 — Locksmiths: mobile and storefront locksmiths that open, repair, rekey and install locks, design and service master-key systems, cut and program vehicle keys, and open and service safes and vaults
**Market Size:** $2.63B in 2022 receipts across 3,895 firms and 3,983 establishments employing 17,378 people (Census Economic Census 2022). It is an industry of very small firms: 2,867 firms (74%) have fewer than five employees and together take $840M of receipts, and only 101 firms have twenty or more (Census SUSB 2022). California ($369M), New York ($230M), Texas ($217M) and Florida ($195M) are the largest state markets
**Tech Maturity:** Low — the trade runs on pick sets, key machines, code books and handheld key programmers; jobs arrive by phone from search ads; master-key records sit on bitting charts in binders; and the judgement that decides a job, whether reading a failing door or choosing how to open a car, stays in the technician's head
**Workforce:** Locksmiths and safe repairers (14,790 wage-and-salary jobs in May 2023, 10,200 of them in investigation and security services firms, plus owner-operators BLS does not count), automotive key specialists, commercial door hardware technicians, safe and vault technicians, counter staff cutting keys, and dispatchers on 24-hour lockout lines. Another 1,090 locksmiths work in-house at colleges and universities and 620 in schools

## Key Pain Themes
A lockout is a distress purchase made on a phone, usually by searching "locksmith near me", and that search has been gamed for well over a decade. Google and UC San Diego's 2017 study of suspended Maps listings found that about two in five fake listings came from people posing as locksmiths, plumbers and other contractors, and that 11% of search results for locksmiths were fraudulent. Behind those listings is a call centre that answers under a local-sounding name, quotes low and sends a subcontractor who charges far more; Ohio's attorney general sued one in 2014 after customers quoted $19 were billed much higher. Google now requires every locksmith business in the US and Canada to pass Advanced Verification before it can appear in Local Services ads. Only 13 states license locksmiths at all, and Illinois' licence is set to sunset on 1 January 2029. At the door, the technician must then decide whether the person asking to be let in actually lives there. California makes that a recorded duty: each vehicle or registered-property job needs a work order with the requester's ID, registration and signature, kept for two years.

The money in established firms is commercial. Every door in an office, school or hospital is an opening: a door, a frame, hinges, a lock or exit device, a closer, and increasingly an electrified strike wired to access control. When a facility manager reports "the front door won't lock", the cause may be the closer's latch-speed valve, which one hardware supplier calls the most commonly misadjusted valve on commercial doors. It may equally be a sagging hinge, a strike out of line or a worn exit-device latch. An experienced technician tells which at a glance and arrives with the part. A newer one makes a second trip. Fire-rated openings add an external clock: NFPA 80 has required an annual inspection of every fire door assembly since its 2007 edition, and health facilities surveyed under CMS's Life Safety Code had to complete them by July 2017. Master-key systems add a records problem. A campus may run thousands of cylinders off one bitting hierarchy, kept on paper charts by whichever locksmith designed it. A lost grand master or a terminated employee then means working out, door by door, what has to be rekeyed.

Automotive work has moved from cutting metal to security data. A replacement key for a recent car needs its transponder or smart fob programmed to the immobilizer. For that, independent locksmiths draw codes and PINs through the NASTF Secure Data Release Model: a $100 registry application, a criminal background check, a credential valid for two years, and an electronic authorization form for each job filed within five days. The data is single-use and may not be stored. Each job is also a gamble on tools: the locksmith finds out whether this VIN is covered only once the programmer is connected. Meanwhile mechanical work is being eaten at both ends. Smart locks turn a rekey into a code reset, and Aliro 1.0, released on 26 February 2026 with Apple, Google and Samsung behind it, puts door credentials in phone wallets. KeyMe's 8,000-plus kiosks have duplicated more than 47 million keys.

## Current Tech Landscape
Locksmiths cut keys on code and duplicating machines and program vehicles with handheld tools such as Autel's MaxiIM, Xhorse's VVDI line and Advanced Diagnostics' Smart Pro. They look up key codes in code software and design master-key systems in small programs such as MKExpress and Aero Key, or have the lock manufacturer's factory keying service do it. Jobs, invoices and dispatch run on general field-service software (Workiz, Housecall Pro, Jobber, ServiceTitan) or on paper and QuickBooks. Hardware comes from manufacturers' distributors such as Banner Solutions, IDN Global and Accredited Lock Supply, and leads come from Google Local Services, Angi, Thumbtack and pay-per-call networks. Large clients track keys in cabinets such as KeyTrak and Traka; apartment owners increasingly run access through SmartRent-style platforms and residential smart locks from Schlage, Yale and Kwikset. Each tool holds its piece well: the key code, the job ticket, the cabinet log, the lock event. No system holds the per-opening history: which door, which hardware, which bitting, who holds which key, what failed and what fixed it. That record is the one that would say why a door keeps failing, what a lost master really exposes, and which technician's fixes stick.

## Analysis
The industry's central unmeasured judgement is the diagnosis at the door. A technician reads an opening and decides what failed, what will fix it and whether it now meets fire and egress rules. The same technician, at a different door, decides whether the person on the step is entitled to be let in. Neither judgement is recorded in a form that can be checked against what happened next. A callback is logged as a new job, a fire-door deficiency surfaces only at the next inspection, and an opening for the wrong person surfaces as a police report. What blocks the fix is structural. Three in four firms have fewer than five employees. Key-system records are often treated as the client's property, held on paper by one locksmith. NASTF forbids storing vehicle security data at all. And the call itself is intermediated by search platforms and lead brokers who own the demand data. The commercial opening is therefore not the solo locksmith, who is a poor direct buyer and buys through distributors, tool vendors and field-service platforms. It is the commercial door-hardware specialists and multi-site facility clients that pay for openings to work and pass inspection. A second opening is the platforms that already sit between the caller and the technician (search ads, lead networks, smart-lock systems), which could turn verification and diagnosis into a product. Access control itself, the credential lifecycle behind electrified doors, belongs to [[industries/security-alarm-integrators|Security Alarm Integrators]].

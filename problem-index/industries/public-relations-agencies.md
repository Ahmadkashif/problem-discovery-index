# Public Relations Agencies

## Profile
**Category:** Professional Services
**NAICS 2022:** 541820 — Public Relations Agencies (including the public relations consulting, lobbying and political consulting establishments the code covers)
**Market Size:** ~$15.8B in US receipts from 8,125 firms and 8,604 establishments employing 65,010 people (2022 Economic Census); IBISWorld's broader measure puts the US industry at ~$25.7B in 2026. Globally the 250 largest agencies billed more than $18.25B in fees in 2025 (PRovoke)
**Tech Maturity:** Medium — every agency licenses a media database, a monitoring feed and a newswire account, but the work those tools serve (deciding which reporter gets which story, reading whether an issue will blow up, judging whether coverage carried the message) still runs on senior people's memory, phone calls and PowerPoint.
**Workforce:** Account coordinators and account executives who build media lists, pitch reporters and compile coverage reports; account supervisors and directors who run client retainers; senior vice presidents and partners who counsel executives and lead crises; media relations specialists; writers and content strategists; measurement analysts; and, in public affairs firms, registered lobbyists, grassroots organisers and former government staff

## Key Pain Themes
A PR agency is paid on retainer to earn coverage that its client could not buy, and the decisions that earn it are rarely written down. Which reporter is right for this story, what angle will make her run it, whether to offer an exclusive or an embargo, and when to pitch so the piece lands before a competitor's launch are judgements senior publicists make in seconds from years of watching what particular journalists cover and ignore. Juniors, who send most of the pitches, make the same choices from a database filter. Journalists notice: in Muck Rack's 2025 survey only 3% said pitches always match their beat, and 86% disregard the ones that do not. The agency records every placement in its monitoring tool and every pitch in its email or outreach platform, and never joins the two, so it cannot say which judgements produced the coverage it bills for, and the senior publicist's read of the newsroom leaves when she does.

The proof of value is the other chronic weakness. Clients ask what the retainer bought, and the answer is a monthly coverage report assembled by an account executive from monitoring exports: clip counts, reach figures, share of voice and, still at many firms, an advertising value equivalent that AMEC's Barcelona Principles 4.0 (June 2025) again called invalid. Whether coverage carried the client's three key messages is coded by hand, inconsistently, if at all, and whether any of it moved reputation, search demand, sales or a policy outcome is almost never tested. Earned media has also acquired a new audience: Muck Rack's tracking finds that more than 80% of the links AI models cite are earned media, which makes coverage more valuable and measurement more confusing at the same time.

Around the craft sit hard external clocks. A public company that decides a cyber incident is material has four business days to file a Form 8-K under the SEC's 2023 rule, and its PR firm drafts the statement, the customer notice and the media holding lines inside that window. Healthcare clients' press materials pass medical-legal-regulatory review and can count as promotion that must be filed with FDA on Form 2253 at first use; FDA's September 2025 letter batch treated even third-party interview programmes as promotional. Public affairs work answers to legislative calendars, comment deadlines and, for foreign principals, FARA's 48-hour filing rule for informational materials. The industry is small-firm at its base, with 74% of establishments under five employees in 2023, and consolidating at its top, where Omnicom's acquisition of Interpublic in November 2025 put Weber Shandwick, FleishmanHillard, Ketchum and Golin under one owner.

## Current Tech Landscape
Media databases and outreach come from Cision (about 1.4 million contacts claimed), Muck Rack, Meltwater and smaller tools such as Prowly and Prezly; monitoring and broadcast clips from the same vendors plus Onclusive, Critical Mention and TVEyes; distribution from PR Newswire, Business Wire and GlobeNewswire; social listening from Brandwatch, Talkwalker and Sprinklr; and measurement from vendor dashboards, specialist analysis firms and the agency's own spreadsheets. Real-time risk alerts come from Dataminr and Signal AI, and AI-answer visibility trackers such as Muck Rack's Generative Pulse are the newest category. Agencies run time, budgets and retainers on Harvest, Function Point or holding-company finance systems. Each tool is competent within its boundary. The record that matters most to the agency (which pitch, to which reporter, with which angle, produced which coverage, carrying which message, with what effect) spans all of them and is owned by none.

## Analysis
The industry's central unmeasured judgement is news judgement: the senior publicist's ability to match a story to the one reporter who will run it, and the crisis counsellor's ability to tell which issue will escalate. Both produce outcomes that are recorded in detail (the placement appears in the monitoring feed; the issue either reaches national media or dies), and neither is joined to the decision that produced it. What blocks fixing it is partly structural (pitches live in individual inboxes and outreach tools, coverage in a separate monitoring contract, the client's outcomes in the client's systems), partly commercial (a retainer priced on senior time does not reward recording that time's reasoning) and partly cultural (publicists treat reporter relationships as personal capital). Most agencies are poor buyers of an insight engine: 74% of establishments have fewer than five staff, operating margins fell to 16.6% in 2024 by Gould+Partners' benchmark, and even the largest firms monetise insight through counsel rather than invoicing it. The strongest commercial opening sits beside the agencies, in the measurement and media-analysis firms that already sell coded coverage as their product and have never shown that their codes predict anything; at 49 they fall just short of the Pass 2 bar because three vendors dominate.

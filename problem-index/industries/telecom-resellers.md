# Telecom Resellers

## Profile
**Category:** Media, Telecom & Information
**NAICS 2022:** 517121, 517810 — Telecommunications Resellers; All Other Telecommunications. The first code covers firms that buy access and network capacity from network owners and resell wired and wireless service under their own brand, mobile virtual network operators (MVNOs) included. The second is a residual code, but its relevant part is the providers that deliver VoIP and internet service over connections the customer already has: hosted voice, UCaaS and SIP-trunk providers riding someone else's broadband. Both buy the network as an input, own the customer, the number and the bill, and carry the same federal obligations as a carrier (USF contributions, robocall mitigation, 911, CPNI) without owning the plant. The rest of 517810 (satellite tracking, telemetry, radar stations) is touched only by the ground-station work in [[industries/satellite-operators|Satellite Operators]], which is built on the separate satellite telecommunications code, and isn't analysed here or as an industry of its own. Carriers that hold spectrum belong to [[industries/wireless-carriers|Wireless Carriers]], authorised dealers paid activation commissions belong to [[industries/wireless-retail-agents|Wireless Retail Agents]], and MSPs that resell a seat as an agent belong to [[industries/it-managed-services|IT Managed Services]].
**Market Size:** ~$36B in 2022 receipts across the two codes (2022 Economic Census: 517121 at ~$14.7B across 955 firms; 517810 at ~$21.3B across 1,673 firms, of which ~$0.7B is legacy dial-up and non-broadband ISPs). The resale economy is larger than the codes: the biggest MVNOs sit inside cable operators (Charter's Spectrum Mobile had 11.8M lines at end-2025) and the biggest UCaaS providers are classified as software (RingCentral reported $2.5B of 2025 revenue), so ~$60B+ is a fair estimate of the whole (estimate).
**Tech Maturity:** Medium — the product is software-defined (softswitches, SIP trunks, eSIM provisioning, white-label UCaaS platforms), but the back office runs on other companies' records: port orders matched by hand against a losing carrier's CSR, host-network wholesale bills reconciled in spreadsheets, regulatory filings prepared by consultants, and the decision to let a new customer send traffic made by one experienced person's read.
**Workforce:** Founder-operators and small teams (County Business Patterns counts 2,107 of 3,744 reseller establishments under five employees in 2023), provisioning and porting specialists, NOC and tier-2 support engineers who read SIP traces, sales and channel managers working master agents and MSPs, billing and revenue-assurance analysts, fraud and traffic-vetting staff at wholesale and SIP-trunk providers, regulatory and tax compliance managers, and at MVNOs, product, pricing, retention and Lifeline enrolment teams. The Economic Census counts 22,547 employees in 517121 and 31,723 in 517810 (2022).

## Key Pain Themes
A voice reseller's most consequential decision is who to let onto the network. Wholesale and SIP-trunk providers earn per minute from customers whose traffic they can't see in advance, and the FCC holds every provider in the call path responsible for illegal robocalls. A provider whose certification is removed from the Robocall Mitigation Database is cut off: every downstream provider must stop carrying its traffic. The FCC removed more than 1,200 providers in August 2025 and 14 more in September 2026. The Industry Traceback Group ran 3,606 tracebacks in 2024, and every provider must answer one within 24 hours. Whether a new account is a legitimate call centre or a robocaller, and whether a change in its traffic is a campaign or a problem, is judged by a few experienced onboarding and NOC staff who "just know" from the sign-up, the caller IDs requested and the first day's call durations. Their judgement is written down nowhere and leaves when they do.

The rest of the work runs on records the reseller doesn't own. A port depends on the losing carrier's customer service record. Typical rejections are a misspelt company name, a suite number or a forgotten billing number, and they arrive two to three weeks after submission. An MVNO pays its host network by the line and the gigabyte and has to reconcile a wholesale bill it didn't compute. A support engineer chasing one-way audio is diagnosing a network path through a customer's firewall and an underlying carrier, and controls neither.

The clocks are federal and frequent. Form 499-A is due April 1 and Form 499-Q quarterly. CPNI certification and RMD recertification are due March 1. The proposed USF contribution factor for Q4 2026 is 42.0%, a record. Taxes and fees now make up 28.8% of an average wireless bill (Tax Foundation, 2026), and every dollar must be classified by jurisdiction and by whether it is resold. Lifeline resellers recertify ~8 million subscribers against eligibility rules, and copper retirement is pushing businesses' alarm and elevator lines onto replacement services.

## Current Tech Landscape
Voice and UCaaS resellers run on white-label platforms such as Cisco BroadWorks and NetSapiens, or on wholesale and CPaaS networks such as Bandwidth, Twilio and Telnyx. MVNOs run on an enabler's BSS and charging stack such as Telgoo5, and billing on telecom-specific platforms such as Rev.io, with communications tax from Avalara or CCH SureTax. Calls are signed for STIR/SHAKEN with an SPC token from iconectiv through vendors such as TransNexus. Robocall labelling comes from call-analytics engines (Hiya, TNS, First Orion), and 911 location comes from providers such as RedSky. Each is competent inside its boundary. The record nobody owns is the customer's history joined end to end: who they said they were, what traffic they sent, which complaints and tracebacks followed, and what the provider did about it. The same goes for the port order's trail across both carriers and for the classification of each billed dollar across invoice, tax return and Form 499.

## Analysis
A telecom reseller owns the customer and rents everything else, so its central judgement is about customers rather than networks: whose traffic to carry, whose port to trust, which subscriber will stay long enough to repay the cost of winning them. The trust judgement matters most, because it is the one the FCC now enforces with removal from the call path. It is unmeasured because its labels are sparse and late. A traceback arrives weeks after the calls, covers a few thousand campaigns a year across the whole industry, and names the provider rather than the account. Experienced staff fill the gap with pattern recognition nobody has recorded. Three things block a fix: commercial (every bad customer was paying revenue until it wasn't), legal (CPNI rules limit how call records and customer data can be pooled across providers) and structural (most resellers are tiny: 56% of reseller establishments have fewer than five employees). The operators are therefore poor buyers one at a time. The strongest openings sit with the platforms that host thousands of them (wholesale carriers, CPaaS networks and white-label UCaaS hosts), which see the traffic of many resellers at once and carry the downstream liability when one goes bad. Above the operators, the insight layer is thin. Its richest datasets (fraud records, telecom invoices, porting and numbering registries, member credit files) belong to someone else, and no pocket here clears the Pass 2 bar.

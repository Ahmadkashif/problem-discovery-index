# Online Tutoring Platforms

## Profile
**Category:** Platform Labour & Digital Work
**Market Size:** ~$8B US in online tutoring and homework help, a market substantially reshaped since 2023 as generative assistants absorbed the homework-answer segment that several large businesses depended on
**Tech Maturity:** Solid scheduling, video and payment infrastructure; no measurement of the thing being sold. Platforms match tutor to student on price, ratings and availability, take a share of the hourly rate, and neither party ever learns whether the tutoring produced any learning.
**Workforce:** Tutors as independent contractors, most part-time; matching and marketplace engineers; tutor quality and support teams; parent and student support; curriculum staff at the platforms that produce content

## Key Pain Themes
The platform sets the economics and holds the relationship. It determines the commission, ranks tutors in search, controls whether a tutor is surfaced to a student, and in most cases prohibits taking the relationship off-platform — which is commercially rational and means a tutor who builds a strong relationship with a family cannot convert it into an independent one. Commission rates in this sector have moved against tutors over time in several well-publicised cases, and a tutor has no leverage in that change.

The unpaid work is substantial and invisible. Lesson preparation, marking, communication with parents, writing progress notes and reviewing a student's material before a session are all necessary to tutoring well and are not compensated by a per-session rate. A tutor who prepares thoroughly earns less per hour worked than one who does not, and the rating system does not reliably distinguish them.

The third theme is that learning outcomes are unmeasured. Matching is by price, subject and rating; success is measured by rebooking. A tutor who is warm and ineffective rebooks well, and the platform's data cannot tell that apart from a tutor who is effective — which means its matching optimises for satisfaction rather than for learning.

## Current Tech Landscape
Marketplace platforms — Wyzant, Preply, Varsity Tutors and a large set of regional and subject-specific competitors — handle discovery, scheduling, video delivery and payment. Video classrooms include shared whiteboards, document sharing and recording. Some platforms supply curriculum and diagnostic assessments; most do not. Payment is per session with platform commission deducted. The homework-answer segment that supported businesses like Chegg has been substantially displaced by generative assistants, which has reshaped demand across the sector and put pressure on tutor rates.

## Analysis
A tutoring marketplace holds recordings of millions of one-to-one teaching sessions with the same students returning over months, alongside the assessments and grades that would indicate whether anything improved. That is an extraordinarily rich record of what effective teaching looks like, and it is used to compute a rebooking rate. The platform's matching, its tutor rankings and its commission structure all optimise satisfaction because satisfaction is what it can measure, and the consequence is a market where a tutor's income depends on being liked rather than on being effective — with the unpaid preparation that distinguishes the two systematically unrewarded. Measuring learning is genuinely harder than measuring rebooking, and it is the only thing that would align what the platform optimises with what families are buying.

# Title Insurance Carriers

## Profile
**Category:** Insurance
**NAICS 2022:** 524127 — Direct Title Insurance Carriers
**Market Size:** ~$18.5B in title premiums written in 2025, up 13.8% (ALTA; NAIC counts $18.6B of direct premiums written), with $807.7M of losses and loss adjustment expense incurred and a 102.8% combined ratio (NAIC); Census counts 1,004 firms and $22.4B of receipts in 2022, 92% of it at 24 firms with 500+ employees (SUSB)
**Tech Maturity:** Medium — policy issuance, agent portals and title plants are digital, and automated decisioning has reached low-risk refinances, but underwriting approvals, claims handling and agent oversight still run on counsel's judgement, email and spreadsheets
**Workforce:** Underwriting counsel and commercial underwriters, claims counsel and claims administrators, agency managers and field auditors, policy and remittance processors, actuaries and statistical reporting staff, title plant and data staff, and the examiners and escrow officers of the carriers' own direct operations; First American alone employs 19,102 people, and BLS counts 13,390 title examiners, abstractors and searchers inside insurance carriers (May 2025)

## Key Pain Themes
A title underwriter sells a promise about the past. The policy insures that the recorded chain of title is what the commitment said it was, and it pays when that turns out to be wrong: a forged deed, a mortgage never released, a missed judgment, an heir nobody found. Claims are rare and slow. Title insurers pay out a few cents per premium dollar (ALTA counts $667M of claims paid in 2025 against $18.5B of premium), and First American reports that only 65–75% of claim amounts become known in a policy's first six years. The consequence sits on the balance sheet: of First American's $1.15B in title claims reserves at the end of 2025, $1.10B was IBNR, and a 50 basis point miss on the last six policy years moves that reserve by $162.7M. The judgement that decides how much each claim costs belongs to claims counsel: whether to cure the title, defend the insured, negotiate with the adverse claimant or pay. That call is made early, on a thin file, and graded years later by nobody. Fraud has made it heavier. A Milliman study for ALTA found fraud and forgery claims averaged over $143,000 against about $26,000 for everything else, and in 2025 one in four title firms that saw a seller-impersonation attempt went on to pay a claim.

Most of the premium never reaches the underwriter. Non-affiliated agents wrote 62.7% of direct premium in 2025, and amounts paid to or retained by agents were 72.6% of the industry's operating expenses (NAIC). The underwriter keeps the risk and writes the rules, while thousands of independent agents search, examine, close and hold the escrow money. So the carrier's daily work is oversight at a distance. Underwriting counsel field approval requests above an agent's authority on closing day. Agency managers decide which agents to appoint and keep. Field auditors reconcile escrow accounts that, under closing protection letters and some states' defalcation statutes, the underwriter may end up making good. Remittance staff chase policies that were issued and never reported.

Outside clocks govern the rest. In Texas the commissioner sets the rates: a 10% cut ordered in February 2025 went to appeal, and Order 2025-9697 cut basic rates by 6.2% from 1 March 2026 on statistical data the agents and underwriters supply. New York and Pennsylvania rates are filed through licensed rating bureaus. Statutory annual statements, Schedule P development and state audits run on regulators' calendars. And the product itself is under pressure from the GSEs: Fannie Mae's title acceptance pilot added Westcor as a second vendor in July 2025 for refinances under 80% loan-to-value, letting an automated review decide whether a lender needs a policy at all.

## Current Tech Landscape
Agents issue jackets and closing protection letters through underwriter portals such as First American's AgentNet, on top of production systems (SoftPro, Qualia, RamQuest) they choose themselves. The four families own most of the country's title plants, profiled as a Pass 2 pocket under [[industries/land-surveyors|Land Surveyors]]; the agent-side workflows sit under [[industries/title-and-settlement-offices|Title & Settlement Offices]]. Doma's engine (now inside Title Resources Group), WFG's DecisionPoint and ANTIC's REalSearch clear low-risk refinances without an examiner, and AI review of search packages arrived in 2026 with First American's AgentNet Assist. Claims, approvals and agency audits run on in-house systems, general claims and document platforms, email and spreadsheets. Each tool covers one step. None holds the record that joins an underwriting decision, the agent who made it, the claim that followed and what it finally cost.

## Analysis
The industry's central unmeasured judgement is the claims counsel's early read of a claim: cured with a phone call, defended through a quiet-title action or paid as a full loss of title. That read sets the case reserve, drives loss adjustment expense and, in aggregate, the IBNR assumptions that dominate the balance sheet, and it is rarely recorded in a form that can be graded. Two more ungraded judgements sit behind it: the underwriting approval that let the risk through, and the agency manager's sense of which agent is in trouble. Three things block a fix. Outcomes arrive years late and each counsel sees few claims of any one type in any one state. The evidence is split across the agent's file, the approval email and the claim file. And coverage positions carry bad-faith exposure, so no carrier will let a model decide coverage. The carriers own the claims corpus but are poor Pass 2 buyers: their analysis prices a premium rather than being invoiced, and the industry's research assets are already profiled elsewhere in the vault. The commercial opening is inside the carriers: claims triage and grading, agent-risk monitoring on escrow and remittance data, and an approval desk that records its reasons, all buildable on data the carrier already owns. The regional underwriters, which cannot staff the data science, are the most reachable buyers.

**The tool:** Press+ — a hosted metered paywall that let a newspaper give readers a set number of free pageviews a month and then charge, with the price, the meter and the bundle set by the publisher
**Builder:** Journalism Online
**Builder in vault:** **ABSENT**
**Verification:** partial — see Sources

## The Problem That Came First

A local paper's website was built to be free, and by 2009 free was not paying for the newsroom.

The industry had put its journalism online on the advertising model, which worked while print carried the cost. Once classified and display advertising collapsed, the website was a large and growing audience that paid nothing, run by a company whose print revenue was shrinking every year.

Charging was the obvious answer and a hard one to execute. A fully closed site lost the search and social traffic that advertising still depended on. Building a subscription system — accounts, payments, access rules, a way to recognise a print subscriber online — was an engineering project that a paper with a few dozen journalists had no staff to run.

## What Got Built

**Press+**, a paid-content platform that a publisher could install around its existing site.

Its central mechanism was **the meter**: a reader got a set number of free pages each month, then hit a request to pay. The publisher chose the number and the price, and could sell day or week passes, monthly or annual subscriptions, and print-and-online bundles. The meter sat between a fully free site and a hard wall — casual readers from search got through; the heaviest users were asked to pay.

The first public deployment, in July 2010, shows how local the use case was. LancasterOnline, the site of the Lancaster newspapers, in Pennsylvania, metered only its obituaries, and only effectively for readers outside the county, who made up 52 percent of obituary viewers. Seven free obituary pages a month, then $1.99 a month or $19.99 a year. The editor thought the realistic yield was around $10,000 a year.

## Who Built It, And Why Them

**Journalism Online**, founded in April 2009 by Steven Brill and L. Gordon Crovitz with Leo Hindery Jr.

Crovitz had been publisher of *The Wall Street Journal*, which under his leadership had become the largest paid-subscription news site in the world, with over a million paying subscribers by the end of 2007. He left when News Corp acquired Dow Jones. Brill had founded *The American Lawyer* and Court TV. Between them they had run, at national scale, the one thing local papers had not: a newspaper website readers paid for.

**That is why it came from outsiders rather than from a newspaper chain.** The paid-content experience the industry lacked existed at a financial title with a readership that could expense it. The founders' proposition was to take that capability and rent it to publishers who could not build it themselves — for a cut of what they sold. Journalism Online was bought by RR Donnelley in March 2011 for a reported $45 million, and by the time Press+ merged into Piano Media in 2014 it served 560 customers, mostly in North America.

## What It Cost

**The meter reprices only the reader, and only the most loyal ones.** It asks the people who already read the paper most to pay for a newsroom that advertising once funded, and leaves the advertising problem exactly where it was. Lancaster's projected obituary revenue shows the gap between what a meter recovers and what a classified section used to carry.

**It made a paper's free allowance into a tuning variable.** The number of free pages became something to set, test and reset — Press+ reported that fewer free pageviews brought more subscriptions — which turned editorial reach and subscription revenue into two ends of the same dial.

And the platform was rented. A paper using it depended on a vendor's roadmap, fee and later owners for its route to the readers who paid.

## What You Still Touch

"You have 2 free articles remaining this month" on a local paper's site is the Press+ meter, whoever supplies it now.

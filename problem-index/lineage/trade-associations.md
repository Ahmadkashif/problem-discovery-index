## The Problem That Came First

An association's income arrives as a renewal, once a year, per member.

That single fact shapes the back office. The organisation has to know who belongs, at what membership class, paid through when, and who has not renewed yet. Around that core sit the other ledgers: meeting registrations, publication orders, committee rosters and the budget. Before packaged software, each of these tended to live wherever the staff member responsible kept it.

The expensive part was not storing any one list. It was that dues, events and budget were separate records of the same members. Every renewal season someone reconciled them by hand to see who owed what.

## What Got Built

A system organised around the member record and the dues transaction.

**Advanced Solutions International (ASI)** was founded in **1991** and launched **iMIS** that year. The company describes it as a complete packaged solution for association management. A contemporary *Washington Post* account described it as a program that helps professional associations with budgeting, membership tracking, dues collection and other tasks.

The category it helped define is now called an **association management system (AMS)**. The vault's own industry note lists iMIS first among the AMS vendors, alongside Aptify, MemberClicks and YourMembership. Its described scope is dues processing, event registration and basic CRM.

Adoption can be dated only through named customers. A 1996 account reported the United Fresh Fruit and Vegetable Association using iMIS from 1992. By 1996 its clients reportedly included the US Tennis Association, the NFL Players Association and the American Electronics Association.

## Who Built It, And Why Them

**Advanced Solutions International**, founded by **Bob Alves** and **Don Robertson** as a six-person start-up with two US locations. It is headquartered in **Alexandria, Virginia**.

The company says it chose the name "International" as an ambition, not a description. It now has offices in the US, UK, Canada and Australia.

What is established is that ASI was a vertical vendor from day one. It did not start as a general accounting or CRM house that later added an association module. Its product was framed around the transactions specific to membership organisations. The obvious explanation is geography: Alexandria sits beside Washington, DC, where national associations cluster to be near federal government. That would put a large pool of buyers with the same dues-driven back office within a short drive of the founders. **That explanation is inference.** I could not reach a source giving the founders' own reasoning, and I have not established the density figure that would support it.

Also not established: whether iMIS was the first packaged AMS. The company's "only complete, all-in-one" language is marketing, not evidence of priority.

## What It Cost

The system was built around the event that pays, the renewal. It was not built around the behaviour that predicts it.

An AMS records whether a member renewed, what they paid and which meetings they registered for. It knows transactions. Engagement ended up elsewhere: forum posts, email opens, committee work and hallway conversation. Higher Logic hosts communities, Informz and Real Magnet send email, and Cvent runs events. The vault records that engagement scores exist but are rule-based and rarely calibrated against actual renewal outcomes. None of that is a flaw in 1991 terms. It is what happens when the system of record is shaped around the invoice.

## What You Still Touch

A membership director running renewal outreach from a list of lapsed paid-through dates is using the member-and-dues record iMIS packaged. The same director is guessing at who is drifting, because the signals that would say so live in four other systems.

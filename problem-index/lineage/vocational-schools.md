**The tool:** the ACCSC Graduation and Employment Chart: a per-program cohort table of starts, graduates and graduates employed in a training-related field, judged against a 70% employment benchmark
**Builder:** ACCSC
**Builder in vault:** **ABSENT**

## The Problem That Came First

A trade school sells a job. The school does not control whether a job follows.

A welding or HVAC program is bought on one promise: finish this and you will be employed in the trade. Federal student aid pays for much of it. So the question of whether graduates actually get jobs matters to three parties at once: the student, the school and the taxpayer.

Before the 1992 reauthorisation of the Higher Education Act, fraud and abuse among proprietary schools, and weak oversight by their accreditors, were the stated concern. Placement claims were a school's own marketing. There was no common way to count them, so no way to compare them.

## What Got Built

A form that turns a cohort into two ratios.

The **Graduation and Employment Chart** ("G&E Chart") is filed per program for a reporting period set by the program's length. For a 12-month program reported in July 2016, for example, the cohort is students who started between October 2013 and September 2014. Each start is counted forward to 150 per cent of normal program length.

Line by line the chart records starts, graduates and students removed as "unavailable for graduation", for example for military deployment. It then records graduates employed **in a training-related field**, and graduates removed as "unavailable for employment", for example for a medical condition. Two rates fall out: graduation and employment.

The Commission compares each program's rates to benchmarks. The employment benchmark is **70 per cent**. ACCSC's own reports describe the benchmarks as set one standard deviation below average reported rates, as the point where heightened monitoring begins. Every number must be **verifiable through documentation**: transcripts, and records of each graduate's initial employment. In **2014** ACCSC required every institution seeking initial or renewed accreditation to submit graduate employment records for independent third-party verification.

## Who Built It, And Why Them

**ACCSC**, the Accrediting Commission of Career Schools and Colleges.

It began in **1965** as the accrediting commission of a trade association, the National Association of Trade and Technical Schools (NATTS). It was federally recognised in 1967. In **1993** the accrediting unit separated from the association and became independent, as ACCSCT. It took its present name in October 2009.

The separation was not voluntary housekeeping. The **Higher Education Amendments of 1992** (Pub. L. 102-325) required a recognised accreditor to be "separate and independent" of any related trade association. The same statute required it to assess student achievement, "including, as appropriate, consideration of State licensing examinations, consideration of course completion, and job placement rates."

That is why the chart exists and why an accreditor built it. The accreditor holds the gate to federal aid eligibility, and Congress had just told it to judge placement. It needed a count that was identical across hundreds of schools and auditable on a site visit. A school had every reason to define "placed" generously. The accreditor was the party with the mandate and the leverage to fix the definition.

## What It Cost

**It measures initial, training-related employment at one point in time.** It does not measure wages, retention, or whether the job existed a year later. A graduate who takes a training-related job for a week counts. One who takes a better-paid job outside the field does not.

It also placed the evidence burden on the school. The records must be assembled cohort by cohort, graduate by graduate, and re-assembled at each review. The chart made placement comparable by making it a documentation task.

## What You Still Touch

The career services coordinator making cold calls to confirm where last spring's graduates are working is gathering the chart's documentation. The months of self-study assembly before a review are the chart's evidence file.

# Lineage: Wealth Management RIAs

**Wave:** [[series/eras/wave-01-mainframe-batch|1 — Mainframe & Batch]]
**The tool:** the Investment Adviser Registration Depository — the electronic Form ADV filing system and its public IAPD record
**Builder:** FINRA
**Builder in vault:** **ABSENT**
**Verification:** verified — see Sources

## The Problem That Came First

"RIA" is not a qualification. It is a filing.

The Investment Advisers Act of 1940 made advising for compensation a *registered* activity rather than a licensed profession — no competence examination, no entry standard, only an obligation to tell the regulator who you are, what you charge and what you have done wrong. The artefact of that obligation is **Form ADV**, and for six decades it was paper.

That works for one adviser in one state and degrades badly beyond it: a firm doing business in a dozen states filed a dozen times, paid a dozen fees, renewed on a dozen calendars. **The regulators were in the same position as the filers.** No jurisdiction could see the population it supervised, and a prospective client had nowhere to look.

## What Got Built

One national depository that every adviser files into and every regulator reads from.

**From January 2001, SEC-registered advisers filed Part 1 of Form ADV electronically through IARD**, and all federally registered advisers had to be registered electronically by **30 April 2001**. One submission satisfies the SEC and every state selected on it. Fees are metered by assets under management and drawn from a prefunded **IARD Daily Account** — nothing files until the money is sitting there — which quietly solved the collection problem that made paper registration unscalable.

Two mechanics still shape the industry's year. The **annual updating amendment is due within 90 days of fiscal year end**, so a December-year profession files in the last week of March. And **Form ADV Part 2A**, adopted July 2010, replaced a check-the-box schedule with a **plain-English narrative brochure**, first due 31 March 2011.

## Who Built It, And Why Them

The SEC and NASAA sponsored it. **NASD Regulation — now FINRA — built and operates it under contract to both.**

The stated reason is instructive: FINRA was chosen for its regulatory and technical expertise and for the success of **Web CRD, its web-based licensing system deployed in 1999** for broker-dealers. The Central Registration Depository behind it had been collecting registrations, routing fees and holding disclosure records across every US jurisdiction.

**That is why them, and it is not about software.** The hard part of national registration is the plumbing, not the form — a different fee from a different regulator for the same entity, tens of thousands of renewals on one cycle, a disclosure record many authorities write to and none owns. NASD had built that machine once already, for a different industry.

So the SEC did not build its own filing infrastructure. It rented one — from a self-regulatory organisation funded by the brokerage industry that competes with independent advisers for the same clients.

## What It Cost

**Disclosure instead of a standard.** IARD records what an adviser says about itself and is indifferent to whether the advice is any good. Form ADV captures AUM, fees, conflicts, custody and disciplinary history; it has never asked about competence, because the system inherited the 1940 Act's decision not to. So compliance became **document maintenance** — an examiner tests whether the narrative filed in March still describes the firm in October, which is a paper trail rather than an outcome.

And the register became a market. Every adviser's assets, client counts, custodians and fees are public and machine-readable through IAPD, so the filing is now raw material for recruiting, prospecting and practice valuation — a disclosure artefact turned into the industry's commercial database, which nobody designed and nobody can opt out of.

## What You Still Touch

Anyone can pull your adviser's regulatory life out of IAPD in ten seconds — and almost nobody does, because the document it returns was written for an examiner, not a client.

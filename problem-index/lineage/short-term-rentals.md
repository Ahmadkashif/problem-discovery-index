# Lineage: Short-Term Rentals

**Industry:** [[industries/short-term-rentals|Short-Term Rentals]]
**Wave:** [[series/eras/wave-05-commercial-web|5 — The Commercial Web]]
**The tool:** the VRBO listing — a flat-fee annual classified ad on vrbo.com carrying an owner's photos, description and direct contact details, with the site taking no part in the booking or the payment
**Builder:** VRBO
**Builder in vault:** **ABSENT**
**Verification:** partial — see Sources

## The Problem That Came First

An owner who wanted to rent a ski condo to strangers had to hand it to someone who already knew the strangers.

Guests for resort property came through local property management companies. They held the phone lines, the brochures and the walk-in trade, and they priced that reach accordingly: by the founder's own account, the standard commission in his market was around **45% of rental revenue.** The alternative was to find guests yourself, which in practice meant paying for classified advertising in a national magazine and answering the phone.

So distribution cost almost half the product. The owner had the asset; the manager had the audience; and nothing connected a family in another state to the owner directly.

## What Got Built

A website of classified ads. **Vacation Rentals By Owner** launched in **1995**, and each listing was a page of photos and text about one property, with the owner's contact details. The guest found the page, then called or emailed the owner. Everything after that — dates, deposits, keys, cleaning — happened off the site.

The site charged owners a flat subscription, not a cut. The founder says the first 250 listings were free and the charge after that was **$72 a year**; he describes running it until 2006 without ever inserting VRBO into the transaction — "We always treated it like a classified ad in the newspaper." By 2006 the site carried more than 465,000 listings.

## Who Built It, And Why Them

**David Clouse**, a programmer in Aurora, Colorado, who worked on reservation systems at United Airlines and owned a condo in Breckenridge. He had moved to Colorado in 1987 and bought the ski unit; refusing the management commission, he first advertised it himself in *Ski Magazine*'s classifieds — about $700 for five months, plus an 800 number.

That is the whole business case in miniature. Clouse was the customer before he was the builder: an owner already paying to reach guests directly, who knew how to put a listing on the web and could see that a web page cost less than a magazine ad and reached more people. He built for owners like himself, which is why the product was a subscription sold to owners rather than a commission taken from bookings — his grievance *was* the commission.

The site stayed a side project for three years. He left United in **1998**, when VRBO's revenue passed his salary, and sold to **HomeAway in February 2006**; Expedia bought HomeAway, VRBO included, for $3.9 billion, announced in November 2015.

## What It Cost

**A classified ad knows nothing about the booking.** The model that made VRBO cheap for owners gave it no calendar, no transaction data and no price information — and gave owners no protection from guests or guests from fraud, because the site was deliberately not a party.

It also invited in the people it was built to route around. Clouse offered a **10% discount to owners with more than ten properties**, and has said since that he would instead charge property managers more. The managers came anyway.

The flat fee did not survive its new owner. In **February 2016** HomeAway imposed a traveller service fee of roughly 5–9% and pushed owners toward pay-per-booking — turning the classified ad into a transaction platform taking a commission, the arrangement Clouse had built it to escape.

## What You Still Touch

The owner still sets the nightly price. That was a side effect of a classified ad — the site never priced anything because it never saw the booking — and it is why short-term rental pricing is still an owner's guess with a tool bolted on, rather than a hotel's revenue-management system.

# Exchanges & Market Makers

**Layer:** Origin — a parent industry, not a prospect
**Primary Wave:** [[series/eras/wave-01-mainframe-batch|1 — Mainframe & Batch]] *(stretches forward to Wave 9 — the story does not stop in the 1970s)*
**Founding event:** Instinet, the first electronic communication network, live December 1969; NASDAQ quotation system launched 8 Feb 1971
**Children in this vault:** crypto exchanges, robo-advisors, wealth-management RIAs

## Profile

**What it is:** Matching buyers and sellers of securities, and standing ready to be the counterparty when a natural match is not immediately available. The product traded is, literally, a price — and increasingly, the *speed* at which that price is known.

**Who pays:** Every investor pays the bid-ask spread, whether they notice it or not. For most of this industry's history that spread was set, in practice, by a small number of dealers who quoted both sides of the market — and, as this origin's [[origins/exchanges-market-makers/the-fight|fight]] shows, sometimes quoted it wider than a competitive market would have produced.

**The economics:** Revenue comes from capturing the spread, from exchange fees, and — in the industry's most recent and most contested phase — from being structurally first to see a price change and trading on it before the rest of the market catches up. Few industries in this vault have a product so literally reducible to information latency.

## Why This Is an Origin

This origin contains this vault's single cleanest teaching case for **technology as the product, not the delivery mechanism for the product.** A yield-management system, an EHR, a credibility-weighted premium — all of these are tools that help a business decide something. In modern securities trading, the technology that gets a quote across a wire microseconds faster *is* the competitive advantage, undiluted by any other business logic. See [[origins/exchanges-market-makers/the-mechanism|the mechanism]] for the sharpest version of that story: a $300M physical asset built to win that game, obsoleted within two years by a cheaper one.

## The Contested Decision

> **When a price can be known everywhere in the market within microseconds, who gets to know it first — and what should the rules do about that?**

This question has had at least three different regulatory and technological answers across sixty years, and none of them has been final. See [[origins/exchanges-market-makers/the-fight|The Fight]].

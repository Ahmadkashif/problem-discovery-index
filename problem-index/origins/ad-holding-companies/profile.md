# Ad Holding Companies

**Layer:** Origin — a parent industry, not a prospect
**Primary Wave:** [[series/eras/wave-09-programmatic|9 — Programmatic]]
**Founding event:** The 15% media commission, codified as industry standard by the 1920s; Right Media exchange live 1 April 2005; ANA/K2 Intelligence transparency report published 7 June 2016; Omnicom's acquisition of IPG closed 26 November 2025
**Children in this vault:** marketing agencies (SMB), performance marketing agencies, programmatic ad platforms, marketing attribution vendors, retail media networks, app marketing firms

## Profile

**What it is:** Groups of advertising, media-buying, PR and data agencies operated under a single corporate parent — WPP, Omnicom, Publicis, IPG, Havas — that buy media on behalf of advertiser clients and, increasingly, operate the technology that prices and places that media in real time.

**Who pays:** Advertisers, historically through a commission paid *by the media owner* rather than billed directly to the advertiser — a structure that put the agency's fee inside the same transaction the agency was supposed to be negotiating on the advertiser's behalf. That structure is the whole story.

**The economics:** Aggregation of buying power and, since the mid-2000s, of data and bidding technology. A holding company's value proposition is scale — better rates from media owners, better data than any single advertiser could build alone — but scale is also what let the compensation model drift from a fee the advertiser could see into a set of rebates and margins the advertiser frequently could not.

## Why This Is an Origin

This industry contains the vault's clearest documented case of an intermediary's incentives diverging from its client's for a century, being formally investigated, and having the divergence measured. See [[origins/ad-holding-companies/the-fight|The Fight]] for the numbers.

It is also the origin of **programmatic real-time bidding**, the mechanism by which media buying was turned from a negotiated, human, block-purchase process into an automated per-impression auction — see [[origins/ad-holding-companies/the-mechanism|The Mechanism]].

## The Contested Decision

> **Whose interest does the agency serve, when it is paid — in cash, in rebates, or in undisclosed margin — by the same media owner whose price it is supposed to be negotiating down on the advertiser's behalf?**

The industry answered this question differently in different eras without ever fully resolving it, and the 2016 transparency report is the moment the answer was measured rather than assumed.

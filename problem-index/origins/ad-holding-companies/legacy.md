# Legacy: What Ad Holding Companies Bequeathed

**Origin:** [[origins/ad-holding-companies/profile|Ad Holding Companies]]

## The Direct Inheritance

| Child | What it inherited |
|---|---|
| [[industries/marketing-agencies-smb\|Marketing Agencies (SMB)]] | The same commission/fee-transparency tension, at a scale with even less leverage to demand disclosure than a large advertiser has — small buyers lack the ANA member base's sophistication, which is why the in-housing figures below should not be read as market-wide. |
| [[industries/performance-marketing-agencies\|Performance Marketing Agencies]] | A model built to exist *inside* the mechanism in [[origins/ad-holding-companies/the-mechanism|The Mechanism]] — compensation tied to measurable outcome, positioned as the transparent alternative to the rebate-based model the 2016 report exposed. |
| [[industries/programmatic-ad-platforms\|Programmatic Ad Platforms]] | The direct technical descendant of Right Media and DoubleClick Ad Exchange — the exchange/DSP/SSP architecture that turned media buying into a per-impression auction. |
| [[industries/marketing-attribution-vendors\|Marketing Attribution Vendors]] | Born from a need programmatic itself created: once buying happened per-impression with granular bid data, advertisers wanted to know which impressions drove outcomes — a measurement problem that barely existed when media was bought in blocks. |
| [[industries/retail-media-networks\|Retail Media Networks]] | The RTB auction, re-deployed inside a retailer's own digital shelf — Amazon Ads and peers are structurally the same architecture Right Media built, with the retailer now standing in the media-owner position agencies used to negotiate against. |
| [[industries/app-marketing-firms\|App Marketing Firms]] | The **mobile RTB wave of 2013**, and its abrupt limit: [[series/eras/wave-09-programmatic|Wave 9]] records this era "ended" by **Apple's App Tracking Transparency, iOS 14.5, 26 April 2021** — the one wave in the spine with a clean death date. |

## The Consolidation Endpoint

**Omnicom announced its acquisition of Interpublic Group (IPG) on 8 December 2024.** The FTC cleared it on **23 June 2025**, and the deal closed on **26 November 2025** — an all-stock transaction valued at roughly **$13 billion**, with Omnicom shareholders holding 60.6% of the combined company and former IPG shareholders 39.4%. The result is the largest advertising holding group by revenue, with combined revenue above **$25 billion** and targeted synergies of roughly **$750 million**.

This consolidation is the practical answer to the margin pressure [[origins/ad-holding-companies/the-mechanism|The Mechanism]] describes: rather than each group absorbing programmatic's fee compression alone, the largest players combine to spread fixed technology and data costs across a bigger revenue base — the same scale logic that has justified agency consolidation since the industry's earliest arguments for size.

## The In-Housing Trend, Correctly Caveated

Advertiser in-housing of media functions — bringing programmatic buying, and sometimes creative, inside the advertiser's own organisation rather than routing it through an agency — has grown by ANA member survey from **42% in 2008, to 58% in 2013, to 78% in 2018, to 82% in 2023**, with the ANA projecting a ceiling around 85–90%.

**This figure needs its caveat attached every time it is used.** It is drawn from **ANA members**, who are self-reporting and skew toward larger, more marketing-sophisticated advertisers than the market as a whole. Treat 82% as a plausible **upper bound** for large, sophisticated advertisers — not as a market-wide adoption rate for every business buying media.

## What an Episode Should Take From This

1. **A compensation structure's death is rarely a single event.** The 15% commission took over a century to fully unwind, and arguably never fully did — it changed shape and moved to a harder-to-see layer, as [[origins/ad-holding-companies/the-mechanism|The Mechanism]] documents.
2. **Exposure changes negotiating leverage, not necessarily the underlying incentive.** The 2016 report gave advertisers language and evidence to demand disclosure; it did not remove the agency's incentive to prefer the better-paying arrangement when disclosure isn't enforced.
3. **Consolidation and margin compression can coexist without contradiction.** The largest players are combining and expanding margin at the same time programmatic squeezes the middle of the market — because scale, not technique, is what absorbs a fee-compression shock.

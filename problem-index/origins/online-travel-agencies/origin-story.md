# Origin Story: The Layer Under the Layer

**Origin:** [[origins/online-travel-agencies/profile|Online Travel Agencies]]
**Wave:** [[series/eras/wave-05-commercial-web|5 — The Commercial Web]]

## What Was True Before

By the mid-1990s, booking a flight or a hotel room meant a travel agent querying a Global Distribution System — a terminal-based descendant of the reservation infrastructure [[origins/airlines/origin-story|Airlines' origin story]] describes. The traveller never touched the system. The agent did, for a commission the supplier paid.

Three GDSs mattered: **Sabre**, built by American Airlines and running nationwide from 1964, spun off into an independent company with its own 2000 IPO; **Amadeus**, founded in **1987** by Air France, Lufthansa, Iberia and SAS as the European carriers' answer to Sabre's dominance; and **Galileo**, which merged with **Worldspan** to form **Travelport** in 2001. Every online travel agency that followed was, underneath its consumer-facing brand, a website that queried one or more of these three systems.

> **Be precise about this date.** Amadeus is frequently conflated with Sabre's 1960s origin, as if all GDSs date from the same moment. They do not. Amadeus is a full generation younger — founded 1987, twenty-seven years after Sabre's first live operation — built specifically because European carriers did not want to depend on an American one.

## What Got Built on Top

**Expedia** was founded inside Microsoft on **22 October 1996**, spun off as an independent company in 1999 and fully separated by its 2001 IPO. **Travelocity** was not a startup at all — it was launched by **Sabre itself in 1996**, the GDS operator building a consumer storefront on its own inventory rather than waiting for someone else to do it. **Priceline** was founded in 1997 by inventor and entrepreneur Jay Walker; its consumer service — the "Name Your Own Price" model discussed in [[origins/online-travel-agencies/the-mechanism|The Mechanism]] — launched in **April 1998**.

**Booking.com** started earlier and smaller than its later dominance suggests: founded in **1996** in Amsterdam as a scrappy Dutch site called **Bookings.nl**, years away from the market position it would eventually hold.

## The Acquisition That Decided the Industry

The single most consequential transaction in this origin's history is not a launch. It is Priceline's purchase of Booking.com in **July 2005**, for approximately **$133 million**.

At the time this was a modest deal for a company (Priceline) that had spent the early 2000s recovering from the dot-com collapse. It is now widely described in industry retrospectives as **the best acquisition in internet history**: Priceline Group went from a $19 million loss in 2002 to roughly $1.1 billion in profit by 2011, driven overwhelmingly by Booking.com's hotel-commission model scaling across Europe and then globally.

The lesson generalises past travel: the winning business was not the one with the more famous American brand (Priceline, Expedia, Travelocity) but the one with the cleanest, most scalable unit economics (Booking.com's take-rate-on-commission model, requiring no inventory risk), acquired for a price that looked unremarkable at the time it was paid.

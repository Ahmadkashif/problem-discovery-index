# Online Travel Agencies

**Layer:** Origin — a parent industry, not a prospect, and itself a child of [[origins/airlines/profile|Airlines]]
**Primary Wave:** [[series/eras/wave-05-commercial-web|5 — The Commercial Web]]
**Founding event:** Booking.com founded 1996 in Amsterdam (as Bookings.nl); Expedia founded 22 October 1996 inside Microsoft; Priceline founded 1997, consumer service launched April 1998; Priceline's acquisition of Booking.com, July 2005
**Children in this vault:** short-term rentals, boutique hotels, online marketplaces

## Profile

**What it is:** A retail layer that sits between the traveller and the supplier — airline, hotel, car-hire desk — and sells inventory it does not own, on margin the supplier grants it for access to demand the supplier cannot generate as cheaply itself.

**Who pays:** Nominally, nobody extra — the OTA's cut is folded into the price or paid as a commission by the hotel or airline. In practice the traveller pays through reduced supplier willingness to discount direct, and the supplier pays through a commission that, for hotels, still commonly runs 15–25% of the booking.

**The economics:** Near-zero marginal cost per additional booking once the platform exists, funded almost entirely by performance marketing — OTAs are among the largest buyers of search advertising on earth, which is a detail worth sitting with: the intermediary that displaced the travel agent spends its margin buying its own customers back from Google.

## Why This Is an Origin

Online travel agencies are the point where the airlines' distribution fight — who stands between the carrier and the passenger, and who profits from that position — moved from the terminal-based GDS network onto the open web. [[origins/airlines/legacy|Airlines' legacy]] file already flags this: "the GDS layer that grew out of SABRE then became its own industry — and then the fight over who controlled distribution moved to the web." This is that industry.

It is also the cleanest documented case in the vault of an **intended disintermediation producing the opposite of its intent** — see [[origins/online-travel-agencies/the-fight|The Fight]].

## The Contested Decision

> **If you let a technology layer stand between you and your customer because it is cheaper than doing distribution yourself, what stops that layer from becoming a more powerful gatekeeper than the one you were trying to remove?**

Every airline that cut agent commissions to push travellers online, and every hotel that lists on Booking.com to reach demand it cannot otherwise buy, is answering this question in the negative and living with the answer.

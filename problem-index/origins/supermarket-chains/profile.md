# Supermarket Chains

**Layer:** Origin — a parent industry, not a prospect
**Primary Wave:** [[series/eras/wave-02-departmental-item-level|2 — Departmental & Item-Level]]
**Founding event:** First commercial UPC scan, June 26 1974, 8:01am, Marsh Supermarket, Troy, Ohio
**Children in this vault:** retail POS platforms, retail media networks, independent retailers, subscription commerce, specialty food retail

## Profile

**What it is:** High-volume, low-margin retail of packaged and fresh goods. Net margins of 1–3% are normal, which means the business is decided by inventory turns and shrink, not by price.

**Who pays:** Shoppers at the till — and, increasingly and invisibly, **the brands**, through slotting fees, trade promotion funds and retail media. A modern grocer's profit is disproportionately supplier money, not grocery money.

**The economics:** Thousands of SKUs, perishability on a clock, fixed shelf space, and a customer who will not wait. The scarce asset is not capital or labour. It is **shelf**, and the whole commercial apparatus exists to allocate and monetise it.

## Why This Is an Origin

The UPC is the second great "handle" in this spine — after MICR in [[series/eras/wave-01-mainframe-batch|Wave 1]] — and it is the one that reached the physical consumer world. It made every item on every shelf machine-readable, and everything downstream of that fact is inherited by half the retail industries in this vault.

It also created something with no prior existence: **selling the resulting data back to the people who made the products.**

## The Contested Decision

> **What goes on the shelf, and who pays for the space?**

Every grocer faces identical suppliers, similar real estate and a shopper who compares prices. The contested ground is assortment and space allocation — and after 1974, for the first time, that decision could be made from measured fact rather than the buyer's judgement.

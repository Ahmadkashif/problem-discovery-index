# Airlines

**Layer:** Origin — a parent industry, not a prospect
**Primary Wave:** [[series/eras/wave-01-mainframe-batch|1 — Mainframe & Batch]]
**Founding event:** SABRE — built 1957–60, first live 1960, nationwide 1964; yield management weaponised 1985
**Children in this vault:** boutique hotels, short-term rentals, charter bus operators, and every dynamic-pricing note in the index

## Profile

**What it is:** Scheduled passenger air transport. A capital-intensive business selling a product that ceases to exist at a fixed moment — a seat on a departed flight is not inventory, it is nothing.

**Who pays:** Passengers, in fare classes that differ by an order of magnitude for physically identical seats. The business traveller booking on Tuesday for Wednesday subsidises the leisure traveller who booked in March.

**The economics:** High fixed cost, near-zero marginal cost per passenger, and perishable inventory. Once the aircraft is flying, the cost of carrying one more passenger is a meal and some fuel. This combination — expensive to operate, free to fill, worthless once gone — is what makes the pricing decision the entire business.

## Why This Is an Origin

Airlines built the first large-scale real-time commercial computing system, and then built the first large-scale commercial optimisation system on top of it. Both are ancestors of things this vault documents as ordinary.

More importantly: **airlines are where the industry learned that the pricing decision is a prediction problem.** Every dynamic-pricing note in the vault — hotels, short-term rentals, charter buses, freight lanes, ride-hailing surge — is a restatement of a question American Airlines answered in 1985.

## The Contested Decision

> **How many seats do I refuse to sell cheaply today, betting that someone will pay more tomorrow?**

Answer too conservatively and the plane departs with empty seats worth nothing. Answer too aggressively and you turn away revenue you will never recover. Every competitor faces the identical question on every flight, and whoever answers it best wins the route.

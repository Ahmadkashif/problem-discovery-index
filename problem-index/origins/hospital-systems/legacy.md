# Legacy: Epic Everywhere, HITECH Nowhere

**Origin:** [[origins/hospital-systems/profile|Hospital Systems]]

## The Diagnostic Fact

Search this vault and **"Epic" appears in 92 files**, almost always as a constraint — the system a new tool has to integrate with, the interface a workflow has to work around, the vendor whose API access a startup has to negotiate. Search the same vault for **"HITECH" and it appears in zero.**

That gap is the entire legacy in one fact. Every downstream industry inherited the platform. None of them inherited the memory of why the platform exists, what it was paid for, or what it was supposed to achieve beyond "adopted." Epic reads, to everyone building near it today, as a fact of nature — an incumbent to integrate with — rather than what it actually is: the surviving winner of a vendor race that a 2009 statute funded and dated.

## The Direct Inheritance

| Child | What it inherited |
|---|---|
| [[industries/healthcare-practice-software|Healthcare Practice Software]] | The integration tax. Every practice-management and clinical tool built after 2015 competes for a place beside an EHR incumbent it did not choose and frequently cannot displace. |
| [[industries/medical-billing|Medical Billing]] | Structured, electronically generated claims data — and the same underlying billing complexity as before, now machine-readable rather than reduced. Digitisation did not simplify the CPT/ICD layer; it made the complexity queryable. |
| [[industries/telehealth-platforms|Telehealth Platforms]] | A regulatory and technical foundation — certified EHR infrastructure, structured problem lists, e-prescribing rails — built years before telehealth needed it, then repurposed rapidly in [[series/eras/wave-11-covid-dislocation|Wave 11]]. |
| [[industries/urgent-care|Urgent Care Centers]] | Certified EHR technology as a baseline cost of entry, whether or not a walk-in clinic's workflow resembles the hospital inpatient setting the certification criteria were largely designed around. |
| [[industries/home-health-agencies|Home Health Agencies]] | The same baseline, applied to care delivered outside any building with reliable connectivity — a mismatch the certification criteria did not anticipate. |
| [[industries/behavioral-health-clinics|Behavioral Health Clinics]] | A documented latecomer status. **Psychiatrists (as MDs) qualified as HITECH "eligible professionals"; psychologists and licensed clinical social workers, who deliver the majority of talk therapy, originally did not.** Behavioral health EHR adoption lagged the rest of the sector for exactly this reason, and it is a direct, traceable consequence of how the incentive was written, not a story about the sector being technologically conservative. |

## What an Episode Should Take From This

1. **A subsidy with a deadline can move an entire sector faster than any competitive dynamic in this vault.** 9% to 96% in seven years has no rival elsewhere in these origins.
2. **The proxy the subsidy measured became the ceiling, not the floor.** Interoperability was the stated goal and the weakest-enforced criterion; it remains the weakest-solved problem in health IT a decade later, and the 21st Century Cures Act's 2016 information-blocking rule is the government's own admission of that gap.
3. **Who the incentive's eligibility rules included and excluded still shows up as an adoption gap today.** Behavioral health's EHR lag is not a mystery to be solved with better software marketing. It is downstream of a 2009 statutory definition.

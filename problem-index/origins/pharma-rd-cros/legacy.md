# Legacy: What Pharma R&D and CROs Bequeathed

**Origin:** [[origins/pharma-rd-cros/profile|Pharma R&D and CROs]]

## The Direct Inheritance

| Child | What it inherited |
|---|---|
| [[industries/medical-device-mfg\|Medical Device Manufacturing]] | The same regulator-facing validation culture, transplanted almost intact: design controls, verification and validation records, and an audit trail discipline that traces to the same "prove it to an inspector, years later" requirement Part 11 established for trial data. |
| [[industries/data-labeling-services\|Data Labeling Services]] | Not the clinical content — the **outsourcing shape**. The CRO solved episodic, specialist, quality-critical work by building a standing third-party industry around it rather than asking every client to staff for peak demand. Data labeling as a service is the same structural answer applied to a different episodic, specialist, quality-critical task. |
| [[industries/healthcare-practice-software\|Healthcare Practice Software]] | The legal architecture of a valid electronic record — audit trails, access controls, signature-to-record binding — predates HIPAA's and Meaningful Use's own record-integrity requirements by roughly a decade. Part 11 is the older sibling EHR compliance rarely credits. |
| [[industries/compliance-consulting\|Compliance Consulting]] | The most direct descendant of all: an entire profession — computer system validation (CSV) consulting — exists specifically to produce the evidence package Part 11 requires, for sponsors and CROs who would rather buy that expertise than build it in-house every time. |

## The Deeper Inheritance: validation cost exceeds software cost

The single most transferable finding in this origin is stated plainly in [[origins/pharma-rd-cros/the-mechanism|The Mechanism]]: in a regulated-computing domain, **the expense is disproportionately in proving the system worked, not in building the system**. That is not unique to pharma — medical devices inherit it directly, and any FDE selling software into a regulated vertical (finance, aviation, energy) will meet a version of the same asymmetry — but pharma R&D is where the vault's research found it most cleanly documented and most honestly quantifiable.

## The Second Inheritance: the hype-gap discipline

[[origins/pharma-rd-cros/the-mechanism|The Mechanism]]'s treatment of AI-in-drug-discovery is not a one-off aside. It is a template for evaluating any AI claim in a pipeline with uneven validation cost: **ask which stage the claimed gain occupies, and weight the claim by how cheap it is to be wrong at that stage.** A pitch that is silent about which stage it targets should be treated as a pitch that has not yet located its own bottleneck.

## What an Episode Should Take From This

1. **A regulation, not a product, was the unlock — again.** Part 11 (1997) is this origin's version of E-13B (1956) and the UPC (1974): the industry-wide agreement on what counts as valid, not a piece of software, is what made a market possible.
2. **The adoption curve can be much slower than the enabling event.** Real trial units were still converting from paper as late as 2012, fifteen years after Part 11 took effect. Regulatory permission is necessary, not sufficient.
3. **Where the money is spent is not where the AI headlines are pointed.** Preclinical screening gets the press. Clinical validation gets the budget. An FDE should always ask which one a prospect is actually buying software for.

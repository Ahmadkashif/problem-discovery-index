# Pharma R&D and CROs

**Layer:** Origin — a parent industry, not a prospect
**Primary Wave:** [[series/eras/wave-04-client-server-erp|4 — Client–Server & ERP]]
**Founding event:** FDA 21 CFR Part 11 issued March 1997, effective 20 August 1997; CRO industry model formalised late 1970s–early 1980s, Quintiles founded 1982, Covance spun off from Corning 1997
**Children in this vault:** medical device manufacturing, data labeling services, healthcare practice software, compliance consulting

## Profile

**What it is:** Designing, running, and proving the validity of clinical trials — plus, increasingly, the preclinical discovery work that decides which compounds ever reach a trial. A regulated-computing environment where the software is often the easy part.

**Who pays:** Pharmaceutical and biotech sponsors, who pay Contract Research Organisations to run trials they have decided are inefficient to staff permanently in-house, given that trial volume for any single sponsor is episodic rather than continuous.

**The economics:** Enormous fixed cost per approved drug — commonly cited in the hundreds of millions to over a billion dollars per successful approval once failed candidates are amortised in — against a regulatory gate that can invalidate the entire spend if a single record cannot be proven trustworthy. This is the industry's defining trade: **the cost of validating that a system worked correctly frequently exceeds the cost of the system itself.**

## Why This Is an Origin

Pharma R&D produced the regulatory event that made electronic clinical-trial records legally usable at scale — see [[origins/pharma-rd-cros/origin-story|Origin Story]] — and it produced the outsourcing model that turned trial operations into a standing industry rather than an in-house department, one sponsor company at a time — see [[origins/pharma-rd-cros/the-fight|The Fight]].

It is also, as of 2026, the industry where the vault's honest scepticism about AI hype is most load-bearing: see [[origins/pharma-rd-cros/the-mechanism|The Mechanism]].

## The Contested Decision

> **Can you prove to a regulator, years after the fact, that this electronic record is exactly what your system produced at the moment it was produced — and is that proof, not the underlying software, actually the expensive part?**

Every EDC vendor, every CRO, and every recent AI-drug-discovery pitch answers this question implicitly. Most of the industry's real cost sits in the answer, not in the clinical or computational work the answer is meant to validate.

# Retail Banking

**Layer:** Origin — a parent industry, not a prospect
**Primary Wave:** [[series/eras/wave-01-mainframe-batch|1 — Mainframe & Batch]]
**Founding event:** ERMA unveiled Sept 1955, in production at Bank of America Sept 14 1959; MICR adopted as the national standard 1956
**Children in this vault:** payment processors, neobanks, BNPL providers, lending marketplaces, credit unions

## Profile

**What it is:** Deposit-taking and lending to consumers and small businesses. The bank borrows short from depositors and lends long to borrowers, earning the spread and managing the risk that the two do not line up.

**Who pays:** Depositors pay in forgone interest; borrowers pay in interest; everyone pays in fees. Increasingly, merchants pay through interchange — a fact that made [[origins/airlines/profile|an entire other industry]] possible and is invisible to almost every consumer.

**The economics:** Enormous transaction volume at vanishing per-transaction margin, against a fixed cost base of branches, compliance and core systems. Scale is everything, which is why computerisation arrived here first and why the resulting systems have proved impossible to remove.

## Why This Is an Origin

Retail banking produced the first large-scale commercial computer deployment — not a research machine, a *production* system doing the bank's actual work every night — and it produced the design that everything downstream inherited: **the overnight batch.**

Every fintech in this vault sits on rails whose timing was decided in the 1950s and 60s for reasons that no longer apply.

## The Contested Decision

> **How much of the bank's clerical work can be done by machine before the machine becomes the bank?**

The answer turned out to be: all of it, and then the machine became load-bearing in a way nobody could undo.

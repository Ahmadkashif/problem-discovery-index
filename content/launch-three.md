# Launch Three — Finance Research Workflows

The first three pieces for the personal brand. The packaging follows `content/what-gets-clicked.md` (one click lever + one value promise, short title, outsider's tension first). The substance comes from the Phase 4 vault layer.

**They work as a mini-series:**

1. **The question everyone has:** why hasn't AI fixed research?
2. **The bottleneck nobody names:** compliance clearance.
3. **The asset nobody uses:** the answer key that arrives every quarter.

That order goes from broad reach to trust to "this person sees things I don't". Each piece ends on a habit the viewer can start on Monday. That habit is also the exact workflow your company automates, so the commercial alignment is built in without a pitch.

**Each piece includes:**
- three title options (A/B test them)
- cover text
- the promise
- a 60–90 second video script (about 180–220 spoken words)
- a LinkedIn post version
- the line built to be repeated
- the vault receipts
- what not to claim

## 1. AI Was Supposed to Replace Analysts. Why Hasn't It?

| | |
|---|---|
| **Title A (lead)** | AI Was Supposed to Replace Analysts. Why Hasn't It? |
| **Title B** | AI Didn't Fix Research. Here's What It Missed. |
| **Title C** | Why Analysts Still Copy Numbers at 7 a.m. |
| **Cover text** | "AI fixed the easy part." |
| **Lever → promise** | Broken expectation → mechanism. This is the same shape as *"AI Was Supposed To Take Your Job. Why Hasn't It?"* (~12k views/day, 3.1% like rate, top decile on both). |
| **The line to repeat** | **"AI automated the parts of research that were already fast."** |

### Video script

> **[0–5s, cold open]** We were told AI would be doing the analyst's job by now. So why is a research team still copying numbers into Excel before the market opens?
>
> **[5–20s, the turn]** Because AI fixed the easy part. Getting the numbers was never the problem. You can already buy them clean, structured, linked to the filing.
>
> **[20–45s, the mechanism: three things it missed]**
> One: the last mile. The numbers have to land in *your* model, with your layout, your adjustments, your segment splits. That mapping lives in someone's head.
> Two: permission. Every expert call, every dataset needs compliance to say yes first. The queue doesn't care how fast your AI is.
> Three: judgement. The best analysts know which CFO always sandbags guidance. It's never written down, so there's nothing for AI to learn from.
>
> **[45–60s, the payoff]** So AI made the fast parts faster, and left the slow parts exactly where they were.
>
> **[60–75s, the Monday move]** Try this. Next earnings morning, write down where every 15 minutes actually goes. I'd bet most of it isn't "getting data". That list is your real automation roadmap.

### LinkedIn version

> We were told AI would be doing the analyst's job by now.
>
> Walk into a research team on earnings morning. Someone is still typing numbers into a spreadsheet before the open.
>
> Not because AI failed. Because it fixed the wrong part.
>
> Getting the numbers was never the bottleneck. You can buy them clean today.
>
> The slow parts are still slow:
> → Getting numbers into *your* model, your layout, your adjustments
> → Waiting for compliance to clear every call and dataset
> → The judgement that lives in a senior analyst's head and was never written down
>
> AI automated the parts of research that were already fast.
>
> Try this: next earnings morning, log where every 15 minutes goes. That list is your real automation roadmap.
>
> Where does yours actually go?

### Receipts and guardrails
- **Vault:**
  - `problems/sell-side-equity-research/low-impact-2.md` (the last-mile model fill; clean data already sold by Daloopa/Canalyst/CapIQ/FactSet)
  - `problems/hedge-funds/low-impact-2.md` (compliance queue)
  - `problems/sell-side-equity-research/high-impact.md` (judgement never recorded)
- **Don't claim:**
  - any percentage of time spent ("80% of an analyst's day…")
  - that AI "failed"
- **Keep it fair.** Say "AI fixed the wrong part", not "AI doesn't work".

## 2. The Research Bottleneck Nobody Talks About

| | |
|---|---|
| **Title A (lead)** | The Research Bottleneck Nobody Talks About |
| **Title B** | It's Not the Data. It's Waiting for a "Yes." |
| **Title C** | Your Research Is Fast. Your Approval Isn't. |
| **Cover text** | "It's not the data." |
| **Lever → promise** | "Nobody talks about" (the strongest template in the corpus: 75% sweet-spot, 4.1% like rate, highest comment rate) → a Monday action. |
| **The line to repeat** | **"Treating every expert call the same isn't caution. It's just slow."** |

### Video script

> **[0–5s, cold open]** At most funds, the slowest step in research isn't the data, the model or the analyst. It's waiting for a "yes."
>
> **[5–20s, the setup]** Every expert call, every channel check, every new dataset carries one risk: someone tells you something they shouldn't, which is inside information. So before research happens, compliance has to clear it.
>
> **[20–45s, the mechanism]** And here's the thing nobody says out loud: most firms clear these in the order they arrive. A retired consultant talking about an industry trend waits in the same line as a current employee of a supplier, the week before earnings.
> Those are not the same risk. One deserves an hour of scrutiny. The other deserves a two-minute approval with the reason written down.
>
> **[45–60s, the twist]** The tools most firms own don't help. They're surveillance tools, built to catch problems *after* the conversation. Research needs the opposite: a decision *before* it.
>
> **[60–80s, the Monday move]** So try this. Take last month's requests and sort them: routine, worth a look, genuinely risky. Most will be routine. Clear those fast, and give the risky few the attention they actually need. Faster *and* safer.

### LinkedIn version

> The slowest step in finance research isn't the data.
>
> It's waiting for a "yes."
>
> Every expert call, dataset and channel check has to be cleared for inside-information risk before research can happen. Fair enough.
>
> But most teams clear them in the order they arrive.
>
> A retired consultant discussing an industry trend waits in the same queue as a current supplier employee the week before earnings.
>
> Those aren't the same risk.
>
> Treating every expert call the same isn't caution. It's just slow.
>
> Sort last month's requests into routine / worth a look / genuinely risky. Clear the routine ones in minutes, with the reason recorded. Spend the saved time where the risk actually is.
>
> Faster and more defensible. Rare combination.

### Receipts and guardrails
- **Vault:**
  - `problems/hedge-funds/low-impact-2.md` (clearance is manual; surveillance tools are after-the-fact; Investment Advisers Act policies-and-procedures basis)
  - `niches/hedge-funds/research-compliance-mnpi/build.md` (risk is concentrated; arrival-order review)
  - `problems/expert-networks/low-impact-1.md`
- **Don't claim:**
  - a specific share of requests that are "routine"
  - wait times in days
  - that any firm "ignores" risk
- **Tone:** compliance officers are part of the audience. The villain is the *process* (arrival order), never the people.

## 3. Wall Street Gets Its Homework Graded. Nobody Looks.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Title A (lead)** | Wall Street Gets Its Homework Graded. Nobody Looks. |
| **Title B** | Your Research Has an Answer Key. Nobody Checks It. |
| **Title C** | Every Forecast Gets Graded. Nobody Keeps Score. |
| **Cover text** | "The answers come back every quarter." |
| **Lever → promise** | Named actor (Wall Street) + broken expectation → a repeatable line and a Monday action. The verdict shape is ~36% sweet in the corpus. |
| **The line to repeat** | **"Your team publishes the forecasts. The market publishes the answers. Nobody is matching them."** |

### Video script

> **[0–5s, cold open]** Here's something strange about finance research. Almost every call you make gets graded, publicly, on a date you know in advance. And almost nobody checks the grade.
>
> **[5–25s, the examples]** An analyst forecasts revenue. The company reports. That's the answer.
> A data provider estimates sales before earnings. Earnings day, answer.
> A valuation team marks a private company every quarter. One day it's sold. Answer.
>
> **[25–45s, the gap]** The forecasts live in one place. The answers arrive somewhere else. Nobody joins them. So nobody knows which analyst is actually right about which kind of company, or which kind of evidence ever moved the needle.
> And when that analyst leaves, the judgement walks out with them, ungraded.
>
> **[45–60s, the payoff]** The irony is that this is the cheapest edge in finance. The answer key is free. You just have to write the question down *before* the answer arrives.
>
> **[60–80s, the Monday move]** So start a three-column log. The call. The date. What would prove you wrong. Thirty seconds per idea. At quarter-end, grade it. In a year, you'll know something about your own judgement that most of the industry never will.

### LinkedIn version

> Finance has a strange blind spot.
>
> Almost every research call gets graded, publicly, on a known date.
>
> → Revenue forecast? Earnings day is the answer key.
> → Pre-earnings sales estimate? Same.
> → Quarterly valuation mark on a private company? The exit price, eventually.
>
> And almost nobody matches the forecast to the answer.
>
> Your team publishes the forecasts. The market publishes the answers. Nobody is matching them.
>
> So nobody knows whose judgement is actually good, on what kind of call. And when that person leaves, the judgement leaves too, never measured.
>
> The cheapest fix in finance:
> One line per idea. The call. The date. What would prove you wrong.
> Grade it at quarter-end.
>
> A year from now you'll know something about your own judgement most of the industry never will.

### Receipts and guardrails
- **Vault:**
  - `_scorecard-index-phase4.md` § The pattern (the same finding across all seven Phase 4 industries)
  - `niches/hedge-funds/alt-data-kpi-research-providers/build.md` (estimates vs the print)
  - `niches/private-equity-firms/portfolio-valuation-advisory/build.md` (marks rebuilt quarterly, rarely graded against exits)
  - `problems/asset-managers/high-impact.md` and `problems/sell-side-equity-research/high-impact.md` (judgement never joined to outcomes; leaves with the analyst)
- **Don't claim:**
  - "nobody in the industry" does this. Some vendors already weight consensus by past accuracy; "almost nobody" is the honest version
  - any hit-rate or accuracy statistic
- **Be careful with the "Wall Street" framing.** The video is about a habit, not an accusation against any firm.

## How to run the launch

- **Week 1:** piece 1, as video plus LinkedIn post. It has the broadest audience and is designed for reach.
- **Week 2:** piece 2. It's the most comment-prone: people will argue about their own compliance queue.
- **Week 3:** piece 3. Its "start this log" habit is the most shareable takeaway of the three.
- **What to watch:**
  - Did they click? Impressions-to-views for video, impressions-to-"see more" on LinkedIn.
  - Was it worth it? Saves, shares and comment *length*, not the like count.
  - If a piece gets clicks but no saves, it tipped into bait. Strengthen the Monday move.
  - If it gets saves but no clicks, the title is too insider. Swap to the B or C title.
- **Commercial thread:** each Monday move is a manual version of a research-workflow automation:
  - the time log is workflow discovery
  - triage is clearance automation
  - the forecast log is capture-and-grade
  
  You never pitch. The audience arrives already understanding why the product exists.
